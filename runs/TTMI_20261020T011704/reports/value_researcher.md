# TTM Technologies (TTMI) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price $99.10 (Oct 19, 2026) | Market cap $10.45B | Net debt $570M | EV ≈ $11.0B | Diluted sh. 107.6M**

Context that matters: TTMI traded near **$12.90 in Nov 2023** and peaked at **$143 in Aug 2026** — a ~10x re-rating on AI data-center PCB demand. It is now 31% off that high after a sector-wide de-rating in electronic components. This is a business whose *story* changed far faster than its *financials*.

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: NARROW

TTM is a contract manufacturer of printed circuit boards and RF/microwave components (~$2.9B FY25 revenue, 18,200 employees), split between commercial PCBs, advanced HDI/substrate-like boards, and a defense/aerospace "Mission Systems" franchise.

**Where a moat genuinely exists:**
- **Qualification barriers in defense/aerospace.** DoD-trusted-supplier status and 2–3 year customer qualification cycles create real switching costs on mission-systems work. This segment is the durable core.
- **Capability in high-layer-count and SLP/IC-substrate boards** for AI accelerators — a supply-constrained niche where capacity, not price, is the constraint today.

**Why the moat is not wide:**
- Gross margin of **19.6% (FY25), 21.1% (Q2'26)** and operating margin of 9.0% / 10.9% are the arithmetic of a negotiated, dual-sourced manufacturing business. A wide-moat business does not earn sub-20% gross margins.
- **ROIC has been 9.1% (2025), 4.5% (2024), 1.7% (2023), 8.4% (2022)** — persistently at or below any reasonable WACC. The wide-moat test (ROIC > WACC *consistently*) fails outright. The current ROIC improvement is cyclical, not structural.
- Revenue fell **-10.5% in 2023**. That is a cyclical, not a franchise, signature.
- The moat **has not widened** over five years on the metrics that matter (returns on capital); only the demand backdrop improved.

**Strengths:** Revenue inflecting sharply (Q2'26 $1.0B, +37% YoY; Q3 guided ~$1.13B), margins grinding higher, balance sheet sound — net debt/EBITDA ≈ 0.8x, interest coverage ~10x, no dividend obligation.

**The problem:** **FCF conversion is essentially zero.** Four-year cumulative FCF is $247M against $310M of net income, and FY25/H1'26 FCF is negative because capex has ballooned to ~13% of sales. Owner earnings (NI + D&A – capex) were **$32M in FY25** — a 0.3% yield on the market cap. Meanwhile goodwill + intangibles are $807M, **42% of equity**, and stock comp runs $42M/year with share count rising from 102.7M to 107.6M.

Financial quality score is capped at 6 because the growth is real but is being *purchased* with capital at returns below cost of capital.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — Fair value ≈ $85–90; price at a ~12–15% premium

Trailing multiples (FY25 basis) flatter nothing: **P/E 59x, EV/EBITDA 27.5x, P/B 5.9x, P/FCF n.m.** The peer median P/E of 44x is a poor comp (the sector tool returns NVDA/AAPL/MSFT), but note TTMI commanded **10–15x earnings for most of the last decade** at similar or better margins.

**Forward math (my estimates, GAAP):** FY26 EPS ~$3.5, FY27 ~$4.8–5.0 → **28x FY26, ~20x FY27.** EV/FY26E EBITDA (~$675M) ≈ **16x**. Not absurd for a 35% grower — but let me test what the price demands.

**Reverse DCF — what $99 requires:** At a 20x owner-earnings multiple, an $11.0B EV requires **~$550M of owner earnings**. FY26E owner earnings are roughly $340M (NI ~$340M + D&A ~$160M – maintenance capex ~$160M). So the price already capitalizes another **~60% growth in owner earnings** — 3–4 years of flawless 15–20% revenue growth *with* continued margin expansion *and* capex normalizing. You are paying today for 2029–2030 outcomes.

**Forward DCF, base case:** Revenue $6.0B by 2028 (above management's own 15–20% growth framing), 12% operating margin, capex normalizing to ~1.1x D&A, 10% discount, 3% terminal → **≈$6.8B equity value, ~$63–70/share.**

**Bull case** (AI cycle runs to $7.5B revenue, 14% margins, FCF $650M, 9% WACC): **$120–130/share.** **Bear case** (AI capex digestion in 2027, margins revert to 7% mid-cycle, FCF negative): **$50–60.**

Probability-weighted (50/25/25): **intrinsic value ≈ $85–88.** At $99.10 you pay a premium to intrinsic value and get **no margin of safety** — the opposite of what I require.

## 4. Management Quality — Adequate, unproven in this cycle

- CEO **Edwin Roks** (succeeded Tom Edman) inherits a strategy of aggressive capacity expansion into AI data-center demand. Ambition is evident; execution through a full cycle is not yet proven.
- **Capital allocation is the central question.** FY26 capex is running at ~$350–400M — roughly 2.5x depreciation. If high-end PCB capacity earns 15%+ ROIC, that's value-accretive; historically this company earned 8–9% ROIC on its asset base. The goodwill-heavy M&A history (Anaren, Telephonics, via Holdings) is mixed — the defense assets improved mix, but $807M of intangibles against $1.9B of equity gives limited room for error.
- **No dividend; buybacks minimal and non-opportunistic** ($18M, $34M in recent years) — and they were buying back stock at $20–40 while issuing shares via SBC at $100. SBC of $42M (~2% of market cap) is a real, ongoing transfer to employees.
- Insider ownership data was not available to me in this dataset — **noted as a gap**; I would want it before owning this.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate; would trim into strength)

This is a genuinely improving business riding a real demand wave, run by competent people, with a defensible niche in defense electronics and advanced AI boards. But my job is to buy great businesses at a discount to intrinsic value, and TTMI offers neither of those two things at $99:

1. **Returns on capital have never cleared the cost of capital** across the cycle — the opposite of a moated compounder.
2. **Free cash flow is negative at the top of a boom** while capex triples. The growth is real but it is consuming capital, not generating it.
3. **The price embeds the bull case.** Fair value ~$85–88 versus $99.10. My entry discipline (20–30% margin of safety) implies buying **at or below ~$68–70**, roughly 30% lower, or on evidence of 2027 revenue/margin durability at a much lower price.

If I already owned it after a 7x run, I would hold a reduced position — the AI-capacity franchise is worth keeping a ticket on — but I would not add here.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (both directions)

**Downside:**
- **AI capex digestion.** One hyperscaler pause in 2027 hits a business with 13% capex/sales and 2.14 beta. The stock's 31% drawdown in six weeks (Oct 12–16 alone: -6%, -13%, -8.5%) proves how violently it re-prices.
- **Margin mean-reversion.** 10.9% operating margins are near the top of the historical range (2023 was 1.9%). Peak margins on peak volume is the classic value trap setup.
- **Capex overbuild** into a cyclical peak would leave $400M/yr of underutilized capacity and negative FCF for years — exactly the pattern of 2019–2024.
- **Concentration**: receivables up 24% YoY to $1.03B, top customers in hyperscale/defense; tariffs and export controls on a US-China footprint.
- **Dilution**: share count +4.8% in two years from SBC.
- **Event risk**: Q3 results Oct 22 — a miss on $1.25 adj. EPS resets the narrative.

**Upside risk to my caution (why not Sell):** if TTM's high-layer-count and substrate capacity is structurally allocation-constrained through 2028, 15–20% revenue growth with 13–14% operating margins is achievable, and FCF inflects hard once capex normalizes — the bull case ($120–130) is not fantasy. Analysts' targets have followed the stock up ($231 Truist, $175 Needham), which I regard as momentum, not evidence.

**Where I'd be wrong:** if TTM proves this is a structurally higher-margin, capacity-constrained business (sustained ROIC >15%, FCF conversion >70%) rather than a cyclical PCB maker, my fair value is too low and I would need to re-underwrite the moat rating from Narrow to Wide. I do not yet see that in the numbers.
