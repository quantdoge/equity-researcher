# TTMI — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company:** TTM Technologies, Inc. (NASDAQ: TTMI) — largest US-based PCB manufacturer, 18,200 employees, HQ Santa Ana, CA. FY2025 net sales $2.91B (+19% YoY); Q2 2026 net sales $1.0B (+37% YoY). Sector: Technology / Hardware. Market cap ~$10.4B.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US restrictions on China-fabricated PCBs in defense systems (10 U.S.C. §4873 / DFARS)
- **What:** DoD issued an Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking on **2026-07-02** to implement 10 U.S.C. §4873, which **effective January 1, 2027 prohibits DoD from acquiring "covered printed circuit boards" from entities located in or controlled by China, Russia, Iran, or North Korea.** The rule turns on *geographic point of fabrication* of bare/partially manufactured boards, and previews a four-pillar waiver regime (ISO/IEC 20243 certification, IPC-1782 Level 3/4 machine-level traceability, routing through an IPC-1791 Trusted Assembler or DoD Hardware Assurance Lab, CUI protection), 10-year data retention, government inspection rights, and **mandatory flow-down at every subcontract tier**. Comments closed 2026-08-31. ([Wiley alert, 2026-07-07](https://www.wiley.law/alert-DoD-Considers-Certification-Based-Framework-for-Restricting-Certain-Printed-Circuit-Boards-in-Defense-Systems))
- **Probability:** High (statutory deadline is fixed; only the implementing detail is open).
- **Impact:** **Net ambiguous, skewing positive but with execution risk.** TTMI fabricates defense boards in the US, so it is a structural *share gainer* as Chinese-origin boards are excluded from DoD systems — consistent with its **$1.7B defense backlog and $7B+ pipeline**. The risk is compliance cost and certification failure: if any TTMI board routes through a China-affiliated entity or lacks IPC-1782 traceability, it becomes non-compliant and primes (who retain "affirmative, ultimate responsibility") may de-source. Also watch the **Strengthening Protections Against Chinese PCBs Act** lineage, which sought to extend the ban beyond military hardware to "critical national security commercial items" including cloud servers/networks — directly relevant to TTMI's data-center vertical ([Rep. Blake Moore release](https://blakemoore.house.gov/media/press-releases/congressman-blake-moore-and-senator-josh-hawley-introduce-strengthening)).

### Risk 2 — China operational/asset concentration and cross-Strait escalation
- **What:** TTMI operates a substantial PCB fabrication footprint in mainland China. China controls roughly **half of global PCB production**, making the sector a named target in US supply-chain-security policy ([USCC, Chained to China, Ch. 9](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2025-11/Chapter_9--Chained_to_China_Beijings_Weaponization_of_Supply_Chains.pdf)).
- **Probability:** Medium for tariff/export-licence friction (ongoing); Low–Medium for genuine disruption (blockade, forced divestiture).
- **Impact:** High if realized. China-sited plants represent a meaningful share of commercial capacity and would be difficult to repatriate quickly; a forced divestiture or licence regime would hit commercial (automotive/networking) revenue, not defense.

### Risk 3 — Chinese export controls on critical raw materials and tariff escalation
- **What:** Beijing has imposed export controls on **gallium, germanium, rare earths and at least seven other materials since 2023**, with the 2025–26 round restricting even trace-Chinese-content items ([CFR](https://www.cfr.org/reports/dismantling-chinas-supply-chain-weapon); [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/chinas-new-rare-earth-and-magnet-restrictions-threaten-us-defense-supply-chains)). These are direct inputs to PCB substrates, RF/microwave components and ceramic RF parts — TTMI's core products. Layered on top: US Section 301 tariffs on Chinese PCBs and the 2025 IEEPA tariff regime, which raise landed cost on China-to-US flows.
- **Probability:** High (already in force).
- **Impact:** Medium — margin compression and lead-time volatility; partially passable through pricing but difficult in fixed-price defense contracts. Note the 10-K explicitly cites US–China trade measures as a risk factor.

*Secondary:* the Ophir acquisition introduces Israel-based manufacturing and R&D into a conflict-affected geography, though defense/IR optics demand is a tailwind and the deal is >18x forward EBITDA on a $650M all-cash outlay — financing/leverage and integration risk more than geopolitical risk.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **No material pending litigation disclosed.** No securities class action, DOJ/FTC/SEC enforcement action, or IP dispute of size appears in recent filings or news. The FY2025 10-K and Q2 2026 10-Q were filed on schedule (no NT 10-K/10-Q delay). Treat "no disclosed material litigation" as the base case; confirm against the 10-K Part I, Item 3.
- **Data breach class action (December 2023):** a putative class action arising from a 2023 breach is referenced by claims aggregators ([classaction.org](https://www.classaction.org/data-breach-lawsuits/ttm-technologies-december-2023)). Likely immaterial financially; the real exposure is **data-privacy/CUI compliance**, which becomes materially more important under the new DFARS traceability and CUI-protection requirements above.
- **Regulatory posture — favorable tailwind:** defense reshoring policy, trusted-supplier standards (IPC-1791/IPC-1782), and DoD hardware-assurance frameworks all advantage the largest US-domiciled PCB fabricator against Asian competitors. This is the single most important regulatory fact for TTMI.
- **Export controls (ITAR/EAR):** as a defense supplier, TTMI is subject to ITAR and EAR; the Ophir acquisition adds infrared-optics technology that is export-controlled and will require CFIUS/DDTC review and integration of an Israeli entity into a US defense supply chain.

## 5. Overall Rating: **MEDIUM–HIGH**

**Why not lower:** ~15% of revenue and a meaningful slice of fabrication capacity sit in China, which is the explicit target of a statutory US import prohibition taking effect **January 1, 2027**; input costs are exposed to live Chinese export controls on gallium/germanium/rare earths; and customer concentration jumped to 55% of sales, which amplifies any single prime's compliance or sourcing decision.

**Why not higher:** the direction of US policy is *toward* TTMI, not against it. As the largest US PCB manufacturer with a $1.7B defense backlog, TTMI is the intended beneficiary of Trusted Supplier / §4873 reshoring — the geopolitical risk is largely a **share-shift and compliance-cost risk rather than an existential one**. Legal exposure is genuinely low: no material active litigation, no sanctions or anti-corruption findings, and legacy environmental/False Claims items are small and dated.

**Key monitorables:** (1) the DFARS proposed rule text and whether TTMI's certifications satisfy the Independent Hardware Assurance Framework; (2) any expansion of PCB sourcing bans beyond defense into "critical national security commercial items" (data-center servers); (3) Chinese rare-earth/gallium licensing decisions; (4) CFIUS/DDTC clearance and Israel-based operational continuity for Ophir; (5) 10-K geographic revenue footnote for confirmation of China's exact share.
