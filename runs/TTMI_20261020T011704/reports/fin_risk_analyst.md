# TTMI (TTM Technologies) — Financial Risk Assessment

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: Sub-Investment Grade (BB+/B+), but on a clear improving trajectory — LOW near-term distress risk.**

TTM is rated by S&P at **BB+ (secured) / B+ (unsecured)** — squarely high-yield, reflecting the cyclicality of PCB manufacturing and a history of leveraged M&A-adjacent capital structure. However, the *fundamental* credit profile is materially better than the ratings imply and is strengthening:

- **Net leverage has fallen sharply:** FY2025 net debt/EBITDA **1.53x**; on trailing-twelve-month (TTM through Q2-2026) EBITDA of ~$495M vs. net debt $616M, leverage is **~1.24x** — a conservative level for a BB+ issuer.
- **Debt is cheap and long-dated.** The stack is dominated by the **4.000% senior notes due 2029** and a **$500M 6.75% notes due 2034**, backstopped by a new **$1.0B multi-currency revolver maturing May 2031**. The nearest maturity is 2029 — **no refinancing cliff**.
- **Coverage is strong and rising.** FY2025 EBIT/interest = **5.6–5.8x**; TTM EBIT coverage ≈ **7.8x** as EBITDA has surged on AI/data-center PCB demand.
- **Altman Z = 2.14 (Grey zone)** — but the model uses *book* equity; substituting $10.4B market cap (x4) pushes Z comfortably into the safe zone, so the grey reading is a mechanical artifact of the book-equity convention, not a real solvency flag.
- **Piotroski F-Score = 8/9 (Strong)** — profitable, cash-generative, deleveraging, improving margins and turns. Only miss is **F7 (no dilution)** from routine SBC.

TTMI is a high-beta AI/data-center infrastructure proxy. A beta >3x vs. the S&P means a broad market drawdown is amplified roughly threefold, and 85% realized vol implies the stock can halve on no change in fundamentals. The 57.6% max drawdown is empirical evidence of that tail. **Momentum and multiple-expansion risk are extreme** — the equity is priced for continued AI capex acceleration.

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue −20%

**Base case FY2025:** Revenue $2,906M · Gross margin 19.6% · EBIT $256M · EBITDA $403M · Interest $45M · Net income $177M · Net debt $616M.

Modeling a 20% revenue decline ($581M) with fixed-cost deleverage (~$450M of COGS fixed, SG&A ~90% fixed, SG&A cut 10%), gross margin compresses to ~15.5%:

| Line | Base FY25 | Downside (−20% rev) | % change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $2,906M | $2,325M | −20% |
| Gross profit | $568M | ~$365M | −36% |
| SG&A | $278M | ~$250M | −10% |
| EBIT | $256M | ~$115M | **−55%** |
| EBITDA | $403M | ~$262M | **−35%** |
| Interest | $45M | $45M | flat |
| EBIT / interest | 5.6x | **~2.6x** | still covered |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 1.53x | **~2.35x** | elevated |
| Net income | $177M | ~$52M | −71% |

**Severe / tail case (−30% revenue, GM to ~12%):** EBITDA falls to **~$150M**, net leverage jumps to **~4.1x**, EBIT coverage to **~0.5x** — the zone where a maintenance covenant on a drawn revolver and goodwill impairment ($825M, 47% of equity) become live concerns.

**Empirical anchor:** In FY2023 TTM's revenue fell only **~10.5%** and *net income swung to a −$18.7M loss* with EBIT collapsing to $42M (aided by one-time charges). This confirms the business carries **high operating leverage** — earnings are far more cyclical than the modest revenue beta suggests. The 2022→2023 episode is a real-world rehearsal of the downside.

**FCF resilience:** FY2025 FCF was **−$0.7M** only because capex hit $293M (10% of sales) on growth capacity. In a downturn capex is discretionary and would be cut hard, so FCF would likely *improve* even as EBITDA falls — liquidity is not the binding constraint.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

- **Credit/solvency risk — LOW-MODERATE:** ~1.2–1.5x net leverage, 7–8x coverage, $501M cash + $1B revolver, first maturity 2029. A 20% revenue drop is absorbed; only a severe multi-year −30% scenario stresses covenants.
- **Market/equity risk — HIGH:** 85% vol, >3x beta, 57.6% historical drawdown, stock up ~5x — the *share price* is the fragile item, not the balance sheet.
- **FCF quality — WATCH:** near-zero trailing FCF yield (−0.01%) driven by peak growth capex.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Negative free cash flow (−$0.7M FY25; ~0% FCF yield)** — capex (10% of revenue) consumes all operating cash flow; FCF is entirely dependent on capex discipline.
2. **Extreme market risk** — 85% vol, 3.28x beta, 57.6% max drawdown; a ~5x re-rating leaves little valuation cushion.
3. **Sub-investment-grade ratings** (BB+/B+) and a **B+ unsecured** notch.
4. **High operating leverage** — FY2023 proved a ~10% revenue dip drives net income negative.
5. **Working-capital build** — receivables +24% (to $1,032M) outpaced revenue +19%; −$85M WC drag and rising DSO.
6. **Goodwill of $825M = 47% of equity** — impairment risk in a prolonged downturn (already amortizing down from $1,048M).
7. **Mild share dilution** from SBC (the sole Piotroski miss).

**Data gaps:** I did not obtain a formal contractual covenant schedule (leverage covenant levels/step-downs) or a peer-multiple comparison; covenant-breach risk is therefore inferred from typical high-yield revolver structures and should be confirmed against the credit agreement. The debt-maturity figures rely on public press releases (2029/2034 notes, 2031 revolver) rather than a full indenture schedule.

**Bottom line:** TTM can comfortably *survive* a prolonged downturn — the balance sheet, liquidity, and maturity profile are the strongest parts of the story. The financial risk is concentrated in **market/valuation risk and thin FCF**, not in solvency. Monitor working-capital creep, capex discipline, and any deceleration in AI/data-center demand as the primary triggers.
