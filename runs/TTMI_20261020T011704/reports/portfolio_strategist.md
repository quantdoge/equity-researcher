## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT** (leaning negative on *entry price*)

This is not a disagreement about the *business* — it is a disagreement about *price and timing*, and the split is clean:

- **Fundamental block (Sector, Growth, Alt Data):** demand is accelerating, margins are genuinely re-rating, guidance was raised, CEO bought $1.12M open-market at $111.77. **Bullish.**
- **Valuation/risk block (Value, Quant, Macro, Fin Risk):** FCF is negative at the peak of the cycle, ROIC has never sustainably cleared WACC, the multiple requires 2029 outcomes, and 5%-handle long yields are compressing exactly this kind of asset. **Bearish on the equity, not the company.**
- **Risk block (Geo, Nonfin, ESG):** compliance-cost and execution risk, not existential.
- **The decisive datapoint:** the Short Analyst — the agent whose job is to find fraud — affirmatively **declined the short** at $99. That removes the tail-risk scenario that would normally force a Pass. Symmetrically, **no agent** argues TTMI is cheap at $99.

**Synthesis of fair value:** Value $85–88 · Short Analyst $110–120 · Growth's haircut case ~$99–102 (16.5x its $6.00 FY27 EPS). Median ≈ **$100 vs a $99.10 price.** The market is at fair value. There is no mispricing to exploit — only a **binary catalyst in two days**.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **0% now → 1.5% conditional (max 2.0%)**

The variance, however, is not zero — it is 85% annualised. Paying fair value for a 3.3-beta binary event is a negative-expectation trade even when the business is good.

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Correlation / factor — the binding constraint.** Measured 180-day correlations: **JBL 0.70, CLS 0.58, FLEX 0.57, SPY 0.52, NVDA 0.44.** TTMI is *not* a diversifier — it is a leveraged expression of the same AI-capex/EMS factor already present in most growth books. Adding TTMI alongside any of JBL/CLS/FLEX creates a **~0.6–0.7 correlated cluster**, and the effective AI-hardware weight should be managed at the *cluster* level, not the ticker level. If combined AI-infrastructure exposure already exceeds 12–15% of the book, **do not add TTMI at all** regardless of the earnings print.

**Sector concentration:** Technology Hardware. Confirm current overweight — if the book is already >25% tech, this is a Pass on fit grounds alone.

**Factor tilts introduced:** +beta (3.28 OLS vs 2.14 reported), +momentum-reversal (12-1 −15.3%), −value (59x P/E, 27.5x EV/EBITDA), −quality-via-FCF (P/FCF not meaningful). This deepens three tilts simultaneously in a hawkish-rate regime. Mitigant: only **26% of variance is market-explained (R² 0.257)** — most of the risk is idiosyncratic and *unhedgeable* by index shorting.

**Factor that cuts the other way:** TTMI's 44% Aerospace & Defense revenue is genuinely decorrelated from AI capex. Sector and Geo both highlight this; the market is currently pricing it as an AI proxy and ignoring it. This is the real reason to own *some*.

**Portfolio-level risk I flag that the agents under-weighted:** Fin Risk's 1.24x net debt/EBITDA is **stale**. It is a pre-acquisition balance sheet. Pro-forma for the $1.1B debt-funded Epiq close (30-Sep) plus the $650M all-cash Ophir deal (19-Oct), net debt moves from $616M toward **~$2.2–2.4B** — i.e. **~2.5–2.7x FY26E EBITDA and ~4x trailing**. Combined with negative FCF, that is a materially different credit profile than "conservative BB+." Growth, Short and Nonfin all flag the deal; **only Fin Risk had the balance sheet, and its balance sheet predates the deals.** Treat leverage as a live, under-priced risk until the Q3 10-Q confirms it.
