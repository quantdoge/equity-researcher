## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Quantified (FY2025 CSR report):**
- Energy use: **743,708 MWh** → ~256 MWh per $1M revenue — genuinely energy-intensive for a component maker
- Scope 2 emissions: **121,328 tCO2e**; management states **>90% of emissions are Scope 2** (purchased electricity), implying Scope 1+2 of roughly **130–135k tCO2e**
- Carbon intensity: **≈46 tCO2e per $1M revenue** — mid-pack for electronics manufacturing, low in absolute terms versus process industries
- **>90% of hazardous waste recycled**; water consumption per revenue dollar declining three consecutive years
- Reports GHG and water data annually to **CDP**; target of **carbon neutrality by 2040** (Scope 1+2)

**Assessment.** TTM's footprint is a classic "small absolute, high intensity" profile: its emissions are immaterial to global climate outcomes but material to its own cost base and to customer qualification. Because >90% of emissions are purchased electricity, the decarbonisation lever is straightforward (PPAs, renewable tariffs) but capital-constrained by geography — a large share of load sits in China, where grid carbon intensity is high and renewable procurement options are narrower. The 2040 carbon-neutrality target is **not** SBTi-validated and sits behind peers who have committed to 2030–2035 Scope 1+2 reduction with science-based methodology; there is **no disclosed Scope 3 target**, which is the gap most likely to be raised by data-center and hyperscaler customers. Water is the sharper risk: PCB fabs in water-stressed Chinese and Taiwanese regions face tightening industrial water allocation, and TTM's own history includes a **$9.7M water-pollution penalty** (legacy Tyco Printed Circuit Group, 2004). No material environmental enforcement in the last five years is a genuine positive.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- **Safety:** Total recordable incident rate has declined year-over-year and management states it remains **well below the U.S. industry average**; a global Safety Observation Reporting programme with site-level review is in place. Enforcement history is trivial — two OSHA citations since 2000 totalling **$10,000**.
- **Workforce:** ~18,200 employees across North America and Asia. Restructuring is an active theme — closure of Anaheim and Santa Clara (CA) PCB operations and Hong Kong operations carried **$22M+ in charges**, and the February 2026 retirement of two long-serving directors accompanies broader refresh. Severance/transition execution risk is modest and well within normal manufacturing norms.
- **Product safety:** Exposure is real but well-governed — A&D, medical and automotive end markets demand AS9100/IATF 16949-class quality systems. A single high-profile field failure in a defence or medical platform would be a licence-to-operate event; no such incident is disclosed.
- **Supply chain:** The most under-appreciated social item. A China/Asia-weighted supply base creates **UFLPA** and conflict-minerals (tantalum, tin, tungsten, gold) diligence obligations. TTM publishes supplier requirements and a supplier code, and has reduced China manufacturing exposure (sale of four China plants for $550M), which structurally lowers this risk.
- **Recent penalty record:** five **EBSA benefit-plan-administrator violations 2021–2025** (~$270k cumulative) — administrative/plan-governance failures, immaterial financially but a small repeat-offence signal in benefits administration.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **7.5 / 10**

**Strengths**
- **Independent Chair** (Geveden) separate from the CEO — no CEO/Chair duality
- Board overwhelmingly independent, with two designated audit-committee financial experts (Ali, Franklin) and relevant industry depth (England ex-Texas Instruments, Franklin ex-Littelfuse)
- **Annual** say-on-pay frequency recommended by the board; single class of stock (no dual-class voting structure identified); KPMG as auditor; whistleblower policy published
- Active refresh: two new independent directors appointed Feb 2026, two retirements — genuine board renewal, not entrenchment
- CEO transition executed in Sept 2025 (Roks succeeding Edman) without a governance dispute

**Detractors**
- **Classified (staggered) board** — Class II directors serve to 2029; a structural anti-takeover feature that dilutes annual shareholder accountability
- **Very limited compensation disclosure in accessible sources** — I could not verify the precise weighting of long-term versus short-term metrics or whether ESG/quality metrics enter the incentive plan. This is a genuine evidence gap, not a clean bill of health
- New CEO is also a board nominee, and the CFO/CEO transition period raises key-person and oversight-transition risk
- Capital allocation aggressiveness: the **$650M all-cash Ophir Optics deal at >18x forward EBITDA**, on top of an already capital-intensive fab expansion programme, is a leverage decision taken with limited apparent shareholder consultation — a governance item worth monitoring through the credit lens

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

|---|---|---|
| **SEC Conflict Minerals Rule (13p-1)** | **Applies** — 3TG in PCB substrates | Hard legal obligation; highest-certainty ESG compliance cost |
| **UFLPA** | **Applies** — China-sourced inputs | Detention/seizure risk on inputs; mitigated by China footprint reduction |
| **US export controls (BIS/EAR), DFARS/CMMC** | **Applies, high salience** | 44% A&D revenue makes export-control compliance a core licence-to-operate issue; sector enforcement is escalating (cf. $252.5M BIS settlement precedent) |
| **Section 301 China tariffs** | **Applies** | Upheld by Supreme Court denial of certiorari (June 2026); a cost-of-goods item disguised as trade policy |
| **EU CSRD** | **Partly applies** — European operations plus newly acquired Ophir (EU/Israel footprint) | Scope expands post-Ophir; likely indirect via large EU customers first |
| **CBAM** | Indirect | PCBs not in scope, but aluminium/copper inputs are — pass-through cost |

| **ESG-linked debt covenants** | None identified | Positive — no financing contingent on ESG metrics |

Notably, **S&P Global Ratings has stated ESG factors have "no material influence" on its credit analysis of TTM** — an independent confirmation that environmental and social factors are not currently a cost-of-capital driver for this issuer.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (upper half of Average; not yet a Leader)

TTM sits at the better end of the PCB peer group: independent chair, a real energy/water/waste disclosure regime with a 2040 neutrality target, materially declining injury rates, and no live ESG controversy worth the name. It falls short of Leader status because its climate target is not science-based, Scope 3 is unaddressed, its emissions data is largely self-reported without third-party assurance confirmed in my sources, and it carries a staggered board with thin public compensation-metric transparency.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

ESG is **not** a driver of the TTMI thesis in either direction, and should not be weighted in a valuation call. The three ESG items with genuine financial transmission channels are:

1. **Water and chemical permitting at Asian fabs** — a tail risk with seven-figure historical precedent; monitor for any new discharge enforcement.
2. **Conflict minerals / UFLPA / export control compliance** — the most probable source of an unpleasant surprise given the A&D concentration; this is a governance and legal-risk item more than an ethical one.
3. **Scope 3 and renewable-procurement demands from hyperscaler data-center customers** — the one place where lagging ESG performance could translate into lost share in the fastest-growing end market.

The item I would flag to the investment team as **under-priced relative to its ESG framing** is not environmental at all: the **$650M all-cash Ophir acquisition at >18x EBITDA**, layered on heavy fab capex, is a capital-allocation and balance-sheet-leverage decision that a governance-focused analyst should scrutinise more than any emissions metric.
