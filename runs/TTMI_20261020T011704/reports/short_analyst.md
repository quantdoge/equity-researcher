## 2. Key Red Flags Found

1. **Negative FCF at cycle peak** — capex $338M TTM vs OCF $323M. The AI upcycle is being *purchased*, not harvested.
2. **Working capital is a cash furnace** — H1'26 WC drain of **–$203.5M** vs –$87.4M in H1'25 (more than doubled). Q1'26 alone: –$139.0M.
3. **Receivables growth > revenue growth** (FY25: AR +24.3% vs revenue +19.0%). Same flag in FY24 (+16.5% vs +9.4%). Persistent, two years running.
4. **Anomalous receivables swing, unexplained** — net receivables fell from $1,131.1M (Q1'26) to $720.1M (Q2'26), a **–$411M drop while revenue rose 19% sequentially.** Inferred DSO collapses from ~122 days to ~65 days in one quarter. This could be a large collection, a customer prepayment — or receivables monetisation/factoring. **This is the single item I would demand from management before putting on any long.** I flag it honestly as possibly a data/reclassification artifact, but the FY-level AR-vs-revenue red flag stands independently.
5. **Non-GAAP gap widening** — Q2'26 GAAP EPS $0.77 vs non-GAAP **$0.99** (29% gap). SBC has roughly doubled YoY ($61.4M TTM = 26% of net income) and sits entirely outside the "adjusted EPS" number the sell-side anchors to.
6. **Altman Z-Score 2.135 — grey zone.** Book-equity based, so flattered only mildly; the market-cap version is better, but see #7.
7. **$1.1B acquisition at cycle peak + $500M new senior notes.** Buying scale at a top-of-cycle multiple, debt-funded, into a de-rating stock. Goodwill + intangibles already $806.6M = **42% of equity** — impairment risk if the AI board thesis sours. Pro-forma net debt moves from $570M toward ~$2B; interest expense steps up from ~$42M/yr.
8. **Inventory dollars +23% sequentially** ($250M → $280M → $308M) against COGS +19%. Inventory *days* are fine (39.0 vs 41.8), so this is likely ramp-building — but it is unproven demand.

## 3. Beneish M-Score & Accruals Analysis

**M-Score -2.40 → manipulation probability low. Accruals -2.98% → conservative.** The drivers of the negative accruals are rising D&A and, in FY25, a working-capital *release*. Note that the FY25 accrual figure is now stale relative to the H1'26 WC drain described above — I would expect the accruals ratio to deteriorate materially when FY26 is computed. **This is a "clean history, deteriorating present" pattern, not a restatement risk.**

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

This is where the genuine bear case lives.

- **Simultaneous industry-wide capacity race.** Zhen Ding raised 2026–27 capex by **66%** over plan and is targeting server/optical revenue to more than double in 2026; Unimicron is allocating ~45% of NT$18.6B to PCB; TTM itself is adding **$400M** across four China sites plus Penang. Everyone is building for the same AI accelerator boards, with 2027–28 delivery dates.
- **Commoditisation risk on high-layer-count boards.** Ultra-HDI/HLC PCB capability is a manufacturing-yield asset, not a patent moat. It has historically been cloned by Asian producers within one to two generations. TTMI's gross margin (21.1%) has expanded only ~170bp in two years *during the greatest AI buildout in history* — that is not pricing power, that is a pass-through of utilisation.
- **Two themes = 80% of sales.** Management states ~80% of net sales tied to AI infrastructure + defence modernisation. That is thematic concentration dressed as diversification across 1,300 customers. A single hyperscaler platform transition (GPU→ASIC board redesign, dual-sourcing) can gut a quarter.
- **Demonstrated downside.** FY2023 revenue –10.5%, net income swung from +$94.6M to –$18.7M. This business has shown it can go to roughly zero margin.

## 5. Management Red Flags

- **Insider selling cluster at the exact top.** Dozens of Form 4s filed 25–29 June 2026 (e.g., EVP Soder sold 6/25), with the stock printing its all-time high of $223.83 on 22 June. Filing at that density in that window is a textbook distribution signature.
- **Mitigant that must be stated:** CEO Edwin Roks made a **non-10b5-1 open-market purchase of 10,000 shares at $111.77 (~$1.12M)** on 25 August 2026. That is the opposite of what a manager expecting collapse does.
- Audit/board: no auditor changes, no related-party flags surfaced, no restatements. Revenue recognition appears orthodox. **Governance is the weakest link, not fraud.**
- **Dan Loeb's Third Point is building a significant position** and the company *raised* FY26 guidance to ~$4.4B revenue / ~$5.00 non-GAAP EPS. I am not going to pretend that is irrelevant.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — Is There a Credible Bear Case?

Yes, but it is a **cyclical/valuation** bear case, not a fraud case.

**The bear case:** TTMI is a capital-intensive, historically low-margin PCB fabricator that has been re-rated as an AI infrastructure compounder. It is converting 100% of its operating cash flow into capacity at precisely the moment every competitor is doing the same, producing negative FCF, rising leverage, a widening non-GAAP gap, and a 42%-of-equity goodwill balance built on peak-cycle M&A. When the 2027–28 capacity wave lands into any AI capex digestion, utilisation falls and the 22x EV/EBITDA multiple compresses toward the 8–12x a PCB maker has historically deserved. Severe case: EBITDA to $350–400M at 8x → **$21–25/share**.

**The other side — and it is stronger than most bears admit:**
- At $99 the stock is **19.8x FY26 non-GAAP EPS (~$5.00)**, not the 44x trailing GAAP headline. On consensus 2027 ($7.24) it is **13.7x**. That is not an expensive multiple for 30%+ growth.
- Consensus 2027 EBITDA of $1.33B at even 14x supports ~$115–130 — i.e. **the current price already embeds a meaningful de-rating.**
- Revenue is *accelerating*, margins are *expanding*, guidance was *raised* post-collapse, the CEO is buying, and Third Point is involved. Short float is only ~4% — the crowded-short fuel is absent.
- The 42–56% drawdown from the June peak has already delivered most of the mean-reversion.

The failure mode of the short is therefore specifically **estimate cuts**, which require visible AI capex digestion or share loss. Neither is yet in the data — Q3 guidance of $1.12B and $1.21–1.27 non-GAAP EPS is a sequential acceleration.

## 7. Contrarian Rating

The fraud/accounting short is **falsified** by the data: M-Score -2.40, accruals -3.0%, F-Score 8, no receivable/inventory deception at the annual level, no restatements. I decline to manufacture a manipulation narrative the numbers do not support.

The cyclical bear case is **real and analytically sound**, but it has already been paid out — the stock has halved from $223, and shorting a company that just beat, raised guidance, and whose CEO bought $1.1M of stock four weeks ago is poor risk/reward. My edge here is negative.

**Honest verdict:** this is a company whose bull case is stronger than its bear case right now. The forensic work says the numbers are real. The risk is price, not accounting — and price has already moved a long way toward me.
