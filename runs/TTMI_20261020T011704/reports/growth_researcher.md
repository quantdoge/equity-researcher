## TTM Technologies (TTMI) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $99.10 (Oct 19, 2026) · Mkt cap $10.45B · EV ≈$11.1B · Beta 2.14*

---

### 1. Growth Story

TTM has been re-rated from a low-margin, cyclical PCB job-shop into a **picks-and-shovels supplier of the AI data-center buildout** — high-layer-count boards, HDI, substrate-like PCBs and RF/microwave modules that sit inside AI accelerators, 800G/1.6T switches and power delivery. Data Center Computing has gone from a minor slice to ~24% of FY25 net sales, and Q2'26 revenue grew **37% YoY to a record $1.00B** with non-GAAP EPS +71%. Layered on top is a 44%-of-sales Aerospace & Defense franchise (radar, MOSAIC maritime surveillance, RF/SIGINT via the $1.1B Epiq Solutions deal), which funds credibility and backlog while AI scales. The stock has round-tripped **-55% from its June 2026 peak of $221** as investors balked at a debt-funded capex super-cycle; the growth engine, however, has not yet slowed.

### 3. TAM and Penetration

- **Global PCB industry ≈$80–90B**, but the relevant slice — high-layer-count, HDI/SLP, IC-substrate-class boards for AI accelerators, switches and power — is the fastest-growing, highest-specification segment (industry estimates circulating at 25–30%+ CAGR through 2028 as rack power density and layer counts rise).
- **Penetration:** TTM's data-center exposure is ~24% of a ~$4.4B FY26 revenue base, i.e. ~$1.0–1.1B against a high-end AI PCB pool plausibly in the $10–15B range today and expanding rapidly. Share gains, not just market growth, are driving the model.
- **Why TTM:** US-domiciled, defense-cleared, high-mix/low-volume specialist with **design-for-manufacturability and RF design-to-spec services** — the qualification barriers in AI boards (layer count, thermal, signal integrity) are real switching costs. It is one of few Western-headquartered suppliers hyperscalers can dual-source alongside Asian incumbents.
- **Moat quality:** thin. 19.5% gross margins and a 10% capex/revenue ratio describe a **capital-intensive enabler, not a software-style platform**. Pricing power is cyclical; the advantage is capacity, certification and geography, not IP royalties.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Q3'26 results, Oct 22, 2026 (pre-market)** — guidance $1.10–1.14B revenue, consensus EPS $1.25. The critical tell is the **Q4 guide** (consensus requires ~$1.4B) and whether adj. EBITDA margin clears 17%. This is a binary 3-day event.
2. **Epiq Solutions integration** (closed Sept 30, 2026, $1.1B debt-funded) — first consolidated quarter plus a deleveraging path; defense revenue mix and margin accretion.
3. **Penang, Malaysia ramp (2026–2027)** — non-China AI capacity is the antidote to the "hyperscalers won't source AI boards from China" overhang that knocked 10–17% off the stock on the Oct 13 8-K.
4. **Debt refinancing / leverage update** — the "2026 targets and refinancing" 8-K; any term-loan repricing or equity-linked raise caps the bear case.
5. **Analyst coverage expansion and PT revisions** — Truist raised to **$231** on Oct 13 (vs. $110 tape) while Needham trimmed to $175 in September; a wider bull camp would compress the current dispersion.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (staged entry, above-average volatility)

This is the cleanest "growth at a reasonable price after a violent de-rating" setup in the advanced-PCB complex: 37–51% revenue growth, a 7-for-7 beat record, +20% forward EPS revisions, a CEO buying stock at $112, and a multiple that has compressed from ~45x to **~22x forward EPS / ~14–16x 2027** while the order book accelerated. The 55% drawdown was about **financing and geography, not demand** — and both are addressable (Penang capacity, refinancing, defense cash flow).

My discipline: this is **not a Cathie Wood-style platform monopoly** — it is a cyclical, capital-hungry enabler whose gross margins cap terminal economics. So I underwrite a haircut to consensus (≈$4.20 FY26 / ≈$6.00 FY27 EPS), get 16–17x 2027, and size accordingly. I would build the position in two tranches: half now, half after the Oct 22 print confirms the Q4 ramp and the margin trajectory.

### 7. Key Risks

- **Negative free cash flow meets rising leverage.** $1.1B debt vs. $510M cash, ~10% capex/revenue, a **$1.2B 2025–2029 plan plus a new $400M program**, on top of a debt-funded $1.1B acquisition. FCF is ~$0. If AI demand digests for two quarters, high fixed-cost capacity deleverages fast and the equity — with a 2.14 beta — gaps down.
- **China concentration.** The incremental $400M goes largely to four Chinese plants; hyperscaler procurement, tariffs and export controls are live policy risks. Malaysia is the fix but lands 2027.
- **Estimate credibility.** Two analysts modeling revenue, and consensus margins above company guidance. The +20% revision "upgrade" may be extrapolation rather than insight; a single guide-down resets the whole table.
- **Customer concentration / AI capex cyclicality.** A Broadcom-style miss already triggered a sector-wide 12% single-day move in TTMI; hyperscaler digestion is the primary demand risk.
- **Competition.** Ibiden, Unimicron, Zhen Ding and AT&S are better capitalized in substrates; TTM's differentiation is service and Western geography, which can be replicated with capital.
- **Thin margin of safety on GAAP earnings.** GAAP EPS FY25 was $1.68 vs. ~$2.44 non-GAAP — stock comp, amortization and interest are real. On GAAP run-rate the stock is ~25x, not cheap.
