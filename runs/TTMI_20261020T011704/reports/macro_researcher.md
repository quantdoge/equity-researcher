## 1. Macro Environment Summary — "Transitional, with a hawkish tilt"

The US economy is in a **late-cycle, re-acceleration-with-fiscal-dominance** regime. Growth is holding near trend (~2.2%), unemployment is stable at 4.2%, but **inflation has re-accelerated to 3.72% YoY in September from a 3.30% July trough** — and the Fed has stopped cutting and begun to lean hawkish (Fed Funds 3.75%, up from a 3.63% trough).

The defining macro fact for TTMI is the **long end**: the 10Y is at 5.09%, up ~65bp since late May, with the 30Y at 5.37%. The curve has **bear-steepened** (2s10s +34bp) — not a recession signal, but a **term-premium/fiscal-risk signal**. High-multiple, long-duration growth equities are the most directly penalised asset class in that regime.

Meanwhile credit is still wide open (HY OAS 3.31%, near cycle tights) — so the *fundamental* backdrop is supportive even as the *discount rate* backdrop deteriorates. Classic transitional: **risk-on earnings, risk-off multiples.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**TTMI's macro sensitivity map.** TTMI is a high-beta (2.14) contract manufacturer of PCBs, HDI/SLP substrates, IC substrates and RF components. Demand is driven by (a) AI/data-centre capex, (b) defence budgets, (c) auto/industrial/medical. Cost base is driven by copper, laminate, energy and Asian labour. That makes it an **AI-capex beta proxy with commodity-cost leverage and tariff exposure**.

**POSITIVE**
- **Growth ~2.2% and low unemployment 4.2%** → healthy end-market volumes across auto, medical, industrial.
- **AI/data-centre capex** is the dominant revenue driver; the equity is currently the market's purest liquid way to express that (Zacks notes ~86.6% projected Q3 EPS growth).
- **Defence**: $1.7B backlog and $7B+ pipeline — defence outlays are relatively rate-insensitive and politically supported.
- **Credit conditions easy** (HY OAS 3.3%) → cheap financing available for the $650M Ophir Optics acquisition.
- **2s10s normalised** (+34bp) — the recession signal has turned off, supporting capex confidence.

**NEGATIVE**
- **10Y at 5.09% / 30Y 5.37%** is the single biggest macro headwind. TTMI is a long-duration growth asset with a rich multiple; a ~65bp rise in the 10Y mechanically compresses its fair value and has coincided almost exactly with the ~55% drawdown from the June $223.83 peak.
- **Copper +35% YoY (+62% vs Jan 2024)** — copper foil, heavy copper cores and plating are direct COGS. This is a **gross-margin squeeze unless passed through**, and PCB pricing is contractually sticky with OEMs.
- **25% Section 232 semiconductor tariffs (eff. Jan 2026)** — TTMI's China-based manufacturing and its customers' derivative products face duty/classification risk and supply-chain re-routing costs.
- **Firming USD (+2.5% since August)** — translation headwind on non-US revenue; partially offset because a large share of its cost base is also non-USD (natural hedge).
- **Refinancing/acquisition financing risk**: the all-cash $650M Ophir deal (>18x FY26 EBITDA) is being struck **into a 5%+ cost-of-debt environment** — the highest-rate M&A financing window in ~20 years.

**NEUTRAL**
- Inflation at 3.7% is mid-pack: TTMI has some pricing power in tight AI substrate capacity, but not enough to fully absorb copper.
- Employment stability is neutral; labour is a modest but not dominant cost line.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL (negative skew on the multiple)**

**Rationale:** The *operating* macro is genuinely favourable — 2.2% growth, tight credit, AI capex and defence spending are all tailwinds. But the *valuation* macro is clearly unfavourable: 5%-handle long yields, re-accelerating inflation, a hawkish Fed, and 25% semi tariffs hit exactly the profile of a 2.14-beta, high-multiple, copper-intensive, China-manufacturing growth name. The company's ~56% peak-to-trough drawdown is a **discount-rate event layered on an idiosyncratic AI de-rating**, not a demand collapse — which is why I do not call this outright Unfavourable.

**Key tension to flag to the committee:** HY spreads at 3.31% say "risk-on," 10Y at 5.09% and CPI at 3.72% say "risk-off." When those diverge, high-beta equity is usually the release valve. TTMI's 2.14 beta is doing precisely that.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**Bear (highest probability of surprise, ~30%): A "fiscal term-premium shock" pushes the 10Y through 5.50%.**
With CPI already printing ~4.6% annualised over three months, the Fed hikes rather than holds (the 2Y at 4.65% is already ~90bp above fed funds, so the market is leaning this way). The 30Y at 5.37% shows a fiscal supply premium is building. In that scenario:
- TTMI's multiple compresses another leg lower independent of earnings;
- the $650M all-cash Ophir financing gets materially more expensive, and analysts begin questioning the >18x EBITDA price;
- copper stays bid as an inflation hedge, squeezing gross margin.
Downside tail: a retest of the **$75–85 range** (−15% to −25% from $99.10).

**Bull (~20%): Inflation rolls over on a copper/base-effects reversal and the 10Y back to ~4.50%.**
TTMI is the highest-beta liquid AI-PCB proxy; a 60bp rally in long yields plus any AI capex reaffirmation at the 22 October Q3 call could re-rate it violently — the stock has shown 40%+ monthly moves repeatedly (Jan, Apr, May, July, August 2026). Upside tail: **$150–175 (+50% to +75%)**, and the Ophir deal becomes accretive on cheaper financing.

**Base case (~50%):** 10Y chops 4.90–5.30%, CPI sticky at 3.5–4.0%, Fed on hold with hike risk. TTMI trades on the **22 October Q3 print** and AI-capex headlines, not the macro; range-bound $90–130 with elevated realised volatility.
