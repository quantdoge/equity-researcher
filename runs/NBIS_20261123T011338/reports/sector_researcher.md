# SECTOR BRIEF — NBIS (Nebius Group N.V.)
**Sector: AI Infrastructure / "Neocloud" (GPU-as-a-Service) | Tech, Software-Infrastructure | Data as of 20-Nov-2026**

---

## 1. Sector Overview

The neocloud sector — purpose-built GPU cloud rented to AI developers — is roughly **$23–35B of 2026 revenue** (Synergy: ~$23B in 2025 growing >200%/yr; Mordor: $35.2B in 2026) with forecasts of **$180–400B by 2030–31 (58% CAGR)**. Demand is underwritten by hyperscaler capex of **~$780–800B in 2026 rising to $1.2–1.3T in 2027** (Morgan Stanley), and by Nvidia's supplier commitments ballooning from **$119B to $279B** in a single quarter — the memory/HBM queue is now the binding constraint. The listed pure-plays — CoreWeave (~$22.0B mkt cap), Nebius ($19.5B), IREN ($5.0B), Applied Digital ($3.5B) — have collectively re-rated from "growth at any price" to "show me the funding plan": NBIS is **−72% from its June peak**, CRWV **−71% from May**, IREN **−85% from its Nov-2025 peak**. We are in the **late-hypergrowth / buildout-shock phase**: revenue still tripling, but equity multiples collapsing because capex is running 3–8x revenue and is funded with debt and prepayments, not cash flow.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Contracted demand exceeds installed supply.** Nebius carries **$38.7B of RPO** against ~$3.2B of 2026E revenue (12x cover), including **Microsoft up to $19.4B/5yr and Meta up to $27B**. CoreWeave's backlog is of similar character. This is not speculative demand — it is take-or-pay paper.
- **Hyperscaler capex is still accelerating, not decelerating.** Big-5 AI capex moves from ~$780–800B (2026) to $1.2–1.3T (2027); Nvidia's own 2027 guidance came in above Street. Neoclouds capture the overflow that Microsoft/Amazon/Google cannot build fast enough (power interconnection queues of 3–5 years).
- **Prepayment funding model is emerging.** Nebius disclosed that **prepayments on Q3 agreements covered >100% of the associated capex** — a structural improvement over the 2023–25 model of debt-financed GPU purchases, and the single most important development for sector credit quality.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **GPU obsolescence / depreciation risk.** Gartner warns **>$3.5T of AI infrastructure spending could be exposed to impairment by 2030**. Useful-life assumptions (5–6 yrs) are the sector's largest un-audited judgement; a 1-year haircut on NBIS's or CRWV's D&A would erase EBITDA (CRWV: $2.47B EBITDA vs $2.45B D&A — it is a rounding error away from zero).
- **Supplier squeeze at the top of the value chain.** Nvidia and HBM/memory suppliers hold the pricing power; power and land are scarce. Neoclouds are price-takers on inputs and must pre-commit billions of dollars of capacity before they know the rental rate in 2028.
- **Financing and customer concentration.** NBIS guides **$40–45B of 2027 capex vs $12–13B of 2027 revenue** (vs $28.9B consensus capex — the market's shock on 12-Nov). Customers are two hyperscalers who are also competitors building their own capacity. A capex-digestion pause or a single contract renegotiation is an existential-scale event.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position — **STRONG** (with a funding caveat)

- **Growth and monetisation:** Q3-26 revenue $980.8M (+571% YoY, +68% QoQ; ~$656M excluding $325M non-recurring), ARR $6.4B (+113% QoQ), adj. EBITDA margin 47.5%. No peer converts capacity into revenue this fast.
- **Differentiation:** genuinely full-stack — GPU clusters plus cloud platform plus inference tooling (and Toloka/Avride/TripleTen optionality), not just leased capacity like APLD or a rental book like IREN. 76% gross margin evidences real software/pricing layer.
- **Contract quality:** $38.7B RPO with Microsoft and Meta; prepayments >100% of associated capex. This is best-in-class demand visibility.
- **Where it is only average:** the funding plan. $27B of 2026 capex and $40–45B of 2027 capex against a $19.5B market cap means the equity is a levered call on capital markets staying open. 2026 revenue was guided to the **low end of $3.0–3.4B** on colocation delays — execution, not demand, is the constraint. Note also that FY25 and Q1-26 net income (+$82.5M, +$621M) are flattered by non-operating gains; judge the business on operating income and EBITDA.

## 6. Sector Rating — **NEUTRAL**

Demand is not the question: a $23B→$400B market with 12x contracted revenue cover at the leader is a genuine secular tailwind. But sector economics are being diluted by **supplier capture, obsolescence risk, and a capex race funded by prepayments and debt** — and the market has begun pricing that, correctly, in the equity. Attractive for the **#1 share-gainer with prepaid contracts and the best gross margin (NBIS)**; unattractive for sub-scale, debt-funded capacity (IREN) and for landlords (APLD). We would not underwrite the sector on TAM; we would underwrite NBIS specifically on the funding gap closing before 2027 capex season.
