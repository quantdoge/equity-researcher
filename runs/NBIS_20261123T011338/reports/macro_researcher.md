# Macro Assessment: Nebius Group N.V. (NBIS)

*Data as of 20–23 November 2026. FRED series cited with observation dates.*

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-Off, Transitional to Reflation**

The US economy is in a **reflationary, higher-for-longer regime with a bear-steepening yield curve** — the single most hostile macro configuration for a long-duration, capex-intensive growth company. Growth is *accelerating* (Q3 2026 real GDP +3.4% SAAR after +2.2% in Q2), unemployment has fallen to 4.1% from 4.5% a year ago, and CPI has re-accelerated to **3.82% y/y** (Oct 2026) from ~3.3% in mid-2026. The Fed, having cut to an effective 3.63% by mid-2026, has been forced to reverse: the effective rate is back to **3.88% (Oct 2026)**, and Fed Governor Waller has publicly flagged a **December hike** as likely.

Crucially, this is *not* an inflation-expectations story. 10Y breakevens have **fallen** from 2.38% (28 Oct) to **2.15% (20 Nov)** while the 10Y nominal has **risen** — the entire move is **real rates and term premium**. The implied 10Y real yield is ~**2.9–3.0%**, a cycle high. That distinction matters enormously: NBIS cannot "grow out of" a real-rate shock, because the discount rate applied to 2028–2032 cash flows is rising in real terms.

What is *not* broken: **credit**. HY OAS is 3.52% (19 Nov), up a modest ~87bp from 2.65% (1 Sep) but still historically tight. Capital markets are open — this is an **equity-duration and valuation event, not a credit event**. Yet. That is the single most important thing to monitor.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

NBIS is a **long-duration, capital-devouring asset** with a 1.56 beta: 2027 guidance is $12–13bn revenue against **$40–45bn of 2027 capex**, with pre-tax breakeven only in Q4 2027. Market cap is $19.5bn — i.e. **planned 2027 capex is 2.1–2.3× the entire equity value**. The macro variable that matters is not AI demand; it is the marginal cost and availability of ~$40bn.

| Macro Variable | Impact | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Real GDP 3.4% | **Positive** | AI inference/training demand is pro-cyclical; strong growth sustains the capex supercycle that NBIS sells into |
| CPI 3.82%, re-accelerating | **Negative** | Forces Fed tightening; also inflates power, land, construction and GPU-adjacent input costs |
| Fed Funds 3.88% + Dec hike risk | **Negative** | Raises the floating/refinancing cost on a business that is pre-profit until Q4 2027 |
| 10Y at 5.13%, real ~3.0% | **Strongly negative** | Effective cash-flow duration of ~15–20yrs; a 72bp real-rate rise alone justifies ~11–15% of de-rating. Observed drawdown is −72% (peak $286.69 on 18 Jun → $81.31 on 20 Nov), so rates are the *trigger* for a multiple compression driven mostly by funding and positioning |
| Bear steepening (5s30s +54bp) | **Negative** | Term premium is exactly what discounts 2030+ terminal value |
| HY OAS 3.52% (tight) | **Neutral-positive** | Funding access intact — but all-in cost is ~8.5% (5.13% + 340bp), and spreads are widening |
| USD +3.9% since Sept | **Mildly positive / mixed** | USD revenue against EUR/ILS opex (Amsterdam, Israel, Finland) is a modest translation tailwind, but a strong dollar tightens global financial conditions for a 1.56-beta name |
| Energy / grid power costs | **Negative (unquantified)** | European data-centre power is the largest non-GPU opex line; no FRED series fetched for EU power |
| Tariffs / export controls | **Negative (unquantified)** | GPU and networking gear are tariff- and control-exposed inputs |
| AI equity supply (Anthropic IPO) | **Negative** | Massive new AI paper competes for the same marginal capital dollar, siphoning flows from neoclouds |

**Financing arithmetic (illustrative, my estimate):** if ~$20bn of the 2027 capex is debt-funded at ~8.5%, that is ~$1.7bn/yr of interest — roughly **13% of guided 2027 revenue** — against a business that does not turn pre-tax profit until Q4 2027. Every 100bp on that debt costs ~$200M/yr. This is why the 10Y, not the GPU order book, is NBIS's binding constraint.

**Currency:** second-order. Revenue is USD-contracted AI cloud capacity; the cost base is materially EUR/ILS. A firmer dollar is a mild margin tailwind, but it also coincides with the tighter global conditions that have driven the −52% one-month move (20 Oct $171.45 → 20 Nov $81.31).

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

*(Valuation- and funding-cost-driven, not demand-driven.)*

The demand side of the macro backdrop is genuinely supportive — 3.4% GDP growth, 4.1% unemployment, tight credit spreads. What is hostile is the **price of money and the price of duration**: real 10Y yields near 3%, a Fed that has resumed hiking into 3.8% CPI, and a bear steepener that specifically taxes back-end-loaded cash flows. For a company that must raise roughly twice its market capitalisation in a single year to execute, this is close to a worst-case rate regime. The stock is now flat on the year ($83.71 on 31 Dec 2025 → $81.31) after a full round trip to $299.86 — a pure de-rating of the funding model, not of the demand thesis.

**Macro offsets to note:** at $19.5bn market cap on $12–13bn of 2027E revenue (~1.5–1.8x EV/Sales), the equity is now pricing substantial execution failure. Tight HY spreads mean the capital is *available*; the question is at what price and with what dilution.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario: **Bear — "Term Premium Breaks 6%"**

Consensus now assumes the Fed's hiking is nearly done and that long yields peak near 5.3%. The materially surprising outcome is the opposite: **December hike delivered, and the 10Y pushes through 5.75–6.0% in H1 2027** on fiscal supply and term premium, with breakevens *still* falling (pure real-rate tightening).

Transmission to NBIS is mechanical and fast:
1. All-in debt cost moves from ~8.5% to ~9.5–10%; on $20bn that is an extra ~$200–400M/yr of interest against a Q4 2027 breakeven target — pushing breakeven out a full year.
2. HY OAS breaks 4.5–5%, at which point neocloud paper is effectively shut and prepayments (the current funding crutch) get repriced or withdrawn.
3. The equity becomes the currency of last resort **at $60–80**, not $200 — a heavily dilutive raise against a $19.5bn cap.
4. The AI IPO calendar (Anthropic et al.) supplies competing paper into a risk-off tape.

That is a **$40–55 downside** path driven entirely by macro-financial variables, with the operating business potentially hitting its revenue numbers throughout. The bull-side surprise is symmetric and equally violent: with breakevens already at 2.15% and falling, if CPI rolls over toward 3% in H1 2027 and the December hike proves terminal, the real-yield unwind would re-rate exactly these beaten-down, funding-secured neoclouds — a 1.5x EV/Sales multiple on $12–13bn of 2027 revenue has substantial room to re-expand. **The macro call, not the AI call, is the trade.**
