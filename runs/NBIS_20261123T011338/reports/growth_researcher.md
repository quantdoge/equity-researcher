## NEBius Group (NBIS) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $81.31 | Market cap ~$20.1B | EV ~$21.4B | As of Nov 20, 2026**

---

### 1. Growth Story

Nebius is a pure-play AI "neocloud" — GPU clusters, proprietary cloud software, and increasingly owned data-center shells — built to rent compute to the model builders who cannot get enough of it. Revenue went from $91.5M (FY24) to $529.8M (FY25) to a $3.0B run-rate in FY26E, with Q3'26 revenue of **$980.8M (+571% YoY, +68% QoQ)**, a **$38.7B RPO backlog**, and management guiding **$12–13B for 2027**. The thesis is simple and violent: if the contract book is real, the equity at ~1.6x 2027E sales is mispriced by an order of magnitude. The stock's 72% collapse from its June 2026 peak ($286.69 → $81.31) says the market no longer believes the contract book converts — and that is the entire bet.

### 3. TAM and Penetration

- **TAM:** Global AI infrastructure spend is on a trajectory toward $500B–$1T annually by 2030 across training, inference, and sovereign AI. Nebius' SAM — merchant GPU/compute capacity for third parties — is plausibly $150–250B by 2030.
- **Penetration:** Nebius at a $12–13B 2027 revenue base is capturing **mid-single-digit** share of that SAM. The runway is not the constraint.
- **Differentiation:** (1) Full-stack in-house — proprietary hardware design, cloud software, and now owned data-center shells, which improves margin capture vs. pure lessors; (2) European/US footprint serving sovereign-AI demand that hyperscalers are politically constrained from owning outright; (3) RPO of $38.7B from marquee counterparties (Microsoft-scale deals) validates enterprise grade; (4) adj. EBITDA margin of ~47.5% is materially above CoreWeave's model — evidence of pricing power and software value-add.
- **Key structural observation:** Nebius carries **far less leverage than CoreWeave** (EV $21.4B vs. $20.1B market cap = near-zero net debt at FY25; CRWV: $43B EV on $17.2B market cap = ~$26B net debt). Same equity value, one-third the balance-sheet risk per dollar of revenue capacity.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Q4/FY26 results — Feb 11, 2027.** Must deliver ≥$3.0B FY26 and re-affirm the $12–13B 2027 guide. The single highest-variance event on the calendar.
2. **$4.5B convertible note close + share exchange terms (imminent).** Conversion premium and dilution math will set the per-share ceiling and floor.
3. **$17–20B of 2027 prepayments** converting from LOI to binding, funded contracts (management says 40–50% of 2027 capex).
4. **New Jersey third-party data-center energization** — the exact asset that caused the guidance cut. Resolution removes the execution overhang.
5. **Additional hyperscaler/sovereign contract announcements** — each $1B+ award re-rates the backlog and validates prepayment financing.
6. **ClickHouse stake monetization (IPO/round)** and **Avride robotaxi commercialization** — sum-of-parts optionality largely valued at zero today.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (high conviction, volatility-sized)

This is a textbook disruption-at-a-discount setup, but not a clean one — hence Buy, not Strong Buy.

**Why own it:** 500%+ growth, 68.6% gross margins and rising, operating margin inflecting positive (+5.1% in Q3 from −89% a year earlier), 47.5% adj. EBITDA margins, $38.7B RPO, and $17–20B of customer prepayments funding the build. At **1.7x 2027E sales and 0.85x 2028E sales**, the equity discounts near-total failure of the contract book. Every prior quarter of this thesis (85.7% beat rate, +40% average surprise) has beaten.

**Why not Strong Buy:** management just cut FY26 guidance on third-party capacity delays — the specific execution risk bears were watching. And the financial math is unforgiving: **$40–45B of 2027 capex against a $20B market cap.** Prepayments cover 40–50%; another 25–45% comes from other sources. The residual must be debt and equity — and equity raised at $81 is brutally dilutive. This is a race between capacity delivery and the capital markets' patience.

**Position sizing:** this is a 2–3% position with a 3–5 year horizon, added in tranches on either (a) a Q4 print that holds the $3.0B and reaffirms 2027, or (b) further weakness toward the low-$60s. A move above ~$150 without new contract disclosure would warrant trimming, not adding.

---

### 7. Key Risks

1. **Financing/dilution (highest severity).** $40–45B of 2027 capex vs. $20B market cap. If the AI credit window narrows, Nebius either slows the build (killing the 2027 guide) or issues equity at distressed prices. Share count (248M diluted) could rise substantially; per-share value is what matters, not enterprise value.
2. **Execution / third-party dependence.** The FY26 cut was caused by a *third-party* New Jersey data center. Nebius does not fully control its own capacity timing. Power, transformers, and shell delivery are the binding constraints industry-wide.
3. **Revenue quality.** One-third of Q3 revenue was one-time partner revenue; management has *stopped presenting ARR as the right measure* — a disclosure retreat that should be treated skeptically.
4. **Customer concentration & circularity.** A handful of hyperscalers dominate the $38.7B RPO. Prepayment-funded capex for a concentrated customer base is a structure that works beautifully until one counterparty renegotiates.
5. **Asset obsolescence.** D&A was $418M in FY25 and is scaling into the billions. If GPU useful life is shorter than assumed (Rubin/next-gen cadence), the 47% EBITDA margin converts to a much thinner FCF margin — and 0.85x 2028E sales becomes deserved, not cheap.
6. **Competition.** CoreWeave, Oracle OCI, IREN, Lambda, plus every hyperscaler building its own capacity. Merchant GPU compute is converging toward commodity pricing with a handful of well-capitalized players.
7. **Governance/geography.** Amsterdam-domiciled, Yandex legacy, founder-controlled, with sanctions and geopolitical sensitivity. Beta 1.56 and a 72% drawdown describe the risk profile better than any ratio.

**Bottom line:** the growth story is real and the price is extraordinary — the question is whether Nebius can fund $40B+ of steel and silicon without handing the upside to new shareholders. Buy, size small, watch the February print and the convertible terms.
