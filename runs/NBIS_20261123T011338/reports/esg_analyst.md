# ESG ANALYSIS — NEBIUS GROUP N.V. (NASDAQ: NBIS)

**Sector:** Technology / AI cloud infrastructure · **Domicile:** Netherlands (The Hague/Amsterdam) · **Reporting regime:** Foreign private issuer (20-F), Dutch N.V. · **Market cap:** ~$19.5bn @ $81.31 · **Employees:** 1,543 · **CEO:** Arkady Volozh · **Beta:** 1.56

Material note on timing: the stock is ~72% below its June record and Q3 revenue was $981m with ARR of $6.4bn against a 2026 capex plan of $27bn and a 2027 guide of $40–45bn. ESG factors here matter almost entirely through **cost of capital, permitting/delay risk on the capex programme, and licence to operate for 800MW-scale US siting** — not through reputational scoring.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (rising to High on trajectory)

**Genuine strengths — verified, not just self-reported:**
- Portfolio average **PUE 1.25 in 2025** vs global industry average 1.54; best monthly own-facility PUE 1.15; Mäntsälä (Finland) runs without mechanical cooling at ~1.0–1.1.
- **WUE 0.018 L/kWh** at a liquid-cooled owned facility vs projected US average 0.45–0.49 (LBNL).
- **100% renewable electricity in Iceland, France and UK in 2025**; Finland moved from 95% low-carbon to 100% renewable. Market-based intensity was **0.04 tCO₂e/MWh** in 2024 — genuinely best-in-class.
- **19.5 GWh of waste heat exported** to Mäntsälä district heating in 2025, cutting connected households' heating costs ~10% and reducing the municipal utility's heat emissions ~65% (~4,000 tCO₂e avoided); design capacity >250 GWh/yr.
- 102 GWh of electricity avoided through in-house hardware/design efficiency; first WUE disclosure; formal **double materiality and climate risk assessments** completed.

**The offsetting risk:** the centre of gravity of the portfolio is moving to the US on **dedicated new fossil generation** — an 800MW gas plant (scalable to 1.1GW) financed and operated by Independence Power Partners, plus gas-based Bloom fuel cells. At full buildout this single campus's Scope 2 emissions will dwarf the entire current European footprint's low-carbon advantage. Nebius will therefore likely shift from an ESG "efficiency leader" to a mid-carbon-intensity compute provider within 2–3 years, with rising exposure to any future US carbon price, state clean-energy standards, or customer Scope 3 procurement requirements (Microsoft and Meta both impose supplier decarbonisation terms — a contract-renewal risk, not just a reputational one).

**Data gap (stated honestly):** I could not retrieve audited **absolute** Scope 1/2/3 totals — the 2025 Sustainability Report PDF did not parse and the 20-F returned HTTP 403. Intensity metrics are excellent; absolute emissions at GW scale are unverified here.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

- **US siting conflicts are the live issue.** Independence, MO: $6.6bn of tax abatements (~90% property tax relief) and $651m PILOT over 20 years against **125 permanent jobs** for an 800MW, 2.5m sq ft campus — a poor benefit-to-subsidy ratio that invites political backlash. The city announced the deal on Facebook before most residents knew; three residents (Gonzalez, Magers, Vaughn) sued for a public vote and Judge Jennifer Phillips denied them on 25-Mar-2026. **The project proceeds without electoral consent — legal exposure is reduced but political and reputational exposure is elevated**, and Chapter 100 statutes can be revisited.
- **New Jersey:** 300MW project advanced via municipal "redevelopment" designations that bypass standard notice; Pinelands Alliance opposition centred on Kirkwood–Cohansey aquifer drawdown, backup-generator air/noise, and environmental justice (roughly half of Vineland's population sits in NJDEP overburdened census tracts). NJ has passed legislation requiring large data centres to fund their own grid upgrades — a direct opex/return headwind.
- **Grid and ratepayer politics** is the systemic social risk for neoclouds; Independence Power & Light asserts residential rates won't rise via a market-rate pass-through, but this is contested nationally and is now a live legislative theme in multiple states.
- **Labour:** 1,543 employees; construction-phase heavy with thin permanent-job commitments. Toloka (crowd-based data solutions) carries sector-wide fair-work risk — low pay, precarious status, opaque terms for annotators — a supply-chain labour standard gap with no public supplier code evidence retrieved.
- **Mitigants:** Nebius Academy partnered 16 universities in North America and Europe; Rowan University (NJ) pathways programme; STEM, skilled-trades apprenticeships, first-responder support. Meaningful but small relative to the scale of disruption being created.
- No product-safety or consumer-protection incidents identified.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**Positives:**
- **5 of 7 directors independent** (71%): John Boynton (Independent Chair), Arne Grimme, Kira Radinsky, Charles Ryan, Matthew Weigand. Non-independent: Volozh (CEO), Elena Bunina, Ophir Nave (COO).
- **CEO/Chair separation** with an independent chair — no duality.
- **Disclosure culture is well above peer norm for a company of this size:** voluntary ESRS-referenced sustainability reporting since 2024, formal double materiality assessment, climate risk assessment, ISO 14001 — ahead of most US neoclouds.
- Clean corporate event: NASDAQ-only since Moscow Exchange delisting (Jul-2024); no securities class action identified in my search.

**Negatives (why not higher):**
- **Controlled company.** Class B (10 votes) ≈ 58% of total votes on 238.4m Class A vs 33.5m Class B; the CEO's family trust alone carries **~52% of voting power**. Takeover protection for minorities is effectively nil and shareholder resolutions cannot succeed without Volozh.
- **Capital allocation whipsaw:** authorising a 20% buyback while simultaneously issuing equity, converts and $14bn of debt reads as opportunistic rather than disciplined.
- **Russia nexus:** founder/CEO was personally EU-sanctioned (2022–Mar 2024). The $5.4bn Yandex sale was settled partly in **NBIS Class A shares** to a Russian consortium — a standing overhang and a KYC/AML screen for sanctions-sensitive holders.
- **Customer-as-creditor structure:** prepayments from Microsoft/Meta funding capex, deferred revenue +57% QoQ, negative CFO — a governance/credit entanglement that concentrates power with two counterparties.
- NVIDIA holds **9.3% of Class A** — strategic, but a customer/supplier/shareholder overlap.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD/ESRS** — in scope as a Dutch large undertaking; timing affected by the Omnibus "stop-the-clock" delay, but Nebius is voluntarily ESRS-referenced already, so compliance cost is largely sunk. **Low transition risk, high readiness.**
- **EU Energy Efficiency Directive (recast) Art. 12** — mandatory annual energy/water reporting for data centres ≥500kW. All EU sites in scope. Low cost, already measured.
- **EU AI Act** — obligations as provider/deployer of AI infrastructure phase in 2025–2027; governance and documentation burden, not a licence-to-operate threat.
- **Dutch Corporate Governance Code** — "comply or explain"; the dual-class control structure is a departure requiring explanation. Persistent governance-discount driver with Dutch/EU institutional holders.
- **US** — no federal climate disclosure rule in force (SEC rule stayed). Real exposure is **state and municipal**: Chapter 100 industrial revenue bonds and public-vote statutes (MO), large-load interconnection and cost-allocation rules (NJ, VA, TX, GA), local permitting.
- **Not applicable:** CBAM (no goods trade). **Indirect:** EU ETS phase-down raises European power prices — partially hedged by renewables PPAs.
- **Sanctions/AML** — an EU/US/UK Russia-sanctions compliance programme is a permanent, if now low-intensity, control requirement.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified across the $14bn debt stack — a gap and an opportunity (no green-financing penalty, but no greenium either).

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (with a governance-related negative tilt)

The ESG analysis does not change the direction of the thesis, but it sharpens three risks that a purely financial model underweights:

1. **Governance is the binding constraint, not carbon.** A ~52% voting bloc plus serial dilution against a $40–45bn capex plan means minority holders bear the funding risk without the control. This justifies a persistent valuation discount and should be modelled in the discount rate, not the growth rate.
2. **Environmental quality is a real, verifiable asset — but it is being spent.** PUE 1.25, WUE 0.018, 100% renewables in three countries and waste-heat recovery are genuine competitive advantages (lower opex per MW, better enterprise salability to Microsoft/Meta). The 800MW of dedicated gas in Missouri trades that advantage for speed. Watch for customer Scope 3 procurement conditions becoming a contract-renewal issue.
3. **Social licence is now a revenue-timing variable.** With 800MW in Missouri approved 5-2 over resident litigation and 300MW in New Jersey facing Pinelands opposition, permitting friction is a credible source of the colocation delays already narrowing 2026 guidance. ESG due diligence here should be read as capex-schedule diligence.

**Bottom line:** NBIS screens as an **ESG "Average"** — unusually good environmental engineering and disclosure wrapped around a controlled-company governance structure and a contested US siting strategy. It is **not** investable for exclusion-screened ESG mandates on governance grounds, and the Russia-sanctions heritage will keep it out of some screens regardless of current facts. For a generalist mandate, treat ESG as **neutral to the thesis but incrementally negative on the cost of capital**, and monitor three specific items: (i) whether Missouri/NJ litigation escalates into delay, (ii) the first full-year Scope 1/2 absolute emissions after the gas plant energises, and (iii) any move to collapse the dual-class structure or add an independent-majority check on Volozh's control.
