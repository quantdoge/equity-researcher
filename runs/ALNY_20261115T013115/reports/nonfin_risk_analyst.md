## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Alnylam Pharmaceuticals (ALNY)

**Snapshot:** $165.04 (13-Nov-26) · Market cap ~$22.1B · Biotechnology · ~2,500 FTE · CEO Yvonne L. Greenstreet (since Jan 2022) · Independent Chair Amy W. Schulman (since Jan 2023)

**Context that dominates this assessment:** ALNY has fallen ~49% from its July 2026 high ($321.59 on 7-Jul → $165.04), driven by **two separate >25% one-day collapse events** — 30-Jul-26 (−28.3%, close $205.48) on a Q2 earnings miss plus a **cut to full-year TTR product sales guidance**, and 29-Oct-26 (−25.1%, close $174.00) on **slowing Amvuttra demand** (US TTR demand growth of $81M vs $129M in the prior quarter). Both events spawned securities-fraud investigations. This is now a disclosure-credibility story, not just a commercial-growth story.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate** (negative watch)

**Positives**
- **CEO/Chair roles are separated.** Amy W. Schulman (director since 2014) has been independent Chair since January 2023; Greenstreet is CEO only. No concentration of power.
- **Board refresh executed:** Michael Bonney and Carolyn Bertozzi departed, **Stuart Arbuckle** (former J&J Innovative Medicine EVP) appointed independent director (Dec-2025) — adds commercial/launch expertise relevant to the current problem.
- **Directors bought the dip with personal cash:** Schulman bought **1,910 shs @ $156.82 (~$300K)** on 9-Nov-26; Charles Elliott Sigal bought **1,600 shs @ $152.25 (~$244K)** on 10-Nov-26. Chair buying after a 49% drawdown is a meaningful alignment signal and argues the board believes the demand issue is fixable.

**Concerns**
- **Guidance credibility is now a governance issue.** The July guidance cut, followed one quarter later by demand deceleration, goes to whether guidance had "a reasonable factual basis" when issued — precisely the allegation plaintiff firms are pursuing. That implicates disclosure controls and the Audit/Disclosure Committee's oversight, not merely commercial execution.
- **Zero officer purchases in 2026.** Alignment runs one direction.
- **Board turnover removes institutional memory** at exactly the moment launch-execution oversight is most needed.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary
- **No SEC enforcement action or DOJ investigation identified.** EDGAR full-text enforcement search for ALNY returned **empty**. This is the single most important mitigant: the current exposure is *private civil litigation risk*, not a government case.
- **Securities fraud activity (active, escalating):** At least **seven firms** publicly investigating/pursuing claims — Glancy Prongay Wolke & Rotter, Law Offices of Howard G. Smith, Frank R. Cruz, **Pomerantz**, **Kessler Topaz**, **Kessler Topaz/KTMC** (which references a filed class action) and **Bronstein, Gewirtz & Grossman**. Theories: false/misleading statements on business prospects, failure to disclose material adverse information, and guidance lacking reasonable basis. Bronstein Gewirtz notes "no complaint filed yet" on its Oct-29 trigger, so the **class period is still being defined** — a live, expanding liability.
- **FDA/commercial regulatory:** Amvuttra's ATTR-CM label expansion cleared with no new safety signals; 5-year HELIOS-B open-label extension data (AHA, Nov-26) showed sustained benefit — a genuine regulatory/label durability positive. Historical friction point: a **3-month FDA review-period extension for vutrisiran because a new facility had to be identified to pack and label the product** — evidence that CMC/fill-finish changes have already cost the company launch time once.
- **Competitive-conduct scrutiny:** Rival **BridgeBio publicly accused Pfizer and Alnylam of "not playing fair"** in ATTR-CM (Bloomberg, Sept-25) over promotional tactics; Pfizer separately challenged BridgeBio ads referred to federal agencies. Alnylam's promotional conduct in a three-way fight is a live FDA-OPDP / FTC-adjacent tail risk. No action against ALNY confirmed — flag, don't overstate.
- **Lobbying:** ~$70K disclosed — immaterial but consistent with heavy drug-pricing policy engagement.

### 4. Top Operational Risks
- **Extreme single-product concentration.** Amvuttra was **87.5% of product sales** (~$1.13B of ~$1.29B in Q3-26) while Onpattro has collapsed to **$20M/quarter**. A demand-deceleration problem in one molecule is, for practical purposes, a company-wide problem — and that is exactly what has now occurred twice.
- **Third-party manufacturing / CMC dependency.** Oligonucleotide synthesis and fill-finish run through a limited set of CMOs; the prior vutrisiran packaging-site change triggered a 3-month FDA delay. A facility change, inspection failure, or shutdown at a single node would hit the franchise with no internal redundancy demonstrated. *(No supply interruption disclosed — this is structural, not an active event.)*
- **Key-person and commercial-execution dependence.** The ATTR-CM launch narrative is personally identified with Greenstreet, and CCO Tanguler's commercial organization is the swing factor in the guidance reset. With the board having just lost two long-tenured directors, institutional knowledge depth is thinner than a year ago. Also: revenue is partly royalty/collaboration-dependent (Novartis/Leqvio, Regeneron), layering partner-execution risk onto the P&L.
- **Cybersecurity:** no disclosed breach or incident found; standard biotech IP/clinical-data exposure applies. *Not verified — data gap.*

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Elevated**
- **Guidance credibility is the reputational core.** Two misses in one quarter, with a formal fraud-investigation cottage industry now built around the stock, is the classic pattern that forces institutional holders to re-underwrite management trust. "Poor financial results" framing in coverage has become "possible securities fraud" framing.
- **Litigation-amplified headline risk:** a steady drumbeat of "Lost Money on Alnylam?" press releases (Oct–Nov 2026) contaminates retail sentiment and will persist for quarters regardless of merit.
- **Mitigants:** the underlying science franchise is intact and generating *positive* news — 5-year HELIOS-B durability, brezosiran's 83% huntingtin reduction with a 2027 pivotal planned, Regeneron's CIDEBsiRNA first-in-human data. Amvuttra still grew ~105% y/y in Q2. This is an **execution/demand-visibility story, not a product-quality or safety story** — a materially better place to be.
- **ESG:** no material controversy score issue identified; drug-pricing politics is the standing sector-wide exposure. Data gap on third-party ESG controversy scores.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: structurally sound governance (independent chair, separated roles, director buying) and **no government enforcement action** keep this out of "Critical." But *active multi-firm securities litigation over disclosure credibility*, *two >25% guidance-driven drawdowns in one quarter*, *87.5% single-product concentration*, and *officer-only selling through the decline* collectively justify High. Upgrade to Moderate requires: (a) litigation consolidating without an SEC subpoena, (b) two consecutive quarters of stabilizing Amvuttra demand prints, (c) evidence of officer purchases.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation
1. **🚩 Multi-firm securities fraud investigations with an undefined class period** (Pomerantz, Kessler Topaz, KTMC class action, Glancy Prongay, Howard G. Smith, Frank R. Cruz, Bronstein Gewirtz). Escalate to legal/PM: monitor for **SEC subpoena or 8-K disclosure of an SEC inquiry** — that would flip this to Critical. No SEC/DOJ matter exists today per EDGAR.
2. **🚩 Guidance credibility failure twice in one quarter** — risk that FY26 guidance is cut a *third* time; treat management's forward demand commentary as low-reliability input to the model.
3. **🚩 87.5% revenue concentration in Amvuttra** into a decelerating US TTR demand curve, with BridgeBio's Attruby and Pfizer's Vyndaqel ($6.6B 2025) actively taking share.
4. **🚩 CLO sale 27 days before the Oct-29 collapse** (~$399K) — optics/compliance check warranted; no evidence of impropriety.
5. **⚠️ Board turnover** (Bonney, Bertozzi out) coinciding with peak oversight demand; watch for further departures or a CFO/CEO transition.
6. **⚠️ Competitor allegation of unfair promotional conduct** (BridgeBio vs Pfizer/Alnylam) — potential FDA-OPDP or FTC advertisement scrutiny.

Governance findings rely on the corporate-governance page, an 8-K/director-bio filing, and Form 4 data. No cybersecurity incident history or third-party ESG controversy score retrieved.

---
**Bottom line for the memo:** the non-financial risk here is *credibility and concentration*, not integrity of the science or of the board's structure. The bull case (5-yr HELIOS-B durability, brezosiran optionality, 105% Amvuttra growth, Chair buying at $157) is real; the risk is that disclosure-controls weaknesses turn every future demand wobble into a further 25% gap down plus incremental litigation.
