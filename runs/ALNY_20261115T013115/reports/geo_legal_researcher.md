### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**Risk 1 — U.S. drug-pricing policy (MFN + IRA Medicare negotiation). Probability: High / Impact: High**
Alnylam is actively lobbying on **most-favored-nation (MFN) pricing, IRA implementation, and Medicare home-infusion coverage** (Legis1, "Alnylam Lobbying Rises Amid Medicare Drug Pricing Debate"). Its growth engine, AMVUTTRA, is a high-price, single-source specialty biologic — exactly the profile targeted by MFN and by later IRA negotiation cycles (CMS confirms negotiation rounds continue with prices effective 2028+). A 20–30% price reset on the TTR franchise would hit the majority of U.S. revenue, where ~70%+ of sales sit.

**Risk 2 — Tariffs / onshoring of pharmaceutical supply. Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium**
Sector-wide Section 232-style pharma tariff and origin-tracing regimes are live in 2026 (Exiger, Thomson Reuters, Feb 2026 ISM commentary). Alnylam's revenue is U.S.-concentrated while its manufacturing is a hybrid of its Norton, MA site and third-party CMOs — the CMO tier is where tariff/API-origin exposure sits. Alnylam has not quantified tariff exposure in the disclosures reviewed; this is an under-modeled, second-order margin risk rather than an existential one.

**Risk 3 — Alliance/partner-country concentration and Japan/Europe launch execution. Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium**
International growth is explicitly dependent on three markets (Japan, Germany, Italy) plus "timing of orders in partner markets." Single-market reimbursement decisions (e.g., German AMNOG pricing, Japanese price cuts, NHS/NICE) can materially swing international revenue, and partner-dependent markets add counterparty and currency-control friction (Brazil, Taiwan). This is a *revenue-visibility* risk more than a political-instability risk — Alnylam does not operate in any high-expropriation jurisdiction.

*Macro overlay:* the tape is now pricing Fed tightening and above-target inflation (Collins/Williams/Jefferson comments, Nov 12–14, 2026; ~100% odds of a December hike). For a long-duration biotech trading at a premium, discount-rate risk dominates country risk.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Securities litigation — the single largest legal overhang.**
- On **July 30, 2026**, ALNY fell **28.31% (‑$81.14) to $205.48** on disclosure, and fell a further **~25% on Oct 30, 2026** despite a Q3 beat (Zacks: "slower Amvuttra patient starts and demand concerns").
- A **federal securities fraud class action** is pending, with a class period beginning **February 12, 2026** (Howard Smith complaint, filed 7/2026); Kessler Topaz (KTMC) is running the case.
- Multiple firms are soliciting: **Pomerantz** (PRNewswire 11/12/2026), **Glancy Prongay Wolke & Rotter** ("possible securities fraud after 28.31% share price drop," 10/29/2026), **Frank R. Cruz**, **Bronstein Gewirtz & Grossman**, **ZLK** (alleging overstatement of ONPATTRO efficacy/safety).
- Assessment: derivative of the disclosure event, not an operating-liability claim. Expect a consolidated complaint in the coming quarters and a 12–24 month resolution path; settlement ranges are typically low-hundreds-of-millions for a company of this size but remain unquantified in filings. **Watch for a formal SEC investigation** — none disclosed to date.

**Patent / IP litigation — largely resolved, and net value-destructive to the upside case.**
- **Alnylam v. Moderna** (LNP delivery, COVID vaccine): **resolved and dismissed with prejudice** (claims cannot be refiled); terms undisclosed — i.e., no repeat of the Arbutus/Genevant outcome. For context, Arbutus/Genevant extracted **up to $2.25B** from Moderna ($950M paid July 2026 + up to $1.3B), which frames the value Alnylam did *not* capture (FiercePharma; MedPath; drugpatentwatch).
- **Alnylam v. Pfizer/BioNTech**: **dismissed August 2025** on non-infringement by Chief Judge Colm Connolly (Willkie client win). The Federal Circuit had earlier reversed/remanded parts of the Moderna case (23-2357, 6/4/2025), showing forum volatility in this estate. Net: the LNP monetization optionality that bulls once underwrote is now essentially gone.
- Core RNAi/GalNAc patent estate (Alnylam's foundational IP) remains intact and is the real moat; no material invalidity challenge to the platform is disclosed.

**Regulatory posture — constructive on labels, tightening on price.**
- **FDA**: AMVUTTRA approved for **ATTR-CM** — "first and only therapeutic approved by the FDA" for the indication (company release) — a major label win.
- **EU**: European Commission granted the **ATTR-CM indication** for AMVUTTRA; EMA retained **orphan drug designation** for ATTR amyloidosis (positive opinion May 2026).
- **Japan**: ATTR-CM launch underway.
- **Pipeline gating**: brezosiran (Huntington's) pivotal study targeted **2027 "pending regulatory alignment"** — a live FDA/EMA interaction risk on a CNS endpoint; TRITON-CM enrollment expanded to ~2,250 with nucresiran launch targeted "by 2030."

**Governance/management churn**: Nov 4, 2026 announcement of a **$1B buyback plus a transition to new commercial leadership** — a change in the executive most accountable for the very demand metrics at issue in the litigation. Worth flagging to the governance team.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **No disclosed FCPA, AML, sanctions, or DOJ/OIG investigation.** Chinese/Russian revenue exposure is immaterial, so sanctions and export-control risk is low. No disclosed data-privacy enforcement (GDPR/HIPAA) actions.
- **Amber flag 1 — promotional and disclosure controls.** The class action centers on statements about demand/patient starts and ONPATTRO safety/efficacy. Even absent fraud, this pattern often attracts SEC follow-on inquiry; the Q3'26 gross-to-net favorability (~$10M) and inventory benefit ($17M) in U.S. TTR revenue are the kind of items plaintiffs' experts will dissect.
- **Amber flag 2 — patient-access/support programs.** U.S. specialty-launch economics plus Medicare home-infusion lobbying raise Anti-Kickback Statute / False Claims Act sensitivity (industry-wide DOJ focus). Not a company-specific allegation — monitor only.
- **Green flag** — buyback authorization implies the board and counsel are comfortable with disclosure exposure, a mild mitigating signal.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale.** Operating geography is overwhelmingly **low-risk OECD** (U.S., Western Europe, Japan) with **negligible China/Russia/EM exposure**, so classic geopolitical risk (expropriation, capital controls, sanctions, conflict) is **Low**. The Medium rating is driven by **U.S. policy risk** (MFN/IRA pricing on a >70% U.S. revenue base) and by a **live securities class action with a 28% and a 25% single-day drawdown behind it** — legal/regulatory headline risk that is already impairing the multiple. IP litigation overhang has *decreased* (Moderna settled, Pfizer dismissed), removing an upside tail but also removing a distraction.

**Monitoring triggers:** (1) filing of a consolidated securities complaint or any SEC subpoena disclosure; (2) inclusion of AMVUTTRA/patisiran in an IRA negotiation cycle or an MFN Most-Favored-Nation pricing term sheet applied to Part D/B; (3) German/Japanese reimbursement outcomes on ATTR-CM; (4) any tariff rule that captures Alnylam's CMO network; (5) FDA feedback on the brezosiran pivotal design.
