### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW**

- Scope 1/2/**material Scope 3** reported **with third-party assurance**; 2025 CR Report published May 2026 — above-average disclosure for a company of this size.
- First **Scope 1 & 2 reduction framework through 2030** introduced (renewables, fleet electrification, facility optimisation, on-site solar); Norton, MA manufacturing expansion paired with `siRELIS` enzymatic ligation platform (lower material/plant input).
- No environmental fines, spills or remediation liabilities identified in searches.
- Residual risk: Scope 3 intensity rises with commercial scale; the 2030 target covers **Scope 1 & 2 only** — no validated Scope 3 or SBTi-style pathway disclosed.

**Financial impact:** immaterial to cash flows; no carbon-tax or CBAM exposure (US manufacturing, EU sales of a low-volume biologic).

---

### 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM** (elevated by promotional conduct)

**Positives**
- Genuine unmet-need products (hATTR, AHP, PH1, ATTR-CM); UK **NICE recommended Amvuttra** (Nov 2025), indicating a workable value story in a strict HTA market.
- Formal **Patient Access Philosophy**, early-access programme, genetic testing/counselling support, published **Human Rights Principles**.
- Structure: dedicated Chief Ethics & Compliance Officer on the executive leadership team.

**Negatives**
- **Two FDA untitled letters in eight months** on Amvuttra promotion: a Sept 2025 TV ad implying patients could be "carefree" about cardiomyopathy, then an **April 23, 2026** letter finding the consumer website **misbranded** the drug by presenting open-label-extension survival claims ("36% lower risk of death over 3.5 years") with no concurrent control. FDA gave 15 working days to respond; claims were **still live on the site as of May 7, 2026**. Closed out 24 Aug 2026.
- Pricing/access tension: Amvuttra at ~$476k/yr versus cheaper oral/competitor alternatives (Pfizer tafamidis, BridgeBio Attruby) — the commercial weakness that triggered the 2026 guidance cuts is partly an **access/price-competitiveness** problem, not purely clinical.
- Active lobbying on **MFN pricing, IRA implementation and Medicare home-infusion coverage** — raises political/ reputational exposure as the franchise scales.

### 4. Governance Quality Score — **6 / 10**

**Supports the score**
- **Chair/CEO separation**: Amy W. Schulman chairs the board; Dr. Yvonne Greenstreet is CEO and a director. A **lead independent director** role exists (Arbuckle/Sigal referenced in 2026 proxy materials).
- Board overwhelmingly independent (Nasdaq standards applied; Michael Bonney, resigned Dec 2025, confirmed independent).
- Mature committee architecture: Audit; **Nominating & Corporate Governance**; **People, Culture and Compensation**; Science & Technology. Tiered conduct framework (Code of Business Conduct & Ethics, Third-Party Code of Conduct, Comprehensive Compliance Program).

**Detracts**
- **Promotional-compliance control failure.** Two misbranding letters in one year is a tone-at-the-top and commercial-pressure signal, arriving precisely as growth targets were missed — the worst combination for a 87.5%-concentration franchise.
- **Guidance credibility.** Q2 2026 (30 Jul, −28.3%) and Q3 2026 (29 Oct, −25.1%) — the latter on US TTR demand growth of $81M vs $129M prior quarter — with a ~$200M guidance cut. Multiple securities-fraud investigations opened (Pomerantz, Glancy, Howard G. Smith, Kessler Topaz, Bronstein Gewirtz & Grossman) probing whether guidance had a reasonable factual basis. **No complaint filed** as of Nov 2026, so this is investigation risk, not established liability.
- Board turnover (Bonney resignation) during a period of stress.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US FDA OPDP enforcement** — *highest-severity, live exposure.* Alnylam is now a repeat untitled-letter recipient amid the agency's DTC crackdown; escalation to a **Warning Letter** would be the key leading indicator to monitor.
- **IRA Medicare Drug Price Negotiation** — Amvuttra is a plausible future selected drug as ATTR-CM spend scales; MFN pricing would compress US net price directly.
- **UK NICE / EU HTA (EUnetHTA)** — already navigated for ATTR-CM (positive), but price/access pressure is structural.
- **SEC climate disclosure rule** — currently stayed/uncertain; low relevance given immaterial emissions. Voluntary assured Scope 1/2/3 reporting already exceeds requirement.
- **EU CSRD** — limited direct exposure (US-domiciled; EU revenue small) but EEA subsidiaries may face phased scope.
- **CBAM** — not applicable.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants** — none identified.
- No Delaware/dual-class issues; single share class, standard shareholder rights.

---

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** *(with a Governance / Product-Stewardship watch flag)*

Not a Laggard: environmental disclosure is genuinely stronger than biotech peers, product social value is high and demonstrable, and board structure is orthodox (independent chair, separated CEO, functioning committees, named compliance officer).

Not a Leader: two FDA misbranding letters in a year, two >25% guidance-driven equity shocks with securities investigations attached, and revenue concentration in one asset whose promotion the regulator has twice found misleading. The gap between the **stated** control environment (Comprehensive Compliance Program, Human Rights Principles, assured ESG data) and **observed** outcomes (repeat promotional violations, guidance misses) is exactly the "policy-to-outcome" gap that ESG analysis exists to catch.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (not a thesis driver — but a live tail risk)

- **E:** No valuation impact. Not a differentiator either way.
- **S:** The drug portfolio is a genuine social positive and supports payer/HTA arguments; but Amvuttra's **promotional-compliance record is a real, under-priced operational risk** to the single franchise carrying ~88% of product revenue. A Warning Letter or a mandated DTC pullback would hit the growth narrative while the franchise is already decelerating vs. Pfizer and BridgeBio.
- **G:** The securities investigations and guidance-credibility damage raise the cost of capital modestly and are the reason governance scores in the 6/10 range rather than 8/10. Watch for a filed complaint; none yet.
- **Position in an ESG-screened portfolio:** **eligible** under most frameworks (no thermal coal, weapons, tobacco, controversies-based exclusions triggered). Under a **norm-based screen** (UN Global Compact Principle 7-equivalent product-marketing integrity), the repeat FDA findings put ALNY in "monitor," not "exclude."
- **Bottom line:** ESG does not change the investment case in either direction. The commercially relevant takeaway is that ALNY's non-financial risk is now **concentrated in regulatory promotion and disclosure credibility, not in carbon** — and management has a two-quarter track record of surprises. That argues for a governance discount in position sizing rather than exclusion.
