# HubSpot, Inc. (NYSE: HUBS) — Value Investment Analysis

*Price $250.76 (Oct 20, 2026) · Market cap ≈ $12.8B · Net cash ≈ $1.2–1.4B · EV ≈ $11.6B · Latest reported period: Q2 FY2026*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Narrow (and under pressure)**

**What the business is:** An all-in-one, cloud-hosted CRM and go-to-market platform (Marketing, Sales, Service, CMS hubs) aimed at SMB and mid-market B2B, with a genuine upmarket push. 306,000+ customers, 49% of revenue international. I understand this business; it is inside my circle of competence.

**Evidence for a moat:**
- **Gross margin 83.8%** (2025), up from 80.1% in 2021 — pricing structure and software economics intact.
- **Switching costs are real**: multi-hub attach is deepening (64% of new Pro Plus customers land with multiple hubs, +3pts YoY), agentic usage spreading across four products, 306k installed base.
- **Upmarket traction**: deals >$120k ARR grew **38% YoY**, upmarket buyers buying HubSpot for platform consolidation and TCO reduction.
- **Efficient scale in the mid-market**: neither Salesforce (enterprise-first, complex) nor Microsoft (bundled, generic) serves the SMB/mid-market "easy to use, integrated" niche as cleanly.

**Evidence the moat is NARROWING — this is the heart of the thesis:**
- **Net revenue retention of 102%**, down a point YoY, versus 110%+ historically. A 102% NRR means the installed base is barely expanding; growth now depends on new logos, not on the moat getting deeper.
- **Average subscription revenue per customer +2% in constant currency.** This is the single most damning number in the file: essentially zero pricing power.
- **Net new customers collapsed to 7,000 in Q2 vs. a 9,000–10,000 target**, with FY guidance cut to 5,000–6,000/quarter (~7% annual logo growth on the base).
- **Revenue growth has decelerated in a straight line**: 47% (2021) → 33% (2022) → 25% (2023) → 21% (2024) → 19% (2025) → ~17–18% guided FY2026. Calculated billings grew only **14%** in Q2 — billings lead revenue, so further deceleration is baked in.
- **The pricing model is being dismantled.** Management abandoned per-seat pricing for trials and outcome/consumption-based pricing on AI agents, and *cut prices* on several agents. A company with a wide moat does not cut prices and change its monetization model under competitive duress. This is an explicit admission that AI agents erode seat-based software economics.

**Moat verdict:** Narrow. The asset (installed base, integration depth, brand in mid-market) is real, but the mechanism that converted that asset into pricing power is weakening. This is a good business in an industry-level transition, not a "wonderful business" I can underwrite for 20 years with confidence.

| **Owner earnings (FCF – SBC)** | **(95)** | **(181)** | **56** | **179** | **~220** |

**The central accounting fact of this investment:** FCF of $708M in 2025 is *not* $708M of value to owners. Stock-based compensation was $528M — 75% of reported free cash flow. GAAP net income was $46M against a $12.8B market cap (292x). Reported "FCF conversion" of 1,540% of net income is not excellence; it is the signature of compensation paid in equity rather than cash.

- **True owner earnings** (FCF less the cash-equivalent cost of neutralizing dilution) were ~$179M in 2025 and ~$220M TTM. That is a **1.7% owner-earnings yield** on the market cap — versus a headline FCF yield of 5.3%. This is the whole ballgame.
- The **good news**: SBC is falling as a share of revenue (19.9% → 16.9% → ~16%), and FCF margin is expanding fast (10.5% → 22.6%). Every point of SBC/revenue on a $3.7B base is ~$37M of owner earnings. This is a genuine, mechanical value-creation lever.
- **Balance sheet: strong.** $1.70B cash vs. $262M debt at YE2025 (net cash $1.44B), reduced somewhat by $500M of 2025 buybacks. Negative working capital dynamics (deferred revenue) fund the business. No solvency risk whatsoever.
- **Returns on capital are poor on paper, adequate on a cash-adjusted basis.** GAAP ROIC was **0.49%** in 2025 (−2.55% in 2024, −9.47% in 2023). Adding back SBC: NOPAT ≈ (11.4 + 528.2) × 0.78 ≈ **$421M** on invested capital of ~$2.3B (equity + debt) = **~18% ROIC**. That clears a ~9–10% WACC, but the entire spread depends on treating SBC as free. A wide-moat business earns 25–30%+ ROIC *and* doesn't need 17% of revenue in equity comp to retain talent.

Score rationale: pristine margins, real FCF, fortress balance sheet (would be 8–9) versus negligible GAAP returns, 16% SBC, and 102% NRR (drags it to 6).

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples:**
- EV/Sales ≈ **3.4x** TTM (versus 10–15x during 2020–2024 — the multiple has collapsed ~70%)
- EV/FCF ≈ **14.5x** FY2026E (~$800M)
- EV/(EBITDA+SBC) ≈ **15.8x** — this is the fair "cash EBITDA" multiple
- **P/E (GAAP) 292x**; P/E on non-GAAP EPS ~$10–11 ≈ **23x**
- **EV/Owner earnings ≈ 52x** — the honest, SBC-adjusted multiple
- No dividend. FCF yield 5.3% headline, ~1.7% owner-earnings yield.

**Intrinsic value — my own three-stage owner-earnings DCF** (SBC deducted, 10% discount rate, 3% terminal growth, net cash added):

| Scenario | Key assumptions | Value/share |
|---|---|---|
| **Severe** (AI disintermediation; growth stalls to mid-single digits, OE margin stuck ~10%) | 25% weight | **~$145** |
| **Bear** (growth 12% fading to 6%, OE margin plateaus at 15%) | 35% weight | **~$235** |
| **Base** (17%→11% revenue growth, FCF margin to 27%, SBC to 10.5%, OE margin to ~17%) | 30% weight | **~$355** |
| **Bull** (agent monetization works, FCF margin >30%, growth reaccelerates to 20%+) | 10% weight | **~$480** |
| **Probability-weighted** | | **≈ $275–290** |

Sanity check on the base case: $355/share implies ~$18.1B equity value, ~4.9x 2027E sales and ~44x 2027E owner earnings. Given 15% growth and 84% gross margins, that is not a heroic exit multiple.

**Read the setup carefully:** the current price of $250 sits *almost exactly on my bear case*. The market has fully priced a permanently impaired growth story, and nearly everything between bear and bull is free. But my probability-weighted value of ~$280 is only **10–14% above the current price** — short of the 20–30% margin of safety I require. A "fair price" is not a buy.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good, with a capital-allocation blemish**

- **Candor: above average.** CEO Yamini Rangan (since 2021, ex-Shopify COO) and CFO Kate Bueker explicitly disclosed the self-inflicted nature of the Q2 miss — the deliberate shift to trials and outcome-based pricing that stretched sales cycles — rather than hiding behind "macro." Co-founder Dharmesh Shah remains CTO and is buying stock.
- **Strategic courage:** pre-emptively cannibalizing its own per-seat model and cutting agent prices is the right long-term bet if agents become the interface for GTM work. It is also a genuine signal of conviction.
- **Capital allocation: mixed.**
  - $500M repurchased in 2025 — but *weighted diluted shares still rose* from 51.8M to 53.2M, meaning the buyback didn't even offset dilution, and it was executed at prices far above today's $250. Buying back stock at 20–30x sales while paying 17% of revenue in equity comp is not disciplined.
  - A new **$1B authorization** (~8% of market cap) taken at ~$200–250 is a far better use of cash — this I approve of, if executed.
  - Small tuck-in M&A (goodwill/intangibles $540M, up from $401M) — modest, not empire-building.
- **Insider ownership/alignment**: founder-led culture intact; compensation heavily equity-based (which is the cost, but also the alignment).
- **Ding:** repeated mid-year guidance resets. Credibility on forward numbers is a work in progress.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

*(Accumulate below ~$185–195; upgrade to Buy on evidence of stabilization.)*

The bull case is easy to state and quantitatively real: a 70% drawdown has taken HUBS to 3.4x EV/sales and 14.5x forward FCF, with 84% gross margins, net cash, 18% revenue growth, and 20% non-GAAP operating margins that are still *expanding*. If management's SBC-to-FCF conversion lever keeps working, owner earnings can compound from ~$220M today to ~$1B by 2030, and the stock is materially undervalued.

But three things keep me from buying at $250:

1. **The moat is narrowing, not widening.** NRR of 102% and ARPC growth of 2% in constant currency are not the marks of a business with pricing power. Growth is now purely a function of logo acquisition, and logo acquisition just missed by 25%.
2. **My margin of safety is insufficient.** Probability-weighted intrinsic value of ~$280 versus a $250 price is a 10–14% discount — inside the range where I'd rather wait. The severe case (~$145, −42%) is a live scenario, not a tail fantasy, because AI-native tools and agent platforms attack exactly the workflow HubSpot monetizes.
3. **Earnings quality is thin.** 292x GAAP earnings, 52x owner earnings, and 16% of revenue paid in stock. The "cheap" 14.5x FCF multiple is only cheap if you believe equity comp is not a cost. It is a cost.

**What would make me a buyer:** price below $190 (a 30% discount to base-case value), *or* two consecutive quarters showing NRR back above 105%, net new customers above 7,000, and SBC below 14% of revenue. Either the price or the evidence must improve; ideally both. If I owned it, I would hold and add at $185 — the downside is already largely underwritten, and the optionality on agent monetization is free at this price.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI disintermediation (the dominant risk).** If AI agents absorb the workflow (lead gen, content, nurture, support triage) that HubSpot charges seats for, the per-seat model erodes faster than outcome-based pricing replaces it. The price cuts on agents are evidence this is already happening.
2. **Consumption-pricing transition risk.** Moving from predictable subscriptions to usage/outcome pricing raises revenue volatility, complicates billings, and may compress revenue per customer before expanding it. Calculated billings +14% vs. revenue +20% shows the tension.
3. **SMB/mid-market macro sensitivity.** Larger buying committees, C-suite and board approval on mid-sized deals, and elongated cycles are already in the guidance — a recession would compound them. HubSpot has no enterprise backlog to cushion it.
4. **SBC and dilution.** ~16% of revenue in equity comp persists indefinitely; if the FCF-margin expansion stalls while SBC stays at 15%+, owner earnings barely grow and my base case breaks.
5. **Competitive intensity.** Salesforce and Microsoft can bundle CRM into suites; AI-native startups can price at near-zero for point solutions. HubSpot's mid-market "all-in-one" advantage is defensible but not impregnable.
6. **Guidance credibility / estimate risk.** Two consecutive resets in customer-add targets mean the FY2026 "17–18%" growth number itself carries risk, and consensus may still be too high.

---

**Summary:** A good business, honestly run, with an excellent balance sheet and a real long-term AI option — trading at a fair-to-slightly-cheap price after a 70% collapse. Not yet at the discount that compensates for a narrowing moat in a structurally uncertain industry. **Hold; buy below $190.**
