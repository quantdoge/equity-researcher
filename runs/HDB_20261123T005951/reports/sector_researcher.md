## SECTOR BRIEF — INDIAN COMMERCIAL BANKING / HDFC BANK (HDB)

**Data notes before we start (important for reading the tables):** FMP classifies HDB as "Financial Services / Banks – Regional," which returns a peer list polluted by US ETFs and money-market funds, and the sector-aggregate endpoint returned only 1–4 contributing companies (unusable). NSE local tickers (SBIN.NS, KOTAKBANK.NS, AXISBANK.NS) are blocked on this subscription. I therefore built the peer set from the two US-listed lines that resolve cleanly — **IBN (ICICI Bank)** and **SBKFF (State Bank of India)** — plus supplementary reported metrics from broker/rating-agency coverage. Indian banks report in INR with a March fiscal year; FY26 = year ended March 2026. Latest reported quarter is Q2 FY27 (Sep-2026).

---

### 1. Sector Overview

Indian commercial banking is a ~₹190 lakh crore (~US$2.2trn) credit system growing high-single to low-double digits, dominated by State Bank of India (~22–23% of bank credit), HDFC Bank (~13–14%, the largest private lender) and ICICI Bank (~13%) — top-3 concentration of roughly 48–50%. It is one of the most heavily regulated, highest-barrier financial systems in the world: RBI licensing, CRR/SLR, priority-sector lending obligations and Basel III capital rules make de novo entry effectively impossible, while the deposit franchise is the scarce asset. After a 16–17% credit boom in FY24, system credit growth decelerated to roughly **9.7–10.3% in FY26** (ICRA) with deposits lagging at ~11.5% and a persistently high credit-deposit ratio, and is forecast to re-accelerate toward **~14.5% in FY27** (CARE Ratings). Asset quality is at multi-decade lows (~2.3–2.6% GNPA). The sector is in a **mid-cycle margin trough**: NIMs fell ~10bp in FY26 (Fitch, post the 25bp repo cut), and consensus now models NII growth re-accelerating to **16.1% in FY27 from just 5.3% in FY26** (Equirus).

**Porter's Five Forces:** Rivalry **high** (deposit price war); Buyer power **high** (depositors are mobile and rate-sensitive); Supplier power (of funds) **rising**; Substitutes **rising fast** (mutual funds, equities, UPI-linked fintech credit diverting household savings); New entrants **very low** (regulatory moat).

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Funding-cost tailwind from the rate-cut cycle.** Repo cuts are repricing deposit costs faster than asset yields in FY27, with management at HDFC Bank guiding NIM higher from the December quarter — the sector's core earnings driver inflects from a 5.3% NII year (FY26) to ~16% (FY27E).
- **Structural credit demand.** Nominal GDP growth of ~10%, bank credit/GDP still only ~55–60% versus 100%+ in developed markets, MSME formalisation, and digital public infrastructure (UPI, Account Aggregator, OCEN) lowering cost-to-serve and expanding addressable retail/SME credit.
- **Pristine balance sheets + capital.** GNPA at multi-decade lows, provision coverage high, credit costs near cycle trough and CAR comfortably above regulatory minima — banks can grow without equity dilution, and CARE expects "resilience to continue in FY27."

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Structural deposit scarcity.** Deposit growth (~11.5%) lagging credit growth (~14.5%) forces banks to pay up for term deposits and caps loan growth; the elevated CD ratio is the binding constraint on the whole sector.
- **Margin compression is not evenly over.** Fitch sees a further ~10bp of NIM decline; Q2 FY27 results showed divergent margins (ICICI 4.18% vs HDFC Bank 3.26%), with foreign-currency deposit costs and loan-reset lags driving "uneven bank margins."
- **Regulatory and disintermediation risk.** Risk-weight and project-finance provisioning changes, curbs on unsecured retail, and the migration of household savings into capital markets (aided by a weak HDB-style equity tape) structurally erode low-cost CASA funding.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG franchise, ERODING relative momentum**

*Rationale:* HDFC Bank retains the best-in-class Indian banking franchise — largest private-sector balance sheet, unmatched distribution (6,300+ branches, 211k employees), deepest retail/SME liability base and a pristine asset-quality record. It is one of only three banks with genuine system-level pricing influence. However, three years after the HDFC Ltd merger it is still digesting the cost: **NIM has compressed to 3.26%** (from a historical 4.0–4.3%) versus ICICI's 4.18%, pre-provision margins trail ICICI by ~420bp, profit growth has decelerated to mid-single digits, and the stock has de-rated to ~15.5x versus ICICI's ~20.5x — a **~38pp relative share-price underperformance over 12 months**. Management's guide that NIM inflects from the December quarter is the single most important swing factor; a new external CEO and a securities class-action overhang (class period Jul-2023 to May-2026) add execution and headline risk. Verdict: **strong moat, average current earnings momentum.**

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL (tilting Attractive into FY27)**

The operating environment is genuinely improving — double-digit credit growth, a 20-year-low credit cost cycle, and a forecast doubling of sector NII growth to ~16% — which argues for an Attractive structural rating. What holds it at Neutral today is that the margin inflection is **forecast, not yet visible** in reported numbers (Q2 FY27 NIMs fell again at HDFC Bank), deposit competition is structural rather than cyclical, and the sector's re-rating in 2026 has been highly selective: ICICI and SBI have captured the recovery while HDFC Bank has de-rated. **Sector: Neutral / improving. Within it, prefer ICICI (margin leader, momentum) and SBI (credit-growth leader, cheaper) over HDB until HDFC Bank demonstrates an actual, not guided, NIM trough.**
