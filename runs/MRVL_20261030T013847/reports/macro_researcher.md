---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — risk-on in growth and credit, risk-off in duration. "High nominal growth, high real rates."**

The US is in a late-cycle re-acceleration, not a slowdown. Q3 2026 real GDP grew **+3.4% SAAR** (Q2 +2.2%, Q1 +2.5%) and unemployment is **4.2%** — effectively full employment. But inflation has re-accelerated: CPI rose **+3.72% YoY in September 2026**, up from a 3.30% July trough, and the Fed **hiked 25bp on 16 September to 3.75–4.00%**, then held on 28 October with a 10–2 vote. This is the first tightening since the 2024–25 easing cycle took the funds rate from 5.33% to 3.63%. The easing cycle is over.

The critical feature is **where** rates are rising. 10Y breakevens are pinned at **2.35%** (down from 2.49% in May) while the nominal 10Y has risen from 4.38% at end-June to **5.05%** — implying the real 10Y has jumped from ~2.16% to **~2.70%, +54bp in four months**. Combined with a 30Y at **5.37%** and a positively-sloped curve (+34bp 2s10s), this is a textbook **bear steepening driven by real yields and term premium, not inflation expectations**. Meanwhile HY OAS sits at **3.14%**, near cycle tights. Put differently: **credit markets are relaxed, but the discount rate for long-duration equity is being repriced aggressively.** For a 2.26-beta, long-duration AI semiconductor that is the single most important macro fact in this report.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**① Real yields / discount rate — NEGATIVE (highest conviction).** Marvell's equity value is concentrated in 2027–2028 custom AI silicon ramps, making it one of the longest-duration names in the S&P 500. A +54bp move in the real 10Y mechanically compresses the terminal-value component of its DCF by roughly 15–20%. The tape confirms the transmission: MRVL peaked at **$316.28 (4 June)** and sits at **$218.34, −31%**, while the 10Y rose 67bp over the same window. It remains **+161% over twelve months** from $83.69, so it is a crowded, high-multiple asset being repriced — not a broken business.

**② AI capex cycle — POSITIVE, but newly rate-sensitised.** Goldman projects hyperscaler capex +50% to ~$1.2T in 2027, decelerating to +12% in 2028; UBS is more cautious at +25% for 2027. The macro-relevant change: **roughly one-third of 2027 capex is expected to be debt-funded**. At 5.05% on the 10Y, the marginal AI dollar now has a cost of capital attached to it. Data-centre private credit and ABS spreads are the leading indicator for Marvell's 2028 custom-ASIC backlog — watch those, not the Fed.

**③ Growth / employment — POSITIVE.** 3.4% GDP and 4.2% unemployment support the non-AI franchise (carrier infrastructure, enterprise networking, storage controllers) that is still recovering from the 2024–25 telecom inventory correction. This is the cyclical ballast that partially offsets AI sentiment swings.

**④ Inflation on cost structure — NEGATIVE.** Re-accelerating CPI coincides with AI supply-chain input inflation: leading-edge wafer price increases, advanced packaging (CoWoS) scarcity, and memory/HBM cost pressure. Marvell is fabless — it absorbs TSMC pricing rather than setting it — and its pricing power is concentrated in multi-year custom ASIC contracts. Margin risk is real in the commodity networking product lines.

**⑤ US dollar — NEGATIVE (modest).** The broad dollar at 121.3 is up 2.8% in six weeks. Marvell generates the majority of revenue outside the US (China, Taiwan, Japan, Korea, Singapore) while reporting in USD: translation headwind plus a competitiveness drag. Partial offset — its Israeli and Asian R&D cost base becomes cheaper in dollars.

**⑥ Trade / tariffs — NEGATIVE tail risk.** The Section 232 framework includes a 25% tariff on certain semiconductors and related equipment, layered on top of a tightening China export-control regime (Oct 2025 rare-earth retaliation remains live). Marvell's China revenue exposure and Asia-centred supply chain make it a direct tariff and export-licence target. This is a policy binary, not a gradual trend.

**⑦ Balance sheet / refinancing — NEUTRAL to mildly POSITIVE.** Fabless, modest leverage, largely fixed-rate converts: not a REIT- or bank-style rate-sensitive balance sheet. Cash earns 3.75–4.00%, a genuine carry benefit. Caveat: the reported **all-stock Lumentum acquisition** uses equity as deal currency — and equity just got 31% cheaper, which mechanically raises the true cost of the transaction. Shares fell **13.2% on 29 October** on dilution concerns.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Breaking this down honestly: **fundamental demand conditions are favourable** (3.4% GDP, 4.2% unemployment, capex boom intact, credit spreads tight), but **macro-financial conditions are unfavourable** for the valuation of this specific security. A 2.26-beta, 30x-plus-multiple, long-duration asset is the single most exposed position in a bear-steepening regime where real yields are the marginal driver. The rating is Unfavourable at the **multiple** level, Neutral at the **earnings** level. An investor should expect Marvell to trade as a high-beta levered expression of the 10Y, not as an idiosyncratic AI story, until real yields stabilise.

---

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (Bear)

**A term-premium shock, not a recession.** The market has correctly priced inflation expectations (breakevens 2.35%) but is only beginning to price fiscal supply and institutional credibility risk at the long end. If the 30Y breaks 6.0% — driven by midterm-era fiscal expansion and a new Fed chair's inflation-fighting credibility being tested by 3.7% CPI — the real 10Y goes to ~3.25% with **no change in earnings estimates**.

**Transmission to MRVL:** a pure discount-rate event. High-multiple AI semis de-rate a further 25–35% with the AI capex narrative fully intact; Marvell, at beta 2.26, leads the drawdown. Stacked on top, the all-stock Lumentum deal means the company is paying with a depreciating currency, magnifying dilution precisely when shareholders are least tolerant of it. This is a $150–160 stock in an otherwise unchanged fundamental world.

**The offsetting bull surprise** is equally mechanical: if goods deflation resumes and CPI returns to ~2.5% by Q1 2027, the Fed reverses the September hike, the 10Y retraces to 4.30%, and Marvell re-rates back through $300 on the same earnings. Watch the monthly CPI print and the 10Y breakeven/real-yield split — that decomposition, not the headline number, is the trade.
