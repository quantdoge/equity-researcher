**As of 18 November 2026** | Price $96.50 | Market cap $38.9B | Beta 3.61

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Risk-off, in a hawkish repricing regime.**

The US economy is in a "no-landing-lite" configuration that is specifically hostile to MSTR's business model. Real GDP grew ~2.1% YoY in Q3 2026 ($24,611B vs $24,113B), re-accelerating to ~3.4% annualized in the quarter. Unemployment has *fallen* to 4.1% from 4.5% a year ago. Against that resilient labor market, CPI inflation has re-accelerated to **3.82% YoY** (Oct 2026), up from a ~3.3% trough in July — monthly CPI rose 0.35% in October, a 4.3% annualized pace.

The Fed responded by **hiking 25bp in September 2026**, lifting effective fed funds from 3.63% to **3.88%** — the first tightening of this cycle after cuts through 2025. Policy is now roughly neutral ex-post (3.88% nominal vs 3.82% CPI), but the **10Y at 5.13% implies a ~1.3% real yield**, which is genuinely restrictive for zero-cash-flow, long-duration assets. The curve is bear-steepened: 2Y 4.74%, 10Y 5.13%, 30Y 5.47%, 2s10s **+39bp**. No inversion — no recession signal — but the steepening is term-premium/fiscal-supply driven, not growth-driven. That is the worst shape for MSTR: it raises the discount rate on terminal-value assets without the offsetting liquidity easing a recession would eventually force.

Credit is the one cushion. HY OAS at **3.34%** remains historically tight and signals functioning capital markets, but it has widened ~70bp from the 2.80% September low. The window for refinancing is open but closing. The dollar has strengthened 3.2% since mid-September (broad index 122.65), a headwind for a USD-priced global liquidity asset.

For MSTR this regime is uniquely damaging because the company's equity is a **leveraged, perpetual-duration claim on bitcoin funded with rate-linked liabilities**. The macro disconfirmation is stark: CPI rose from 3.3% to 3.82% between July and October 2026 while **bitcoin fell from ~$85k to $64.9k**. BTC is trading as a long-duration risk asset in this regime, not as an inflation hedge — which invalidates the core macro thesis the equity premium was built on.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Strategy holds **~850,364 BTC** (as of 9 Nov 2026) at an average cost of **$66,385** — a ~$56.4B aggregate outlay. At $64,915, the stack is worth **~$55.2B**, i.e. the entire position is now underwater by roughly $1.2B. Against a $38.9B market cap, **equity trades at ~0.70x bitcoin NAV**. Adding the convertible debt and the preferred stack (STRK/STRF/STRD/STRC), enterprise value is approximately at parity with BTC value — **the mNAV premium has fully collapsed to roughly 1.0x EV basis** *(my estimate; debt/preferred balances not verified from filings — confirm before relying on it)*.

**Monetary policy — strongly negative.** Three distinct channels:
1. *Discount rate.* MSTR is the longest-duration instrument in the listed market. A 1.31% real 10Y yield discounts bitcoin's terminal value brutally; BTC −49% from its October 2025 peak is the transmission mechanism.
2. *Liability cost is literally rate-linked.* STRC is a perpetual preferred paying **12.00% annually, reset monthly to market**. It was raised to 12% as rates rose. This is the single most direct macro-to-P&L channel: 12% versus a 5.13% 10Y is a ~687bp structural/credit premium, and every hawkish repricing resets the coupon *up*.
3. *Reflexivity.* Above 1.0x mNAV, issuing equity at a premium accretes BTC-per-share — a self-reinforcing flywheel. **Below 1.0x, ATM issuance is dilutive**, and the company has been selling common stock to fund a **$1.44B USD reserve** covering **>$700M in annual preferred dividends** (a $21.1M draw in the week of 2–8 Nov alone ≈ $1.1B annualized, implying the reserve covers barely ~1.3 years). The funding engine has reversed direction.

**Inflation — negative.** Higher inflation does not help MSTR. It raises the cost of the preferred stack, keeps the Fed hiking, and — critically — the 2025–26 record shows BTC *falling* as CPI rose. The inflation-hedge narrative is empirically failing in this cycle.

**Interest rate sensitivity — extreme.** Empirically, MSTR's beta to BTC is ~1.6–1.7x and amplifying: bitcoin fell 19.2% between 4 and 17 November ($80,400 → $64,915) while MSTR fell 32.3% ($142.54 → $96.50). From the cycle peak, BTC −48.6% vs MSTR −78.9%. A rate-sensitive asset with a 3.6 stated beta, an inverted capital structure, and 12% preferred coupons ahead of common in the waterfall.

**Credit conditions — mildly negative.** HY spreads of 3.34% mean the convertible market is still functioning; bankruptcy is not the near-term risk. But widening from 2.80% raises the cost of rolling converts whose equity conversion options are now deeply out-of-the-money, converting them into a straight debt overhang.

**Currency — negative.** The broad dollar is up 3.2% in two months and 2.3% since June. Bitcoin is a dollar-denominated global liquidity asset; dollar strength drains the marginal non-US buyer. Note MSTR reports in USD with international software operations, but at $38.9B market cap versus a sub-$500M software business, FX translation is immaterial — **the relevant currency exposure is the dollar's effect on BTC price, not on reported revenue**.

**Fiscal/trade — neutral-to-negative.** Term premium at the long end (5.47% 30Y) reflects heavy Treasury supply. Fiscal expansion keeps real yields elevated, which is the binding constraint on the whole digital-asset complex.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

This is close to a worst-case macro configuration for MSTR: growth strong enough to force the Fed to hike, inflation re-accelerating to 3.82%, a 5.13% 10Y with +1.3% real yields, a strengthening dollar, no recession signal to trigger a liquidity pivot, and precisely the asset class that bears the highest duration cost. Every macro variable that matters is pointed the wrong way, and the company's structural response — selling equity below NAV to pay a 12% coupon on a drawn reserve — is the *mechanically dilutive* branch of its own operating model.

The one genuine stabiliser is credit: HY OAS at 3.34% means capital markets are open and near-term solvency is not the issue. That caps the downside at "sustained de-rating" rather than "forced liquidation" — for now.

## 5. Surprise Scenario

**Bear (higher probability, ~40%): The Fed hikes again on 9 December and the "below-NAV trap" becomes visible.**

Two more months of 0.3%+ monthly CPI prints push the Dec 9 FOMC to a second consecutive 25bp hike. The 10Y clears 5.50% and real yields approach 1.7%. Bitcoin breaks the June 2026 low of $58,524 and heads to **$50–55k**. At 850k BTC that puts gross BTC value near $44–47B against ~$16–18B of debt and preferred — **EV/mNAV falls below 1.0x and equity mNAV to ~0.55–0.6x**. The binding constraint becomes the $1.44B dividend reserve: at a $1.1B annual run-rate, an equity raise into a collapsing stock is the only funding source, and any reduction in the STRC monthly reset would be read by preferred holders as a credit event. Reflexivity inverts — dilution begets a lower price begets more dilution. Downside on the equity is another 35–45% from $96.50.

**Bull (tail, ~15%): October's 3.82% CPI marks the peak.**

If November and December prints revert toward 3.2%, the December hike is priced out, the 10Y retraces to ~4.6%, and the dollar rolls over from 122.65. Bitcoin re-rates through $85k toward the $90–100k zone on returning ETF flows. At $90k, the stack is worth $76B and equity mNAV snaps back to ~1.2x, instantly restoring the accretive ATM flywheel and letting management raise common equity *at a premium* to term out the 12% preferred. The equity re-rates 70–90% in that scenario because the operating leverage to BTC price is now roughly 1.7x on a de-rated base. Note the asymmetry: the same mechanical leverage that produced −79% from the peak works in both directions, and the stock is now discounting a substantial portion of the bear case.

**Asymmetry call:** the macro setup argues for patience, not heroism. The single number to watch is the **November CPI print (mid-December) versus the 9 December FOMC**. A soft CPI alongside a Fed pause is the only macro path that reopens MSTR's capital-markets flywheel; a hike confirms the de-rating has further to run.
