# Strategy Inc. (MSTR) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $96.50 (last close 17-Nov-2026) · Market cap ~$40.6B basic / ~$43.9B fully diluted · 850,849 BTC held*

---

## 0. First, What You Actually Own

Before any moat talk, be precise about the object of study. Strategy is **not a software company that owns bitcoin**. It is a **levered, closed-end bitcoin vehicle with a small (~$1B) software stub attached**, funded by a $19.7B senior claims stack.

| Component | Value / Size |
|---|---|
| Bitcoin held (16-Nov-26) | **850,849 BTC** = 4.05% of all BTC that will exist |
| BTC market value (~$64,300/coin) | **$54.7B** |
| BTC cost basis / avg cost | $64.2B / **$75,455 per coin** → *unrealized loss –$9.5B (–15%)* |
| Cash / USD Reserve (30-Sep-26) | $5.9B |
| Convertible notes (0% coupon, 2029+) | $6.71B notional |
| Preferred: STRC $7.99B, STRD $1.40B, STRK $1.40B, STRF $1.28B, STRE/other $0.86B | **$12.93B** |
| **Total senior claims** | **$19.7B** (≈306,000 BTC, 36% of the pile) |
| Software: Q3-26 revenue / EBIT | $125.4M (71% GM) / ~$21M |

Every subsequent judgment follows from this: the equity is a **residual claim on 850,849 coins minus $19.7B of fixed-dollar promises**.

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NONE**

I cannot find one attribute from the moat checklist that applies to the asset that constitutes ~98% of enterprise value:

- **No pricing power.** Bitcoin is a commodity with a market-clearing price. Strategy is a price-taker on 100% of its purchases and has no ability to influence the price of its inventory.
- **No cost advantage.** It buys at spot, sometimes above (recent buys: 485 BTC on 15-Nov-26; $255M for 3,273 BTC in September). Scale confers *no* discount.
- **No switching costs, no network effects accruing to MSTR.** Bitcoin's network effect accrues to Bitcoin, not to a holder of it.
- **No intangible assets.** No patents, brands, or licenses protect the treasury. The software business has genuine switching costs, but it is ~2% of enterprise value and has grown revenue at roughly 3% CAGR ($499M FY22 → $477M FY25).

**The one genuine edge — and how it broke.** From 2020–2024 Strategy possessed a real, if unusual, advantage: it was the *only* scaled, listed, levered bitcoin vehicle, and it could sell equity at 2–3× NAV and buy coins, mechanically raising BTC-per-share. That capital-markets arbitrage was a moat of a sort.

It is gone. Spot bitcoin ETFs now provide cost-effective, unlevered, no-key-man bitcoin exposure for ~20bps. Strategy's substitute-product protection has evaporated precisely as its premium has. Current readings: **mNAV ~1.07× gross (52-week range 0.93×–1.32×); on the conservative CEBE basis — netting the senior stack against the coins first — the common trades at 1.10–1.26× the bitcoin actually backing it.** The arbitrage has inverted from accretive to dilutive.

**Has the moat widened or narrowed over five years?** Dramatically narrowed. The company's own disclosure proves it:

> At 25-May-2026 the company reported **220,900 sats per share**. Today the same metric is **~202,300 sats** — a **–8.4% decline in bitcoin-per-share in under six months**, despite raising on the order of $15B+ of capital over that stretch. Share count rose from 294M (FY25 weighted) to 400M (Q3-26 weighted, +36% in nine months) while BTC rose from 650,000 to 850,849 (+31%).

That is the whole story in two numbers: **the ATM machine is running, and bitcoin-per-share is going backwards.** Management brands this "BTC yield" when it is positive; in 2026 it is negative and the metric has been quietly redefined toward "BTC $ gain."

**Moat verdict: None (0/10).** Not a criticism of bitcoin — a criticism of the claim that holding it confers a durable competitive advantage.

| Gross / operating margin (software) | 68.7% / –8.6% FY25; ~71% / +17% Q3-26 | Improving after cost cuts |

| **Free cash flow (FY25)** | **–$112M** (FY24 –$56M, FY23 –$1.89B) | Persistently negative |
| FCF conversion (FCF/NI) | Not meaningful | — |
| ROIC / ROE | Not meaningful | The "invested capital" (bitcoin) generates **zero** operating cash return — only mark-to-market |
| Cash preferred dividends (FY25 paid) | $381M | **Run-rate now ~$1.3–1.5B** (STRC alone: 12% × $7.99B ≈ $959M) |
| Net cash / (debt) | +$5.9B cash vs $6.78B debt carrying value, but **$19.7B total senior claims** | Thin at the top of the stack |
| Dilution | 294M → 400M shares (+36%) in 9 months | Severe |

**The bear case in one arithmetic fact:** Strategy must pay roughly **$1.4B per year in cash preferred dividends and interest**, against a business generating $477M of revenue and negative operating cash flow. That cash comes from three places: the $5.9B USD Reserve (≈4 years of coverage), new preferred issuance, or new common issuance. With mNAV at ~1.0×, the third option **transfers** value from common holders to preferred holders every time it is used. That is precisely what the sats-per-share decline measures.

Credit must be given where due: the converts are **0% coupon and all six tranches are out of the money**, and management retired $1.5B of 0% 2029 notes at ~92 cents in May 2026 — buying back debt below par is genuinely accretive. At current BTC, senior claims are covered ~2.8× by the coin pile (**break-even BTC ≈ $23,100**, 64% below spot). So: **not a solvency story — a value-transfer and negative-carry story.**

Because GAAP earnings are a mark-to-market artifact, the income statement is not merely uninformative, it is misleading: Q1-26 net loss –$12.5B, Q2-26 –$8.2B, Q3-26 net income **+$19.5B** (EPS $44.75), then BTC fell ~23% in Q4. Discount the headline entirely.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

DCF on owner earnings is not applicable — the asset produces no cash flow and therefore has no discountable stream. The correct conservative approach is a **NAV bridge with the senior claims deducted first**, which is exactly what a value investor would do buying a closed-end fund at a discount (or premium).

**Per-share NAV bridge** (net senior claims = $19.7B notional – $5.9B cash = $13.8B; software at 10–12× ~$85M EBIT = ~$1.0B):

| Bitcoin price | BTC value | – Net senior claims | + Software | Equity value | Per share (420.5M basic) | vs $96.50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $30,000 | $25.5B | $13.8B | $1.0B | $12.7B | **$30** | –69% |
| $40,000 | $34.0B | $13.8B | $1.0B | $21.2B | **$50** | –48% |
| $50,000 | $42.5B | $13.8B | $1.0B | $29.7B | **$71** | –27% |
| **$64,300 (spot)** | **$54.7B** | **$13.8B** | **$1.0B** | **$41.9B** | **~$100** | **+3%** |
| $80,000 | $68.1B | $13.8B | $1.0B | $55.3B | **$131** | +36% |
| $100,000 | $85.1B | $13.8B | $1.0B | $72.3B | **$172** | +78% |
| $150,000 | $127.6B | $13.8B | $1.0B | $114.8B | **$273** | +183% |

**Intrinsic value at spot bitcoin ≈ $95–100 per share** (the range reflects basic vs. fully diluted share count; converts are out of the money, so basic is arguably the fairer denominator).

**Conclusion: the stock trades at ~0.97–1.02× intrinsic value. There is no margin of safety. On the CEBE-mNAV basis the market is charging a 10–26% premium to the bitcoin actually backing each share.** Multiples are otherwise meaningless: P/E –6.3×, P/B 0.56× (a book figure that is simply a bitcoin mark and ignores the $12.9B preferred seniority), EV/EBITDA –6.3×, FCF yield negative.

**The asymmetry is roughly symmetric.** Upside/downside beta to BTC is ~1.7–1.9× in both directions. You are not being paid to take that risk — you are paying a small premium for it.

*Sell-side targets of $175–$435 (B.Riley $180, Cantor $208, Clear Street $211, H.C. Wainwright $250, StoneX $435) are bitcoin price forecasts dressed as valuations; a $435 target implies BTC well above $200,000. They are not evidence about intrinsic value.*

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Mixed, trending acceptable on capital allocation, poor on disclosure**

**Positives:**
- Saylor's 2020 pivot created enormous wealth for early holders; the 0%-coupon convert structure was genuinely innovative and shareholder-friendly (low cost, long-dated, no covenants of note).
- The May 2026 retirement of $1.5B of converts at 92 cents was disciplined — debt repurchase below par is unambiguous value creation.
- No common dividend, no empire-building M&A, no evidence of value-destructive acquisitions; the software business has been harvested, not expanded.

**Negatives:**
- **The cost of capital is indefensible.** Issuing perpetual preferred at 12% (STRC) to buy a zero-yield asset is a guaranteed negative carry of ~$960M/year on that tranche alone. That is a ~12% hurdle the underlying asset cannot clear in cash terms. Historically cheap converts have been exchanged for expensive perpetual capital.
- **Metric shopping.** "BTC yield" was the headline KPI while it compounded; in 2026 it went negative and the framing shifted to "BTC $ gain." Reporting "years of coverage" by counting the BTC reserve against fixed cash dividends implies treating a volatile asset as a liability match.
- **Governance.** Dual-class structure (Class B, 10 votes/share) leaves Saylor with effective voting control on a small economic stake; a board widely viewed as aligned with the founder; a 1,539-person company run as a bitcoin-first vehicle with the operating business an afterthought.
- **Reflexivity risk in the model itself.** The strategy only works when the stock trades above NAV. Management has not articulated a plan for a persistent sub-1.0× mNAV regime other than continuing to issue.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

For a Buffett-style portfolio this fails on every gate that matters:

1. **Outside the circle of competence in the precise sense** — there are no cash flows to discount and no owner earnings to value. "Intrinsic value" collapses to net asset value of a commodity.
2. **No moat** — and the one structural edge (premium capital raising) has inverted.
3. **Negative free cash flow with a ~$1.4B annual cash obligation** funded by securities issuance.
4. **Bitcoin-per-share is shrinking (–8.4% YTD 2026)** — the mechanism that justified paying a premium to NAV is running in reverse.
5. **No margin of safety** — ~1.0× NAV, i.e. you pay full price for the coins plus the leverage plus the fees.

I stop short of *Strong Sell* only because the balance sheet is not fragile: 2.8× asset coverage, 0% coupon converts all out of the money, no near maturities, $5.9B liquidity. **This is a "don't own it," not a "short it"** — shorting is a directional bet on bitcoin, not a value judgment, and the equity has ~1.8× upside beta if BTC re-rates.

**What would make me a buyer:** (a) CEBE-mNAV ≤ 0.70×, i.e. ~$67/share at today's bitcoin; **or** (b) hard evidence that sats-per-share is compounding again (sustained mNAV > 1.4× with disciplined issuance); **or** (c) management pivoting to selling bitcoin to repurchase stock at a discount to NAV — the one genuinely accretive move available to them today, and the opposite of current policy. Existing holders should use strength toward the 52-week mNAV highs to reduce.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (i.e., how I'm wrong)

1. **Bitcoin rallies sharply.** At $150k BTC the equity is worth ~$273/share. This is the dominant risk and it is not small — the position is a levered call.
2. **The premium returns.** At mNAV > 1.5× the flywheel restarts and BTC-per-share rises again; the model is reflexive and self-repairing in bull markets. A 1.32× 52-week high shows how fast it can re-inflate.
3. **STRC's dividend is variable and monthly-reset.** In a falling short-rate environment its 12% coupon could reset materially lower, cutting the ~$1.4B cash burden sharply — a large, underappreciated improvement to the carry.
4. **Strategic actions.** Index inclusion, a sale/IPO of the software business, activist pressure on the dual-class structure, or an acquisition of Strategy at a premium would all invalidate a "fairly valued" call.
5. **Accounting optics.** The fair-value regime means book equity tracks spot; in an up-year, GAAP net income will print tens of billions and can force momentum and quant re-rating regardless of economics.

**Honest gaps in this analysis:** I did not verify the full convertible maturity ladder, the exact USD Reserve balance at 17-Nov-2026 (I used the 30-Sep-26 $5.9B), the precise reset terms on STRC/STRD/STRF, or current insider ownership/transaction data. The ~$1.4B preferred run-rate is my calculation from disclosed coupons, not a reported figure. None of these gaps changes the direction of the conclusion — the stock is being offered at approximately the value of the bitcoin behind it, and that is not a value investment.
