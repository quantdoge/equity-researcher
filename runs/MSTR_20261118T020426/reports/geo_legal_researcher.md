## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**① Index-provider exclusion contagion — HIGH probability, HIGH impact (already materializing)**
- MSCI **confirmed removal of Strategy from MSCI USA, MSCI World and MSCI ACWI at the Nov 30, 2026 close** under its new "non-operating company" / digital-asset-treasury rule (Reuters, 2026-11-11; MSCI Nov 11 index review announcement). Strategy's shares fell ~6% on confirmation.
- JPMorgan estimated up to **$2.8B** of passive outflows from MSCI alone; earlier estimates ranged $1.6B–$8.8B.
- **Contagion is the live risk:** FTSE Russell has instead built a *separate* "FTSE Digital Asset Treasury Index Series" (classification rather than exclusion — favorable), and **MSTR retained its Nasdaq-100 slot at the annual reconstitution**. But Nasdaq-100 December 2026 reconstitution and S&P Dow Jones methodology reviews remain open vectors. A second exclusion would compound forced-selling into an already distressed tape.
- This is quasi-sovereign rule-making risk: index providers are private but *de facto* gatekeepers of the passive capital that underwrites MSTR's premium-to-NAV financing model. Strategy has publicly campaigned against the rule (strategy.com/msci) and argued it is discriminatory — escalation to litigation against index providers is conceivable but legally weak.

**② US tax regime on unrealized Bitcoin gains (CAMT) — MEDIUM probability of adverse change, VERY HIGH impact**
- The 15% corporate alternative minimum tax on adjusted financial statement income threatened to tax *unrealized* Bitcoin gains — at one point an exposure measured against ~**$27B of paper profits** (WSJ, Bloomberg, Oct 2025).
- The **Treasury/IRS clarified on ~Sep 30, 2025 that unrealized crypto gains are excluded from CAMT**, driving a rally in MSTR; Strategy and Coinbase jointly petitioned the IRS for durable guidance (Tax Notes).
- **Residual risk:** this rests on *administrative guidance, not statute*. A change of administration, a reversal of the guidance, or adverse statutory drafting in a future revenue package could re-expose the company to multi-billion-dollar cash tax on non-cash gains — precisely the scenario that would force Bitcoin sales. Note MSTR *did* sell 32 BTC in late May 2026 (~$2.5M at ~$77,135 avg.) per 8-K — evidence the "never sell" doctrine is not absolute.

**③ US crypto-policy reversal / political-cyclical risk — MEDIUM probability, HIGH impact**
- The supportive 2025–26 US posture (SEC's March 2026 crypto interpretation; proposed **Regulation Crypto Assets**, Aug 18, 2026; the **CLARITY Act**, H.R.3633) is politically contingent. MSTR's model depends on favorable securities treatment of its equity/preferred complex and on no adverse federal intervention in digital-asset treasury (DAT) structures.
- A Fed tightening cycle is the proximate macro threat in the current tape: a December 9, 2026 hike is being modeled to push Bitcoin toward ~$56.5K, below Strategy's aggregate cost basis. Q1 2026 already produced a **$1.73B** loss as cost basis exceeded fair value (SEC filing, Apr 2026); Bitcoin has roughly halved over 13 months.
- Secondary: reputational/political backlash risk tied to the Saylor political profile (a prior **$40M D.C. Attorney General tax settlement** involving Saylor/company) — low financial materiality, non-trivial headline risk.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Securities class actions (multiple, stacking):**
- **Filed May 2025** — class period **Apr 30, 2024 – Apr 4, 2025**; alleges the company misled investors about the profitability and volatility of its Bitcoin strategy in connection with adopting fair-value accounting (The Block, 2025-05-19; Kessler Topaz; Pomerantz; lead-plaintiff deadline July 15, 2025).
- **Second wave, 2026** — Rosen Law Firm investigation and filed action with class period **Feb 27, 2026 – Oct 30, 2026**, explicitly covering **MSTR, STRF, STRC, STRK and STRD** (Business Wire, 2026-06-24). *This is the more dangerous case*: it post-dates the fair-value regime and the drawdown, and it names the preferreds — i.e., it attacks the retail-preferred distribution channel.

**Delaware preferred-stock class action (structurally important):**
- Action alleging **violation of DGCL §242** — that Strategy amended the **STRK** (8.00% Series A Perpetual Strike) terms **without a common-stockholder vote** — plus breach of fiduciary duty; seeks **rescission** and declaratory relief (8-K, July 21; SEC filing 2026-02-23). Rescission risk on a series that underpins the preferred-issuance engine would be more damaging than a damages award.

**Derivative litigation:**
- Shareholder **derivative suit filed June 19, 2025, E.D. Va.** against MicroStrategy/board (SEC 8-K, 2025-06-16).
- A separate matter was resolved with the company paying **$550,000** in plaintiff attorneys' fees (8-K) — a small, settled item indicating nuisance-value resolution pattern for some claims.

**Regulatory posture:**
- **SEC:** no enforcement action identified. The SEC is in a *permissive rule-making* mode toward crypto (Regulation Crypto Assets proposal, Aug 2026), which is supportive but adds disclosure/offering compliance obligations over time.
- **FINRA/retail distribution:** STRC (12% annual dividend, paid daily) and sister preferreds are now distributed through retail channels (Robinhood listing reported). FINRA's 2026 focus on higher-risk structured/complex products and its Rule 2210 amendments (SEC-approved Oct 15, 2026, permitting projected/targeted returns) create a **suitability and marketing-standards exposure** surface for the preferred complex. No formal action identified, but this is the most plausible *new* regulatory front.
- **Tax:** CAMT guidance as above; DC AG matter settled (2024).

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Classic geopolitical risk is genuinely *low* — MSTR is a US-domiciled, US-listed company with ~57% US and ~34% EMEA software revenue, no sanctions, no EM expropriation, no export-control or tariff exposure, and de minimis currency-repatriation risk. **But the rating is driven up by regulatory and legal concentration in a single jurisdiction**, where three forces converge: (i) *confirmed* exclusion from MSCI's flagship indices with a multi-billion-dollar passive-outflow overhang and unresolved Nasdaq-100/S&P contagion; (ii) tax treatment of unrealized Bitcoin gains resting on **reversible administrative guidance** rather than statute, with a prior exposure measured in the tens of billions; and (iii) an escalating, overlapping litigation stack — including a 2026 class period naming all four preferred series and a Delaware §242 rescission claim — set against a >50% Bitcoin drawdown that has already produced a $1.73B loss and forced a (small) Bitcoin sale.

**Not rated "Very High"** because: no government investigation or enforcement action is outstanding, the current US administration and SEC are affirmatively accommodative (Regulation Crypto Assets, CLARITY Act), FTSE Russell chose classification over exclusion, and the Nasdaq-100 retention shows index contagion is not automatic.

**What would move this to Very High:** (1) CAMT exclusion guidance being withdrawn or legislated away; (2) a second major index exclusion (S&P Dow Jones or Nasdaq-100) or a court granting relief in the STRK §242 rescission action; (3) any DOJ/SEC inquiry into disclosures or preferred marketing; (4) a sustained Bitcoin price below cost basis forcing large-scale sales.
