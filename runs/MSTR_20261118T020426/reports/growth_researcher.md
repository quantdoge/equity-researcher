# MSTR — Strategy Inc. | Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $96.50 (Nov 18, 2026) · Market cap ~$43.9B fully diluted · Beta 3.61**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Strategy is no longer a software company that owns Bitcoin — it is a **Bitcoin-native capital markets franchise** that happens to run a small analytics business. Its innovation is not the analytics suite but the *securitization of a monetary asset*: a four-tranche preferred stack (STRK 8%, STRF/STRD 10%, STRC 12% variable) layered on $19.7B of senior claims, used to convert Bitcoin's volatility into yield products for income investors. It holds **850,849 BTC — roughly 4% of all Bitcoin that will ever exist**, the largest corporate treasury on earth and the single deepest TAM capture of any listed company in the asset class.

The problem is that the machine which funded this — issuing common equity at a premium to Bitcoin NAV — has seized up. mNAV has collapsed from **2.4x at the 2024 peak to 1.07x today**, and Q3 2026 BTC Yield (BTC-per-share accretion, the company's own primary KPI) turned **negative at −3.7%**. The stock is down **76%** from its June 2025 high of $404, and Google search interest has fallen 88%. This is a post-hype repricing of a levered Bitcoin vehicle, not a growth company executing a demand curve.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

There are two TAMs, and they should be underwritten separately.

**TAM 1 — Bitcoin as a monetary asset (~$1.3T).** Strategy has captured ~4% of terminal supply. This is genuinely remarkable penetration for a single balance sheet. But note: penetration of *supply* is not penetration of *demand* — the company owns 4% of the asset and its market cap is ~80% of the gross value of that asset. There is no further "share gain" available here; only price beta.

**TAM 2 — Bitcoin credit / digital-asset fixed income (the real innovation).** This is the Cathie Wood leg. Strategy raised **$21.2B YTD 2026** across the preferred stack and convertibles, against a global fixed-income market of roughly **$140–300T**. Creating a yield curve on a non-sovereign monetary asset — now with **daily dividends** (STRC effective Nov 1, 2026; STRF/STRK/STRD Jan 1, 2027) to mimic money-market mechanics — is a genuine financial-product innovation with essentially no scaled competitor. Strategy is the incumbent.

**But the cost of that capital is punishing.** With the 10-year Treasury at 5.10%, Strategy must pay 8% (STRK) to 12% (STRC) — a 300–700bp spread. Bitcoin must compound at **>12%/yr simply to cover the marginal STRC tranche** before the common earns anything. That is an aggressive hurdle for a levered closed-end structure, and it is the core reason the model only works when mNAV is *above* ~1.5x and BTC is rising.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4/FY2026 results — Feb 4, 2027.** Consensus EPS +$1.88, revenue $129M. Expect a **~$16B mark-to-market loss** (BTC fell from ~$83,600 at Sept 30 to ~$64,250 now, −23%). GAAP optics will be ugly; watch BTC Yield and whether they resumed MSTR repurchases under the new $1.0B program.
- **Daily dividend rollout completion — Jan 1, 2027** (STRF, STRK, STRD). Success here would broaden the income-investor bid for the preferreds and lower future cost of capital — the single most important structural development.
- **Preferred buyback expansion ($2B → $3B).** $2.04B of STRC already repurchased. Aggressive deleveraging of the senior stack would *mechanically raise* CEBE per share — genuine, cash-funded accretion that doesn't require a premium mNAV.
- **mNAV re-expansion above ~1.3x.** The only condition that restarts the ATM accretion flywheel. Currently 1.07x, in a 52-week range of 0.93x–1.32x — it has been knocking on the door.
- **Management transition risk/opportunity:** CFO Andrew Kang stepped down post-Q3. A new CFO's capital-allocation stance is a live re-rating variable.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

**This is a Hold, not a Buy, and the distinction matters.** On this framework's own metrics — Rule of 40 of −20.6, revenue −2.6% YoY, gross margin down 13pp over four years, negative FCF, 115% share dilution in two years — MSTR **fails every growth quality test**. It is not a disruptive-growth compounder; it is a **1.25x-levered Bitcoin NAV vehicle trading at 1.07x net asset value with a ~3.7%/yr preferred-dividend drag**.

Why not Sell: at 1.07x you are no longer paying the absurd 2.4x premium that made MSTR indefensible in 2024. There is a **64% cushion to the $23,106 break-even BTC price**, $5.8B of USD reserves covering ~3 years of dividends, and the preferred buyback is *genuinely* accretive to CEBE per share. Attention and sentiment are at five-year lows. The asymmetry is no longer negative.

Why not Buy: the accretion engine requires mNAV > ~1.5x and it is at 1.07x. **Q3 BTC Yield went negative (−3.7%)** — the first real evidence that the model dilutes BTC-per-share at current spreads. Raising capital at 12% to buy an asset you must hold through a 23% drawdown destroys per-share value. And a ~$16B Q4 mark-to-market loss is about to hit the tape.

**Positioning:** wait for either (a) BTC Yield turning durably positive, or (b) mNAV sustaining above 1.25x, or (c) a Q4 print confirming preferred deleveraging is lifting CEBE. Anyone who wants Bitcoin beta should own IBIT at 0.25%/yr rather than pay a 7% premium plus a 3.7% dividend drag. Only levered-Bitcoin mandates with a >12% BTC return assumption should hold MSTR here.

## 7. Key Risks

**Execution / structural (highest severity):**
1. **Negative BTC Yield is the thesis-killer.** If Q3's −3.7% persists, the company is a value-destroying converter of equity into Bitcoin. Every preferred dividend paid at 8–12% while BTC yields nothing is a real cash cost against a non-cash-flowing asset.
2. **Refinancing / senior-claim risk.** $19.7B of claims (36% of the BTC pile) sit ahead of the common, with the majority of convertibles struck at Bitcoin prices well above spot. If BTC stays near $64k, converts refinance expensively or convert dilutively.
3. **Forced-seller dynamics.** A leverage stack plus a falling collateral price is the classic reflexive loop. The $23,106 break-even is 64% away — comfortable today, not comfortable at 3.6 beta if Bitcoin halves again.
4. **Rising rates.** A 5.10% 10-year makes 8–12% preferred capital progressively harder to place; the daily-dividend innovation is partly a response to that pressure.

**Competitive:**
5. **The ETF complex has commoditized the core product.** IBIT, FBTC et al. deliver clean, low-fee, unlevered spot exposure. MSTR's historic reason to exist — "the only compliant way to own Bitcoin in a brokerage account" — is gone. The remaining differentiator is leverage, which is a feature few allocators want at a 7% premium.
6. **Software business is subscale and decelerating** against Microsoft/Salesforce/Tableau, with gross margins structurally eroding as AI inference costs bite. It is worth perhaps $1.5–2.5B on ~5x revenue — under 5% of the market cap.

**Governance / disclosure:**
7. CFO departure at the exact moment the capital stack is most stressed; a $1.0B MSTR buyback authorized but unused (signals management prefers optionality over deploying capital).
