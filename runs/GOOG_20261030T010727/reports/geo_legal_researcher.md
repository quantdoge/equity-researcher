## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Regulatory Risks

### Risk #1 — EU structural remedy on ad tech (probability: Medium; impact: High)
The European Commission fined Google **€2.95B in September 2025** for abuse of dominance in ad tech and, per EVP Teresa Ribera, signalled a fine alone was "not enough" and that a **structural remedy such as selling part of its ad tech business** appeared "necessary and proportionate" (EU Commission press release IP/25/1992; ProgressChamber analysis, 3-Oct-2026). The EC's decision is on appeal, but the finding stands during appeal.

*Contrast:* In the parallel US case, Judge Leonie Brinkema's **106-page remedies opinion unsealed 16-Sep-2026** in *United States v. Google* (E.D. Va.) **rejected DOJ's divestiture request** and accepted Google's behavioural remedies — Google keeps AdX and DFP. This is a meaningful positive precedent that the EU may reference, but the EU is not bound by it and has historically been more structural in remedy design. Google's counter-argument (Sept-2026) that DMA-driven changes already cut free direct-booking traffic to European businesses by ~30% shows how remedies directly degrade product economics.

**Assessment:** A forced divestiture of AdX/DFP remains a tail risk (est. 10–20%) but **behavioural-only outcomes are now the base case (~60–70%)**, reinforced by Brinkema. Even behaviour-only, ongoing product redesign in EU search/ads removes monetisation efficiency.

### Risk #2 — EU DMA enforcement ratchet (probability: High; impact: Medium-High)
On **23 July 2026 the Commission fined Google €890M** in two non-compliance decisions under the Digital Markets Act (Commission DMA case page). In **September 2026** Google began revamping European search results to avoid further DMA fines while publicly warning of "lower quality" results and a 30% drop in free direct-booking traffic to EU businesses (Reuters, 8-Sep-2026). DMA fines can reach 10% of global turnover for repeat infringement, and the Commission has open investigations into Google's DMA compliance.

**Assessment:** Near-certain recurrence of 8–9 figure annual EU fines; the bigger cost is the **permanent degradation of EU search/ads product differentiation** versus the US. Probability of a further ≥€500M DMA fine within 18 months: >60%.

### Risk #3 — US antitrust overhang and remedies implementation (probability: High; impact: Medium)
- **Search case:** Google filed its **Notice of Appeal on 16-Jan-2026** challenging both the monopoly finding and remedies — including **search-index and user-interaction data-sharing with "Qualified Competitors" and a court-supervised technical committee**. The D.C. District Court **denied Google's motion to pause core remedies** pending appeal, so data-sharing obligations are live now. DOJ has cross-appealed seeking stronger remedies (D.C. Circuit; CRS Legal Sidebar LSB11362).
- **Ad tech:** behavioural remedies from Brinkema, on appeal by both sides.
- **Structural overhang:** Gemini's rapid market-share rise is being used by advocacy groups to argue remedies should be *strengthened*, keeping the political pressure on.

**Assessment:** Remedy-compliance cost is moderate and quantifiable; the tail risk is a D.C. Circuit affirmance plus a DOJ cross-appeal win that reopens divestiture — low probability (<10%), but non-zero and highly public.

**Also material (not top-3 but worth flagging):** **China.** SAMR's February 2025 Android antitrust probe has been **suspended ("zhongzhi") during US–China trade talks** (FT) — i.e., it is now a **geopolitical bargaining chip that can be reactivated at will**. Separately, China's **new export curbs on AI technologies (Oct-2026)** and US rules targeting Chinese remote access to advanced chips intersect directly with Alphabet's **new external TPU sales business** (launched April 2026, first revenue in Q2 2026), exposing that fledgling silicon line to two-sided export-control risk.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Antitrust / competition**
- **EU ad tech:** €2.95B fine (Sep-2025); structural remedy under active consideration; on appeal.
- **EU DMA:** €890M in two decisions (23-Jul-2026); ongoing compliance investigations; search results redesigned Sep-2026.
- **US search (D.D.C.):** liability affirmed 2024; remedies = data sharing + technical committee; **no Chrome divestiture**; Google appeal filed 16-Jan-2026; stay denied; DOJ cross-appeal.
- **US ad tech (E.D. Va.):** liability Apr-2025; **remedies opinion unsealed 16-Sep-2026 — divestiture rejected, behavioural remedies accepted**; both sides appealing.
- **UK CMA:** initial SMS (Strategic Market Status) investigation into general search and search advertising under the DMCC Act; designation would impose conduct requirements enforceable with turnover-based penalties (CMA case page; Steptoe analysis).
- **Other jurisdictions (background):** India CCI Android order (₹1,337 crore, remedies contested), Korea, Turkey, Japan — recurring conduct-remedy risk, individually immaterial, collectively a persistent ~low-hundreds-of-bps margin drag.

**Securities litigation (new, active)**
- **Federal securities class action** alleging Alphabet misrepresented development progress of its **Gemini 3.5 Pro AI model**; class period **19-May-2026 to 16-Jul-2026** (trigger: Bloomberg report, 16-Jul-2026); **lead-plaintiff deadline 1-Dec-2026**; multiple firms (Kirby McInerney, Bragar Eagel & Squire, Kaplan Fox, Rosen, Kessler Topaz) soliciting. This is a routine-framed AI-disclosure suit but it lands precisely as the market repriced Alphabet on AI execution (‑13%, ~$500B market cap on 29-Oct-2026), so **amended complaints and a broadening class are likely**.

**Regulatory/disclosure adjacent**
- The 29-Oct-2026 selloff was driven by Q3 EPS of **$2.67 adjusted vs $3.02 expected** and negative FCF from capex/inventory build — this raises the probability of **additional disclosure-oriented securities suits** around AI capex and search-monetisation commentary.

**Sanctions / export controls**
- No OFAC/DOJ sanctions enforcement action against Alphabet identified. Watch items: **Russian subsidiary insolvency and accumulating Russian court fines** (uncollectible but reputationally/legally noisy); **China AI export curbs** and US remote-compute rules vs. Google Cloud and TPU sales.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **HIGH**

**Rationale.** Political/geopolitical risk is *moderate* — Alphabet has negligible direct revenue in sanctioned or expropriation-prone markets (Russia, Iran), and US–China tensions touch it mainly through the **suspended-but-revivable SAMR probe** and **AI chip export controls**. The binding constraint is **regulatory and legal**, not geopolitical: Alphabet faces the deepest, most diversified antitrust remediation programme of any company globally — two US monopolisation remedies regimes on appeal, a €2.95B EU ad tech fine with **structural relief still formally on the table**, €890M of DMA fines in a single July-2026 day, and a new UK SMS conduct regime. Add a fresh AI-disclosure securities class action and a TPU business that is newly exposed to two-sided export controls.

**Practical read for the investment case:** the *base case* is behavioural remedies everywhere, which is survivable and already partly priced after the 29-Oct-2026 drawdown. The **asymmetric risk is Europe**: a Commission decision to pursue an ad tech divestiture (or to escalate DMA penalties toward turnover-linked levels) would hit the highest-margin part of Google Services, and unlike the US, the EU has explicitly reserved that option. Treat headline-fine risk as recurring operating cost; treat EU structural action as the tail to monitor.

**Key catalysts to watch (next 12 months):** D.C. Circuit search-remedy appeal; EU ad tech structural-remedy follow-through and any Article 7 divestiture order; further DMA non-compliance decisions; Gemini 3.5 Pro class-action consolidation (Dec-2026); reactivation of China's SAMR probe as trade leverage; scope of US/China AI-chip export rules on external TPU sales.
