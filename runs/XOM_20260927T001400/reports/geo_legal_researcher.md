# EXON MOBIL (XOM) — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment

**Scope:** XOM is a US-headquartered integrated oil & gas major (mkt cap ~$665B; CEO Darren Woods; HQ Irving, TX). Business spans Upstream (Permian, Guyana, LNG), Downstream (US Gulf Coast, Singapore), and Chemicals. Analyst note: the firm's official 10-K reports revenue by *business line*, not geography, and the 10-K excerpt endpoint was unavailable this session — geographic splits below are triangulated from a third-party aggregator (bullfincher) and segment news.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1. Guyana stability and contract risk — Probability: High | Impact: High (crown-jewel asset).**
Guyana is now a top-tier value driver and Venezuela's 2023 referendum formally claimed the Essequibo region, overlaying the Stabroek block; the dispute simmers. Domestically, the controversial 2016 PSA (incl. stability clause and tax waivers) faces persistent domestic and ruling-party pressure for renegotiation (Guyana energy conference 2025), with the US embassy publicly warning against it — creating political uncertainty around a 20–30-year contract, though President Ali has said existing Exxon contract will not be renegotiated. Watch: Venezuelan GOPac/SECLIC actions under resumed oil diplomacy, Guyana–Venezuela arbitration of the 1899 boundary, and Guyana's implementing windfall-profit-tax ideas.

**2. Climate-deception and plastics litigation wave — Probability: High | Impact: Medium-High.**
State AGs (California Bonta, Sept 2024 re plastics-recycling deception; Connecticut; Massachusetts) plus municipal climate suits are converging. The Boulder, Colorado case against Exxon + Suncor is heading to the **US Supreme Court** — a ruling remanding to state court would unlock a wave of claims. Consumer-protection and RICO claims against the plastics business add a distinct product-liability-style exposure to the legacy nuisance suits.

**3. LNG geopolitics: Mozambique FID, PNG fiscal instability, Qatar/US project exposure — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium-High.**
Rovuma LNG sits in a country with an insurgency history in the north and weak fiscal institutions; PNG has a track record of renegotiating LNG terms (PNG LNG). Golden Pass pairs Exxon with QatarEnergy, tying part of US LNG growth to Gulf-state dynamics and to US DoE permit/policy swings. Any LNG demand shock or tariff/export-control shift (Europe, Asia) directly hits the growth pipeline.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Climate litigation (active):** State AG suits (CA, CT, MA); Boulder climate-deception case now at the US Supreme Court (cert granted, judgment under review); ongoing local-government damages suits around the country. Material, multi-year liability plus reputational drag.
- **SEC / federal climate rulemaking:** SEC climate-disclosure rule litigation paused (8th Cir.) under Acting Chair Uyeda (Feb 2025) — near-term disclosure burden easing, but California's climate statutes (SB 253/261) impose parallel reporting from 2026–27.
- **EPA methane fee (Waste Emissions Charge):** IRA methane fee regime ($900/t, rising) — Exxon's Permian and offshore volumes are material methane emitters; the fee is being litigated and targeted for CRA repeal — a swing factor in US regulatory cost.
- **EU windfall/solidarity tax:** Exxon units (e.g., Netherlands) challenged the EU/state-level solidarity contributions; ongoing, modest EBITDA impact, but sets a European fiscal-policy precedent for oil/gas majors.
- **Guyana fiscal tension:** Windfall-profit-tax proposals (2022) focal point of periodic re-ignition; stability clause is the shield — litigation/renegotiation risk if invoked.
- **Russia arbitration:** Post-2022 exit from Sakhalin-1; ~$4–5B expropriation arbitration vs Russia is ongoing — a recovery/reputational item, not current operational exposure.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **High-risk-license environments:** Kazakhstan (Samruk-Kazyna freeze litigation, rule-of-law concerns), Nigeria, Angola, Mozambique, PNG, Vietnam — classic FCPA/AML surface area around licensing, customs, and local-content obligations. No major recent FCPA settlement on record, but the footprint mandates strong third-party due diligence.
- **Greenwashing scrutiny:** State AG deception claims around plastics "advanced recycling" expand into consumer-protection and advertising-law territory — a compliance area beyond traditional hydrocarbon regulation.
- **Sanctions posture (clean, with caveats):** No Iran exposure; Russia operations exited (arbitration only); Venezuela holdings expropriated (settlement/arbitration only). Residual exposure is indirect: CPC pipeline transit via Russia-adjacent infrastructure and U.S. "secondary sanctions" vigilance on commodity trading desks.
- **Guyana small-state integrity:** US-embassy-publicized contract politics raise reputational and potential procurement-integrity review risk around the PSA.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Highly diversified and US-anchored (~42% home-market revenue, stable rule of law) argues against "Very High." But the *share of value* concentrated in Guyana (in a flashpoint region with live contract-renegotiation politics), a Supreme Court decision pending that could dramatically expand climate/plastics liability, pre-FID LNG projects in Mozambique/PNG, and Kazakhstan/Caspian transit exposure collectively push the rating to **High** — elevated versus US integrated peers mainly by the litigation trajectory and Guyana concentration. Monitoring triggers: SCOTUS Boulder ruling (2025–26 term), Guyana contract/windfall-tax developments, Rovuma FID, and EPA methane-fee litigation outcome.
