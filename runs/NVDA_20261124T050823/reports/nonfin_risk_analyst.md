# NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**As of:** November 24, 2026 | **Price:** $165.05 | **Market cap:** ~$3.98T | **Beta:** 2.25 | **Employees:** 42,000 | **Sector:** Technology / Semiconductors

> **Context that frames everything below:** NVDA fell **–47.5%** from its Oct 6, 2026 close of $239.24 to $125.53 on Nov 20, then rallied **+31.5% on Nov 23** (a record single-day gain of ~$952B in market cap) on news that founder-CEO Jensen Huang was *reinstated* — 11 days after he resigned amid a foreign-intelligence/export-control scandal, reportedly after White House intervention. This is not a normal risk profile.

Board had **initiated an independent investigation Nov 8** (committee of independent directors + outside counsel). |

A board reversing a national-security-linked termination in 11 days on political suasion is a **serious independence defect**, regardless of the merits. |

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — **SEVERE / MULTI-FRONT**

1. **SEC civil investigation into accounting practices** (reported Nov 20). Focus: **revenue recognition with a major customer**, and whether Nvidia **misled investors in its disclosures**. Critically, the SEC is examining a **2025 change to Nvidia's geographic revenue reporting methodology** — this matters because it coincides precisely with the alleged period of chip diversion to China.
2. **Securities class action** (multiple firms, incl. Rosen). Class period **Dec 19, 2024 – Nov 12, 2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline **Jan 8, 2027**. Core allegation: misleading statements about China/export-restricted sales and Megaspeed.
3. **DOJ / export-control exposure.** DOJ is investigating Megaspeed; 15 national-security and AI leaders have formally urged DOJ to investigate **Nvidia itself** for knowledge of illegal transfers. Commerce added **Megaspeed and six other Southeast Asian firms** to the trade blacklist (Nov 23). Reporting links Megaspeed to **~$12bn of chips moved Mar 2024–Jun 2025** despite controls; a separate **$160M smuggling network** was dismantled.
4. **Congressional oversight.** House Select Committee on China (Chairman Moolenaar) has written to the lead independent director; Huang has been summoned to testify; lobbyists expect **board members to be called "repeatedly."** Bipartisan.
5. **State AG action.** California AG Rob Bonta has opened an investigation into whether Nvidia/Huang's actions resulted in legal or national-security harm.
6. **Prior SEC history.** Nvidia previously settled SEC charges (crypto-mining revenue disclosure in Gaming) — a prior disclosure-integrity precedent, which raises the profile of the current probe.
7. **Mitigating actions.** Board formed a **National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee** (Nov 17) and Q3 FY2027 results were still published on schedule (Nov 17) — the reporting machine has not seized up.

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis — **NET SELLING, ZERO BUYING**

- **Aggregate:** press tallies put insider sales at **~$1.0–1.4bn** in the months before the scandal broke. **No open-market insider purchases were identified** at any point — including during a 47% drawdown. Insiders did not put personal capital behind the story.
- **Tench Coxe (director, historically ~2nd-largest shareholder):** sold/transferred roughly **$946M** in stock in the months preceding Huang's resignation. Press reporting asserted **no trading plan was flagged** for the large sale. **Important caveat:** other Form 4s show Coxe acting under a **Rule 10b5-1 plan adopted March 19, 2026**, so the "no plan" characterization is contested. This is a *flag for verification*, not a conclusion — but the timing (pre-scandal) plus the dispute over plan status warrants legal/forensic review.
- **Jensen Huang:** approximately **$1.35bn** of sales in 2026 under a pre-arranged plan; he retains ~70M shares directly.
- **Teter family trust:** sold 30,000 shares **on Nov 19, 2026** — mid-crisis, at $146–$157, *before* the reinstatement rally. Sequencing suggests no access to privileged non-public information (a poorly timed sale, not a well-timed one).
- **Filing cadence:** dense Form 4 flow through Nov 23; no post-reinstatement purchases disclosed.
- **Insider-trading risk is also a *subject* of litigation** — the derivative complaint expressly alleges insider trading, meaning insider data will be litigated, not merely observed.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Extreme customer concentration + circular financing.** Two unnamed customers accounted for **~39–40% of Q2 FY2026 revenue** (up from 14% and 11% a year earlier); three customers exceeded **40% of H1 revenue** — record concentration. Hyperscalers ≈**55% of data-center revenue**. Layered on top: **customer financing commitments reported near $200bn**, i.e., Nvidia increasingly financing buyers of its own product. CFO Colette Kress has been *reducing* this financing — prudent, but the de-risking itself signals the concern was real. A single large-customer deferral or a credit event cascades directly into revenue.
2. **Supply-chain single points of failure (Taiwan-concentrated).** Dependence on **TSMC** for leading-edge wafers, on **CoWoS advanced packaging** (Nvidia holds a large share of allocation; CoWoS supply still short of demand), and on **SK Hynix/Samsung HBM**. Geopolitical concentration in Taiwan sits inside a company now at the center of a U.S.–China intelligence dispute — an adverse policy event would hit both supply *and* demand simultaneously.
3. **Acute key-person risk plus espionage/IP exposure.** The company's strategy, customer relationships and (per sell-side framing) the entire U.S. AI leadership narrative are bound to one 63-year-old founder. The board has no disclosed successor. Separately, the CEO himself was the **target of a foreign intelligence effort** — so trade-secret and roadmap-compromise risk is not hypothetical, and the board states it is still investigating whether "Nvidia secrets were compromised."

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **SEVERE, AND STRUCTURALLY DIFFICULT TO REPAIR**

- **Partner trust is impaired, not just headlines.** Market commentary notes "still trust lost between Nvidia and its many partners." Customers and governments are now pricing Nvidia as a **sovereign-risk counterparty**, not just a vendor.
- **The purity of the reinstatement is the problem.** A founder returning in triumph, cheered at HQ, with the President claiming credit, resolves the *stock* question and worsens the *governance* question. Both parties in Congress want answers; the "too big to fail" framing (D.A. Davidson) is politically unstable — it invites condition-setting (export licensing, customer-of-record audits, board changes) as the price of support.
- **Trust deficit is now measurable in policy risk:** blacklists of its own channel partners, a state AG probe, and a live SEC accounting inquiry are all reputational events that convert into cash costs.
- **Contagion is already spreading** — Alibaba has been sued by an investor in the "Nvidia fallout," showing Nvidia-specific risk transmitting to peers/partners.
- **Social/political sentiment:** a namesake memecoin ripping 90% is a useful tells — public attention is speculative and unstable, not franchise-affirming.
- **Silver lining:** product roadmap commentary (Moor Insights, Deepwater) says technology leadership is intact; the demand side has not broken. Reputational damage is currently concentrated in *governance and trust*, not product quality.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **CRITICAL**

**Rationale.** Three independent, simultaneously-live enforcement threads (SEC accounting, DOJ/export-control, state AG) plus federal securities and Delaware derivative litigation, plus bipartisan congressional investigation — with the SEC specifically probing **the integrity of reported revenue** at a $4T company. Add a board that reversed its own CEO decision under apparent political pressure, a conflicted lead independent director, ~40% two-customer concentration, ~$200bn of customer financing, and unresolved espionage exposure around the CEO. The financial statements and the governance process are both under question at the same time; that combination is what earns Critical rather than High. This is **not a going-concern call** — operations and product leadership appear intact, and Q3 FY2027 results were delivered on schedule.

**What would move this to High:** SEC probe closed without restatement or charges; independent investigation concluded with disclosed findings and no confirmed secret compromise; credible CEO succession plan; independent chair installed; customer concentration stabilized.

**What would make it uninvestable:** any restatement or revenue-recognition adjustment; a DOJ action naming Nvidia; evidence that Huang's relationship was used to facilitate diversion; further board reversals that demonstrate the board cannot act independently.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **SEC examining revenue recognition with a major customer *and* a 2025 change to geographic revenue reporting.** A disclosure-methodology change in the same window as alleged China diversion is the single highest-severity item here — restatement risk is live. **Escalate to forensic accounting review immediately.**
2. **Board reversal under political pressure.** Directors were reportedly persuaded by the White House to reinstate a CEO they had terminated 11 days earlier over a national-security matter. This compromises board independence on *every* future decision, including the SEC probe, the derivative defense, and succession.
3. **Conflicted lead independent director.** Cooley chairman emeritus overseeing the governance committee while Cooley has been Nvidia's counsel since 1993 — the person running the CEO investigation has a structural conflict with the person under investigation.
4. **~$200bn of customer financing commitments alongside record customer concentration.** This is the classic revenue-quality combination that the SEC is already looking at (and that recalls the 2001-era vendor-financing playbook). Watch receivables, DSO, and any quarter where financing commitments rise faster than cash collections.
5. **~$946M director sale pre-scandal with contested 10b5-1 plan status.** Requires Rule 10b5-1 date verification against the disclosure timeline; if the plan adoption postdates material non-public knowledge of the Megaspeed issue, this is an enforcement matter.
6. **Zero insider open-market buying through a 47% drawdown.** Insiders — including a founder who just won a public vindication — did not buy a single disclosed share at trough prices. Treat as an absence of internal conviction signal.
7. **No disclosed CEO succession plan and unresolved espionage findings.** The board states it has "no reason to believe" secrets were compromised but its inquiry is not concluded. This must be closed out with disclosure before the risk can be re-rated.
