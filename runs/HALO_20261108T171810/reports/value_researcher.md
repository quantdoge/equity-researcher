## HALO — Halozyme Therapeutics | Value Analysis
*Price $103.63 (Nov 6, 2026) · Mkt cap ~$12.2B (≈118M sh) · Net debt ~$1.5B · EV ~$13.7B*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW** (widening near-term, structurally time-limited)

Halozyme is not a conventional biotech; it is a **royalty annuity on other companies' blockbusters**. Its ENHANZE platform (rHuPH20) converts IV biologics to subcutaneous form for Roche, J&J (Darzalex Faspro), argenx (Vyvgart Hytrulo), BMS (Opdivo Qvantig), Pfizer and others.

**Evidence of a real moat:**
- **Intangible assets:** patented enzyme + formulation IP embedded inside *approved* partner products. Barriers ~2032–2035.
- **Switching costs:** a partner cannot swap out rHuPH20 without reformulation, bridging studies and re-approval — a multi-year, high-cost redo of a marketed asset.
- **Economics that only a moated business produces:** 83.6% gross margin (FY25), 57.5% operating margin, capex 0.5% of revenue, **ROIC 36.6% vs WACC ~9%** — and rising every year (16.0% → 21.3% → 29.5% → 36.6%, 2022–2025).
- Multi-year pricing power: FY26 royalty guidance +42–45% *on top of* +37.6% total revenue in FY25. Royalties grew to $341M in Q3'26 (+44%).

**Why not Wide:** the moat is a **patent estate, not a brand or network**. It is single-technology dependent, partner-controlled commercially, and demonstrably contestable — Merck's own berahyaluronidase alfa (Keytruda SC) is an explicit attempt to design around it, which is why Halozyme is litigating rather than simply licensing. Concentration is severe: a handful of partner products (Darzalex Faspro above all) drive most royalty dollars, and those molecules face their own loss-of-exclusivity in the late 2020s/early 2030s. The moat is **widening today and narrowing by 2032** — an important distinction from a durable franchise.

**Circle of competence:** understandable — it is a toll-road on injectable biologics, not a binary clinical bet.

**Critical analytical point:** reported FY25 GAAP net income of $316.9M *fell* despite 37.6% revenue growth. Deductions from a perfect score: (a) leverage — $2.14B debt, only $49M book equity, $1.56B of goodwill/intangibles post-Elektrofi; (b) royalty revenue is inherently third-party-controlled; (c) GAAP earnings will stay muddy from amortization.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**FY2026 guidance (raised Nov 5):** revenue $1.860–1.925B (+33–38%), royalties $1.235–1.255B, **adj. EBITDA $1.24–1.29B (67% margin)**, non-GAAP diluted EPS $8.75–9.05.

- EV/adj. EBITDA (FY26E mid) ≈ **10.8x**
- P/non-GAAP EPS ≈ **11.6x**
- FCF yield ≈ **8%** (FY26E FCF ~$1.0B after cash interest and taxes)
- Peer median P/E 29.8x — but the 40.5x headline P/E is a GAAP artifact; comparison is not clean.

**Intrinsic value (own DCF — the built-in DCF tool returned a corrupted result, discounting at "850%", so I discarded it).** Base case: FY26 owner earnings ~$1.00B (SBC treated as a real expense), growth +15%/+12%/+10%/+6%/+4%/+2% through 2032, flat 2033, then –5%/yr as Darzalex/Roche SC royalties step down; discount 9%; terminal growth –1% to 0%.

**Intrinsic value: ~$117/share** (range **$105–135**; $133 requires terminal growth of +2%, i.e., successful perpetual pipeline replenishment; $105 assumes a harsher cliff).

**What the price implies:** at 10.8x EBITDA and an 8% FCF yield, the market is already pricing a meaningful post-2030 royalty decline. That is a *reasonable* expectation, not a fearful one. But the current **~12% discount to base-case value is not a 20–30% margin of safety** — and the terminal value is doing ~45% of the work, which is exactly where patent-cliff uncertainty lives.

**Sell-side** targets cluster $105–$130 (H.C. Wainwright/Stifel $130, Morgan Stanley $105, Wells Fargo $95) — consensus sits close to my base case.

### 4. Management Quality — **Strong (B+)**

- **Helen Torley (CEO since 2014)** executed one of biotech's cleanest business-model transformations: from cash-burning therapeutics developer to capital-light royalty collector with 57% operating margins. Capital allocation has been disciplined and shareholder-aligned.
- **Buyback record is genuinely value-accretive:** $1.19B repurchased 2022–25 at average prices far below today's $103; share count down 16%. This is the rare case where buybacks compensated for a decaying-per-share business risk.
- **M&A:** Elektrofi ($750M + $150M milestones) is a sensible adjacency — ultra-high-concentration formulation extends the platform beyond ENHANZE — but it is pre-revenue, unproven technology, funded partly with debt at a moment when the balance sheet was already levered. Watch this.
- **Litigation posture is aggressive and rational** (German injunction Dec 2025; Dutch court blocked Keytruda SC in eight countries, Oct 7, 2026) but it is management choosing a binary path over a negotiated license.
- *Gap:* I could not verify insider ownership or the exact convertible-note maturity ladder within budget — both should be confirmed in the DEF 14A before sizing a position.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (accumulate below ~$88)

This is a high-quality, capital-light, 36% ROIC royalty compounder trading at 11.6x non-GAAP earnings and an 8% FCF yield — statistically cheap. It is *not* a Buffett buy at $103.63, for two reasons:

1. **No margin of safety.** $103.63 vs. ~$117 intrinsic value is a 12% discount. My discipline requires 25%+ when the terminal value depends on a patent estate I cannot underwrite to 2040.
2. **The intellectual honesty problem:** the durability of the earnings stream is set by courts and by partners' molecule lifecycles, not by the company. Moat duration is the single variable that matters, and it is the one variable I cannot reliably value.

**Action:** Hold existing positions. **Add aggressively below $88** (25% discount to base IV; ~13.5x FY26 EPS with management still repurchasing stock). Trim above $145 (25%+ premium, ~16x EBITDA) absent a Merck resolution. If Merck takes an ENHANZE/MDASE license for subcutaneous Keytruda — a $30B+ franchise — base-case value moves materially higher and the verdict becomes Buy; that optionality is currently free in the price.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Merck litigation is binary.** Halozyme won European injunctions, but Merck is contesting validity (US PGR2025-00004; EP 2 797 622). A US loss could invalidate the MDASE claims central to licensing leverage and unwind the European wins.
2. **Darzalex Faspro concentration / royalty cliff.** J&J's SC Darzalex is the largest single royalty source; its exclusivity erosion in the late 2020s/early 2030s is the core reason the stock trades at 11x. A steeper-than-modeled step-down is the fastest route to a permanent capital loss.
3. **Design-around risk** from alternative hyaluronidases and from partners simply abandoning SC switch programs — royalty revenue can vanish without warning and without recourse.
4. **Leverage + acquisition risk.** ~$2.0B gross debt against $49M book equity, expanded to fund a pre-revenue Elektrofi deal whose technology (ultra-high concentration) has yet to produce a commercial product.
5. **IRA / reimbursement policy.** Management projects "zero to minimal" royalty impact through at least 2035, but Medicare negotiation reshaping the economics of partner products (and the IV-vs-SC site-of-care differential that drives SC adoption) is a live variable.
6. **Buyback at higher prices.** The repurchase program that created value at $50 could destroy it at $125; watch the average price paid in FY26 filings.
