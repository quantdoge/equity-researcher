## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Late-cycle / transitional, tilting risk-off at the margin.**

The US economy is in an unusual re-tightening regime. Real GDP accelerated to **+3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026** (Q2 24,408 → Q3 24,611 chained $bn, +2.1% YoY), unemployment sits at a full-employment **4.1%**, yet inflation has *re-accelerated*: CPI rose from **+3.30% YoY in July to +3.72% in September 2026** (index 332.813 → 336.298), with the last three months running at a **~4.5% annualised** pace. The FOMC responded by **hiking 25bp on 16 Sept 2026 to 3.75–4.00%** — reversing part of its 2026 easing (effective Fed funds bottomed at 3.63% in Jun–Aug, now 3.88%) — then **paused in October**, with policymakers reportedly "spooked" by 10Y yields near quarter-century highs (Reuters, 26 Oct 2026).

The bond market is the binding constraint. The **10Y touched 5.31% on 5 Oct** before easing to 4.84% on 5 Nov; the **30Y sits at 5.47%** and the **2s10s is +39bp** — a bear-steepening driven by term premium and fiscal supply, not by growth optimism. Real 10Y yields (TIPS) at **2.60%**, down from 2.95% in early October, imply a **~2.24% breakeven**. Meanwhile **HY OAS has widened ~83bp since 1 Sept (2.65% → 3.48%)** even as equities made highs — credit is diverging from equity, a classic late-cycle warning. The **broad USD index has firmed 2.9% since 11 Sept (118.21 → 121.67)**, a direct translation headwind for dollar reporters with ex-US revenue.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact — HALO

Halozyme is a **royalty machine**, not a conventional biotech: FY26 guidance of **$1.860–1.925bn revenue (+33–38%), $1.235–1.255bn royalties (+42–45%), $1.240–1.290bn adj. EBITDA (66–67% margin) and $8.75–9.05 non-GAAP EPS (+111–118%)**, generated by 423 employees off partners' (Roche, J&J, BMS, Lilly, argenx, Takeda, Pfizer) subcutaneous drug sales. That structure dictates its macro exposures.

| Macro variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| GDP / demand (3.4%) | **Positive** | Partner oncology/immunology volumes are largely non-cyclical, but strong GDP supports launch ramp (e.g. RYBREVANT FASPRO HNSCC approval, 6 Nov) and commercial expansion. |
| CPI (3.72%) | **Neutral to slightly positive** | Royalty is a percentage of partners' *nominal* net sales, so partner price increases flow into HALO's revenue; its own cost base is wage-only (423 FTEs). HALO is one of the few healthcare names with near-zero input-cost inflation exposure. |
| Fed funds (3.88%, re-hiking) | **Mildly negative** | No direct cash-flow effect (see debt below), but each hike compresses small/mid-cap biotech multiples and raises the opportunity cost of holding a royalty name. |
| Long-end yields (10Y 4.8–5.1%; 30Y 5.47%) | **Negative** | This is the single largest macro transmission channel. HALO's royalty stream is a ~7–8-year-duration asset; **every +100bp on the discount rate is worth roughly −7% to −8% of NPV**, and a structurally 5-handle long bond caps the multiple the market will pay for patent-life cash flows. The ~11.6x FY26E non-GAAP EPS ($103.63 vs $8.90 midpoint) is partly a discount-rate artefact. |
| Real yields (2.60%) | **Negative, but easing** | Real yields are the correct hurdle for royalty streams; the 35bp October retreat is the one supportive rates datapoint. |
| USD (+2.9% since Sept) | **Negative** | Royalties are booked on ex-US partner sales (Roche/Swiss, argenx/Belgian, Takeda/Japanese), plausibly 30–40% of the base, plus operations in Switzerland, Ireland, Belgium and Japan. A 3% USD move is roughly a 1% translation drag on royalties, with no natural hedge (royalty receipts lag sales by a quarter). |
| Credit spreads (3.48%, widening) | **Neutral for solvency / negative for sentiment** | HALO needs no external capital. Debt is **$1.5bn of 1.5% convertible senior notes due 2033** (priced Nov-2025, upsized from $1.3bn), with proceeds used to retire $151.7m of 0.25% 2027s and $220.0m of 1.00% 2028s, plus capped calls. Effectively no refinancing risk this decade and negligible coupon cost. The widening spread matters only as a risk-appetite signal. |
| Tariffs (Section 232 pharma) | **Neutral to small negative** | Royalties are levied on partners' net sales, not on HALO's imports; partner landed-cost pressure and any volume/pricing response is a second-order risk. HALO's own product sales (Hylenex) are small. |
| Drug-pricing policy (IRA/OBBBA) | **Positive** | The 6 Nov 2026 CMS final rule for IPAY 2029 confirms HALO's guidance of **zero-to-minimal royalty impact through at least 2035** — removing the single largest policy overhang on the equity. |

**Context on the tape:** HALO ran from **$62 (30 Mar 2026) to $130.91 (30 Oct)** before falling **21% in five sessions to $103.63**, including −15.8% on 6 Nov despite a Q3 beat (revenue $493m, +39%; royalties $341m, +44%; EPS $2.39 vs $1.72) and a guidance raise. Notably, the September–October melt-up happened *while* 10Y yields rose 4.75% → 5.31%, so **rates have not been the marginal driver of HALO's price this year** — idiosyncratic factors (royalty momentum, the Merck patent dispute flagged on the Q3 call) dominate. That cuts both ways: macro is a slow multiple tax, not a trigger.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL**

HALO is close to macro-insulated on fundamentals: no cost-inflation exposure, no funding need, debt fixed at 1.5% to 2033, non-cyclical end demand, and a policy overhang (IRA) now formally cleared. Offsetting this, every price channel points the wrong way for a long-duration royalty asset — a 5%-handle long bond, 2.6% real yields, a firming dollar and widening credit spreads. The company will likely beat nominal GDP by ~15x regardless of the cycle; the macro question is purely what multiple the market grants that stream, and today's answer is a discount rate that does not help.

## 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **Bear: "Term-premium shock meets momentum unwind"**

If October's hot CPI print (last 3m ~4.5% annualised) persists, the Fed's October pause proves temporary and it hikes again in December, **the 30Y breaks 6% and the 10Y retests 5.75%**, with HY OAS at 500bp+ (from 3.48%). Small- and mid-cap biotech de-rates indiscriminately. HALO — a 2x YTD winner with a live Merck patent-dispute headline and ~1.2x the daily volatility of a large-cap — gets marked at **8x FY26E EPS ≈ $70–75, another ~30% down**, despite 42–45% royalty growth and a 66% EBITDA margin. The trigger is not HALO's cash flows; it is the price of duration.

The symmetric bull case is a fast disinflation (CPI back to ~2.5% by Q1-27), a Fed pivot, 10Y to 4.0% and a 5% weaker dollar: royalty translation and multiple both improve, supporting **15–16x $9 ≈ $140–145**.
