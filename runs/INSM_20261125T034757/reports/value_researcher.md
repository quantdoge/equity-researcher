# Insmed Incorporated (INSM) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $121.16 (Nov 13, 2026) | Market cap ≈ $26.5B | EV ≈ $26.1B | 52-wk range: $92–$212**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW (and time-limited)**

Insmed is a commercial-stage respiratory biopharma with two marketed products and a deep pipeline:

| Asset | 2026 Status | Moat Source |
|---|---|---|
| **BRINSUPRI** (brensocatib) | Launched mid-2025; Q3'26 sales $400M (+29% QoQ); FY26 guide raised to $1.35–1.40B | **First-and-only** DPP1 inhibitor approved for non-CF bronchiectasis (US, EU, Japan). NCE + orphan exclusivity, method/composition patents into the late 2030s |
| **ARIKAYCE** | $124M in Q3 (+9% YoY), 9th year on market; FY26 guide $460–470M | Orphan drug exclusivity + limited generic threat; maturity/decline ahead |
| **TPIP** (treprostinil palmitil) | 4 Phase 3 programs (PH-ILD, PAH, PPF, IPF) — first data not before ~2028 | None yet — pure optionality |
| Early pipeline | INS1148/IPF, INS1033 (RA), INS1201 (DMD), INS1202 (ALS) | Pre-proof |

**Evidence the moat is real (but not durable in the Buffett sense):**
- Gross margin **84%** TTM ($1.28B on $1.52B) — classic specialty-pharma economics.
- Brinsupri's ramp is exceptional: $28M (Q3'25) → $400M (Q3'26), ~14x in four quarters. That is genuine unmet need (no approved therapy existed), not marketing spend alone.
- Japan approval/launch (Oct 2026) extends exclusivity geography.

**Why it is not wide:** this moat is a **patent/exclusivity lease, not a compounding franchise**. Pricing power expires on a known date (brensocatib US LOE ~2037–38; Arikayce sooner). There are no network effects, negligible switching costs, and DPP1 is a crowded mechanistic space — competitors are in the clinic. Buffett's test — "will this business earn high returns on capital in 15 years with high confidence?" — cannot be answered yes here. This sits **at the outer edge of my circle of competence**: I can underwrite the commercial execution, but the value rests on binary clinical readouts.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Multiples:** EV/FY26E revenue (guide midpoint ~$1.84B) ≈ **14.2x**; EV/TTM revenue ≈ **17.2x**; no P/E, no EV/EBITDA, no FCF yield (all negative). For context, a 14x forward-sales multiple on a launch-stage asset is a **growth premium**, not a value multiple.

**Intrinsic value — scenario-weighted (my own DCF, 9% discount, terminal multiples applied to post-peak earnings, shares 222M):**

| Scenario | Prob. | Peak revenue | Net margin | Terminal value | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear**: Brinsupri peaks ~$3B (competition/pricing), Arikayce erodes, TPIP fails | 40% | $3.2B (2032) | 30% | ~$13B @ 14x | **~$36** |
| **Base**: Brinsupri ~$5.5B, Arikayce ~$0.6B, TPIP ~$1.5B | 45% | $7.6B (2033) | 33% | ~$40B @ 16x | **~$100** |
| **Bull**: management's "$14B combined peak sales" achieved; TPIP wins all four indications | 15% | $14B (2034) | 35% | ~$88B @ 18x | **~$200** |

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $95–105/share.** Current price **$121 = a 15–25% premium to intrinsic value**, not a discount.

**Implied expectations:** at $121, the market is pricing roughly a $7–8B peak-sales franchise with a successful TPIP. That is plausible — but it is *already* the base case, and I am being asked to pay full price for it while absorbing: (a) four Phase 3 programs that could fail, (b) a patent cliff in the late 2030s that caps terminal multiple, and (c) continued dilution. **I require a 25–30% discount to intrinsic value; that means ≤$80–85.**

Gap noted: sell-side consensus targets cluster at **$154–$213** (Guggenheim $201, Morgan Stanley $213, Stifel $195) against a price of ~$112–121 — a ~60% implied upside I believe is built on bull-case peak sales and no terminal-cliff haircut. I do not adopt it.

## 4. Management Quality: **Good (8/10 on execution, 5/10 on owner alignment)**

- **Will Lewis (Chair/CEO)** has executed one of biopharma's better capital-allocation runs: took the stock from ~$20 (2023) to a $212 peak, got brensocatib approved and launched at record pace, and built a genuinely differentiated pipeline. Board additions of **Paul Hudson (ex-Sanofi CEO)** and Utpal Koppikar signal seriousness about late-stage governance and M&A readiness.
- **Capital allocation discipline is the weak link for a value investor:** four years of uninterrupted equity issuance (+78% share count), zero buybacks (correctly, at this stage), no dividend. Growth is being bought with shareholders' equity. To management's credit, no empire-building M&A has been done — the pipeline is largely internal.
- **Sanofi takeover speculation** (reported chatter, unconfirmed) provides optionality but is not a thesis.
- Insider ownership data was unavailable in this data set — a gap I flag rather than assume.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD — Do Not Initiate at $121**

**Reasoning:** This is a *good business at a full price*, which is precisely the situation my framework tells me to refuse. The franchise quality is genuine (84% gross margins, first-in-class asset, large unmet need, near-breakeven cash burn), and the operating trajectory is excellent. But:

1. **No margin of safety.** Price is ~15–25% *above* my probability-weighted intrinsic value.
2. **The moat has an expiry date**, so a terminal-value haircut is mandatory — and the market isn't applying one.
3. **Value depends on clinical binaries** (TPIP × 4 indications) outside what I can underwrite with confidence.
4. **78% dilution in four years** means per-share value creation meaningfully lags enterprise value creation.

**Action:** Existing holders can reasonably hold — the trajectory toward 2027 profitability/FCF breakeven is credible and the bull case is not fantasy. **New money should wait for ≤$85** (~30% discount to base-case IV), which would also roughly equal my probability-weighted IV with a proper margin of safety. A confirmed TPIP Phase 3 win would justify raising my base case and therefore my entry price.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Clinical failure risk (largest):** TPIP in four Phase 3 programs (PH-ILD enrollment not complete until 1H2028) and early-stage assets. A miss removes a large slice of the bull case; the stock already fell **–24% in a single day (May 7, 2026)** on a merely *good-not-spectacular* Brinsupri quarter — the market's tolerance for disappointment is near zero.
2. **Launch deceleration / elevated buy-side bar:** Brinsupri grew 29% QoQ in Q3'26 vs. 49% in Q2'26. Any further decay in sequential growth will compress a 14x forward-sales multiple hard.
3. **Competitive entry in bronchiectasis** (other DPP1 inhibitors, anti-inflammatories) and payer pressure as the $/patient is high; NCFB is a large population, inviting pricing scrutiny.
4. **Patent cliff** — brensocatib US exclusivity ends ~2037–38; Arikayce earlier. The market may begin discounting this well before it arrives.
5. **Continued dilution / financing:** net cash of $405M against a 2026 YTD burn of ~$363M leaves little cushion if burn re-accelerates; equity issuance is the likely funding tool, further eroding per-share value.
6. **Single-franchise concentration:** ~74% of revenue is one molecule launched 18 months ago.
7. **Data gaps in this analysis:** insider ownership/Form 4 activity, detailed R&D expense line, and convertible-debt terms were unavailable; peer multiples were not meaningful (FMP's "sector" returns $200B+ diversified pharma, not commercial-stage biotech comparables).

---

**Bottom line:** Insmed is a high-quality, well-managed growth franchise masquerading as a value opportunity. Excellent business, excellent execution — **wrong price for a patient, fundamentals-driven buyer.** Hold, do not add; revisit below $85 or on de-risked TPIP data.
