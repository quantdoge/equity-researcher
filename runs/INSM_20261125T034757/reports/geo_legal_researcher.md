# INSM — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
**Insmed Incorporated (Nasdaq: INSM)** | Healthcare / Biotechnology | HQ Bridgewater, NJ | ~1,664 employees | Mkt cap ~$37.3B (pre-deal price $170.15)

> **Critical context:** On **Nov 24, 2026**, Sanofi (SNY) agreed to acquire Insmed for **$175.00/share in cash, ~$41.0B / €37.6B equity value**, expected to close **H1 2027**, subject to Insmed shareholder approval plus **U.S. and certain non-U.S. regulatory approvals** (Sanofi press release, 24-Nov-2026; PR Newswire/GlobeNewswire 24-Nov-2026). Insmed is therefore now a **deal-completion story**, and the dominant "legal/regulatory risk" is merger clearance and shareholder litigation — not operating jurisdiction risk.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1. US drug-pricing and trade policy (MFN pricing + Section 232 tariffs) — Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High**
- Section 232 pharma tariffs were proclaimed **2-Apr-2026** and took effect **31-Jul-2026** for Annex III companies, **29-Sep-2026** for all others: **100% duties** on patented pharmaceuticals/ingredients, reduced to **20% for companies with approved onshoring plans**, and **0% for UK-origin** goods (White House proclamation; EY Tax News 2026-0786; Mallory Group/Crane trade advisories). Insmed's exposure depends on import content of BRINSUPRI/ARIKAYCE (unverified).
- Pricing: the sector-wide **Most-Favored-Nation** push is already cited as a reason launches in **Europe and Japan** may be delayed (industry commentary, Neil Grubert), and IRA Medicare negotiation continues to reset US net prices. ~87% US revenue concentration makes Insmed structurally exposed to any US price/reimbursement reform.

**2. Merger completion / antitrust clearance — Probability of closing high (~85–90%) / Impact: Very High (price-defining)**
- Required: Insmed shareholder vote, **HSR/US antitrust**, and **non-US approvals** (EU, and likely Japan/UK filings given overlapping respiratory franchises) (Sanofi PR; RTT News). Overlap between Sanofi's respiratory/immunology portfolio and brensocatib/ARIKAYCE is **limited** — no direct NCFB competitor — so a Phase 2 EU review is possible but a prohibition is unlikely. Timing risk (H1 2027 target) and any second-request-driven slip matter for arbitrage spread.
- **Foreign-investment/CFIUS angle:** not identified as a condition; French acquirer of a US biotech with novel gene-therapy manufacturing could theoretically attract scrutiny, but no filing or condition disclosed (**gap**).

**3. Ex-US market access deterioration (UK NICE, EU HTA, Japan pricing) — Probability: High / Impact: Low-Medium near term**
- **NICE terminated/paused the Brinsupri appraisal** in Sept-2026 ahead of a QALY-threshold change — the UK, one of the earliest ex-US approvals (MHRA 20-Feb-2026), may deliver little near-term revenue.
- EU launch economics hinge on AMNOG/national HTA and EU HTA JCA; Japan reimbursement will be repriced. This caps the ex-US leg of Sanofi's "global reach" thesis but is <10% of current revenue.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **None of the classic FCPA/sanctions/AML type surfaced.** No Russia, China or other sanctioned-market revenue or distribution identified; Insmed's international commercial footprint is limited to Japan and Europe, all low-corruption-risk jurisdictions.
- **Watch items:** (a) **launch-era promotional compliance** — a first-in-class NCFB launch with heavy spend invites FDA OPDP/DOJ scrutiny of promotion and of patient-support/PAP programs (Anti-Kickback Statute); (b) **GDPR/UK GDPR + HIPAA** obligations across EU/Japan patient programs; (c) **Section 232 tariff compliance** — any onshoring commitment used to obtain the 20% (vs 100%) rate creates auditable supply-chain/tariff-classification obligations for the combined Sanofi entity; (d) **merger-period conduct** — deal-protection covenants restrict Insmed from new partnering/pricing moves, and any pre-close deviation is a litigation hook.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Insmed has **no material exposure to geopolitical hotspots** (Russia, China, Middle East, EM currency controls) and derives ~90% of revenue from the US, Japan and Western Europe — all strong rule-of-law jurisdictions. The risk profile is instead **concentrated in three policy binaries**: (i) US pricing/tariff policy applied to a ~87%-US revenue base; (ii) successful completion of a $41B cross-border merger with US, EU and other clearances required by H1 2027; and (iii) FDA/PMDA label-expansion decisions (PDUFA 28-Jan-2027). Legal exposure is currently **ordinary-course**: merger-objection investigations under Section 14(a)/fiduciary duty, no product-liability, antitrust or FCPA matters identified.

**Key gaps to close before relying on this:** audited geographic revenue note from the FY2025 10-K (SEC accession 0001104506-26-000009) and Q3-26 10-Q (0001104506-26-000047); the merger agreement's termination fee/go-shop terms; and the manufacturing-site/import profile underpinning Section 232 tariff exposure.
