## Forensic Short-Seller Analysis — INSM (Insmed Incorporated)
*Data as of the Nov 24, 2026 close ($170.15); latest filings Q3 2026 (10-Q filed 2026‑10‑29) and FY2025 10‑K (filed 2026‑02‑19).*

**Bottom line up front:** the fundamentals here are *not* fraudulent and the accounting is *not* dirty — but the valuation has been extreme for two years, and that valuation has now been taken out of the market's hands by a signed all-cash merger. At $170.15 against a $175 cash offer, there is **no short edge left**. Below is the full forensic work-up, including what would have been a legitimate bear case two weeks ago.

---

### 1. Earnings Quality Assessment — **MEDIUM (no fraud evidence)**

| Test | Result | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Accruals ratio (NI–OCF)/assets | **−0.151** (FY25); −0.114 FY24; −0.160 FY23 | Negative accruals = cash *outflows* exceed accounting losses. Conservative, not aggressive |
| Cash conversion (OCF/NI) | 0.73 | <1 only because loss-making + SBC; accruals direction is favourable |
| TATA component of Beneish | −0.151 | Strongly *anti*-manipulation |
| Piotroski F | 5/9 (fails: negative ROA, negative OCF, ROA deterioration, dilution) | Weak *profitability*, not weak *quality* |
| Gross margin | 79.4% FY25 → **85.1% Q3'26** (76.4% FY24) | Expanding; no pricing-power erosion |

Verdict: earnings are low-quality only in the trivial sense that there are no earnings yet. Reported losses understate cash burn, not the reverse. **No evidence of revenue-recognition abuse.**

### 2. Key Red Flags Found (and how each resolves)

1. **Receivables growth 170.8% vs revenue growth 66.7% (FY25)** — the classic channel-stuffing template. **But it resolves on DSO:** AR/revenue-days = 45 days (Q4'24), 49 days (Q4'25), **45 days (Q3'26)**. AR is tracking the launch ramp, not front-running it. Flag downgraded to noise.
2. **Inventory days 386 (FY25)** — high in absolute terms, but falling (463 → 419 → 386 → **~191 at Q3'26** on annualised COGS). Launch build, not demand failure.
3. **Serial dilution:** shares 112.1M (2021) → 219.75M (Nov 2026), **+96% in five years**. SBC was $153M in FY25 = **25% of revenue** ($128M / 9M26, improving to ~9% of revenue). This is the real quiet value leak — GAAP losses understate the economic cost of the equity-comp machine.
4. **Cost of capital:** ~$20M interest expense per quarter on ~$741M debt ≈ **~11% effective** — the price paid for non-dilutive capital. Accumulated deficit **−$5.91B**; book equity only $726M.
5. **Distress metrics:** Altman Z = **−3.96 ("distress")** — but this is a *book-equity artifact*: substituting the $37.4B market cap for book equity yields Z ≈ +27. **I do not endorse a solvency thesis**; cash of $1.15B at 9/30/26 vs ~$120M/qtr burn gives ~2+ years of runway.
6. **Pipeline breadth failing:** brensocatib failed Phase 3 in CRSsNP (discontinued, Dec 17–18 2025; stock −16–19%) and Phase II in hidradenitis suppurativa (CEDAR). Two indication expansions torpedoed in twelve months — the mechanism is narrower than the 2024–25 narrative implied.
7. **Reimbursement economics — the single most important red flag:** list price **$88,000/yr**, strict prior authorisation, and **ICER's final report concludes the launch price is "substantially higher than a cost-effective price"**, estimating a cost-effective price of roughly **$3,100–$3,700/yr** (net price modelled at $61,600). A 24–28x gap between price and health-economic value is the franchise's structural vulnerability, not a footnote.

### 3. Beneish M-Score and Accruals Analysis

- **M-Score −1.96 → "moderate risk" band** (−2.22 to −1.78).
- Decomposition matters more than the headline: the flag is driven by **SGI 1.667** (sales growth index) and **DSRI 1.624** (receivables index) — both mechanically inflate in any hyper-growth product launch. Offsetting: **GMI 0.962** (margins improving), **AQI 1.028**, **LVGI 0.784** (leverage falling), **TATA −0.151** (accruals strongly conservative).
- **Conclusion: no manipulation signal.** A pre-profit launch company scoring −1.96 with negative total accruals is a false positive, and I will not build a short on it.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment — **the genuine bear case**

- Concentration: **Brinsupri = $400.1M of Q3'26's $524.4M (76%)**. ARIKAYCE is only +9% YoY ($124.4M) and is a toxicity-constrained, historically marginal-evidence product.
- Efficacy is modest (~19% exacerbation reduction in PATINA) with no lung-function benefit; plausible cheap substitutes (azithromycin, hypertonic saline) remain standard of care for mild patients. Payers can and will step-edit.
- First-mover DPP‑1 advantage is real but not durable on its own: the class is druggable by others, EU/Japan launches (Japan approved Aug 2026) invite reference pricing, and the two failed indications show the label won't widen easily.
- This is a franchise that can plausibly do $3–4B in 2028 (consensus) *if* access holds — but every dollar is price-and-access dependent, not efficacy dependent.

### 5. Management Red Flags — **minor, not disqualifying**

- No auditor change, no restatement, no related-party scheme identified in the filings I reviewed.
- **Governance concern:** 25 Form 4s in roughly six months with no open-market purchases surfaced in the metadata (transaction detail unavailable to me — data gap, stated honestly), alongside ~96% share-count growth and SBC at 25% of revenue. Insider activity reads as compensation monetisation, not conviction.
- The Dec 2025 "clinical and business update" quietly discontinued a flagship-adjacent programme while the narrative remained expansionary — standard biotech optimism, worth noting, not indictable.
- **Data gaps:** short interest, borrow cost, and detailed Form 4 buy/sell codes were not available; peer multiple table was uninformative (FMP returns large-cap pharma — LLY, ABBV, MRK — not single-asset launch comps).

### 6. Short Thesis Summary — **credible two weeks ago, dead today**

The standalone bear case was coherent and, briefly, very profitable: **$211.41 (Dec 1, 2025) → $98.60 (Jul 31, 2026), −53%**, as the CRSsNP failure, HS failure and payor friction compressed the multiple. At the peak the stock was ~21x trailing sales and ~55x *2028* consensus EPS ($3.20) — priced for flawless multi-indication execution by a company with a $5.9B accumulated deficit and a $88k price that ICER says should be ~$3.5k.

**Then the tape changed the game:**
- **Nov 23, 2026: Sanofi signs a definitive merger at $175.00/share cash — $41.0B equity (~$41.3B EV), ~22x 2026E revenue (~$1.81–1.87B guided), ~10x 2028E revenue, ~19x 2030E earnings.** Not a bubble price for a strategic buying a Dupixent successor.
- Structure risk is procedural, not financial: **>2/3 of *all* 219.75M shares outstanding must vote yes, and unvoted shares count as NO.** Expected close H1 2027. No financing condition.
- Spread: **$4.85 (2.8%) ≈ 4.8% annualised vs a 4.49% 26-week T-bill** — i.e., you are paid ~30bps over the risk-free rate to underwrite vote-turnout and antitrust risk. That is the entire remaining "trade."

**The other side, stated plainly (my honest read):** the fundamentals beat the bears. Brinsupri did $400M in a single quarter five quarters post-launch, guidance was *raised* to $1.35–1.40B, gross margin is 85%, inventory and DSO are clean, accruals are conservative, operating cash burn has narrowed to ~$36M in Q3'26, and revenue is still accelerating (Q3 +268% YoY). Reported numbers reflect a real commercial ramp, not accounting fiction.

### 7. Contrarian Rating: **NO EDGE — REJECT SHORT THESIS**

**Do not short INSM at $170.15.**
- Maximum gain on completion is **2.83%**, realised in ~7 months, while the borrow and the equity's beta to a break cost you more than a T-bill. Negative carry against a cash deal is a bad use of short capital.
- The break scenario is the *only* payoff, and my standalone valuation on a break is roughly **$100–125/share** (10–13x ~$2.6–2.9B 2027E revenue) — attractive downside, but it requires a *specific*, monitorable catalyst I do not currently see: no financing condition, no obvious antitrust overlap, and a large-cap acquirer with strategic urgency.
- **The one thing worth watching (the only actionable bear idea):** the **2/3-of-outstanding, unvoted-equals-no** threshold. Passive/index holders (Vanguard, BlackRock-scale) plus an arb-heavy register create genuine turnout risk. If the DEFM 14A shows a low turnout base or a disclosed large holder dissenting, the break probability is mispriced relative to a $4.85 spread — *that* would be a legitimate, catalyst-driven short (or a put structure), not a fundamental one.

**Asset quality vs. price:** high-quality asset, extreme historical valuation, clean accounting, and a now-capped share price. The forensic work says *"no fraud, no short"* — the trade here is merger arb, and a thin one at that.
