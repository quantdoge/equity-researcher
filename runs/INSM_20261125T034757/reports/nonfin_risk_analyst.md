## NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — INSMED INCORPORATED (INSM)

**Critical context that re-frames this analysis:** Insmed signed a definitive merger agreement with Sanofi on **23 Nov 2026**, announced **24 Nov 2026**, at **$175.00/share cash (~$41.0B equity value)**. The stock closed at **$170.15** on 24 Nov. Non-financial risk for INSM shareholders is therefore no longer primarily "will the commercial franchise execute" — it is **deal-completion risk** plus **conduct/disclosure risk around the sale process**. Data note: no 10-K like-for-like risk-factor section or DEF 14A was retrievable through my filing tools (FY26 10-K not yet filed; SEC direct fetch returned 403), so governance findings draw on the company's 2026 proxy (via secondary sources), the 8-K merger terms, and trade press.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE — trending Weak** (material conflict flag)

**Positives**
- All board committee members are independent; a **Lead Independent Director (David R. Brennan)** with "robust and defined responsibilities" has existed since Nov 2018 — a genuine counterweight to CEO/Chair duality.
- Two substantive independent additions on **20 Oct 2026**: Paul Hudson and Utpal Koppikar (CFO of Verily, biopharma finance background) — relevant expertise, not a token refresh.
- No related-party transaction disclosures surfaced; no SEC enforcement history.

**Negatives / flags**
- **CEO/Chairman duality**: William H. Lewis has been Chair and CEO since Nov 2018 — concentration of power at exactly the moment a $41B sale was negotiated.
- ⚠️ **The Paul Hudson conflict**: Hudson is the **former CEO of Sanofi** and was appointed an *independent* director of Insmed on **20 Oct 2026 — roughly five weeks before Insmed signed away to Sanofi**. Even with recusal, this compromises the "arm's-length board" narrative, invites 14(a) disclosure scrutiny, and will be the centrepiece of plaintiffs' "process was designed to deliver this buyer" theory. His independence designation should be tested, not assumed.
- **$1.125B termination fee** (~2.7% of equity value) — within market norms, not egregious, but it is a meaningful deterrent to a topping bid given the >2/3-of-outstanding vote hurdle makes rival entries harder still. The **reverse termination fee payable by Sanofi is undisclosed in my data** — a genuine gap that matters for antitrust break-risk allocation.
- **One-directional insider selling**, zero open-market purchases (detail in §3).

- **Nicole Schaeffer Orlov (Chief People Strategy Officer): 101,100 sh @ $145.26 = $14.69M** (19 Nov) — **executed during live press speculation** (Bloomberg report 18 Nov; stock +18.4% on 19 Nov) and **three business days before signing**. ### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Commercial concentration and launch-execution strain.** Q3'26 revenue $524.4M (BRINSUPRI $400.1M, +29% QoQ; ARIKAYCE $124.4M, +9% YoY), with FY26 guidance raised to $1.35–1.4B and $460–470M respectively — an extraordinary ramp (~$1.9B total FY26, ~1,664 employees) that stresses manufacturing scale-up, CMO/supplier concentration, and gross-to-net/payer contracting. Complex inhaled-liposomal and tablet supply chains are single-point-of-failure prone.
- **Key-person concentration at the worst possible moment.** Lewis is CEO *and* Chair and is the public architect of the franchise; with 1,664 employees facing a 6–9 month pre-close limbo, **retention of the CEO, CMO, CFO and commercial leadership through H1 2027** is the top operational dependency for both the franchise and the deal.
- **Clinical/regulatory binary inside the window.** ARIKAYCE's full-approval conversion (PDUFA 28 Jan 2027) is a live binary, and the four-arm **PALM** Phase 3 programme (PAH/ILD/PPF/IPF — Insmed's largest parallel effort) carries pipeline risk. A prior brensocatib hidradenitis-suppurativa trial failure already demonstrated pipeline attrition. No cybersecurity incident or business-continuity failure is disclosed.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Moderate**

- **Drug-pricing/political exposure**: rare-disease and respiratory franchises at premium price points are squarely in the IRA/payer- scrutiny zone; ARIKAYCE carries serious boxed safety warnings (ototoxicity, nephrotoxicity, embryofetal toxicity) that make any safety signal a headline risk.
- **Deal optics**: a 46% premium to the undisturbed price (18 Nov close $119.80) yet, per trade press, still **~17% below the all-time high** — that gap is precisely the grievance animating the plaintiffs' bar and any "sell too cheap / sold to a conflicted buyer" campaign. Expect a noisy proxy contest-adjacent news cycle.
- **Litigation-driven headline risk** into the shareholder vote: three or more firms issuing press releases by name on day one is a reputational tax even when meritless.
- No product recalls, ESG controversies, or consumer-backlash episodes identified; employee/culture brand ("people-first") is currently intact.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

The company is **not** a governance or fraud story — no enforcement history, no restatements, credible lead-independent structure, and a cash deal with committed financing. What keeps it out of "Low" is a cluster of process-and-conflict issues (former Sanofi CEO on the board during the sale; $14.7M officer sale amid rumours; CEO/Chair duality; >2/3-of-outstanding vote) sitting on top of a genuine thin-margin arb:

- **Spread math**: $170.15 → $175 = **$4.85, or 2.8%**, ≈ **4.8% annualised to 30 Jun 2027** against a **4.49% 26-week T-bill** — only **~30bp of excess return** for bearing multi-jurisdiction antitrust, a two-thirds turnout hurdle, and litigation. The market is not pricing this as a near-certain close.
- **Asymmetry**: ~2.8% upside to terms vs. an undisturbed value near **$120** (18 Nov close) — roughly **-30%** on break. Poorly compensated.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **HIGH — Paul Hudson appointed "independent" director on 20 Oct 2026, ~5 weeks before signing with Sanofi, his former employer as CEO.** Verify recusal from all deal deliberations, his independence determination, and whether his equity/change-in-control treatment was disclosed. Direct 14(a) exposure.
2. **HIGH — Chief People Strategy Officer's $14.7M sale on 19 Nov, during press-reported deal talks.** Obtain the **10b5-1 plan adoption date**. Plan adopted pre-rumour = clean; any post-rumour adoption or discretionary trade = refer to counsel/insider-trading policy review immediately.
3. **MEDIUM-HIGH — Vote hurdle: >2/3 of all outstanding shares, unvoted = no.** Model turnout explicitly; a passive/retail-heavy register can sink a deal the market assumes is done.
4. **MEDIUM — Undisclosed Sanofi reverse termination fee.** The $1.125B Insmed-side fee is known; the allocation of antitrust-break cost to Sanofi is not. If Sanofi can walk on antitrust at low cost while INSM bears a 30% downside, risk is mispriced.
5. **MEDIUM — ARIKAYCE PDUFA 28 Jan 2027 falls pre-close.** Read the MAC/MAE carve-outs for regulatory and accelerated-approval outcomes before sizing the position.
6. **LOW-MEDIUM — CEO/Chair duality plus ~100%-sell/0%-buy insider pattern** over 12 months; also 6 Aug and 9 Sep director/officer sales into local highs. No fraud indicator, but no insider conviction signal either.
7. **GAP TO CLOSE**: board composition detail, comp alignment metrics, and the actual FY26 10-K risk factors could not be retrieved (10-K not yet filed; DEF 14A unavailable via tool; SEC direct fetch blocked). Confirm break fee reciprocity and any side agreements in the 8-K/merger agreement before final sign-off.

**Bottom line:** the non-financial risk in INSM is no longer operational — it is **process integrity and deal mechanics**, and the arb spread is too thin to compensate for the conflict-related vote and litigation risk. Recommend the investment team read the merger 8-K exhibits (including 10b5-1 plan dates and the Sanofi reverse fee) before treating $175 as a floor.
