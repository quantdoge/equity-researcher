## 1. Macro Environment Summary — *Reflationary tightening; "risk-off" at the margin*

**Stance: Transitional, tilting risk-off.** As of 25-Nov-2026 the US is in a re-acceleration regime, not a slowdown:

- **Growth is strengthening, not fading:** real GDP rose at a **~3.4% SAAR in Q3-26** (24,611 vs 24,408 Q2), following 2.2% in Q2 and only 0.05% in Q4-25. GDP is +2.1% y/y.
- **Inflation has turned back up:** CPI **3.82% y/y (Oct-26)** vs 3.72% (Sep), 3.35% (Aug) and 2.65% (Dec-25) — roughly +0.4pp per quarter. Nominal PCE is accelerating too (+6.3% y/y Sep-26 vs +5.1% Nov-25).
- **The Fed has stopped cutting and is leaning hawkish:** effective fed funds **3.88% (Oct-26)**, up from a 3.63% trough held May–Jul-26 after the 4.33%→3.63% easing cycle. The **3M bill (4.37%) now sits 49bp *above* fed funds**, i.e. the front end is pricing further tightening.
- **Long end is the problem:** 10Y at **5.13%**, 30Y **5.47%**, curve **+39bp (2s10s)** — a **bear steepener** driven by term premium/fiscal supply, not growth. With 10Y breakevens at just **2.12%**, the **real 10Y yield is ~3.0%** — historically restrictive and the single most important number for long-duration equity valuation.
- **Cracks in credit:** HY OAS has widened **+98bp in 12 weeks (2.65% on 1-Sep → 3.63% on 23-Nov)**, alongside a 400+ point Nasdaq decline. Absolute spreads are still tight, but the direction is unfriendly to leveraged, event-driven financing.
- **Dollar is firm:** broad USD **123.60** (20-Nov) vs 118.0 (10-Sep), +4.7%; **EUR/USD 1.0981** vs 1.163 in September.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact — **the thesis has changed character**

**Critical fact: on 24-Nov-2026 Sanofi agreed to acquire INSM for $175.00/share all-cash (~$41.0B equity / €37.6B), expected to close in H1-2027.** INSM closed 170.15 on 79M shares (36% of the float) in a $0.52 intraday range — it is now a **merger-arb instrument, not a biotech growth stock.** Macro transmission channels have flipped:

| Macro variable | Effect on INSM | Channel |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation re-acceleration / Fed hiking | **Mildly negative** | Raises discount rate on the fixed $175 terminal cash flow and raises arb financing cost, so the spread must widen for returns to clear T-bills. No operational impact — Insmed's pricing power and COGS are now Sanofi's problem. |
| 10Y at 5.13%, real 10Y ~3.0% | **Negative (carry only)** | Duration of the position collapses from ~15yrs (pre-deal biotech multiple) to **~0.5–0.6yrs**. The upside is capped at $175; high rates only make waiting costlier. |
| HY OAS +98bp in 12 weeks | **Watch item** | Sanofi funds via debt + cash with a **$35B bridge from JPMorgan**, and has "secured committed financing." IG issuer, so refinancing risk is low — but a disorderly spread blowout is the one macro path that threatens the cash consideration. |
| USD strength (+4.7% since Sep) | **Neutral to slightly positive** | $41.0B ÷ 1.0981 = **€37.34B** vs €37.6B struck at announcement — dollar strength trims Sanofi's euro cost. Consideration is fixed in USD, so INSM holders bear **no FX risk**. |
| Growth / employment | **Neutral** | Recession would not impair an all-cash IG balance-sheet deal. |
| Biotech funding environment / XBI beta | **Neutralised** | Beta 0.808 becomes meaningless; INSM decouples from XBI. Ironically, a risk-off biotech selloff now *understates* INSM. |

**Deal arithmetic (the real "macro" of this position):**
- Gross spread: $175.00 − $170.15 = **$4.85, or 2.85%**.
- To close H1-2027 (~6–7 months) → **~5.0–5.7% annualised**, versus ~4.4–4.7% on 3–6M T-bills: **only ~50–100bp excess return**. Thin.
- Break price = **$119.80** (18-Nov close, pre-Bloomberg-rumour) → downside **−29.6%**. Implied completion probability: **~91%**.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL**

The macro backdrop is **unfavourable for biotech as an asset class** (3.8% CPI, 5.13% 10Y, ~3.0% real yields, widening spreads) but **largely irrelevant to INSM's terminal payoff**, which is a fixed $175 in cash from an investment-grade acquirer with committed financing. Residual macro exposure is narrow and rate/financing-based. The rating is Neutral rather than Favourable only because the elevated front end caps the arb's excess return — you are being paid ~50–100bp over T-bills to underwrite regulatory and timeline risk, not macro risk.

## 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**Bear (higher probability of the two tails): a credit/term-premium shock breaks the financing math.** If inflation pushes CPI toward 4.5% and the 10Y through 5.75% while HY OAS gaps past 500bp, Sanofi's $35B bridge becomes expensive to term out. Combined with any antitrust second request, the spread would widen to 8–12% (stock $155–162) as the market repriced completion to 70–75%. On a break, INSM reverts to a long-duration brensocatib-launch story valued in a 3% real-rate world — plausibly **$90–110**, well below the $119.80 pre-rumour price.

**Bull: a topping bid or an accelerated close.** Insmed's shareholder suits already argue $175 undervalues the franchise, and the premium is only 46% to the undisturbed price. A competing offer at **$185–195** is not implausible for the "beyond-Dupixent" asset, and an early-termination clearance would pull the close into Q1-27, roughly **doubling the annualised return**.

**What I would monitor:** HSR/EC clearance and the shareholder vote date; Sanofi's bridge take-out in the IG market (watch HY OAS and the 5.13% 10Y); and the **3M bill–fed funds basis (+49bp)** — each further 25bp of priced hikes raises the hurdle rate the 2.85% spread must clear.
