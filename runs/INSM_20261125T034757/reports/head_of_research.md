**Investment Thesis**

On 24-Nov-2026 Sanofi agreed to acquire Insmed for **$175.00/share all-cash (~$41.0B, ~46% premium, ~27x TTM sales)**, with committed financing, no financing condition, and an H1-2027 close. INSM is therefore no longer a biotech growth stock — it is a **merger-arbitrage claim** whose payoff is +2.85% if the deal closes and roughly −30% (to the $119.80 undisturbed price) if it breaks. At the market-implied ~92% completion probability, expected return over ~7 months is **≈0% versus ~2.6% on T-bills** — the position is dominated by cash, and all 11 specialists agree there is no fundamental alpha left for INSM shareholders: the excellence of the franchise is precisely why Sanofi paid up, and that value has already been collected.

**Bull Case**
- **Deal closes on terms (~90%+ probability)**: all-cash, committed financing, no financing condition, low antitrust overlap, strategic urgency on Sanofi's side (Dupixent succession). Holders collect $175 in ~6–7 months.
- **Topping-bid optionality (~5–8%)**: shareholder suits argue $175 undervalues the franchise; the board retains a fiduciary out; a $185–195 rival bid roughly doubles the return. Selling now forfeits this free option.
- **The floor is higher than the undisturbed price suggests**: clean accounting, 85% gross margins, near-breakeven run-rate, and the Sanofi bid itself re-rate standalone value toward $105–125 rather than the $93 June low.

**Bear Case**
- **Negative expected value versus cash**: at the market-implied ~92% completion rate, expected 7-month return ≈ 0% vs ~2.6% risk-free; the spread pays ~30–100bp to underwrite antitrust, turnout and litigation risk. Kelly criterion says size zero.
- **The vote is a real hurdle**: >2/3 of *outstanding* shares, unvoted = no, on a register with heavy passive/arb ownership — apathy can sink a deal the market assumes is done.
- **Process-integrity overhang**: ex-Sanofi CEO on the board during the sale, a $14.7M officer sale amid rumours, and an undisclosed reverse termination fee give plaintiffs a credible narrative and create small-but-real injunction/disclosure-delay risk to the H1-27 timeline (each quarter of slip costs ~1–1.5% of annualised return).

**Key Risks**
1. **Antitrust second request / EC Phase 2 referral** — collapses the timeline and the annualised spread (low probability, deal-defining).
2. **Shareholder vote failure** on the >2/3-of-outstanding, unvoted-equals-no threshold.
3. **Reverse termination fee proves nominal** — Sanofi walks cheaply on antitrust while INSM holders bear −30%.
4. **Sanofi credit/bridge stress** in a 5.13% 10Y, widening-spread tape — the load-bearing assumption of the cash consideration.
5. **Break scenario**: stock reverts to a long-duration launch story in a 3% real-rate world — $100–125, with PDUFA (28-Jan-27) and Brinsupri payer-access data as the swing factors.
