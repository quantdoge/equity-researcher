## INSM — Full ESG Analysis
**Insmed Incorporated** (Nasdaq: INSM) | Healthcare / Biotechnology | US | Mkt cap ~$37.3B | 1,664 FTE | CEO & Chair: William H. Lewis

> **Material event that dominates this assessment:** On **24 Nov 2026**, Sanofi agreed to acquire Insmed for **$175.00/share in cash (~$41.0B equity value)**, a 46% premium to the pre-report close. INSM trades ~$170 (≈3% deal spread). For a cash deal already struck, most ESG factors have ceased to be independent valuation drivers — they now transmit only through **deal-completion risk** and **litigation**. The analysis below separates (a) the standing ESG profile of the operating business and (b) the residual ESG exposure embedded in the deal.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**
Hard 2025 data (third-party **limited assurance** on Scope 1 & 2 — above-average rigour for a $37B-cap biotech):

- **Scope 1: 2,622 tCO₂e** | **Scope 2: 2,264 tCO₂e (location) / 2,069 (market)** → Scope 1+2 ≈ **4,691 tCO₂e**
- Revenue ~$0.5–0.9B in 2025 (2026 guidance $1.35–1.4B) ⇒ **carbon intensity ≈ 5–10 tCO₂e per $1M revenue** — among the lowest of any sector
- **Renewables: 19%** of 5,626 MWh electricity — the one weak spot vs peers
- Water: **1.5M gallons**; **77% withdrawn from low-to-medium-high water-stress regions** (small absolute volume — immaterial)
- Waste: 11.03 t hazardous, 4.2 t regulated medical, 1.74 t non-hazardous; HAZWOPER/COSHH programmes; independent annual hazardous-waste audit
- **Disclosure quality: strong** — TCFD report (2025 edition explicitly addresses "GHG emissions reporting improvements and readiness for emerging regulations"), SASB index, annual Responsibility Report, dedicated Director of Corporate & Environmental Responsibility reporting to the Nominating & Governance Committee
- **Gap:** **no quantitative Scope 3 disclosure.** For an inhaled-therapy company this is the real footprint — device/nebuliser components, contract manufacturing, distribution, and patient-use electricity for daily nebulised ARIKAYCE. Stranded-asset risk ≈ nil.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**
This is the crux of the INSM ESG case.

- **Pricing/access — the dominant controversy.** BRINSUPRI (brensocatib, FDA-approved Aug 2025) launched at **$88,000/year list**, the first-ever therapy for non-CF bronchiectasis. **ICER's final evidence report** judged that a cost-effective annual price would be **$3,100–$3,700** at $100k–150k/QALY — i.e., the launch price is ~25x the value-based benchmark; an ICER panel publicly characterised the pricing as **"overreach."** ARIKAYCE carries a similarly high price point.
- **Safety:** ARIKAYCE carries a **boxed warning** for hypersensitivity pneumonitis, hemoptysis and bronchospasm, with documented respiratory-adverse-event and mortality imbalances in the post-approval experience. Manageable but it caps label expansion and invites FDA scrutiny of the **Jan 28, 2027 sNDA PDUFA** (MAC label expansion).
- **Access friction:** UK **NICE appraisal paused** ahead of a QALY-threshold change → UK launch deferred; EU (EC approval 18 Nov 2025) and Japan (launched Oct 2026) reimbursed access still being negotiated country-by-country.
- **Labour:** ~1,664 employees, specialised scientific talent, no disclosed safety incidents of note; high M&A-related retention risk through close. **No material D&I, supply-chain-labour, or community controversies identified** in the evidence reviewed (a genuine strength — my controversy-feed tool returned no company-specific incidents).
- **Product-safety litigation:** no material product-liability action found.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**Positives**
- Independent-majority board; **David R. Brennan serves as Lead Independent Director** (since Nov 2018)
- **No dual-class share structure**; single class, one-share-one-vote; Delaware; no poison pill evidenced
- Auditor **Ernst & Young ratified 190.4M for / 7.0M against (~96%)** — clean
- Robust ESG oversight architecture (NGC committee, executive-level ESG owner, annual TCFD update)
- Strong disclosure cadence; Section 16/13D hygiene appears normal

**Negatives**
- **CEO/Chairman duality:** William Lewis has been **Chair and CEO since Nov 2018** — a persistent structural weakness, mitigated but not cured by the Lead Independent Director
- **Staggered/classified board** (Class II directors elected to 2029 at the 2026 meeting) — entrenches management, reduces shareholder leverage
- **Say-on-pay support fell to ~87%** (162.1M for / **23.7M against**) at the May 2026 meeting — a ~13% protest vote, a material deterioration from the ">95% in favour" cited in the 2024 proxy. Signals real unease with NEO pay (large equity/option awards in a stock that 6x'd)
- **Director-level protest:** Elizabeth McKee Anderson received **32.1M withheld of ~186M voted (≈17%)**; Desjardins ~11.5% withheld — elevated against typical biotech norms
- **M&A process conflict:** the all-cash sale has drawn shareholder investigations from **Amparo Law Firm, Ademi LLP and Halper Sadeh LLC**, probing (i) whether $175 is the best obtainable price, (ii) whether the board ran an adequate sale process, (iii) **change-of-control benefits to insiders**, and (iv) **Section 14(a) proxy disclosure adequacy**. Standard "strike-suit" pattern, but it does focus scrutiny on whether the staggered board and CEO/Chair concentration served minority holders
- Management churn: a **CFO transition** was flagged as a sentiment negative in 2025-26 coverage

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US SEC climate rule:** stayed/not in force — no current obligation
- **California SB 253/261:** Insmed has CA operations (San Dimas manufacturing). The **$1B revenue threshold is likely crossed in FY2026** (guidance $1.35–1.4B), pulling INSM into **Scope 1/2/3 disclosure and TCFD-style climate-risk reporting from FY2026** — a genuine, near-dated compliance lift, and the reason the 2025 TCFD cites "readiness for emerging regulations"
- **EU:** not EU-listed and EU turnover likely below the CSRD €150M threshold ⇒ **not in scope standalone**. However, on closing, **Insmed consolidates into Sanofi's CSRD reporting** — expect intensive Scope 3, due-diligence (CSDDD) and taxonomy data requests during integration
- **IRA / Medicare:** Part D redesign and MFN pricing pressure; **watch-point** — ARIKAYCE's IRA orphan-drug exemption could be **forfeited if the Jan-2027 sNDA adds a second, non-orphan MAC indication**, pulling it into Medicare negotiation. Verify in the sNDA label
- **EU HTA Regulation / national HTA:** NICE (paused), German GBA, French HAS — the binding constraint on EU BRINSUPRI economics
- **EU MDR:** the ARIKAYCE delivery device remains a device-compliance dependency
- **Anti-corruption:** multi-jurisdiction commercial footprint (US, EU, Japan); Global Healthcare Compliance programme, Sunshine Act/EFPIA transparency reporting
- **CBAM:** not applicable (no covered goods); indirect exposure via glass/aluminium packaging only
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

**Why not Leader:** first-in-class science, best-in-sector E disclosure and assurance are offset by a **25x price-to-value gap flagged by ICER**, CEO/Chair duality, a staggered board, and a 13% say-on-pay revolt. **Why not Laggard:** no environmental, labour, safety-victim, or integrity controversy; assured emissions data; independent-majority board with a Lead Independent Director; and no controlling-shareholder abuse.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

- **For the live thesis, ESG is no longer a valuation input.** In an all-cash $175 deal with a ~3% spread, the return is a function of **antitrust/regulatory clearance, shareholder vote and the Section 14(a) litigation**, not of carbon intensity or pay ratios. ESG-mandated holders (ex-tobacco/controversial-weapons style screens) face no exclusion trigger — INSM passes standard negative screens.
- **Where ESG still bites, in order of importance:** (1) **pricing controversy** = the single factor that could either invite a political intervention into the deal or, conversely, support the strategic logic of Sanofi paying up to own the access/reimbursement relationship; (2) **proxy litigation** = a timing risk to close; (3) **California SB 253** = a modest incremental compliance cost in FY2026.
- **Post-close, ESG diligence migrates to Sanofi (SNY)** — an EU CSRD reporter with a materially more complex access-pricing and Scope 3 profile than INSM standalone.
- **Recommendation:** **Neutral on ESG grounds; no action.** Do not size or de-size an INSM position on ESG factors at this stage — the risk is event/deal risk. If the deal breaks, re-underwrite on the Social pillar first: the ICER value gap and payer access, not the environmental profile, is what would compress the multiple.
