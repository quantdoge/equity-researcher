# Sector Brief — FTAI Aviation Ltd. (FTAI)
**Sector: Industrials / Aerospace & Defense — sub-segment: Commercial aircraft & engine leasing and engine aftermarket (USM/MRO)**
*Current price $196.01 | Market cap $20.1bn | Beta 1.57 | FY25 revenue $2.51bn*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

FTAI sits at the intersection of two large, structurally linked markets: **commercial aircraft/engine leasing** (global aircraft leasing ~$207bn in 2025, forecast to grow at a high-single-digit/low-double-digit CAGR through 2035) and the **engine aftermarket**, where the engine-leasing sub-market alone was ~$11.1bn in 2025 and is projected to reach $17.7bn by 2032 (6.9% CAGR). The operating backdrop is unusually favourable: CFM56 engines are forecast to represent ~39% of engine-family shop-visit demand in 2026, with forecasts of **>23,000 CFM56 overhauls still required** over the fleet's remaining life, while new-generation LEAP/GTF durability problems and OEM delivery shortfalls force airlines to keep legacy narrowbodies in service far longer than planned (3,500+ commercial engines parked/short, ~60 Airbus aircraft reportedly delivered engineless in 2025). This has driven narrowbody lease rates up **~20–30% versus 2019** and created acute scarcity value in owned engines and serviceable material. The catch is that the sector is **late-cycle on tightness**: ISTAT commentary from August 2026 noted narrowbody lease rates easing from exceptional highs, and multiple forecasters flag a potential narrowbody/engine surplus emerging as early as 2027–28. Pricing power today is exceptional but explicitly cyclical.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **CFM56/V2500 shop-visit super-cycle.** CFM56-7B/5B fleets are entering peak maturity; >23,000 lifetime overhauls forecast, with CFM56 ~39% of 2026 shop-visit demand. Each shop visit is an addressable event for USM/LLP material and module exchange — FTAI's core product.
- **New-build aircraft scarcity.** Engine and airframe OEM bottlenecks (LEAP/GTF turnaround times, ~3,500 engine shortfall) are pushing retirements right and lifting lease rates 20–30% above 2019, supporting both lease yields and residual/asset-sale gains on FTAI's ~360+ asset portfolio.
- **Two new demand pools adjacent to the core:** (i) data-centre power generation — FTAI has secured North American contracts for **100+ power units for delivery in 2027–28** using aero-derivative engines; (ii) OEM endorsement of the "open aftermarket" (1,000+ approved CFM56-7B repairs), which legitimises the USM/PMA channel FTAI and its partner AAR serve exclusively through 2030.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Cycle-peak risk / coming supply normalisation.** Consensus industry view (Aviation Week, ISTAT, Fleet-Wire) is that narrowbody lease rates have peaked and an engine-then-airframe oversupply could arrive in 2027–28 as LEAP/GTF capacity catches up and retirements accelerate. FTAI's earnings are levered to asset values, so multiple compression risk is real.
- **Capital intensity, leverage and negative FCF.** FY25 interest expense **$248m** against EBITDA of $1.08bn (~4.3x gross), funded partly via ABS (MRE 2026-2 is the second FTAI-sponsored aviation ABS). Free cash flow is negative and earnings rely materially on asset-sale gains — a fundamentally lower-quality, rate-sensitive earnings mix versus fee-based peers.
- **Margin compression and OEM/competitor encroachment.** Gross margin has slid from 37.8% (FY21) → 33.3% (FY23) → **31.1% (FY25)** as mix shifts to product sales; GE/CFM and Safran are aggressively adding licensed MRO capacity (e.g. $80m second Querétaro shop), while HEICO, AAR, Willis and independent USM traders compete for the same feedstock. Feedstock (donor engine) inflation is the key margin swing factor.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Strong (in a narrow niche) — not a broad-moat franchise**

*Rationale for "strong":* FTAI is arguably the only scaled, vertically integrated owner of CFM56/V2500 feedstock with in-house module/LLP capability (Aviation Leasing + Aerospace Products), which gives it (a) captive donor-engine supply that pure USM traders must bid for, (b) an exclusive distribution channel via AAR through 2030, and (c) a 3x-peer growth rate with peer-median operating margins. In its specific niche — CFM56/V2500 module exchange and serviceable material — its position is the strongest in the market.

*Why not "very strong":* (i) gross margins 18–28pts below TransDigm/AerCap/HEICO show limited pricing power and no proprietary IP moat; (ii) returns are levered to asset values that are at cyclical highs; (iii) negative FCF and ~4x gross leverage make it the most fragile balance sheet in the comp set if the engine market normalises in 2027–28; (iv) the CFM "open aftermarket" that benefits FTAI today can be re-tightened by GE/Safran, who control repair specifications and are expanding licensed capacity. Porter's Five Forces: **supplier power HIGH** (OEM-controlled specs; donor-engine scarcity), **buyer power MODERATE** (airlines/lessors have alternatives but few scaled USM providers), **threat of new entrants MODERATE-LOW** (capital + technical barriers), **rivalry MODERATE-HIGH** (HEICO, AAR, Willis, OEM shops), **substitutes MODERATE** (new-build engines, OEM parts, part-outs).

## 6. Sector Rating: **Attractive (late-cycle)**

The aerospace aftermarket/engine-leasing sector offers the best fundamental demand visibility in Industrials right now — a multi-year, contract-like shop-visit backlog, an aircraft supply shortage that is structural rather than cyclical, and 6–10% structural market CAGRs. That justifies an Attractive rating. The qualifier is cycle position: lease rates and USM pricing are at or near peak, and the 2027–28 supply normalisation risk is well flagged by ISTAT and the trade press. Within the sector, FTAI is the highest-beta, highest-growth, highest-leverage way to own the theme — best suited to investors comfortable underwriting the durability of CFM56 aftermarket demand through the next downturn, rather than to those seeking defensive aftermarket exposure (TransDigm/HEICO are the lower-beta expressions).
