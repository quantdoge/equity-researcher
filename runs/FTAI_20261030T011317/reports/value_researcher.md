### 1. Business Quality Assessment — **Moat: Narrow (and narrowing in places)**

FTAI is two businesses wearing one ticker:
- **Aerospace Products (engine aftermarket):** buys used CFM56/V2500 engines, tears them down, and sells refurbished modules/parts through four MRO facilities (311 CFM56 modules in Q3'26, 877 YTD vs. a 1,200 full-year target). Revenue +76% y/y to $910M; Adj. EBITDA $282M (31% margin).
- **Aviation Leasing / "Strategic Capital":** a lessor now pivoting asset-light via SPV vehicles (SCI I: 212 of 301 aircraft closed; SCI II launched Sept '26 with 109 aircraft and a $2.0B warehouse facility). Leasing Adj. EBITDA *fell* to $104M from $134M y/y as assets migrate off balance sheet.
- **FTAI Power (new, Dec '25):** CFM56s converted to 25MW aeroderivative turbines for data-center/grid power; 100+ units contracted for 2027–28; Mod-1 hit 25MW peak / 23MW baseload test targets.

**The genuine moat argument** is real but temporary in nature: the CFM56-5B/7B is out of production with a ~20,000-engine installed base and a multi-decade parts tail, while new-engine lead times push airlines to refurbished material. FTAI's edge is **feedstock access and scale** (buying whole aircraft/engines, teardown capacity, JV relationships) — a cost advantage, not a brand, patent, or network effect. It competes with HEICO, AAR, Satair, and every other lessor/teardown shop, and ultimately at the pleasure of GE/Safran's aftermarket strategy.

**Moat trajectory: stable-to-narrowing.** Margins are compressing (gross margin 37.8% in 2021 → 31.1% in 2025; 22.8% in Q1'26), Aviation Leasing EBITDA guidance was cut from $575M to $475M for 2026, and the mix is shifting toward sold product and gain-on-sale revenue rather than recurring lease cash flow. A truly moated business does not need to sell assets to SPVs to earn its returns.

**The core financial problem for a Buffett-style buyer:** this company has never produced free cash flow. Every year of growth has been financed by ~$750M–$1.16B of capex plus working-capital build, funded with debt. FCF/NI conversion is deeply negative — the opposite of the >80% I require. And the headline "Adjusted EBITDA" ($348M in Q3'26) is roughly 2.3x net income ($149M), because for a lessor adding back depreciation on engines — an asset that is literally consumed — flatters economics. On GAAP net income, TTM is ~$530M (EPS ~$5.10), and that figure itself benefits from engine/JV sale gains whose recurrence is unproven.

**Intrinsic value (owner-earnings DCF).** Using normalized owner earnings of ~$530M (TTM net income, which I treat generously as a proxy given maintenance capex ≈ D&A), a 10-year growth fade and 9.5–10% discount rate:

- At **12% growth** (a conservative reading of the aftermarket runway): equity value ≈ **$13–16B → $125–155/share**.
- At **18–20% growth for a decade** (requires flawless execution *and* FTAI Power delivering $450–750M of 2027 EBITDA, a business with essentially no operating history): equity value ≈ **$22–25B → $215–240/share**.

So the current $196 price already embeds roughly **17–19% compounding for ten years** with no execution slippage, no accounting surprise, no OEM pushback, and no power-business delay. The 2027 guidance looks impressive (10x EBITDA), but $450–750M of that $2.3B target comes from pre-revenue Power units — strip it out and you pay ~13x EBITDA and ~20x 2027E earnings, which is *fair*, not cheap.

**At $196 there is no margin of safety — the price is a fair-to-full price for the optimistic case.** My estimate of intrinsic value is ~$150–170/share on a conservative basis, meaning the stock trades at a 15–30% *premium* to value, not a discount. Analysts' $290–360 targets are momentum extrapolations, not value anchors.

### 4. Management Quality — **Mixed: operationally excellent, capital allocation undisciplined**

**Positives:** Joe Adams has built something real — module throughput up 50% y/y, 20%+ ROIC, a genuine first-mover in CFM56 teardown, and credible new verticals. The Audit Committee completed an independent review of the Jan-2025 short-seller allegations and filed its 10-K on time without restatement — that matters.

**Concerns:**
- **Capital allocation runs against value discipline.** In FY2025 FTAI repurchased $124M of stock while burning $1.06B of FCF and carrying $3.4B of debt; in 2026 it has launched a **$500M ASR plus $80M of open-market buys while doubling authorization to $1.0B** — buying back stock at ~38x earnings, financed with borrowings, in a business with negative FCF and a $334M equity base. That is momentum-financing, not owner-operator discipline.
- **Earnings quality architecture.** The shift to "Strategic Capital" SPVs and JVs converts recurring lease income into gains-on-sale and fee income; the short seller's central allegation (transferring depreciated engines into the products segment to inflate aftermarket margins) was answered with a committee review and a margin guide-down to ~30%, not with a clean, disclosed unit-economics reconciliation.
- **Auditor change to KPMG** following the short-seller episode is a yellow flag that deserves scrutiny in the next proxy.
- **Insider ownership looks thin** (Form 4 activity shows small holdings), so management's incentives are salary/equity-comp-driven rather than owner-driven. Short interest ~6% of float remains a persistent overhang.

### 5. Investment Verdict — **HOLD (Pass at this price)**

This is a genuinely impressive growth machine with a real, if temporary, aftermarket tailwind and improving ROIC. It is also a leveraged, cash-consumptive, structurally complex financial-industrial hybrid whose reported profits depend on depreciation assumptions and JV/SPV structuring — precisely the areas where I insist on simplicity and transparency.

At $196 I am being asked to pay ~38x trailing GAAP earnings, ~19x TTM adjusted EBITDA, and 61x book, for a company with negative free cash flow, 10x debt-to-equity, and 2027 guidance that hinges on a business (Power) that hasn't shipped a unit. **A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy — and this one trades above my estimate of intrinsic value with no margin of safety.**

- **Revisit as a Buy below ~$130–140** (a 20% discount to conservative intrinsic value, ~13x 2027E earnings ex-Power).
- **Downgrade to Sell** if: operating cash flow stays negative into 2027, Power slips its 2027 delivery schedule, the leasing EBITDA decline accelerates, or the KPMG audit raises any restatement/control issue.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis
1. **Accounting/earnings-quality risk** — engine transfer pricing, SPV gain recognition, and the depreciation schedule on leased engines remain the crux of the bear case; the auditor transition to KPMG is unresolved evidence.
2. **Leverage risk** — $3.45B debt against $334M equity; interest coverage 3.4x. A rate shock or asset-value markdown (used engine values are cyclical) could impair equity rapidly.
3. **Negative FCF / refinancing dependence** — growth is entirely debt- and working-capital-funded; a credit-market closure would stall the flywheel.
4. **FTAI Power execution** — $450–750M of 2027 EBITDA from a business with 100+ units contracted but zero delivered; turbine qualification, GE Vernova competition, and data-center capex cyclicality are live risks.
5. **CFM56 aftermarket normalization** — as OEM new-engine output recovers, module/parts scarcity premiums erode; margin compression is already visible (Q1'26 gross margin 22.8%).
6. **Governance/dilution** — thin insider ownership, related-party JV structures, and buybacks at high multiples funded with debt transfer value from long-term owners to sellers.
7. **Sentiment volatility** — a 1.57 beta and a ~45% drawdown from the Jan-2026 high (~$290) to the Oct-2026 low (~$168); this is not a sleep-well-at-night holding.
