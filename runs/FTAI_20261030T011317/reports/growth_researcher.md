## FTAI Aviation (FTAI) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Snapshot:** Price ~$196 (data feed; latest Oct-2026 prints ~$165–176), market cap ~$20.4B, EV ~$23.5B, net debt ~$3.1B. Stock is ~35% below its Feb-2026 all-time high (~$290).

---

### 1. Growth Story

FTAI has converted a commodity aircraft-leasing business into the only scaled, vertically integrated **Maintenance-Repair-Exchange (MRE)** platform for CFM56/V2500 engines — owning both the engines *and* the repair shops, which lets it swap a serviceable engine in days instead of a 200-day MRO queue, and monetize "green time" by mixing and matching life-limited modules. Market share has gone from **<2% in FY22 to ~14% (Q2'26)** against a CFM56 aftermarket that is an aging, captive, multi-billion-dollar annuity. The second, newer leg — **FTAI Power** — adapts that same CFM56 core into a 25MW aeroderivative gas turbine (Mod-1) for AI data centers, with a **$1.465B anchor order** and a targeted 100 units in 2027. That is the re-rating catalyst: an aerospace aftermarket compounder with a zero-to-one power generation option attached.

### 3. TAM and Penetration

- **Engine MRE (core):** the global CFM56 fleet is ~20,000+ engines with shop-visit spend in the several-billions annually; add V2500. FTAI holds ~14% share with **module capacity disclosed to support ~25%** — i.e., roughly a doubling of share *without* any market growth. Management expects the CFM56 aftermarket to keep expanding until at least 2028.
- **Power (option):** 25MW aeroderivative turbines into a structurally power-short AI data-center market. The $1.465B order (~$14.7M/unit) alone equals ~58% of 2025 total company revenue; capacity is targeted at 100+ units/yr. Segment EBITDA guidance of **$450–750M in 2027 from a base of zero** against a ~$21B EV is the definition of asymmetric.
- **Differentiation:** sole operator at scale that owns engines *and* repair shops; PMA parts cost advantage; multi-year airline contracts (e.g., expanded United CFM56-7B exchange program, Oct-2026). Competitors (GE Aerospace, independent MROs) cannot replicate the module pool.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Mod-1 first deliveries / commercial production start (26Q4–2027)** — the binary event; batches deliver through Nov 2027 under the J&F Power Systems order.
2. **Additional hyperscaler power contracts** — each announcement re-rates the Power segment from "option" to "segment."
3. **FY2026 results (~Feb 24, 2027)** — first full year of $1.525B EBITDA; watch FCF and working capital.
4. **$500M accelerated share repurchase**, authorization doubled to **$1.0B** — meaningful at 13x forward earnings.
5. **Strategic Capital Initiative (SCI)** — new vehicle now investing ($3.0B+ target); asset recycling frees equity capital and supports the 25%+ share target.
6. **Continued airline MRE wins** (United expansion already banked).

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (high conviction; becomes Strong Buy on Power execution proof)

At the latest observable ~$168: **13.5x 2026E EV/EBITDA, 9.0x 2027 guidance EBITDA, 6.3x 2027 consensus EBITDA, 13x 2027E EPS, ~4.5x 2026E sales** — for a business growing revenue 55–70% with EBITDA roughly doubling annually. At $196, still only ~15x 2027E EPS. Six recent targets cluster at **$290–$360 (median ~$310)**, implying 58–85% upside. A PEG far below 1 plus a free Power option is the setup we want; the reason this is Buy-not-Strong-Buy is that the FCF line and the accounting history demand position sizing, not conviction alone.

---

### 7. Key Risks

- **Accounting opacity / credibility overhang.** Muddy Waters (Jan-2025) alleged misleading revenue reporting around Aerospace Products; the stock fell ~30% in a day and has been volatile since. Disclosure complexity (SCI vehicles, gains on asset sales, adjusted metrics) is a permanent discount factor.
- **Negative free cash flow and leverage.** Four straight years of negative FCF (–$648M, –$1.35B, –$1.06B), $248M interest expense, ~$3.1B net debt. Growth is financed by debt, working capital, and third-party SCI capital; a capital-markets shutout would bite.
- **Margin mix erosion.** Gross margin 42.1% (FY22) → 30.5% (1H26) as mix shifts to heavy restoration builds; module supply scarcity could force further low-margin work.
- **Power execution.** 100 units in 2027 from zero, first-of-kind manufacturing ramp, single anchor customer, unproven hiring/R&D footprint. Slippage would remove the multiple.
- **Fleet-cycle risk.** CFM56 aftermarket eventually contracts (post-2028) as engines retire; share gains must outrun it.
- **Estimate fragility.** Only 2–7 analysts; 2030 EPS range $32–$42; GAAP EPS has missed in 5 of 7 quarters.

**Bottom line:** own it as a share-gaining aftermarket franchise at a growth multiple that hasn't yet priced the Power segment — but underwrite it on Adj. EBITDA and module volumes, and treat the FCF conversion inflection as the metric that would justify a Strong Buy.
