### 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT — but not the way it looks**

The eleven reports are not eleven independent opinions. They divide cleanly, and the division is diagnostic:

**Consensus #1 (High agreement, 6 of 11 agents): the cash-conversion problem is real.** Fin Risk, Short, Value, Quant, NonFin and ESG converge independently on the same three facts: net income +$501M against operating cash flow −$311M; accruals at 19.3% of assets; five consecutive years of negative FCF funded by $3.45B of debt against $334M of equity. **No agent anywhere in the file rebuts this.** The Growth Researcher concedes it ("Rule of 40 = 0.84 on a −42% FCF margin"); the Sector Researcher concedes it. This is the file's only genuine consensus and it is bearish.

**Consensus #2 (High agreement): the operating momentum is real.** Revenue +43% to +76% YoY, 311 modules (+50%), the United expansion, the $1.465B power order. No agent disputes this either.

**Split (Low agreement): what those two facts are worth.** Growth and Alt Data value the forward multiple (9–15x 2027E). Value and Quant value the trailing multiple (38–42x P/E, ~19x TTM EV/EBITDA) and object that **$450–750M of the $2.3B 2027 EBITDA guide comes from a business that has delivered zero units.** Both are arithmetically correct. They disagree only on whether management's 2027 number is an asset or a liability.

**Resolution rule applied:** when the bull and bear agree on the facts and diverge only on price, the price decides. The value anchor is $150–170; the price is $196, and it got there on a one-day gap. Separately, my framework is explicit that fraud/legal risk takes precedence over value bullishness — and two agents flag an unresolved strict-liability sanctions tail plus a live securities class action headed for a motion-to-dismiss ruling. That alone removes FTAI from the "high conviction" bucket regardless of what the growth math says.

**Net: split file, negative skew, binary catalyst unadjudicated → "multiple risk flags."**

### 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Factor exposure — this is the objection that kills the trade at size.** FTAI is a concentrated short of the quality factor dressed as a growth stock: negative FCF, top-decile accruals (0.186, worst in peer set by a factor of 25), 10.3x debt/equity. Any portfolio running a quality or earnings-stability screen will show FTAI as the worst name in the book on two dimensions at once. It also adds **duration** exposure into a regime where real yields are up 67bp since May and the 2Y sits +90bp above effective Fed Funds — i.e. the market is pricing hikes, not cuts. Most growth books are already long that factor; FTAI is a levered expression of a bet the portfolio probably already has.

**Beta and correlation.** Measured, not asserted:

| | β vs SPY | Correlation to FTAI |
|---|---|---|
| **FTAI** | **2.61** (t ≈ 9.2) | — |
| GE Aerospace | — | 0.59 |
| HEICO | — | 0.55 |
| AerCap | — | 0.52 |
| SPY | — | 0.50 |

At β 2.61, a 1.25% position injects **3.3% of portfolio beta** — it behaves like a 3%+ position. Correlation to SPY is only 0.50 (R² 0.25), so a 1.25% position leaves ~0.9% of *pure* unhedgeable name risk. Note that a 20% market drawdown mechanically implies 35–40% downside here, and the tape proves the stock can do −52% in four sessions (Jan-2025).

**Sector and theme concentration.** FTAI would add to Industrials/Aerospace & Defense — an overweight in most growth books — and, more importantly, it now carries a **second, highly crowded factor: AI data-centre power**. The FTAI Power narrative is the same trade as utilities, gas turbines, and AI-capex infrastructure. A portfolio with AI-infrastructure exposure would be doubling a crowded theme through a 68%-vol, high-accrual, speculative-grade issuer. That is precisely the wrong instrument to add correlation with.

**What it *does* diversify:** the macro reports make a genuine case that the engine aftermarket is counter-cyclical to airline P&L (Aerospace Products +76% YoY *during* a 121% jet-fuel spike). That is real, but it argues for owning the theme via GE or HEICO at β ~1.1 rather than FTAI at β 2.61.

### 7. Final Portfolio Action: **PASS**

**No new initiation at $196.01.** Existing holders: **HOLD, trimmed to ≤1.25%**, with the stop and invalidation rules above applied without discretion.

**Rationale in one paragraph.** FTAI is a genuinely good operating business — 43–76% revenue growth, rising ROIC at 20.4%, a real share-gain story from 2% to 14%, and a Power option with a hard $1.465B order behind it. The growth and alt-data agents are not wrong about any of that. But six independent agents converge on a single unrefuted fact — five straight years of negative free cash flow and an $812M accrual gap — and two agents flag a binary, unadjudicated legal tail (a live 10b-5 class action at the core EBITDA line, plus a strict-liability Iran sanctions allegation that OFAC broadened in September 2026). Against that, the price is $196 against an intrinsic-value anchor of $150–170, reached via a +17% one-day gap, with 252-day momentum at −24.6% and beta at 2.61. My expected value calculation produces +6.3% against 68% volatility: a real but very thin edge, entirely dependent on an execution path (100 power units from a standing start) that has no operating history. **The correct size for that edge, after haircutting for a binary legal catalyst and 73% idiosyncratic risk, is under 1% — which is not worth owning.** The disciplined action is to wait: the re-entry conditions are specific, monitorable, and roughly 20–25% below the current price.

**What would change the call to Initiate (1–2%):** dismissal of *Shannahan* + the first positive operating-cash-flow quarter + price at or below $165. Two of those three are events; one is a price. All three are watchable.

**Honest gaps in this synthesis:** no true engine-lessor peer multiples were available to any agent (all comps were mega-cap A&D), so no relative-value cross-check exists; FTAI's debt maturity ladder and covenant thresholds were never retrieved, which is the single most important missing input for the leverage assessment; and no CDS or OFAC penalty estimate exists to price the legal tail. The recommendation is built with a wider margin of safety precisely because those gaps sit on the bear side.
