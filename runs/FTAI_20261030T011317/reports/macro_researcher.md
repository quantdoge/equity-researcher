## 1. Macro Environment Summary — "Reflation with a Bear Steepener" (Risk-On, Fragile)

The US economy is in a **reflationary, late-cycle regime**: growth is running above trend, inflation has re-accelerated after a brief 2026 spring trough, and the Fed's easing cycle has effectively stalled — yet credit markets are pricing near-zero risk. Three forces define the backdrop for FTAI:

**(a) A second inflation wave, energy-led.** Headline CPI bottomed at **+2.39% YoY in January 2026** and has re-accelerated to **+3.72% YoY in September 2026** (CPI index 336.298 vs 324.245). Brent crude drove it: **$66.60/bbl (Jan 2026) → $114.16/bbl (Sep 2026), +71% YTD** and **+77% YoY**. IATA projects jet fuel averaging **$152/bbl in 2026**, with global jet fuel prices up **121% between April 2025 and April 2026**.

**(b) The easing cycle is over — the market is now pricing hikes.** Fed Funds settled at **3.75%** (Sep 2026), down only 58bp from the 4.33% plateau held through mid-2025. Critically, the **2Y sits at 4.65% vs. 3.75% effective FF — a +90bp gap implying the market expects the Fed to *tighten* ~90bp over the next two years**, not cut.

**(c) Real rates, not inflation expectations, are doing the damage.** The 10Y nominal has risen **+60bp since 29 May 2026 (4.45% → 5.05%)**, but the 10Y TIPS real yield rose **+67bp (2.00% → 2.67%)**. The 10Y breakeven is only **2.38%** — *anchored*. This is a **term-premium/fiscal-supply shock**, not an inflation-expectations shock. The 30Y at **5.37%** and a bear-steepening 2s10s of **+34bp** confirm it.

**(d) Credit is wide open — and that is the anomaly.** HY OAS at **3.14%** (28 Oct 2026) is within 54bp of the cycle tight (2.60%, Aug 2026). All-in HY yields ~8.1%. Liquidity is abundant; risk premia are complacent.

Growth is solid: **real GDP +3.37% annualized in Q3 2026** ($24,611B), **+2.07% YoY**; unemployment **4.2%**, stable. Nominal PCE +6.28% YoY.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

FTAI is **not a typical industrial** — it is a leveraged, long-duration aviation-asset compounder (beta 1.57, $20.1B cap) that buys CFM56/V2500 engines, monetises them through lease, part-out and aftermarket, and funds this with **$3.5B of senior notes at a 6.5% weighted-average coupon (4.2yr WAM, first corporate maturity 2028)** plus a **$2.0B SPV warehouse facility**.

| **10Y / real yields ▲** | **NEGATIVE (high)** | Primary driver of the 2026 de-rating. Real yields +67bp since May → long-duration multiple compression. Stock fell **-49.9% peak-to-trough ($308.30 on 26 Feb → $154.37 on 23 Oct)** while Q3 Adj. EBITDA *grew* +17% YoY to $348.3M. Pure multiple compression, not fundamental deterioration. |
| **Fed Funds / 2Y ▲** | **NEGATIVE (moderate)** | 2Y at 4.65% drives the floating-rate **$2B warehouse facility** cost. Also lifts the hurdle rate on every engine acquisition — FTAI is a spread business: borrow at ~8%, earn aftermarket returns. Rising base rates compress that spread. |
| **Refinancing risk** | **NEUTRAL near-term / NEGATIVE 2028+** | Well-laddered: no corporate maturity until **2028**, and 6.5% coupons are locked. But if 10Y holds >5%, the 2028 refi reprices upward. FTAI is *insulated today, exposed tomorrow*. |

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL (negative skew)**

**Rationale.** The macro backdrop is a headwind on precisely the two variables that dominate FTAI's valuation: (1) the **real discount rate**, now 2.67% and rising, which has already halved the share price from its February peak despite 17% EBITDA growth; and (2) the **cost of incremental leverage**, with the 2Y at 4.65% implying the Fed's next move is a hike. The oil shock is crushing FTAI's airline counterparties.

These are substantially offset by: **wide-open credit** (HY OAS 3.14%), **no aerospace tariffs**, **above-trend GDP (+3.4% annualized)**, an **improving capacity growth trajectory into 2027**, and a **genuinely new secular driver in AI/data-centre power generation** that is largely immune to aviation macro.

Net: **operational momentum is strong, the macro multiple is hostile.** FTAI is a good business in a bad rate regime. The rating would move to **Unfavourable** if the 10Y breaks 5.50% or HY OAS widens beyond ~4.50%; it would move to **Favourable** if real yields retrace below 2.25% while Brent normalises.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **Bear: "The Failed Easing"**

Consensus (evidenced by HY OAS at 3.14% and FTAI's +27% four-day rebound to $196) assumes the oil spike is transitory and the Fed resumes cutting. **The market is not pricing the alternative.**

**The chain:** Brent holds above $110 through the northern-hemisphere winter on supply disruption → headline CPI breaks **4.5% by Q1 2027** → the Fed, with the 2Y already at 4.65%, is forced into an explicit **hike in H1 2027** → the 10Y clears **5.75%** and real yields break 3.25% → HY OAS mean-reverts from 3.14% toward **5.50%**.

**Transmission to FTAI:**
1. **Valuation:** a 1.57-beta, long-duration, negative-FCF compounder at a premium multiple absorbs a second leg of real-rate repricing. The Feb–Oct de-rating (-50%) repeats on a smaller scale.
2. **Funding:** the $2B floating-rate warehouse reprices instantly; the **2028 senior-note refi** would clear at 8%+ vs. the current 6.5%, permanently impairing the spread model.
3. **Counterparty:** airlines cutting capacity hard (Q3 2026 growth is already +0.2%) cuts flight hours and shop-visit volume, while lease-rate pressure builds on older CFM56 assets.
4. **Feedstock/part-out economics** and the **2027–28 power-unit deliveries** (which need capital at exactly the wrong moment) both deteriorate.

**Downside:** $120–135/share (-31% to -39%), reverting toward the October low of $154 and below.

**The mirror-image bull** (lower probability, high magnitude): an oil collapse to ~$70 on a supply/ceasefire resolution → CPI back to 2.5% → Fed resumes cutting → 10Y to 4.0% and real yields to 2.0% → FTAI re-rates on both multiple *and* the AI-power narrative, retesting **$300+**.

**Asymmetry:** the bear is the *unpriced* tail. Credit spreads at 3.14% and a stock that rallied 27% in four sessions on an earnings beat show the market is positioned for the benign path. FTAI's macro risk is therefore **skewed to the downside even though the base-case rating is Neutral.**
