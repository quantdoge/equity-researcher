## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**NBIS profile:** AI infrastructure operator (GPU cloud in the US/Finland/France, plus Toloka AI, TripleTen, Avride). 2024 revenue was only $117.5M (+462% YoY) but Q2 2026 capex alone was ~$5.7B — extreme hyper-capex, with a $17.4B five-year Microsoft contract, an NVIDIA $2B strategic investment, and a $775M GPU-collateralized secured debt facility. This is a long-duration equity whose value sits almost entirely in contracted future cash flows.

**Interest rates & financing — NEGATIVE.** This is the single most material macro headwind. NBIS funds $5B+/quarter of GPU and data-center capex; its debt is GPU-collateralized ("senior secured" against deployed clusters and contracted cash flows), which cushions refinancing risk, but the strategy relies on cheap, available capital. The 10Y backup from 4.26% to ~5.2% since April directly raised the discount rate applied to very-long-duration growth cash flows — the cleanest macro explanation for NBIS's ~50% drawdown (peak ~$299 intraday 17–18 June → $145.8 low 29 July) even while fundamentals were beating. With the 2Y at 4.65% pricing tightening ahead, the forward path is hostile to further multiple expansion.

**Yield curve — NEUTRAL/NEGATIVE.** A +34 bp 2s10s slope is a non-recessionary but not steeply steepened curve; it says the market is demanding more term premium, not pricing relief. For a high-duration asset, this is the bad side of the curve environment: no inversion recession signal, but no "Fed puts rates down" signal either.

**Inflation — MIXED, tilted POSITIVE on pricing power, NEGATIVE on costs.** Sticky 3%+ CPI is keeping the long end elevated. But NBIS demonstrated real pricing power — raising pay-as-you-go prices 17–21% on selected NVIDIA instances and ~25% on CPU-only workloads in September 2026 amid booming compute demand. However, power is its #2 variable cost behind GPUs, and electricity prices (especially EU grid pricing in Finland/France) are inflating: "AI data center power surge" is a live opex risk that inflation pass-through only partially recovers. Net: pricing power offsets most of the CPI cost drag.

**Growth — POSITIVE.** US GDP ~2.2% YoY and the global AI capex supercycle (NVIDIA at ~$5.7T market cap, hyperscaler GPU commitments racing toward 12 GW across the neocloud complex) are the primary demand engine. NBIS's contracted backlog (~$20B incl. Microsoft) converts nominal GDP and corporate IT capex growth directly into revenue. Risk: if US growth rolls over into recession, hyperscalers backload/grace-period contracts, and prepayment-funded capex is the first casualty — the loosening labor market (4.0% → 4.2%) is the amber flag.

**Currency & trade — MILDLY POSITIVE.** Revenues are substantially USD-denominated (Microsoft contract, US Vineland NJ cluster); a material share of opex (EU power, staff in Finland/France/Israel) is EUR-denominated. A broad USD index at ~120 makes EUR costs cheaper in USD terms — a mild net tailwind (offset by a minor competitive friction for EUR-based customers). No meaningful tariff exposure (GPUs are export-controlled inputs, not tariff-exposed outputs, and NBIS is a licensee, not a hardware importer into the US).

**Sector comparability — NEUTRAL.** NBIS sits in the AI/semis complex (NVDA, MSFT, TSM, AVGO, MU) but is fundamentally different from its peers: hardware/software vendors sell at high margins while NBIS is a capital-consumer. The macro regime that inflates NVDA (AI capex boom) is the same one that stresses NBIS's balance-sheet leverage — rates cut both ways at betas above 1.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating

- The two legs roughly offset today; the rating flips to **Unfavourable** if long yields break decisively above 5.5% or the US enters recession.

## 5. Material Surprise Scenario (Bear)

**"The Term-Premium Shock + AI Capex Discipline" (probability-weighted material bear):** Inflation stays stuck at 3.2–3.5% into 2027 while the US fiscal deficit keeps Treasury supply heavy; the 10Y breaks through 5.5–5.75% and the curve steepens further without any recession developing. The Fed, having already stopped easing, is forced to talk about **hikes** (the 2Y at 4.65% is already pricing this). Under this regime, every long-duration asset — NBIS most acutely at beta 1.46 with ~$5B/quarter of external-financing-dependent capex — de-rates again: a repeat of the June–July 2026 drawdown (‑51%) is plausible as the discount-rate leg reasserts itself. Simultaneously, hyperscalers respond to higher capital costs by slowing the *next* wave of prepayments (Microsoft's $17.4B is signed, but NBIS's next 10–20 GW of expansion needs new contracts under materially worse financing terms), and EU grid power prices add another 8–12% opex inflation. Combined, this is a true valuation halving: multiple compression from higher duration risk **and** a hard ceiling on the contracted-backlog growth that justified the premium. **Watch item:** the October 2026 trajectory of DGS10 — a sustained close above 5.3% is the tripwire.
