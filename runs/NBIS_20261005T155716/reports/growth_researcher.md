# NBIS — Nebius Group N.V. — Growth Investment Analysis

**As of late September 2026 | Price ~$235.60 | Market Cap ~$56.5B | EV ~$61B**

## 1. Growth Story

Nebius is a pure-play AI infrastructure company (NASDAQ-listed, Amsterdam HQ — the AI-cloud successor to Yandex N.V.) building a full-stack, GPU-native cloud: owned NVIDIA fleets, the Nebius AI Studio inference platform, and the Toloka data business. It is the clearest publicly listed "neocloud" challenger to hyperscaler capacity, and it has converted this positioning into hard money: a **~$17.4B (expandable to $19.4B) multiyear Microsoft contract through 2031** and a **$2B strategic equity investment from NVIDIA** (March 2026). Q2 2026 revenue grew **+454% YoY** with gross margin at **77%** and adjusted EBITDA positive — the model is inflecting from "build" to "scale."

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

- **TAM:** The AI compute "neocloud" market is a subset of global AI infrastructure, which carries ~$1T+ of cumulative capex through 2030 and hundreds of billions in annualized serviceable spend; IDC-style forecasts put AI-cloud/GPU-as-a-service SAM in the **$170–200B+ by 2030** range.
- **Penetration:** Nebius is generating ~$3B 2026E revenue — **roughly 1.5–2% of a $150B+ near-term SAM**. Even reaching 5–8% share by 2030 fully supports the analyst revenue path. The Microsoft contract alone (~$17–19B through 2031) backstops 2–3 years of consensus revenue.
- **Differentiation / why Nebius:** (1) Full-stack AI-native cloud (compute + inference + data) rather than a bare GPU reseller; (2) NVIDIA co-investor alignment and preferential GB300 allocation (100k+ chips committed to MSFT deal); (3) Europe/Israel talent base with a US listing — a scarce "Western-ex-China AI infra" vehicle; (4) 400k+ GPU fleet target by end-2026 — one of the largest independent fleets outside the hyperscalers.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **Strong Buy** (5-year disruptive-growth horizon)

This is precisely the profile a growth-first framework seeks: a company at the epicenter of the largest capex cycle of the decade, with **revenue growing 4–6x per year, gross margin expanding toward ~75–80%, a contracted-$17B+ anchor backlog through 2031, a strategic $2B NVIDIA investment, and accelerating sequential growth**. At ~18x 2026E and ~4.7x 2027E sales, the market is paying a premium — but for a +450% grower with an EBITDA turn and locked-in capacity revenue, the multiple is justified if the backlog executes. We would underweight GAAP-based Rule-of-40 math (destroyed by depreciation-accelerated FCF) in favor of gross-margin dollar growth and EBITDA trajectory.

## 7. Key Risks

- **Customer concentration (highest risk):** Three customers were **~59% of Q2 2026 revenue**; Microsoft is effectively the anchor tenant. A renegotiation, capacity-downgrade, or hyperscaler insourcing decision would truncate the consensus ramp.
- **Execution/capex financing risk:** $20–25B capex guidance requires debt/convert issuance and equity-linked dilutions; the model is FCF-negative (-$3.7B FY25) until fleets amortize. Convert-note dilution and insider-selling headlines have already pressured shares.
- **Supply-chain dependency:** Heavily reliant on NVIDIA allocation; delays to GB300/Rubin deliveries impair the contracted capacity schedule.
- **Competition & oversupply cycle:** CoreWeave, Crusoe/Lambda, and hyperscaler in-house capacity are flooding the same market; a 2027–28 AI-compute oversupply scenario would compress GPU cloud pricing and margins.
- **Valuation fragility:** Consensus embeds ~90% CAGR through 2030; any deceleration in AI capex headlines re-rates the stock violently — recent history shows 30–40% drawdowns are routine.
- **Geopolitical/legacy overhang:** Yandex heritage invites periodic EU/US scrutiny headlines; corporate-governance overhangs tend to reappear even though the Russian business was divested.
