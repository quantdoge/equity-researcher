# Sector Brief: Nebius Group (NBIS) — AI Infrastructure / GPU Cloud

## 1. Sector Overview

Nebius (formerly Yandex N.V., re-listed Oct 2024) builds end-to-end AI infrastructure: GPU cloud (Nebius), data annotation (Toloka), edtech (TripleTen) and autonomous driving (Avride). It competes in the **AI infrastructure / high-performance GPU-cloud** arena — the fastest-growing corner of the Technology sector — where hyperscalers (MSFT, ORCL), chip vendors (NVDA, AMD) and pure-play GPU clouds (CoreWeave) are all expanding capacity to serve insatiable AI-training demand. The sector is in **early, hyper-growth cycle**: contracted capacity is finite and pre-sold years ahead, GPU leasing prices are being *raised* (NBIS lifted pay-as-you-go rates from Oct 1), and leading suppliers are backlogged. NBIS itself guides to a **$7–9B end-2026 revenue run-rate vs $1.25B at end-2025**, with contracted AI capacity >3.5GW (target >4GW by 2026) and ~75% of infrastructure owned rather than leased. *Caveat: FMP's sector-aggregate endpoint returned unreliable single-company rows, so benchmarks below use direct peer financials.*

## 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Structural demand > supply:** AI training/inference demand vastly outstrips GPU capacity. NBIS booked a **$17.4B (up to $19.4B) five-year Microsoft deal** — larger than its market cap at announcement — plus a **$2B NVIDIA agreement** to deploy >5GW by 2030; AI-cloud ARR reportedly up ~674% YoY.
- **Scarcity-driven pricing power:** Nvidia GPU wait times stretch into 2027 and NBIS is raising GPU lease prices — evidence of pricing authority typical of early-cycle capacity markets.
- **First-mover technical position:** NBIS was the first cloud to offer leading Nvidia accelerators at scale; owning ~75% of infrastructure (vs competitors' leased fleets) secures better unit economics once utilisation ramps.

## 3. Key Headwinds
- **Massive capex/financing burden:** GPU + datacenter capex is exploding; NBIS funds a >4GW build-out while generating only $530M revenue. Financing costs and depreciation ($404M D&A in 2025) pressure an already-loss-making P&L.
- **Power and execution constraints:** ~50% of planned US datacenter builds are delayed/cancelled; grid connection queues (~2,600GW) and skilled-labour shortages are the binding constraint, not chips.
- **Competition & substitution:** Hyperscalers increasingly self-build (MSFT/OpenAI, Oracle), and alternative silicon (AMD, custom ASICs) plus model-efficiency gains (distillation) could compress GPU-cloud economics; a demand-air-pocket or AI-capex pause would hit a 100%+ revenue multiple hard.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (growth) / AVERAGE (current profitability)**

- **Growth: exceptionally strong** — +351% YoY (vs +17–65% for incumbents), $17.4B anchor contract, 4GW contracted capacity, price increases in force; it is *gaining* share against hyperscalers in dedicated AI capacity.
- **Margins: average-to-behind** — gross margin is competitive, but heavy depreciation, SGA and pre-revenue build-out keep it deeply loss-making; ROIC is negative until capacity ramps to the $7–9B run-rate.
- **Balance-sheet optionality** tilts positive: owned infrastructure, anchor customers (MSFT, NVIDIA) de-risk financing relative to debt-levered peers like CoreWeave.

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE** (with valuation caution)

The AI-infrastructure sector is in an early, demand-pulled hyper-growth phase with verified pricing power and contracted, pre-sold capacity — a structurally attractive backdrop. However, entry risks (power, chip allocation, capital intensity, hyperscaler self-build) are high, and NBIS's ~107x trailing-revenue valuation embeds flawless execution of its $7–9B run-rate target; any AI-capex slowdown would compress this aggressively.
