## 1. ESG Materiality Map (AI-Infrastructure "Neocloud" Sector)

Nebius is a capital-intensive AI-infrastructure business: GPU cloud (Nebius), data labeling (Toloka AI), self-driving/robotics (Avride), and edtech (TripleTen). It is mid-transformation from a sanctioned Russian internet conglomerate into a Western AI compute provider (~1,543 employees, ~$56.5B market cap, 5 GW contracted-capacity target under a ~$250B capex plan by 2030; $17.4B Microsoft compute deal; $5.75B convertible notes issued).

| Factor | Financial materiality | Why it matters for NBIS |
|---|---|---|
| **E — Energy procurement & grid access** | **High** | Electricity is the #1 operating input of GPU clouds. Power availability/price caps capacity economics; PUE and renewable PPAs determine cost and carbon trajectory. |
| **E — Embedded/Scope 3 emissions** | Medium | Server/GPU manufacturing emissions dominate lifecycle footprint; unexplored → future procurement/regulatory cost. |
| **E — Water & physical climate** | Low–Med | Nordic sites (Finland) water-secure; US expansion adds water/heat exposure; cooling efficiency is a cost lever. |
| **S — AI data governance & privacy** | **High** | Enterprise AI customers' data handling is the license to operate for the cloud business; breaches would be existential for trust. |
| **S — Gig workforce (Toloka)** | Medium | Crowd-labeling model attracts wage/conditions scrutiny (class-action/vendor-audit risk); reputational. |
| **S — Talent & product safety** | Medium | Intense AI-talent competition; Avride public-road safety; TripleTen consumer-outcome scrutiny. |
| **G — Founder/dual-class control** | **High** | CEO family trust holds Class B shares ≈ **52% of voting power vs ~11% economic interest**; "Controlled Company" exemptions from Nasdaq independence requirements. |
| **G — Internal control quality** | **High** | Material weaknesses disclosed (below) at a company raising multi-billion-dollar convertibles. |
| **G — Sanctions legacy / geopolitical** | Medium | EU sanctions history of CEO and Russia divestment; US AI-export-control regime overlays chip procurement. |

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM** (trending higher)

**Emissions (company-reported, via DitchCarbon):**
- FY2024: ~2,000 tCO2e total
- FY2025: Scope 1 ~3,800 t; Scope 2 (market-based) ~61,200 t; total ~65,000 tCO2e — **~30× YoY increase** as the GPU fleet scales.
- Scope 3 (server/GPU embodied emissions, supply chain): **not reported** — a major omission for a hardware-heavy AI cloud; this is where most of the lifecycle footprint will sit as 5 GW comes online.

**Positives:** 2024 average PUE ~1.3 (reportedly better in 2025); Finnish data centers run on one of Europe's lowest-carbon grids; market-based Scope 2 is their dominant, controllable lever via PPAs and grid geography.

**Risks:** No net-zero / SBTi-style commitment on record ("none on record" per DitchCarbon); absolute Scope 1+2 will scale geometrically with the 5 GW/$250B plan absent committed renewable procurement; US expansion increases grid-intensity, water, and physical-climate variance. Power-supply constraints (grid connection delays, curtailment) are already a stated capex-timing risk in the 20-F.

**Physical climate:** Low-to-moderate — Finland core site is cool/water-sufficient; new US locations vary. Not the binding environmental constraint; **energy cost and carbon intensity are.**

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM**

- **AI data governance/privacy (highest social weight):** Nebius processes third-party AI workloads; enterprise trust hinges on data isolation, EU GDPR compliance (Amsterdam HQ), and DPA strength. No major breach/controversy surfaced in the news sweep (0–180 days).
- **Toloka AI gig workforce:** distributed crowd labeling is structurally exposed to pay-transparency and working-condition scrutiny (industry-wide pattern: litigation and client audits). No NBIS-specific incident found; risk is sectoral, not company-specific — to date.
- **Avride (autonomous delivery/robotics):** public-road safety oversight in US/Israel deployment; early-stage exposure.
- **TripleTen (edtech):** "career-outcomes" marketing invites consumer-protection/FTC-type scrutiny; also the subject of a revenue-recognition control weakness (see governance) — reputational overlap.
- **Workforce:** ~1,543 employees, tech-heavy; turnover/retention risk in AI labour market is real but not ESG-flagging.

No labour, safety, or community controversies found in the recent-news sweep.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **4 / 10 (Weak)**

Positives:
- Stated non-executive chair with independent board members (e.g., Charles E. Ryan, Matt Weigand per Reuters profile); CEO/Chair roles appear split.
- Completed the $5.4B Russia divestment (July 2024) and cleared CEO's EU sanctions (March 2024) — a formal severance of the sanctioned-asset nexus.

Negatives (weighted heavily):
1. **Dual-class founder control:** CEO's family trust ≈ 52% of vote / ~11% economics → minority shareholders have no realistic removal or M&A recourse; classic value-of-control discount and key-person concentration risk.
2. **Material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting** (fixed-asset accounting and TripleTen revenue recognition) **remained unremediated as of December 31, 2025**, with remediation targeted by end-2026. For a company booking fast-scaling, hard-to-value GPU/fixed-asset capex and converting revenue streams, this is a live restatement/ICFR certification risk.
3. **"Controlled Company" exemptions** from Nasdaq independence requirements weaken board-leadership accountability relative to peer standards.
4. **ESG disclosure immaturity:** no climate commitments, no Scope 3, no sustainability report surfaced — behind hyperscaler/DC peers.
5. Sanctions legacy (CEO personally sanctioned 2022–2024) is a reputational and counterparty-due-diligence overhang even after legal separation.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD / ESRS (highest):** As a Dutch N.V. "large undertaking" (well over 250 employees; revenue/assets far above €50M/€25M thresholds), Nebius is in scope — first CSRD-aligned reporting covers FY2025/FY2026, requiring ESRS E1 (climate: Scope 1/2/3, transition plan), S1 (own workforce) and value-chain disclosures. With no prepared sustainability report on record, this is a compliance/readiness gap.
- **EU AI Act:** Compute-infrastructure provider to EU model developers — indirect exposure via customers' GPAI obligations and data-governance expectations.
- **US SEC climate disclosure rule (if reinstated):** Nebius files as a foreign private issuer (20-F); would trigger Scope 1/2 (and Scope 3 if material/scoped) reporting.
- **US export controls (BIS) / "AI diffusion" rules:** the binding geopolitical overlay — chip procurement and the geography of where NVIDIA GPUs can be deployed (US vs EU vs third countries) is a genuine license-to-operate constraint for a neocloud. Regulatory change here moves capacity economics.
- **CBAM:** immaterial (no primary-metals/energy-intensive manufacturing).
- **Dutch Corporate Governance Code / AFM:** applies as a Dutch N.V. listing vehicle; currently NYSE-governed as FPI.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating — **LAGGARD**

Nebius is not an "ESG Risk" name — there are no environmental or social fines, no active sanctions exposure, and no labour scandal. But it sits bottom-quartile versus AI-infrastructure/DC peers on the factors that matter: **no climate commitment or Scope 3 accounting while scaling a 5 GW (~$250B) energy-intensive build-out, dual-class founder voting control with Controlled-Company carve-outs, and open material weaknesses in financial reporting.** The governance stack is the decisive downgrader.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication — **NEUTRAL (with governance discount)**

- **Environmental factors do not block the thesis:** energy is a pass-through-ish input and Nordic siting gives cost/carbon optionality; but watch whether committed renewable PPAs keep pace with 5 GW — power-price and grid-access risk is the single largest non-financial item in this model.
- **Social factors are watch-items, not investment-drivers** at current scale (Toloka labour standards, enterprise data trust).
- **Governance is the negative:** founder-vote concentration plus open ICFR weaknesses create asymmetric downside — settling/restatement risk, dilution optics, and a governance discount that a $56B valuation leaves little room for. Until the control weaknesses are remediated and a credible transition/climate plan is published, I would (a) apply a **control-company/multi-class discount** in DCF, and (b) treat the **material-weaknesses remediation timeline (target: end-2026)** as a hard catalyst to monitor for restatement risk ahead of the next 20-F.

**Net:** Positive for the AI-infrastructure growth narrative; **neutral for the equity thesis once discounted for governance** — validate (i) ICFR remediation progress and (ii) renewable-energy contracting before adding to any position sized on the 5 GW plan.
