# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — AT&T Inc. (NYSE: T)

**Company snapshot:** Communication Services / Telecommunications Services · Market cap ~$150.1B · 133,030 employees · CEO & Chairman: John T. Stankey · Beta 0.43
**Assessment date:** Late October 2026 (data through Q3 2026 reporting)

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

| Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **CEO/Chairman duality** — Stankey elected Chairman of the Board in Feb 2025 while retaining CEO and President titles | Concentration of power. The single most important governance negative. |
| **Lead Independent Director** — William Kennard designated Lead Independent Director (Feb 2025) | Partial mitigation; provides an independent counterweight and separate meeting authority. |
| **Board independence** — cannot be fully verified (DEF 14A not retrievable via tool); no evidence of a classified/staggered board | Neutral-to-adequate; the 2026 proxy confirms an independent lead director structure exists. |
| **Compensation alignment** — 2026 outlook tied to adjusted EPS (~$2.40) and double-digit adjusted EPS CAGR through 2028; capex cut to $22–23B for 2027–28 | Incentives lean toward EPS/EBITDA and free cash flow — generally shareholder-aligned but creates incentive pressure around "adjusted" metrics and buyback-funded payouts. |
| **Insider activity** — 40 Form 4s, clustered almost entirely on the first business days of June, July, Aug, Sept and Oct 2026 | Pattern is characteristic of scheduled RSU/PSU vesting and tax withholding, not discretionary conviction trading. |

**Verdict:** The dual CEO/Chair role is a genuine governance red flag for a company of this size. The independent lead director and the presence of a Human Resources Committee with authority over executive-officer exclusions suggest functioning checks, but the concentration of agenda-setting power in one person warrants a "Weak" rating with mitigation noted. The new dual role is recent enough (18 months) that its practical effect on board challenge is untested.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**No active SEC enforcement actions** were found in EDGAR full-text search — a meaningful positive. However, the regulatory/legal docket is substantial and multi-front:

| Matter | Status & Exposure |
|---|---|
| **FCC CPNI / location-data fine ($57M)** | FCC fined AT&T for failing to vet third parties before selling customer location data. The **Fifth Circuit held the FCC's enforcement procedure violated AT&T's Seventh Amendment jury-trial right**; AT&T is seeking a refund and Supreme Court review was being sought (Case 25-406). Creates *legal uncertainty in both directions* — a refund is possible, but adverse resolution could validate a $57M+ forfeiture and set precedent for further privacy enforcement. Verizon was fined $47M in parallel. |
| **FCC 911 outage consent decree** | $950,000 civil penalty for the August 2023 911 outage. Resolved, but establishes a documented network-resilience enforcement record. |
| **Data-breach class settlement ($177M)** | Combined settlement covering the March 2024 (2019 data) and July 2024 breaches. Final approval hearing was scheduled for **January 15, 2026**; settlement status remained contested/awaited into early 2026. |
| **Connecticut AG investigation (2025)** | Investigation into unauthorized charges added to customer accounts — consumer-protection exposure with possible multi-state spillover. |
| **Whistleblower litigation (ERATE, 2008 origin)** | After ~17 years, a judge denied AT&T's motion for summary judgment, allowing the case to proceed to trial. Tail risk of an adverse False Claims Act-type outcome. |
| **Pending approvals / structural** | AT&T Mexico sale to Grupo Televisa and Fintech Holdings (announced Oct 2026) requires regulatory clearance and carries closing/execution risk. |

**Sub-rating: HIGH.** Litigation is concentrated in privacy/consumer-protection — the exact area where state AGs and the FCC are most active — and the CPNI matter is on a constitutional-law path that could produce a headline outcome either way.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Cybersecurity and data-privacy failure — recurring, not one-off.** AT&T has suffered at least three distinct customer-data compromises (2019 data disclosed March 2024; July 2024 breach; a further alleged breach reported January 2026). The pattern — repeated breaches across years despite a $177M settlement — indicates a **persistent control-environment weakness**, not a tail event. For a company holding call-detail records and location data, this is the highest-frequency, highest-reputational-cost operational risk.

2. **Legacy lead-sheathed cable remediation liability.** AT&T is a principal subject of the WSJ lead-cable investigation and has responded with voluntary blood-lead-level testing for workers handling lead-clad cable. Cost estimates range enormously — **$2–4B (Goldman Sachs/Morgan Stanley) to $59–60B (bear-case analysts)** for industry-wide removal. The 2021 precedent settlement cost only up to $1.5M, which is not a reliable guide to a national remediation program. This is a **latent, unquantified, multi-decade liability** with regulatory, tort, and ESG dimensions simultaneously. The company has not, in the materials retrieved, disclosed a remediation reserve.

3. **Network resilience and single-point infrastructure failure.** The 2023 911 outage produced an FCC consent decree and a $950K penalty. Telecom network failures now carry public-safety and regulatory consequences disproportionate to their direct cost. Related execution risk sits in the **fiber build program** — management is targeting 60M+ fiber locations by 2030 while *lowering* capex to $22–23B for 2027–28, a combination that tightens the margin for construction or supply-chain slippage.

**Secondary:** supplier concentration in radio access network equipment (limited to a handful of global vendors, with geopolitical exposure); a large unionised workforce (CWA-represented) creating strike/labour-cost risk; and integration/execution risk on the AT&T Mexico divestiture.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **ELEVATED**

- **Breach fatigue is the dominant reputational theme.** Public and forum discussion around AT&T settlements is now characterised by consumer scepticism — complaints about delayed payouts, "unwanted credit monitoring," and "AT&T breached again" framing. The $60M and $177M settlements have generated more distrust than goodwill. Settlement administration delays (final approval still pending into 2026) prolong the negative news cycle.
- **Toxic-lead legacy** is an ESG and public-health story with strong emotional and political salience; it invites congressional and state-level attention and is difficult to rebut while remediation is incomplete.
- **Consumer-protection complaints** — the Connecticut AG probe into unauthorised account charges — reinforce a "billing practices" narrative that is cheap for competitors to weaponise.
- **Competitive narrative risk:** Elon Musk's public framing that incumbents "don't understand" the Starlink threat, and a Starlink spectrum deal that visibly "spooked the entire telecom sector," means AT&T's core franchise is being narrated as structurally challenged. Management's positive Q3 (best consolidated service revenue growth in 4+ years, >1M advanced-connectivity net adds) is a genuine counterweight, but sentiment is fragile.
- **Positives:** brand remains stable and investment-grade; no consumer boycott movement identified; ESG controversy is concentrated in legacy infrastructure rather than current product.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE** (elevated, watch-listed)

**Rationale:** AT&T carries an unusually *broad* set of non-financial exposures — dual-class governance concentration, repeated cybersecurity failures, a large latent toxic-tort liability, and active privacy/consumer-protection enforcement — but these are **well-identified, financially absorbable at a $150B market cap, and largely already discounted by the market**. Critically, **no active SEC enforcement action, no accounting-irregularity finding, and no evidence of improper insider trading** were found. That combination supports "Moderate" rather than "High."

**The rating would move to HIGH on any of:** (a) an adverse Supreme Court or appellate ruling affirming the FCC's $57M forfeiture procedure; (b) a fourth confirmed customer-data breach; (c) a state AG or EPA action compelling a quantified lead-cable remediation program; or (d) a material adverse outcome in the ERATE whistleblower trial.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **⚠️ CEO/Chairman duality (Stankey, since Feb 2025)** — Concentration of board agenda-setting and management authority in one person. Escalate to governance/engagement team; raise at proxy season. Mitigant: independent Lead Director (Kennard) — confirm he sets board agendas and leads executive sessions.
2. **⚠️ Recurring cybersecurity failures** — Three-plus breaches across 2024–2026 despite a $177M settlement. This is a control-effectiveness question, not bad luck. Request management disclosure on remediation spend, CISO reporting line, and board cyber-committee structure.
3. **⚠️ Unquantified lead-sheathed cable liability** — Analyst remediation estimates span an order of magnitude ($2B to $60B). No reserve disclosed in retrieved materials. **This is the largest un-modelled non-financial risk on the book.** Escalate to the credit/valuation team; request the company's own remediation cost estimate and timeline.
4. **⚠️ FCC CPNI fine ($57M) on a constitutional-law path** — Outcome is binary and precedent-setting; monitor Case 25-406 and any Supreme Court grant.
5. **⚠️ Connecticut AG probe into unauthorised charges** — Early-stage but a potential multi-state consumer-protection action; monitor for additional state AGs joining.
