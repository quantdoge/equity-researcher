# AT&T Inc. (NYSE: T) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Sector:** Communication Services / Telecommunications Services | **HQ:** Dallas, Texas | **CIK:** 0000732717
**Fiscal reference:** FY2025 revenue $125.648B (+2.7% YoY); Q3 2026 results reported 21-Oct-2026

---

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

AT&T has completed a multi-year portfolio retreat (WarnerMedia spin-off 2022, DIRECTV deconsolidation) and is now effectively a **domestic US connectivity pure-play**. Its one remaining meaningful international operation — Mexico — is under contract for sale.

| Geography | Revenue Exposure | Basis / Source | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | ~97% of consolidated revenue | FY2025 total operating revenues $125.648B; all core Mobility, Consumer Wireline and Business segments are US-based (AT&T 2025 Annual Report; stocktitan 10-K summary) | **Low** (geopolitical) / **Medium** (regulatory concentration) |
| **Mexico (AT&T Mexico)** | ~3% of *segment* operating revenues; **<1% of total segment operating income** | AT&T 2025 Annual Report; ~24.7M wireless subscribers, 85.4B MXN consolidated revenue at YE2025 | **Medium — declining to nil** |
| **Other international** | Immaterial (<1%) | Global MNC/wholesale network customers; no disclosed material country concentration | **Low** |
| **Mexico divestiture** | Being sold to Grupo Televisa + Fintech Holdings for **$2B cash**; expected close **Q1 2027**, pending Mexican (IFT) approvals | Company announcement; MarketScreener/Bloomberg (Oct 2026) | Transition/execution risk |

**Key takeaway:** Post-close (Q1 2027), AT&T's foreign revenue exposure effectively falls to ~zero. Geographic/geopolitical risk is therefore **structurally low and improving** — unusual for a large-cap. The offsetting concentration is that ~100% of revenue sits inside one regulatory jurisdiction (US federal + 50 state regimes).

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US regulatory-regime reversal (FCC Title II / net neutrality / spectrum policy)
- **Probability:** Medium (3–5 yr horizon) | **Impact:** Medium-High
- The Sixth Circuit ruled in January 2025 that the FCC lacks authority to classify ISPs as Title II common carriers, leaving broadband as Title I (Wikipedia/Omdia 2026 US regulation overview). The current FCC under Chairman Brendan Carr is pursuing a deregulatory "Build America Agenda" and "Reforming Legacy Rules for an All-IP Future" (FCC 26-11), which is **favorable** to AT&T today.
- **Risk:** Policy is now explicitly partisan and oscillates with administrations. A future Title II reinstatement (via a differently constituted FCC or Congress) would re-impose rate/conduct regulation, constrain zero-rating and paid prioritization, and raise compliance costs. AT&T has historically lobbied both ways depending on its position (Cato Institute analysis).
- Secondary: BEAD broadband-subsidy allocation changes and federal spectrum policy (see FirstNet below) directly affect capex economics.

### Risk 2 — Trade policy / tariff-driven network equipment cost inflation
- **Probability:** High (already in effect) | **Impact:** Low-Medium
- 2026 US tariffs include a 12.5% rate on Chinese exports, with tech/IoT supply chains seeing 15–30% device-cost increases (NYT, Jul 2026; industry supply-chain analyses). AT&T is a large importer of network gear (RAN, fiber electronics, CPE) and is simultaneously running a multi-year capex-reduction program ($1B annual fiber capex cut through 2028).
- **Risk:** Tariffs squeeze equipment margins and could delay fiber/5G build schedules. Mitigant: AT&T's vendor base is diversified (Ericsson, Nokia, Samsung) and it has been reducing Chinese-sourced infrastructure exposure. No disclosed material direct tariff hit in Q3 2026 results.

### Risk 3 — Mexico divestiture execution & transition risk
- **Probability:** Low-Medium | **Impact:** Low
- The $2B sale to Grupo Televisa/Fintech Holdings needs Mexican IFT and other regulatory approvals and is not expected to close until Q1 2027. Until then AT&T carries the unit as held-for-sale.
- **Risk:** Approval delay, renegotiation, peso/FX volatility, or security/political conditions in Mexico could impair proceeds or extend losses. Impact is bounded because Mexico is <1% of segment operating income. Note Mexico's broader geopolitical backdrop (security, judicial reform, US-Mexico trade tension) is a live macro theme but has limited financial transmission to AT&T given the pending exit.

**Watch-list (not top 3):** SpaceX/Starlink Mobile is the most-cited competitive/policy threat — AT&T shares fell ~6% on 21-Oct-2026 partly on Starlink Mobile competition fears. The FCC's May 2026 approval of EchoStar's spectrum sale to both SpaceX (50 MHz) and AT&T (50 MHz) formalizes SpaceX as a facilities-based rival (Reuters, 12-May-2026). This is competitive rather than geopolitical, but it is policy-driven.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Lead-clad cable legacy liability (largest contingent exposure)**
- A 2023 Wall Street Journal investigation ("America Is Wrapped in Miles of Toxic Lead Cables") triggered environmental and shareholder litigation. The California Sportfishing Protection Alliance sued AT&T in 2021 over lead-sheathed cables in Lake Tahoe; shareholder class actions followed in 2023. AT&T has expanded voluntary blood-lead testing for technicians and publicly defends its legacy-cable program (about.att.com/legacy-cables).
- **Assessment:** Multi-year, multi-billion-dollar *industry-wide* remediation tail with uncertain apportionment; AT&T is the largest exposed carrier. No single disclosed reserve figure, but this is the key unquantified legal overhang. State AG and EPA action risk is elevated.

**2024 data breaches — largely settled, but governance signal**
- Two 2024 breaches exposed customer records (including the Snowflake-linked vendor cloud breach affecting ~109M records). A **$177M class-action settlement received final approval in October 2026**, with plaintiffs' counsel awarded $59M in fees (Reuters, 5-Oct-2026; USA Today, 6-Oct-2026).
- The **FCC Enforcement Bureau entered a $13M settlement** with AT&T over the vendor cloud breach, requiring "expansive consumer privacy upgrades" and a compliance plan (fcc.gov).
- **Assessment:** Direct financial exposure is now largely capped and reserved; the residual risk is repeat-incident/consent-decree breach, which would carry escalating penalties.

**Consumer protection / deceptive fees**
- FTC data-throttling settlement (refund program ongoing via FTC); a separate "hidden $2 monthly administrative fee" class action settled for ~$14M. These reflect persistent state-AG and FTC scrutiny of fee disclosure and "unlimited" marketing claims.

**Regulatory items**
- **EchoStar spectrum acquisition (~$23B, 50 MHz):** FCC approved 12-May-2026 alongside SpaceX's purchase — regulatory risk **resolved**, but capital intensity and integration remain.
- **FirstNet:** $2B NTIA agreement (2026) under which AT&T reduces FirstNet-borne costs by ~$1B; D.C. Circuit upheld the FCC's FirstNet spectrum plan (3-0). This is a **politically dependent federal contract** — a change in federal priorities could affect economics.
- **Lumen consumer fiber:** Reports of exclusive talks for a >$5.5B acquisition. If pursued, this would face DOJ/FCC antitrust review and could attract conditions — a **forward-looking regulatory approval risk**.
- **Net neutrality:** Currently deregulatory tailwind (Title I; Sixth Circuit 2025), but reversal risk as above.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Data privacy & cybersecurity governance (Red):** Two 2024 breaches plus an FCC privacy settlement indicate control weaknesses at vendor/cloud boundaries. Highest-probability source of repeat regulatory action.
2. **Consumer protection / fee transparency (Amber):** Repeated FTC and class-action actions over throttling and administrative fees — suggests marketing/pricing practices remain a live enforcement target.
3. **Environmental legacy compliance (Amber-Red):** Lead-sheathed cable remediation; potential EPA/state enforcement and disclosure obligations.
4. **Government-contract & foreign-official touchpoints (Amber):** FirstNet (federal) and, until Q1 2027, AT&T Mexico create FCPA/public-procurement exposure. *No FCPA/OFAC/sanctions violation was identified in this review* — but the absence of a disclosed case is not evidence of absence given limited public data.
5. **Routine SEC filings:** Frequent Form 4 insider filings around Q3 2026 — routine, not a red flag.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** AT&T's **geopolitical exposure is Low** — it is a near-pure US business, and the pending $2B Mexico sale (Q1 2027) removes essentially all foreign revenue. There is no material Russia, China, or emerging-market operating exposure, and no sanctions/FCPA matter surfaced. However, the **domestic legal and regulatory stack is heavy and rising**: lead-cable legacy liability (unquantified, multi-billion tail), privacy enforcement after repeated 2024 breaches, consumer-protection class actions, and a partisan, oscillating FCC/spectrum policy regime. The rating is therefore driven by **US regulatory concentration and litigation overhang**, not by cross-border geopolitical instability.

**Key monitorables:** (1) lead-cable remediation cost estimates and state AG suits; (2) IFT approval and closing of the Mexico sale; (3) FCC treatment of the Lumen fiber deal if signed; (4) any post-2028 change in Title II/net-neutrality posture; (5) tariff impact on network-equipment capex.
