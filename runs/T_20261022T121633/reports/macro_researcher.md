# Macroeconomic Assessment — AT&T Inc. (NYSE: T)
**As of 22 October 2026 | Price $21.91 | Mkt cap $150.1B | Beta 0.43**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional / "Late-Cycle Re-Tightening"**

The US economy is **not** in a risk-off regime, but it has shifted out of the 2025 disinflation trade into a **rates-led, fiscal-premium regime**. Three features define the backdrop:

- **Growth is intact but decelerating.** Real GDP rose to $24,408.0B (chained 2017$) in Q2-2026, **+2.2% YoY** and **+2.2% annualised QoQ** — a third consecutive ~2–2.5% quarter. No recession signal in activity data.
- **Disinflation has stalled and reversed.** CPI rose to 336.30 in Sep-2026 from 332.57 in Jun-2026 — a **+1.12% three-month move (~4.6% annualised)**. YoY CPI is **3.72%** (Sep-2026 vs 324.25), up from 3.30% in Jul-2026. The Fed's 2025 cuts (effective funds 4.33% Jan-2025 → 3.63% Jan-2026) have been partly walked back: the effective rate **rose to 3.75% in Sep-2026**, consistent with a 25bp hike at the September FOMC.
- **The long end is doing the tightening.** The 10Y yield rose from 4.36% (6-May-2026) to **5.12% (20-Oct)** — ~+76bp — while 10Y breakevens only moved from ~2.20% (28-Jul) to **2.37%**. So **~47bp of the move is real yield / term premium**, not inflation expectations. The curve is **bear-steepening**: 2Y 4.65%, 5Y 4.80%, 10Y 4.99%, 30Y 5.37%, **2s10s +34bp** (positive since 2025 — no inversion signal).

**Credit conditions remain easy in absolute terms but are deteriorating at the margin:** IG OAS **0.87%** (20-Oct) vs 0.73% (1-Jun); HY OAS **3.33%** vs 2.63% (31-Aug) — a +70bp widening in eight weeks. Tight spreads with rising long yields is the signature of a **fiscal/term-premium shock, not a credit event**. That matters for AT&T: its refinancing window is still open, but the price of long-duration money is rising.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

AT&T is a **domestic, subscription-revenue, capital-intensive, highly leveraged bond proxy** (beta 0.43; ~96% US revenue; ~$120B net debt, company-reported and not re-verified here). Its macro sensitivity is therefore concentrated in three channels: the **long end of the curve**, **housing turnover**, and **telecom pricing power vs. CPI**.

| Macro variable | Impact | Transmission mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **10Y/30Y yields at 5.0–5.4%, bear-steepening** | **Negative (primary)** | Dividend yield ~5.1% ($1.11 DPS / $21.91) is now at **parity with the 10Y**. The historic "yield support" floor that cushioned T is gone; required-yield repricing caps the multiple (currently ~8.5x fwd P/E). Rising 30Y also lifts the discount rate on long-duration, low-growth cash flows. |
| **Fed funds 3.75% and rising; 2Y 4.65%** | **Modest negative** | ~$120B debt: each 100bp on the whole stack ≈ $1.2B pre-tax. But average maturity is long (12yr+), so near-term refinancing is only ~$10–15B/yr → ~$120–150M pre-tax per 100bp — immaterial to EPS. The valuation channel dominates the P&L channel. |
| **IG OAS 0.87% (still tight)** | **Neutral-positive** | Access to investment-grade capital remains open; AT&T can term out debt without a credit event. Watch the 14bp widening trend — a move to 1.50%+ would meaningfully raise the marginal cost of the fiber build. |
| **CPI 3.72% and re-accelerating** | **Positive (net)** | Telecom is a **price-setter**: AT&T has demonstrated wireless/fiber price increases that flow straight to ARPU and EBITDA (Q3-2026 EBITDA +7.3% YoY). Wage and network-cost inflation are the offset, but pricing power exceeds input inflation. Note the reflexivity — AT&T is partly *causing* the sticky-services CPI the Fed is fighting. |
| **Real GDP +2.2%, unemployment 4.2%** | **Positive** | Subscription demand is income-inelastic; 4.2% unemployment supports gross adds, low churn and device upgrade cycles. Low elasticity means downside is limited if growth slows to ~1%. |
| **Housing turnover frozen by 5%+ long rates (30Y mortgage ~6.5–7%)** | **Negative** | Broadband gross adds are historically correlated with household moves. High mortgage rates lock in existing homeowners → fewer moves → softer fiber/DSL gross adds. This is AT&T's most under-appreciated macro drag; it is currently being masked by share gains (**769k internet net adds in Q3-2026**). |
| **USD +2.9% since May (broad index 121.4)** | **Slightly negative** | Latin America segment (~$4B revenue, Mexico/Unefon) translates lower. ~2–3% of revenue; debt is USD-denominated so no FX liability mismatch. Low single-digit EPS sensitivity — not a thesis driver. |
| **Tariffs / import costs** | **Negative (tail)** | Handsets and network equipment are imported; tariffs raise device COGS (low-margin) and fiber capex. Quantification unavailable in this dataset. |
| **Rising 30Y and pension discount rates** | **Small positive** | AT&T's large defined-benefit pension deficit shrinks as the discount rate rises — a modest balance-sheet offset not in consensus models. |

**Important nuance:** the textbook T-vs-10Y inverse correlation **broke down** in 2026 — T rallied from $19.41 (2-Jul) to $26.42 (15-Sep) *while* the 10Y rose ~40bp. Company-specific catalysts (fiber growth, raised guidance, accelerated buybacks) overwhelmed the rate effect. Macro is a **multiple cap, not a directional driver** over 1–3 month horizons — but it reasserts itself at extremes. The two recent air-pockets (−9.8% on 9-Oct, −6.4% on 21-Oct) were competition-driven (SpaceX/Starlink Mobile), not macro.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL — tilted Unfavourable**

**Why not Favourable:** the single highest-beta macro variable for T — the 10Y — sits at 5.0–5.4% with a bear-steepening bias and ~90bp of additional hikes already priced into the 2Y. With dividend yield ≈ 10Y yield, there is no valuation cushion left. The Fed has resumed hiking into a 3.72% CPI, and 2027 consensus cuts are at risk.

**Why not Unfavourable:** the operating backdrop is genuinely supportive — ~2.2% growth, 4.2% unemployment, tight IG spreads, and an inflation rate that AT&T can pass through. Revenue is subscription-based and recession-resistant. Net debt is long-dated and 2027–28 capex is guided down ($1B reduction through 2028), so the FCF trajectory improves even if rates stay high.

**Net:** macro is a **ceiling on re-rating, not a threat to the earnings base**. The stock is a rate-duration instrument trading at 8.5x with a 5.1% yield; the macro setup says the yield support is real but the multiple expansion is capped until the long end breaks below ~4.50%.

## 5. The Material Surprise Scenario — **"Term-Premium Shock" (bear, ~20% odds)**

Consensus is a soft landing: GDP ~2%, spreads tight (IG 87bp, HY 333bp), inflation drifting to target. The non-consensus risk is that **fiscal supply and re-accelerating services inflation push the 30Y through 6.00% and the 10Y to 5.75–6.00%** by mid-2027, forcing the Fed to 4.25–4.50% rather than cutting.

Quantified transmission to T:
- **Dividend-yield repricing:** at a 5.75% required yield, $1.11 DPS implies **$19.30 (−12%)**; at 6.00%, **$18.50 (−16%)**. A retest of the July low ($19.41) becomes base case, not tail.
- **Cost of capital:** ~$12B of annual refinancing at +100bp ≈ $120M pre-tax, ~$0.01/share — trivial. The damage is entirely in the multiple and in the cost of the fiber build, not the income statement.
- **Housing channel:** mortgage rates near 7.25% would cut household mobility further, shaving broadband gross adds and pushing the 2027 fiber-add guide down.

**The asymmetry to note:** the mirror-image bull case (a growth scare that drags the 10Y to 4.25% while AT&T holds its EBITDA guide) is worth **+13% to ~$24.70** on the same dividend-yield arithmetic. Given the stock is already down 12% over 12 months and 23% from its March high of $28.43, the risk/reward on a duration rally is at least as large as on the term-premium shock — but the *macro* catalyst for that rally is not yet in place.
