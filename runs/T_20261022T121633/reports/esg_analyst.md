# AT&T Inc. (NYSE: T) — Full ESG Analysis
**Sector:** Communication Services / Telecommunications Services · **Market cap:** ~$150B · **Price:** $21.91 · **Employees:** 133,030 · **CEO:** John T. Stankey (Chairman, CEO & President)

**Evidence base:** AT&T 2026 proxy (filed 23 Mar 2026) via secondary summary and board disclosures; AT&T Corporate Responsibility KPIs and Efficiency & Emissions disclosures (updated 17 Jul 2026, 2025 data, ERM CVS limited assurance); 2026 annual meeting results; Reuters/FCC/WSJ reporting on data breach, DEI, lead cables and labor. *Data gaps flagged at the end — the 10-K and DEF 14A full texts were not retrievable (SEC 403).*

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (operational) — **HIGH** on legacy contamination tail

**Positives**
- Absolute footprint falling: global Scope 1+2+3 **11.67M MT CO₂e in 2025** vs 18.91M in 2022 (**−38%**); Scope 1 **526,205 MT** (−43% vs 2022); Scope 2 market-based **3.31M MT** (−14%).
- **GHG intensity 30,521 MT CO₂e per $B revenue in 2025**, down from 39,574 in 2022 (**−23%**), and 24.05 MT per 1,000 subscribers vs 32.89 in 2022.
- Credible targets: **Scope 1+2 carbon neutral by 2035**, SBTi-approved 1.5°C interim **−63% by 2030 vs 2015**; Scope 2 **−90% by 2035**; fleet **−76% by 2035**. Offsets are *not* currently counted in the footprint — a credibility positive.
- GHG data carries **limited third-party assurance (ERM CVS)**; TCFD, GRI and SASB reporting, plus 2025 scenario analysis refresh.
- Copper-to-fibre transition structurally lowers network energy; "Gigaton Goal" (1bn t customer-enabled savings by 2035) is a commercial opportunity narrative.

**Negatives**
- **Intensity is high versus peers.** Third-party compilation puts 2024 Scope 1 intensity at ~4.46 tCO₂e per $M revenue versus a telecom peer median of ~1.97 — roughly **2.3x the peer median**. Even on AT&T's own blended metric (~30.5 t/$M), this is an energy-heavy asset base.
- **Renewables penetration is only 23.9% of total energy** (26.7% of electricity) — low for a large-cap telecom, and **data-centre renewable share fell to 25.1% from 49.5% in 2022** while **data-centre PUE worsened to 1.74 from 1.60**. This is moving the wrong way exactly as AI/fibre load grows.
- Total energy consumption **rose** to 15.0M MWh in 2025 from 14.8M, and grid dependence rose to 84.3% — decoupling from revenue growth is not yet achieved.
- **Lead-clad cable overhang:** WSJ's 2023 investigation identified lead-sheathed legacy plant; national removal cost estimates run as high as **~$59B** (industry-wide). AT&T has settled only narrowly (2021 Lake Tahoe agreement, up to **$1.5M**), denies contamination, says it has mapped affected locations, and has **not accrued a material liability**. It also obtained SEC no-action relief to exclude a lead-cable shareholder proposal. This is an unquantified, low-probability/high-severity liability — the single largest E-tail risk in the name.
- Physical climate exposure (Gulf Coast hurricanes, wildfire zones, drought) is material to network opex; AT&T self-rates adaptive capacity "high / low residual risk," which is an unsupported self-assessment given 2024–2026 storm outage frequency.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

**Labour — the dominant issue**
- **43% of employees are under collective bargaining** (a very high ratio for a US corporate). Workforce has fallen **160,700 (2022) → 133,030 (2025)**, i.e. **−17%**, with US employees down from 136,699 to 109,347.
- **August 2024: ~17,000 Southeast employees struck** over unfair labour practices. CWA has since taken **Mobility strike authorisation** (95%+ yes) and alleges **8,000+ call-centre job cuts and offshoring to Mexico**. Contract cycles at Southwest/Mobility remain live risks into 2026–27.
- **Employee engagement fell to 79% in 2025** from 82% in both 2022 and 2023 — directionally consistent with the labour dispute and headcount reduction.
- Mitigants: median employee pay **$139,026**, healthcare/wellbeing offered to ~0.8M employees, retirees and dependents, ~$10M tuition assistance, 99.8% code-of-conduct training completion.

**Diversity, equity and inclusion — a deliberate reversal**
- In **December 2025 AT&T committed to ending DEI programmes**, confirming to FCC Chair Brendan Carr that it "does not and will not have any roles focused on DEI," removing DEI-linked training and race/sex-based hiring quotas. The **NYC Comptroller and pension funds publicly objected**; NYC pension funds **sued AT&T in February 2026** over exclusion of an EEO-1 workforce-diversity proposal from the 2026 proxy. The company effectively traded social-investor goodwill for regulatory goodwill (its **UScellular acquisition was cleared by the FCC** in the same period).
- Consequence: EEO-1 pay/workforce disclosure is no longer reliably available to investors — a disclosure regression, and a governance issue as much as a social one.

**Data privacy / product safety — highest financial materiality**
- Two breaches exposing customer data (including the 2024 vendor-cloud/Snowflake incident affecting ~109M customers) produced a **$177M class settlement granted final approval 2 Oct 2026**, plus a separate **$13M FCC settlement** for the vendor cloud breach. Total quantified cost ~$190M, excluding remediation, credit monitoring and reputational churn. Given AT&T's data assets, recurrence risk is structural, not one-off.

**Community / digital divide — positive**
- ~$72M corporate and foundation giving in 2025, 36% employee volunteering, Connected Learning Centers, Access from AT&T low-cost programme — genuine social-licence assets with state/federal subsidy relevance.

**Not obtained:** injury/TRIR and fatality data (high-hazard tower and field work), diversity representation percentages post-2025, customer satisfaction/churn linkage. These are material gaps for a full S assessment.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Board composition** | 10 nominees, all re-elected 14 May 2026. Governance guidelines require a "substantial majority" independent; **William Kennard** serves as **Independent Lead Director** (and was independent Chairman Jan 2021–Feb 2025). Specific independence count not confirmed — *gap*. |
| **Chair/CEO duality** | **Negative and recent.** Stankey became **Chairman, CEO and President in February 2025**, collapsing the independent-chair structure AT&T had maintained since 2021. A material regression in checks and balances. |
| **Compensation alignment** | Mixed-to-positive. CEO 2025 total **$29.9M** (+13% YoY), **65% equity** ($19.5M), with ROIC, EPS growth and relative TSR metrics — reasonable long-horizon alignment. But **CEO:median pay ratio 215:1** is high, and the 2021 say-on-pay was *defeated*. Current support **90.7%** shows recovery. |
| **Shareholder rights** | **Weak.** 2026 meeting had only two proposals, both defeated: written-consent right (**67.9% against**) and EEO-1 diversity disclosure (excluded pre-vote; litigation followed). No proxy access evident. Management-favourable proxy machinery. |
| **Ethics & transparency** | **Strong.** 99.8% code training, political engagement report, transparency report, ERM-integrated climate risk, independent lead director with FCC/telecom regulatory depth. |
| **Capital allocation vs ESG** | Aggressive buybacks (~$8B planned 2026) plus a ~$45B 2026–28 return plan, alongside **$22.4B capex** — leverage near ~2.5x. Financial flexibility to absorb a lead-cable shock is limited. |

**Rationale for 5/10:** A genuinely independent, well-credentialed board and strong ethics/reporting culture are offset by reinstated duality, restricted shareholder rights, exclusion of a diversity proposal, high pay ratio and a leverage profile that leaves little room for contingent liabilities. Governance is **average but deteriorating**.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE (weak end) — negative momentum**

**Why not a Leader:** high energy/carbon intensity versus telecom peers, only ~24% renewable energy, worsening data-centre efficiency, and an unresolved lead-cable liability.

**Why not a Laggard:** genuine SBTi-aligned 2035 neutrality target with falling absolute and intensity emissions, assured GHG data, strong ethics/compliance and disclosure, independent lead director, and 90.7% say-on-pay support.

**Momentum is negative:** 2025 reinstated chair/CEO duality; December 2025 DEI elimination plus litigation over excluded disclosure; labour unrest; two data breaches; renewable share and PUE moving backwards. Third-party calibration: Sustainalytics ESG Risk **22.1 = Medium Risk**.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL to NEGATIVE**

**Not thesis-breaking — but a modest valuation discount is warranted.** ESG is not the primary driver for a domestic telecom; the investment case rests on fibre/advanced-connectivity subscriber growth, ~$5B quarterly FCF and a ~5% dividend yield at ~8.5x forward earnings. But three ESG items touch cash flow and cost of capital directly:

1. **Lead-clad cable is the binary risk.** An unaccrued, unquantified contingent liability sitting against a $150B market cap and ~2.5x leverage. Even a $2–3B remediation outcome would consume roughly half a year of FCF and threaten the buyback cadence. Low probability, high severity — this is the item to underwrite.
2. **Data privacy is a recurring cost line, not a one-off.** ~$190M in settlements across two incidents, with ~109M records exposed. Budget ongoing penalties/ remediation into the cost base.
3. **Governance regression and DEI reversal cut both ways.** The chair/CEO combination and restricted shareholder rights argue for a small governance discount; the DEI reversal reduces FCC friction (a real, near-term regulatory benefit) at the cost of institutional demand. Net effect on valuation is close to offsetting, but it raises headline/political volatility.

**Offsetting positives:** assured emissions data, credible 2035 targets, high unionised pay/benefits, strong ethics training and community investment, and a genuine commercial "Gigaton" enabled-emissions franchise.

**Positioning view:** **Neutral on ESG grounds**; ESG does not add to the buy case and the lead-cable tail argues for sizing discipline and monitoring of 10-K legal-proceeding language and any state AG action. Watch items: (i) any lead-cable reserve or AG suit, (ii) Mobility/Southwest contract outcomes, (iii) 2027 proxy treatment of an independent-chair or EEO-1 proposal, (iv) data-centre renewable share/PUE trajectory as AI load scales.
