# AT&T Inc. (T) — Financial Risk Assessment

**As of:** FY2025 year-end (period end 2025-12-31, filed 2026-02-09) | Price $21.91 | Market cap ~$150.1bn | Sector: Communication Services / Telecom Services

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: Investment Grade — lower tier (BBB-/BBB equivalent)**

Confirmed agency ratings from AT&T's investor relations page and rating agency releases:

| Agency | Long-Term Issuer Rating | Outlook |
|---|---|---|
| Moody's | Baa2 | Stable |
| S&P | BBB | Stable |
| Fitch | BBB+ | **Negative** (placed on Negative Watch 28-Aug-2025 following spectrum acquisition) |

The balance sheet is solidly investment grade but sits at the bottom of the IG band. The Fitch Negative Watch tied to the spectrum acquisition is the single most important forward-looking credit signal — it explicitly flags re-leveraging risk at the same time the market is pricing higher refinancing costs. Absolute leverage is high in dollar terms ($174.0bn total debt) but **moderate relative to telecom peers**: T's 2.85x net debt/EBITDA compares favourably with Verizon at 3.80x and T-Mobile at 3.73x. The weaker spot is coverage: T's 3.67x interest coverage is the lowest of the three (VZ 4.37x, TMUS 5.33x), meaning less cushion per dollar of debt than peers.

⚠️ **Data-quality caveat on the leverage ratio:** reported EBITDA of $54.70bn does not reconcile to operating income + D&A ($25.00bn + $20.89bn = $45.89bn). Using the reconciled figure, net debt/EBITDA is **~3.39x**, not 2.85x. Peer comparisons were computed on the same reported-EBITDA basis, so the relative ranking holds, but the absolute leverage picture is meaningfully worse than the headline ratio suggests. This should be treated as an unresolved data gap.

**Interpretation:** The two beta readings disagree sharply (–0.38 over 365 days vs. 0.43 over five years). The near-term negative beta is a regime artefact — AT&T has traded as a rate-sensitive, defensive carry vehicle whose price has moved inversely to the index as rates and growth-stock rotation dominated. That decorrelation is **not** a stable risk-reduction property; it reflects duration-like behaviour where rising yields compress the valuation. The negative Sharpe of –0.61 confirms that over the trailing year, total return did not compensate for the 27.3% volatility; the ~5.4% dividend yield did not prevent a –28.9% drawdown. Market risk is therefore **elevated for a utility-like business**, driven primarily by rate sensitivity and ~$174bn of debt duration rather than by operating cyclicality.

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue –20%

Assumption set: revenue falls 20% (–$25.1bn) to ~$100.5bn. Telco cost structure is ~60% fixed (network, spectrum, headcount), so I model two decremental-margin cases. Refinancing cost rises to ~5.0% on rolled maturities.

| Line item | Base FY2025 | Stress A (55% decremental margin) | Stress B (70% decremental margin) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $125.6bn | $100.5bn | $100.5bn |
| EBITDA | $54.7bn | $40.9bn (–25%) | $37.1bn (–32%) |
| D&A | $20.9bn | $20.9bn | $20.9bn |
| EBIT | $33.8bn | $20.0bn | $16.2bn |
| Interest expense | $6.80bn | $7.50bn (partial refi at 5%) | $7.50bn |
| **Interest coverage** | **4.97x** | **2.67x** | **2.16x** |
| **Net debt / EBITDA** | **2.85x** | **3.81x** | **4.20x** |
| Operating cash flow | $40.3bn | ~$27.5bn | ~$24.0bn |
| Capex (cut) | $20.8bn | $18.0bn | $16.0bn |
| **Free cash flow** | **$19.4bn** | **~$9.5bn** | **~$8.0bn** |
| Dividends | $8.18bn | $8.18bn | $8.18bn |
| **FCF / dividend cover** | **2.38x** | **1.16x** | **0.98x** |
| Buybacks | $6.58bn | $0 | $0 |

**Findings:**

- **Stress A (moderate):** Survivable without a dividend cut. FCF covers the dividend 1.16x, buybacks are eliminated entirely, and net leverage rises to 3.81x — roughly in line with Verizon's current 3.80x. Interest coverage falls to 2.67x, which is below investment-grade comfort but not covenant-threatening.
- **Stress B (severe):** The dividend is **uncovered** (0.98x FCF cover). A cut becomes mandatory, not discretionary. Net leverage at 4.20x breaches the ~4.0–4.5x maintenance threshold typically embedded in telecom revolving credit facilities (⚠️ *assumed — no covenant documentation was available*), putting the revolver at risk precisely when liquidity is needed.
- **Liquidity in stress:** $18.2bn cash plus positive FCF means no cash-runway problem — this is not a burn business. But the current ratio of 0.91x and negative $5.05bn working capital mean there is no buffer to absorb a working-capital swing. Near-term debt maturities are modest: $9.01bn short-term debt is covered 2.0x by cash alone. The real exposure is not the 2026 maturity but the ~$146bn long-term stack rolling over at ~4.80–4.99% versus a 3.91% blended cost — each 100bp of refinancing spread adds roughly **$1.5–1.7bn** of annual interest, mechanically reducing coverage from 4.97x toward ~3.6x with no operating deterioration at all.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating

# MODERATE

**Rationale:** AT&T is not a solvency risk. It generates $19.4bn of FCF, covers its dividend 2.4x, holds $18.2bn of cash against only $9.0bn of near-term maturities, and carries lower relative leverage than both major peers. Piotroski at 8/9 confirms improving fundamentals. The rating is held at *Moderate* rather than *Low* by three specific factors: (i) interest coverage of 3.67x is the weakest in its peer group and falls to ~2.7x under stress; (ii) a BBB/BBB+ rating with a Negative Watch means the IG cushion is thin, and a downgrade to BBB- would raise funding costs across $174bn of debt; (iii) the Altman Z-Score of 0.87 is the lowest of the three major telcos. The business is durable but financially leveraged, and its equity behaves more like a rate-duration instrument than a defensive stock.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Fitch Negative Watch (Aug 2025) tied to spectrum acquisition** — explicit re-leveraging signal. Total debt rose $33.1bn in FY2025 ($140.9bn → $174.0bn) while cash rose $14.9bn; net debt increased $18.1bn. Leverage is heading the wrong way at the exact moment ratings outlook turned negative.
2. **The apparent leverage improvement may be accounting-driven.** Net debt/EBITDA "improved" from 3.13x to 2.85x only because reported EBITDA jumped 24% ($44.0bn → $54.7bn) on 2.7% revenue growth — while operating income rose just 1% and D&A was flat. Reported EBITDA does not reconcile to EBIT + D&A. On a reconciled basis leverage is ~3.39x, essentially unchanged. Treat the headline deleveraging as unproven.
3. **Coverage is the binding constraint, not leverage.** 3.67x operating-income coverage vs. 4.37x at Verizon and 5.33x at T-Mobile. With a 3.91% blended cost against a 4.80% 5Y Treasury and 5.37% 30Y, the refinancing tailwind of the last decade has become a headwind.
4. **FCF margin erosion despite revenue growth.** FCF margin fell 16.7% (2023) → 15.1% (2024) → 15.5% (2025) as capex climbed 17.9 → 20.8bn. Absolute FCF is flat-to-down ($20.5bn → $18.5bn → $19.4bn) while revenue grew $3.3bn. Capital intensity is rising faster than the top line.
5. **Negative working capital of –$5.05bn and current ratio 0.91x.** Structurally normal for telecom but leaves zero slack; a 20% revenue shock consumes the entire cushion.
6. **Altman Z-Score 0.87 — deepest distress-zone reading in the peer group** (VZ 1.11, TMUS 1.03). Model limitations acknowledged, but the relative ranking is informative.
7. **Negative Sharpe ratio (–0.61) and –28.9% max drawdown.** A ~5.4% dividend yield did not offset capital loss over the trailing year; equity holders are bearing equity-like volatility for bond-like operating risk.

## Data Gaps & Caveats (explicitly flagged)

- **Credit ratings and debt maturity ladder:** obtained from AT&T investor relations and rating agency releases via search. A dedicated web search for the full maturity schedule **timed out on retry** — the maturity ladder by year is not verified, only the $9.01bn short-term and $146.0bn long-term split from the balance sheet.
- **CDS spreads:** not available from the toolset. No market-implied default signal was obtained.
- **Covenant terms:** unavailable. The 4.0–4.5x maintenance threshold referenced in the stress case is an industry convention, **not** a documented AT&T covenant. Treat covenant breach conclusions as illustrative.
- **Goodwill/intangibles anomaly:** FY2024 goodwill & intangibles of $195.7bn falls to $68.7bn in FY2025 while total assets *rise*. This is almost certainly a reclassification or held-for-sale treatment, not an impairment, but it is unexplained in the data. It does not affect leverage ratios but does affect asset-based measures.
- **FY2022 income statement inconsistency:** operating income reported as –$4.59bn while EBIT is shown as +$3.01bn. FY2024/FY2025 cost-of-revenue classification also shifts materially ($69.8bn → $50.8bn). Gross-margin trend analysis across years is therefore unreliable; the Piotroski gross-margin-improvement point may be an artefact of this reclassification.
- **Quarterly data not pulled** — the cash-runway analysis relies on annual figures; intra-year seasonality in working capital is not captured. This is not a burn business, so runway risk is low regardless.
- **No peer FCF yields or market-risk metrics** were computed for VZ/TMUS, so the 12.4% FCF yield and the volatility/Sharpe figures are standalone, not benchmarked.
