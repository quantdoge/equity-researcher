# Humana Inc. (HUM) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price: $385.78 (last close, Nov 18 2026) | Market cap $46.3B | EV ≈ $51.9B**
*Data note: the vendor's "current price" snapshot (386) is consistent with the Nov 18, 2026 close; all multiples below are recomputed on 120.0M shares (9/30/26) and the latest quarterly balance sheet. Peer comparison in the sector file lumps HUM with drug majors, so I used UNH/CNC as the true managed-care comps.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — **Moat Rating: NONE**

Humana is essentially a **single-product, government-funded business**: individual Medicare Advantage (MA) is the overwhelming majority of earnings, with small Group/Specialty and healthcare-services appendages. Volume: ~$155B revenue in FY2026E, ~0.8% net margin.

**The moat test fails on every axis:**

| Moat source | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Pricing power | **None.** CMS sets the rate. The CY2027 MA rate announcement was **+2.48%** against core medical trend guided at **8–9%** — a structural margin squeeze management cannot price away. |
| Intangible assets (Star ratings) | The one real edge — and it **proved fragile**. The 2026 Star collapse removed ~$3.5B of quality-bonus revenue and turned 2026 into a near-zero-earnings year. Ratings improved for 2027 (six plans at 4.5 stars; ~95% of members in 4+ star plans vs ~20%), but the bonus only lands in 2028. A moat that disappears every rating cycle is not a moat. |
| Scale/cost advantage | **Disadvantage.** UNH has ~10x the membership over which to spread fixed costs, clinical infrastructure and SG&A; Humana is a price-taker at ~1% operating margin. |
| Switching costs | Low for members (annual enrollment), high for the company (nothing). |
| Regulatory asset | **Actively contested.** The proposed federal "CRUSH" rule (Nov 18, 2026) targets MA risk-adjustment/fraud reporting — the very coding lever MA economics depend on. The stock fell **13% in one session** on that headline alone, which tells you what the market thinks the moat is made of. |

**Has the moat widened or narrowed over five years? Sharply narrowed.** Revenue is up ~55% since 2022, yet net income fell from $2.81B (2022) to $1.19B (2025). That is the definition of a business losing economic ground while growing.

**The ROIC evidence is decisive:**

| | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROIC | 13.1% | 11.6% | 5.7% | **4.7%** |
| ROE | 18.3% | 15.3% | 7.3% | 6.7% |
| Operating margin | 3.84% | 3.12% | 1.38% | **1.12%** |

At 4.7% ROIC against a ~9% cost of capital, Humana is **destroying capital** today. For contrast, UNH earned 10.5% ROIC in the same punishing 2025 environment.

**Circle of competence:** adjacent to it. I can understand the mechanics (benefit ratio, Stars, risk adjustment, rate notices) but I cannot forecast the earnings stream — the three inputs that determine profit (CMS rates, Star ratings, medical trend) are all set by third parties and have swung by billions of dollars within 24 months. Unpredictable earnings = unvaluable business.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **3 / 10**

**What's good:**
- Revenue predictability/growth: 10.9% 5-yr CAGR; 10.1% in 2025, ~$155B FY2026E.
- Liquidity/leverage manageable: total debt $12.5B vs $25.1B cash & investments; net debt $5.57B; debt/equity ~66%. A $1.5B debt tender in Nov 2026 is sensible deleveraging.
- Book equity grew to $18.9B ($157/share).

**What's bad:**
- **Margin collapse:** gross margin 18.5% (2022) → 14.5% (2025); operating margin 4.1% → 1.1%.
- **Earnings:** $2.81B (2022) → $2.49B (2023) → $1.21B (2024) → $1.19B (2025). FY2026 GAAP guidance was cut to "at least $6.52" in Q2; adjusted EPS guided to ≥$9.50 after Q3 (9M GAAP EPS $16.99, with a Q4 consensus loss of ~$7.60 — Q4 2025 was −$6.61).
- **FCF conversion is unreliable:** FCF $3.45B (2022), $2.98B (2023), $2.39B (2024), **$0.375B (2025)**; 9M 2026 $2.60B — but that includes a +$1.72B working-capital swing in Q3 against a −$1.80B WC drag in 2025. This is premium/MLR timing, not durable cash generation. Four-year average FCF ≈ **$1.9–2.3B**, ~1.5% of revenue.
- **Balance-sheet quality:** goodwill & intangibles $10.5B = **56% of equity**; tangible book ≈ $70/share (vs $386 price = 5.5x).
- **Owner earnings** (NI + D&A − capex): $2.51B (2022) → $2.40B (2023) → $1.60B (2024) → **$1.47B (2025)**.

Humana is the **most expensive** of the three on earnings and cash flow while earning the **worst ROIC**. There is no valuation support here: 40x trough earnings, 5.5x tangible book, sub-1% yield with a frozen dividend ($0.885/qtr since early 2024).

**Intrinsic value (owner-earnings DCF, 9.5% discount, 3% terminal growth):**

- **Bull (20%)** — margin recovers to the 3%+ operating target by 2028, Stars bonus restores pricing, rate notices stay ≥2.5%: owner earnings → $3.5B; **IV ≈ $520**
- **Base (45%)** — Stars recovery delivers, margin recovers to ~2.5% by 2028 but medical trend (8–9%) caps upside: OE → $3.0B, 4% growth; **IV ≈ $340**
- **Bear (35%)** — CRUSH rule/risk-adjustment enforcement, 2028 rate pressure, Stars slippage; OE ~$1.5B; **IV ≈ $190**

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $325/share.**
Required buy price with a 25% margin of safety: **≈ $244.**

**The current price fails the test.** At $386 the market is paying full freight for a successful 2028 margin recovery — my base-case DCF, built on that recovery *succeeding*, produces roughly today's price. That is the opposite of a margin of safety: you collect the upside only if everything goes right, and you own the downside if it doesn't.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **4 / 10**

- **CEO James Rechtin** (since 2024) inherited the Stars/trend problem. Execution in 2026 has been credible: three guidance raises/beats, benefit ratio 93.7% vs "slightly above 94%" guide, FY26 adjusted EPS raised to ≥$9.50, and a $1.5B debt tender.
- **But credibility has been damaged repeatedly:** the Jan 2024 collapse, the 2024 Q4 loss, the 2025 Stars downgrade, and the FY2026 GAAP guide cut from $8.36 to $6.52. Guidance is being managed quarter to quarter rather than from a position of strength.
- **Capital allocation is the weak spot.** Humana repurchased **$2.10B of stock in 2022 and $1.57B in 2023** near $400–500/share, then effectively stopped ($0.8B in 2024, ~$150M in 2025, ~$110M in 2026). That is buying high and stopping low — precisely backwards. It also consumed the balance-sheet flexibility that a shock-prone business needs. Dividends have been frozen at $0.885/quarter for ~3 years.
- Insider ownership is low (typical of this sector), and the business has no owner-operator dynamic. No empire-building M&A is a modest positive.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

Not because Humana is a fraud or a bad company — it is a real $155B-revenue franchise with genuine customer value — but because it is a **no-moat, single-product, government-price-regulated business currently earning 4.7% on capital, trading at 40x trough earnings and 2.5x book on a recovery that is already fully priced in.**

Buffett's test: *"a wonderful business at a fair price."* HUM is a fair business at a demanding price. I would need ~$245–260 — a 35%+ discount to base-case intrinsic value — before the asymmetry compensates for the policy and medical-trend risk. Until then, this is a macro/CMS bet dressed as a value stock.

**What would change my mind (upgrade watch-list items):**
1. 2027 final rate notice ≥3% and a 2028 preliminary rate that clears core trend.
2. Confirmed Star recovery converting to bonus revenue in 2028 with margin ≥2.5% actually delivered.
3. Capital returns resumed *below* intrinsic value, plus ROIC sustainably >9%.
4. Price below ~$250.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Downside risks (why I'm not "Hold"):**
1. **Rate/trend gap** — CY2027 rate +2.48% vs 8–9% core trend. Humana must cut benefits or lose members; both hurt.
2. **Risk-adjustment regulation** — the CRUSH rule could retroactively reprice coding revenue; a single headline cost 13% of market value.
3. **Star-rating fragility** — one more downgrade cycle removes the 2028 recovery that the current price depends on.
4. **Concentration** — no diversification buffer; a single product, a single payer (CMS), a single demographic.
5. **Valuation risk** — 40x trough EPS with $10.5B of goodwill (56% of equity) supporting 2.45x book. Tangible book is $70/share.
6. **Competitive scale** — UNH can absorb trend shocks Humana cannot; a price war for MA members is unwinnable.

**Upside risks to my Sell (what I could be wrong about):**
1. Star recovery (95% of members in 4+ star plans for 2027) is *large* and could deliver a bigger 2028 margin surge than my bull case.
2. If CMS finalizes a generous 2028 rate (the 2027 proposal went from +0.09% to +2.48% — a big positive surprise), earnings power could reset toward $25–28/share, making today's price ~14x.
3. Humana remains a plausible strategic/consolidation target, and the $2.5B+ of 2026 FCF plus deleveraging reduces balance-sheet risk.

**Bottom line: quality 3/10, moat none, management 4/10, intrinsic value ≈ $325 vs price $386. Verdict: Sell.** Revisit below $250.
