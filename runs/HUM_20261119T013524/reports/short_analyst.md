# HUM — Forensic Short-Seller Analysis
**Price $385.78 · Market cap $46.3B · EV ≈ $55.0B · Sector: Managed Care (Medicare Advantage pure-play)**

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **LOW**

This is not a fraud — it is something more common and more dangerous: **a business whose reported earnings have decoupled from its operating economics, dressed up by non-cash marks and a legally-real one-time subsidy.**

The defining fact: FY2026 guidance is **GAAP EPS "at least $15.09" vs. adjusted EPS "at least $9.50."** GAAP earnings are now **59% above** adjusted earnings. The gap is *non-cash fair-value marks on CenterWell joint ventures* — which management raised by ~$8.57/share (~$1.03B) in a single quarter. When the majority of "earnings" is a Level 3 valuation estimate rather than cash from selling insurance, the income statement stops being evidence.

Meanwhile the operating trend is unambiguous:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 83.6 | 92.9 | 106.4 | 117.8 | 129.7 | ~162 |
| Gross profit ($B) | 14.4 | 17.2 | 18.0 | 17.2 | 18.9 | — |
| Gross margin | 17.3% | 18.5% | 16.9% | 14.6% | 14.5% | ~13.5% |
| Operating income ($B) | 3.42 | 3.56 | 3.32 | 1.63 | 1.45 | — |

**Revenue +55% since 2021. Operating income −59%. EPS −57%.** Gross profit dollars in 2025 ($18.9B) are barely above 2023 ($18.0B) despite $23B of incremental revenue. Every incremental dollar of premium is arriving at roughly zero gross profit.

The 2026 "growth" is the tell. Revenue +26% YoY and EPS *declining* vs. 2025. The largest single membership gain YTD is **+1,565K standalone PDP members** — Humana's structurally worst-margin product, now facing **14–15% Part D cost trend**. Growth is being purchased with revenue that carries trend risk and no profit.

FCF is down **89% from 2022**. FY2025 distributions ($581M) were **155% of FCF** ($375M). FCF yield is **0.8%**. The 2-year cash conversion average (161%) looks fine only because it hides the cliff.

**F2 — Massive negative working-capital swing inside operating cash flow.** Change in WC: +$534M (2022) → +$81M (2023) → +$154M (2024) → **−$1,797M (2025)**. A ~$1.95B YoY swing on $1.2B of net income. In a health insurer this lives in claims payable, risk-adjustment/RADV receivables, and unearned premium. It is the least transparent line in the model and it moved violently against shareholders.

**F3 — Receivables outgrowing revenue three consecutive years.** AR growth vs. revenue growth: **+21.6% vs +14.6% (2023); +32.9% vs +10.7% (2024); +20.9% vs +10.1% (2025).** AR $1.67B → $3.27B (**+95%**) against revenue **+40%**. AR days 6.6 → 9.2. Flagged red all three years.

**F4 — Benefit ratio deteriorating as membership grows.** Insurance benefit ratio **93.7% in Q3 2026 vs. 91.1% in Q3 2025 (+260bp)**, with FY guidance "slightly above 94%." Management attributes part of the rise to **"thinner prior period reserve development."** Translated: the actuarial cushion that has historically flattered managed-care earnings is being consumed. At a 94% benefit ratio the business has ~6 points of gross spread to cover all admin, interest, and tax.

**F5 — GAAP/adjusted divergence and ~$1B non-cash markup** (see §1). This also **inflates book equity**, which flatters both P/B and Altman's x4 term.

**F6 — Leverage rising into a cash-flow trough.** Total debt $12.26B → $12.94B; net debt $8.74B; D/E 73%; net debt/EBITDA ~3.0x. A **$1.5B debt tender** was completed alongside the **−13.12% single-day drop on Nov 18, 2026** (4.2M shares, ~2.5x normal volume) — two days after the 52-week high of $462.87. The market's reaction to a capital-structure action of that size is the opposite of confidence. *(The 8-K dated 2026-11-17 is the relevant filing; I could not retrieve its text — see gaps.)*

**F7 — Goodwill is 61% of book equity.** G&I $10.81B vs. equity $17.74B → tangible book $6.92B. **Price/tangible book ≈ 6.7x.** Goodwill has already drifted down $11.24B → $10.81B over two years, implying serial small impairments in the services/CenterWell complex. The acquisition-built care-delivery strategy has not yet demonstrated a return above its cost of capital.

**F8 — Management explicitly calls the 2028 uplift one-time.** On the Q3 call management tied the star recovery to bonus year 2028 and said it "expects a **one-time tailwind in 2028**, part reinvested and part returned to shareholders." Consensus 2028 EPS is **$37.81 — 4.0x the 2026 level**. Consensus is capitalizing a benefit that management itself describes as non-recurring.

## 3. Beneish M-Score and Accruals Analysis

**Beneish M-Score: −2.281** ("low risk"; thresholds −1.78 / −2.22). Components: DSRI **1.099** · GMI 1.001 · AQI 0.954 · SGI 1.101 · DEPI 1.054 · SGAI 1.000 · LVGI 0.986 · **TATA 0.0055**.

**Read this score with heavy skepticism — it is near-useless here, and I'd argue it is actively misleading:**
- It clears the −2.22 softer threshold by only **0.06**.
- **GMI = 1.001**, i.e. the model sees *no* gross-margin deterioration. But that's because Beneish only looks at one year: margins were already destroyed between 2022 (18.5%) and 2024 (14.6%). The model is blind to the crime because the crime happened in the prior window.
- **TATA = 0.0055** is mechanically tiny because net income is tiny relative to a $48.8B balance sheet — a shrunken denominator, not clean accruals.
- Beneish was calibrated on non-financial industrials. **For an insurer, receivables and payables are the business, not working capital** — DSRI and TATA lose their meaning. The one component that *is* informative, **DSRI 1.099**, corroborates the AR red flag.

**Accruals ratio: 0.0055 ("ok").** Same caveat — the ratio is small because NI is small. The more revealing accruals diagnostic is the **WC outflow of −$1.797B**, which the balance-sheet ratio mechanically nets out.

**Cash conversion 77.5%** (below the >100% quality standard) and **FCF conversion 31.6%**. **Composite earnings-quality score: 46/100.** That is the honest number; Beneish's −2.281 is not.

**Piotroski F: 6/9 ("moderate")** — but note where it fails: ROA improvement (**0**), accruals quality (**0**), gross-margin improvement (**0**). Three of the four profitability/quality points fail. The points it earns (leverage, equity change, no dilution, asset turnover) are the weakest signals in the model. F=6 overstates health.

**Altman Z: 3.909 "Safe"** — low information. x1 (WC/assets) is **negative at −0.0583** and the current ratio is **0.72** ($7.47B vs. $10.31B). Altman does not distinguish insurance reserves from financial debt, and x4 uses book equity that is itself inflated by the JV marks. I would not cite this score in support of the company.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment: **Severe, and structural**

This is the weakest part of the bull case and the strongest part of the short.

- **No pricing power.** ~85%+ of revenue is Medicare-derived. Prices are set by CMS. Gross margin 18.5% → 14.5% over two years is what "loss of pricing power" looks like on a chart.
- **No durable moat — a coin-flip metric.** Member-weighted Star Ratings went from **20% of members in 4+ star plans (2026) → 95% (2027)**. A single input that can swing 20→95 in one year is not a moat; it is a regulatory lottery that managers do not control. The 2026 collapse is precisely what halved EPS from $20 to $10. Reversion is the base case, and management admits the 2027 score (4.17, "close to the highest ever achieved") is at a cyclical peak — achieved while growing membership 23% and *thinning* supplemental benefits, which feeds CAHPS and complaint metrics in the *next* measurement cycle. **The mechanism that created the 2028 tailwind is the same mechanism that can remove it in 2029.**
- **Vertical integration disadvantage.** UnitedHealth (market cap $313B, ~6.8x HUM) owns Optum and a PBM at scale; CVS owns Aetna + Caremark; Humana has no comparable integrated cost engine. In a 94% MLR business, a 150–200bp structural cost disadvantage is existential, not cosmetic.
- **Benefit cuts invite churn.** For 2027 Humana cut plans 887→861, exited 61 counties, cut supplemental benefits ($69.8 → $62.8), and pushed 58.5% of plans into the top $700 Part D deductible tier. Management's stated 2027 posture is **"margin first, retention second, growth a distant third"** — an explicit admission that the 2026 growth was uneconomic. That admission *is* the bear case, spoken by management.
- **The 2026 growth cohort is a liability, not an asset.** +23% individual MA, +25% D-SNP, +1,565K PDP — acquired at a benefit ratio 260bp worse YoY. In MA, new members are unprofitable in year one and require retention for 3+ years to pay back. If "margin first" pricing drives them out at AEP, the acquisition cost is stranded.

## 5. Management & Governance Red Flags

1. **Non-GAAP divergence inverted.** Normally bears flag companies where *adjusted* EPS exceeds GAAP. Here GAAP exceeds adjusted by 59%, driven by **non-cash JV fair-value marks** raised ~$1.03B in one quarter. Whether it flatters or obscures, valuation-driven marks should never be the majority of net income.
2. **Guidance whipsaw.** FY2026 GAAP guidance moved from "at least $6.52" to "at least $15.09" — a **132% mid-year revision** to full-year GAAP EPS, from non-cash items. Earnings that can be revised 132% by marks are not forecast
