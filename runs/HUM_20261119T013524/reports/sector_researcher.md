# Sector Brief — Managed Care (Medical – Healthcare Plans)
**Target:** Humana Inc. (HUM) | **Sector:** Healthcare / Managed Care | **Data as of FY2025 (filed 19-Feb-2026) and market close 27-Feb-2026**

---

## 1. Sector Overview

US managed care is a ~$1.7tn-revenue oligopoly (the seven names below) whose economics are set by government reimbursement rather than by pricing power. Growth is structurally reliable — Medicare Advantage penetration, Medicaid expansion, and rate updates have delivered 8–13% revenue CAGRs across the group — but **margins are cyclically depressed**: every one of the seven comps saw operating margin compress between FY2022 and FY2025, with a median decline of 260bp, as post-COVID utilisation normalcy, elevated medical trend (behavioural health, oncology, inpatient), and an adverse Star Ratings cycle hit an industry that reprices only once a year. The sector is in a **late-cycle margin trough, not a structural decline**: 2026 is consensus the peak-cost year, with the final CY2027 MA rate announcement (+2.48%, or +4.98% including risk-score trend) restoring a positive rate-versus-trend spread for the first time in three years. Competitive intensity is high on MA bid discipline and Star Ratings (which swing 3–5% of revenue via quality bonus payments), and low on product differentiation — plans are largely price-takers on benefit design.

**Structure / Porter's Five Forces:** Supplier power (hospitals, physicians, pharma) is **high and rising** — this is the single largest driver of the group's margin compression. Buyer power is **moderate**: individual Medicare members are sticky but price-elastic at Annual Election Period; CMS as ultimate payer is the binding constraint. Barriers to entry are **high** (state licensure, CMS sanction risk, MLR rebate rules, provider network scale, capital/risk-based capital requirements). Threat of substitutes is low-moderate (original Medicare fee-for-service, Medicare Advantage alternatives, I-SNP/DP-SNP niches). Rivalry is **high and rationalising** — the industry is actively shedding unprofitable MA membership rather than buying share (HUM individual MA −7.3% YoY to 5.2M; UNH and others shrinking similarly).

**Concentration:** Top-3 share of the seven-company revenue pool is ~66% (UNH 26%, CVS 24%, CI 16%), implying a revenue-based HHI of roughly **1,860** — concentrated. Caveat: CVS and CI revenue is inflated by low-margin PBM/pharmacy pass-through, so true MA-market concentration is lower (UNH ~29% of MA; HUM ~13%, #2 in MA).

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **CY2027 MA rate reset is materially better than feared.** The Advance Notice implied just **+0.09%** net; the final Rate Announcement delivered **+2.48%** ($13bn+) and **+4.98%** including expected risk-score trend, with the risk-model hit cut from −3.32% to −1.12%. This converts a cut scenario into a modest real increase against an effective growth rate of 5.33%.
- **Cost repricing is catching up to trend.** Two consecutive years of aggressive benefit reduction, premium increases, and network/prior-authorisation tightening mean 2027 bids are being filed against re-based cost structures. The group's operating margin has stabilised sequentially FY2024→FY2025 (HUM operating income flat at $1.45bn on 10% revenue growth).
- **Secular MA penetration and demographic demand.** ~10,000 Americans age into Medicare daily; MA now covers roughly half of eligible beneficiaries and continues to take share from fee-for-service. Value-based care, home health, and pharmacy integration (Humana's CenterWell, oncology and gold-card prior-auth initiatives) offer structural cost-of-care levers unavailable to FFS Medicare.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Star Ratings volatility is unhedged and idiosyncratic.** HUM's count of 4-star-or-better MA plans "significantly declined" for Bonus Year 2026, and FY2026 guidance explicitly embeds a **YoY earnings decline** from that headwind. Quality bonus payments are ~3–5% of MA revenue — a one-notch downgrade is a multi-hundred-basis-point margin event, and it is not diversifiable across the sector.
- **Continued risk-adjustment tightening.** CY2027 retains a −1.53% "sources of diagnoses" cut; CMS is structurally shrinking the coding-intensity uplift that historically funded benefit richness. Combined with the risk-model revision, this caps how much of the rate increase reaches margin.
- **Medical cost trend remains above rate trend on a multi-year view**, and utilisation is still normalising. Operating margins across the group are 130–480bp below FY2022 levels; only CVS (+90% over 12 months) has been rewarded, and only because it is a cost-recovery/retail story rather than a pure MA one.

## 5. Humana's Competitive Position: **Average (weakening at the margin)**

**Supporting:**
- **Scale and franchise:** #2 in Medicare Advantage at ~5.2M individual MA members, with an integrated care-delivery asset (CenterWell primary care, home health, pharmacy) that is genuinely differentiated versus pure insurance peers.
- **Growth still above the long-run average:** 10.9% five-year revenue CAGR (FY21→FY25), and FY2025 revenue growth of +10.1% is only 1.7pp below the peer median — in line, not losing share on the top line.
- **Gross margin (14.5%) is above the peer median (13.0%)** and roughly in line with CVS, indicating the underlying book is not structurally sub-scale.

**Against:**
- **Operating margin of 1.12% is the second-lowest in the group** (ahead only of CNC's loss) at less than half the 2.52% peer median, and net margin has collapsed from 3.0% (FY22) to 0.92% (FY25) — diluted EPS fell from $22.08 to **$9.84** in three years.
- **FY2025 was an outright loss on an adjusted basis in Q4** ($796M GAAP loss; −$3.96 adjusted EPS), with FY2026 guidance calling for a further YoY decline on the Star Ratings headwind — HUM is the only name in the group guiding *down* from an already-trough base.
- **Concentration risk:** HUM is the most MA-pure large cap (~85%+ of earnings from MA), so it has no commercial or PBM buffer against a single reimbursement or Star Ratings shock — the precise exposure that took the stock from $309 (Sep-25) to $168 (11-Feb-26), a **−46% drawdown**, and left it −28.6% over 12 months versus −22.8% for the peer median.
- **Contracting membership:** individual MA −7.3% YoY. Some of that is deliberate margin defence, but it cedes scale to UNH and ELV at the exact moment fixed-cost leverage matters.

**Verdict: Average and deteriorating.** HUM owns a defensible MA franchise and a differentiated integrated-care asset, but it is a sub-scale, single-market operator in a business where reimbursement is set exogenously and where it has the group's thinnest cushion to absorb Star Ratings and risk-adjustment shocks. It is a **high-beta play on the 2026 margin trough inflecting in 2027** — not a quality compounder at current fundamentals.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Managed care is a **structurally sound, cyclically impaired** sector: reliable mid-teens revenue growth, high barriers, oligopolistic structure — but margins at a multi-decade trough and a payer (CMS) actively tightening risk adjustment. The CY2027 rate finalisation (+2.48% / +4.98% with trend) is the first genuine positive catalyst in three years and argues the earnings trough is FY2026. That is enough to avoid an "Unattractive" call, but not enough for "Attractive" while Star Ratings dispersion, medical trend, and risk-model revisions remain unresolved and while the group is guiding down rather than up.

**Positioning implication:** prefer names with diversified earnings and self-help (ELV, CI) or idiosyncratic re-rating (CVS) over MA-pure, sub-scale exposures (HUM, CNC, MOH). HUM is a legitimate **turnaround/mean-reversion candidate** contingent on (a) 2027 Star Ratings recovery and successful litigation/quality remediation, and (b) evidence of MA membership stabilisation — until then it screens as the highest-risk way to own a Neutral sector.
