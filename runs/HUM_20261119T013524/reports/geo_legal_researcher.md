## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

### Risk #1 — CMS "CRUSH" Proposed Rule (CMS-6098-P): retroactive MA audit extrapolation
**Probability of material finalization: 60–70% | Impact: High**

Announced ~Nov 17, 2026, this is the dominant new risk. Key mechanics:
- Codifies **sampling and extrapolation** of RADV audit findings **beginning with payment year 2018**, and **removes the fee-for-service adjuster** — reversing precisely what Humana won in court.
- Makes **fraud referrals by MA and Part D plans mandatory**; requires MA-billing providers to enroll in Original Medicare two years after finalization.
- CMS estimates **~$9B recovered per payment year** and frames the rule as targeting **~$90B in taxpayer savings**.

**Market reaction:** HUM fell 4.24% on Nov 17 to $441.71 (whole managed-care group sold off), then **>9% on Nov 18 to $404.69** — a two-day drawdown of roughly 14% (Yahoo/GuruFocus; Seeking Alpha). Comments close **Jan 19, 2027**; nothing is effective until a final rule. The critical issue is **retroactivity** — eight years of prior revenue becomes potential liability, and the size is unquantifiable until finalization. Given Humana's ~$120B+ premium base and heavy risk-adjustment reliance, a several-hundred-million to low-single-digit-billion cumulative exposure is plausible (analyst estimate, not company-disclosed). *Watchpoint: CMS is already voluntarily recalculating certain 2027 Quality Bonus Payments (Healthcare Finance News).*

### Risk #2 — DOJ False Claims Act / anti-kickback litigation
**Probability of adverse outcome: Medium | Impact: Medium-High**

- **US ex rel. Shea v. eHealth Inc. et al.** — DOJ intervened and filed an FCA complaint against Aetna, Elevance and **Humana**, alleging the plans paid unlawful broker kickbacks and, per DOJ, that "Aetna and Humana each conspired with the broker defendants to discriminate against Medicare beneficiaries" — including pressuring brokers to enroll fewer disabled beneficiaries. The court **denied the insurers' motion to dismiss** (except as to unjust enrichment), so Humana now faces discovery and potential treble damages plus per-claim penalties (DOJ press release; Becker's; VitalLaw).
- **$90M Part D FCA settlement** — Humana paid $90M to resolve a whistleblower case alleging fraudulent Part D bids submitted to CMS, plus ~$32M in fees/relator share (Phillips & Cohen; Healthcare Finance News). Paid September 2026 — a precedent that materially raises settlement expectations in the Shea matter.
- **HHS-OIG risk-adjustment audits** of HumanaChoice (contract H5216) for high-risk diagnosis codes remain active (OIG 2026 report series).

### Risk #3 — Rate-setting, Star Ratings and "Medicare for More" political pressure
**Probability of adverse rate action: Medium | Impact: High (but currently offsetting)**

- **Positive offset:** 2027 Star Ratings were a major turnaround — members in 4-star-or-above plans jumped from **~42% to ~95%**; ~3M members (42%) sit in 4.5-star contracts, with an enrollment-weighted average of ~4.18. This drives a 5% quality bonus payment uplift into 2028 revenue (Humana press release; Becker's; HFMA). Analysts named HUM a top beneficiary.
- **Negative:** the offsetting risk is that this improvement is *relative* — competitors' ratings could converge, and CMS has shown willingness to recalculate QBPs. Meanwhile, political momentum behind **"Medicare for More"** (per STAT, Nov 17, 2026) and continued MAHA-era scrutiny of prior authorization and MA coding intensity create headline risk. HUM has already cut FY2026 GAAP EPS guidance to "at least $6.52" from "at least $8.36" (Q2 2026 8-K), underscoring limited cushion.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Anti-Kickback Statute / broker compensation** — the single clearest red flag. DOJ has alleged a *pattern* across multiple national carriers (Aetna, Elevance, Humana) involving third-party marketing organizations. Humana's downstream broker/TMO oversight is a live enforcement target.
2. **Risk-adjustment coding integrity** — Humana sits at the center of the RADV/CRUSH crosshairs; the 2018 retroactive lookback converts historical coding practices into current liabilities.
3. **Part D bid accuracy** — the $90M settlement signals DOJ's willingness to pursue bid-submission theories, not just claims submission.
4. **Mandatory fraud self-reporting** (CRUSH) — would create a new disclosure obligation with FCA exposure for imperfect reporting.
5. **Data privacy / HIPAA** — ~17M medical members and full pharmacy claims data; no breach disclosed in materials reviewed, but SEC cyber-disclosure obligations apply.
6. **FCPA / AML / sanctions / export controls** — **no meaningful exposure**; purely domestic payer with no foreign subsidiaries of consequence outside Puerto Rico.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Cross-border geopolitical risk is **Very Low** (no foreign revenue, no sanctions/tariff/export-control exposure). But Humana's risk profile is dominated by **single-jurisdiction, single-payer regulatory concentration** — effectively ~90% of revenue from US federal health programs administered by CMS and HHS, an administration that has just proposed a rule explicitly designed to recover ~$9B per year from Medicare Advantage plans with **eight years of retroactive reach**.

Supporting the HIGH rating: the CRUSH rule's retroactivity (unquantifiable but material), two active FCA fronts (Shea broker kickbacks; OIG coding audits), a demonstrated $90M+ settlement track record, and thin earnings cushion (FY26 GAAP EPS guidance cut to ≥$6.52).

**Mitigants pulling the rating back from Very High:** (i) a strong legal position on RADV — Humana has already won once and the 5th Circuit appeared sympathetic in October 2026; (ii) the **2027 Star Ratings step-change (42% → 95% of members at 4+ stars)**, which secures a quality bonus uplift into 2028; (iii) no foreign, sanctions, or export-control exposure whatsoever; (iv) proactive de-escalation of prior-authorization risk. Note also that the CRUSH rule cannot take effect before a final rule and is certain to be litigated — the same procedural playbook Humana used successfully in 2023–25.

**Key catalysts to monitor:** CRUSH comment period close (Jan 19, 2027) and final-rule timing; the 5th Circuit RADV decision; any Shea/eHealth settlement or damages figure; CMS 2028 rate announcement and Star Ratings round; Florida/Puerto Rico Medicaid funding actions.
