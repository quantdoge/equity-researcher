# EchoStar Corporation (ECHO) — Value Investment Analysis

## Reference Data Note
ECHO is the post-merger EchoStar/DISH Network entity. Reported figures span fiscal 2021–2025 plus Q2 2026 updates. Observed share price most recently ~$92.64 (valuation tool), within a 2026 trading band of roughly $90–$110, versus ~$14–16 in early 2024. Diluted shares 287.6M → market cap ≈ **$26.6B**. The company profile's stated $14.7B market cap is stale (it implies ~$51/share) and should be disregarded.

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NONE in the operating businesses / NARROW and non-operating in the spectrum portfolio.**

The operating businesses fail every moat test:

| Segment | Competitive position | Moat verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Pay-TV (DISH TV, Sling) | Structural cord-cutting; ~3M remaining DBS subs, shrinking double digits | None — declining asset |
| Retail Wireless (Boost Mobile) | Sub-scale #4 national prepaid brand vs. Verizon, T-Mobile, AT&T; relies on MVNO offload; no cost or network advantage | None |
| Broadband & Satellite (HughesNet) | Being displaced by LEO competition (Starlink); Jupiter 3 helps but does not reverse the trend | None-to-narrow |
| Spectrum / Other | ~600 MHz, AWS-4, H-Block licenses; finite, FCC-regulated, replacement cost in the tens of billions | **Narrow, asset-based only** |

The one genuine economic asset is federally licensed spectrum — a scarce intangible with enormous barriers to replication. But that is a *liquidation* moat, not an operating moat. It generates value only when sold, and EchoStar has now sold the crown jewels: 50 MHz to AT&T for **$23B** and AWS-4/H-block to SpaceX for **$17B** (**$40B** total), FCC-approved. SpaceX additionally assumes ~$2B of EchoStar debt.

Critically, the moat has **narrowed** over five years. Revenue fell from $19.8B (2021) to $15.0B (2025), a **–6.7% four-year CAGR**, with declines of 6.0%, 8.7%, 7.0%, and 5.2% each year — never a single growth year. Pay-TV and broadband subs are in secular retreat; wireless never achieved the scale needed to compete. The company sold its best assets to fund survival.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **2 / 10**

| Metric | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 19.82 | 18.63 | 17.02 | 15.83 | 15.00 |
| Gross margin | 36.9% | 34.5% | 29.8% | 36.0% | **25.8%** |
| Operating margin | 17.3% | 12.0% | –1.6% | –1.9% | **–0.6%** |
| Net income ($B) | 2.49 | 2.48 | –1.70 | –0.12 | **–14.50** |
| FCF ($M) | +160 | +571 | –668 | –292 | **–1,065** |
| ROIC | n/a | 4.7% | –0.6% | –0.6% | **–0.25%** |

**What is broken:**
- **Revenue predictability and growth:** four consecutive declines; no evidence of a bottom in Pay-TV or broadband.
- **Margin stability:** gross margin collapsed 1,110 bp from 2021 to 2025. This is not cyclical — it is mix deterioration (low-margin wireless hardware and MVNO costs displacing high-margin video) plus pricing pressure.
- **FCF conversion:** cumulative FCF 2021–2025 is **–$1.29B**. FCF was negative in three of five years, and –$1.07B in 2025. FCF/net income conversion is meaningless because both are negative. Owner earnings 2022–2025 sum to roughly **–$16.2B** (–$2.3B excluding the 2025 impairment).
- **Returns on capital:** ROIC averaged well under 1% over four years and never exceeded 4.7%, versus a WACC of ~9%. The business has **destroyed capital relative to its cost of capital every year since 2022** — the single clearest disqualifier in this framework.
- **Balance sheet (12/31/2025):** total debt **$31.0B**, cash $2.06B, **net debt $29.1B**, equity only $5.8B (BVPS $20.21), retained earnings **–$2.9B**. Current ratio **0.42** — $12.4B of current liabilities against $5.1B of current assets, including **$8.2B of short-term debt**. That is an acute liquidity profile; the company skipped ~$183M of interest payments in June 2025, and DISH DBS only stabilized via Chapter 11, eliminating $4.35B of debt and producing a $9.7B non-cash deconsolidation gain in Q2 2026.
- **The 2025 net loss of $14.5B** is impairment-driven (goodwill/spectrum write-downs), but it is not innocent: it is management's own admission that ~$18B of previously reported asset value did not exist.

**What is not entirely broken:** the business still generates ~$15B of revenue and ~$3.9B of gross profit, and adjusted OIBDA inflected upward in Q2 2026 ($681M vs. $279M) on cost reduction. Post-restructuring long-term debt is down to ~$16.0B as of June 30, 2026. So solvency has improved materially — but from "existential" to merely "highly leveraged."

## 3. Valuation Analysis

Standard tools break down here and should be reported as failures rather than fudged:
- **P/E: –1.84** (peer median +16.33) — not meaningful.
- **EV/EBITDA: –3.52** — impairment-distorted, not meaningful.
- **P/FCF: –25.0; FCF yield –4.0%** — no cash return to owners.
- **P/B: 4.58x** on $20.21 book value — 4.6x book for a negative-ROIC, negative-FCF business.
- **DCF (9% WACC, 0% terminal growth):** produces a *negative* equity value (–$14.6B) because the base FCF is –$1.07B. The model is arithmetically valid and economically useless. It does, however, document that **no earnings-based valuation supports a positive value at all.**

### Sum-of-the-parts is the only usable lens

| Component | Conservative | Optimistic |
|---|---|---|
| Spectrum proceeds (AT&T + SpaceX) | $40.0B | $40.0B |
| Less tax leakage on low-basis gains (NOLs shield much) | –20% → $32.0B | –0% → $40.0B |
| Remaining spectrum (AWS-3/2GHz etc.) | $0B | $12.0B |
| Residual operations (Pay-TV, Boost, Hughes) | $0B | $3.0B |
| Less net debt (post-restructuring, ~$18–20B) | –$20.0B | –$18.0B |
| **Equity value** | **≈ $12B** | **≈ $37B** |
| **Per share (287.6M diluted)** | **≈ $42** | **≈ $129** |

Probability-weighted, I place intrinsic value in the **$55–75/share** range, with a wide band of $40–$120 driven almost entirely by tax treatment and how much spectrum remains. Note that Morningstar carries a **$20 going-concern fair value estimate** for EchoStar as an *operating* business — a useful anchor for what the residual company is worth once the spectrum is gone.

**At ~$92.64, the stock trades at or above the midpoint of a realistic liquidation SOTP and roughly 4.6x going-concern value.** There is no margin of safety; there is a premium. The 7x move from ~$14 to ~$110 front-ran the spectrum sale — the market has already arbitraged the asset value that this thesis depends on. The bull case ($115–150 targets from UBS, Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank; a $122 probability-weighted five-year target) requires tax-free proceeds, meaningful value for retained spectrum, and successful reinvestment of a $5B buyback authorization — three favorable outcomes stacked on top of each other.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment: **4 / 10**

**Positives:** Charlie Ergen is a founder-owner with substantial insider ownership and decades of satellite/spectrum conviction. The dual-class structure means he is genuinely aligned with long-term value — for himself. He executed the $40B spectrum sale and the DISH DBS Chapter 11 deleveraging, converting a near-insolvency into a survivable balance sheet. The recent capital allocation — sell assets, retire debt — is the right call.

**Negatives, and they are severe:** over the prior decade, management spent roughly **$30B on spectrum (>$100/share)**, funded with $30B+ of debt, while the core Pay-TV franchise eroded and the wireless build-out never reached competitive scale. Diluted shares went from 83M (2022) to 288M (2025) — a 3.5x dilution. Retained earnings are now **negative $2.9B**. A shareholder who bought at the 2022 peak has been diluted, impaired, and — absent this fire-sale — would have been wiped out. The dual-class structure also insulates Ergen from accountability for that record. The $40B sale is an excellent outcome *relative to the alternative*, but it is a liquidation of strategic assets, not evidence of operating excellence. M&A discipline over the full cycle: poor.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

Applying the stated discipline — only businesses with durable moats, reliable FCF, high returns on capital, honest capital allocation, and a 20–30% discount to intrinsic value — EchoStar fails on every single criterion except insider alignment, and it trades at a *premium* to a generous asset-based valuation.

- **Moat:** none operationally; narrowed over five years.
- **ROIC vs. WACC:** negative spread in three of four years.
- **FCF:** negative cumulatively and in three of five years.
- **Balance sheet:** $29B net debt at 12/31/2025; current ratio 0.42; missed interest payments in 2025.
- **Valuation:** ~$93 price vs. ~$55–75 realistic SOTP; ~$20 going-concern value. No margin of safety, and no business earnings to capitalize.

This is an event-driven distressed asset-liquidation story that happens to be sitting at a price where the event is already priced in. A Buffett-style investor does not buy a capital-destroying, structurally declining telecom at 4.6x book for the privilege of waiting on tax rulings and further asset sales. **Sell existing positions; do not initiate.**

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to the bear/Sell case (i.e., what could make me wrong):**
1. **Tax leakage comes in far below 20%** — if NOLs fully shield the $40B gains, net proceeds rise by $8B+, lifting SOTP toward $100–110/share.
2. **Retained spectrum is worth more than assumed** — EchoStar still holds AWS-3/2GHz and other licenses; a further $10–15B of sales would materially raise equity value.
3. **Aggressive capital return** — a $5.0B buyback authorization executed at depressed prices would be highly accretive and could re-rate the stock.
4. **Operating inflection is real** — Q2 2026 adjusted OIBDA of $681M vs. $279M suggests OpenRAN cost cuts and MVNO offload reductions could make Boost/Hughes cash-generative, supporting the residual value.

**Risks to the investment thesis (why an owner should be wary):**
1. **Execution and closing risk** on $40B of spectrum sales — regulatory conditions, disputes over license work, and political scrutiny of market concentration could delay or reprice proceeds.
2. **Continued subscriber erosion** in Pay-TV and broadband eats cash while awaiting close; the business still burns ~$1B/year.
3. **Refinancing risk** — even post-restructuring, ~$16B of long-term debt plus current maturities against a shrinking cash-generating base leaves little room for error; a failed or delayed spectrum close reopens the liquidity crisis.
4. **Governance** — dual-class control means minority holders cannot force a value-realizing path; capital could be redeployed into further spectrum or network bets rather than returned.
5. **Accounting opacity** — a $14.5B impairment followed by a $9.7B deconsolidation gain within eighteen months shows reported book value has repeatedly proven unreliable. Any valuation built on book assets deserves a heavy discount.

**Bottom line:** the asset value is real, but the price already reflects it — and then some. No moat, no free cash flow, no margin of safety. Pass.
