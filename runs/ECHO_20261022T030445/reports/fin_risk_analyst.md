## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

### Rating: **SUB-INVESTMENT GRADE (speculative — CCC+/B3)**

The independent rating evidence corroborates the quantitative screen: S&P rates EchoStar **CCC+** (upgraded on improved liquidity from fresh capital and debt transactions) and Moody's upgraded the CFR to **B3** (from Caa2), with Moody's citing leverage of **24.9x at end-2025** falling to an estimated 4.4x by end-2027 contingent on execution. A CCC+/B3 profile means the company is *currently vulnerable and dependent on favourable conditions* — that is precisely what the FY2025 financials show.

The core credit story is not an earnings story; it is a **balance-sheet and refinancing story**. FY2025 produced a net loss of **-$14.50B**, but roughly **$14.2B of that is non-cash impairment** (goodwill collapsed from $39.5B to $54M; total assets fell from $60.9B to $43.0B; PP&E from $12.4B to $2.46B). Underlying EBITDA before impairment was still **positive at ~$1.50B** (revenue $15.00B less COGS $11.13B less SG&A $2.38B), i.e. a ~10% margin. So the business generates positive operating cash economics — but nowhere near enough to service **$31.0B of debt** carrying **$1.52B of annual interest**.

The deterioration in the cost of debt is the single most alarming trend in the file. Interest expense went **$79M (2022) → $90M (2023) → $482M (2024) → $1,522M (2025)** — a 3.2x step-up in one year and ~19x over three years. That is the signature of debt refinanced at distressed rates. Reported interest coverage is **-0.06x**; on underlying (pre-impairment) EBITDA the coverage is only **~0.98x** — the company does not cover its interest bill from operations. **Debt/equity is 5.33x book** and rising as equity is consumed by losses (equity fell from $20.2B to $5.81B in one year). Net debt of **$29.1B exceeds the stated market capitalisation**, meaning the equity is effectively a levered call option on the asset base rather than a claim on earnings.

The reported **Net Debt/EBITDA of -1.83x is not meaningful** — the denominator is negative because impairment is embedded in reported EBITDA. The economically honest figure is **~19.5x** ($29.1B / $1.495B underlying EBITDA), which sits close to Moody's cited 24.9x and confirms extreme leverage.

**Mitigants (genuine but unquantified in this dataset):** The balance sheet is asset-heavy — only ~$6.2B of the $43.0B asset base is identified in the data (cash, receivables, inventory, PP&E, goodwill). The residual **~$36.8B is unclassified**, consistent with a large spectrum-licence and intangibles portfolio that is saleable. Asset disposals are the credible deleveraging path — and the reason ratings were upgraded rather than downgraded. This is a **data gap I am flagging explicitly**: the balance sheet feed does not break out spectrum licences, so I cannot size the liquidation cushion. Every conclusion on recoverable value should be treated as conditional on that.

| Free cash flow | -$1.07B | **~-$1.8B** | -70% |
| Cash balance | $2.06B | ~$0.3B after one year | Near zero |

**Interpretation.** Operating leverage is brutal: a 20% revenue decline cuts underlying EBITDA by roughly two-thirds because COGS is largely variable while SG&A is largely fixed. The company would post an **EBITDA/interest ratio of ~0.33x** — an outright interest-coverage failure — and FCF burn would widen to ~$1.8B/year, exhausting the entire $2.06B cash balance within about twelve months **while $8.2B of debt matures**. In this scenario the outcome is not a covenant breach alone but a **liquidity event**: acceleration, distressed exchange, or forced asset sales at cyclical discounts. Note the gross margin has already fallen 10 points in FY2025 without a 20% revenue shock — the margin trajectory means this stress case is not hypothetical, it is a continuation of the current trend.

**What protects the downside:** (i) the goodwill/PP&E impairment is already taken, so the P&L will no longer carry ~$14B of non-cash charges and reported losses should shrink dramatically even at flat revenue; (ii) underlying EBITDA remains positive; (iii) the large unclassified asset base (spectrum) provides disposal optionality; (iv) capex is already being cut ($3.1B → $0.97B), which is a real FCF lever. **What breaks it:** any loss of refinancing access or an asset-sale failure coincides exactly with the $8.2B short-term wall.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating

# **CRITICAL**

EchoStar combines every marker of a distressed credit: **Altman Z-Score of -1.19 (distress zone), Piotroski F-Score of 2/9 (weak), net leverage of ~19.5x underlying EBITDA, interest coverage below 1.0x, a current ratio of 0.42, negative working capital of $7.2B, three consecutive years of negative FCF, and an $8.2B short-term maturity wall against $2.06B of cash.** The sub-investment-grade CCC+/B3 ratings are consistent with this, not contradictory. The mitigating factors — positive underlying EBITDA, an already-cleaned balance sheet after impairment, spectrum disposal optionality, and capex reduction — are real, but they are **execution-dependent and unquantified in the available data**. The company's solvency rests on continued access to capital markets and successful asset monetisation, not on internal cash generation.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **$8.21B short-term debt vs $2.06B cash** — an 8.7x year-over-year jump in current maturities created a refinancing cliff with only 0.25x cash coverage. This is the dominant risk in the file.
2. **Interest expense up 19x in three years** ($79M → $1.52B) — evidence the debt has already been repriced to distressed levels; coverage is now below 1.0x.
3. **Negative free cash flow three years running** (-$0.67B, -$0.29B, -$1.07B) with operating cash flow turning negative in FY2025 — the business no longer funds itself.
4. **Gross margin collapse of 10.1 points** (35.9% → 25.8%) — pricing/cost pressure is structural, and the Piotroski score gives no credit for margin improvement.
5. **Current ratio 0.42 and working capital of -$7.23B** — balance sheet is funded by short-dated liabilities against long-dated, illiquid assets; a classic maturity-mismatch.
6. **Altman Z at -1.19 and F-Score at 2/9** — both models independently place ECHO in the worst decile of financial health.
7. **Net debt ($29.1B) exceeds stated market cap (~$14.7B)** — the equity is deeply out of the money on a balance-sheet basis; valuation is purely option value on spectrum.
8. **Market risk is understated in the profile** — measured beta 1.54 vs reported 0.95, with 52% annualised volatility and a -40.8% max drawdown. Treat the equity as a leveraged credit proxy.

**Data gaps flagged:** (a) **Spectrum licence / intangible asset values are not broken out** — ~$36.8B of the $43.0B asset base is unclassified, so recovery-value analysis is incomplete; (b) **market cap discrepancy** — the profile reports ~$14.7B while price × diluted shares ($92.64 × 287.6M) implies ~$26.6B; FCF yield is therefore **-4.0% on the higher figure and ~-7.3% on the profile figure**; (c) **US Treasury yield curve unavailable** (tool error) — I could not quantify the macro refinancing backdrop, though at CCC+/B3 the issuer's spread, not the base rate, is the binding constraint; (d) no debt-maturity schedule by year was returned, only the current/non-current split.
