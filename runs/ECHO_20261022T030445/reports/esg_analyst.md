## ESG ANALYSIS — ECHO (EchoStar Corporation)

**Issuer note:** The profile tool maps ECHO to EchoStar Corporation (CIK 0001415404, CUSIP 278768106), Class A shares, HQ Englewood, CO — Communication Services / Telecommunications. The same issuer has historically traded as SATS; filings under both tickers refer to the same entity. Market cap ~$14.7bn, ~12,100 employees, CEO Charlie Ergen. **Data gap:** no company sustainability report, CDP disclosure, or third-party ESG rating (MSCI/Sustainalytics) could be retrieved; the 10-K and DEF 14A full text were not accessible via the filing tool (only a 10-Q exhibit and news/regulatory sources). Conclusions below rest on regulatory filings referenced in news, FCC/SEC documents and reporting — not self-reported ESG scores.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Orbital debris is the sector-defining environmental issue, and EchoStar is the precedent-setter.** The FCC Enforcement Bureau entered a $150,000 consent decree with DISH over the **EchoStar-7** satellite, which was not deorbited as planned — the first-ever US space-junk fine. Financial impact is trivial; the *precedent* is not. It establishes EchoStar as the reference case for debris enforcement, raising the cost of licence renewal and insurance at a moment when the company is selling its spectrum.
- **Disclosure is effectively absent.** No CDP response, GRI/SASB report or Scope 1/2 emissions baseline was locatable. For a ~$15bn-revenue, 12,100-employee operator with a satellite fleet, ground stations and a national wireless network, this is a genuine reporting gap rather than evidence of low emissions. It also constrains the company's access to sustainability-linked capital and to enterprise/government customers with Scope 3 procurement requirements.
- **Carbon intensity is structurally modest** — satellites and pay-TV do not carry the process emissions of industrials — so there is no near-term carbon-cost cliff. The residual exposure is electricity price and grid-mix risk at data centres/ground stations, plus e-waste from set-top boxes.
- **Physical climate risk:** teleport/ground-station and launch-schedule exposure, but geographically diversified and not a valuation driver.

**Verdict:** Medium — low carbon-cost exposure, but a material *governance-of-environmental-risk* problem (debris compliance + zero disclosure).

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Workforce:** ~12,100 employees following the DISH merger. Reporting and employee-review sources describe repeated annual reduction rounds from 2009 through 2024, with further cuts at Hughes and speculation of additional 2025–26 rounds tied to Ergen's financial review. Indeed aggregate rating ~3.2/5, with work-life balance ~3.0 and complaints about communication. Restructuring intensity of this kind is a real execution risk: it degrades engineering and customer-operations capability precisely when the company is selling infrastructure and running a network.
- **Data privacy/cybersecurity — the single most concrete social liability.** The February 2023 ransomware attack at DISH caused a multi-day nationwide outage and extraction of customer information; multiple investor lawsuits alleged overstated operational efficiency and deficient controls. In a business holding subscriber billing, location and viewing data, this is a recurring, unhedged risk and a direct hit to cost of capital (insurance, litigation, remediation).
- **Customer treatment:** DISH has repeatedly featured among the weakest-rated pay-TV providers in ACSI-based rankings, and consumer-complaint channels show chronic billing/equipment and arbitration-clause grievances. Wireless (Boost) is a low-ARPU, high-churn brand competing against larger carriers — poor service quality compounds churn.
- **Positive offsets are thin but real:** rural/underserved connectivity is central to the licence story and to the SpaceX/AT&T transactions; safety-critical obligations (E911) are taken seriously; no significant product-safety or union-conflict controversies surfaced.

**Verdict:** High — labour and privacy risks are proven, not hypothetical, and they bite in a declining-revenue operating base.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **2 / 10**

**Rationale — this is the dominant ESG driver and it is severe:**

1. **Controlled company with near-total voting control.** Class B shares carry 10 votes. Per the July 2025 Schedule 13D/A, Ergen-affiliated entities hold ~50.7% of the economics and **~90.3% of total voting power**. Independent shareholders cannot influence board composition, compensation or strategy. EchoStar qualifies for controlled-company exemptions from majority-independent board and independent-compensation-committee requirements.
2. **CEO/Chairman duality, re-established.** In November 2025 Charles Ergen was appointed **Chairman, President and CEO**, consolidating the roles after a period of CEO churn (Akhavan era). Founder control plus dual-class plus combined chair/CEO is a triple governance discount.
3. **Capital allocation and creditor treatment are the red flag.** The company **skipped roughly $500m of debt interest payments**, weighed a Chapter 11 filing (shares fell ~23% after the WSJ report), scrapped a second contentious debt-exchange after creditor backlash, then executed a restructuring agreement with major creditors; Dish DBS exited Chapter 11 with ~$4.35bn of debt reduction. A 10.75% senior secured spectrum note stack (due 2029, plus 6.75% exchange notes and 3.875% convertible notes due 2030) signals a balance sheet priced at distressed levels. Coercive-exchange behaviour toward creditors is a governance signal that travels to equity holders.
4. **Related-party intensity.** The DISH/EchoStar combination, spectrum transfers among Ergen-affiliated entities and the family's overlapping holdings create structural conflict-of-interest exposure. Spectrum monetisation ($23bn to AT&T for 600 MHz/3.45 GHz; ~$17–20bn to SpaceX for AWS-4/H-block) was conducted under duress — FCC filings describe "severe uncertainty" as the motivation — which raises questions about whether minority holders received full value for the crown-jewel asset.
5. **Regulatory/political entanglement.** An FCC probe into 5G buildout compliance (opened May 2025) was closed in September 2025 following the spectrum sales, after reported White House intervention between Ergen and Chairman Carr. Whatever the merits, resolution via political channel rather than process is a rule-of-law/governance concern for a licence-dependent issuer. A DOJ civil fraud suit over buildout commitments was also moved to termination.
6. **Mitigants (why not 1/10):** an actual majority-independent board on paper, a genuine special committee process around the DISH transaction, no evidence of accounting restatement/fraud finding, and the SpaceX transaction structure (potential ~$11bn stake) that could crystallise value for all holders.

**Score: 2/10 — deep structural control, combined chair/CEO, and creditor-adverse capital allocation outweigh board optics.**

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **FCC (primary, critical):** spectrum licences and buildout milestones; orbital debris mitigation and 5-year deorbit rules (directly triggered by EchoStar-7); E911 and universal-service obligations; national-security/Team Telecom review of foreign-linked transactions. Licence value is the company's principal asset, so FCC goodwill is a core intangible.
- **SEC climate disclosure:** as a large accelerated filer, EchoStar would be subject to Scope 1/2 rules if the 2024 rule survives litigation (currently stayed); no voluntary disclosure to pre-empt it.
- **State-level:** California SB 253/261 (revenue >$1bn doing business in CA) and AB 1305 greenwashing rules likely reach EchoStar; CCPA/CPRA and other state privacy statutes apply directly given the breach history.
- **EU:** limited direct exposure via Hughes European operations; CSRD could capture EU subsidiaries and large customers' Scope 3 requests. **CBAM: not applicable** (no cement/steel/aluminium/fertiliser/hydrogen imports at scale).
- **ESG-linked debt:** none identified — the secured notes are spectrum-collateralised, not sustainability-linked. This is a positive: no ESG-covenant step-up risk.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **ESG RISK (Laggard)**

Governance is the binding constraint (2/10), social risks are proven rather than speculative (breach, serial layoffs, weak customer scores), and environmental disclosure is effectively non-existent for a fleet-and-network operator — offset only by a structurally modest carbon footprint and no CBAM/ESG-covenant exposure. On a conventional ESG screen this sits in the bottom decile of Communication Services.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE**

- **The thesis here is distressed asset monetisation, not sustainability** — spectrum sales to AT&T (~$23bn) and SpaceX (~$17–20bn), a potential ~$11bn SpaceX equity stake, Dish DBS's Chapter 11 exit and ~$4.35bn debt reduction. ESG factors are not the primary return driver, but they are the primary *risk* driver, and they are not diversifiable within the position.
- **Governance is a valuation discount, not a footnote.** ~90% voting control, combined Chair/President/CEO, creditor-adverse exchanges and politically brokered regulatory outcomes mean minority holders bear full downside with no governance recourse. Expect a persistent holding-company/control discount on any sum-of-the-parts value.
- **Social liabilities are cash costs.** Cybersecurity remediation and litigation, restructuring execution and churn in a declining-revenue base all draw on the same liquidity that creditors are already contesting.
- **For ESG-mandated capital: uninvestable** (no disclosure, no rating coverage, governance screen failure). **For generalist capital:** the ESG analysis supports sizing discipline and a credit-first approach — the equity is an option on spectrum/SpaceX value, and governance determines whether that option is honoured for non-controlling holders.
- **What would change the view:** publication of a Scope 1/2 emissions baseline and debris-mitigation policy; separation of Chair and CEO; a special-committee-led disposal process with independent valuation; resolution of creditor litigation and a de-levered capital structure; measurable improvement in privacy controls and customer satisfaction scores. None of these is currently signalled.
