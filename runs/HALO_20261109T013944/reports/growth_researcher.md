# Halozyme Therapeutics (NASDAQ: HALO)

## 1. Growth Story

Halozyme is not really a biotech — it is a **royalty compounder built on a drug-delivery standard**. Its ENHANZE platform (rHuPH20 hyaluronidase) converts IV biologics into subcutaneous formulations, and the company collects a royalty on partner sales with near-zero incremental cost. FY2025 revenue hit $1.397B (+37.6%) at an **83.6% gross margin and 46% FCF margin**, and FY2026 guidance was raised again in November to $1.86–1.925B. The disruptive kicker is a **second, legally distinct patent estate — MDASE** — which Halozyme is enforcing against Merck's subcutaneous Keytruda (QLEX), a Dutch court having already granted an eight-country injunction in October 2026. Investors are being asked to pay ~10x peak earnings for a royalty stream that has a known expiry date (US ENHANZE composition-of-matter Sept 2027, licensed patents to March 2029) plus a free option on a royalty/damages claim against the world's largest drug franchise.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

**ENHANZE (the base business).** The addressable pool is the $200B+ global IV biologic market, where subcutaneous conversion is worth billions in payer preference, patient convenience, and reduced infusion-center costs. Halozyme currently has **7 commercial ENHANZE products** — DARZALEX Faspro (J&J), VYVGART Hytrulo (argenx), Phesgo/Herceptin SC/MabThera SC (Roche), Rituxan Hycela — generating an implied **~$35–40B of underlying partner end-market sales** against ~$1.25B of 2026E royalties (a ~3–3.5% effective royalty rate). Penetration of the eligible IV pool is still **low-teens**, and there are **12 products in development**, with three new ENHANZE programs entering Phase 1 in Q3 2026 (Pfizer, Incyte, argenx) plus collaboration expansions with argenx, Incyte, and J&J. **The problem is not TAM — it's the clock.** The US composition-of-matter patent expires September 2027 and licensed patents lapse March 2029, so the ENHANZE TAM is time-limited rather than debate-limited.

**MDASE (the option).** This is the genuinely under-appreciated asset. The MDASE estate stems from ~7,000 engineered hyaluronidase modifications filed from 2011 and is **explicitly NOT part of the ENHANZE licensing program** — so its enforcement neither depends on nor cannibalizes the royalty base. If enforceable, it gives Halozyme a claim on **every** subcutaneous conversion attempt by a non-licensee, starting with Keytruda (~$30B franchise). Even a 1–2% royalty on US Keytruda SC would be $300–600M of annual high-margin revenue — a 25–50% uplift to peak revenue — and unlike ENHANZE it would run well past 2029. **This is the entire re-rating thesis, and it is legally binary.**

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (moderate conviction, position-sized for binary legal risk)

Halozyme scores **83.7 on Rule of 40**, compounds revenue at 39%, converts 46% of revenue to FCF, and trades at **11.6x FY2026E and 9.9x FY2027E earnings with an ~8% FCF yield**. The market is capitalizing peak earnings at a deep discount precisely because it does not believe the earnings are durable — and that is the correct default assumption. What it is *not* pricing is that Halozyme has quietly proven its second patent estate is enforceable in a European court, on the largest drug in the world, while the market is fixated on the 2027 ENHANZE cliff.

**Scenario framework (12-month):**
- **Bull ($175–190, ~30% probability):** Favorable PTAB outcome plus a US injunction or Merck settlement-licensing deal. MDASE becomes a $300–600M+ royalty stream extending the earnings duration past 2032; multiple re-rates to 15–18x.
- **Base ($125–135, ~45%):** Litigation grinds on unresolved. FY2026 beat-and-raise, 2027 EPS ~$10.40, buyback shrinks the float ~5%/yr, multiple holds ~11–12x. Modest upside plus buyback accretion.
- **Bear ($62–70, ~25%):** MDASE patents invalidated at the PTAB, European injunctions dissolve, and 2027 guidance frames peak revenue. Stock reverts to a depleting-asset multiple of ~6–7x on ~$9–10 EPS.

**Probability-weighted value ≈ $124**, or roughly **+20%** from $103.63 — with a downside that is cushioned by a genuinely enormous FCF yield and an aggressive buyback. That asymmetry justifies **Buy**, not Strong Buy.

**Why not Strong Buy:** This is a *harvest-plus-option* story, not a durable compounder. In Cathie Wood terms, ENHANZE has a spectacular TAM but a **patent-countdown clock instead of a network-effect moat**, and TAM capture actually *reverses* after 2028. That caps the multiple structurally. I would add aggressively on a PTAB win or a Merck licensing settlement, and I would exit on an adverse PTAB ruling — this is a thesis where you must trade the catalyst, not hold through it.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **The 2027/2029 patent cliff (highest-probability risk).** ENHANZE US composition-of-matter expires Sept 2027, licensed patents March 2029. Royalties are ~90% of revenue. Consensus already models revenue *declining* from 2029 — and the 2030 EPS estimate has been cut 7.5%. The bull case cannot be "growth persists"; it must be "duration extends."
2. **Binary MDASE litigation risk — both ways.** Merck has filed post-grant reviews at the USPTO. Invalidity at the PTAB would simultaneously erase the option value *and* unwind the German/Dutch injunctions. A single adverse ruling could take 25–35% out of the stock in a day.
3. **Customer and royalty-base concentration.** Royalty growth is carried by three products: DARZALEX Faspro (J&J), VYVGART Hytrulo (argenx), and Phesgo (Roche). Partner clinical failure, a pricing reset, or slower SC conversion in any one hits the top line directly.
4. **IRA drug-price negotiation.** Both DARZALEX and Keytruda are squarely in the CMS negotiation crosshairs. Lower partner net prices mechanically lower the royalty base.
5. **Competitive enzyme technology.** Alteogen's ALT-B4 is why Merck is litigating rather than licensing — Halozyme has to *enforce* rather than *win commercially*, which is slower, costlier, and less certain.
6. **Execution/scale risk from a very small base.** 423 employees supporting a $12.8B market cap and a global legal campaign against a $360B competitor. Legal spend is already visible in the operating margin.
7. **Valuation/sentiment risk.** +54% in 2026 and the stock is a crowded litigation story. A −24% single-day drawdown in May 2025 demonstrates how quickly the narrative can break. Note also the ~$2.0B of net debt (EV $14.8B vs. $12.8B cap).
