# Halozyme Therapeutics (HALO) — Value Analysis
**Price: $103.63 (Nov 6, 2026) | Market cap ~$11.8B | Net debt $2.22B | EV ~$14.0B**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow, but widening (wide-moat economics on existing contracts)**

Halozyme is not really a biotech; it is a **royalty-collection machine**. Its rHuPH20 enzyme (ENHANZE) permits large-volume biologics to be delivered subcutaneously instead of by IV infusion. Partners (J&J, Roche, argenx, BMS, Pfizer, Lilly, AbbVie) pay Halozyme a mid-single-digit royalty on sales — revenue with ~84% gross margins and near-zero incremental cost (2026 capex guided at roughly $10M).

**Moat sources:**
- **Switching costs (the real moat):** once a partner's subcutaneous formulation is approved with rHuPH20, replacing the enzyme requires new clinical/bioequivalence work and regulatory re-filing. Partners do not switch mid-cycle.
- **Intangible assets:** patent families and co-formulation IP, plus 20 years of regulatory intimacy with major pharma.
- **Efficient scale:** no realistic second supplier is embedded across this many approved products.

**But the moat has a fuse.** Core collaboration patents run to **March 2029**, with Darzalex SC/Faspro royalties contractually secured **through 2032**. Halozyme is therefore a *melting ice cube* beyond the early 2030s unless it renews the royalty base through new programs or litigation. The moat has widened operationally over five years (royalty revenue up ~6x since 2021, three ENHANZE agreements expanded in Q3 2026), but it is contractual and date-stamped, not perpetual pricing power.

**Evidence of quality:** ROIC 16.0% → 21.3% → 29.5% → **36.6%** (2022–2025), far above any reasonable WACC. Revenue CAGR ~39% with 84% gross margins.

On near-term numbers this looks cheap, and the peer screen (median biotech P/E 29.8x, HALO "premium" 36%) is misleading — it is computed on depressed trailing GAAP earnings and against drug developers, not royalty vehicles. HALO is genuinely cheaper than both its growth rate and its own history.

**The entire valuation question is duration, not the next two years.** I modelled owner earnings with an explicit patent-cliff fade (9% WACC):

- **Bear ($50–60):** royalty rates step down 2029–2032; the Keytruda SC litigation fails and Alteogen's ALT-B4 displaces the platform for new contracts. FCF peaks ~$1.05B in 2029 then declines ~8%/yr.
- **Base ($80–90):** Darzalex holds to 2032, VYVGART (+89% YoY) and Roche/BMS programs offset rate step-downs; FCF grows to ~$1.12B by 2030, then fades 4–6%/yr as patents lapse. **PV of equity ≈ $85/share.**
- **Bull ($130–150):** Halozyme converts the Merck/Keytruda SC injunction wins into a royalty (Keytruda SC at ~$15B peak × mid-single-digit royalty would add $500M+ of annual FCF) and new Phase 1 ENHANZE programs extend the runway past 2035.

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $88/share.** At $103.63 the stock trades **~18–20% above my base-case value**, and the market is implicitly capitalizing ~$1.1B+ of FCF in perpetuity with no decline — i.e., near my bull case. There is no margin of safety; a 25% discount would require ~$66–70 (where the stock sat as recently as August 2026). Note the DCF tool output was unusable (it parsed WACC as 900%), so the above is my own build; small changes in the terminal fade swing the answer by ±$20/share, which is itself the point — this is a duration bet, not a precision exercise.

**Management note:** buybacks have retired ~23% of shares since 2021 (146.8M → ~113.5M diluted), $1.54B deployed — well-executed at low multiples ($342M repurchased in 2025; $67M in Q3 2026 at $79.47). No dividend, correctly, given an ~8% FCF yield on the equity. But M&A discipline is a concern: the 2024 unsolicited $2.3B Evotec approach (rejected, stock −15%) showed empire-building instinct, the Surf Bio deal burned a $285M IPR&D charge, and $1.1B of fresh debt is now sitting in cash.

## 4. Management Quality: **7 / 10**

- Helen Torley (CEO since 2014) executed a genuine transformation from cash-burning enzyme developer to royalty compounder; capital returns have been aggressive and per-share intelligent.
- Royalty rates are contractually durable and management has repeatedly *raised* guidance through 2026 (revenue guidance lifted three times).
- **Deductions:** opportunistic large-cap M&A risk, minimal economic book equity, and dependence on litigation outcomes (Merck) that are outside management's control. Insiders own relatively little. Also, the Q3 2026 beat-and-raise was rewarded with a **−15.6% day** (Leerink cut to Market Perform on partner concentration in Merck/Keytruda) — a reminder that at 11x forward earnings a lot of good news is already paid for.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate at this price)

This is a genuinely good business — 37% ROIC, 67% EBITDA margins, ~100% FCF conversion, a widening if time-limited moat, and a shareholder-friendly share count. But the price has run from $61 to $132 and back to $104 in twelve months, and at $103.63 you are paying approximately the bull case. **"A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy" — and this one trades above fair value.**

- **Own it?** Hold; the balance sheet is sound, growth is real, and buybacks at 11x earnings compound per-share value.
- **Buy it?** Only below **~$70** (25% margin of safety to a ~$88 base case) or on a permanent resolution of the 2029–2032 royalty duration question.
- **Trim?** Above ~$130 the risk/reward inverts; that is where the bull case gets fully priced.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Royalty duration (the dominant risk).** Core collaboration patents lapse March 2029; Darzalex protection to 2032. Post-2032 the FCF stream requires *new* economics. If the base is not renewed, my base case is generous, not conservative.
2. **Partner concentration.** Darzalex (J&J) is ~45% of royalty revenue ($153.5M of $341M in Q3 2026). VYVGART (argenx) is the fastest grower (+89%) and is becoming a second pillar — dependency is shifting, not disappearing.
3. **Merck/Keytruda litigation is binary.** Halozyme has won preliminary injunctions in Germany (Dec 2025) and the Netherlands (Oct 2026), but Merck uses Alteogen's ALT-B4 enzyme — Halozyme's upside here is a litigation call option, not a base-case royalty. A loss, or an appellate reversal, removes the main growth narrative.
4. **Competing hyaluronidase platforms (ALT-B4 and others).** If new partner deals go to Alteogen, the moat narrows at exactly the moment the old patents expire.
5. **IRA / Medicare drug-price negotiation.** Management asserts zero-to-minimal royalty impact through 2035, but negotiated prices on partner drugs (Keytruda, Darzalex) shrink the royalty base — a slow, structural haircut.
6. **Capital allocation.** $1.1B of new debt held in cash plus a history of large M&A (Evotec) creates real risk of value-destructive deployment; buybacks above intrinsic value would also destroy the compounding engine.
7. **Negative equity / ~1.75x leverage** leaves less cushion than the headline margins suggest if royalties ever step down abruptly.
