## SECTOR BRIEF — HALO (Halozyme Therapeutics)

**Note on classification:** Screens tag HALO as "Biotechnology," and the sector-peer tool returns mega-cap pharma (LLY, JNJ, ABBV, MRK). Those are HALO's **customers/licensees**, not comparables. HALO is economically a **royalty-monetisation / drug-delivery licensing** business, so I built a purpose-fit comp set: Royalty Pharma (pure royalty aggregator) plus high-margin commercial-stage specialty biotech (UTHR, NBIX, EXEL, ALNY, ARGX).

---

### 1. Sector Overview

HALO sits at the intersection of two sectors: **subcutaneous (SC) biologics drug delivery** and **pharmaceutical royalty licensing**. Its ENHANZE platform (rHuPH20) enables high-dose biologics to be delivered subcutaneously instead of by IV infusion, and HALO is paid mid-to-high-single-digit royalties on partner sales rather than taking direct commercial risk. The SC biologics market is growing ~9–12% CAGR (third-party estimates range $286bn in 2026 at 11.75% CAGR to ~$499bn by 2031), driven by patient preference, home administration, and payer pressure to avoid infusion-centre costs. The royalty-licensing niche is highly concentrated and structurally high-margin: HALO, Royalty Pharma and a handful of platform owners (Alteogen's ALT-B4 being the emerging direct rival) capture economics with essentially no R&D or SG&A intensity. Critically, the sub-sector's economics are **contractually, not perpetually, protected** — HALO's core US rHuPH20 composition-of-matter patent expires **September 2027**, which is the single defining structural fact of the sector.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **IV→SC conversion is a durable, still-early-in-the-money cycle.** HALO's royalty revenue grew **+44% YoY in Q3 2026 to $341m**, with total revenue +39% to $493m; FY2026 guidance is royalty revenue of **$1.235–1.255bn (+42–45%)** on total revenue of **$1.860–1.925bn (+33–38%)**. This is being driven by existing partner products scaling (DARZALEX FASPRO, OCREVUS SC, VYVGART Hytrulo, RYBREVANT FASPRO), not just new launches — a high-incremental-margin mix shift.
- **New label expansions and partner add-ons extend the runway.** FDA approval of RYBREVANT FASPRO in head-and-neck cancer (Nov 2026) is a volume expansion on an existing royalty stream, and three current partners **expanded ENHANZE collaboration agreements** in Q3 2026 with three programs advancing to Phase 1 — evidence the licensing model is still winning new business at scale.
- **Royalty model = extreme operating leverage and cash conversion.** Gross margin **83.6%**, operating margin **57.5%** (FY2025), with only 423 employees generating ~$3.3m of revenue per head. Adjusted EBITDA is guided to **$1.240–1.290bn (+89–96%)** for FY2026, and management asserts its analysis of the CMS final rule for IPAY-2029 under OBBBA implies **zero-to-minimal royalty impact through at least 2035** — removing a tail risk the market had priced.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **The September 2027 US composition-of-matter patent cliff.** This is the dominant risk and the reason HALO trades at ~11.6x FY26 non-GAAP EPS ($8.75–9.05 guide) and ~9x adjusted EBITDA despite 35%+ growth. Royalty terms are contract-defined and Mdase/manufacturing patents run longer (EU EP622 upheld to 2029; new European patent to 2029), but the market is correctly discounting the durability of the highest-margin revenue line.
- **Substitution is now real, not theoretical — and it is litigated.** Merck's subcutaneous Keytruda uses **Alteogen's ALT-B4** recombinant hyaluronidase, not ENHANZE. HALO has won preliminary injunctions in **Germany (Dec 2025)** and, via The Hague (Oct 2026), in **Belgium, Denmark, France, Ireland, Italy, Sweden, Switzerland and the Netherlands**. The flip side: Merck's US patent challenge **invalidated a US HALO patent (May 2026)**, and The Hague separately dismissed one PI bid — outcomes are jurisdiction-dependent and binary. A global loss would validate "design-around" as an industry template.
- **Severe partner concentration plus counterparty power.** A small number of mega-cap partners (J&J, Roche, argenx, BMS, Pfizer) drive the majority of royalties. These counterparties have the legal resources to litigate, the incentive to internalise delivery tech, and the option to demand renegotiated royalty tiers post-2027 — a classic concentrated-buyer dynamic that caps long-run pricing power.

### 5. HALO's Competitive Position: **STRONG** (with a dated expiry)

**Rationale:**
- **Pricing power / moat:** Proprietary, patented enzyme with regulatory precedent across 7+ commercial products and a decade of partner-validated safety data — a genuine barrier to entry that competitors cannot replicate quickly. The 2026 injunctions in Germany and eight European markets demonstrate the patents are enforceable, not merely paper.
- **Growth vs peers:** +37.6% vs +16.0% peer median, and FY2026 guidance of +33–38% shows no deceleration — remarkable at $1.4bn of revenue.
- **Profitability:** 57.5% operating margin is best-in-class ex-RPRX; the royalty model means incremental partner sales drop through at very high rates, so EBITDA is guided to nearly double (+89–96%) in FY2026.
- **The caveat that keeps this from being "fortress":** the moat has a **known expiry date (Sept 2027 US)** and the substitution threat is now embodied in a well-funded competitor (Alteogen/ALT-B4) backed by the world's largest pharma. HALO's competitive position is *strong today and structurally impaired on a 3–5 year view*. The valuation (~9x EV/EBITDA, ~11.6x forward non-GAAP EPS) is the market pricing exactly that arc.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE** (near-to-medium term; degrading beyond 2027)

The SC-conversion cycle is one of the more durable, high-margin growth vectors in healthcare, and the royalty-licensing model converts that growth into cash at 80%+ gross margins with minimal capital intensity. HALO is the best-positioned scaled participant on growth-adjusted profitability, and its 2026 guidance raise plus the CMS IPAY-2029 clarity remove two overhangs.

I stop short of "highly attractive" because the sector's core asset class — the composition-of-matter patent — rolls off in 2027, litigation outcomes against design-around competitors are jurisdictionally inconsistent, and partner concentration gives the buy side of the table disproportionate leverage in any renewal. **Positioning implication: this is a cash-return/valuation story with a hard catalyst clock, not a durable compounder.** The investment case rests on management converting the FY2026–27 free cash flow (guided ~$1.25bn+ adjusted EBITDA) into buybacks/dividends or replacement royalty assets before the cliff hits — that, not the royalty growth rate, is what the memo should underwrite.
