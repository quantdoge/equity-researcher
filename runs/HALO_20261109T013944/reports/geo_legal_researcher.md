## HALO — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Halozyme Therapeutics (Nasdaq: HALO) | Biotech / drug-delivery royalties | $11.8B mkt cap | HQ San Diego | ~423 employees*
*Research cut-off: latest available filings/coverage = Q3 2026 (quarter ended 30-Sep-2026, reported 5-Nov-2026)*

---

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

**Structural note:** HALO is not a conventional multi-national. It is an **IP-royalty and enzyme-supply business** — ~66% of 2026E revenue is royalty revenue earned from ~15 large pharma partners on *their* end-market sales, plus bulk rHuPH20 supply, Hylenex/XYOSTED product sales, and drug-device auto-injector manufacturing (US sites: San Diego CA, Minnetonka MN, Ewing NJ, Boston MA). Physical footprint outside the US is limited to commercial/subsidiary entities in **Switzerland, Ireland, Belgium and Japan** (company profile). Geographic risk is therefore **derived from partners' end markets, not from HALO's own operations.**

The FY2025 10-K discloses revenue on a geographic basis into exactly five buckets — **United States, Switzerland, Belgium, Japan, Other foreign** (confirmed from the geographic-concentration XBRL axes in 10-K FY2025, accession 0001159036-26-000024) — and separately discloses collaborator concentration for **Roche, Janssen, BMS, argenx and Takeda**. Exact percentages could not be retrieved (SEC archive returned HTTP 403 on the filing index/summary files); the bands below are my estimates derived from partner disclosure, and are flagged as such.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Legal Risks

**Risk #1 — The Merck (MSD) MDASE patent war: a genuinely binary, multi-front dispute. Probability: 100% of continued volatility; Impact: Medium–High**
- HALO sued Merck in the **US District Court of New Jersey (2:25-cv-03179, filed 24-Apr-2025)** alleging Merck's subcutaneous **Keytruda QLEX** infringes **15 MDASE patents** filed from 2011 onward (Halozyme IR/PRNewswire, 7-Oct-2026).
- **Europe is going HALO's way:** Germany — preliminary injunction 4-Dec-2025 blocking Keytruda SC launch (Munich Regional Court); **The Hague — cross-border injunction (7-Oct-2026)** against MSD BV and Merck's EU MAH/packaging hub covering **Belgium, Denmark, France, Ireland, Italy, Sweden, Switzerland and the Netherlands**, with EP 2,797,622 held valid and infringed (Halozyme release, 7-Oct-2026; Reuters, 7-Oct-2026).
- **The US is going against HALO:** PTAB Final Written Decisions of **12-May-2026 ('600 patent) and 18-May-2026 ('262 patent)** found **all challenged claims unpatentable** for lack of written description/enablement (Venable BiologicsHQ, 22-May-2026). Roughly **12 further instituted PGRs** remain pending. The USPTO did deny institution on the '685 patent (PGR2025-00087) — a partial win.
- **UK: the UK part of the patent was revoked by consent (21-May-2026), with indemnity costs awarded against Halozyme** — a jurisdiction-specific loss that shows forum-shopping risk in Europe.
- **Why it matters economically:** Keytruda QLEX is *not* a royalty product for HALO; the case is defensive/enforcement revenue (potential damages/royalty on a mega-blockbuster SC franchise) and, critically, **precedent for the MDASE portfolio** that HALO intends to license beyond ENHANZE. Management explicitly states MDASE is *separate* from ENHANZE and that the litigation "will not impact" ENHANZE licences or fees — so the **core royalty stream is insulated**, but the optionality value of MDASE and any damages award are at risk. Keytruda QLEX did only ~$40M of sales in 2025, so near-term P&L impact is contained.
- **Watch item:** no evidence found of a Merck antitrust/Walker-Process or sham-litigation counterclaim being adjudicated; given MDASE enforcement against a rival SC platform, antitrust counter-attack remains a tail risk I could not confirm or refute from available sources.

**Risk #2 — US drug-pricing policy transmission into partner economics. Probability: Medium; Impact: Low–Medium (management-guided)**
- The **CMS final rule for the Medicare Drug Price Negotiation Program (IPAY 2029), issued 5-Nov-2026**, plus the statutory framework under the **One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA)**: HALO on 6-Nov-2026 "reaffirmed" it projects **zero-to-minimal impact to royalty revenue through at least 2035**. That is a management assertion, not an audited fact — but it is a specific, dated, falsifiable claim backed by an "IRA Commentary" disclosure channel on the IR site.
- Residual risk: **MFN/most-favoured-nation pricing** and European payer pressure compress partner net prices, and royalties are levied on *net sales*, so price deflation hits HALO revenue with a lag even where volume is protected.

**Risk #3 — Trade policy: Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs now live. Probability: 100% (in force); Impact: Low directly, Low–Medium indirectly**
- **Section 232 pharma tariffs took effect 31-Jul-2026**: 100% on patented pharmaceutical imports into the US under a tiered structure, with **15% for EU and Japan-origin, 10% for UK-origin**, and exemptions/onshoring-deal carve-outs (WilmerHale, 15-Oct-2026; Crowell; White House presidential action, Apr-2026).
- HALO's royalty receipts are **IP-based and not dutiable**, and its proprietary products (Hylenex, XYOSTED) and rHuPH20 bulk supply are US-manufactured. Direct exposure is therefore small. Indirect exposure: (i) partners' US import economics for EU-manufactured SC products, and (ii) **HALO's own exports of bulk rHuPH20/enzyme and auto-injector devices to EU/Japanese partners** face reciprocal/counter-tariff risk not quantified in any source I found. EU retaliation against US biopharma is the under-modelled tail.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights
- **Litigation docket (multi-jurisdiction, active):** US D.N.J. (2:25-cv-03179) patent case; 12+ instituted PGRs at PTAB; German PI (Munich, 4-Dec-2025); Dutch cross-border PI (The Hague, 7-Oct-2026); UK revocation with **indemnity costs against HALO**. Adverse costs and cross-undertakings in European PI practice are a real (unquantified) cash exposure — a Dutch/German PI can require a damages undertaking if later overturned.
- **Regulators:** US FDA (product approvals — e.g. RYBREVANT FASPRO accelerated approval in HNSCC, 6-Nov-2026); EMA/national EU patent courts; CMS (IPAY negotiation); USPTO/PTAB. **No DOJ, SEC enforcement, FCPA, OFAC or FTC action against HALO was identified in any source reviewed.**
- **M&A / integration legal risk:** Elektrofi (Hypercon™, closed Nov-2025) and Surf Bio (Aerocon™, closed Dec-2025) — new IP estates to diligence and enforce; Surf Bio/Fresenius-related licence terms and the "McDermott Laboratories (Viatris)" arrangement add contract-complexity.
- **Governance/financial:** multiple convertible notes outstanding (0.25% 2027, 1.00% 2028, 0.00% 2031, 0.875% 2032) — Irish-domiciled issuance structure typical of US biotech; conversion/hedge documentation is a standard legal review item, not a risk flag.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags
- **None material.** No sanctions exposure (no Russia/Iran/Syria/Cuba nexus), no AML/MFSA surface (no consumer payments), no FCPA pattern (423 employees, Western-jurisdiction partners, no disclosed third-party-agent reliance in high-risk markets).
- **Two watch items, both low:** (i) **GDPR/EU data-protection** obligations flowing from EU commercial subsidiaries and EU-based partners — low data intensity; (ii) **indirect China exposure** via J&J's China approvals of ENHANZE-based products, which imports US–China trade/export-control headline risk into a royalty stream HALO does not control.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**
*(Geopolitical sub-rating: **LOW–MEDIUM**; Legal/regulatory sub-rating: **HIGH**)*

Rationale: the operating footprint is almost entirely US + Western Europe + Japan, with no emerging-market asset base, no sanctions exposure and no China manufacturing — which on a pure country-risk screen would score **Low**. That is offset by (a) an unusually **active, multi-jurisdiction, competitively consequential patent war with Merck** that has already produced adverse PTAB rulings and an adverse UK costs order alongside favourable EU injunctions, and (b) **extreme counterparty concentration** — Roche, J&J/Janssen, argenx, BMS and Takeda carry the royalty book, so a single partner's pricing, supply-chain or geopolitical shock transmits directly to HALO's revenue. Policy exposure (IPAY-2029 Medicare negotiation, MFN, Section 232 tariffs) is real but, on management's own dated guidance, **not material to royalty revenue through 2035**.
