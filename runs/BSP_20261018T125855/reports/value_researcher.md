# BSP — Bending Spoons S.p.A.
**Equity Value Research | Price $47.07 (16-Oct-2026) | Mkt cap ≈$28.2–29.9B | EV ≈$30.2B**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow (trending toward None)**

**What it is:** An Italian acquirer of mature consumer software properties. It buys products with proven product-market fit — Evernote, Meetup, WeTransfer, StreamYard, Vimeo, AOL — rewrites the codebase with AI-assisted full-stack teams, raises prices, and strips cost. Founder-led, 2,187 employees.

**Moat evidence, honestly assessed:**

| Moat source | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Network effects | **Absent.** These are single-user utilities. |
| Switching costs | **Low.** Consumer note-taking, file transfer, meeting tools have trivial switching costs. B2B video (Vimeo/StreamYard) has some, not much. |
| Intangible assets | **Weak and decaying.** Brand equity in Evernote/AOL is a wasting asset, not a compounding one. |
| Cost advantage | **Real but not durable.** 65.6% gross margin and near-zero reported capex reflect software economics, not a protected position. |
| Efficient scale | **No.** Highly contestable markets. |

**The one genuine asset is a capability, not a moat:** a repeatable operating system for integrating and monetizing acquisitions. It is real — revenue went $387M → $671M → $1,306M (83.7% CAGR), and gross margin *expanded* 61.1% → 63.9% → 65.6%, which is the signature of pricing power plus cost discipline on acquired assets. But a capability that must be re-purchased on every deal is not a moat; it is a treadmill.

**The decisive datapoint — does the moat widen?** No, and that is the crux:

> **ROIC: 11.6% (2023) → 8.5% (2024) → 7.6% (2025)**

Returns on capital are *falling* as scale rises, and 7.6% sits **below** any reasonable cost of capital for this business (beta 1.48, Italian small-cap-turned-mega-cap, $2.7B levered). Growth is being bought at prices that earn sub-WACC returns. Goodwill and intangibles are $3.50B against $4.76B of total assets (74%); **tangible book value is ≈ −$2.51B (−$4.19/share)**. Every incremental dollar of "growth" is a cheque written, and the returns on those cheques are deteriorating.

**Circle of competence:** I can understand this business. What I cannot underwrite is a model whose revenue is a function of the size and price of the next acquisition — that is a capital-allocation bet, not a franchise.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Strengths**
- Revenue +94.7% (2025), +73.4% (2024); H1-2026 revenue $1,305M ≈ all of FY2025 — FY26 run-rate ~$2.8–3.0B
- Gross margin 65.6%, expanding; operating margin 21.3%
- FCF positive and rising: $55M → $204M → $290M; FCF/EBITDA 50% → 90% → 71%
- Q2-26 beat: revenue $704M vs $683M est, EPS $0.46 vs $0.24 est

**Weaknesses — and they are serious**
- **Net income ≈ $0 (−$136K) in FY2025** despite $411M EBITDA. Interest expense went $17M → $33M → $143M in two years. **EBIT/interest coverage is only 1.8x** — thin for a serial acquirer.
- **Leverage:** total debt $2.67B, net debt $2.04B = **5.0x FY25 EBITDA** (~2.4x on 2026 run-rate, but only if the EBITDA ramp holds and no new deal is struck).
- **Reported FCF overstates reality.** Capex of $0.5M against $1.3B revenue is a classification artifact — real reinvestment sits in capitalized software and, far larger, in acquisitions. Acquisitions are *growth capex*, not free cash. SBC of $55.5M is also a genuine cost to owners.
- FY2025 FCF is flattered by ~$111M of non-cash addbacks bridging $111M pretax income to zero net income. I treat the $290M as roughly real cash generation of the *current* portfolio — but it excludes the cost of replacing the earnings base as these apps decay.
- No dividend, no buybacks. Zero return of capital.

## 3. Valuation — No Margin of Safety

| Metric | FY2025 | 2026 run-rate (est.) |
|---|---|---|
| EV/EBITDA | **73.6x** | ~35x |
| P/FCF | **97.2x** | ~60x |
| FCF yield | **1.03%** | ~1.5% |
| P/B | **28.4x** (BVPS $1.66) | — |

**What the price requires.** Working backwards from EV $30.2B, WACC 11%, terminal growth 3%, the market is discounting roughly **25–32% annual FCF growth for a full decade**, i.e. FCF from ~$0.4B to ~$4.5B+ by 2036. That is ~10x growth, achieved by a company whose ROIC is 7.6% and falling. The growth would have to be bought — and bought *better* than it has been.

**My own scenarios (10-yr DCF, WACC 11%, terminal 3%):**

| Scenario | FCF path | Equity value | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bear (harvest, no new deals, decay) | ~flat | ~$6.3B | **~$10** |
| **Base** (20% yrs 1–5, 8% yrs 6–10) | $420M → $1.5B | $10.5B | **~$18** |
| Bull (30% yrs 1–5, 12% yrs 6–10, WACC 10%) | $420M → $2.7B | $22.2B | **~$37** |

**Intrinsic value: ~$20–25/share central (~$22).** Note the discipline of this result: **even my bull case — including a lower discount rate — fails to reach $47.07.** The current price embeds assumptions beyond the optimistic case.

Cross-check: the tool's own DCF, generously extrapolating 30% FCF growth for ten years at 11%, yields $25.8B EV → ~$39.7/share — still ~16% *above* fair value at today's price. Sell-side targets ($36, $36, $40, $45, $47, $48, $72) have a median ≈$44, already **below the market price**, and a 2x spread that signals no analytical consensus.

**Verdict: ~50% overvalued. Negative margin of safety.** To own this with a 30% discount to a $24 base, I would need ~$17.

## 4. Management Quality: **7 / 10 — capable, honest operators; empire-building incentive**

**Positives:** Founder-led (Luca Ferrari, co-founder/CEO) with substantial post-IPO insider ownership and an explicit long-duration mandate ("hold acquired products for the long term"). The operating track record is genuinely impressive: margin expansion on acquired assets, threefold revenue growth, real FCF from near-zero-capex software. They have raised and serviced debt skillfully.

**Negatives:** Capital allocation is entirely M&A — no buybacks, no dividend, no organic growth engine. Incentives favor scale (and the executives' equity is worth more at a higher market cap) over per-share value. Declining ROIC is the empirical test of that incentive, and it is failing. As a foreign private issuer the disclosure burden is lighter than a domestic filer's. The model's reliance on price increases plus headcount reduction carries reputational and regulatory tail risk in Europe.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

Not a "Strong Sell" — the business generates real cash, the operators are competent, and the franchise is not broken. But Buffett's first filter is price, and this fails it decisively:

- Wide-moat business? **No — narrow at best, and narrowing** (ROIC 11.6% → 7.6%).
- Consistent high returns on capital? **No — below cost of capital.**
- Margin of safety? **Negative.** Paying ~2x a generous intrinsic value.
- Understandable 10-year economics? **No** — the earnings stream depends on unknowable future deal prices.

> *"For every dollar retained, have at least one dollar of market value created."* Bending Spoons retains everything and reinvests into acquisitions earning 7.6%. At 73x EBITDA and a 1.0% FCF yield, the buyer is prepaying a decade of flawless, value-accretive M&A. That is a speculation on capital allocation, not an investment in a franchise.

**What would change my mind:** ROIC stabilizing above 12%; a demonstrated organic growth line (not just acquired revenue); net debt/EBITDA under 2x while *not* diluting; FCF yield above 4% (≈$20–22/share); and 8+ quarters of public reporting establishing that acquired app cohorts do not decay faster than amortization assumes. Then I would look again with interest.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to owning it (downside):**
1. **AI commoditization is an existential threat, not a tail risk.** Evernote, WeTransfer, Remini, Splice-style utilities are precisely what general-purpose LLMs and multimodal models absorb for free. The company's own AI tooling lowers the barrier for competitors too.
2. **Churn acceleration from price increases.** The growth model is "raise price on a shrinking base." That works until it doesn't, and the inflection is not visible in advance.
3. **Refinancing and coverage risk.** 1.8x EBIT/interest with $2.67B debt; rates or a covenant breach would force a dilutive equity raise.
4. **One bad acquisition permanently impairs the thesis** — $3.5B of goodwill against $995M of equity leaves almost no buffer.
5. **Lockup expiry** (~180 days post the 1-Jul-2026 IPO) is a near-term supply overhang.
6. **Price action is disordered:** a 47% two-day spike (14–15 Oct, $37.86 → $55.77 on 19.7M shares) followed by a reversal to $47.07, and a 23% decline on 13-Aug *despite* an earnings beat. Unexplained by data available to me; either way, it is not a price set by fundamentals.

**Risks to my cautious view (I may be wrong if):** acquisition multiples stay low and integration keeps working, EBITDA compounds faster than my 20% base, margin expansion continues past 65%, and index inclusion re-rates the shares. The bull case is real — it just tops out near $37.

**Appropriate action: avoid. If held, trim into strength. Revisit below $20.**
