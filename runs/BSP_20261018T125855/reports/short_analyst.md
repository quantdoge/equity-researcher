## FORENSIC SHORT-SELLER ANALYSIS — BSP (Bending Spoons S.p.A.)
*Price $47.07 · Mkt cap $29.9B · EV ≈ $34.1B (6/30/26 net debt) · Nasdaq IPO 7/1/26*

**Bottom line up front:** This is **not** a fraud short. It is a **quality-of-earnings, leverage and multiple-compression short**. The company is a genuinely well-executed app roll-up, but 126% headline growth masks **3% organic growth**, GAAP net income is manufactured by a volatile tax line, and the entire $1.26B of book equity sits on $6.3B of acquired intangibles against **negative $5.0B of tangible equity**.

---

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **LOW**
(Not "Fraudulent Risk" — I found no evidence of fabricated revenue. But headline metrics materially overstate economic reality.)

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $387.1M | $671.1M | $1,306.4M | $1,305.5M |
| GAAP operating income | $84.3M | $127.4M | $277.9M | $360.4M |
| GAAP net income | $160.8M | $89.5M | **–$0.1M** | $204.5M |
| Operating cash flow | $59.2M | $204.9M | $290.6M | $206.3M |
| Interest expense | $17.2M | $32.6M | $142.6M | $201.2M |

The forensic tell is the **net income line vs. pre-tax income**: FY2025 pre-tax was ~$135.3M and net income was ~–$0.1M — an **implied ~100% effective tax rate**. Two quarters later the tax line flips to a **benefit** (Q2'26 pre-tax $132.3M → net income $177.0M, ~$45M of tax benefit). A company whose EPS is being swung $45M–$135M per period by a tax line has **no reliable earnings anchor**. Q2's $0.29 GAAP / $0.46 adjusted EPS was partly a tax artefact.

Meanwhile FY2023 net income ($160.8M) exceeded EBIT ($74.7M) by ~$86M — **non-operating gains funded 53% of that year's profit**, and operating cash flow was only $59.2M (37% conversion). That is the year the accruals ratio flashed **+10.85% of assets** (warning).

## 2. Key Red Flags

1. **Organic growth is collapsing — this is the whole thesis.** 13% (FY25) → **6% (Q1'26) → 3% (Q2'26)**, against a reported +126%. Within the portfolio, **Remini and Splice declined**; AOL, Eventbrite and Vimeo grew "low single digits." The 3% is likely price-led on stagnant/declining user bases (the Evernote/WeTransfer/Meetup playbook: cut 60–80% of staff, raise prices).
2. **Margins are borrowed from the future.** Q2'26: R&D **8.3%** of revenue, S&M **10.3%** — combined **18.6%**, vs. HubSpot ~68% and Datadog ~70% on the same lines. The 54% "adjusted" operating margin is purchased by under-investing in product and demand generation. That is *why* organic growth is 3% and falling. It is a self-liquidating margin, not a durable one.
3. **Adjusted-vs-GAAP gap is large and widening.** Q2'26 adjusted operating income $381M (54%) vs GAAP $240M (34%) — a **$141M quarterly adjustment** ($51M "reorganization" + ~$84M intangible amortization). Adjusted EPS is 37% above GAAP. Serial-acquirer amortization add-backs are defensible in principle, but "reorganization costs" appearing every single quarter for eight straight acquisitions are simply the cost of the business model.
4. **GAAP profit has never been delivered at scale.** Full-year GAAP net income: $161M (23) → $90M (24) → **–$0.1M (25)**. Consensus FY2027 net income of **$1.46B** implies a **33.6% net margin** — a margin this company has never once printed.
5. **Receivables ran ahead of revenue in FY2024**: AR +127.9% vs revenue +73.4% (flagged). Normalized in FY25, but receivable days have drifted from ~33 to ~40.
6. **Equity is an accounting construct.** Goodwill + intangibles **$6.30B = 78.2% of $8.06B assets**; equity only $1.26B (**15.6% of assets**); **tangible book value –$5.04B**. Any impairment on Remini/Splice/WeTransfer flows straight through a thin equity stub.
7. **Dilution:** shares 502M → 559M → 587M → 620M (≈5–7%/yr pre-IPO), plus $55.5M SBC in FY25 (2.2× YoY), plus 10M new shares to Berkshire at $33.

## 3. Beneish M-Score & Accruals

**M-Score = –2.122** → "moderate risk" band (softer threshold –2.22). I will be honest about what drives it: the score is **mechanically inflated by SGI = 1.947** (Beneish penalises high sales growth) plus LVGI 1.194 and SGAI 1.128. For a serial acquirer, M-Score is a blunt instrument and I do **not** read this as manipulation evidence.

**Accruals:** FY25 –6.1%, FY24 –6.1% (both healthy); **FY23 +10.85% (warning)**. **Piotroski F = 4/9** (weak: negative ROA, ROA decline, rising leverage, dilution, falling asset turnover). **Altman Z = 0.65 — distress zone**, though I discount this: the model punishes the negative working capital (–$761M) that is normal for a subscription business carrying $2.1B of current liabilities including deferred revenue. Use it only as a leverage warning, not a solvency call.

**Cash conversion (the bear-relevant version):** Q2'26 OCF $130.6M vs net income $177.0M = **74%**, with a **–$138.8M working capital drag**. TTM OCF is roughly **$377M**. Critically:

> **H1 2026 interest expense ($201.2M) consumed 97.5% of H1 2026 operating cash flow ($206.3M).**

TTM interest ~$289M on TTM EBITDA ~$855M = **~3.0× coverage**; on the Q2 annualized interest run-rate (~$432M) coverage is **~2.0×**. And that was *before* the Oct 2 closing of $1.25B + €395M of term loans. Cash flow is real — but it belongs to the lenders.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

- **The asset base is the competition.** They buy decelerating assets at 2.3–2.7× ARR (Airtable $1.285B/2.7×, Miro $1.355B/2.3× — $1.08B of ARR for $2.64B in 37 days). Each deal must be re-engineered to 54% margins within quarters. **Airtable had 935 employees — more than the entire ~700-person "Spooner" core team.** Integration slip is not a tail risk; it is the base case arithmetic.
- **Platform dependency:** margins are struck after Apple/Google app-store commissions on consumer apps (Remini, Meetup, Evernote). Regulatory relief is upside; platform fee changes are the downside.
- **AI commoditisation:** management states AI writes ≥90% of its code. That is presented as an efficiency edge, but it is also the exact force that makes single-purpose consumer apps (Evernote, WeTransfer, Harvest) easy to substitute. Consumption of the moat by the same tool used to build it.
- **Reputational decay:** repeated 60–80% post-acquisition layoffs (WeTransfer ~75%), aggressive price hikes, documented churn at Evernote. Acquired brands are harvested, not cultivated — finite by construction.

## 5. Management & Governance Red Flags

- **Cluster of five Form 4 filings, all dated 2026-09-25** — direction not resolvable from the filing metadata available to me (gap noted). Timing is notable: they land right after the stock fell from $41 to the $32–33 post-earnings trough.
- **Lock-up expiry ~early January 2027** (180 days post-7/2/26). A concrete, dated supply catalyst for a stock where insiders still hold the majority of shares.
- **Placement pricing:** Berkshire's 10M shares were struck at **$33 on Oct 14**, when the stock closed $37.86 the prior day and printed $47.07 two days later. The most sophisticated buyer in the market negotiated a **13% discount**; retail paid $51–55. Shares were also issued below market = **dilution at the worst price**.
- **Management framing:** "we do not optimise for organic growth." Convenient, and precisely the metric that matters if the M&A window closes. Guidance for FY26 revenue ($2.78–2.82B) landed **below** the $2.9B consensus — the stock fell 16% the next day. Promotional language is outrunning delivery.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary

**The bear case in one line:** *You are paying ~8–10× revenue and ~40× TTM EBITDA (EV $34.1B / TTM EBITDA $854M) for a portfolio whose organic growth is 3% and decelerating, financed with ~$5B+ of debt at ~8%, where the equity cushion is negative in tangible terms and every incremental dollar of "growth" requires buying another declining asset at 3–4× cheaper than the market is valuing the parent.*

The reflexivity is the kill shot: BSP's own multiple is the arbitrage. They buy revenue at 2.3–2.7× ARR and the market marks it at 8–10× revenue **the same day**. If the multiple compresses even to a plain acquirer's multiple — or if one Airtable/Miro integration stumbles and organic growth goes to 0% while interest costs $450M/yr — the equity does not de-rate 15%; it de-rates 40%+. Q2 already showed the mechanism working in reverse: revenue *beat*, but below-consensus guidance took 16% off the stock in one session. High beta (1.48), >20% single-day moves, and a $33 institutional print establish that the marginal buyer is narrative-driven.

**The other side — and it is strong.** I will not pretend otherwise:
- **Berkshire has committed ~$930M** ($600M at IPO + $330M on 10/14) for ~4%. That is a real endorsement of the model, not a meme.
- **Both Moody's and S&P upgraded**; S&P projects ~€490M pro forma free operating cash flow and "solid FOCF." Leverage the company quotes is ~2.4× at 6/30, not distressed.
- The **operating execution is genuinely exceptional**: revenue per core employee >$4M annualised, real GAAP operating margins (34%) that most SaaS companies never reach, deals underwritten to a 25% unlevered IRR, adjusted EPS +167% with only 3% dilution.
- **Accruals are negative and cash conversion is fine.** There is no smoking gun in the numbers. Any "fraud" framing would be wrong and would get run over.

**Where I could be wrong:** if Airtable and Miro transform as cleanly as Evernote/WeTransfer did, FY27 EBITDA of ~$2.5B against a $34B EV is 13.5× — not expensive, and buybacks or index inclusion could squeeze shorts violently.

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **SHORT** (moderate conviction — *not* Strong Short)

**Sizing and expression:** this is a **quality-of-earnings and multiple short**, not a solvency short. Position small, express via puts or a hedged/paired structure, and respect the squeeze: Berkshire halo, post-IPO momentum, and a 1.48 beta with 20%+ daily ranges are hostile terrain for naked shorts. **Catalysts:** Q3 print (Nov 2026 — watch organic growth for the 3% → 0–1% move, and watch whether the tax benefit repeats), Airtable/Miro integration headlines and layoffs, rising rates on ~$5B+ floating/refinancing exposure, **lock-up expiry ~Jan 2027**, and the first impairment on Remini/Splice.

**Verdict:** The bull case is stronger than a typical short target's — which is exactly why the multiple is now priced for flawless execution of a strategy whose only measurable organic output is 3% and falling. I would not short the business. I would short the price.
