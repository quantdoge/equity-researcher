# Sector Brief — BSP (Bending Spoons S.p.A.)

**Sector:** Technology / Software – Application. **Sub-sector (the one that actually matters):** consumer-app & software portfolio "roll-up" — acquiring mature, cash-generative digital products and re-engineering them for profit. BSP IPO'd 2026-07-01 at $40.50 first close; last price $47.07, market cap ~$29.9B.

---

## 1. Sector Overview

BSP competes not in a single product market but in the market for **orphaned software assets** — apps with users, brand and revenue but sub-scale economics (Evernote, WeTransfer, Vimeo, Eventbrite, AOL, Meetup, StreamYard, Komoot, Harvest, Tractive, Remini, Splice). The buyer side is highly concentrated (a handful of scaled consolidators: Constellation Software, Tiny, IAC, Ziff Davis, AppLovin, BSP), while the asset side is fragmented — thousands of seller candidates and no natural floor on price. The defining sector economics are **negative-to-low organic growth offset by acquired growth and post-close margin engineering**: across our peer set FY25 revenue growth ranged from −37% (IAC) to +70% (APP), median **+1.9%**, while EBITDA margins ranged 2%–80%. The sector is in an **early-institutional phase of the consolidation cycle** — Berkshire's ~$930M commitment to BSP (total, incl. $330M private placement on 2026-10-14 at ~$33/share) is the clearest signal that the model has gained blue-chip legitimacy. Note: FMP's sector-aggregate endpoint returned a single-company sample per period, so peer medians below are hand-built from individual filings.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Deep, cheap acquisition pipeline.** AI disruption has made legacy consumer-software owners anxious sellers; buyers with public equity or cheap debt set the clearing price. BSP has closed ~9+ deals since 2023, and revenue more than tripled in two years ($387M FY23 → $671M FY24 → $1,306M FY25).
- **AI-driven cost deflation across the portfolio.** BSP rewrites acquired codebases onto standardised Python/FastAPI/TypeScript stacks; with 2,187 staff on $1.31B revenue it runs **~$597k revenue per employee**. Sector-wide, the same tooling is compressing R&D and support costs, structurally lifting post-close margins (BSP EBITDA margin 31.4%, above the 25.7% peer median).
- **Platform-tax relief and monetisation headroom.** Apple's August 2026 EU changes under the DMA eliminate the initial acquisition and store-services fees and cut commission to 26% (10% for reduced-rate tiers) — a direct gross-margin tailwind for app-based portfolios. Separately, ARPU uplift on installed bases is potent: per S-1 data, BSP's revenue per MAU was ~50% higher in 2025 than 2021 with MAUs up >5x.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Structural organic decay.** Median peer growth of +1.9% and many assets in outright decline (IAC −37%, Dropbox −1%; BSP's own Remini and Splice declined in Q2 2026, per management). Growth is bought, so headline 94.7% overstates franchise health — and the model requires an ever-larger deal to move the needle at $30B market cap.
- **Platform and distribution cost inflation.** App-store commissions, hosting and payment fees remain the single largest COGS block for consumer apps; BSP's **65.6% gross margin is the lowest in its peer set** (median 72.3%, AppLovin 87.9%). Regulatory outcomes (EU DMA, CMA, per-install fees) remain contested and can cut both ways.
- **Leverage and accounting scrutiny.** FY25 interest expense of **$142.6M consumed ~51% of EBIT ($277.9M)**, leaving net income at roughly breakeven (−$0.1M) despite a 21.3% operating margin. Roll-up accounting (intangibles, capitalised development, "organic growth" definitions) is under active press scrutiny (WSJ), and rising cost of capital directly impairs the arbitrage that funds the strategy.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Strong (with caveats)**

- **Share/scale:** fastest grower in the peer set by a wide margin (94.7% vs 1.9% median) and the largest pure-play app roll-up by enterprise value; it is arguably *setting* the clearing price for distressed consumer software, which is genuine structural power.
- **Margins:** above median on both operating margin (+1.7pp) and EBITDA margin (+5.7pp) despite the **worst gross margin in the group** (−6.7pp vs median) — i.e. BSP wins on cost discipline (SG&A 35% of revenue, post-close headcount cuts), not on product pricing power.
- **Weak spots:** (i) growth is inorganic and the model is capital-hungry; (ii) leverage is the highest in the group on our read, with interest cover ~1.9x EBIT; (iii) accounting quality and "organic growth" definitions attract scepticism; (iv) the asset base (note-taking, file transfer, video, events, email) sits directly in the path of AI-native substitutes.
- **Validation:** Berkshire's ~4% stake (~$930M) plus a $330M placement at a **discount** to market is both an endorsement and a reminder that the smart money bought materially below the current $47.07.

## 6. Sector Rating: **Neutral**

Structurally attractive *for scaled operators with an integration edge* — cheap assets, AI-driven cost deflation, deregulating distribution — but unattractive for the average participant: median organic growth of ~2%, a ~26–34% platform/COGS tax, secular substitution risk from AI-native apps, and a small, concentrated buyer set that competes away deal returns. BSP is the best-executing name in a sector whose underlying product markets are mature-to-declining; the sector therefore supports BSP's operating model but not its current 23x sales multiple. **Sector: Neutral (constructive bias for best-in-class consolidators).**
