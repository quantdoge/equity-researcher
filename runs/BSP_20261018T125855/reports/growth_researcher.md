# Bending Spoons S.p.A. (NASDAQ: BSP) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $47.07 | Market cap ~$29.9B | EV ~$34B est. | Sector: Technology / Application Software | Italy | IPO 1 Jul 2026**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Bending Spoons is not a software innovator — it is an **AI-enabled acquisition machine for under-monetized digital assets**, and that distinction is the entire investment case. It buys proven-but-stagnant consumer and SMB software (AOL, Vimeo, Eventbrite, Evernote, WeTransfer, Brightcove, Airtable, and reportedly Miro), rewrites the codebase with a small full-stack/AI-augmented engineering team, strips cost, re-prices, and harvests cash flow — converting $1.31B of 2025 revenue into $290M of free cash flow while growing 95%. The disruptive thesis is a permanent-capital roll-up that arbitrages the gap between legacy internet assets' trapped ARR and public-market growth multiples: Airtable was bought at **2.7x ARR** while BSP itself trades at **~11x forward revenue**. Berkshire Hathaway has now put in $930M (4% stake), validating the model with the ultimate "permanent capital" endorsement.

The catch, and it is a large one: **organic growth is ~3% and decelerating.** Strip out acquisitions and this is a low-growth subscription portfolio with a 97% like-for-like net revenue retention rate.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

| Layer | Size | BSP position |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer subscription apps / digital content | ~$250B+ global | 500M+ MAU, ~9M paying subs (Mar-26) → <1% monetized |
| SMB / mid-market workflow SaaS | ~$200B+ | Airtable ($480M ARR, +20%), Miro, Harvest |
| Video / streaming infrastructure & creator tools | ~$30B | Vimeo, Brightcove, StreamYard |
| Events & marketplaces | ~$10B | Eventbrite, Meetup |

TAM is not the constraint. Even at $2.8B of 2026 revenue BSP has captured low-single-digit penetration of a several-hundred-billion-dollar pool. The **binding constraint is deal flow and integration throughput**: 2,187 employees absorbing AOL, Vimeo, Brightcove, Eventbrite, Airtable and Miro within roughly eighteen months. The true addressable market is "the installed base of under-monetized legacy internet assets," which is large and — importantly — *counter-cyclical*, since distressed sellers come to them. That is a real, differentiated edge: BSP is the buyer of last resort for assets nobody else wants, at 2–3x revenue.

**Differentiation:** proprietary AI-assisted codebase standardization (Python/FastAPI/TypeScript) enabling one team to run dozens of products; a permanent-capital structure that tolerates short-term revenue decline; and now a public currency plus Berkshire's balance-sheet halo for larger deals.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (medium conviction, position-size discipline)

The valuation is not demanding for the growth being delivered *if* the roll-up continues:

| Multiple | 2026E | 2027E |
|---|---|---|
| EV / Revenue | 12.1x | 7.8x |
| EV / EBITDA | 22.5x | 13.4x |
| P/E | 34.6x | **19.3x** |
| PEG (P/E ÷ growth) | 0.30 | 0.35 |

*(The tool-reported 73.6x EV/EBITDA and 97x P/FCF are on stale 2025 EBITDA of $411M and should be ignored.)*

At 19x 2027 EPS for 55% consensus growth, with gross margins expanding, FCF inflecting, and Berkshire anchoring at $33, the asymmetry favors owning it. But I am deliberately not calling this a Strong Buy, because the growth is **bought, not earned** — 3% organic and a 97% LFL NRR means the compounding engine is the capital markets, not the customer base. Every quarter that the multiple depends on is a quarter in which management must find, close, and integrate a $1B+ asset. That is a spread business wearing a growth multiple, and spreads compress when capital gets expensive or deal flow dries up.

**What would move me to Strong Buy:** two consecutive quarters of like-for-like growth accelerating above 5%, plus a clean Airtable integration with disclosed post-synergy margins.
**What would move me to Sell:** LFL growth turning negative, a leverage ratio above ~4x net debt/EBITDA, or a skipped M&A quarter with revenue guidance cut.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Organic growth is ~3% and decelerating (from ~6%).** This is the thesis risk. Reported growth of 114% in 2026 is arithmetic, not demand. If the M&A pipeline pauses for even two quarters, revenue growth collapses to low-single digits while the stock still trades at ~11x sales.
2. **Leverage and interest burden.** ~$4.4B+ of debt post-2025 (term loan B add-on placed Sept 2026), with quarterly interest expense of $108M against $240M of GAAP operating income in Q2'26. The $4.6B acquisition spree has been debt-funded; Berkshire's $930M is equity relief, not a cure.
3. **Integration saturation.** AOL, Vimeo, Brightcove, Eventbrite, Airtable and Miro absorbed in ~18 months by 2,187 employees. The model's failure mode is not a bad deal — it is doing too many at once and letting product quality decay, which then shows up as the NRR decline already visible at 97%.
4. **AI as a headwind, not just a tool.** Evernote, AOL, Eventbrite and Airtable compete in categories where AI-native entrants are deflationary for incumbents. The Airtable Trends data (−45% YoY) is a live illustration that the acquired assets may be in structural decline faster than BSP can re-price them.
5. **Monetization-model regulation.** Aggressive upsell, trial-to-paid conversion and price-increase tactics are directly in scope of FTC "click-to-cancel" and EU subscription rules — a single adverse rule could hit the revenue-lever mechanics across the whole portfolio.
6. **Key-person and governance concentration.** A Milan-based founder-controlled structure, no dividend, a one-quarter public reporting history, and a large GAAP-vs-adjusted gap (Q2'26 adjusted operating income $381M vs. GAAP $240M; adjusted net margin 42% vs. reported ~25%).
7. **Dilution and float.** 10M new shares to Berkshire at $33 plus a Jan-2027 lock-up expiry on a thin float; expect continued issuance as the acquisition currency.
