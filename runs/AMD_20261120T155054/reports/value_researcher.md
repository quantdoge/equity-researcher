# AMD — Value Investment Analysis
**Price $446.23 (20 Nov 2026) | Market cap ~$730B | Fully diluted (incl. OpenAI warrant) ~1.80B sh**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: Narrow (widening in CPUs, unproven in AI accelerators)**

AMD is a fabless designer with two very different economic characters inside one ticker:

- **Server/client CPU (EPYC, Ryzen) — genuinely moated.** x86 sockets carry real switching costs (validation cycles, software stacks, platform lock-in), and AMD's chiplet architecture plus TSMC process parity gave it a cost/performance advantage that Intel's manufacturing stumble turned into share. This is a *narrow but widening* moat, earned largely by a competitor's failure.
- **AI accelerators (Instinct, Helios racks) — no durable moat yet.** Q3 FY26 data-center revenue was a record $8.5B, +97% YoY, but it is being won on performance-per-dollar against a CUDA-locked incumbent, sold to hyperscalers who explicitly want a second source. The tell: AMD granted OpenAI a warrant for **160M shares (~10% of the company)** to secure the 6GW commitment. A business with true pricing power does not pay a customer 10% of its equity to buy its product. Non-GAAP gross margin of 56% (vs. NVIDIA's ~70%+) is the quantitative signature of a price-taker in that segment.
- Structural advantages are real: fabless capital intensity, TSMC partnership, IP portfolio (Xilinx), and now rack-scale system capability.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **8 / 10**

| Gross margin (GAAP) | 46.1% | 49.4% | 49.5% | 54% (Q3'26) |
| Operating margin (GAAP) | 1.8% | 7.4% | 10.7% | 21% (Q3'26) |

| FCF ($B) | 1.12 | 2.41 | 6.74 | 7.06 |
| ROIC (reported) | 0.7% | 3.2% | 5.5% | ~12%+ |

**What's good:** Explosive, margin-accretive growth with visible operating leverage (non-GAAP op margin 29% in Q3'26). Fortress balance sheet: net cash ~$1.1B, debt $4.5B, equity $63B. FCF conversion is fine on a TTM basis (93% of net income).

**What's not:** Reported ROIC of 5.5% is far below any honest cost of capital — an artifact of $41.8B of goodwill/intangibles from Xilinx, but a reminder that returns on *total* capital are unproven. Stripping goodwill, ROIC was ~14% in FY25 and is annualizing north of 30% in FY26 — excellent, but only one year into the AI ramp. Working capital is consuming cash aggressively ($3.4B drag in Q3'26 alone; FCF of just $554M on $2.4B of net income), and stock comp of ~$1.9B/yr is a real, recurring cost. Deduct a point for the working-capital/FCF quality and a point for capital-return discipline (buybacks of only ~$1.3B vs. $7B+ of FCF; no dividend).

**Intrinsic value (my bottom-up DCF).** Revenue $62B (FY26) → $95B (FY27) → $120B (FY28) → ~$200B by 2036, GAAP operating margin expanding to 31%, 15% cash tax, capex 4.5% of sales, working capital 8% of incremental revenue, 10% discount rate, 3% terminal:

- **Base case equity value: ~$500B → $280/share (fully diluted), $305 (basic)**
- **Bull case** (35% FCF CAGR through 2030, no 2028 digestion): ~$750B → **~$420/share**
- **Bear case** (AI capex digestion in 2028, revenue plateaus ~$110B, NVIDIA price war): ~$300B → **~$170/share**

For cross-reference, the mechanical 10-year DCF (29% FCF CAGR, 10% WACC, 3% terminal) yields $682B — i.e. **$380–417/share** even on generous, dilution-ignoring assumptions.

**Reverse DCF:** $730B today requires AMD to earn roughly **$70–75B of free cash flow by 2036** (~10x today's) — a ~25–29% FCF CAGR for a full decade with a 20x terminal multiple. That is not a forecast being priced; it is the *best case* being priced.

**Verdict on price: the stock trades at or above my bull case.** The shares are already 29% off their September 2026 high ($630) while fundamentals were accelerating — a classic sign that the multiple, not the business, was the risk.

## 4. Management Quality: **A–**

- **Lisa Su / Jean Hu** are among the best operator/CFO pairs in semis. The Xilinx acquisition was transformative and remains the right call; the 5-year revenue CAGR of ~29% with expanding margins is execution, not luck.
- **Capital allocation is mostly disciplined** — buybacks modest and roughly offset dilution; no dividend; net cash preserved.
- **Two blemishes for a Buffett-style owner:** (1) the OpenAI warrant is a ~10% equity payment for revenue — economically a customer-acquisition cost that flatters the growth optics, and the underlying transaction has a whiff of vendor-financed circularity; (2) an active M&A cadence (ZT Systems, World Labs, Taalas) that requires watching for empire-building. Insider ownership is low by owner-operator standards.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID at $446**

| | |
|---|---|
| Business quality | Very good, narrow moat, widening in CPUs |
| Management | Excellent |
| Balance sheet | Excellent |
| **Price vs. intrinsic value** | **~50% above base-case IV; at/above bull case** |
| **Margin of safety** | **Negative** |

This is a fine business at a price that assumes everything goes right for ten years. My base-case intrinsic value of ~$280–305 is not a bearish forecast — it assumes AMD compounds revenue at ~13% for a decade off a $62B base and roughly triples free cash flow to $50B. Even my bull case (~$420) sits *below* the current quote. There is no margin of safety, and the 0.97% FCF yield offers no compensation for beta of 2.49 and the volatility (three 20%+ drawdowns in 24 months) that comes with it.

**A price becomes interesting in the low-$200s** (roughly 25–30% discount to base-case IV). Until then, the discipline is to admire it from the sidelines.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI capex digestion / circular financing.** The OpenAI relationship (warrant-for-revenue) mirrors other vendor-financing structures in the buildout. If hyperscaler capex growth decelerates in 2028, AMD's mix — now majority data center — cuts both ways fast.
2. **NVIDIA's counterattack.** Rubin-generation pricing and CUDA lock-in could cap AMD's accelerator share and its 56% gross margin.
3. **Customer concentration.** Five named ramp customers (OpenAI, Meta, Microsoft, Anthropic, OCI); one or two are likely >10% of revenue each, with the semi-custom console business adding further concentration. A single program delay is a material earnings event.
4. **China export controls** remain an unresolved regulatory swing factor on both revenue and inventory write-downs.
5. **Dilution.** The 160M-share warrant vests on milestones; my valuation uses the fully diluted 1.80B count for this reason.
6. **Supply.** Single-sourced TSMC leading-edge wafers and CoWoS-class packaging; allocation, not demand, may determine the revenue line.
7. **Multiple compression risk.** At 33x FY27E GAAP earnings, a de-rating to 20x costs 40% of the share price even if earnings deliver.
