## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **HIGH — the dominant risk**

**Confirmed / factual:**
- **No SEC enforcement actions** appear in EDGAR full-text search for AMD. Clean historical enforcement record.
- **$800M writedown** on MI308 AI chips (April 2025) already realized from prior export restrictions — precedent that policy shifts hit AMD's P&L materially, not hypothetically.
- **BIS expanded export restrictions** to Southeast Asia transit countries, effective on/around **Nov 20, 2026**. AMD's Singapore billings are directly in scope.
- China + Hong Kong = **22.4% of FY2025 revenue (~$7.75B)**, roughly 2.5x Nvidia's 9.1% — AMD is *more* exposed to the exact geography being regulated than its larger rival.

**Alleged (Hunterbrook, Nov 19, 2026 — unverified by CNBC/Reuters):**
1. **IEIT Systems**, blacklisted by Commerce (Entity List, March 2025), continued receiving *newer AMD firmware* — a potential deemed-export/BIS violation.
2. **$67M of 2025 "licensing income"** traceable to two Chinese chip ventures blacklisted in 2019 — this is the most serious allegation because it implicates **reported financial statement line items**, not just operations.
3. AMD "depends on a Chinese partner to finish most of its chips" (OSAT/assembly-test concentration).

**Legal overhang:** Within hours, **Rosen Law Firm, Kaplan Fox & Kilsheimer, and SBS Law** all announced securities-fraud *investigations* soliciting AMD shareholders. These are pre-complaint solicitations — routine and low-cost at this stage — but a formal complaint is likely within 60–90 days if the stock stays depressed.

**AMD's response:** "The claims misrepresent AMD's business practices and relationships… AMD complies with applicable US export control laws." Su told investors China is "important" and AMD wants full compliance.

**Open gap:** No publicly confirmed BIS/Commerce subpoena or formal inquiry into AMD specifically (unlike Nvidia). This is the single most important item to monitor — a confirmation would move this rating toward Critical.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Export-control / compliance regime fragility.** 22.4% of revenue sits in a jurisdiction whose rules changed twice in a week. The Nov 20 BIS expansion into Southeast Asia transit countries threatens the channel AMD uses to serve the region. A prior restriction cost $800M in a single quarter; a broader one is not modeled.
2. **Single-point foundry and assembly-test concentration.** Leading-edge wafers are sole-sourced at TSMC; the Hunterbrook allegation of reliance on a Chinese partner for back-end assembly/test means AMD's supply chain physically traverses the regulated geography. A decoupling event is a multi-quarter revenue interruption, not a price adjustment.
3. **Integration and key-person risk from World Labs ($8.2B, all-stock, closed Nov 13, 2026).** A pre-revenue spatial-intelligence startup now carries an $8.2B balance-sheet asset with impairment risk, plus a new dependency on Fei-Fei Li as EVP/Chief Scientist — a second irreplaceable individual alongside Su. Absorbing this during a 32% drawdown and an active short campaign strains management bandwidth.

*Noted gap: no cybersecurity incident history available from the tools used. Beta of 2.49 implies the market prices AMD as a high-dispersion asset regardless.*

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **ELEVATED AND RISING**

- **Nov 19, 2026:** AMD closed **−7.72%** to $489.19 on **71.7M shares (~2.7x average volume)**; extended to $446.23 (−6%) on Nov 20. Peak-to-trough from the Oct 6 high of $658.52 is **−32% in six weeks.**
- The charge is not a valuation dispute — it is an **integrity allegation** (chips/firmware reaching a blacklisted entity, licensing revenue tied to sanctioned ventures). That is the most durable form of brand damage because it invites government attention and cannot be rebutted with a spreadsheet.

The most prominent public defender is **Jim Cramer**, who called it a "hitjob" — retail-oriented commentary, not institutional validation. Absent a detailed, specific rebuttal (e.g., firmware distribution audit, licensing counterparty disclosure), the allegation will stick in the narrative.
- Sector context compounds it: chip stocks are having their **worst two months since 2008**, Nvidia is under investigation for Asia sales, and Nvidia's own leadership turnover has made the whole group a governance-headline trade. AMD is being treated as the highest-beta way to short AI China exposure.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Three independent high-severity vectors are now correlated rather than diversified — (a) a regulatory/compliance allegation that goes to the accuracy of reported revenue, (b) active securities-fraud solicitation by three firms, and (c) >$320M of uninterrupted CEO selling with zero insider buying. Correlated non-financial risk at a 2.49-beta, ~90x-earnings multiple is precisely the setup where a governance discount gets applied quickly.

**Why not Critical:** No confirmed SEC/BIS enforcement action, no restatement, no whistleblower complaint on record, no executive departures, and an independent lead director with real authority. The company has a genuine, detailed denial on the record. Critical is reserved for confirmed enforcement or accounting irregularity.

**Why not Moderate:** 22.4% China revenue against a tightening rule set, an $800M prior writedown as proof of transmission, and class-action counsel already circling is not a moderate profile.
