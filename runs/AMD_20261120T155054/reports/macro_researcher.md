## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional → risk-off. This is a reflation-plus-hawkish-Fed regime, and it is the single worst macro mix for a hyper-duration equity like AMD.**

The defining feature of this cycle is that the easing phase has ended and the Fed has *reversed*. The FOMC raised the target range to **3.75%–4.00%** and held on 28 Oct 2026; the minutes released 18 Nov show **"almost all officials expect more rate hikes in the months ahead."** Effective fed funds sits at **3.88% (Oct-2026)**, up from a 3.63% trough in mid-2026.

Critically, inflation is *accelerating*, not sticky: CPI has compounded at roughly a 4.0% annualised rate over the last ten months (326.6 in Jan → 337.5 in Oct) and headline CPI YoY has re-accelerated from **2.65% (Dec-2025) to 3.82% (Oct-2026)**. Fed staff put core at ~3.2% in August. Growth is not cooperating with the tightening either — real GDP grew **+3.4% annualised in Q3-2026** and nominal personal consumption is running at **+6.3% YoY**. This is overheating, not a landing.

The bond market's verdict is the important part. The 10Y sits at **5.07–5.13%** and the 30Y at **5.47%** — a bear steepening, with the long end leading. But **10Y breakevens are only 2.16%** and *falling* (from 2.38% in late October). The market believes the Fed will win on inflation. Which means the entire ~65bp rise in the 10Y since June 2026 is **real yield**. Implied 10Y real yield = **~2.91%**, up from ~2.25% in June — the highest real cost of capital in a generation. That is a pure, unhedged multiple-compression machine.

Credit is where the warning light is. HY OAS has widened from **2.63% (31 Aug) to 3.52% (19 Nov)** — +89bp in eleven weeks. Still tight in absolute terms, but the *rate of change* alongside a 5.47% 30Y is the transmission channel that matters for a capex boom being funded increasingly with debt. The broad USD has strengthened to **122.65** from ~118.0 in mid-September.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**AMD today: $446.23, market cap ~$733B, beta 2.49. Down 32% from its $658.52 all-time high (6 Oct 2026) but still +72% YoY.** The drawdown is not a coincidence — AMD peaked within one day of the 10Y's cycle high (5.31% on 5 Oct). The stock is behaving exactly like what it is: a long-duration cash-flow stream priced off the real yield curve.

**NEGATIVE — Real discount rate (the dominant channel).** AMD's interest-rate sensitivity is *not* through its balance sheet. It carries modest debt relative to a $733B equity base, so direct interest expense is immaterial and refinancing risk is negligible. The sensitivity is entirely through the discount rate. At a 5.13% nominal and ~2.91% real risk-free rate, a $733B market cap requires an enormous forward earnings stream to clear the hurdle. Every 25bp of real yield costs a high-multiple semiconductor name roughly 6–9% of fair value. Beta of 2.49 tells you the market has already voted on this.

**NEGATIVE — AI capex financing, not AI capex demand.** Consensus sees hyperscaler capex rising to **$1.2–1.3T in 2027** (GS) from ~$780–800B in 2026. AMD's entire growth thesis is levered to that number. But S&P Global explicitly flags **negative free operating cash flow across hyperscalers** — i.e., the build-out is increasingly debt-financed. With the 30Y at 5.47% and HY spreads +89bp in three months, the marginal cost of funding a data hall has risen materially. This is the transmission mechanism that turns a rates story into an AMD revenue story, with a 12–18 month lag.

**NEGATIVE — Memory/HBM cost inflation.** This is the underappreciated one. DRAM contract prices are up ~47% in 2026 (Gartner), 16GB DDR5 contracts have roughly quadrupled, HBM4 is cannibalising DRAM supply and inventories are under 10 days. AMD is hit on **both** sides: (a) HBM is a large BOM input into Instinct/Helios racks, so gross margin is squeezed or ASPs rise enough to defer orders; and (b) unlike a pure datacenter play, AMD has meaningful **client PC exposure** through Ryzen — and memory-driven PC price inflation is driving a PC shipment contraction. AMD wears the cost inflation in its consumer segment and the margin compression in its datacenter segment.

**NEGATIVE — USD strength.** The broad dollar at 122.65, +4% since September. AMD's revenue is majority non-US by geography (much of it USD-invoiced through ODMs, which cushions but does not eliminate the effect). Q4-26 and FY27 guidance face a translation headwind, and a strong dollar plus high real yields is a global tightening impulse that pressures China/EM demand.

**NEGATIVE — Trade & China policy.** The 20 Nov selloff (-6%, on top of -7.7% the prior session) was a Hunterbrook short report on China exposure, with Commerce records showing **879,000 Nvidia/AMD AI chips approved for China in six months** and a December congressional hearing pending. AMD's China datacenter revenue has been a swing factor all cycle; policy, not the cycle, now sets the range of outcomes.

**POSITIVE — Nominal growth and pricing power.** GDP +3.4% SAAR and 6.3% nominal consumption growth mean end-demand is not the problem. Semiconductors have genuine pricing power in an inflationary world, and AMD is taking share (Venice EPYC ramping, Helios racks). Nominal revenue growth of 30%+ offsets a lot of multiple compression.

**NEUTRAL — Labour costs.** 4.1% unemployment and 31,000 employees — wage inflation is immaterial to the P&L. The relevance is that there is no labour-market slack to force the Fed's hand downward.

**TAIL RISK (not in the table above) — Taiwan concentration.** TSMC is AMD's sole source for leading-edge nodes. The 20 Nov tape showed Taiwan's worst week since 1991. This is an un-hedgeable, binary geopolitical exposure that no multiple adequately prices.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating

### **UNFAVOURABLE**

The **fundamental** macro backdrop is fine — arguably good — for AMD. Nominal growth is strong, AI capex is still compounding, and the company is executing. The problem is that AMD is a 2.49-beta, $733B long-duration asset in an environment where the **real** risk-free rate has risen 65bp in five months and the Fed has explicitly told you more hikes are coming. The discount-rate channel dominates the earnings channel over a 6–12 month horizon.

The composition of the squeeze is what makes it unfavourable rather than merely neutral: AMD is simultaneously absorbing **input cost inflation (HBM/DRAM)**, **translation drag (USD +4%)**, **a consumer PC unit decline**, and **policy-driven China revenue uncertainty** — while its customers' ability to fund the $1.3T 2027 capex number deteriorates with every basis point added at the 30Y. That is four headwinds and one tailwind, with the tailwind (nominal growth) the one the market already pays for.

I would upgrade to Neutral on either of: (a) 10Y real yield back below ~2.4%, or (b) HY OAS re-tightening below 3.0% while capex guidance holds. Neither is close.

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**Bear case — the AI capex financing stall (probability I'd assign: ~20–25%, impact: -40% to -50%).**

The consensus assumption embedded in every AI semiconductor model is that hyperscaler capex is *demand-constrained, not financing-constrained* — that $1.3T gets spent regardless of the cost of capital. S&P's own note that hyperscalers are running negative free operating cash flow is the crack in that assumption.

The specific trigger: a large AI-datacenter debt or neocloud financing deal fails to clear at 5.47%, HY spreads gap from 3.5% toward 5.0%, and two of the top-five hyperscalers guide 2027 capex down from ~$1.3T to ~$900B in the same quarter. AMD — priced for share gain *within* an expanding TAM, and already down 32% — would de-rate a second time, because the market would simultaneously cut the revenue line and raise the discount rate. The tell to watch is HY OAS, not the CPI print. It has already moved 89bp in eleven weeks.

**Bull case — the disinflationary growth scare (probability ~15%, impact: +60–80%).** With the Fed minutes saying "almost all officials expect more hikes," essentially nobody is positioned for a rapid easing cycle. If unemployment breaks above 4.6% in H1-2027 while breakevens stay pinned at 2.16%, the Fed is forced to abandon the hiking bias, real yields fall 60–80bp, and AMD — beta 2.49, 32% off its high — re-rates violently. Note that breakevens *already* signal the market expects the Fed to win; they are the leading indicator for this scenario, and they are falling while nominals rise.

**The asymmetry worth flagging:** the bear case is a slow-burn credit event that announces itself in spreads, giving time to react. The bull case would arrive as a gap. For a name that has already absorbed a 32% drawdown, the risk/reward on the macro is more two-sided than the current tape implies — but the rating on the *observed* regime stays Unfavourable.
