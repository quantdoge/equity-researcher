## AMD — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $446.23 (Nov 20, 2026) | Market cap $733B | Beta 2.49*

---

### 1. Growth Story

AMD has crossed from "credible second source" to genuine AI infrastructure platform. Q3'26 Data Center revenue hit a record **$8.5B, +97% YoY**, driven by the MI450 launch and 6th Gen EPYC, with **Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, and Oracle Cloud** all running production workloads on Helios rack-scale systems. Crucially, the operating leverage long promised by Lisa Su has arrived: Q3'26 GAAP gross margin expanded to **54%** (56% non-GAAP) and non-GAAP operating margin hit **29%**, with non-GAAP EPS +74% YoY against revenue +45%. Management guides **2027 revenue growth above 80%** — the stock, however, has de-rated 32% from its October high on a sector-wide AI correction, leaving a rare gap between accelerating fundamentals and contracting multiple.

**Reverse-engineering the multiple:** at $733B market cap, a 25x terminal P/E and a 10% discount rate, the market implies ~$43B of 2030 net income — roughly **$145–155B of 2030 revenue (~28–30% net margin)**. That is only **~60% of consensus** ($243B) and about **12–13% AI-accelerator share** — a level AMD can reach simply by holding its current trajectory. The market is discounting a competitive rout that the order book does not support. Consensus itself requires ~15% accelerator share, which is the aggressive end — but not fantasy given a five-hyperscaler customer roster already in production.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Feb 2, 2027 — Q4'26 results (highest conviction).** Guide: ~$17.0B revenue (+66% YoY), $2.90 EPS, ~30% non-GAAP op margin. First full Helios quarter; the 100% beat-rate pattern makes a raise the base case.
- **H1 2027 — Helios/MI450 volume ramp.** Watch for named multi-gigawatt commitments beyond the existing OpenAI/Anthropic/Meta/Microsoft/Oracle deployments.
- **2027 — 6th Gen EPYC "Venice" production scale-up** on TSMC N2; OCI E7 and Google/Microsoft storage instances are early share-gain proof points.
- **ROCm 10 / ROCm.AI ecosystem adoption** — the single largest swing factor in the durability of the growth rate; hyperscaler-standardization announcements would be a re-rating event.
- **World Labs (world models) and Taalas (inference silicon) acquisitions** closing — reframes AMD as a full-stack inference play, but carries integration and dilution risk.
- **Gaming trough** — segment -40% YoY; a console refresh cycle in 2027–28 would remove a structural drag.

### 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Google Trends, "AMD Instinct": 100 (May 31, 2026) → 12 (Nov 8, 2026), -88%.** Genuinely mixed. The May peak was launch-event and hyperscaler-announcement driven; the collapse confirms the *hype premium* has drained, consistent with the 32% drawdown. Attention, not demand, is falling — Data Center grew 97% through this decay. Read as multiple-compression risk, not demand deterioration.
- **Hiring: strongly supportive.** ~75 "AMD AI" roles, ~50 ML engineer roles, 200+ software roles in Austin, with specific "Software Engineer – AI Inference and Performance Optimization" reqs. AMD is hiring into the right place — the ROCm software gap. This is the highest-value signal in the dataset.
- **Volume/price: capitulation, not distribution.** Nov 5 fell 17% on 71M shares (vs ~30M avg) as the Nasdaq entered correction on an "AI tech wreck"; the quarter itself beat and raised. Painfully high beta (2.49) is being priced, not a broken thesis.
- **Guidance credibility:** seven consecutive beats averaging +5.2% supports treating the >80% 2027 guide as conservative rather than promotional.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (high conviction)

The setup is unusually favorable: growth *accelerating* into the high-40s with margins inflecting 700bps, a Rule of 40 near 70, a five-hyperscaler production customer base, and a 32% de-rating that has taken the multiple to 25x FY27E earnings and 7.6x FY27E sales — below the incumbent's trailing multiple. Every sell-side target (median $650, +46%) sits above spot and revisions are positive. The market is embedding only ~60% of management's own 2030 path.

I stop short of Strong Buy for two reasons: 55x FY26E earnings on a 2.49-beta name offers no cushion against a hyperscaler capex pause, and the durability case rests entirely on ROCm closing the CUDA gap — which I can infer from hiring but cannot yet verify in revenue. **Upgrade trigger: two consecutive quarters of ROCm-driven enterprise (non-hyperscaler) design wins, or a pullback below ~$380 (≈21x FY27E).**

### 7. Key Risks

1. **Execution risk on the ramp.** Rack-scale integration (via ZT Systems) is new to AMD; Q4's +28% QoQ revenue step is the steepest in company history. Any slip breaks the 2027 +80% bridge.
2. **Customer concentration.** Five hyperscalers dominate AI demand. A single capex deferral is worth multiple quarters of growth.
3. **Gross margin ceiling.** GM was flat QoQ at 54%/56%; HBM cost inflation is the binding constraint. Consensus 2030 EBITDA margins (~35%) look inconsistent with 31% net margins — treat out-year margin estimates as low confidence.
4. **NVIDIA.** A $3.3T incumbent with a durable CUDA moat, larger absolute R&D, and a cheaper multiple (27.9x trailing vs AMD's 55x forward).
5. **AI capex cyclicality.** The November drawdown was a sector-wide de-rating; AMD's 63% Data Center mix makes it among the highest-torque victims if capex normalizes.
6. **GAAP/non-GAAP gap (~30%).** $1.46 vs $2.09 EPS; SBC $1.64B and acquisition amortization are real costs, and AMD is now an acquirer (World Labs, Taalas).
7. **Working capital.** FY25 change in WC -$2.38B reflects aggressive inventory builds — a write-down risk if demand shifts.
