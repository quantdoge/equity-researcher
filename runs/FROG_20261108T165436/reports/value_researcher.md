## JFrog Ltd. (FROG) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $100.98 | Market cap $12.23B | EV ≈ $11.55B | 129.5M diluted shares | Data as of Q3 FY2026 (reported 11/5/26)*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW (widening, but unproven on returns)**

JFrog's Artifactory is the de-facto universal binary/artifact repository — the system of record for software supply chains at large enterprises. That position creates genuine **switching costs**: once build pipelines, CI/CD systems, and security scans point at Artifactory, ripping it out means re-architecting delivery infrastructure. The evidence that the moat is *widening* is unusually concrete:

| Metric | Q3 FY2026 | Signal |
|---|---|---|
| Net dollar retention | **124%** (from ~117%) | Expanding seat/consumption value |
| Customers >$100K ARR | 1,357 (+21% YoY) | Land-and-expand intact |
| Customers >$1M ARR | 112 (+58% YoY) | Platform consolidation winners |
| Cloud revenue | $101.5M, +60% YoY (57% of total) | Successful SaaS transition |
| Gross margin | 79.6% (vs 76.8% FY25) | Pricing power holding |

But two caveats keep this from "Wide":
- **Competition from bundlers.** AWS CodeArtifact, Azure Artifacts, GitHub Packages, GitLab, and Sonatype Nexus all compete on price, often bundled "free" into cloud commitments. JFrog's defense is multi-cloud/hybrid neutrality — real, but it caps pricing power at the margin.
- **The return test fails.** ROIC has been **-10.5% to -13.5%** for four straight years (FY25: -9.9%) against a ~10% WACC. A Buffett moat is *defined* by sustained ROIC > WACC. JFrog has not yet earned that badge; it has only earned the right to try.

**Verdict: narrow moat with clear widening trajectory, but the moat has not yet been converted into economic returns.**

---

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**What's excellent:** 79.6% gross margins; revenue CAGR of 28.7% over five years that is now *re-accelerating* (24% FY25 → 31% in Q3 FY26); FY26 guidance $680–682M (+28%); FCF of **$207M TTM (32% FCF margin)**; fortress balance sheet — $705M cash, $19M debt, **$686M net cash**; Q3 FY26 was the **first GAAP-profitable quarter** ($6.6M NI, -1.0% operating margin vs -33% in FY21); four consecutive EPS beats.

**What's poor — and it is decisive:** stock-based compensation.

| FY | Revenue | FCF | SBC | SBC % of Rev | FCF − SBC |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $280M | $17M | $69M | 24.5% | -$52M |
| 2023 | $350M | $72M | $95M | 27.2% | -$23M |
| 2024 | $428M | $108M | $131M | 30.6% | -$23M |
| 2025 | $532M | $142M | $157M | 29.4% | -$14M |
| TTM Q3'26 | $642M | $207M | $162M | 25.2% | **+$46M** |

~78% of reported free cash flow is a non-cash add-back that is paid for with shareholders' equity. Diluted shares went from 94.8M (2021) to 129.5M today — **+36%, roughly 6% per year**. The good news: the inflection to positive SBC-adjusted owner earnings ($46M TTM) is real, and SBC as a % of revenue is finally trending down.

**Score rationale:** best-in-class balance sheet and margin structure, offset by negative ROIC, GAAP unprofitability until this quarter, and chronic dilution. 6/10, not higher.

---

### 3. Valuation — Intrinsic Value ≈ **$50/share** (range $25–$70); price is ~2× that

*I explicitly reject the DCF tool output of $12.65B intrinsic value — it mechanically projects 30% FCF growth for ten years and ignores stock compensation entirely. That model simply re-derives the market price; it is not an independent estimate.*

**Current multiples:** 17.0× FY26E EV/sales; **51× EV/FY26E FCF**; FCF yield 1.9% forward / 1.2% trailing; P/B 13.2×. Beta 1.31, with the 10-year Treasury at 4.86% — a hostile discount-rate regime for long-duration equity.

**Owner-earnings DCF** (FCF minus SBC as the true cost of the business; 10% discount rate, 3% terminal):

| Scenario | Rev CAGR '26–'31 | FCF margin '31 | SBC % rev '31 | Year-10 owner earnings | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | 15% | 32% | 18% | ~$307M | **$25** |
| Base | 20% | 33% | 10–16% | ~$555M | **$51** |
| Bull | 25% | 35% | 8–14% | ~$997M | **$62** |

**Reverse-engineered expectation:** at $101, the market is paying for roughly **26% annual FCF growth for five straight years with an exit at 25× FCF** — before deducting a dollar of dilution. Hitting the current price on an SBC-honest basis requires owner earnings to reach ~$700M by 2031, i.e., ~34% owner-earnings margin on $2.1B of revenue. That is a superb outcome, and it still only earns you a market return.

Cross-check: private-market value for a 28%-growth, 79%-gross-margin infrastructure software asset is typically 7–9× revenue (~$5–6B), and public high-growth software peers trade 12–20× forward revenue. Even the *most generous* market-consistent anchor lands at $8–13B — but that reflects multiple-based pricing of future perfection, not discounted cash a business owner actually receives.

**Conclusion: the stock is priced at roughly twice my base-case intrinsic value and above even my bull case. No margin of safety — negative, in fact.**

---

### 4. Management Quality — **Above average operator, below average steward of per-share value**

- **Founder-led:** CEO Shlomi Ben Haim with co-founders Fred Simon and Yoav Landman; owner-operator DNA, 1,800 employees, $378K revenue/employee. Execution on the cloud transition has been genuinely excellent, and guidance has been raised repeatedly — management credibility is high.
- **Capital allocation is the weak link.** Two acquisitions (Vdoo ~$300M in 2021, Qwak ~$230M in 2024) have left $411M of goodwill/intangibles on the books, and the combined business is still GAAP-loss-making. Buybacks are symbolic ($2M in Q2 FY26) and there is no dividend — appropriate for a growth company, but it means **the only return of capital mechanism is dilution running the other direction.**
- **Compensation policy is shareholder-unfriendly.** 25–30% of revenue flowing to employees in stock is not a rounding error; it is a structural transfer of ~6% of the company per year from owners to staff. A founder-led firm that owns meaningful equity should be able to align employees with an SBC ratio half this size.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** *(great business, wrong price)*

This is the archetypal modern software company that a Buffett-style investor must admire and decline. The moat is real and widening; the financial trajectory is inflecting the right way; the balance sheet is pristine. But you are being asked to pay **51× forward free cash flow and ~197× SBC-honest owner earnings** for a business that has never earned its cost of capital, and the stock **gapped +35% in a single session** on AI-narrative enthusiasm three weeks ago, putting it at the very top of its recent range ($74.62 → $100.98).

A business trading at a *fair* price is not a buy. This one trades at nearly double intrinsic value.

- **Would become interesting at $45–55** (a 20–30% discount to base-case value), and only if NRR holds ≥120% and SBC continues compressing toward 15% of revenue.
- **Position guidance:** if held for tax/long-term reasons, trim aggressively into strength. Do not initiate here.

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Multiple compression (the dominant risk).** At 51× FCF, a move to a still-generous 30× is a 40% drawdown with zero change in fundamentals. Rate sensitivity is high (beta 1.31, 10-yr at 4.86%).
2. **SBC never normalizes.** If dilution stays at ~6%/yr, per-share owner earnings grow far slower than enterprise earnings — the exact trap this analysis is built to avoid.
3. **Hyperscaler bundling.** AWS/Azure/GitHub can subsidize artifact registries to protect cloud consumption. This is the most plausible path to a narrowing moat and NRR decay below 115%.
4. **Cloud growth quality.** The 60% cloud acceleration was materially driven by *converting usage overages into multi-year annual commitments* — partly pull-forward, not purely new demand. That growth rate is unlikely to be durable at 60%.
5. **Concentration inside the 124% NRR.** Expansion is increasingly driven by a small set of very large accounts ($1M+ customers +58%); a handful of churned whales would move the needle sharply.
6. **AI-thesis reversal.** The current price embeds an "AI tsunami of binaries" narrative. Narrative momentum cuts both ways — the -13.6% single-day drop on 11/5 is a preview.
7. **My thesis could be "wrong" the way value investing often is short-term:** markets can price software on revenue multiples for years. The risk isn't permanent capital loss from a bad business — it's paying a price that requires flawless execution to merely break even.

**Bottom line: quality 7/10, price 2/10. Sell.**
