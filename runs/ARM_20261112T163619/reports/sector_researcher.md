# Sector Brief — Arm Holdings (ARM)
**Sector:** Technology / Semiconductors (CPU IP licensing & compute subsystems) | **Mkt cap:** $215.7B | **Price:** ~$202 (12-Nov-26) | **Beta:** 3.94

## 1. Sector Overview

Semiconductors are in the strongest upcycle on record: IDC puts 2026 industry revenue at ~$1.29T (+52.8% YoY), Deloitte at ~$975B, with AI silicon now ~30% of total industry revenue and hyperscaler capex exceeding ~$630–800B. The cycle is **bifurcated**: AI infrastructure (accelerators, HBM/DRAM, advanced foundry, custom CPU) is capacity-constrained and pricing-rich, while consumer/industrial end-markets (smartphones, IoT, auto) grow at low-to-mid single digits. Arm sits in an unusual niche — it sells **architecture IP and royalties**, not silicon, so it captures a percentage of *every* chip its partners ship across both sides of that bifurcation. Critically, the group is showing classic **late-cycle signature** in memory (Micron FY26 revenue +256%, gross margin 80.7% vs 22.4% two years ago) even as logic/AI-infrastructure names remain mid-cycle. Concentration is high: NVDA, TSM and AVGO alone exceed $7.9T in market cap, and the AI supply chain is a de facto oligopoly at every layer.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Arm-based data-center CPUs are inflecting.** Data-center royalties *more than doubled* YoY in FQ2'27; Arm's share of server CPU is rising via NVIDIA Grace/Vera, hyperscaler custom silicon and Arm's own new AGI CPU (management guides up to **$2B revenue across FY27–FY28**, with first commercial deliveries on track). This is the highest-royalty-rate, highest-growth pool in the portfolio.
- **Mix shift to higher royalty rates.** Armv9 adoption plus CSS/compute-subsystem bundling raises Arm's take per chip and per device, letting revenue outgrow underlying unit volumes — a structural, price-driven tailwind independent of cycle.
- **Record licensing backlog.** Licensing revenue is outgrowing royalties (FQ2'27 total revenue +27% to $1.44B, ahead of consensus), reflecting agentic-AI and edge-inference architecture decisions being locked in now, which convert to royalties for years.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Royalty growth is decelerating sharply — the core problem.** Royalty revenue rose just **+8% YoY in FQ2'27** (to ~$670M) versus **+22%** in FQ1'27. With royalties the bulk of revenue and the smartphone/IoT base maturing, the "AI story" is not yet large enough to offset consumer softness. This is what broke the stock.
- **Memory shortage is taxing Arm's end-markets.** The 2026 DRAM/HBM shortage and price shock is inflating bill-of-materials for phone and PC OEMs, crowding out non-AI budgets and pressuring the unit volumes that generate Arm royalties.
- **Policy/cycle risk is acute and stock-specific.** A Democratic midterm sweep triggered data-center-slowdown fears and the worst single-day index selloff since April 2025; export controls and China exposure (Arm China) remain live. Combined with a 3.94 beta, ARM has fallen **~55% from its June high of ~$452** — the market is repricing duration risk, not just fundamentals.
- **Margin compression from the own-silicon pivot.** Operating margin fell to **13.9% in FQ2'27** from 29.5% in FQ4'26 as Arm funds AGI CPU development; GAAP net income fell 45% YoY despite +27% revenue. Selling its own chip also creates **channel conflict** with licensees (Qualcomm, MediaTek, NVIDIA).

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (franchise) / AVERAGE (current financial momentum)**

**Rationale.** Arm's moat is arguably the widest in the peer group: a 92.5% gross margin — the highest of any name here, 32pts above the peer median — reflects an asset-light IP model with essentially zero marginal cost per additional chip shipped, and its instruction-set ecosystem plus software toolchain create enormous switching costs across ~30M+ developers. Barriers to entry are very high on ecosystem, moderate on technology (RISC-V is a credible long-run substitute, and Qualcomm/Apple/hyperscalers can internalise designs — Qualcomm's Nuvia litigation is the clearest expression of licensee pushback).

However, on the metrics that drive near-term estimates, Arm is **below median**: revenue growth of 22.8% vs a 31.6% peer median, and operating margin of 18.5% vs 39.9%. That gap is the crux of the investment debate — Arm trades at roughly **~39x TTM revenue and ~230x TTM GAAP earnings** ($5.46B TTM revenue, $936M TTM net income), a multiple that requires the data-center royalty inflection and the AGI CPU ramp to arrive on schedule. The FQ2'27 royalty deceleration to +8% is direct evidence that the inflection is not yet visible in reported numbers.

**Verdict:** best-in-class business model, best-in-class gross margin, but currently a *share-gainer in a segment too small to move the total* — while the dominant segment (smartphones) stagnates and management spends margin to buy a seat in the data center. Watch two things: (a) reacceleration of royalty growth above ~15%, and (b) whether the AGI CPU ships on time without further operating-margin erosion.

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE (with high dispersion and elevated cycle risk)**

AI infrastructure remains a genuine structural tailwind — trillion-dollar industry revenue, hyperscaler capex still expanding, and a supply chain that is capacity-constrained rather than demand-constrained. But this is **not a rising-tide sector**: memory is at an obvious cycle peak (Micron's 80.7% gross margin is unsustainable), consumer semis are shrinking (Qualcomm −2.6%), and post-midterm policy risk has re-rated the entire complex. The winners will be those with pricing power and AI-revenue mix already in the P&L (NVDA, TSM, AVGO); Arm has the pricing power but not yet the mix. Rating is Attractive at the sector level, but stock selection within it matters more than at any point in this cycle.
