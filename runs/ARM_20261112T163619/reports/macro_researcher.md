### 1. Macro Environment Summary — "Late-cycle reflation with a term-premium shock"

**Stance: Risk-off for long-duration growth equity; risk-on in credit.** This split is the single most important feature of the current regime, and ARM sits precisely on the wrong side of it.

The US economy is reaccelerating, not slowing: real GDP rose from $24,408bn (Q2'26) to **$24,611bn (Q3'26), +0.83% q/q (~+3.4% annualised), +2.1% y/y** — a sharp pick-up from the near-stall in Q4'25 (+0.05% q/q). Unemployment has fallen to **4.1%** from 4.5% a year ago. Inflation, however, has turned back up: **CPI +3.82% y/y in October** (index 337.476), up from ~3.3% in July, and **nominal PCE +6.3% y/y** in September.

The Fed responded by **hiking 25bp on 16 September 2026 to 3.75–4.00%**, then holding on 28 October with the explicit language that "inflation remains elevated." Effective fed funds is **3.88% (Oct)** — roughly **0bp real** against headline CPI. This is the least restrictive stance of the cycle, which is why the Committee is not cutting.

The critical nuance: **the long end is selling off for real-yield, not inflation, reasons.** The 10Y is **4.88% (FRED, 9 Nov)**, up from 4.48% in early July, and a same-week curve snapshot showed **5.13%** on 12 Nov — intraweek long-end volatility of ~25bp is itself the signal. Yet the **10Y breakeven has fallen to 2.20%** from 2.40% in June. That means the **real 10Y has risen ~43bp to ≈2.68%**. The market believes inflation normalises but demands a far higher real premium for duration — fiscal supply, term premium, and Fed independence risk. This is the classic de-rating environment for cash flows that sit 10–20 years out.

Credit is *not* stressed: **HY OAS 3.30% (11 Nov)**, only ~65bp off September's 2.66% cycle tights. So the equity drawdown is a discount-rate and sentiment event, not a fundamental credit event.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**① Rates / discount rate — STRONGLY NEGATIVE (dominant variable).**
ARM is the purest long-duration asset in large-cap semis. GAAP net income was only **$130m on $1.44bn of Q2 FYE27 revenue** (−45% y/y), implying an earnings multiple in the hundreds on a **$215.7bn** market cap. With almost no debt and a low capital-intensity model, ARM has no refinancing risk — but ~100% of its valuation sensitivity runs through the discount rate. At a 15–20 year effective duration, **+43bp of real yield is worth roughly −7% to −11% of intrinsic value**, before any growth revision. Beta of **3.94** doubles the market transmission. The tape confirms the mechanism: ARM is **−54% from its 18 June peak ($439.46 → $202.34)**, while HY spreads are essentially unchanged-to-tighter over the same window. That is a de-rating, not a demand collapse.

**② Growth — POSITIVE.** 2–3% real GDP with 4.1% unemployment supports smartphone, PC, auto and industrial unit volumes, which is what royalty revenue actually keys off. Q2 FYE27 revenue grew **+27% to a record $1.44bn**, licensing **+50% to $773m**, and Q3 guidance of **$1.54bn** implies continued acceleration. But note the irony: strength is precisely what keeps the Fed hawkish, so good growth data is currently *bad* for ARM's multiple.

**③ Inflation — MODESTLY NEGATIVE, via the memory channel.** ARM's own pricing power is excellent (licensing +50%), and royalties are per-unit rather than ad-valorem, so ARM is a *taker* of inflation, not a pass-through beneficiary. The real damage is in the supply chain: **royalty growth decelerated to +8% ($670m) from +22% in Q1 FYE27, missing the ~$720.8m consensus**, with reporting pointing to memory cost/capacity pressure squeezing device bill-of-materials. AI datacentre memory demand is crowding out consumer-device DRAM/NAND supply — a genuine inflation shock transmitting into ARM's highest-volume end market. Separately, wage inflation across the UK, Israeli and Indian engineering base pressures opex and is a key reason GAAP income fell 45%.

**④ Currency — MILDLY NEGATIVE.** ARM reports in USD. The broad dollar is **+3.1% since August (121.88)** on hawkish Fed + high real yields, a translation headwind on non-USD royalties (China is historically roughly a quarter of revenue; plus Taiwan, Korea, Japan, Europe). Partial natural hedge: a weaker GBP reduces the UK R&D cost base. Net drag on revenue, modest benefit on costs — roughly neutral to −1% on earnings.

**⑤ Trade policy / tariffs — NEGATIVE, indirect.** The **Section 232 25% tariff on specified semiconductor articles and derivative products (proclaimed 14 January 2026)** does not hit ARM directly — it is an IP licensor, not an importer of record. But it taxes the ASP of chips ARM collects royalties on and raises the landed cost of servers and devices containing them. That is a direct tax on ARM's royalty base, and it amplifies the AI-datacentre cost-inflation narrative.

**⑥ AI capex / fiscal-policy risk — HIGH VARIANCE.** Datacentre royalties doubled y/y and the new Arm AGI server CPU moves ARM from pure IP into merchant silicon — higher growth ceiling, higher cyclicality, higher operating leverage. The **Democratic sweep in the 3 November midterms** triggered data-centre-slowdown fears and a **−5% Nasdaq day on 5 November**. This is now a political/regulatory variable layered on top of the capex cycle, and it is unhedgeable from a macro seat.

**⑦ Curve shape — MILDLY NEGATIVE.** No inversion (+39bp) removes the classic recession warning, but the *manner* of steepening matters: long-end-led, real-yield-driven bear steepening is the worst possible curve regime for equities with back-ended cash flows.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Operationally the backdrop is fine — solid growth, tight labour, no credit stress, an AI royalty tailwind. **Financially it is close to a worst-case configuration for ARM specifically**: a hawkish Fed, a real-yield-led bear steepening of the curve, a rising dollar, and 25% semiconductor tariffs that tax the royalty base — all landing on a stock whose valuation is ~100% duration and whose beta is 3.9. The −54% drawdown is a rational repricing of that combination, not a dislocation. Macro is not the reason to own ARM today; it is the reason to size it small.

### 5. The Surprise Scenario (Bull)

**Disinflation resumes faster than the Fed expects, forcing a pivot to cuts in H1 2027.** Breakevens at **2.20%** — down 20bp since June — say the bond market already believes inflation normalises; the Fed's September hike may prove to be a policy error. If Q4 CPI prints back below 3% and the Committee cuts 50bp by mid-2027, the real 10Y likely retraces from 2.68% toward ~2.10%. On ARM's duration and 3.94 beta, that is **+30% to +45% of pure multiple expansion from $202**, before any upgrade to the AI datacentre royalty line (already doubling y/y) or the AGI CPU ramp. Positioning is washed out after a −54% drawdown and sentiment is at a low — precisely the setup where a rate pivot produces a violent re-rating rather than a grind.

**The symmetrical bear tail:** if the term premium keeps building (30Y through 5.75%, real 10Y to 3.00%) *and* memory inflation delivers another royalty miss, the de-rating compounds toward the $150–160 area — roughly another −20% — with no earnings floor to catch it, given the ~400x GAAP multiple.

**Monitor:** monthly CPI (is 3.82% the peak?), the 10Y breakeven vs real-yield decomposition, 30Y auction tails, the December FOMC dots, DRAM contract pricing, and any post-midterm data-centre policy legislation.
