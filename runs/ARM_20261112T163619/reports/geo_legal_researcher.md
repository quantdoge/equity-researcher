## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US–China decoupling severs or degrades the Arm China channel
**Probability: Medium-High (~55%) | Impact: High (~$0.9B revenue, high-margin royalty)**
- 18% of revenue flows through a JV Arm doesn't control, "substantially all" PRC revenue via the IPLA (20-F). Arm has historically been unable to direct Arm China (the 2020–22 Allen Wu governance crisis required two years of litigation to resolve — Reuters/FT).
- The US–China truce is **fragile**: China on 9 Nov 2026 extended the truce by only **two months**, continuing suspension of 24 tariff lines and strategic export controls (Reuters, 9 Nov 2026).
- Export-control salience is *rising*, not falling: the Nvidia/Huang spying scandal has put chip export policy at the center of US national-security politics (WSJ, 9–11 Nov 2026). CRS has explicitly flagged Arm-design IP within the scope of advanced-semiconductor controls (congress.gov R48642).
- China's counter-pressure: anti-foreign-sanctions/"long-arm" blocking rules and state promotion of **RISC-V** alternatives (CSIS) create a structural substitution threat to Arm's China royalty base.

### Risk 2 — Coordinated global antitrust attack on the licensing model
**Probability: High (~70% that investigations persist/expand through 2027) | Impact: Medium-High**
- **FTC opened a probe in May 2026** into whether Arm is illegally monopolizing semiconductor segments and will refuse or degrade license quality as it moves into selling its own silicon (Bloomberg/Reuters, 15 May 2026; PYMNTS).
- Qualcomm has filed competition complaints in the **US, EU and South Korea** (Bloomberg, 25 Mar 2025; Yahoo Finance) — a multi-front campaign, with the EC complaint lodged Dec 2024.
- **Political overlay:** Democrats swept Congress and many governorships in the Nov 2026 midterms (CNBC, 6 Nov 2026), raising the probability of a more aggressive antitrust/tech-regulatory posture in Washington. Arm's conflict-of-interest position — licensing to customers while competing with them via the AGI CPU — is precisely the fact pattern regulators target.

### Risk 3 — Taiwan-Strait concentration in Arm's newly vertical silicon business
**Probability: Low-Medium (~10-15% of a significant disruption over 3 years) | Impact: High (new growth engine)**
- Arm's first own chip (AGI CPU, 64/128/136 Neoverse V3 cores, N3P) is **sole-sourced at TSMC Taiwan** (CNBC; Omdia; Taiwan News).
- Arm has now put hard capital behind this: **$672.0M of non-cancellable supplier purchase commitments** through 2027, and a **$200.0M advance** in H1-CY2027 to reserve manufacturing capacity 2027–2029 under a take-or-pay style agreement with shortfall payments (Arm 6-K, 4 Nov 2026). This converts a design-only, fabless-light model into one carrying physical supply-chain and blockade/tariff exposure.
- CEO targets **$2B of AGI CPU revenue within a year** and ~60% data-center share by year-end (CNBC) — i.e., the exposed segment is the growth story.

*Honorable mention:* SoftBank (~90% voting control) related-party dependence — $401.6M of 6M revenue — creates governance/perception risk (self-dealing optics if SoftBank entities are also anchor buyers of Arm silicon) but is not a conventional geopolitical risk.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Arm v. Qualcomm (D. Del.) — the central legal overhang**
- Dec 2024 jury verdict: **Qualcomm won** on central claims (Nuvia licensing). Court upheld and **dismissed Arm's remaining claims as a matter of law on 30 Sept 2025** (Qualcomm press release; Futurum).
- Arm **appealed to the Third Circuit** — *Arm Ltd. v. Qualcomm*, No. **25-2923**, docketed 7 Oct 2025 (Justia).
- **New trial opened 5 Oct 2026**: Qualcomm alleges Arm withheld owed chip-testing tools and leaked a 2024 termination threat, damaging a prospective **Meta** deal. Qualcomm seeks to **stop paying royalties for up to 5 years — "potentially worth billions"** — though Judge Maryellen Noreika is weighing whether to strike that remedy. A concurrent bench trial examines whether Arm bargained in **good faith** on the next-gen architecture. **The ALA runs to 2033.** Qualcomm has recorded **no accrual**; Arm calls the harm speculative (Quartz/Reuters, 5 Oct 2026).
- *Read-through:* even an Arm win in the appeal is partly mooted if royalty terms are reset judicially — a direct attack on the royalty line (53% of FY26 revenue).

**Antitrust (see Risk 2):** FTC inquiry (May 2026); EC complaint; KFTC complaint. No formal EC/KFTC decision disclosed — **gap: I could not confirm whether the EC has opened a formal Article 102 proceeding.**

**Structural/regulatory history:** MOFCOM's 2022 block of the Nvidia–Arm deal remains the precedent for Chinese regulatory leverage over Arm's corporate actions; any future SoftBank-related transaction (re-IPO, stake sale, merger) faces multi-jurisdictional review.

**Commercial-contract contingency:** the Nov-2026 capacity reservation agreement carries **minimum purchase volumes with shortfall payments**, and supplier adjustments may change quantities/pricing — an unbooked financial commitment if AI demand forecasts miss (Arm 6-K).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

| Flag | Detail | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **Export-control compliance through a non-controlled JV** | Arm's IP is sublicensed by Arm China, which Arm does not control. This is the classic diversion risk vector (restricted PRC entities, e.g., the 2019 Huawei suspension precedent). Arm has limited audit rights/visibility in practice. | **High** |
| **Dual-use classification of Arm's own silicon** | Shipping complete AI-class CPUs (Neoverse V3, 3nm) shifts Arm from licensing IP to exporting hardware — squarely within EAR advanced-computing controls and potential PRC retaliatory restrictions. No disclosed export-license conditions yet. | **Medium-High** |
| **Related-party revenue / governance** | ~15% of 1H-FY27 revenue from SoftBank affiliates; SoftBank is controlling shareholder. FCPA/related-party disclosure scrutiny, plus minority-holder litigation risk. | **Medium** |
| **Antitrust-driven conduct remedies** | Consent-decree-style obligations (mandatory licensing, royalty caps, non-discrimination) would be operational, not just financial. | **Medium** |
| **Sanctions (Russia/Iran/etc.)** | No material exposure disclosed in sources reviewed. | **Low — unverified** |

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Arm's *pure* geopolitical exposure (China revenue at 18% and falling) is moderate-to-high; the escalation to **High** comes from the combination of (a) an **uncontrolled China JV** sitting on the revenue, (b) a **live FTC antitrust investigation** plus a three-jurisdiction Qualcomm antitrust campaign against the royalty model itself, (c) a **billion-dollar royalty-suspension claim** in active Delaware trial, and (d) new **Taiwan sole-source manufacturing commitments** ($672M purchase obligations, $200M capacity advance) that import supply-chain geopolitical risk into the fastest-growing business line.

**Key monitoring triggers:** Third Circuit ruling in 25-2923; verdict in the Oct-2026 Delaware trial and Judge Noreika's ruling on the royalty-suspension remedy; any formal EC/KFTC Article 102 proceeding; renewal or renegotiation of the Arm China IPLA; expiry of the two-month US–China truce (early Jan 2027).
