## Arm Holdings plc (ARM) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price ~$202 (Nov 2026); market cap ~$216B; EV ~$213B. Data as of FY26 (ended 3/31/26) plus TTM through 9/30/26.*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **WIDE, but narrowing and at risk of self-inflicted damage**

Arm licenses CPU instruction-set architectures and core designs. The economics look like a toll road: gross margin 92.5%, no inventory, negative working capital in good years.

Evidence of a genuine moat:
- **Ecosystem lock-in / switching costs.** Two decades of compilers, OS ports, EDA flows and developer training are built on the Arm ISA. A customer cannot change architecture without rewriting software. This is the strongest form of switching cost in technology.
- **Intangible assets.** ~99% of smartphone application processors run on Arm; cumulative shipments in the hundreds of billions. Royalty per chip is a tiny fraction of the customer's bill of materials — trivially cheap to keep, expensive to replace.
- **Efficient scale in mobile.** The fixed R&D base (~$1.9B in FY26, +43% YoY) is amortized over an enormous installed base.

But the moat is **narrowing**, and the evidence is internal, not cyclical:
- **Royalty growth decelerated to +8% YoY in FQ2 FY27** ($670M) — the highest-ever Q2, but slowing — while **licensing, the lumpy, low-quality revenue line, grew +50%** to $773M. When a licensing model must lean on one-off license deals to hit growth, pricing power on the recurring royalty stream is not compounding as the story implies.
- **ROIC has deteriorated:** 15.9% (FY23) → 1.8% (FY24) → 11.5% (FY25) → 10.4% (FY26). A wide-moat IP licensor should earn 30%+. At ~10%, Arm is barely clearing its cost of capital. The moat exists structurally but is being consumed by R&D and stock comp rather than harvested.
- **The strategic pivot is moat-destroying.** Arm is moving to sell its own chips (Bloomberg, Mar 2026; ~$2B of AI chip sales targeted for next year). The moment Arm competes with Apple, Qualcomm, NVIDIA and MediaTek in silicon, it converts its licensees into adversaries with a direct incentive to fund RISC-V. The company is monetizing harder precisely because the pure-IP model is maturing.

**Moat verdict: Wide (IP/ISA), trend negative.** Five years ago this was one of the cleanest toll roads in tech. Today it is a toll road where the owner has decided to start driving trucks on it.

| Owner earnings ($M)** | 601 | 325 | 756 | 608 | ~630 |

\*Q2 FY27 gross margin 96.4% on a revenue-mix shift. \*\*Net income + D&A − capex.

**What is good:** revenue CAGR of 19.4% over three years and 22.8% in FY26; 92%+ gross margins; pristine balance sheet — $3.62B cash and short-term investments against $500M debt, **net cash $2.75B**; no inventory, no refinancing risk.

**What is disqualifying for a Buffett-style owner:**
- **Stock-based compensation is 131% of TTM net income ($1.19B vs $904M).** Deduct SBC from free cash flow and Arm generated **negative $277M of economic free cash flow over the last twelve months**. The reported $910M of FCF exists only because SBC is added back as "non-cash." It is not non-cash to the owner — it is paid in ownership.
- **Reported FCF conversion of 108% is an illusion**; economic conversion is ~0%.
- **Operating margin compressed from 25.3% (FY23) to 18.5% (FY26)** and roughly 14% TTM, despite 22%+ revenue growth. Revenue is being bought with opex.
- **Operating cash flow is violently unstable** — from $1,524M (FY26) to a working-capital whipsaw of −$1,465M in FY25. This is license-timing, not a durable cash machine.
- **Capex is tripling** for the chip program: $93M (FY23) → $219M (FY25) → $545M (FY26); PP&E up from $714M to $1,417M in nine months. The model is becoming capital-intensive.
- **Zero capital returned** in its history as a public company. No dividend, no meaningful buyback (the $202M "repurchase" in FQ4 FY25 is vesting-related withholding, ~0.1% of market cap).

Balance sheet: 10/10. Cash generation quality: 3/10. **Composite 6/10.**

### 3. Valuation Analysis — the price already discounts a plan that hasn't started

**Current multiples (on ~$202):**

| FCF yield | 0.45% | — | — |
| Owner-earnings yield | 0.28% | — | — |

**Intrinsic value, three scenarios.** I use a 9.5% discount rate (elevated for beta 3.9, ~90% controlling shareholder, and no dividend), 3% terminal growth, and owner earnings — not SBC-inflated FCF — as the base.

**Base case (my independent view): ~$45–55/share.**
Revenue compounds 10% to ~$8.2B by FY2031 (royalties high-single-digit, licensing lumpy, own chips ~$2–3B), operating margin recovers to 28% on operating leverage, net income ~$1.8B, owner earnings ~$1.6B. At a 28x exit and 9.5% discount rate, plus $2.75B net cash, fair value is ~$25B of equity. **The market is paying ~8.6x that.**

**The company's own plan, fully executed: ~$135–165/share.**
Management targets **~$25B revenue in FY2030 at a 98.2% gross margin and 43% operating margin** (SoftBank Group Report 2026). Give them full credit:
- $25B × 43% = $10.75B EBIT; ~20% tax → **~$8.6B net income**; ~$7.8 EPS.
- At a generous 30x: $235/share in FY2030. Discounted back four years at 9.5% **= $163 today.**
- At 25x: **$136 today.** At 40x: $218 today.

**This is the crux: if Arm nails its own five-year plan — revenues 5x today, margins doubling — and the market still pays a premium 30x multiple, you earn ~3.8% annually from $202.** You take equity-like risk for bond-like return, before considering execution risk on a business Arm has never run.

**What the price implies.** $216B needs ~$10–12B of sustainable owner earnings at 30–35x. That is **10–13x today's earnings**, or roughly the FY2030 plan delivered on time and on margin — with no discount for risk. Note also management's 98.2% gross-margin assumption on *selling silicon* is internally implausible: a merchant die sale carries real COGS. Either the target is IP-content revenue dressed as chip revenue, or the margin assumption is aspirational. Either way, I do not underwrite it at 100%.

**Sanity check on the drawdown.** The shares peaked near $440 in June 2026 and sit near $200 today, −55%. That is not a margin-of-safety event; it is the deflation of a mania back to merely very expensive. Beta is 3.9. Sell-side targets are scattered from $160 (Goldman, Sell) to $325 (Barclays) with an outlier at $641 — a 4x dispersion is the market admitting it has no anchor.

### 4. Management Quality: **5 / 10**

**Positives.** CEO Rene Haas has been an excellent operator: the v9 architecture renegotiation lifted royalty rates, CSS expanded Arm's share of customer silicon value, and the 2023 IPO was well executed. R&D is being invested aggressively and on-shore (own compute, chiplets), which is the right long-term direction if Arm is to escape the mature-IP trap.

**Serious concerns for an owner:**
- **Capital allocation is now directed by the controlling shareholder, not by ROIC.** SoftBank owns ~90% and has raised a **margin loan backed by Arm shares to ~$25B**. Arm is being used as collateral for SoftBank's AI ambitions. That is a governance structure in which minority shareholders are passengers — and a forced-selling overhang if the shares fall further.
- **The own-chip pivot trades a high-ROIC licensing model for a capital-intensive merchant-silicon model that antagonizes every existing customer.** Whether that is brilliant or value-destructive will be decided by SoftBank's strategic needs, not by a dispassionate assessment of Arm's returns on capital.
- **No capital returned, ever.** A company that generates $0.9B of FCF and returns none of it while paying employees $1.2B in stock is not being run for the outside owner.
- **Guidance pattern:** licensing beats, royalties miss. Growth is being manufactured in the least durable line item.
- I could not retrieve the 10-K/20-F text (foreign private issuer; the filing tool returned no document), so I cannot verify Arm China's exact revenue concentration or related-party terms with SoftBank. Historically Arm China has been roughly a fifth to a quarter of revenue through a JV whose governance Arm does not fully control. **Treat this as an unquantified risk in this analysis.**

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL**

This is a **valuation verdict on a high-quality business**, not a judgment that Arm is impaired.

- The franchise is real and the balance sheet is immaculate ($2.75B net cash, no leverage).
- But a wide moat is only worth paying for at a sensible price. At 238x earnings, 40x sales, 221x FCF and 0.28% owner-earnings yield, **there is no margin of safety — there is a negative margin of safety of roughly 300–400% against my base case, and ~25% against the company's own fully-delivered five-year plan.**
- Economic free cash flow is **negative** after stock compensation. The "cash cow" characterization does not survive contact with the cash flow statement.
- The multiple requires the market to keep paying an AI-option premium in perpetuity. Multiple expansion is not a value thesis; it is the absence of one.

**Re-entry range:** I would become interested below **~$110–130** — roughly 35–45% lower — which is where the plan-execution case offers a genuine 20–30% discount. Below ~$65 the base case starts to protect capital.

**What I would need to change my mind:** two consecutive years of royalty growth ≥20%, operating margin expanding (not compressing) toward 30% on a GAAP basis, economic FCF positive after SBC, and proof that own-chip revenue is additive rather than a channel-conflict tax on the licensing base.

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (i.e., how I could be wrong)

1. **The AI royalty step-up is faster than I model.** Arm's royalty per chip is rising via CSS and datacenter cores; server share gains against x86 could compound royalties at 25%+ rather than 8%. This is the single largest upside risk.
2. **Own-chip execution surprises positively.** The $2B FY2028 chip revenue target, if hit at high margins, would validate the $25B plan early and force a re-rating.
3. **A SoftBank take-private.** With ~90% ownership, SoftBank could squeeze out minorities at a premium ($250–350). A cash-strapped SoftBank using Arm as loan collateral makes this less likely near-term, but it caps downside in a melt-up.
4. **Momentum/AI-bubble persistence.** Valuation has never been a catalyst. The stock can go much higher on flows alone.
5. **Terminal economics understated.** If Arm becomes an unavoidable "compute tax" on all AI inference, my terminal value is far too low.
6. **Downside risks I weight heavily:** further SoftBank share pledges or sales; Arm China losing effective control or revenue; RISC-V adoption accelerating once Arm competes with its own licensees; and a capex/R&D step-up that keeps economic FCF negative for years while GAAP earnings stay flat — exactly the pattern visible in FQ2 FY27 (revenue +27%, GAAP net income −45%).

**Bottom line:** Wonderful franchise, deteriorating returns on capital, negative economic free cash flow, a controlling shareholder using the equity as collateral, and a share price that only works if management's most optimistic five-year plan arrives on schedule *and* the market keeps paying a premium multiple for it. That is speculation wearing a moat. Sell.
