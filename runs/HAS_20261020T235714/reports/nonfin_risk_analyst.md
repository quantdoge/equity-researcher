# Hasbro, Inc. (HAS) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment

**Snapshot:** Consumer Cyclical / Leisure · Pawtucket, RI · CEO Christian "Chris" Cocks · Market cap ~$15.4B · Price $109.52 · Beta 0.44 · ~4,520 employees · FY ends late December
**Context:** Q3 2026 (reported 10/20/26) was a blowout — revenue $1.6B (+15%), adjusted operating profit $541M (+52%), Magic: The Gathering >$600M (+34%), profit $394.5M ($2.77/sh), guidance raised to 7–9% revenue growth, buyback doubled. Risk work here is about what the good numbers are *hiding*.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (structurally strong, conduct-tinged)

**Positives — verifiable:**
- **No CEO/Chairman duality.** Richard S. Stoddart serves as independent Chair; Cocks is a director but not Chair. Proxy discloses a policy requiring a Lead Independent Director if the Chair is not independent.
- **Clean 2026 annual meeting (June 11, 2026):** all eleven nominees elected, say-on-pay approved, KPMG ratified for FY2026. No majority-withhold votes, no activist contest, no proxy fight disclosed.
- **Active board refresh:** Carla Vernón and Doug Bowser joined in early 2026 (global brand ops; digital gaming/global franchise — well matched to the WotC pivot). Mary Beth West retired at the 2026 meeting; prior long-tenured directors (Alan Batkin, 25 years; Basil Anderson) have rolled off. This addresses the staleness concern that fueled the 2022 Alta Fox campaign.
- **No SEC enforcement actions** returned by EDGAR full-text search. Clean enforcement record.

**Negatives:**
- The live securities class action alleges **board-level fiduciary and disclosure failures** around the company's flagship franchise — a conduct allegation, not a technical one. Governance structure looks good; governance *culture* is what's being litigated.
- Insider activity is unidirectionally sell-side (§3).
- Metrics in the executive comp program could not be verified (proxy text inaccessible) — see data gaps.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**Enforcement:** No SEC/DOJ action identified. This is a genuine positive and distinguishes HAS from peers with disclosure trouble.

**Active private litigation (the real overhang):**
- **West Palm Beach Firefighters' Pension Fund & City of Miami Gen. Employees' v. Hasbro**, S.D.N.Y. No. 24-cv-8633. Class period 9/16/2021–10/26/2023. Lead plaintiffs appointed 8/29/2025; **amended complaint filed 11/26/2025**; pending.
  Allegations: (i) the "customer segmentation" narrative for Magic's release cadence was false — growth was actually driven by an internal **"parachute" strategy** of cheap, rushed sets to plug revenue shortfalls in other divisions; (ii) the 30th Anniversary set's **"out of stock" message was fabricated** on the sale site; (iii) "upcoming releases" in inventory were actually unsold overprinted backlist. Fraud-flavored, documents-based, and squarely about disclosure controls.
- **Derivative suit** (*Crocono & McGlone v. Cocks et al.*, D.R.I., filed Jan 2026) alleging breach of fiduciary duty, unjust enrichment, waste, gross mismanagement — **voluntarily dismissed without prejudice on 2/17/2026**. *Without prejudice* = refilable. Not a resolution.
- **Consumer/contract suit** over the Special Edition / 30th Anniversary set (breach of contract, good faith, negligence).
- **Cyber/privacy class action** following the March 2026 breach (§4c).

**Regulatory/trade:**
- **Section 301 "forced labor" tariff ~12.5%** on China and Vietnam toy imports (HTS 9503) took effect **July 24, 2026** — toys lost their long-standing exemption. Hasbro has absorbed a prior ~**$1B tariff-related charge**; litigation challenging IEEPA tariff authority creates a possible recovery path but also uncertainty (guidance is not creditable).
- **CPSC e-filing rule** adds compliance/systems burden on import documentation.
- **COPPA / children's privacy** is a structural exposure for connected toys and digital platforms (MTG Arena, D&D Beyond, Hasbro Pulse DTC). No HAS-specific COPPA action found — this is a sector-pattern risk, flagged as unconfirmed, not an existing violation.

- 40 Form 4s since March 2026, clustered on predictable dates (3/17–3/18, 6/15, 7/29–7/31, 10/2) — consistent with annual equity grants, vesting and tax-withholding, plus scheduled 10b5-1 sales.
- Notable: **CEO Cocks sold 5,200 sh @ $100.85 (2/26/26, ~$524K)** plus an 8,030-share tax disposition; **CFO Goetter sold 11,000 sh @ $95.44 (7/28/26, ~$1.0M)**.

- Three separate Form 4 filers on 10/2/2026 suggests broad-based officer/insider liquidation rather than a single event.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

**a) Single-franchise profit concentration — the defining risk.** Wizards of the Coast / Magic now carries consolidated profitability: Magic alone did >$600M in one quarter (+34%) and drove the +52% operating-profit jump while Consumer Products remains soft on tariffs. The company's earnings quality now rests on one brand's release cadence and player monetization. Risks compound: the class action alleges overprinting has *already* damaged the brand; competitive digital CCG substitution; collector fatigue from premium reprint products; and a hot secondary market that turns pricing decisions into community flashpoints. Studio talent dependency is real (repeated WotC layoffs in 2023–2025; the new Montréal studio, opened 10/8/26, partly mitigates).

**b) Trade regime / supply-chain transition.** China- and Vietnam-heavy sourcing under a 12.5% duty effective mid-2026, with a management program to move **500+ SKUs out of China targeting <10% China reliance by 2027**. Dual-sourcing carries cost inflation, quality/recall risk in new factories, and timing risk into the Q4 holiday peak. Retailer concentration (Walmart/Target/Amazon) limits ability to pass price through.

**c) Cybersecurity — realized, not hypothetical.** On **March 28, 2026**, Hasbro detected unauthorized access to its corporate network, took systems offline, and **disrupted its ability to manufacture and ship for several weeks** (~$11M quantified cost). A follow-on breach notification exposed **SSNs and financial data** (608 RI and 436 MA residents in state filings — employee/former-employee data), triggering a class action and national "worst breaches of 2026" coverage. Residual concerns: insurance coverage, whether an SEC Item 1.05 materiality determination was made, multi-state AG inquiry, and repeat-attack risk during Q4 peak revenue.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: ELEVATED-MODERATE

- **The Magic overprinting controversy is durable, not transient.** It has already cost the stock ~10% on the November 2022 BofA "killing its golden goose" note and ~8% in January 2023. It has now migrated from fan grievance to **securities litigation** — the reputational and legal channels are the same channel now. Core collectors are the highest-ARPU cohort; trust erosion there is a revenue risk, not just a PR risk.
- **Creative-community relations:** ~1,100 layoffs in December 2023 on top of ~800 earlier that year generated lasting negative coverage ("brain drain" framing) across D&D and Magic.
- **Data breach headlines** put HAS on national cyber-failure lists — but exposure was employee PII rather than consumer accounts, which caps brand damage to the consumer franchise.
- **Tariff politics:** the CEO publicly warned of toy price increases and was publicly dismissed by the administration. Price-hike messaging invites consumer and political backlash in a value-sensitive category.
- **Offsets:** currently strong sentiment (Q3 beat, raised guidance, doubled buyback), an asset-light ~$700M licensing business, and an "adults are fueling a toy boom" tailwind that de-risks the demographic story.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE (high end of band)**

**Why not High:** no SEC/DOJ enforcement, no liquidity or going-concern stress, a functioning and recently refreshed board, an independent chair, clean say-on-pay, and a diversified licensing/entertainment royalty stream. Civil litigation is a settlement-probability event, not an existential one, against a $15.4B cap.

**Why not Low:** the two largest risks have both already *fired* — a fraud-tinged securities class action about the flagship brand, and a material network intrusion with a manufacturing outage. Zero insider buying alongside ~$54M of sales. Profit concentration is increasing, not decreasing, which means volatility in a single franchise now transmits to the whole equity story.

**Risk trajectory: worsening on litigation/cyber, improving on governance.**

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **SDNY 24-cv-8633 — disclosure-controls integrity.** Allegations that a "out of stock" message was *fabricated* and that a documented internal "parachute" strategy existed are the kind of particularized claim that survives motions to dismiss and generates damaging discovery. Cross-check against 10-K Item 3 / 10-Q Part II Item 1 legal proceedings and against ICFR conclusions. **Escalate to legal/compliance.**
2. **Zero insider purchases, ~$54M sold, CEO+CFO sales pre-Q3 beat.** Obtain 10b5-1 plan adoption dates. Escalate to trading-compliance review.
3. **Derivative suit dismissed *without prejudice*** — refiling risk through 2026–27; monitor for a related demand-futility ruling.
4. **March 2026 breach disclosure adequacy** — verify 8-K Item 1.05 assessment, insurer recovery against the $11M figure, and any state AG/FTC inquiry. Assess Q4 peak-season continuity posture.
5. **Compensation alignment** — confirm whether executive incentives are tied to *Magic-specific durability* or consolidated adjusted EBITDA. A consolidated-EBITDA target structurally rewards overprinting into shortfalls — precisely the alleged behavior. **This is the highest-value single item to verify in the proxy.**
