# Hasbro, Inc. (HAS) — Financial Risk Assessment

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: Investment Grade (low-BBB), with a thin equity cushion**

External rating confirmed: **Fitch 'BBB-' Long-Term IDR**, outlook revised to **Stable** (previously Negative), on ~$3.4B of unsecured notes maturing 2026–2044. This is a bottom-rung investment grade credit — one notch of deterioration from high-yield.

The two headline metrics in the data must be read carefully, because **FY2025 contains a ~$1,083M non-cash goodwill/intangible impairment** (goodwill & intangibles fell $2,797M → $1,713M):

| Metric | Tool output | What it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| Interest coverage | **0.07x** | GAAP operating income $11.1M / interest $163.4M. Distorted by impairment. Pre-impairment EBIT ≈ $1,094M → **~6.7x**; EBITDA basis $1,200M → **7.4x** |
| Debt/Equity | **6.01x** | Genuine and severe — but an artifact of a **$565M book equity base** depleted by successive writedowns, not of new borrowing |
| Net debt/EBITDA | **2.10x** | Genuinely moderate. On GAAP EBITDA ($147M) it would be 17.2x — but that basis is economically meaningless |

**Interpretation:** Hasbro is not a leverage problem, it is a **capital-structure erosion problem**. Debt has been essentially flat for three years ($3,496M → $3,411M → $3,401M) while equity collapsed from $1,087M to $565M via impairments. Net debt has actually *declined* ($2,952M → $2,519M). The cash-flow engine is servicing debt comfortably; the balance sheet simply has almost no equity left to absorb another write-down.

## 3. Liquidity Risk

- **Current ratio 1.38x / quick 1.24x** — adequate; inventory is only $260M, so the quick ratio carries little distortion risk.
- **Refinancing cliff:** Short-term debt jumped from **$0 to $527.6M**, matching Fitch's note that the next maturity falls in 2026. Cash of $882M covers this **1.67x**, and FY2025 FCF of $830M covers it 1.6x. **This is manageable but must be refinanced into a higher-rate market** — the 5Y UST is 4.80% and 10Y 4.99% versus Hasbro's ~4.8% blended coupon, so refinancing is roughly cost-neutral, not accretive.
- **No going-concern risk.** Liquidity is not the binding constraint; the equity cushion is.

FCF yield **5.4%** (FCF $830M / mkt cap ~$15.4B). Four consecutive years of FCF expansion is genuinely impressive.

**But two red flags undermine the quality of that FCF:**
1. **Capex has been cut to the bone** — $63M, or **1.3% of revenue**, down 70% from $209M in 2023. FCF is being flattered by underinvestment. Sustained sub-2% capex is not a durable run-rate for a consumer products company and likely front-loads future underperformance.
2. **Working capital is a growing drag** — a **−$320M** swing in FY2025 (vs. +$60M in FY2024). Receivables rose $1,060M ← $920M on revenue growth, so partly legitimate, but it means the $893M OCF was earned despite, not because of, the balance sheet.

Dividend of $393M is covered **2.1x** by FCF — safe at current levels, though in a severe downside it becomes the first lever pulled.

**Severe case, −30% revenue:** Operating income ≈ $680M, coverage ≈ **4.2x**, net debt/EBITDA ≈ **2.7x**, FCF ≈ **$400M** — the dividend becomes only ~1.0x covered and would very likely be cut. Still no covenant breach, but equity at 10% of assets means another impairment would push book equity toward zero.

## 7. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Split verdict — and the split is the point:

- **Credit risk: MODERATE, trending stable.** 2.1x net leverage, 6.7x pre-impairment coverage, BBB- confirmed with a Stable outlook, $882M cash against a $528M 2026 maturity, and four years of rising FCF. This company services its debt through a 20–30% revenue decline.
- **Balance-sheet risk: HIGH.** Equity of $565M against $5,552M of assets (10%), debt/equity of 6.0x, Z-score of 2.09 grey (1.47 on GAAP EBIT), F-Score 4/9. There is almost no book cushion left after three years of writedowns.
- **Market risk: HIGH.** 30% volatility, a stock up 135% off its low, and valuation now dependent on the turnaround continuing rather than on asset protection.

## 8. Key Red Flags

1. **🔴 Equity cushion nearly exhausted.** $565M book equity, 10% of assets, 6.0x debt/equity. A second impairment of the remaining $1,713M goodwill/intangible balance would render book equity negative — technically insolvent on a balance-sheet basis, regardless of cash generation.
2. **🔴 FCF quality is deteriorating beneath the surface.** FY2025's $830M was manufactured by cutting capex to 1.3% of revenue (−70% since 2023) while absorbing a **$320M working-capital outflow**. Sustainable FCF is materially below the reported figure.
3. **🟠 Impairment cadence is chronic, not one-off.** FY2023 saw a $1.53B operating loss; FY2025 another ~$1.08B writedown. Two large impairments in three years indicates the acquisition carrying values (eOne/Entertainment) remain unproven.
4. **🟠 Refinancing needs are near-dated.** $528M due in 2026 against a ~4.8–5.0% rate environment, above Hasbro's blended ~4.8% coupon. Manageable, but it consumes the liquidity that provides downside protection.
5. **🟠 Revenue still ~20% below the 2022 peak** ($5,857M → $4,701M). The FY2025 rebound (+13.7% YoY) is a recovery off a depressed base, not growth above prior highs.
6. **🟡 Market risk premium.** At ~$100–110 the equity embeds successful turnaround execution. Volatility of 30% with a 0.44 long-run beta means drawdowns will be company-specific and abrupt.
