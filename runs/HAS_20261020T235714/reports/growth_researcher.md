# Hasbro, Inc. (HAS) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $109.52 (Oct 20, 2026 close) · Market cap $15.45B · EV $17.87B**

## 1. Growth Story

Hasbro is no longer a toy manufacturer — it is becoming an IP royalty machine, and Q3 2026 proved it. Wizards of the Coast revenue rose **31% to $747M** (46.8% of total revenue) with a **49.2% segment operating margin**, while Magic: The Gathering printed a **record $616M quarter (+34%)** on the back of Universes Beyond crossovers (*The Hobbit*, *Reality Fracture*). Group gross margin has climbed from 57.3% (FY23) to **75.9% in Q3'26** as mix shifts from low-margin plastic to high-margin digital, licensing and TCG royalty streams — the model now scales operating profit roughly 3x faster than revenue (adj. op profit +52% vs revenue +15%). This is a margin-plus-mix re-rating story masquerading as a toy cyclical, and management just raised guidance for the third time.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

- **Global toys & games:** ~$110–120B, low growth; Hasbro's Consumer Products (~$800M/qtr) is the mature half.
- **Trading card games:** the fastest-compounding category in hobby entertainment. MTG is the category's #2 asset with a 30-year, tournament-anchored, secondary-market network effect. Q3 MTG at $616M **exceeds annual revenue of most mid-cap game publishers** — quarterly.
- **Licensed merchandise / brand out-licensing:** a ~$350B retail royalty pool. Hasbro's asset-light pivot (Evolution live-casino deal, Broadway *Monopoly*, apparel, home goods) captures royalty revenue at near-100% incremental margin.
- **Digital gaming:** the real prize. Digital/licensed gaming was only ~$472M in 2024, of which **$112M came from Monopoly Go! alone** — and Hasbro's take is a royalty (no development cost, no CAC, no marketing spend). Penetration of its own IP into digital is still single-digit. Every incremental dollar here flows through at digital gross margins, which is precisely what drove the 76% gross margin.

**Differentiation:** no peer owns both a 30-year TCG ecosystem and a 100-year brand library. Mattel is a branded-goods licensor; Hasbro is a **content/IP-royalty platform with a collectibles flywheel**.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4 2026 results + initial FY2027 guidance — Feb 9, 2027.** Highest-impact event. Watch: (a) whether 2027 revenue guide lands above the stale $5.44B consensus, (b) the magnitude of the warned Wizards margin compression.
- **MTG 2027 Universes Beyond slate** — announced release dates **Apr 9, Aug 6, Nov 19, 2027**, with X-Men/Guardians crossovers rumored; **MagicCon Detroit, Feb 26–28, 2027** for set reveals.
- **$400M buyback execution** (doubled at Q3) plus capacity for a dividend increase (~39% payout).
- **Post-Q3 analyst PT chase:** consensus mean PT ~$101 sits **below** the $109.52 spot; Q3's beat forces upward revisions, typically a multi-week tailwind.
- **Digital licensing monetization** — new game launches (D&D digital, Monopoly Go content) and tariff refund/cost-savings cadence.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY**

Not a Strong Buy because the multiple (~3x sales, 12x EBITDA) already reflects part of the margin story and I cannot independently verify consumer engagement trends. But the setup is compelling: **14.8x forward EPS for a business compounding adjusted EPS at 30%+, expanding gross margin 1,860bp in three years, throwing off ~$900M FCF, retiring debt, and doubling its buyback.** On a PEG basis (0.45) this is the cheapest quality-growth name in Consumer Cyclicals. Mattel, the closest comparable, is flat-to-declining, has a 7.1 Rule of 40, and trades at 13.7x earnings / 8.8x EBITDA — Hasbro's ~25–35% multiple premium is more than earned by a 10-point revenue-growth gap and 1,600bp of gross-margin advantage. I would add on any pullback toward $95 (the pre-print breakout level / analyst consensus cluster).

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Single-brand concentration — the dominant risk.** MTG is **38.6% of total Q3 revenue** in one brand and one product cycle. A weak set, secondary-market saturation, or player fatigue (a recurring MTG bear case) could cut revenue mid-teens in a single quarter.
2. **Management explicitly flagged 2027 Wizards margin pressure from new game launches.** The 49.2% segment margin is likely a cyclical peak — consensus margin extrapolation is the biggest analytical trap here.
3. **Implied Q4 softness.** FY26 guidance (7–9% cc) implies Q4 revenue of ~$1.30–1.39B versus $1.45B last year — a decline — while consensus models $1.45B. The guidance raise and the Q4 math don't fully reconcile.
4. **Tariff and supply chain.** Margin beat was partly *tariff refunds* (one-time). Consumer Products still sources from Asia; a refund reversal or new tariff action hits the ~50% of revenue that is structurally low-growth.
5. **GAAP quality and balance sheet.** FY25 GAAP net loss of −$322M (Q2'25 included an ~$856M impairment), book value just **$4.03/share (27x P/B)**. No book cushion; the equity rests entirely on future cash flows.
6. **Key-person/execution risk** on CEO Chris Cocks' "Playing to Win" reset and CFO/COO Gina Goetter's cost program; retailer concentration (Walmart/Target/Amazon) plus a holiday-season-dependent Q4.
7. **Analyst PTs below spot ($101 mean vs $109.52)** — if Q4 disappoints, there is no valuation support from the sell side.
