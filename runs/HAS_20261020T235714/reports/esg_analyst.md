## ESG Analysis — Hasbro, Inc. (NASDAQ: HAS)
*Price $109.52 · Market cap $15.4bn · ~4,520 employees · Consumer Cyclical / Leisure (toys, games, IP)*

**Evidence base note:** The 10-K / DEF 14A retrieval tools returned no filing for HAS, and direct SEC archive fetches were blocked (403). Findings below draw on Hasbro's FY2025 Global Modern Slavery Statement, the CSR/ESG report site, published 8-Ks, and news/regulatory sources. Where I could not verify a metric (say-on-pay vote %, exact board composition, third-party ESG scores), I say so rather than invent it.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Carbon intensity: low in absolute terms.** Third-party estimates put total emissions at roughly 0.7–1.3 MtCO₂e (figures vary materially by estimator, a data-quality red flag in itself), implying ~150–280 tCO₂e per $1m of revenue — an order of magnitude below industrial sectors. **Scope 3 is >90%** and largely calculated on a *spend-based* method, meaning reported reductions can be accounting artefacts rather than real abatement.
- **Targets credible on paper:** SBTi-aligned near-term goals of −47.5% Scope 1+2 and −42% Scope 3 by 2030 off a 2019 base, net zero by 2050; a Climate Action Plan with a risk-and-resilience framework.
- **Regulatory cost exposure is real and near-term:** EU Toy Safety Regulation (tighter chemical limits, digital product passport), EU PPWR (recyclability/recycled content ~2030), EUDR (paper/wood fibre). These drive multi-year reformulation, tooling and packaging-conversion capex. Packaging initiatives (elimination of millions of lbs of virgin plastic; ~40% recycled/renewable content claims) are genuine but self-reported.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** no evidence found of sustainability-linked bonds or margin ratchets. **CBAM: not applicable** (toys/games not covered).
- **No material environmental enforcement actions or spills identified.**

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (with a High-severity tail)

- **Product safety:** The strongest mitigant — Hasbro has historically gone many years without a major CPSC recall and operates in-house Quality Assurance & Regulatory Affairs under the Chief Purpose Officer. My recall searches surfaced no HAS-attributed CPSC recall; the absence of a confirmed record is a verification gap, not proof of zero recalls.
- **Human capital: the weakest link.** Roughly **1,900 roles cut (~29% of workforce) through 2023**, a further **~150 (~3%) in June 2025**, with headcount down from ~4,985 (FY2024) to ~4,520. The cuts reached **Wizards of the Coast**, the profit engine driving the 2026 results (MTG revenue +34% to >$600m in Q3 2026). Simultaneously the CEO was awarded a contract at a **$1.5m base salary**. The "cut staff, pay the CEO" optics carry real retention risk in a creative/IP business where value walks out the door nightly — and this is exactly the sort of factor that eventually shows up as slower franchise cadence.
- **Supply chain:** Structural exposure to UFLPA/Xinjiang-adjacent risk through Chinese manufacturing. Mitigants are above-average: publicly published third-party factory list, RBA Code + Responsible Labor Initiative forced-labour commitment, no-worker-fee policy, Human Rights Policy, and reporting under UK/Australian/Canadian modern slavery regimes. Residual risk remains (tier-2 subcontractors, cotton/textile inputs).
- **Digital consumer protection:** randomized-item monetisation and AI-art disputes (WotC pulled AI marketing art after backlash) create recurring reputational friction with the core adult collector base — the segment now carrying growth.
- Positive recognition: Ethisphere World's Most Ethical, 3BL 100 Best Corporate Citizens, JUST Capital 2025 Industry Leader, Civic 50.

Score rationale: 6/10 = structurally sound (independence, one-share-one-vote, decent transparency) but with a lived record of strategic reversal, comp misalignment, and now a securities suit. Not a laggard; not a leader.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU:** Toy Safety Regulation (chemical limits, digital product passport) — high relevance; **PPWR** (packaging) — high; **EUDR** (fibre) — medium; **CSRD/CSDDD** — in scope via EU subsidiaries, timing pushed by the Omnibus simplification; **EU AI Act** transparency — low-medium (generative content); loot-box/consumer-protection scrutiny — medium.
- **US:** CPSIA and CPSC electronic certificate filing; **UFLPA** forced-labour import presumption — high relevance; state PFAS/juvenile-product chemical bans (CA, ME, MN, NY) — high; SEC climate-disclosure rule effectively dormant; no CBAM exposure.
- **Other:** UK/Australia/Canada modern slavery reporting (compliant); EPR packaging fees across Europe.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

A well-instrumented ESG programme (SBTi targets, published factory list, board-approved ethics code, repeated external recognition) sitting on top of a genuinely higher-than-average-risk business model (children's products, Asian contract manufacturing, aggressive digital monetisation) that has been through severe workforce contraction and strategic reversal. Disclosure is better than the sector norm; governance delivery and human-capital stability are the weak points.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

ESG is **not the thesis driver here, and it is not a disqualifier.** Three financially relevant channels for the investment case:

1. **Tariffs/supply chain (negative, quantified):** up to ~$300m of earnings exposure is the real ESG-adjacent risk — larger than any environmental or social item. Sourcing migration is a multi-year cost and quality-control risk; watch for factory-audit and product-safety slippage as production moves.
2. **Human capital at Wizards (negative, leading indicator):** repeated cuts inside the unit generating the growth (MTG >$600m/quarter) are the most under-priced sustainability risk in the name. Track MTG set cadence, designer attrition and community sentiment.
3. **Governance/disclosure (watch item):** the MTG-overproduction securities suit plus the 2008-and-2015-style capital-allocation history argues for a discount on management's forward guidance credibility, not on the ESG score itself.

Net: exploits no ESG-driven re-rating catalyst (no green premium, no index exclusion risk), and carries no imminent ESG-driven de-rating catalyst. Treat ESG as **neutral to the thesis**, with the tariff line as the only item material enough to move estimates — and that is already in guidance.
