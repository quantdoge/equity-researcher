# Hasbro, Inc. (NYSE: HAS) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $109.52 (20 Oct 2026 close, +11% post-Q3); market cap ~$15.5B; net debt ~$2.08B; EV ~$17.6B*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow** (with a Wide-moat nucleus in Wizards of the Coast)

Hasbro is now two businesses wearing one ticker.

**Wizards of the Coast & Digital Gaming (~50% of revenue, majority of profit): Wide moat.**
- **Magic: The Gathering** is a 30+ year-old collectible ecosystem with genuine network effects: a deep secondary market, tournament/community infrastructure, and a collector base with high switching costs (collections are illiquid outside the ecosystem).
- Demonstrated **pricing power**: Secret Lair drops, premium set pricing, and the *Universes Beyond* strategy (Lord of the Rings, Fallout, Final Fantasy, Marvel) which imports external IP into MTG's mechanics rather than renting shelf space. The Final Fantasy set was reportedly the best-selling MTG set ever and drove FY2025 Wizards revenue up 45%.
- **Dungeons & Dragons** adds a durable licensed IP with a network-effect community (actual-play, VTT).
- Economics prove the moat: Q3 FY2026 consolidated **gross margin 75.9%** and operating margin 32.4% — impossible without a high-margin royalty/IP-mix engine. Wizards contributed ~$251M of Q3's $342M segment operating profit per trade press.

**Consumer Products (NERF, PLAY-DOH, TRANSFORMERS, MONOPOLY, G.I. Joe): Narrow-to-none.**
- Beloved brands but **no pricing power against Walmart/Target/Amazon**, heavy China/Vietnam sourcing (tariff-exposed), fad risk, and kids' attention share migrating to screens. Revenue has drifted down for most of the last five years.

**Moat trend (5 years):** Clearly *widening* at Wizards, *narrowing* at Consumer Products, and management has correctly reallocated capital toward the moat (divested eOne production assets, licensed film/TV content out instead of owning it, expanded high-margin digital licensing — Monopoly Go! royalties, Baldur's Gate 3). Net effect: the profit pool is migrating to the better business, which is a real quality improvement, but the enterprise still carries a cyclical, capital-intensive toy business alongside it.

**Verdict on moat:** Narrow overall. The 10-year question is whether Wizards' collector economics compound or revert after the current hype cycle.

**DCF / owner-earnings (WACC 8.5%, terminal 2.5%, FCF base $1.05B):**
- **Base** (5% FCF growth yrs 1–5, 3% yrs 6–10): EV ~$20.6B → **~$130/share**
- **Bull** (8% then 4%, Wizards remains a double-digit grower): **~$145–150**
- **Bear** (MTG normalizes, FCF reverts to ~$800M, 0% growth, 9% WACC): **~$65**
- Probability-weighted (50/25/25): **~$118**

At $109.52 the shares sit **~7% below probability-weighted intrinsic value and ~16% below base case**. That is a fair price for a good-but-uneven business — not a Buffett price. My required entry (25–30% discount to base case) is **~$85–88**, a level the stock traded at as recently as late July 2026. Peer P/E comparison is unhelpful here (GAAP losses make median-P/E screens meaningless); the relevant anchor is that a mid-teens multiple on peak-ish adjusted EPS leaves nothing for error.

## 4. Management Quality — **Improving / Above average** (grade: B)

- **Chris Cocks (CEO since 2022)** inherited an empire-building mistake — the ~$4B eOne acquisition — and has spent his tenure unwinding it: eOne sold for a fraction of cost, Entertainment production de-risked via licensing, cost base cut, $2B of debt retired, dividend preserved through the trough, and buybacks restarted only now that leverage is repaired. Sequenced correctly, in my view.
- **Guidance credibility is currently strong:** three beats in FY2026 (Q1 $1.47 vs $1.20; Q2 $1.28 vs $1.16; Q3 $2.90 vs $1.86) with a 10%+ raise to EBITDA guidance.
- **Blemishes:** repeated impairments (FY2023, FY2025) indicate the prior M&A underwriting was poor; payout discipline on the dividend (held at $0.70/qtr through loss years) was defensible but consumed ~$390M/yr while debt was elevated. Insider ownership data was not available to me — a gap worth closing before purchase.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (accumulate below ~$88)

Hasbro is a materially better business than it was three years ago: the profit mix has shifted to a genuine wide-moat asset, margins have structurally re-rated (consolidated gross margin from 58% to ~70%+), and the balance sheet is repaired. If Wizards compounds at low-double digits, base-case value is ~$130 and today's price is merely full, not silly.

But the discipline test cuts the other way: at $109.52, after an 11% one-day pop, an investor earns ~6.7% FCF yield for a company with a cyclical toy division, a hit-driven collectibles franchise at a cyclical peak, and a demonstrated history of destroying capital in M&A. **Fair price ≠ buy price.** I want 25%+ off intrinsic value. At ~$85–88 the risk/reward flips to attractive (buy); at $130+ (bull multiple on bull earnings) I would trim.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis
1. **MTG hype-cycle reversal** — *Universes Beyond* and premium pricing have pulled forward demand. Secondary-market softness or collector fatigue (as seen in 2023's overprinting episode) could cut EBITDA by $300–500M quickly. This is the single largest risk.
2. **Consumer Products + tariffs** — concentrated retail customers (Walmart/Target/Amazon) and Asian sourcing; a tariff or holiday-season miss hits FY2027 guidance.
3. **Licensing dependency** — Disney/Marvel, Square Enix, and digital partners hold renewal leverage over the highest-margin revenue.
4. **Balance-sheet/accounting quality** — $3.5B gross debt, thin $0.9B equity, $1.67B goodwill/intangibles; a third impairment round is plausible if Consumer Products deteriorates.
5. **Execution concentration** — Cocks' turnaround is the thesis; a leadership change or a reversion to empire-building M&A would be a sell signal.
