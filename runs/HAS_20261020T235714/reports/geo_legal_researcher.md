## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US tariff architecture is legally unstable, not resolved. (Probability: High / Impact: Medium, two-sided)**
On **February 20, 2026** the Supreme Court held (6-3) that IEEPA does not authorize tariffs, invalidating the tariffs imposed under IEEPA since February 2025. Crucially, **the decision did not prescribe refund mechanics** — that was left to the CIT/CBP process, with refunds projected at up to **$175B** industry-wide (Penn Wharton). Section 232 measures **remain fully in effect** (BDO, Flexport, Seyfarth). Hasbro is directly in this: the **Q1 2026 10-Q recorded ~$8.3M of tariff costs in cost of sales**, and on **February 27, 2026 Hasbro joined hundreds of companies suing the US government for IEEPA refunds** (Reuters). Implication: tariff cost is a recurring P&L headwind *and* a potential one-time refund receivable — a genuine swing factor in Consumer Products, which posted a **$47.5M operating loss** in Q1 2026. Downside case: refunds are slow/partial and are replaced by Section 232/122/301 measures, leaving rates elevated with new administrative cost.

**Risk 2 — China sourcing concentration amid renewed US-China escalation. (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: High)**
The October 2026 geopolitical tape shows the relationship deteriorating on technology and trade: US sanctions on five Chinese firms under the Protect American Intellectual Property Act (Oct 19), a US ban on DeepSeek in government systems (NYT, Oct 20), and China proposing **export controls on 40 technology categories** plus tighter counter-sanctions/foreign-trade and outbound-investment rules (Reuters; JD Supra). Toys are not a target sector, but Hasbro is a *physical importer* exposed to the second-order channel: retaliatory measures, port/customs friction, container rates, and product-level regulatory retaliation. Separately, Hasbro's highest-margin asset (Magic: The Gathering) is a **counterfeiting target** in China, an IP-enforcement risk that escalates when bilateral cooperation weakens.

**Risk 3 — European regulatory tightening into a soft European consumer. (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
**EU Toy Safety Regulation (EU) 2025/2509**, published **December 12, 2025**, replaces the Toy Safety Directive: new chemical bans, revised warning/labelling, and a **Digital Product Passport** (under ESPR 2024/1781). This is a mandatory redesign, documentation, and data-infrastructure spend across the entire EU toy portfolio — a direct margin drag on the Consumer Products segment that is already loss-making, layered on soft European consumer confidence. Secondary: chemical/recall risk (CPSC and EU RAPEX) is existential for a toy brand, and one adverse recall in a large SKU can outsize a year of tariff cost.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Tariff refund litigation (active, upside-oriented).** Hasbro v. US government, filed **Feb 27, 2026** in the CIT, seeking refund of IEEPA duties (Reuters). Hundreds of co-plaintiffs; refunds administered via CBP post-entry processes from May 1, 2026 entries. Treat as a contingent asset, not a certainty.

**Securities class action (active).** *West Palm Beach Firefighters' Pension Fund v. Hasbro, Inc.*, S.D.N.Y. **1:24-cv-08633**, filed **November 13, 2024** (BLB&G). Alleges misleading statements; ran alongside a BofA report that reportedly triggered the claim. Status of the motion to dismiss was not confirmed in available sources — **open item**. Exposure is a monetary settlement of modest size relative to a $15B cap, plus D&O insurance.

**MTG "overprinting" derivative suit (dismissed, with prejudice-risk).** *Crocono & McGlone v. Cocks et al.*, D.R.I., filed **Jan 21, 2026** naming the CEO and 13 executives plus 7 directors over alleged overprinting of Magic sets and a $55.9M 2022 loss. **Voluntarily dismissed February 17, 2026, without prejudice** — meaning it can be refiled. Record FY2025 Magic growth (+59%) and Q3 2026 Magic revenue >$600M (+34%) largely neutralize the damages theory.

**Cybersecurity / data privacy (material, under-appreciated).** On **March 28, 2026** Hasbro identified **unauthorized access to its network**, took systems offline, and disclosed expected Q2 2026 revenue and operating-profit impact from order-processing, shipping and invoicing delays (cybernews; CSIDB; Q1 2026 10-Q). A **separate later incident affecting employee personal data** has also been disclosed. This creates: SEC cybersecurity-disclosure and ICFR scrutiny, state AG and GDPR/UK-GDPR inquiry risk, and consumer class-action risk. Alongside it, Hasbro's digital gaming footprint (MTG Arena, licensed Monopoly GO!) attracts **COPPA / UK Age-Appropriate-Design / EU GDPR** exposure — the FTC has repeatedly fined toy and gaming peers (VTech, Hyperbeard, Microsoft/Xbox), so the enforcement precedent in this exact vertical is well established.

**IP.** Long history of trademark friction (EU General Court revoked MONOPOLY mark in 2021), ongoing counterfeit enforcement for MTG, and licensing relationships (Universes Beyond) that carry royalty step-ups — Q1 2026 margins were pressured by "higher royalties tied to Universes Beyond sets."

**Other regulators:** CPSC/CPSIA (button-cell batteries, magnets, small parts), EU RAPEX, and FTC (advertising to children). No evidence of an active DOJ/FCPA, OFAC, or antitrust matter in the retrieved record.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Cyber incident + delayed revenue recognition** — disclosed Q2 2026 impact from the March 2026 intrusion. Requires checking whether an Item 1.05 8-K was timely filed and whether ICFR was qualified. Highest-probability source of new litigation.
2. **Derivative suit dismissed *without prejudice*** — refiling is a live option; watch the docket.
3. **Tariff refund recovery depends on entry-level accuracy** — HTS classification, valuation, and First Sale documentation. Weak customs data quality forfeits the refund.
4. **No material sanctions/AML exposure identified** — Russia exposure has been exited; Iran/North Korea appear immaterial. This is a genuine strength versus consumer-cyclical peers.
5. **China counter-sanctions regime** — new foreign-trade/exit-entry powers could complicate sourcing contracts and IP enforcement.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale for MEDIUM (not High):** >60% of revenue is earned in the US and ~20% in Europe — jurisdictions with strong rule of law, predictable contract enforcement, and no sanctions or capital-control exposure. There is no material Russia, Iran, or conflict-zone revenue. The largest single legal matter (the MTG derivative suit) was dismissed and is undermined by record Magic performance, and the securities class action appears containable. Litigation is not an existential vector here.

**What keeps it off "Low":** (a) the tariff regime is legally unstable post-IEEPA rather than settled, with Section 232 intact and refunds unresolved; (b) manufacturing is concentrated in China and China+1 while revenue is US-centric — maximum exposure to trade-policy shocks per dollar of revenue; (c) Consumer Products is structurally fragile (Q1 2026 operating loss of $47.5M), so it cannot easily absorb new regulatory or tariff cost; (d) an unresolved 2026 cyber incident introduces disclosure, privacy, and class-action tail risk.

**Key monitorables for the next two quarters:** CIT refund posture and any Section 232/122 replacement measures; EU 2025/2509 implementation timeline and its quantified compliance cost; D&O/cyber disclosures and any 8-K Item 1.05 amendment; whether the derivative suit is refiled; and China counter-sanction developments affecting sourcing contracts.
