## TransUnion (TRU) — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment

**Bottom line:** TRU is a ~78% US revenue business with a chronic, well-understood domestic regulatory/litigation tax and a ~22% international book spread across six reporting regions. There is no material sanctions, expropriation, or war-zone exposure. The dominant risk is **policy/regulatory**, not geopolitical — specifically US mortgage credit-scoring rules and the FICO/VantageScore competitive-legal fight, plus the fallout from the July 2025 data breach. **Overall rating: MEDIUM.**

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**1) US mortgage credit-scoring regime shift (FICO vs. VantageScore vs. FHFA) — Probability: High; Impact: High**
The FHFA has allowed VantageScore 4.0 on all Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac loans "effective immediately," and TRU responded by extending **99-cent VantageScore 4.0 mortgage pricing through end-2028** to press its advantage. FICO has shed ~15% of staff after losing >50% of its market value. TRU carries 2–3 points of FY2026 revenue benefit from the mortgage/FICO royalty change, and guidance explicitly cites *"uncertainty related to Fair Isaac Corporation's new Mortgage Direct License Program"* as a risk factor. This is a single-policy-event exposure worth multiple points of revenue and a large share of sentiment — a reversal or a FICO direct-licensing model that bypasses bureaus would be materially negative. *Sources: TransUnion 29 Sep 2026 release; PRNewswire/FHFA; Benzinga 30 Sep 2026; 24/7 Wall St 7 Oct 2026; Q2 2026 release risk factors.*

**2) India regulatory tightening + revenue volatility — Probability: Medium; Impact: Medium**
India is the second-largest international market (~5% of group) but has swung from −9% reported (Q4'25) to +8% cc (Q2'26). The RBI actively regulates Credit Information Companies (including mandated consumer alerts on credit-report access) and India's **DPDP Act + DPDP Rules 2025** impose consent, data-principal rights, breach notification and penalties up to ₹250 crore, alongside localisation expectations for financial data. TransUnion CIBIL is the dominant Indian bureau, which raises both regulatory-attention and market-power risk. *Sources: RBI guidance coverage; Economic Times/CISO DPDP Rules analysis; Q4'25 & Q2'26 releases.*

**3) Latin America step-up in exposure via TransUnion de México — Probability: Medium; Impact: Medium**
The Mexico acquisition (2026) drove LatAm revenue from $34.8M (Q4'25) to $93M (Q2'26, +172% reported, +5% organic) — roughly doubling the region's weight to ~7% of quarterly group revenue. That adds peso FX, Mexican data-protection/consumer-credit regulation, and rule-of-law/security dynamics to a book that already includes Brazil and Colombia. Separately, US **tariffs and trade policy** and *"ongoing conflict in the Middle East"* are named in the company's own forward-looking risk disclosures, though direct exposure appears limited. *Source: Q2 2026 earnings release; Q2 2026 10-Q summary.*

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **July 2025 data breach (most material legal item).** Unauthorised access via a **third-party application/vendor** exposed data of **~4.46 million US consumers**; company states core credit-reporting systems were not breached. Multiple plaintiffs' firms have opened class-action investigations (Ahdoot & Wolfson, Lieff Cabraser, Murphy Law). No disclosed regulatory penalty or settlement quantum in the sources reviewed — **treat the reserve/accrual as a disclosure gap to verify in the 10-K "Legal Proceedings" note.** *Sources: security.org; natlawreview; JD Supra Privacy Tip #474; mexicobusiness.news.*
- **FCRA litigation is a structural cost, not an event.** TRU has absorbed a record FCRA class verdict (~$8M compensatory for 8,185 consumers, plus punitive exposure), a $2.5M FCRA settlement (Wilson), and a 2024 Philadelphia class certification for misreporting. **Mitigant:** *TransUnion v. Ramirez* (SCOTUS 2021) materially narrowed Article III standing for absent class members — a durable defence.
- **CFPB history and the changed federal posture.** 2017 consent order ($13.93M consumer redress + $3M penalty) over credit-score marketing; 2022 consent order (~$15M redress/$4M penalty) over "dark patterns," followed by a 2022 CFPB suit alleging violation of that order. Critically, the 2025–26 CFPB has **dismissed 23 pending public enforcement actions** — federal enforcement risk is currently *lower*, shifting weight to state AGs and private plaintiffs. Do not capitalise a benign federal environment permanently.
- **UK FCA:** a 10 Jan 2025 "Dear CEO" letter set the FCA's strategy for Credit Reference Agencies and credit-information providers; the credit-information market study remedies (data-sharing coverage, dispute-resolution quality) continue to shape conduct obligations in a 5.9% revenue market that is growing strongly (+22% reported Q4'25, +9% cc Q2'26).
- **US state layer:** proliferating comprehensive privacy laws, data-broker registration laws, and standalone credit-reporting statutes create a fragmented compliance perimeter across the 78% US book — the practical constraint on the global OneTru platform consolidation.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Third-party/supply-chain privacy risk is now proven**, not hypothetical — 4.46M-record breach via a vendor application. Expect elevated scrutiny from state AGs and possible ICO/FCA interest given UK operations.
2. **Cross-border data localisation vs. platform strategy:** OneTru centralises global data infrastructure while India (DPDP/financial-sector localisation), UK GDPR and Brazil LGPD push the other way. This is a live architectural and compliance tension.
3. **Concentration in a single regulator's discretion** (FHFA/GSE scoring eligibility) — no geopolitical risk in the portfolio compares to this in magnitude.
4. **No material FCPA/AML/sanctions red flags identified.** TRU is a net *seller* of compliance tooling (identity verification, sanctions/watchlist screening), and the geographic footprint disclosed excludes sanctioned jurisdictions. Residual exposure is customer-side (gaming, public sector), not systemic.
5. **CFO transition** (Todd Cello stepping down after nine years, through 31 Dec 2026) — governance item worth monitoring alongside the disclosure-heavy legal agenda.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** ~78% of revenue sits in the lowest-geopolitical-risk jurisdiction in the world, FX is guided immaterial, and there is no Russia/China/sanctions tail. Offsetting this: an internationally active but *sub-scale* EM book (India, Mexico/LatAm, Africa, APAC ≈ 12% of revenue) that is growing faster than the group and therefore rising in weight; a permanent, high-frequency consumer-litigation cost line; an unresolved material cyber/privacy incident; and a US policy dependency (mortgage scoring) capable of moving revenue by several points on a single administrative decision.

**Watch items for the next 12 months:** (i) TransUnion de México integration and any LatAm FX/political shock; (ii) a VantageScore/FICO direct-licensing détente or escalation; (iii) quantification of breach-related reserves and any state-AG action; (iv) India DPDP Rules enforcement commencement and RBI CIC rulemaking; (v) UK FCA credit-information remedy implementation.
