# TRANSUNION (TRU) — NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT
**As of 20 Oct 2026 | Price $78.23 | Mkt cap ~$15.0B | Chicago, IL | 13,500 FTE | CEO: Christopher Cartwright**

**Bottom line:** TRU is exposed to a live, politically-driven regulatory repricing of its core U.S. mortgage business, layered on top of a 4.4M-consumer data breach, a CFO vacancy-in-progress, and insider selling into strength. This is the highest regulatory-risk profile I would assign to a large-cap data provider outside of active DOJ litigation.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (trending weaker)

**Positives — structural checks and balances are real:**
- **No CEO/chairman duality.** Pamela Joseph has served as independent Chairman since January 2020; Cartwright sits on the board as a director only. This is a genuine independent-leadership structure, not a fig leaf.
- Board includes credible outside directors (e.g., Suzanne Clark), and the chair transition in 2020 was orderly — evidence of succession discipline at the board level.
- The company is investing in the executive bench rather than contracting: a **new Chief Strategy & Corporate Development Officer (Malte Bernholz)** was created and filled on 28 Sep 2026.

**Negatives — the concerns are about continuity and timing, not capture:**
- **CFO departure at the worst possible moment.** Todd Cello notified the company on **19 Sep 2026** that he will resign as EVP/CFO after 29 years, including nine as CFO. He stays through 31 Dec 2026 and as a full-time advisor to 1 Mar 2027. Critically, **no successor has been named** — only an external search has been "initiated." A nine-year CFO walking out mid-way through a regulator-driven repricing of the revenue base, and mid-way through a platform migration, removes the principal external voice of financial credibility exactly when investors need it. A six-month handover is better than the alternative, but it also means ~5 months of lame-duck financial leadership.
- **Repeated enforcement history implicates process control, not just legal budget** (see §2). A consent order in 2017 followed by a 2022 allegation of violating that same order is a classic indicator that compliance remediation was cosmetic. That the 2022 case was subsequently **dropped by the CFPB** reflects a change in enforcement philosophy in Washington, not proof the underlying controls were fixed.
- **Executive tenure skew.** Cartwright has been CEO since 2013; the CFO had 29 years of service. Long tenure is generally positive for cultural consistency, but it raises a board-oversight question: the same leadership team has now presided over two CFPB actions, a 4.4M-record breach, and the loss of pricing power in its most profitable vertical.

**Insider alignment:** Insiders hold meaningful equity (one insider still holds ~78,888 shares after selling), which is a mild positive, but there is no evidence of conviction buying at current depressed levels.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: HIGH — the dominant risk in this name

**A. FHFA / political pricing campaign (escalating, not resolving)**
FHFA Director Bill Pulte has run an explicitly public, personal campaign against credit bureau pricing since mid-2025, using X posts to signal policy. Concrete milestones:

- **Jul 2025:** FHFA approved VantageScore 4.0 for GSE loans while retaining tri-merge — the first crack in FICO's mandate and, by extension, in the bureaus' bundled pricing power.
- **Jan 2026:** Pulte publicly stated, *"I do not know what the credit bureaus are doing with their pricing. They are inviting a lot of scrutiny and that is only intensifying by the day,"* and signaled he would take steps to "protect consumers" in response to an MBA letter requesting relief from the requirement to buy reports from all three bureaus.
- **The MBA proposal** would allow lenders to skip the full tri-merge for borrowers scoring above 700 — a direct attack on the unit economics of TRU's most profitable product. The industry trade body (CDIA) is lobbying hard against it, which tells you the revenue at stake.
- **Sep 2026:** FHFA announced VantageScore 4.0 availability for all approved Fannie/Freddie lenders; **TRU responded by extending 99-cent mortgage pricing for VantageScore 4.0 through end-2028** — a three-year commitment to price at a level far below historical tri-merge economics. This is a voluntary margin sacrifice to defend share, and it locks in the damage.
- **Oct 2026:** Reports that FHFA is weighing a **two-bureau and/or single-file mortgage rule**; credit-reporting stocks sold off on the headline.

**B. Disintermediation by FICO**
FICO is rolling out **direct mortgage-score licensing that cuts the bureaus out of the transaction**. For a company whose moat is the bundle of file + score, this is an existential-adjacent threat: TRU loses the score attachment revenue and becomes a commoditized data pipe. FICO has lost more than half its value and is cutting 15% of staff, so this is a desperate, aggressive competitor — the most dangerous kind.

**C. Consumer-finance enforcement history (repeat-offender pattern)**
- **2017 consent order:** $13.9M restitution + $3M civil penalty (deceptive credit-score/monitoring marketing).
- **Apr 2022:** CFPB sued TransUnion **and longtime executive John Danaher** (former TransUnion Interactive president) for violating that 2017 order. Suing an individual executive is a signal of alleged personal accountability, not just corporate error.
- **Oct 2023:** Joint FTC/CFPB order (~$15M, incl. a $5M CFPB civil money penalty) over tenant-background-check accuracy failures, plus a **separate $8M redress-and-penalty order for failing to timely place or remove security freezes**. Freeze failures are a consumer-protection flashpoint that attracts state AG attention.
- **Status note:** the 2022 action was later **dropped** by the CFPB — favorable, but it means the 2022 allegations were never adjudicated on the merits.

**D. Data privacy / breach-related exposure** — see §5. State AG notifications were filed (e.g., Vermont, Sep 2025), which is the typical precursor to multistate inquiry.

*Data gap:* EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned zero results for TRU despite the 2023 orders being publicly documented — a tool limitation, not evidence of a clean record. The 10-K risk factors, 10-Q, and DEF 14A could not be retrieved, so I could not verify the company's own litigation-reserve disclosures or pending-matter language.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Revenue concentration in U.S. mortgage credit reporting facing structural (not cyclical) repricing.** The tri-merge bundle — three reports, mandatory, at $100+ per pull versus $15 in 2010 — is the profit pool under attack. TRU's own 99-cent commitment through 2028 demonstrates it expects to compete on price permanently. This is a business-model risk, not a cost-of-goods risk, and it sits at the center of the most profitable segment.
2. **Cybersecurity and data-integrity failure.** TRU disclosed a breach (discovered **30 Jul 2025**) affecting **4.4 million Americans'** personal information, notified state AGs in early Sep 2025, offered 42 months of credit monitoring, and faces class-action investigation. For a custodian of the nation's credit files, a breach is a franchise event: it invites regulatory scrutiny, weakens its negotiating position with lenders, and — with bitter irony — undercuts the industry's "cybersecurity costs justify our pricing" defense, which Pulte is already attacking.
3. **Platform migration execution (OneTru) plus leadership churn.** OneTru migrations and cloud modernization are the growth narrative, but large-scale data-platform migrations at a regulated bureau carry real risk of service degradation, accuracy errors, and cost overruns. Running that program while the CFO exits and a new CSCO ramps is an underappreciated execution risk. Layered on this is meaningful debt from the $3.1B Neustar acquisition (2021), which limits slack if mortgage revenue steps down.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: ELEVATED

- **The brand is being publicly attacked by a federal regulator by name.** "The credit bureaus are inviting scrutiny" from the FHFA Director is not routine criticism; it is a signal to lenders and to Congress that the industry's pricing is illegitimate. Consumer trust in the "Big Three" is already structurally weak — credit bureaus rank near the bottom of consumer-sentiment surveys — so there is no reservoir of goodwill to absorb this.
- **Breach optics are unusually bad.** A company that sells identity protection and credit monitoring exposed 4.4 million consumers' data. Expect it to be cited in every consumer-advocacy filing and in any state AG action.
- **Countervailing evidence:** TRU is actively investing in brand and product credibility — a MediaPost OMMA award and a TIME Best Inventions 2026 nod (Branded Call Display), plus a new synthetic-identity/credit-washing scoring suite launched 20 Oct 2026. Marketing execution is strong; the reputational problem is structural (its industry position), not a messaging failure.
- **Price-war optics:** publicly slashing mortgage score prices to 99 cents reads as either consumer-friendly or as an admission that the old pricing was indefensible. Competitors and regulators will frame it the second way.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rating is driven by (a) a credible, well-advanced path to structural reduction in mortgage credit-reporting revenue via FHFA rulemaking, (b) active competitive disintermediation by FICO, (c) a 4.4M-person breach with unresolved litigation, and (d) net insider selling with zero insider buying. These are partially offset by a genuinely independent board chair, a credible compliance/legal track record of resolving matters (the 2022 case was dropped; 2023 orders were satisfied), and strong product-innovation cadence.

**What would move this to Critical:** a final FHFA rule eliminating the tri-merge or mandating two-bureau/single-file reports; a multistate AG action or new CFPB/FTC complaint over the 2025 breach; or an insider sale pattern that can be tied to non-public information.
**What would move it to Moderate:** a final FHFA rule that preserves tri-merge optionality, a named and credible CFO successor, and evidence of insider buying at these levels.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **[ESCALATE — HIGH] CFO vacancy during peak regulatory stress.** Nine-year CFO resigns 19 Sep 2026; no successor named; search "initiated." Combined with a new CSCO and an ongoing OneTru migration, this is a continuity risk the market has not fully priced.
2. **[ESCALATE — HIGH] FHFA rulemaking on two-bureau/single-file reporting is live.** A Bloomberg report alone moved the stock ~6% on 1 Oct. Binary policy event; the 99-cent/2028 pricing commitment signals management expects permanent price deflation.
3. **[ESCALATE — MEDIUM-HIGH] FICO direct-to-lender score licensing.** Disintermediation risk to the score attachment layer of the bundle; watch for any TRU disclosure of mortgage score-attach volumes.
4. **[ESCALATE — MEDIUM] 4.4M-consumer breach (discovered 30 Jul 2025).** State AG notifications filed; class actions pending; quantify reserve adequacy and any insurance recoveries before underwriting.
5. **[WATCH — MEDIUM] Achanta's $1.79M sale at $77 on 10 Sep, 10 days before the CFO notification.** Confirm 10b5-1 plan adoption date and whether the plan was amended within the prior 90 days. Absent that documentation, treat as a timing flag.
6. **[WATCH — MEDIUM] Repeat CFPB/FTC enforcement pattern (2017, 2022, 2023), including an individual executive named in 2022.** Suggests compliance remediation has historically lagged; verify the current state of the 2023 tenant-screening accuracy remediation.
7. **[NOTE] Zero insider purchases through a 29% drawdown.** No director has bought the dip.
