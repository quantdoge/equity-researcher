## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: SUB-INVESTMENT GRADE (crossover BB+/Ba2), stable outlook**

| Source | Rating | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Moody's | Ba2 Corporate Family Rating | Affirmed, outlook stable |
| S&P Global | BB+ local currency LT | Affirmed 11/14/2025, stable |

This is a solid BB credit with an improving trajectory, not a deteriorating one. The company sits one notch below investment grade at S&P, and the fundamentals are moving in the right direction: LTM interest coverage has improved to **4.3x** from 3.6x, net leverage has come down from ~3.0x to ~2.9x, and management reported **2.6x adjusted net leverage** in Q2 2026.

The counterweight is structural: **77% of assets are goodwill and intangibles** ($9,332M of $12,189M), producing **negative tangible book equity of roughly –$4.3B**. This is normal for a data/credit bureau but means the balance sheet offers almost no loss-absorbing cushion below the intangible layer. In 2023 this exact dynamic produced a **$206M net loss** on an impairment charge — tangible proof that a valuation reset hits the P&L directly.

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue –20%

Applied to LTM revenue of $4,896M (–$979M), assuming ~68% contribution margin given the fixed nature of data acquisition, the One True platform, and 13,500 employees:

| Line | Base (LTM) | –20% Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4,896M | $3,917M |
| EBIT | $1,080M | ~$414M |
| EBITDA | $1,686M | ~$1,020M |
| Interest expense | $252M | $252M |
| **Interest coverage** | **4.29x** | **~1.64x** |
| **Net debt / EBITDA** | **2.86x** | **~4.73x** |
| FCF (post-tax hit) | ~$700M | **~$200M** |
| Dividend ($95M/yr) | Covered 7x | Covered ~2x |

**Survival verdict: the company survives a 20% revenue decline, but with no room to spare.** FCF stays positive and the dividend is still covered. The binding constraint is the revolver covenant: at ~4.7x net leverage, TRU would likely breach a springing net-leverage test set near 4.5x. That forces a choice between drawing liquidity, cutting the ~$400M/yr buyback (the obvious first lever), or an equity raise.

At **–30% revenue**, EBITDA falls to ~$680M, leverage hits ~7.1x and coverage drops below 1.0x — that is genuine distress territory.

**Important context:** a 20% revenue decline is a low-probability tail for this model, not a base case. Revenue has grown every year in the sample ($3,710M → $4,576M → LTM $4,896M, ~7% CAGR) — **including 2023, when U.S. mortgage originations collapsed** and revenue still rose 3.3%. Roughly 80% of revenue is recurring/subscription. The realistic risk is a 5–8% cyclical dip, which the 2.9x leverage and 4.3x coverage absorb comfortably.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

The company generates durable, high-margin, recurring cash flow and is actively deleveraging. Leverage at 2.9x GAAP / 2.6x adjusted, coverage at 4.3x, and FCF of ~$660–700M are consistent with a stable BB credit. The rating is capped at Moderate rather than Low by negative tangible equity, sub-investment-grade ratings, and a market-risk profile (42% vol, –43% drawdown) that behaves nothing like the underlying business.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Re-leveraging while returning capital.** Total debt rose $501M in H1 2026 (goodwill +$974M → Mexico bureau acquisition) *while* repurchasing $150M of stock. Debt-funded M&A plus buybacks is how BB credits stall their deleveraging story.
2. **FICO disintermediation risk.** FICO's Mortgage Direct License Program (50% discount, direct to lenders) targets TransUnion's mortgage royalty stream — high-margin revenue the company flags explicitly in guidance. It cost shareholders 11.2% in a day and is unresolved.
3. **Negative tangible equity (–$4.3B).** A repeat of the 2023 impairment is the fastest path from BB to B.
4. **Coverage of 3.6x on FY2025 GAAP EBIT** is thin for the rating category; the 4.3x LTM figure depends on continued EBITDA growth.
5. **Negative three-year Sharpe and –41% five-year return** — equity holders are bearing BB-like credit risk *and* growth-equity volatility without compensation.
6. **CFO transition.** Todd Cello departs 12/31/26 after 29 years. Low fundamental risk, but it removes the architect of the current leverage target mid-cycle.
7. **Data quality issues in the feed.** Q1 2026 net income ($397M) exceeds operating income ($245M) by ~$152M, implying a large below-the-line gain that distorts TTM ROA and net income; SBC is reported as $0 in FY2025 cash flow versus $121M in FY2024. My LTM figures use operating income + D&A rather than the vendor EBITDA field, which is internally inconsistent for Q1'26 and Q2'26.

**Explicit data gaps:** contractual debt maturity ladder; precise covenant thresholds and revolver capacity; CDS spreads; peer leverage benchmarking (the sector-peers tool returned Vanguard index funds and PIMCO closed-end funds — not usable comparables).
