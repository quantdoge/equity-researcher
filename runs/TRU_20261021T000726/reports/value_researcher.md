## TransUnion (TRU) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $78.23 (20-Oct-2026 close) · ~193.7M diluted shares · Market cap ~$15.1B · Net debt $4.82B (Q2'26) · EV ~$19.9B*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **WIDE** (at the operating level)

**What it is:** TransUnion is one of the three U.S. consumer credit bureaus (with Equifax and Experian) — a regulated data oligopoly. It aggregates data from ~100k furnishers into credit files, then rents access via an entrenched, contract-based, high-incremental-margin model. Roughly 55% U.S. Markets / 45% International + Consumer Interactive.

**Moat sources:**
- **Intangible assets / regulatory license:** FCRA-regulated, decades-old data assets that cannot be replicated. New entrants are legally and economically foreclosed.
- **Network effects:** more furnishers → richer files → more lenders → more furnishers. Self-reinforcing.
- **Switching costs:** bureau data is embedded in underwriting engines, scorecards, decisioning workflows and compliance processes. Re-pricing pushes through annually; churn is low.
- **Cost advantage:** near-zero marginal cost of a credit report; capex is ~7% of revenue.

**Evidence (5-yr):** Revenue $2.53B (2020) → ~$4.90B TTM (+10.9% CAGR), with *organic* constant-currency growth re-accelerating to 8–9% in 2026 (11% in Q1'26). Adjusted EBITDA margin ~36%. Pre-goodwill returns on tangible capital are extraordinary (>100%), confirming real pricing power.

**The honest caveat — moat ≠ per-share returns:** Reported ROIC has been only 6.2% (2022) → 8.8% (2025), below my ~9–10% WACC estimate. The cause is not the business; it is **$8.4B of goodwill + intangibles** (77% of total assets) from Neustar ($3.1B, 2021) and other deals bought near peak multiples, plus a 2023 impairment. Management converted a wide moat into sub-WACC total-capital returns by paying too much. That is a capital-allocation debit, not a moat debit, but for a value investor it is the single most important nuance in the file.

**Moat direction:** Stable. The triopoly is intact; the FHFA's opening of GSE mortgage scoring to VantageScore is a potential *tailwind* (TRU is a VantageScore owner). Data-privacy/AI-driven alternative data is a slow erosion risk, not an imminent one.

TRU is the cheapest of the three data oligopolists — a persistent discount justified by higher leverage and a worse M&A record, not by a worse business. That is where the opportunity *could* be.

**Intrinsic value (owner-earnings DCF, base FCF $780M normalized):**

| Scenario | WACC | FCF growth (yr 1–5 / 6–10 / terminal) | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 10.0% | 7% / 4% / 2.5% | **~$54** |
| Base | 8.5% | 7% / 4% / 2.5% | **~$64** |

**Triangulated intrinsic value: ~$66 (range $54–$74).** Cross-checks: 15x 2027E adj. EPS (~$5.30) = $79; 18x 2026E = $85. A 10% required return on today's $19.9B EV implies roughly a decade of ~9–10% FCF growth — i.e., **the current price already assumes consensus goes right on organic growth, margin expansion and a mortgage recovery.**

**Current $78.23 is ~18% ABOVE base intrinsic value.** There is *no* margin of safety; there is a modest premium. A 25% margin-of-safety entry is **~$50**, or ~$55–58 for a 15% discount.

**What the market is paying for:** The stock has been violently cyclical around this valuation — $43 (Oct'23) → $105 (Oct'24) → $61 (Sep'26) → $78 now. At $61 it was a genuine fat pitch (13x adj. EPS, 6.5% FCF yield). It has since re-rated ~28% in three weeks. That move, not a change in fundamentals, is why the recommendation changes.

## 4. Management Quality — **B / B+**

- **CEO Christopher Cartwright** (since 2019): operator-minded, credible, delivered revenue CAGR of ~11% and steady margin discipline. Communication is forthright on mortgage cyclicality.
- **Capital allocation is the weak link.** Neustar and adjacent acquisitions expanded the revenue base but consumed ~$3B+ at peak multiples and produced a 2023 impairment; ROIC still sits below WACC. This is empire-building of the mild, disciplined-looking variety — the kind that quietly compounds at 8% instead of 15%.
- **Better recent behavior:** leverage down from 4.6x to 2.7x; buybacks restarted and growing ($302M in 2025, $111M in H1'26) executed largely in the $60–85 zone; dividend raised every year ($0.095 → $0.105 → $0.115 → $0.125/qtr, Feb'26).
- **Insider ownership is low (~1%)** — a real demerit under my owner-operator test. Management is well paid in options/PSUs, aligning them with the stock price but not necessarily with per-share intrinsic value.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Mortgage/credit-cycle cyclicality** — origination volumes drive a meaningful slice of U.S. Markets; a rate backup or housing freeze cuts revenue mid-single-digit and the multiple compresses on top of it (see 2022–23: −44% drawdown).
2. **Leverage and refinancing** — $5.66B debt at a ~1.575 beta; if rates rise, interest expense (~$250M) crowds out buybacks.
3. **Regulatory/legislative** — FCRA and CFPB rulemaking, state privacy statutes, and any restriction on permissible-purpose data use are existential-adjacent to the pricing model. Also ongoing data-breach/consumer litigation exposure.
4. **Moat erosion from alternative data & AI** — lenders building proprietary cash-flow/alternative underwriting models could disintermediate bureau data at the margin; TRU must keep buying capability (M&A risk loop).
5. **Capital allocation repeat** — another large, richly priced acquisition would cement the sub-WACC ROIC problem.
6. **Adjusted-vs-GAAP gap** — ~$575M D&A, much of it acquisition-related; if amortization is genuinely economic (customer relationships decaying), owners should treat more of it as a real cost, which would cut my intrinsic value toward the $54 end.
7. **Concentration in a duopoly-plus peer set** — Equifax/Experian price competition could cap the annual pricing uplift that underpins my growth assumption.
