# FORENSIC SHORT-SELLER ANALYSIS — TRANSUNION (TRU)

**Price $78.23 | Mkt cap $15.0B | Net debt $4.30B | EV ≈ $19.3B | Sector: Financial Data & Exchanges**

Context: the stock peaked near $105 (Nov-2024), bottomed at $61.12 (30-Sep-2026), and has bounced 28% to $78. Everything below is assessed against a business that has been *de-rating while accelerating*. That divergence is the whole story.

Cash flow is genuinely real — $987.6M operating CF, $661.6M FCF on $4.58B revenue (14.5% FCF margin).

**Verdict: this is not an accounting fraud. Anyone shorting TRU on "the earnings are fake" is going to get run over.** The bear case has to be built somewhere else.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**RF-1 — Equity is an accounting construct: tangible book value is deeply negative.**
Goodwill + intangibles = **$8.358B on $11.113B of assets (75%)**. Total equity $4.545B implies **tangible equity of approximately −$3.81B**. There is no asset cushion beneath the equity; the entire book value is the *price paid* for Callcredit/Neustar/Sontiq/Argus et al.

**RF-2 — Impairment risk is demonstrated, not theoretical.**
In 2023 operating income collapsed from $626M to $128.5M and net income swung to **−$206.2M**, with goodwill/intangibles falling $535.6M (impairment in International/UK and marketing). A $1.45B-EBITDA company can lose money in a single year inside this capital structure. Goodwill has barely moved since ($8.40B → $8.36B) despite continued M&A — the Neustar-era layer remains the obvious target if the identity/marketing franchise erodes.

**RF-3 — Gross margin has compressed ~640bp in four years: 65.5% → 62.7% → 60.4% → 60.0% → 59.1% (2021→2025).**
Management attributes this to mix (Neustar, Argus, marketing are structurally lower-margin). Accepting that, the *direction* still matters: TransUnion has spent $6B+ on M&A and its blended pricing power, as measured by gross margin, is going backwards. This is the opposite of a widening moat.

**RF-4 — Returns on invested capital are below the cost of capital, which is *why* the "adjusted EPS" narrative exists.**
Invested capital ≈ equity $4.545B + debt $5.158B − cash $0.856B = **$8.85B**. 2025 EBIT after tax ≈ $879M × 0.76 = $668M → **ROIC ≈ 7.5%**. Even a generous cash-based measure (EBITDA − capex, taxed) gives **~9.7%**. For a company levered 3x and touted as a high-quality data monopoly, that is thin. The $300M+ of annual intangible amortization is not a non-cash fiction — it is the accounting residue of capital that *has not earned its return*.

**RF-5 — The GAAP-to-"adjusted" bridge is unusually wide and widens the longer you look.**
FY2025 GAAP EPS **$2.34** vs consensus "EPS" **$4.26** — a **1.8x gap**. On price of $78.23, you are paying **16.2x adjusted** earnings but **33.4x GAAP**. Embedded in the bridge: acquired-intangible amortization, recurring "restructuring," acquisition costs, and ~$120–130M/yr of stock comp (2024: $121.2M). Restructuring that appears every single year is an operating cost.

**RF-6 — 2026 headline GAAP growth is being flattered by non-operating items.**
Q1 2026: revenue $1.246B, operating income $244.8M — but **net income $397.2M and GAAP EPS $2.06 vs adjusted EPS $1.18**. Net income exceeded operating income by **$152M**. Something outside the operating line (gain/tax benefit) drove ~43% of reported profit. Any bull citing "EPS growth" off that print is being careless.

**RF-7 — History of violent working-capital swings that disguise cash conversion.**
2022: operating cash flow collapsed to **$297.2M** against $267.3M net income on a **$511.2M** working-capital outflow and FCF of **−$1M**. 2025 shows a further **−$217.6M** WC drag. Cash conversion at TRU is not a smooth, reliable 2x — it is lumpy, and the reported "quality" score flatters the years in between.

**RF-8 — Two consecutive years of receivables growing far faster than revenue.**
2023: AR **+20.1%** vs revenue **+3.3%**. 2024: AR **+22.9%** vs revenue **+9.2%**. Both flagged red. This pattern coincided exactly with the 2023 revenue slowdown — the classic signature of pulling forward recognition to defend a growth number. Credit where due: **2025 normalized** (AR +1.9% vs revenue +9.4%), so the bleeding stopped. But the prior two years were not benign.

**RF-9 — Capitalization of software development is material and flatters both EBITDA and operating income.**
Capex of $326M is **7.1% of revenue**, heavily internally-developed software. Expensing it fully would compress adjusted EPS meaningfully. Industry-standard, but it means "FCF conversion" is not free of accounting choice.

**RF-10 — Shareholder returns ahead of deleveraging.**
In 2025 TRU paid $90.5M of dividends and repurchased **$302M** of stock while carrying $5.16B gross debt at 3.55x gross EBITDA. Buybacks at $85–$100 (the 2025 range) have destroyed value versus the $61–$70 prices available in 2026. EBIT/interest cover is 3.7x — adequate, not comfortable.

**RF-11 — Altman Z of 1.52 "distress" — correctly ignored.**
This is mechanical: x4 uses book equity and the score punishes goodwill-heavy, levered balance sheets. For an asset-light subscription business with 90% recurring revenue, Z is not diagnostic. I flag it only because it is the one metric a lazy bear will cite, and it will lose the argument.

## 3. Beneish & Accruals Detail

The eight Beneish indices are almost uniformly benign: DSRI 0.931 (receivables *not* outrunning sales), GMI 1.015 (margin slightly worse, minimal), AQI 0.974, SGI 1.094 (moderate growth, below the manipulation-prone 1.3+ zone), DEPI 1.036, SGAI 0.933, LVGI 0.974 (deleveraging), TATA **−0.0479**. 

**No manipulation signal. Four consecutive years of negative accruals. This is a company reporting conservatively relative to cash.** Any short thesis must concede this up front or lose credibility.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment: **The real bear case**

The moat is a regulated three-player oligopoly on US credit data — genuinely durable, legally reinforced, with ~90% recurring revenue. I do not think that asset gets commoditised on a five-year view. The threats are specific:

**A. FHFA / regulatory attack on the tri-merge economics — HIGH and ACTIVE.**
Director Bill Pulte has repeatedly and publicly attacked bureau pricing ("*Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion have been overcharging*"), the MBA has formally requested relief from the requirement to pull all three bureaus when a score is above 700, FHFA has opened a **comprehensive review of the credit reporting agencies**, and the industry is now **operationalizing a bi-merge transition**. The stock's two worst days map directly to this: **−10.6% on 2-Oct-2025** (16.3M shares) and **−6% on 4-Sep-2026** (the FHFA review), with a subsequent slide to $61.

This is the one scenario that turns TRU into a genuine short. A two-bureau or score-based-exception regime strikes at the highest-margin, highest-incremental-margin revenue in the company. Mortgage-linked revenue is roughly 10% of total but a much larger share of profit sensitivity, and — critically — **fee compression in mortgage tends to leak into pricing benchmarks across the whole US Markets segment.**

**B. Alternative scores and data — MODERATE.** FHFA's push to expand alternative scoring (VantageScore) attacks the adjacent FICO toll-booth more directly than the bureaus, but it establishes the principle that the data layer is separable from the score layer, which is where TransUnion's analytics margin lives.

**C. AI disruption of the analytics/identity layer — MODERATE, and this is what the multiple is pricing.** Neustar-derived marketing, identity resolution and fraud analytics are precisely the businesses an LLM-plus-alternative-data stack can attack. These are lower-margin, growth-challenged assets carrying a large slice of the $8.4B goodwill. This, not the credit file, is the impairment candidate.

**D. Cyclicality, not erosion.** Credit bureau volumes are levered to mortgage originations and unsecured lending. The 2026 acceleration to +14.9% is a *recovery off a depressed base*, not a new secular trend. Buying/shorting bureaus at cycle peaks/troughs on growth rates is how people lose money.

One confirmed sale: Steven Chaouki, 1,000 shares at $70 on 15-Oct-2026. **No meaningful open-market insider buying occurred anywhere between $105 and $61.** For a management team that publicly trumpets 10 consecutive quarters of high-single-digit organic growth, the absence of a single conviction purchase at a multi-year low is a tell. *Caveat: I could not resolve transaction direction (buy vs. sell) for the bulk of the Q3-2026 filings from available metadata — this is an honest data gap, not a clean finding.*

## 6. The Bull Case (steelmanned — and it's strong)

1. **The accounting is clean.** Beneish -2.66, negative accruals four years running, F-Score 8, OCF/NI 2.17x. There is no fraud.
2. **Growth is organic, not bought.** Ten consecutive quarters of ≥ high-single-digit *organic* growth; FY26 guidance **raised to 12–13% revenue growth** (~$5.15B); Q2-26 revenue $1.31B (+14.9%), adjusted EPS $1.23 vs $1.15 expected. This directly refutes the "M&A mask" bear point.
3. **It's cheap by its own history.** 11.4x FY26E EV/EBITDA and 16.2x adjusted EPS, for a regulated oligopolist with ~90% recurring revenue and 14.5% FCF margin. Five-year average multiples are far higher.
4. **Deleveraging is working.** Net debt $5.22B (2022) → $4.53B (2024) → **$4.30B (2025)**, with 2025 FCF of $662M covering interest 2.8x over.
5. **The de-rating already happened.** Down 26% from the high; the FHFA headline risk has been partly discounted, and the bi-merge rule may land as an exception-based compromise that preserves most tri-merge volume.

## 7. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, but it is a *regulatory de-rating* short, not a forensic short.** The bear case in one line: *a levered, goodwill-financed roll-up earning ~7.5% ROIC that must add back $300M+ of amortization to look profitable, now facing an ideologically hostile FHFA that is actively constructing a two-bureau mortgage regime, with a stock that still trades at 11.4x EBITDA.*

**Downside math (honest version):** If FHFA finalizes an exception-based or two-bureau rule and mortgage-channel pricing compresses 20–30%, that's ~10–15% of EPS. Apply a de-rated 11–12x to a haircut adjusted EPS of ~$4.30–4.40 → **$48–53, i.e., 32–39% downside.** Add a Neustar-layer impairment as a catalyst.
**Upside math:** Rule lands as a narrow compromise, organic growth compounds at 10%+, multiple re-rates to 15x adjusted → **$90–100, i.e., 15–28% upside.**

That is roughly 35% down vs 25% up — asymmetric, but only *conditionally*. The condition is a regulatory outcome nobody can underwrite today.

**What kills this short:** you are shorting a business with negative accruals, 2.2x cash conversion, and accelerating organic growth, at a multiple that is already 30% below its own history. That is a bad combination. Shorts built on "levered + goodwill + adjusted EPS" have been losing to credit bureaus for a decade because the cash flow is real and the oligopoly is legally entrenched.
