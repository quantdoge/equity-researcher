## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — "reflation at the long end," tilting risk-off for rate-sensitive, levered cyclicals.**

The US economy entering Q4 2026 is characterised by *decent real growth, full employment, and re-accelerating inflation against a repricing long end of the curve*. This is a materially different regime from the disinflationary easing cycle of 2024–25, and it is close to the worst possible mix for TransUnion's business model.

- **Growth is positive but decelerating.** Real GDP rose at a **+2.2% annualised** pace in Q2 2026 ($24,408.0B chained 2017$ vs $24,274.4B in Q1), down from +3.9% SAAR in Q3 2025. YoY growth is **+2.2%**. This is a soft-landing growth path, not a contraction — supportive of credit *demand*.
- **Labour market is tight and stable.** Unemployment **4.2%** in Sept 2026, improved from the 4.5% cycle high in Nov 2025. No labour-market crack — a positive for consumer credit quality and lender appetite.
- **Inflation has stopped falling and is rising again.** CPI **+3.72% YoY in Sept 2026** (336.298 vs 324.245), versus a **+2.39% trough in Jan 2026**. The 3-month annualised rate is **~4.6%** and the Sept m/m print was **+0.65%** — unambiguously hot. (Data gap: core PCE/CPI not available in this pull; the PCE series returned is nominal PCE dollars, +6.1% YoY, not a price index.)
- **The easing cycle is over.** Fed funds averaged **3.75% in Sept 2026**, having been cut from 5.33% (Aug 2024) to **3.63% by Aug 2026**, then nudged **up 12bp**. With CPI at 3.72%, the **real policy rate is ~0%** — the Fed has essentially no room to ease into this inflation backdrop. The next move is more likely up than down.
- **The long end is repricing.** 2Y **4.65%**, 5Y **4.80%**, 10Y **4.99%**, 30Y **5.37%**, 2s10s **+34bp** — a **bear steepener**. This is term-premium/inflation-expectation driven, not growth driven, and it is the most damaging shape of the curve for long-duration equities and levered balance sheets.
- **Credit is still cheap but widening at the margin.** HY OAS **3.29%** (19 Oct) — historically very tight, but **+63bp from the 2.66% low on 21 Sept**. Credit conditions remain easy (supportive of TRU's lender customers) while the *direction of travel* has turned adverse.
- **Dollar firming.** Broad USD index **121.40** (16 Oct) vs a **117.88** low on 9 Sept — **+3.0% in five weeks**.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

TransUnion's revenue is a **volume toll on consumer credit activity** (per-inquiry/per-unit pricing) plus a subscription-like analytics layer. Its macro sensitivity therefore runs through four channels, in order of importance:

### (a) Interest rates → mortgage origination volumes — **the dominant channel**
The 30Y mortgage rate at **7.40%**, up **142bp** from the February 2026 trough of 5.98% and **+40bp in just the last three weeks** (7.03% → 7.40% between 24 Sept and 15 Oct), is unambiguously negative. Mortgage is TRU's most rate-elastic line item: refinance activity is a step-function of the primary mortgage rate, and purchase volumes are highly payment-sensitive. With 10Y at 4.99% and the 30Y at 5.37%, the mortgage–Treasury spread is also wide, meaning **mortgage rates will not fall materially even if the 10Y rallies**. Expect the US Markets mortgage line to be a Q4'26/Q1'27 drag and a downside risk to the $5.13–5.16B FY26 revenue guidance midpoint.

**Offsetting this:** the rest of TRU's US Financial Services book (card, personal loan, auto) is driven by *lender willingness to lend* plus employment, not by the long end. With unemployment at 4.2% and HY OAS at 3.29%, credit is available and consumer balance sheets are employed — which is why ~11–12% revenue growth guidance remains credible despite the mortgage headwind. **The macro picture is bifurcated for TRU: short-rate/labour-sensitive lines are fine; long-rate-sensitive lines are deteriorating.**

### (b) Rates → valuation (discount rate / duration)
TRU is a long-duration asset: with revenue compounding at low-double-digits, roughly **60%+ of DCF value sits beyond year five**. A 100bp rise in the discount rate costs roughly **8–10% of intrinsic value** for a name with this duration profile. Beta of **1.58** confirms high sensitivity to risk-appetite and rate shocks.

Importantly, much of the damage is *already in the price*: the stock is at **$78.23**, down **~10.6%** from its 22 Dec 2025 high of $87.53 and roughly flat-to-down over twelve months despite double-digit revenue growth — i.e. **the multiple, not the earnings, has absorbed the rate shock**. On an estimated ~$4.20–4.40 FY26 adjusted EPS (company-disclosed Q2'26 adj EPS $1.23; my estimate, not tool-verified), TRU trades near **18x** — a de-rated multiple that offers less discount-rate downside than a year ago.

### (c) Rates → refinancing cost on the debt stack
TRU carries roughly **$5.3B gross debt at ~3.0x net leverage** (company disclosure, not re-verified here), predominantly fixed-rate senior notes laddered through 2031. Fixed-rate structure means **no near-term refinancing cliff**, but 2027–2029 maturities must be refinanced into a 5%+ 10Y environment, structurally raising interest expense versus the 2021-vintage coupons. This is a slow-burn EPS headwind, not a solvency issue. **The widening HY spread (+63bp in a month) is the variable to watch** — a genuine credit-spread event, not a rate move, is what would make leverage topical.

### (d) Currency → International segment (≈25–28% of revenue)
The broad dollar is **+3.0% in five weeks** and **+2.9% since early May**. With roughly a quarter of revenue outside the US — including meaningful emerging-market exposure in India, Brazil, Colombia and South Africa — a 3% USD move is worth roughly **0.7–0.9 points of consolidated revenue growth** as a translation headwind. Moderate, but it lands directly on the segment that has been TRU's fastest-growing and highest-margin growth engine, and it compounds the optics issue if Q4 guidance needs trimming.

### (e) Inflation → cost structure and pricing power
**Mixed, net modestly negative.** TransUnion is people-light relative to revenue (13,500 employees on a ~$5.2B revenue base) and has demonstrated pricing power — it raises per-unit prices annually, which is a genuine inflation pass-through mechanism and historically a *positive* in inflationary periods. The negative is wage inflation in its India/tech delivery centres (INR-linked cost base against USD revenue — actually a partial natural hedge) and higher cost of capital.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE** (mild)

The macro backdrop is a **headwind, not a crisis**. Growth is positive, unemployment is 4.2%, credit spreads are near cycle tights, and TRU's non-mortgage, non-rate-sensitive revenue (~70%+ of the business) is growing double digits. But every *marginal* macro impulse right now points the wrong way for this specific company:

1. **The single most rate-elastic revenue line is being crushed** — mortgage rates up 142bp since February to 7.40%.
2. **The Fed has no room to help.** With CPI at 3.72% and real fed funds at 0%, the easing cycle that would normally amplify a housing turnaround has ended and may have reversed.
3. **The long end is repricing upward** — the worst possible yield-curve shape for a levered, long-duration, 1.58-beta equity.
4. **The dollar is turning against the fastest-growing segment.**

I stop short of a stronger negative rating for two reasons: the 18x multiple has already absorbed a large portion of the rate shock, and the majority of revenue is volume-linked to a healthy, fully-employed consumer with access to credit. **Rating would upgrade to Neutral if** the 10Y falls back below 4.5% and mortgage rates below 6.75%; **it would downgrade to "strongly unfavourable" if** the Fed hikes and 10Y breaks 5.25%.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario That Matters: **A Restarted Fed Hiking Cycle (Bear)**

**The consensus assumption embedded in TRU's multiple and in credit spreads at 3.29% is that the Sept CPI re-acceleration is a one-off and the Fed's next move is a cut.** The data do not support that complacency: CPI has risen from 2.39% to 3.72% in eight months, the 3-month annualised rate is ~4.6%, and the real policy rate is 0%.

**The surprise:** Q4 2026 CPI prints 4.0%+, the Fed hikes to 4.00–4.25% by Q1 2027, the 10Y breaks 5.50%, and the 30Y mortgage rate goes to **7.9–8.1%**. The transmission into TRU is brutal and *multiplicative*, not additive:
- US mortgage/refi volumes fall a further 25–30%, converting TRU's fastest-recovering US line into a hard negative.
- The discount-rate shock compresses a long-duration multiple from ~18x to ~15x.
- HY spreads widen from 3.29% toward 4.50%, reopening the refinancing question on 2027–2029 maturities.
- The dollar rallies another 3–5% on rate differentials, deepening the International translation drag.

**Simultaneous downside: ~25–30% (to roughly $55–60), even with EPS holding.** Note the stock has *already* demonstrated this reflexivity — it fell from $84.92 on 3 Sept 2026 to $61.12 on 30 Sept (−28% in four weeks) on a combination of rate angst and FHFA Director Pulte's public criticism of mortgage credit-reporting costs and industry structure. That is a live, non-macro policy overhang sitting on top of the macro risk.

**Symmetric bull surprise (worth flagging):** if the Sept CPI spike proves transitory, CPI returns to 2.5% by Q1 2027, the Fed cuts to 3.00%, the 10Y falls to 4.2% and mortgages to ~6.3% — a refi wave plus multiple re-rating to 22x would take TRU to **$95–105 (+25–35%)**. The distribution around this stock is wide and macro-convex in both directions.
