## TransUnion (TRU) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $78.23 | Market cap ~$15.0B | EV ~$19.7B | Sector: Financial Data & Exchanges*

### 1. Growth Story
TransUnion is not a "credit bureau" story anymore — it is a decisioning-data platform. The credit report is a mature, oligopolistic cash cow (~high-teens margins, tariff-like pricing) that funds a land-grab in **Emerging Verticals** (insurance, public sector, media, tenant/employment), identity/fraud, and International, all being re-platformed onto the **OneTru** cloud-native architecture. FY26 organic constant-currency growth accelerated to **8–9%** (from 3.3% in the 2023 mortgage trough) with double-digit EPS growth, yet the stock trades at roughly **half the EV/EBITDA of EFX, VRSK and FICO** because Washington is now questioning credit-report pricing. That gap is the entire investment case: is the discount cyclical/regulatory noise, or a permanent haircut on the moat?

### 3. TAM and Penetration
Core U.S. credit reporting (~$12–15B) is a **~90% penetrated three-player oligopoly** — no growth there beyond price and mortgage cyclicality. The actual TAM is the ~$50B+ global market for risk decisioning, identity verification and marketing analytics, where TRU's share is still low single digits and where data-network effects (contributory bureau data + Neustar identity graph + OneTru) are genuinely proprietary. Penetration math: Emerging Verticals and International are growing double digits off a base of roughly a third of revenue; if they sustain low-double-digit growth, company organic growth holds 7–9% through 2028 even with a flat U.S. mortgage market.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts
- **Q3 2026 results — Oct 27, 2026 (imminent).** The single largest near-term event: 7/7 beat cadence vs a stock that fell ~25% in a month. A beat-and-raise likely closes the gap toward $90.
- **FHFA / administration posture on credit-report pricing** (open-ended) — any formal rulemaking, or its abandonment, is the re-rating trigger for the whole bureau group.
- **OneTru platform completion (2026–27)** — product velocity plus cost savings; watch adjusted EBITDA margin guide moving from +10–11% toward mid-teens.
- **$1B buyback + deleveraging** — net debt ~$4.7B (~2.6x adj EBITDA); sub-2.5x would free capital for accelerated repurchase into a 16x P/E.
- **CFO transition** (disclosed late 2026) — governance overhang to clear.

### 5. Alternative Data Signals
- **Google Trends (US, 12m):** interest 71 → 68, flat to softening; the March 2026 spike to 100 looks event-driven (legal/regulatory headlines), **not demand**. Neutral — no evidence of consumer-credit demand acceleration.
- **Hiring:** ~177 open roles, only a handful in sales/support — consistent with cost discipline and platform consolidation rather than aggressive growth investment. Mildly negative for near-term organic acceleration.
- **Price/volume:** capitulation volume 3–5M shares (vs ~2–3M normal) on the Sept 29–Oct 1 flush to $61, then a **+7% day on Oct 15 and a breakout to $78.23 on above-average volume Oct 20**. Tape says the sellers are done; accumulation has started.
- **Consumer-side:** class-action headline risk around sale of reports to a debt-management firm — small dollars, reputational noise.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (not Strong Buy)
Durable high-single/double-digit organic growth, 100% beat rate, raised guidance, and a valuation at **~16x 2026E adjusted EPS (~$4.80) and ~10.8x 2026E adj EBITDA** against EFX at 33x/14.4x, VRSK at 29x/19.8x, and FICO at 30x/23.7x. Analyst FY30 model implies only ~5.6% revenue CAGR — a low bar. Base case 12 months: **$95–100** (20x 2027E EPS of $5.47); bull $115; bear $68 if FHFA pricing action becomes real.

I stop short of Strong Buy for three reasons: the **GAAP Rule of 40 of 23.8** shows the levered entity still under-earns its growth; the comp discount may be **structural rather than cyclical** (leverage + political exposure + slower growth than FICO); and the FHFA pricing review is an unquantifiable threat to the core franchise's pricing power — the exact thing that justifies a bureau multiple.

### 7. Key Risks
- **Regulatory/political (highest):** FHFA and administration scrutiny of credit-report pricing tied to housing affordability; CFPB/medical-debt rule history; possible GSE scoring changes (VantageScore vs FICO) that could disintermediate bureau economics.
- **Consumer credit cycle / mortgage volumes:** U.S. Financial Services is the growth engine and the most cyclical piece; a consumer credit downturn hits both volume and mix.
- **Leverage & rates:** ~$4.7B net debt, $236M annual interest — limits buyback flexibility and amplifies EPS sensitivity.
- **Competition:** Experian and Equifax in the core; alternative-data and direct-to-lender models at the edges; FICO's scoring economics.
- **Execution:** OneTru re-platforming plus Neustar integration, a CFO transition, and a GAAP-to-adjusted gap ($575M D&A) that leaves little room for organic-margin disappointment.
