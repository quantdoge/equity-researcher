# SS&C Technologies Holdings (SSNC) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $83.13 (26 Oct 2026) | Market cap ~$19.5–20.4B | Diluted shares ~234M | Net debt ~$6.9B (credit-agreement basis)*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW**

SS&C is a 40-year-old (founded 1986) mission-critical software and software-enabled services provider to financial services and healthcare. Its core products — fund accounting, transfer agency, alternative fund administration (GlobeOp), retirement recordkeeping (Bluedoor), wealth platforms (Black Diamond), claims adjudication — sit in the operational plumbing of regulated institutions.

**Evidence of a moat:**
- **Switching costs (strong).** Replacing a fund-accounting or transfer-agency platform is an 18–36 month project with audit and regulatory exposure. Revenue is ~90% recurring, and management reports retention in the high-90s. This is the primary and genuine moat source.
- **Intangible assets.** Decades of accumulated regulatory rule engines, tax reporting, and compliance content across ~30+ jurisdictions — expensive to replicate.
- **Efficient scale in niches.** In alternative fund administration SS&C is one of only ~4 credible global providers.
- **No network effects.** This is a feature of the business, not a flaw, but it caps the ceiling.

**Where the moat is narrower than it looks:**
- **ROIC of 9.85% (2025) vs. ~8.5% WACC** — barely clearing its cost of capital on a total-capital basis. A truly wide-moat compounder earns 15–25%. SS&C's returns are flattered only when you strip out the $14.1B of goodwill and intangibles (63% of assets), which is not how an acquirer's capital should be judged.
- **Pricing power is constrained.** Clients are sophisticated asset managers and insurers who run competitive tenders and squeeze vendor fees annually. Organic growth has been just **5.0–5.2%** in 2026 (7.8% reported, the gap being acquired growth).
- **Growth is bought, not earned.** 5-year revenue CAGR is 5.2%; SS&C has acquired 50+ companies. The moat has held roughly **stable** over five years — neither meaningfully widened nor eroded.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples (price $83.13):** 26.4x GAAP EPS · **11.6x FY2026E adjusted EPS ($7.14)** · ~10.3x EV/adjusted EBITDA (~$27.3B EV / $2.65B) · **~11x P/FCF** · 1.4% dividend yield · 7.9% trailing FCF yield.

**DCF (owner-earnings basis).** Base FCF $1.52B (FY2026E ~$1.8B less ~$250M SBC), 10 years, 234M shares, net debt $6.9B:

| Scenario | FCF growth | WACC | Terminal | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear (organic stalls, M&A value-destructive) | 3% | 9.0% | 2.0% | **$63** |
| Base (5.5%: 5% organic + margin) | 5.5% | 8.5% | 2.5% | **$107** |
| Bull (7% incl. accretive M&A) | 7% | 8.0% | 2.5% | **$147** |

Probability-weighted intrinsic value: **~$106/share**. At $83.13 the shares offer a **~22% discount** — inside, but at the low end of, my 20–30% margin-of-safety band.

**Cross-checks:** At 11.6x forward adjusted earnings and 11x FCF, the market is pricing in essentially **zero real growth** — roughly 2–3% perpetual FCF growth. That is too pessimistic for a business compounding organic revenue at 5% with expanding margins and 15%+ annual adjusted EPS growth. Peer software trades at a median 43.8x P/E (an unreliable comparison — SS&C is a levered services hybrid, not a high-growth SaaS).

**Important caveat:** the stock has run **+34% off its June low of $61.83** and sits 7% below its October high of $89.54. The easy money in this name was made four months ago.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good, not outstanding**

- **Founder-led.** William C. Stone founded SS&C in 1986 and remains Chairman & CEO — a genuine owner-operator with ~40 years of domain history and a substantial (historically high-single-digit to low-double-digit percentage) equity stake, though he has monetized shares over the years. *Note: precise current insider ownership was not verifiable within this analysis — treat as approximate.*
- **Capital allocation is disciplined on the return-of-capital side, mixed on M&A.** FY2025 buybacks of $1.04B; Q2 2026 alone repurchased a record 6.4M shares for $435M (~$68 average) — genuinely value-accretive. Net leverage has fallen from ~3.9x (2023) to 2.63x. Dividend raised every year since inception, now $0.30/qtr ($1.20 annualized, ~17% of adjusted EPS) — a conservatively covered payout.
- **The blemish is acquisition discipline.** Blue Prism ($1.6B, 2022) was bought at the peak of the RPA narrative and has not earned its price; the DomaniRx healthcare JV was exited. SS&C has a long habit of paying up for growth when organic growth slows. This is the single largest drag on the quality score and the reason I don't award a wide moat.
- **Communication** is consistent and specific (organic growth, adjusted EBITDA, net leverage all disclosed quarterly) — better than most.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **BUY** *(moderate conviction; accumulate below $75)*

**The thesis in one line:** A narrow-moat, mission-critical financial infrastructure business converting ~28% of revenue into free cash flow, available at ~11x forward earnings and ~11x FCF against an intrinsic value near $106 — a ~22% discount that compensates for the leverage and the mediocre acquisition record.

**What makes it a Buy and not a Strong Buy:** ROIC of 9.9% only marginally exceeds the cost of capital; organic growth is 5%, not 10%; and the margin of safety is 22%, not the 30% I want for a business with this much goodwill and debt. The bear case at $63 is real and uncomfortably close.

**What would make me sell:** price above ~$105 (intrinsic value), net leverage rising above 3.0x, organic growth falling below 3%, or evidence that agentic AI is commoditizing back-office processing rather than expanding SS&C's addressable market.

**Position sizing:** initiate at 2–3% of portfolio; add toward 5% below $75, which would restore a ~30% margin of safety.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI disintermediation (highest severity, longest horizon).** SS&C's pricing rests on labor-arbitrage plus software. If agentic AI lets clients — or leaner competitors — automate fund accounting and claims adjudication at a fraction of the cost, the 5% organic growth assumption and the switching-cost moat both erode. Management is positioning AI as an opportunity, but this is unproven.
2. **Leverage and refinancing.** $7.65B of debt at ~2.6x net leverage. A rising-rate refinancing of the term loans would directly reduce the FCF available to equity; $426M of 2025 interest expense is a full 24% of EBITDA.
3. **Acquisition dependence.** With goodwill at 63% of assets, sustained growth requires continued M&A. Each deal must clear a WACC that the existing business only barely clears. A large value-destructive deal (a second Blue Prism) would permanently impair intrinsic value.
4. **Cyclical AUM linkage.** A meaningful slice of revenue is tied to client assets under administration and fund counts. A severe equity drawdown shrinks both fees and the client base in the alternatives business.
5. **Client and competitive pressure.** Citco, State Street, BNY, Broadridge, FIS and Fiserv all compete directly; consolidation among asset managers shrinks SS&C's customer count and increases fee pressure.
6. **Earnings-quality skepticism.** Persistent gap between $7.14 adjusted and ~$3.60 GAAP EPS means reported profitability is heavily non-GAAP. If amortization of acquired intangibles is a genuine recurring cost of the business model (it is), the "true" P/E is closer to 23x than 12x — a much less compelling headline.
7. **Post-rally entry risk.** After a 34% four-month move, near-term multiple compression is plausible even if intrinsic value is unchanged; the October 23 drop of 7% on soft Q4 guidance shows how quickly sentiment shifts.
