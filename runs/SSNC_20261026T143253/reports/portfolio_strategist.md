### 2. Consensus Assessment: **MEDIUM** (bullish fundamentals, cautious risk stack — a *price-anchored* consensus)

**Consensus (3+ agents independent):**
1. **Genuine business quality** — 90% recurring revenue, ~48% gross margin *stable*, high switching costs, top-2 in the fastest-growing niche. Sector, Value, Growth, Short, ESG all agree. **No agent alleges fraud or accounting fabrication** — Short explicitly rejects the short thesis.
2. **Cheap on cash flow** — 11.9x EV/EBITDA, 12.6x P/FCF, 7% economic FCF yield, −40% vs peer P/E median. Value + Growth + Quant (value score 68) + Macro all confirm.
3. **Organic growth ≥5% is the single load-bearing variable** — flagged by Sector, Value, Growth, Short, Quant, Alt Data independently. It is the one thing that, if it breaks, breaks everything.

**Contested — and I rule on it:**
- **Leverage: 1.9x vs 3.2–3.4x.** I verified this from the FY24→FY25 balance sheet. **Fin Risk wins.** Cash rose **+$3,006M** while net receivables fell **−$2,392M** and total current assets rose only **$625M**. The $3.57bn "cash" is client/segregated float reclassified out of receivables, not corporate liquidity. Net debt is **$6.4–7.1bn**, leverage **~2.6–3.2x**. The naive 1.9x screen that underwrote the Short Analyst's "no balance-sheet accident" and Growth's "~1.5x" is an artifact. **This is my single most important override.**
- **GAAP vs adjusted.** Short is right that 5-yr GAAP net income is flat ($800M→$797M) and EPS growth is buyback-manufactured. I underwrite on **economic FCF (~$1.46bn after the unrecorded ~$200M SBC)**, not on $7.14 adjusted EPS. That yields ~7% FCF yield — still attractive, but not the 9% advertised.
- **Analyst targets.** Median ~$100–101 (+20%), but the **most recent print (Morgan Stanley, 26-Oct) is $85 — only +2%**. The target dispersion ($85–$104) is itself the thesis: value plus a de-rating ceiling.

**Divergence:** The bull case is *valuation + cash flow*; the bear case is *leverage + organic deceleration + AI disintermediation*. These are not reconcilable by more analysis — they resolve on the **4-Feb-27 Q4 print and the 2027 refinancing**. That argues for sizing, not passing.
