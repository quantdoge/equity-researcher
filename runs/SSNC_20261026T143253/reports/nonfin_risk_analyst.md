## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE** (structural concentration offsets otherwise reasonable practice)

**Evidence supporting:**
- **Single share class, no dual-class structure.** Founder control is economic, not charter-embedded — a meaningful positive versus peer founder-controlled software (no super-voting lock-in).
- **Founder alignment is extreme and positive:** Stone holds ~31.8M shares directly (~13.6% of ~235M shares out) alongside a $25.0M annual package. He is a 10% owner and bears real downside.
- Director equity is substantive, not token: Varsano 89,004 shares; Zamkow 18,404; Walton 7,600; Vanni d'Archirafi 3,804. Annual 3,200-RSU grants vesting at the AGM show a conventional, non-entrenched director comp design.

**Evidence against / watch items:**
- **Combined Chairman & CEO for 40 years** with no disclosed public succession plan. This is the single largest governance structural weakness: board chair, CEO, and founder are the same person, and the Vice Chair (Boulanger, President/COO) is an executive, not an independent lead director.
- **Related-party overlap:** proxy discloses Stone "has an economic interest in SILAC and is a member of its board" — an insurance-related affiliate relationship requiring ongoing audit-committee scrutiny.
- **Pay quantum ($25M)** for a ~$19.5B market cap company with mid-single-digit organic growth is generous and skews the pay-for-performance read.
- Independence math is only partially verifiable at this pass (Conjeevaram, Varsano, Zamkow, Walton, Vanni d'Archirafi appear independent; board appears ~8–9 seats). I could not confirm a fully independent audit/comp/risk committee from primary text.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Key-person concentration in a 40-year founder-CEO.** Strategy (100+ acquisitions, capital allocation, buyback/dividend/debt pacing) is personally identified with Stone. No public succession framework, and the announced "AI in production" pivot (WorkHQ, AI Gateway) further centralizes narrative risk in one individual. A health or reputational event at the CEO level would be a repricing event, not a governance footnote.
2. **Serial-M&A integration and goodwill/impairment risk.** The model is roll-up: >100 acquisitions, including Blue Prism at ~$1.6B (2022) and Battea at ~$670M (2024), funded with debt and cash. This builds a large goodwill/intangible base, ~$5B-class debt, and repeated data-center/technology-stack consolidation. Each deal adds client-migration, attrition, and controls-integration risk; the RPA asset is also directly in the path of agentic-AI substitution.
3. **Third-party operational concentration as a systemically-important outsourcer.** SS&C runs fund accounting, transfer agency, claims adjudication and trade processing for thousands of institutions. A service outage, migration failure, or data incident at a shared platform is simultaneously a client-loss event, a regulatory event across multiple jurisdictions, and a litigation event. No confirmed breach was identified in this pass, but the *absence* of a disclosed incident is a weak assurance for a firm holding this density of client data — and post-DORA/Reg S-P notification clocks shrink recovery windows.

*Secondary:* persistent employee-morale strain from repeated acquisition-driven layoffs (Glassdoor/Indeed/Comparably commentary: "layoffs are common," "heavily outsourced," post-acquisition cuts). In a labor-intensive fund-administration model, attrition is a direct service-quality and error-rate driver.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Moderate, contained**

- **No active consumer-facing ESG or product-safety controversy.** SSNC sells to institutions; it lacks the brand-boycott surface of consumer tech. This lowers baseline reputational beta.
- **Primary exposure is counterparty/legal, not consumer:** a decade-long, bare-knuckle data-licensing fight with BNY Mellon (including a Canadian spoliation ruling) and antitrust counterclaims from D.E. Shaw. These read as "tough counterparty" rather than "bad actor," but they are visible in the buy-side community SS&C sells to.
- **Employer brand is the weakest reputational vector:** a consistent public drumbeat of layoff and morale complaints. In a business whose product *is* people running client processes, this is a slow-burn reputational and operational issue rather than a headline risk.
- **Positive offsets:** management has been consistently affirmative on renewals and cash generation ($1.216B operating cash flow through nine months of 2026; raised FY26 guidance Oct 22, 2026), which suppresses short-term trust-deficit narratives.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

SSNC screens as a **structurally concentrated but transparently governed** business. There is no fraud signal, no restatement, no accounting irregularity, no insider-selling cluster, and no located enforcement action — the four things that typically force a High/Critical rating. What holds it at Moderate rather than Low is the combination of (a) genuine one-person strategic dependency, (b) a debt-funded roll-up model with a large intangible base, and (c) systemic-importance outsourcing exposure where a single operational failure has multi-jurisdiction regulatory and contractual consequences.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Succession vacuum at Chairman/CEO/Founder.** No disclosed plan for a 40-year incumbent who is also the control shareholder and the public face of the AI strategy. *Escalate to: board engagement / proxy-vote risk.*
2. **SEC clearing-agency exemption withdrawal (SSCNet, Aug 2025).** Unexplained strategic retreat from a regulated activity perimeter. *Escalate to: request management explanation; assess revenue and regulatory-perimeter impact.*
3. **Litigation outcome risk in data licensing.** SS&C won liability against BNY Mellon but recovered ~1.5% of claimed damages; D.E. Shaw's antitrust theory was dismissed on timeliness/immunity, not on the merits of the licensing model. *Escalate to: monitor appeal; the licensing-moat narrative bears scrutiny.*
4. **Goodwill and RPA-asset impairment sensitivity** in a rising-rate, AI-substitution environment, on a leveraged balance sheet. *Escalate to: annual impairment testing disclosure review.*
5. **Zero insider open-market buying** into a self-described cheap valuation, with two directors trimming. *Escalate to: low priority — monitor only.*
