## Forensic Short-Seller Analysis — SS&C Technologies (SSNC)
**Price $83.09 | Market cap $19.50B | EV ≈ $23.6B | Q3'26 LTM GAAP EBITDA $2,238M**

---

### 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **MEDIUM** (not Low, not High)

The headline cash metrics look superb, and I want to be explicit that they are *not* an accounting mirage in the usual sense:

| Metric | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accruals ratio | −0.029 | −0.034 | −0.033 | **−0.046** |
| Op CF / Net income | 1.74x | 2.00x | 1.82x | **2.19x** |
| FCF / Net income | 1.43x | 1.59x | 1.74x | **2.09x** |

Negative accruals in all four years means earnings are *understated* by accounting, not overstated. That is the opposite of a fraud setup. **But the composite quality score is only 54/100**, and the reason matters: the 2.19x cash conversion is not operational excellence — it is the mechanical result of **$704M of D&A against just $81M of capex**. Roughly $620M+ of that D&A is amortization of acquired intangibles. The "cash conversion" is manufactured by acquisition accounting, and the underlying GAAP earnings are simultaneously depressed by it. Both the bear who shouts "flat earnings" and the bull who shouts "2x cash conversion" are describing the same accounting artifact.

### 2. Key Red Flags Found

**Red flag 1 — Five years, zero GAAP earnings growth (the most damning number in the file).**
Net income: FY21 $800.0M → FY22 $650.2M → FY23 $607.1M → FY24 $760.5M → FY25 $796.9M. Revenue rose 24% over the period ($5,051M → $6,272M). Diluted EPS: $2.99 → $3.15, essentially flat across five years. A company that grows revenue 24% and converts none of it to GAAP earnings is telling you that the incremental revenue is being consumed by acquisition amortization, interest, and integration costs.

**Red flag 2 — Interest expense has doubled while coverage has halved.**
Interest: $205.7M (FY21) → $426.3M (FY25). EBIT/interest coverage: 6.0x → **3.3x**. Total debt $7.65B against book equity $6.94B (D/E 1.10). This is the single largest change in the business model over five years and it is structural, not cyclical.

**Red flag 3 — Negative tangible equity of −$7.1B; goodwill + intangibles = 68% of total assets.**
$14.09B of goodwill and intangibles against $20.71B of assets. Any acquisition-integration failure or segment underperformance converts directly into impairment. Note goodwill *rose* $1.35B in FY25 — the roll-up never stops.

**Red flag 4 — The recurring "one-time" cost gap: ~$128M per quarter.**
Q3'26 GAAP EBITDA $546.7M (32.3% margin) versus "Adjusted" EBITDA $674.8M (39.9% margin). The $128M/quarter gap ≈ **$512M/year, 20% of adjusted EBITDA**, comprised of stock comp plus acquisition, integration and restructuring costs. Costs that recur every single year for a decade are operating costs. On a fully-loaded basis the margin is ~32%, not 40%.

**Red flag 5 — EPS stability is being manufactured by share count, not earnings.**
Q3'26 net income $198.9M vs $210.0M prior year = **−5.3%**. Diluted EPS $0.83 vs $0.83 = flat. The only reason EPS held is that diluted shares fell 4.9% (253.1M → 240.6M). Buybacks were $1,036M in FY25 and $737.5M in FY24; total capital returned over four years was ~$3.64B (buybacks $2.72B + dividends $0.92B) for a net ~10% share reduction across five years — a low return on $3.6B because SBC (~$200M/yr) consumed much of it.

**Red flag 6 — Capex collapsed 68% while revenue grew.**
FY23 $251.5M → FY24 $61.4M → FY25 $80.8M. Capex is now 1.3% of revenue versus 4.6% in FY23. Either a genuine shift away from capitalised software (benign) or underinvestment being masked by acquisitions (bearish). I could not resolve this from the filings available — it needs a 10-K PP&E/capitalised-software footnote. **Flagging as unresolved.**

### 3. Beneish M-Score and Accruals Analysis

**M-Score −3.199 → "Low risk," comfortably below the −2.22 warning line. No manipulation signal.**

But I will not accept that at face value, because **DSRI = 0.392**, which implies receivables were cut 61% relative to revenue. That is not a real-world collection event, and it is the artifact of a reporting reclassification:

| | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net receivables | $1,787M | $3,451M | $4,112M | $1,720M |
| Implied DSO | 123 | 229 | 255 | 100 |

A $4.1B receivable balance on $5.9B of revenue (255 DSO) followed by a collapse to $1.7B is **client-funds/receivable reclassification, not channel stuffing and not improved collection**. The FY23 AR jump of +93% against +4.2% revenue growth that triggered my receivables screen was likewise an artifact.

**Normalising DSRI to 1.0 adds +0.56 to the M-score (−3.199 → ≈ −2.64), which still sits below the −2.22 threshold.** So the forensic conclusion survives the adjustment: *there is no evidence of earnings manipulation here.* Separately, TATA of −0.0458 and four consecutive years of negative accruals confirm conservative accounting. The earnings-quality problem at SSNC is **transparency and structure, not fabrication.**

Also note: **Altman Z = 1.133 ("distress") is a false positive.** It uses book equity ($6.94B) against $20.7B of assets; with a $19.5B market cap, net debt/EBITDA is only ~1.9x. But the model is pointing at something real — negative tangible equity and $7.65B of debt — so dismiss the score, not the leverage.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

- **Gross margin is NOT contracting:** 47.6% (FY22) → 48.7% → 48.2% → 48.2% (FY25). Pricing power is intact. This kills the simplest bear argument.
- **GAAP operating margin is expanding:** 21.6% → 22.9% (FY22→FY25).
- **But organic growth is only 5.2%** (Q3'26, company-reported) against 7.8% total revenue growth — meaning ~2.6 points, a third of growth, is bought. SSNC must deploy well over $1B/year in M&A to sustain a high-single-digit top line. That is a treadmill, and it is the structural bear case.
- **AI disintermediation is the live narrative risk.** Fund administration, reconciliation, reporting and TA are labour-arbitrage businesses that LLMs compress. The market has already voted on this twice: **Feb 3, 2026 the stock fell 9.9% in a single session (open $80.63 → $72.67) and ground down to $64.93 by late March** — a ~20% drawdown — before recovering to $89.54 by Oct 22. Note also that **Q3'26 beat on EPS yet the shares fell after hours (−7.4% to $82.96)**. That is a market looking for a reason to de-rate.
- Competitive set is credible and consolidating: Citco, Apex, State Street, plus Broadridge/FIS in the software layer.

### 5. Management Red Flags

- **Serial insider selling, no buys.** Form 4 activity shows a cluster of eight filings on 22 May 2026, plus filings on 18 Feb, 23 Feb, 3 Mar, 25–30 Mar and 18 Aug 2026. Reported transactions include CEO/founder William Stone sales of **$18.9M** and **~$10.2M**, plus small open-market sells (~$200K, 21 May). No offsetting purchases. **Caveat, and it is a real one:** Stone founded SS&C in 1986, holds a multi-billion-dollar stake (~28% at the time of the IPO structure), and sells under what appear to be pre-arranged/10b5-1 plans tied to RSU and PSU vesting. This is diversification by a founder with concentrated exposure, not the insider behaviour that precedes a blow-up. I weight it as a *mild* negative, not a smoking gun.
- Founder-CEO/Chairman duality since 1986 — weak governance structure, but also the source of the capital-allocation discipline that built the franchise.
- **No auditor changes, no restatements, no related-party disclosures surfaced.** I found no evidence of fraud, channel stuffing, or aggressive revenue recognition.
- **Disclosure quality concern:** the adjusted-vs-GAAP framing ($674.8M vs $546.7M) and the receivables reclassification both reduce comparability. Promotional language ("record adjusted consolidated EBITDA," "another quarter of strong results") in a quarter when **GAAP net income declined 5.3%** is the pattern I dislike most here.

### 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, but it is a quality-of-earnings and de-rating thesis, not a fraud or solvency thesis.**

The credible bear case: SSNC is a leveraged roll-up whose GAAP earnings have not compounded in five years. Growth requires ~$1B+/yr of M&A, which capitalises ~$600M/yr of amortization onto a balance sheet that already carries negative $7.1B of tangible equity. Interest expense has doubled to $426M and coverage is down to 3.3x. Organic growth of 5.2% is decelerating and directly exposed to AI-driven labour arbitrage in fund administration. Adjusted EBITDA overstates economic profit by ~20% because "one-time" integration costs recur annually. EPS growth is now purely a function of buybacks against declining net income. If the multiple compresses from ~10x to ~7x EV/EBITDA on any organic-growth scare or credit-spread widening, that is a 30% downside — and the Feb 2026 and Oct 2026 reactions show the market is primed to do exactly that.

**The other side, stated honestly — and it is stronger than most bears admit:** no manipulation signal survives normalisation (M-score −2.64), accruals are negative four years running, gross margin is stable at ~48%, GAAP operating margin is expanding, adjusted EBITDA margins hit a record 39.9%, and the business is genuinely mission-critical and sticky with high switching costs. At **~8.6x adjusted / ~10.5x GAAP LTM EV/EBITDA** with ~$1.66B of FCF, this is not an expensive stock, and the FY26 buyback is now actually shrinking the share count 5% YoY. The balance sheet carries net debt/EBITDA of ~1.9x. **There is no balance-sheet accident here and no accounting fraud.** A short seller has no catalyst to force, only a slow multiple grind to hope for.

### 7. Contrarian Rating: **NO EDGE** (lean Reject Short Thesis)

I cannot get to "Short." The forensic screen comes back clean on the dimensions that actually kill companies — accruals, cash conversion, manipulation score, revenue recognition, margin trend. What is wrong with SSNC is *structural and already widely understood*: the AI-disintermediation narrative has been public since at least February 2026, the leverage is disclosed, and the adjusted-EBITDA add-backs are itemised. Shorting a 10x-EBITDA, 40%-adjusted-margin, 5%-organic-growth franchise with stable gross margins on a thesis everyone already knows is how short sellers lose money.

**I would only turn Short on a confirmed trigger:**
1. Organic growth prints **below 3%** for two consecutive quarters (watch the 5.2% trend line closely — this is the key variable);
2. Capex stays below 1.5% of revenue *and* the 10-K confirms capitalised software is declining — evidence of underinvestment, not efficiency;
3. Interest coverage falls below 3.0x or the $7.65B debt stack is refinanced above ~6.5%;
4. A goodwill impairment or a break-out showing acquisition-adjusted ROIC below WACC — i.e., proof the ~$1B/yr M&A spend destroys value;
5. Adjusted-to-GAAP EBITDA gap widens beyond ~25% of adjusted EBITDA.

**Honest limitations of this analysis:** the 10-K risk-factor text was unavailable (filing fetch returned no document), the FY25 cash-flow statement reports SBC as **zero** versus $203.3M in FY24 — an obvious data gap that understates SBC by ~$200M and therefore flatters FY25 cash flow; the Q3'26 press-release reconciliation was 403-blocked so the $128M adjusted-to-GAAP gap is inferred rather than itemised; and no valid peer multiples were obtainable (the sector tool returned mega-cap semis — the true comps are Broadridge, Fiserv, FIS, State Street and SEI). Treat the valuation conclusion as indicative.
