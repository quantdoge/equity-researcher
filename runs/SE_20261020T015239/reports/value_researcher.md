## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow, and modestly widening**

Three segments: **Shopee** (SEA/LatAm e-commerce), **Garena** (Free Fire gaming), **Monee** (formerly SeaMoney — wallet, BNPL, lending, SeaBank).

**Evidence for the moat:**
- Shopee is the largest e‑commerce platform in Southeast Asia (Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand, Philippines, Malaysia) — genuine two-sided network effects plus in-house logistics (SPX) and captive payments. That is a real, self-reinforcing cost + density advantage.
- **Returns on tangible operating capital are excellent.** Reported ROIC is only 11.7% (2025), but that is diluted by $12.8B of cash, much of it SeaBank regulatory capital. Stripping cash: NOPAT ≈ $1.55B on invested capital of ≈$3.2B → **~45% ROIC on the operating business**. That is the hallmark of a moated franchise.
- Gross margin has risen every year: 39.1% (2021) → 44.7% (2025) → 45.6% (Q2’26). Take-rate expansion at Shopee is the driver — pricing power in practice.
- Licences (digital banking in Indonesia/Philippines) are intangible assets competitors cannot simply buy.

**Evidence against:**
- Garena is hit-driven (Free Fire). Gaming bookings are cyclical and have no durable moat — this is the segment that funds the group and it can fade.
- E-commerce competition is intense and well-capitalised (TikTok Shop, Lazada, Temu; MercadoLibre in Brazil). Shopee’s profitability is only ~3 years old and reversible.
- **Monee’s $9.9B loan book (Q1’26), +71% YoY** — this is not a moat, it is a credit bet. Growth that fast in Indonesian/Brazilian consumer credit is unseasoned; the data advantage from Shopee transactions is real but unproven through a downturn.

**Verdict: Narrow moat** (Wide in SEA e-commerce specifically; none in gaming; unproven in fintech). Widening over 5 years as margins turned positive and take rates rose — but the fintech expansion has added a new, less moat-protected risk layer.

**Strengths:** Revenue growth re‑accelerated for three consecutive years (4.9% → 28.8% → 36.4% → +47%) — rare and impressive at $27B of scale. Balance sheet flipped to **$3.05B net cash** with only $3.3B gross debt. Operating margin swung from –16% to +8% in four years. No dividends, no meaningful buyback.

**Why not higher:**
- **Reported FCF of $4.51B overstates distributable cash.** FY2025 FCF = net income $1.58B + D&A $0.37B + **working capital +$1.11B** + other. A large part of that working-capital benefit is the funding float of Monee/SeaBank (deposits and payables) — that is *financing*, not owner earnings. Against a loan book growing 71%, loan disbursements are a recurring cash *use*. I therefore anchor valuation on owner earnings ($1.44B in FY2025, ~$2.0B FY2026E), not headline FCF. The 8% "FCF yield" on the stale-price snapshot is not trustworthy.

- Accumulated deficit still –$6.6B; the company has not yet earned back its 2021–22 losses.
- Margin expansion **stalled in 2026**: Q2’26 operating margin 8.0% vs. 9.3% a year earlier, with SGA up 96% YoY ($2.61B vs. $1.33B) as Monee provisions and reinvestment scale. Growth is being bought again.

## 4. Management Quality — **Good, with two real blemishes**

- **Forrest Li** (founder, chairman, CEO) controls supervoting shares; founder-led with a long operating horizon — the right structure for a build-out, but limited shareholder recourse. *(Insider ownership % not retrieved by tools; dual-class control is well documented.)*
- **Capital allocation track record is genuinely good on the big call:** burned ~$3.7B of cumulative losses in 2021–22 to buy SEA market share and logistics, then pivoted hard to profitability — operating margin went from –16% to +8% in three years. That pivot showed discipline rather than empire-building.
- Tencent’s full exit (2022) removed a strategic anchor without destabilising the company.
- **Blemishes:** (a) no dividend and essentially no buyback (only $14M repurchased) despite $12.8B of cash — partly justified by SeaBank capital needs and the 71% loan-book growth, but capital return discipline is untested; (b) heavy, persistent stock-based compensation dilutes owners ~2–3%/yr; (c) the pivot *back* toward aggressive credit growth (SGA +96% YoY) is the classic pattern of a founder re‑accelerating into a new, less-understood risk.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD — do not commit new capital at $112**

Sea is a **good business at a fair price**, and Buffett’s rule is explicit: a fair price is not a buy. The franchise quality is real (network effects, 45% returns on operating capital, $3B net cash, margin trajectory), and growth is accelerating impressively. But at 43x trailing / 32x forward earnings for a business whose newest growth engine is a 71%-growth EM consumer loan book, there is **no margin of safety**, and the reported cash flow overstates true owner earnings.

- **Existing holders:** hold; the compounding engine is intact.
- **New money:** pass. Build a position at **≤$85** (~25–30% discount to my $110–115 IV), which is roughly where the stock traded in March–May 2026, or revisit if Monee proves credit resilience through a full cycle while margins re-expand toward 10%+.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Monee credit cycle (the #1 risk).** $9.9B loan book, +71% YoY, in Indonesia/Brazil/Philippines. A 5% higher-than-modelled loss rate ≈ $500M pre-tax ≈ **30% of TTM net income**. The book is unseasoned; Shopee-derived underwriting is an untested advantage.
2. **FCF quality / accounting opacity.** Operating cash flow is flattered by bank deposits and payables. If Monee must be funded with real capital rather than deposits, the "16x P/FCF" story collapses toward 35x+.
3. **Competitive re-escalation.** TikTok Shop, Lazada, Temu in SEA; MercadoLibre in Brazil. Shopee’s 8% operating margin is recent and reversible — 2022 is the precedent.
4. **Garena concentration.** Free Fire is a single-franchise hit; a bookings decline removes the group’s traditional profit engine while investments continue.
5. **Regulatory/FX.** Indonesia is the largest market — social-commerce and import rules, banking regulation; plus IDR/VND/BRL/THB translation risk against USD reporting.
6. **Governance & dilution.** Dual-class founder control, ~2–3%/yr SBC dilution, no capital-return policy.
7. **Valuation risk.** 24.5x EV/EBITDA and 43x earnings leave no room for a growth wobble; the March 2026 single-day –17% reaction to a small EPS miss is a preview of the volatility (beta 1.55).
