### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Indonesian regulatory nationalism becomes an operating constraint (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
Indonesia is Sea's largest market and its most interventionist regulator. **Permendag 19/2026 took effect 8 June 2026**, revoking Permendag 31/2023 and imposing: mandatory NIB trade licensing for all sellers (platforms must *reject* unlicensed sellers, 18-month grace for incumbents, 6-month for new); a hard **US$100 FOB per-unit floor** on cross-border imports; mandated algorithmic re-ranking to **prioritise domestic products in the first row of search results**; price-manipulation monitoring with a **3-working-day KPPU reporting obligation**; Indonesia's first commercial AI-disclosure regime; and a five-tier sanctions ladder up to platform blocking and licence revocation (DFDL, 24 Jun 2026). This is a direct hit to Shopee's 3P cross-border economics and recommendation-engine design. Layered on top: **KPPU** found Shopee in breach of monopoly rules in 2024 (Shopee admitted violating anti-competition rules by de-listing couriers) and its **courier algorithm still favours SPX Express** post-reform, keeping it on the enforcement radar; plus **OJK Reg. 32/2025** raising BNPL compliance thresholds and the phased P2P interest-rate caps that tighten in 2026.
*Assessment:* the direction of travel is "platform as regulated utility." Expect recurring fines and re-engineering costs rather than existential damage — but Indonesia is where a policy tail-risk (e.g., social-commerce restrictions extended to marketplaces) would hurt most.

**Risk 2 — Cross-border trade tightening and the end of de minimis (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
The US **ended the de minimis exemption globally on 29 August 2025**, and the SEA bloc has mirrored it: Indonesia's $100 floor, Vietnam's removal of the low-value import VAT exemption, Malaysia/Philippines low-value goods taxes. Brazil — Sea's second engine — has whipsawed: a **20% import duty on parcels up to $50**, with a May 2026 proposal to scrap that tax entirely (Valor, 8 May 2026). Net effect is margin compression on cross-border SKUs and a mix shift toward local sellers, which is strategically fine for Shopee (higher take rates, better delivery density) but degrades near-term gross merchandise economics and increases compliance cost per seller.

**Risk 3 — The global gaming/child-safety regulatory wave hits Garena's highest-margin segment (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium-High)**
Garena is only ~11% of revenue but historically the majority of group profitability, so regulatory damage here is disproportionately value-relevant. Three live fronts: (a) **Brazil** — the Digital Child and Adolescent Act (2025) prohibits paid loot boxes in games accessible to minors, and on **9 June 2026 a Brazilian court issued its first ruling imposing liability for making loot boxes available to minors**, fining Apple, Google, Sony, Nintendo and game makers **R$298M**; the Justice Ministry has separately asked the data regulator to investigate six games with loot-box mechanics (Rio Times, Lexology). Free Fire is a top-tier title in Brazil — direct read-across risk, including class-action and civil-public-action exposure. (b) **Vietnam Decree 147/2024** introduced a 16+ rating tier, expanded playtime limits and **real-name authentication** for all games, domestic and foreign. (c) **India** — Free Fire has been banned under IT Act §69A since February 2022, and as of May 2026 **Free Fire India still has no confirmed launch date** despite a 2023 announcement; Garena is instead developing India-themed titles locally.
*Secondary watch item:* the **Thailand–Cambodia border conflict** (2025: 150+ deaths, ~1M displaced; Thai economy slowed by tariffs plus the dispute) is a live ASEAN conflict risk affecting Thai consumer sentiment and cross-border logistics, though Sea has no Cambodian revenue dependence.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities litigation (resolved):** The consolidated federal securities class action concerning disclosures on Garena and Shopee **settled for $46M** (some filings cite ~$40M) for the class. No evidence of a currently open securities case, but the settlement establishes a disclosure-litigation track record that short-sellers have targeted before.
- **Competition:** Indonesia KPPU — 2024 violation finding and behavioural remedies; algorithm still under scrutiny (Jakarta Globe). Thailand — competition regulator **fined Shopee for unfair trade rules**, with the fine calculated at up to **10% of Shopee's turnover** in the year of violation (MLex) — a far larger theoretical quantum than anything seen in Indonesia. Vietnam — National Competition Commission fined Shopee **VND200M (~$7.6k)** on 9 Apr 2026 for a misleading free-shipping promotion (de minimis in size, but signals enforcement appetite).
- **E-commerce licensing:** **Vietnam's Law on E-Commerce 2025 takes effect 1 July 2026** — platform classification, mandatory **VNeID national-ID verification of all domestic sellers**, livestream-seller rules, and expanded liability for foreign platforms (Duane Morris; Vietan Law). **Philippines RA 11967 (Internet Transactions Act)** has been fully enforced since 20 June 2025, creating an E-Commerce Bureau with marketplace-liability and seller-registration powers.
- **Financial services:** Monee's loan book reached **$11.1B, +63% YoY**, with the **90-day NPL ratio held at ~1%**. The risk is not the current ratio but the *rate of growth against provisioning* — credit-loss provisions rose ~65% YoY. Multi-jurisdiction licensing (OJK Indonesia, BSP Philippines, BCB Brazil, MAS Singapore) means four conduct regulators, each with consumer-protection rate caps and collections rules.
- **Tax:** Indonesia's **PMK 37/2025** requires Shopee/Tokopedia/Blibli/Lazada to collect income tax from online merchants; Vietnam recovered ~$55M in e-commerce tax and fines in 2024 and is tightening penalties.
- **Data privacy:** Indonesia **UU PDP** (full enforcement since Oct 2024), Vietnam **Decree 13/2023** on cross-border transfers, Thailand PDPA, Malaysia PDPA amendments, Brazil LGPD, Singapore PDPA — six regimes, with localisation/transfer-impact-assessment obligations on a single shared data platform.
- **Trade policy backdrop:** Indonesia signed a **US Agreement on Reciprocal Trade on 19 Feb 2026** and an **Indonesia–EU CEPA**, reducing tariff tail-risk but increasing the incentive for Jakarta to keep visible pro-local-protection measures (like the $100 floor) as political cover.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Repeat-offender optics at KPPU** — Shopee admitted a competition violation in 2024 and its courier algorithm reportedly still favours its own SPX Express. A second finding escalates sanctions and invites Thai-style turnover-based penalties.
2. **Loot-box/child-protection liability is now litigated, not theoretical** — Brazil's June 2026 ruling plus a Justice Ministry probe of six titles creates direct exposure for Free Fire if paid chance-based mechanics remain accessible to minors.
3. **Seller KYC and product-standard gating** — Permendag 19/2026 makes Shopee legally responsible for rejecting unlicensed, sub-$100, and non-compliant sellers. Any failure to suspend after the grace period puts Shopee itself in the sanctions pathway (three warnings at 14-day intervals, then blocking).
4. **Consumer-credit conduct risk** — 63% loan growth while NPL90+ is held at 1% invites regulator scepticism on underwriting and collections; OJK rate caps directly compress Monee's unit economics.
5. **Cross-border data transfer** — an under-appreciated FCPA-adjacent/regulatory exposure: six privacy regimes with conflicting localisation rules over a unified platform.
6. **No sanctions, AML, or FCPA enforcement history identified.** No OFAC/DOJ/SEC investigation surfaced in this review, and no sanctioned-market revenue.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Composition of the rating:** this is a **regulatory- and legal-driven "High," not a conflict- or sanctions-driven one.** Sea has zero exposure to sanctions regimes, no expropriation risk, a Singapore holdco under MAS supervision, and a genuinely diversified footprint across SEA, Taiwan and Brazil. What drives the High rating is (i) ~30% of revenue in Indonesia during its most activist regulatory cycle in a decade (Permendag 19/2026, KPPU, OJK), (ii) a second engine in Brazil with a volatile tax and child-safety regime, and (iii) a Garena segment that concentrates group profitability in the single most actively regulated product category (chance-based monetisation aimed at a youth audience) across three jurisdictions at once.

**What would move the rating:**
- **To Very High:** a second KPPU adverse finding with turnover-based penalties; extension of social-commerce bans to marketplace checkout in Indonesia; a Brazilian court finding directly against Garena/Free Fire on loot boxes; or re-election-driven capital controls in Indonesia or Brazil.
- **To Medium:** successful, low-cost compliance with Permendag 19/2026 through the 18-month seller-licensing window; Brazil scrapping the 20% sub-$50 parcel duty; Free Fire India relaunch; and Monee sustaining NPL90+ near 1% through a full credit cycle.
