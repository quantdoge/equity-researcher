## Macroeconomic Assessment — Expedia Group (EXPE)
**As of 22 October 2026 | Data: FRED, FOMC minutes, company filings**

---

### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional, tilting Risk-Off**

The US economy is in a **late-cycle, re-tightening regime**, not a recession. Growth is still running near trend (~2.2%) and unemployment is a low 4.2%, but the disinflation progress of 2025 has stalled and partially reversed. Headline CPI has re-accelerated to **3.7% y/y in September 2026** (from a 3.3% trough in July), and the 3-month annualised rate is running near **4.6%**. The Fed **hiked 25bp in September 2026 to a 3.75%–4.00% target range** (per the 16 Sep FOMC minutes, which show a unanimous vote raising the rate paid on reserve balances to 3.90%), and futures markets have oscillated between a 15% and ~50% probability of a further October hike. The long end has borne the brunt: the **10Y is 4.99%–5.15% and the 30Y 5.37%**, near two-decade highs, with the curve **bear-steepening** (2s10s +34bp, no longer inverted).

For a high-beta (1.36), internationally exposed consumer-cyclical platform like EXPE, this is a **"good demand, bad discount rate"** environment — operationally supportive, valuation-hostile.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**POSITIVE**

- **Demand is holding.** GDP +2.2% with 4.2% unemployment supports travel propensity. EXPE has already **raised FY2026 guidance to $16.05–16.22B revenue (+9–10%)** and gross bookings to **$129.5–130.8B**, citing "resilient domestic travel demand" (Reuters, 5 Aug 2026). Q2 EPS of $5.76 beat the $5.22 consensus by 10%.
- **Nominal price inflation is a mechanical revenue tailwind.** Expedia earns commission on gross booking value; rising hotel ADRs and airfares inflate the ticket even at flat room-nights. Nominal PCE growth of 6.1% y/y supports this channel.
- **B2B diversification.** Expedia Partner Solutions growth is less consumer-credit-dependent than the retail leisure book — a partial hedge against a discretionary slowdown.
- **No inversion / no recession signal yet.** A positively sloped curve plus stable claims means the base case remains a soft landing, which is the single most important support for a beta-1.36 travel name.

**NEGATIVE**

- **Discount-rate compression.** With 10Y at ~5.0–5.15% and rising, the market is repricing long-duration, cyclical growth multiples. EXPE's own tape confirms sensitivity: the shares fell **~21% from the 24 Aug close of $338.64 to $259.04 on 23 Sep**, precisely as the 10Y ran from ~4.8% to 5.2%. At $285.64 today the stock is **16% below its August peak** while guidance has gone up — a pure multiple de-rating.
- **Consumer credit channel.** Travel is disproportionately financed on credit cards and BNPL. With policy at 3.75–4.00% and long yields at 20-year highs, card APRs and personal-loan rates remain restrictive. High-ticket international and premium leisure bookings are the most rate-elastic line items in the book.
- **Real income squeeze from sticky inflation.** Headline CPI at 3.7% and re-accelerating erodes real disposable income; "high airfares and fuel costs" are explicitly cited as a backdrop for Q3 travel demand. Fuel-cost inflation raises airline fares, which suppresses unit volume even as it lifts booking value.
- **Firming dollar.** The broad USD index at 121.4 (+2.3% in six weeks) is a **translation headwind** on EXPE's large non-US revenue base (EMEA brands, Trivago, international B2B) and makes US inbound destinations more expensive for foreign travellers — a drag on inbound bookings to US hotels.
- **Hike risk into a seasonally weak bookings window.** Q3 results land 4 November. A hawkish Fed surprise between now and then would hit sentiment into the print.

**NEUTRAL / MIXED**

- **FX is two-sided.** A strong dollar makes outbound US travel cheaper for Americans — supporting EXPE's outbound booking volumes — while hurting inbound-to-US demand. As a global marketplace, the net effect is roughly a wash on volume, but clearly negative on reported revenue translation.
- **Inflation cuts both ways:** nominal take-rate revenue up, real booking volume down. Near-term net neutral, negative if it persists into demand destruction.

---

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL** (negative bias)

Growth and labour market conditions are **Favourable**; monetary policy, the long end of the curve, inflation momentum and the dollar are **Unfavourable**. The company's own momentum (raised guidance, B2B growth) is offsetting a hostile rate backdrop — but that offset is exactly what makes the risk asymmetric: the macro headwind is being masked by idiosyncratic strength.

---

### 5. Material Surprise Scenario — **Hawkish Re-Tightening (Bear)**

**The consensus is wrong-footed on inflation.** Markets price only ~15–50% odds of an October hike and treat travel demand as structurally resilient. If the September CPI re-acceleration (3-mo annualised ~4.6%) carries into Q4 — energy pass-through plus sticky services — the Fed could hike in October and again in December, taking policy to 4.25–4.50%. The 10Y breaks **5.50%** and the 30Y tests **5.75%**, extending a bear steepening that has already added 76bp since May.

**Transmission to EXPE:** (i) further multiple compression on a 1.36-beta cyclical, (ii) card/BNPL-financed leisure demand softening with a 1–2 quarter lag, (iii) a dollar rally on rate differentials adding translation drag, and (iv) fuel/fare inflation suppressing unit volumes. FY2027 bookings growth decelerates from 9–10% to mid-single digits and management's guidance framework comes under pressure — a plausible **15–20% de-rating** from current levels on top of the 16% already suffered. This scenario is materially under-priced.

*The offsetting bull case — base-effect disinflation in Q1 2027 forcing a Fed pause, 10Y back to 4.50%, and a softer dollar — would allow EXPE to re-rate toward its August highs near $338, but it requires inflation to break the other way and is not the risk the tape is currently flagging.*
