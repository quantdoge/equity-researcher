# FORENSIC SHORT ANALYSIS — EXPEDIA GROUP (EXPE)
**Price $285.64 · Market cap ~$32.7B · Data through 21-Oct-2026 · Q3'26 report due 4-Nov-2026**

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment

**Rating: MEDIUM** (not fraud, but deteriorating in composition)

The headline forensic scores are, unusually, *exculpatory*:

| Metric | Value | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Beneish M-Score (FY25) | **−2.327** | "Low risk" — below the −2.22 soft threshold |
| Accruals ratio | **−10.6%** of assets | Negative = cash earnings exceed book earnings |
| Cash conversion (OCF/NI) | **300%** | Exceptional |
| Earnings quality score | **60/100** | Mid-tier |
| Piotroski F-Score | **5/9** | Moderate |

This is **not** a Beneish-style manipulation case. Accruals are negative in all four years (2022 −14.3%, 2023 −8.8%, 2024 −8.3%, 2025 −10.6%). Reported profit understates cash generation. A short seller claiming accounting fraud here would be wrong.

But the *composition* of recent earnings is deteriorating sharply, and that is where the real finding is:

**Q2 2026: GAAP diluted EPS +188%, adjusted EPS +36%, adjusted EBITDA +23%.** GAAP diluted EPS ($7.16) came in *above* adjusted EPS ($5.76) — meaning roughly **$1.40/share, ~$173M, of GAAP earnings was non-recurring gains or discrete tax benefits.** The Q2'26 tax rate computes to ~9.5% (op income $1,031M, interest $61M, net income $878M) versus ~30% in Q2'25. That is a ~20-point tax-rate collapse doing the heavy lifting. The "beat" that drove the stock up 12% on the print was substantially a tax and one-off artifact, not operating momentum.

**Also flag:** the 2025 income statement shows D&A of **$138M** against **$887M** in the cash flow statement and an implied ~$430M from the EBITDA bridge — three irreconcilable figures. Operating income rose 41.9% in 2025 on 7.6% revenue growth; if D&A genuinely fell ~$700M in the P&L, that alone would more than explain the entire increase. This is most likely a vendor reclassification, but it must be verified in the 10-K. **If real, the entire FY25 margin-expansion story is an accounting artifact.** The 10-K text was unavailable via the tool — this is an open item, not a proven finding.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**A. Receivables outgrowing revenue for three consecutive years — the clearest quantifiable flag.**
| Year | Revenue growth | AR growth | AR/Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | +10.1% | **+33.8%** | 22.1% |
| 2024 | +6.6% | **+14.8%** | 23.8% |
| 2025 | +7.6% | **+29.3%** | 28.5% |

Net receivables went $2.118B (2022) → $4.204B (2025), **+98% against revenue +26%**. AR has gone from 18.1% to 28.5% of revenue. Beneish DSRI of 1.201 is the single index pointing the wrong way. *Caveat:* this is partly genuine — B2B/Expedia Partner Solutions carries longer merchant collection cycles — but the magnitude exceeds what mix-shift explains. Watch for the day cash conversion diverges from book earnings; today it does not.

**B. Negative tangible book equity and hollowed-out capital structure.**
Goodwill + intangibles $7.691B against total equity $2.547B = **302% of equity**. Tangible book equity is **−$5.14B**. Equity has fallen from $3.728B (2022) to $2.547B (2025) *while earning $3.1B cumulatively* — because buybacks ($5.91B over 2023–25) exceeded free cash flow ($7.28B) less dividends. Altman Z-Score **0.843, "Distress" zone** (X1 working capital −0.182; X4 book equity/assets 0.116). Any Vrbo/trivago goodwill impairment puts equity deeply negative.

**C. Cash and "net cash" is largely customer float, not owned capital.**
Cash jumped $4.48B → $7.30B in 2025 while current liabilities rose $13.61B → $16.66B. Working capital is **−$4.46B**. The $1.173B working-capital benefit inside 2025 operating cash flow means reported 300% cash conversion is *a growth artifact*. Strip float: OCF ex-WC ≈ $2.71B vs net income $1.294B = 209%. Still strong — but the mechanism reverses violently when bookings stall. This is precisely the mechanism that nearly broke Expedia in 2020. Net cash of $307M is not a fortress; it is borrowed float.

**D. EPS growth is increasingly financial engineering.** FY25 net income +4.9% ($1.234B → $1.294B) while diluted EPS +9.6% ($8.95 → $9.81). Diluted shares fell 161.8M → 131.9M → 121.0M (Q2'26): **−25% in three years**. Roughly half of reported EPS growth is buyback arithmetic, not business growth. With only ~$5.7B of authorization left and net cash near zero, the buyback lever now depends entirely on FCF — the accelerator is flattening.

**E. Consensus implies abrupt growth deceleration in Q3.** The deceleration tool labels the trend "accelerating" (Q2'26 +14.0% vs Q2'25 +6.4%) and I concede that — it is real. But Q3'26 consensus revenue of ~$4.73B against a Q3'25 base of $4.412B implies only **+7.2%**, versus +14.0% in Q2. The toughest comps land right as margins are being celebrated.

**F. Capex intensity above peers.** Capex 5.2% of revenue ($770M) plus SBC 2.7% ($398M) = 7.9% of revenue in real economic cost against a "20%-margin" narrative. "Adjusted EBITDA" excludes both.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

This is where the genuine bear case lives, and it is structural, not cyclical.

| | Op margin | Rev growth | FCF margin | P/E |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **EXPE** | 14.7% | 7.6% | 21.1% | ~16x |
| BKNG | **34.5%** | **13.0%** | **31.9%** | 27.4x |
| ABNB | 20.8% | 10.3% | 38.0% | 29.9x |

Expedia generates ~57% of Booking's revenue but only ~24% of its operating income. That gap is not a cycle — it is a decade-long chronic under-earning driven by brand fragmentation (Expedia, Hotels.com, Vrbo, Orbitz, Travelocity, Hotwire, ebookers, Wotif, trivago), heavy paid-search dependence, and a multi-year "replatforming" that repeatedly slipped. SG&A is **60.7% of revenue**. The current margin expansion is real but comes largely from marketing efficiency — a lever with a finite number of pulls. You cannot cut your way to Booking's margin structure.

Two live threats:
- **Agentic AI at the top of the funnel.** OTAs monetise ~15% take rates for aggregation and search. LLM agents now perform that function. Expedia's response — being a first-party ChatGPT app partner — is defensive: it preserves volume while ceding the customer relationship, which is the definition of commoditising into an inventory wholesaler. The counter-argument is credible (Skift: agentic AI will likely need incumbents' supply and payments rails), so this is a 3–5 year erosion, not a 2027 event. But the market currently assigns it zero weight.
- **Hotel-commission class action** alleging the ~15% commission inflates consumer prices since 2020. A structural take-rate remedy is a direct hit to the revenue model, not a one-time settlement.

Mitigant: gross margin has *risen* every year (82.3% → 85.8% → 87.7% → 89.5% → 90.1%). No moat erosion in unit economics — the erosion is in customer ownership and acquisition cost.

Aggregate disclosed selling ~$3.8–4.5M against a $32.7B cap is **~0.014%** — noise, largely 10b5-1 and tax-related. **This is a weak signal and I mark it as such.** The meaningful governance read is structural: management has prioritised buybacks over balance-sheet repair to the point of driving book equity to $2.5B, which flatters EPS while raising fragility. That is a capital-allocation critique, not a self-dealing one. No related-party, auditor-change or promotional-language red flags surfaced.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, but it is a quality-of-earnings and peak-cycle thesis, not a fraud thesis — and it is *not* a valuation short.**

The bear case in one line: **EXPE is at a record cyclical peak, reporting EPS growth that is roughly half buyback arithmetic and partly discrete tax benefits, on a balance sheet with negative tangible equity funded by customer float — while the structural margin gap to Booking and the AI disintermediation risk remain unaddressed and unpriced.**

Supporting it: AR +98% vs revenue +26% over three years; tangible equity −$5.14B; Z-score 0.843; Q2'26 GAAP boosted ~$173M by non-recurring items; Q3 comps imply growth halving to ~7%; the stock has made lower highs since the $341.51 August peak (Sep 8 −7.4%, Sep 23 −7.7% on 6.3M shares, and a failed intraday reversal from $301.59 to $285.64 on 21-Oct) while fundamentals "beat" — a classic distribution divergence. Guidance was *raised* (gross bookings $129.5–130.8B), which is exactly what a peak looks like.

**Now the other side, stated at full strength — and it is strong.** Valuation kills this short. The stock is up 31.8% over twelve months but **only +1.5% YTD 2026**, and its P/E has *compressed* from ~19x in February to **~16x trailing** ($32.7B / ~$2.04B TTM net income) because earnings grew faster than the price. FCF yield is ~10% ($3.1–3.3B TTM FCF). Net cash. Operating margin has expanded 11.9% → 14.7% → 25.9% adjusted EBITDA in Q2'26, with five consecutive guidance beats. Revenue growth is *accelerating*, not decelerating. Buyback capacity is ~$5.7B plus ~$3B/yr of FCF — the EPS engine runs for years. Consensus 2026 adjusted EPS ~$21 implies 13.6x. This is not an overvalued stock; it is a mid-teens-multiple compounder with a 10% FCF yield. And the "AI kills OTAs" narrative has been wrong for three years running.

My honest read: **the bear case is analytically sound and tactically mistimed.** The forensic red flags are real but none of them are *proven* fraud, and every one is already visible in a 16x multiple. The single most credible short catalyst is a Q3 print on 4-Nov-2026 where revenue growth decelerates to ~7% and adjusted EBITDA margin expansion stalls — at which point the ~$173M of Q2 one-offs becomes an ugly comp.

## 7. Contrarian Rating

### **NO EDGE** — Reject as a core short position. Tactical short only.

- **As a fundamental short: reject.** Negative accruals, 300% cash conversion, low-risk M-Score, net cash, 10% FCF yield and a *compressing* P/E do not constitute an overvalued or fraudulent situation. The "distress" Z-score is a structural false positive for a float-funded OTA.
- **As a tactical/event short: conditional.** The tradeable setup is a Q3'26 miss/guide-down on 4-Nov-2026, with invalidation at $341.51 and a first target of the $258–$259 September low (−9%). A close below $255 confirms the lower-highs distribution pattern. Position size should reflect that this is a momentum-and-composition trade, not a structural fraud call.
- **What would upgrade this to a genuine SHORT:** AR growth continuing to outrun revenue *while* cash conversion falls below 150%; goodwill impairment or an equity raise; Q3 revenue growth below 7% with margin expansion stalling; or a take-rate remedy in the commission litigation.
- **What would make me abandon the bear case entirely:** sustained 12%+ revenue growth with adjusted EBITDA margin tracking toward 30% — that would prove the Booking gap is closable and re-rate the stock toward BKNG's multiple.

**Bottom line:** I looked hard for fraud and found honest, cash-backed accounting with two genuine blemishes — receivables growing 3–4x faster than sales, and a $173M one-off inflating the Q2 beat. Neither is a short. The real risk here is structural and slow, and the price already pays for most of it. My value in this memo is to say plainly: **the consensus is not wrong about the numbers — it is only possibly wrong about the durability of the cycle.**
