# Expedia Group, Inc. (EXPE) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $285.64** (close 21-Oct-2026) · Market cap ~$37.7B · EV ~$37.1B (net cash ~$0.6B) · Next catalyst: Q3 results 4-Nov-2026

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW** (modestly widening)

Expedia is the world's #2 online travel agency after Booking Holdings, operating three segments: Retail (Brand Expedia, Hotels.com, Vrbo, Orbitz, Travelocity), B2B/white-label distribution (Expedia Partner Solutions), and Trivago metasearch.

**What the moat actually consists of:**
- **Intangible assets (primary source):** A portfolio of genuinely recognized consumer brands and Vrbo, the clear #2 in alternative accommodations behind Airbnb. Brand and app traffic lowers customer acquisition cost relative to smaller OTAs.
- **Scale economics:** ~$14.7B of gross bookings leverage over supplier relationships, technology, and ad budgets. The advertising/media business (high-margin, near-100% incremental margin) monetizes existing traffic at essentially zero marginal cost.
- **Limited network effects:** Two-sided marketplace, but supply is **multi-homed** — hotels list simultaneously on Booking, Expedia, Airbnb, and their own sites. Consumer switching costs are close to zero; One Key loyalty is a partial offset, not a lock-in.
- **Float economics (underappreciated):** Customer prepayments fund operations — current liabilities ($16.7B) exceed current assets ($12.2B) by $4.5B. This is insurance-style float: negative working capital that grows with bookings. It is a real structural advantage, but it is *other people's money* and reverses in a downturn.

**What undermines the moat:** Marketing intensity. SGA of $8.95B on $14.7B revenue (**61% of sales**) means demand must be re-purchased annually — first from Google (SEM), now increasingly from AI platforms. A business that pays 61% of revenue to acquire customers does not have a self-sustaining moat in the Booking/Google sense. Gross margin of 90% flatters the economics; the true take rate after customer acquisition is ordinary.

**Has it widened?** Yes, measurably over 4 years — but from a depressed base:
- Operating margin: 2.2% (2021) → 9.3% (2022) → 8.1% (2023) → 9.6% (2024) → **12.7% (2025)**
- ROIC: 10.6% → 11.0% → 14.1% → **20.3%** — now comfortably above a ~9% WACC
- Gross margin: 82.3% → **90.1%**

That ROIC trajectory is the single strongest evidence for a moat here. But note it was earned on a modest invested-capital base with $7.7B of goodwill/intangibles already written down in prior years; the "20% ROIC" is a recovery-and-discipline story more than an impregnable-franchise story. Booking, by contrast, earns **67.7% ROIC** — the gap is the honest measure of relative moat quality.

**Circle-of-competence check:** The intermediary marketplace model is understandable. But the 10-year durability question — whether AI agents disintermediate the booking layer — is genuinely unsettled. I would not underwrite this as a "hold forever" compounder.

**Strengths:** Cash generation is the standout. FCF exceeded net income in every year because D&A ($887M) exceeds capex ($770M) — the hallmark of an asset-light intermediary. Debt was reduced from $2.35B net debt to roughly net-cash-neutral in a single year while simultaneously repurchasing $1.93B of stock and initiating a dividend. Interest coverage ~6.3x on EBIT; gross debt/EBITDA ~2.4x.

**Weaknesses / quality flags:**
1. **Working capital inflates FCF.** 2025's $3.11B FCF includes a **$1.173B** working-capital inflow from booking growth. Ex-float FCF is ~$1.94B. This is not fraud — it is the nature of the model — but it means FCF is *not* a clean proxy for owner earnings and will reverse hard in a demand shock.
2. **Thin equity, negative tangible book.** Equity of $2.55B against $7.69B of goodwill leaves tangible book value around **−$5.1B**. ROE of 50.8%
