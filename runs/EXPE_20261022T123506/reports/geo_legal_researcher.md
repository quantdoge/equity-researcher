## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1: Middle East Conflict and Mexico Travel Advisories Depressing International Demand
- **Probability:** High (ongoing/active)
- **Impact:** High (direct revenue hit)
- **Analysis:** In May 2026, Reuters reported Expedia shares dropped 8% after the ongoing Middle East conflict and a Mexico travel advisory weighed on booking demand. Because ~40% of revenue comes from non-U.S. points of sale, demand shocks in key leisure destinations translate directly to top-line volatility. The conflict creates uncertainty in air travel and hospitality bookings across the EMEA region. *(Source: Reuters, May 8, 2026)*

### Risk 2: EU Digital Markets Act (DMA) and Rate-Parity Enforcement
- **Probability:** Medium-High
- **Impact:** Medium-High (business model disruption)
- **Analysis:** The EU's DMA bans rate-parity clauses for designated gatekeepers (e.g., Booking.com) and is reshaping how hotels can be priced and displayed online. While Expedia is not currently designated a gatekeeper in the same way as Booking, the DMA's broader market interventions—combined with Google's DMA-driven search changes that have reduced "free direct booking traffic" to European hotels by 30%—alter the competitive dynamics of Expedia's European brands (ebookers, Hotels.com). Non-compliance with DMA or GDPR carries fines of up to 10% of global turnover. *(Source: EU DMA factsheet; PhocusWire)*

### Risk 3: Sanctions Exposure and Legacy OFAC Compliance Gaps in Foreign Subsidiaries
- **Probability:** Medium
- **Impact:** Medium (financial penalties, reputational damage)
- **Analysis:** Expedia has a documented history of sanctions violations. In 2019, OFAC settled with Expedia for $325,406 after finding that the company enabled 2,221 people (including Cuban nationals) to travel to/from Cuba between 2011 and 2014, in violation of the Cuban Assets Control Regulations. OFAC specifically cited foreign subsidiaries for failing to "exercise a minimal degree of caution" and noted a "pattern of conduct." As Expedia expands its B2B arm (Expedia Partner Solutions) and acquires international businesses, the risk of sanctions screening gaps in newly acquired foreign entities remains elevated. *(Source: Visual Compliance/OFAC settlement, June 13, 2019)*

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

### Active Litigation

**A. Antitrust Class Action — *Andrews, et al. v. Expedia Group, Inc.* (Filed Oct 2, 2026)**
- **Court:** U.S. District Court for the Western District of Washington (Case No. 2:26-cv-03743)
- **Allegations:** Expedia violated Sections 1 and 2 of the Sherman Act by using its alleged ~80% dominance in U.S. online travel agency hotel bookings to impose rate-parity restrictions on hotels. Plaintiffs allege Expedia uses pricing algorithms to demote hotels in search results when they offer lower rates on rival sites (e.g., Booking.com), inflating prices for consumers nationwide—including those who never booked through Expedia.
- **Remedy Sought:** Damages and an injunction to end the challenged practices. This is a structural threat to Expedia's U.S. merchant model and pricing power. *(Source: Berger Montague; NYT, Oct 16, 2026)*

**B. Vrbo Wrongful-Death / Product Liability Lawsuit (Filed Sept 2026)**
- **Court:** King County Superior Court (Washington)
- **Allegations:** Families from Massachusetts sued Expedia Group and its Vrbo entities over a deadly June 13, 2026 vacation rental fire in Barahona, Dominican Republic. The suit alleges inadequate safety warnings about properties lacking smoke detectors.
- **Impact:** While the financial damages may be contained, this creates reputational and regulatory scrutiny for the Vrbo alternative-accommodation segment and highlights safety-compliance gaps in emerging-market listings. *(Source: Yahoo Finance/Simply Wall St, Sept 24, 2026)*

**C. U.S. State Attorneys General Antitrust Probe**
- A group of U.S. state AGs is investigating Expedia Group and major hotel chains (Hyatt, Marriott) over hotel booking practices. While this probe is older (2019), it reflects sustained regulatory interest in OTA pricing practices that overlaps with the 2026 *Andrews* class action. *(Source: Reuters)*

**D. Hotel Taxes & Fees Litigation**
- Long-running class action alleging Expedia purchased hotel rooms at wholesale rates and then added "tax and service fees" to consumer rates. This is a recurring, margin-dilutive legal overhang in the U.S. market. *(Source: Hagens Berman)*

### Regulatory Environment
- **EU/GDPR & DMA:** As a platform handling massive volumes of personal and payment data, Expedia faces strict GDPR enforcement (cumulative EU GDPR fines exceeded €7.1 billion by 2026). The DMA's rate-parity bans and transparency requirements add operational complexity.
- **U.S. Consumer Protection:** The FTC and state AGs remain focused on drip pricing and mandatory fee disclosure in the travel sector. The antitrust suit reinforces this scrutiny.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:**
- **Geopolitical:** ~40% of revenue is international, exposing Expedia to demand shocks from the Middle East conflict, Mexico travel advisories, and European regulatory shifts. While Russia exposure is zero, sanctions-compliance integration risk remains from foreign acquisitions.
- **Legal:** The company faces an active, high-stakes antitrust class action targeting its core U.S. business model, a wrongful-death lawsuit in its Vrbo segment, and sustained state AG/regulatory scrutiny over pricing and fees.
- **Regulatory:** The EU's DMA and GDPR impose structural compliance costs and potential fines, while simultaneously altering the competitive landscape in Expedia's key European markets.

The combination of a concentrated U.S. antitrust threat (which could force changes to its merchant model) and international demand volatility in a high-growth region (EMEA/APAC) pushes the risk rating to **High**. The absence of a currently active "Very High" trigger (e.g., a pending DOJ breakup suit or a massive OFAC penalty) keeps it below the top tier, but the litigation docket is material.
