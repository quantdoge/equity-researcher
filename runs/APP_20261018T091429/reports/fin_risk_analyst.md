## AppLovin Corporation (APP) — Financial Risk Assessment
*Data as of the latest available quarter (Q2 FY2026, filed 2026-08-05) and price close 2026-10-16 ($266.13).*

**Headline finding:** This is a company whose *financial* risk and whose *market* risk point in opposite directions. The balance sheet is close to pristine — net leverage is effectively zero, S&P has this at investment grade, and a 20% revenue decline would barely dent coverage. But the equity is a 82%-volatility, 2.2-beta instrument that has lost 64% of its value in twelve months on short-seller allegations, AI-disruption fears and a securities-fraud suit. Solvency risk: low. Equity/drawdown risk: critical.

---

### 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE**
S&P upgraded AppLovin to **BBB+** (S&P Global Research Update), corroborated by tooling-verified leverage: net debt is **$462M** against **$5,550M** TTM EBITDA (**0.08x**). Gross debt/EBITDA is **0.63x**. Interest coverage on TTM EBIT is **26.4x** (FY25 reported 20.06x). All debt is fixed-rate senior unsecured — $3.55B issued in 2024, refinancing the 2028 term loan, including a **$1.0B 5.375% tranche due 2031**. Balance sheet shows **zero short-term debt**, so there is no near-term refinancing cliff; blended coupon ≈5.8% ($205M annual interest on $3.5B) with no floating-rate exposure.

Only caveat on the rating: book equity is just $3.16B while gross debt is $3.52B, so the *accounting* cushion is thin even though cash flow covers it many times over.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**
Split explicitly: **credit/liquidity risk LOW; market/valuation risk CRITICAL.** I cannot rate this "Low" overall given 82% volatility, 2.2+ beta, a realized 64% drawdown, and business-model fragility that no balance sheet can offset. I cannot rate it "High" because there is no plausible operating scenario in which this company fails to service $3.5B of fixed-rate unsecured debt maturing 2029+.

### 6. Key Red Flags
1. **Cash conversion deterioration (watch item).** Q2'26 operating cash flow was $869M against $1,267M net income — **0.69x conversion**, versus 1.07x in Q1'26, driven by a $490M working-capital drag. Receivables rose 11% q/q vs revenue +4.4%; DSO ~103 days. Receivables ($2,171M) now equal 32% of TTM revenue. One quarter is not a trend, but it is the single most important metric to monitor.
2. **Durability of a 76–79% operating margin.** This margin structure depends on auction dynamics and self-preferencing in AppDiscovery/MAX and on a nascent e-commerce advertising push dependent on third-party platforms. Margin durability — not leverage — is the true risk variable.
3. **Platform/regulatory dependency.** Apple/Google policy and de-platforming risk is unhedgeable; regulatory retaliation flagged by sell-side commentary.
4. **Legal/headline risk.** CapitalWatch money-laundering allegations (Jan 2026), a securities-fraud class action after the 20% drop, and multiple short-seller cycles. These explain most of the de-rating; none are quantified in financials.
5. **Aggressive buybacks at high prices.** $3.7B repurchased across FY25–H1'26, largely above today's $266 — capital-allocation risk into a volatile, high-multiple equity, and it keeps book equity thin ($3.16B).
6. **Goodwill & intangibles of $1.87B = 59% of book equity** (23% of assets). FY25 intangibles fell $763M against only $195M of D&A, implying ~$570M of impairment/disposal — consistent with the Apps-segment divestiture (inferred, not directly disclosed by the tooling). Book equity quality is modest.
7. **Customer/geographic concentration** in performance advertising — not quantifiable from available data.
