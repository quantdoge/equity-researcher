*Price $266.13 | Market cap ~$89.4bn | 887 employees | Beta 2.56 | Full-time employees only; no standalone sustainability report*

**Key insight for the thesis:** for AppLovin, "ESG" is ~85% *governance and product-integrity*, ~15% social/regulatory, and effectively zero environmental. The ESG lens here is a **fraud/regulatory tail-risk lens**, not a values lens.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **Low**
- Asset-light software model; no manufacturing, no owned data centres disclosed, no emissions-intensive operations. 887 employees implies immaterial Scope 1/2.
- No environmental controversies, fines, or remediation liabilities identified in any coverage reviewed.
- **Disclosure gap:** No standalone sustainability report, no TCFD/SASB/CDP disclosure, no Scope 3 inventory, no science-based targets. This is a disclosure deficiency rather than an operating risk; it makes APP ineligible for many "ESG-screened" mandates, a marginal negative for passive ESG flows but not for valuation.
- Only forward-looking angle: AI inference energy costs (Axon) — an opex/margin item, not an ESG liability.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **High**
- **Human capital (low risk, low materiality):** tiny, elite workforce. No union exposure, no disclosed safety incidents, no material turnover data. Product-related social risk dominates.
- **Data privacy & minors (critical):** Fuzzy Panda alleged AppLovin "appears to be illegally tracking children" via persistent identifiers in violation of federal law (COPPA) — a statutory-penalty-per-violation regime. Muddy Waters alleged "systematic" violations of app-store terms of service via "impermissible extraction" of proprietary IDs, and reverse-engineering of Meta advertising data (fingerprinting). AppLovin has called these claims "false narratives."
- **Product integrity:** the allegations go to attribution fraud — the single most reputationally and commercially lethal accusation in adtech (advertisers can demand make-goods, refunds, or exit).
- **Whistleblower:** the SEC probe reportedly followed an alleged whistleblower complaint — a signal that the risk is internal, not just media-driven.
- **Counterweight:** the divestiture of the mobile-gaming (Apps) business to Tripledot (completed 1 July 2025, ~$400m cash) removes some conflict-of-interest and content-quality exposure from the ad network, and management has publicly denied related-party revenue duplication.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10**

| Element | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Controlled company** | Multi-class structure; Class B carries ~20 votes/share; the **Voting Agreement Parties (Foroughi, Chen and affiliates) hold majority voting power** per the 2026 proxy. Minority shareholders cannot effect change. **Negative** |
| **CEO/Chair separation** | **Positive, recent:** Craig Billings (director since Dec 2020) became **independent Chairperson in April 2026**, succeeding Foroughi as chair; Billings had been Lead Independent Director. This is a genuine improvement. |
| **Officer exculpation** | **Negative:** stockholders approved a charter amendment (certificate effective 5 June 2026) eliminating officer liability for monetary damages for duty-of-care breaches — reduces personal accountability precisely while litigation is pending. |
| **Board** | Nine nominees, Deloitte as auditor, majority-independent by NYSE standards with a carve-out for controlled-company exemptions; standing committees disclosed. Adequate on paper, but constrained by founder control. |
| **Compensation** | Say-on-pay on the ballot; heavy equity-based NEO pay (Foroughi, Stumpf, Shikin, Valenzuela) aligns with TSR but rewards Adjusted EBITDA/FCF — metrics that themselves are under dispute. |
| **Capital allocation** | $2.2bn repurchased in 2025; authorization raised $3.2bn in Oct 2025; $1.5bn more in H1 2026. Aggressive buybacks shrink the share count but also concentrate voting control and can be read as a support mechanism during a disclosure controversy. |
| **Insider activity** | Frequent Form 4/Form 144 filings through 2026 (e.g., 21 Aug 2026) indicate continued insider dispositions — normal for a founder-led name but notable alongside a live SEC probe. |
| **C-suite churn** | April 2026 succession announcement: chair change *plus* two other executives leaving the C-suite, concurrent with an active SEC investigation. Stability risk. |

**Verdict:** an independent chair and a credible audit committee prevent a worse score, but founder control, officer exculpation, and a contested disclosure record cap this at 3/10.

**The regulatory point that matters:** the binding constraint on AppLovin is not Brussels or Washington — it's Apple's and Meta's terms of service. Loss of signal/ID access is the model-breaking scenario, and the SEC probe is explicitly about whether those agreements were breached.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **ESG Risk** (below Laggard)
- Environmental: Low risk, but no disclosure.
- Social: High risk — privacy, minors, ad-fraud allegations, whistleblower complaint.
- Governance: 3/10 — controlled company, officer exculpation, contested disclosure record, active SEC investigation, serial securities litigation.
- No sustainability reporting framework, no ESG targets, and a live enforcement matter. On any mainstream ESG rating (MSCI/Sustainalytics analogue), APP screens into the bottom decile on governance and controversies, notwithstanding a world-class growth and cash-generation profile ($5.5bn 2025 revenue, $4.0bn FCF).

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **Negative for the thesis**
Not because of anything environmental — because **the ESG risk is the investment risk**. The controversies cluster on exactly the variable that drives valuation: whether reported ad performance and AI-model gains are genuine and durable. Three practical channels:

1. **Cost of capital / ownership base:** controlled-company status + controversy flag + no ESG disclosure excludes APP from most ESG-screened and many large institutional mandates — a structural headwind to the multiple and a source of forced-selling volatility.
2. **Contingent liabilities:** SEC resolution, a multi-hundred-million-dollar securities settlement, and any COPPA/state AG action are real, if not thesis-breaking, cash costs — against ~$4.0bn FCF they are absorbable; a platform ToS termination is not.
3. **Governance discount is earned:** the Aug 2026 "lighter than normal" model-improvement concession, six months after Feb 2026 assurances to the contrary, is precisely the disclosure pattern the short sellers predicted. Until the SEC matter closes without action and a clean audit cycle passes, the governance discount should stay in the price.

**What would upgrade the rating:** SEC inquiry closed with no enforcement; dismissal or modest settlement of the Feb–Aug 2026 class action; sunset of the dual-class structure or elimination of the voting agreement; a first sustainability report with Scope 1–3 and privacy/AI governance metrics; independent-board majority with no controlled-company exemptions.
