## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE** (board improving; shareholder rights weak)

**Positives (real, and recent):**
- **CEO/Chair separation achieved.** Independent director **Craig Billings** was appointed Chairperson, ending Adam Foroughi's duality. This is a genuine post-2025 governance upgrade.
- **Majority-independent board** of nine directors, **fully independent key committees**, and — unusually for a controlled company — AppLovin does **not** use Nasdaq "controlled company" exemptions.
- Equity-heavy, **no-bonus** executive pay tied to long-term performance; compensation recovery (clawback) policy; stock ownership guidelines.
- Auditor **Deloitte & Touche** ($4.08M audit fees of $4.81M total — healthy non-audit ratio; no auditor-consulting red flag).
- $2.2B of Class A repurchased in 2025 plus $3.2B authorization increase — capital returned, though also a lever that can mask per-share deterioration.

**Negatives (structural, and unresolved):**
- **Extreme voting concentration.** Class B carries **20 votes per share**; Foroughi alone holds ~789.7M of ~910.2M combined voting power (**~87%**), with director Herald Chen, via a Voting Agreement. Public shareholders have effectively no ability to remove directors or block charter changes. The "majority-independent board" is independent in composition, not in accountability.
- **Entrenchment architecture:** a **classified/staggered board** and **supermajority voting provisions** remain in place — shareholders have been formally agitating to remove both (per 2026 engagement disclosures).
- **Officer exculpation charter amendment on the 2026 ballot** — this would limit officers' personal liability for breach of fiduciary duty (except bad faith/intentional misconduct). Proposing this *while* defending a securities fraud class action and an active SEC probe is a shareholder-unfriendly signal on accountability.
- **Foroughi remains a 10% owner and CEO** — founder control is intact regardless of the chair title.

**Verdict:** Better than the 2024–25 version of this company, but "adequate" only because the board is genuinely independent in composition and committees. On *shareholder rights*, it is weak.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Platform dependency / existential single point of failure.** AppLovin's targeting efficacy depends on access to iOS and Android device signals governed by Apple's and Google's terms. The SEC theory is precisely that this access was obtained improperly. Apple or Google can change policy or enforce terms unilaterally, with no appeal — this converts a regulatory finding into immediate product impairment. This is the highest-severity operational risk in the business and is not hedgeable.
- **Key-person and leadership-void risk during a crisis.** Founded and run by Foroughi; **CTO Basil Shikin stepped down to "Distinguished Engineer" (announced Apr 2, 2026, Giovanni Ge succeeding) and CAO/CLO Victoria Valenzuela retired effective Aug 1, 2026 (Corina Cacovean succeeding)** — both transitions landing in the same quarter as the SEC probe confirmation and the securities suit. Two of the three executives most needed for the defense (technology and legal) turned over simultaneously. With only **887 employees** supporting an $89B market cap, institutional depth is thin.
- **Model-improvement dependency and disclosure credibility.** Revenue growth is explicitly tied to the rate of AI model iteration. Management guided in February 2026 that gains would not slow, then disclosed in August 2026 that improvement was "lighter than normal." Operationally, this is a research-velocity risk; reputationally, it is the exact fact pattern the class action is built on. **Technology/algorithm execution risk is now inseparable from securities litigation risk.**

*Secondary:* data-privacy and cybersecurity exposure inherent to a business that ingests mobile device identifiers at scale; supplier/customer concentration is moderate but the *platform* concentration above dominates.
