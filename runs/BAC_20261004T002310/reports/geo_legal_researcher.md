## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US–China financial decoupling & trade escalation**
- Probability: High (ongoing). Impact: Medium.
- OFAC has sanctioned Chinese refineries for Iranian crude purchases (2025); tariff escalation (Section 232 investigations) and HK market turbulence (biggest slump since March, Oct 2026) raise volatility and raise compliance costs for BAC's APAC platform. Direct credit exposure is modest, so impact is mainly revenue-side (capital markets fees, client flows) rather than balance-sheet.

**Risk 2 — Energy-supply shock / Middle East conflict spillover**
- Probability: Medium–High (live conflict scenario in current environment). Impact: Medium.
- Record diesel prices, attacks on Russian refining capacity, Iran-war-driven oil spikes and a G7 coordinated fuel release (Oct 2026) imply inflation and rate divergence. For BAC: positive for trading volatility revenue, negative for consumer credit quality and EMEA/global-growth assumptions.

**Risk 3 — European sovereign stress & bond-market disorder**
- Probability: Medium (crisis in early stages; France missing deficit targets). Impact: Medium.
- A global bond rout and French debt selloff pressure BAC's EMEA operations (~10% of revenue) through mark-to-market losses, hedging costs, and weaker EMEA deal flow. Secondary risk: contagion to U.S. rates and net interest margin.

*Other watch items:* Russian sanctions secondary-liability risk (minimal residual), and U.S. fiscal/debt-ceiling politics into the 2026 midterms (medium probability, medium impact on sentiment and market funding).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Resolution tailwinds (overhangs cleared)**
- **Zelle/CFPB lawsuit — dismissed with prejudice (Mar 5, 2025).** CFPB sued BAC, JPMorgan, and Wells Fargo (Dec 2024) over Zelle fraud reimbursement; acting CFPB leadership dropped it. Major consumer-enforcement overhang removed.
- **CFPB overdraft-fee rule (2024) overturned by Congress**; **credit-card late-fee cap ($8) struck down in court (2025).** Two fee-revenue threats largely neutralized.
- **Basel III "endgame" easing:** Fed/regulators signaled lighter large-bank capital rules (2025), a direct capital and return-on-equity tailwind for BAC; Sen. Warren's opposition noted but not decisive.

**Active/ongoing legal exposure**
- **OCC AML consent order (Dec 30, 2024):** BAC ordered to remediate anti-money-laundering/SAR program deficiencies (no monetary fine). Active compliance burden, not yet closed.
- **Fair-lending/redlining history:** ~$225M total federal resolution (2022–23) on discriminatory mortgage lending (CFPB/DOJ), plus the July 2023 CFPB ($90M) + OCC ($60M) penalties and >$100M consumer redress over fake accounts and double-charged unemployment-benefits fees (~$250M package).
- **Jeffrey Epstein-related civil litigation:** BAC settled accuser/class claims in 2024–2025 (amounts not fully disclosed) — residual reputational and litigation risk continues.
- **Off-channel communications (WhatsApp/text):** SEC/CFTC recordkeeping wave has produced >$3B in industry fines since 2022; BAC participated in the September 2022 $1.8B round and remains exposed to further sweeps (SEC has issued fresh subpoenas to large banks).
- **New probes flagged:** mortgage-practice and FX-business investigations reported (details/status not fully confirmed in-session); SEC "situational awareness" messaging subpoenas also reported.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **AML/SAR program deficiencies** — OCC consent order remains open; Zelle-era AML scrutiny cited by regulators as a driver. Highest-severity flag (supervisory order in force).
2. **Recordkeeping failures** — repeat enforcement rounds on off-channel communications; systemic across the industry, with BAC named in major settlements.
3. **Consumer-protection accumulations** — recurring pattern of CFPB/OCC/DOJ fines (fees, fair lending, fake accounts, unemployment benefits); indicates structural compliance gaps in consumer banking rather than one-off events.
4. **Sanctions secondary risk** — Hong Kong/Asia relationships and Chinese-refinery sanctions create ongoing OFAC-screening burden (low direct exposure, elevated process risk).
5. **Epstein matter** — reputational/regulatory tail associated with a high-profile financial-crime file; watch for any non-public regulatory counterpart inquiries.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Geopolitical exposure is structurally LOW — ~80% of revenue is U.S.-domestic, Russia exposure is near zero, China lending is modest, and currency/expropriation risk is immaterial. But the legal/regulatory overlay is ELEVATED: an active OCC AML consent order, a history of large consumer-enforcement penalties, Epstein-related civil tail risk, and industry-wide recordkeeping enforcement keep supervisory risk live. The near-term regulatory *direction* is nonetheless favorable (deregulation, dropped Zelle suit, eased capital rules), which tempers the rating to Medium rather than High.

**Key watch items for the investment memo:** (1) timing of OCC AML order termination; (2) any new DOJ/CFPB consumer action under the 2027 enforcement cycle; (3) European sovereign contagion to EMEA trading revenue; (4) US–China escalation effects on APAC capital-markets fees.
