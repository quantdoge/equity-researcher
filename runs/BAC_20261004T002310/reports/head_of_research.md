### Investment Memo: BAC (Bank of America Corp.) — October 5, 2026

**Verdict**: BUY | Conviction: Medium
**Price Target**: $62 (≈15% upside from current $53.75; 12–24m range $58–66)
**Time Horizon**: 12–24 months

**Investment Thesis**
BAC is the #2 US deposit franchise with a fortress balance sheet (CET1 ~11.2%, $303B equity) operating in a macro regime — Fed easing at the short end with a steep, positively sloped curve (2Y 4.65% / 10Y 4.99%, +34bp spread) — that is close to optimal for its rate-sensitive net-interest-income engine; management guides NII +5–7% with a 100% seven-quarter EPS beat streak and +15.8% upward estimate revisions underpinning a ~14% EPS CAGR (2026–29E) at ~11.7x forward earnings. The stock has already de-rated ~17% from its August peak and trades ~10–15% below central intrinsic ($58–62), making the setup asymmetric-positive — but the absence of a 20–30% margin of safety, a full tangible-book multiple, and Berkshire's seven-quarter exit demand price discipline: buy scaled, not in one tranche.

Steep positive curve = NIM-accretive. Cleared: Zelle/CFPB suit dismissed with prejudice, fee rules repealed/struck down, Basel eased. Live: OCC AML consent order, Epstein tail, recordkeeping sweeps; EMEA sovereign-bond disorder is the macro-adjacent risk.

**Bull Case**
1. Macro is tailor-made: easing Fed + steep curve feeds NII; BAC guided +5–7% (upper end of 6–8%) and proved it with Q1/Q2'26 revenue +10.6%/+19.3% YoY.
2. Fortress balance sheet plus relentless capital return: CET1 ~11.2%, $33.7B returned in FY25 (~110% of NI), dividend +14%, CCAR/Basel relief extends the buyback runway.
3. Asymmetric valuation: ~11.7x forward vs ~14% EPS CAGR (PEG ≈ 0.8), ~12% under intrinsic, 2.2% growing yield, low beta, targets imply 15–30% upside.

**Bear Case**
1. No margin of safety: 10–15% under intrinsic, not the required 20–30%; full ~1.7x TBV; the 15-year ROE discount to JPM argues against a re-rating.
2. NII re-acceleration is the entire EPS bridge: FY25 growth was buyback arithmetic on flat revenue; a 10Y >5.5% + CPI >3.5% regime breaks the ladder while cards/office-CRE credit normalizes.
3. Informed seller risk: Berkshire trimming 7 straight quarters; 0.80 correlation to JPM/WFC means peer beta reprices BAC regardless of merit; quant flags an active momentum reversal.

**Key Risks**
- Macro regime flip (10Y >5.5% + CPI >3.5% for 2 months); credit normalization into 2027; OCC AML escalation or punitive Basel Endgame taxing capital return; NII guide miss (first test: Oct 14); CEO succession execution; Berkshire exit acceleration; fair-lending probe reopen.
