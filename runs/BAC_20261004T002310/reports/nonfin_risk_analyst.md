# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Bank of America Corporation (NYSE: BAC)

**Scope:** Operational, reputational, regulatory, fraud, and governance risks as of early October 2026. Data sources: SEC/EDGAR filings, FMP news & regulatory feeds, OCC/CFPB/SEC actions, insider Form 4 activity, and press coverage.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate (with concentration caveats)**

**Evidence:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality:** Brian Moynihan holds both Chairman and CEO roles (since 2014, CEO since 2010). A shareholder proposal to separate the roles drew only **32.6% support** at the last annual meeting — a meaningful dissenting minority but not a majority revolt. Mitigant: an independent Lead Director (Lionel L. Nowell III per 2023 proxy).
- **Novel scrutiny:** The SEC required BAC to allow a shareholder vote on a **breakup/structural review proposal** (Naylor resolution) — a sign of rising shareholder and regulatory attention to board responsiveness.
- Executive pay was approved by shareholders; no compensation clawback or say-on-pay failure surfaced.
- No related-party transaction red flags, no board independence scandal, no short-seller allegations of accounting manipulation identified.
- **Caveat:** Detailed 2026 proxy (DEF 14A) data was unavailable via SEC filing tools (also no 10-K/10-Q excerpts returned), so current board-composition verification rests on prior-year proxies and press. Re-verify at next proxy in Q1'27.

**Verdict:** Standard mega-cap structure, but long CEO tenure + duality + growing shareholder dissent on structural governance keeps this "Adequate," not "Strong."

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**Active (elevated attention):**
- **OCC cease-and-desist order (Dec 23, 2024):** Deficiencies in Bank Secrecy Act/AML and sanctions compliance programs. No monetary penalty at issuance, but the order mandates program remediation and BAC has warned investors it faces potential future fines. **This is the single most important live regulatory item** — and it appears in the same risk family as the January 2026 SEC fine of Merrill for failing to file numerous Suspicious Activity Reports ($7.5M, SAR-filing failures = AML-adjacent).
- **Democratic lawmakers investigating banks' exits from the Net-Zero Banking Alliance** (BAC, JPM, Citi, Wells) — political/regulatory attention on climate-commitment backtracking, though no formal enforcement action.

**Resolved / legacy (last ~3 years):**
- CFPB 2023 consent order ($12M CMP + remediation + 3-yr monitoring) — **monitoring terminated 3 years early (June 2025)** after "fulfilled obligations." Positive trajectory.
- CFPB 2023: $150M in fines + $100M+ customer refunds for double-dipping overdraft fees and opening accounts without consent; CFPB $10M for illegal out-of-state garnishments; CFPB $12M mortgage-disclosure fine (earlier).
- **Zelle:** CFPB's Dec 2024 suit (BAC, JPM, WF, EWS) alleging ~$870M in P2P fraud losses was **dismissed with prejudice** in 2025 — headline legal tailwind, but private class actions remain outstanding.
- **Epstein:** $72.5M settlement with accusers received **final court approval (Aug 27, 2026)** — resolved, but see reputational section.
- **DOJ declination** for a BAC securities unit re: market-manipulation allegations — favorable outcome.
- Sept 2026: Merrill agreed to pay **$39M** to settle class claims over near-zero rates on idle cash in retirement accounts. Small relative to balance sheet; closes a consumer-disclosure matter.
- No material active DOJ investigation and no SEC enforcement actions flagged in EDGAR full-text search.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Recurring third-party/vendor data-exposure pattern:** 57,000-customer incident (2024), 11,000-customer breach (Feb 2025), lost documents containing sensitive info (Mar 2025), plus unverified claims of ~1M records circulating. BAC's core systems reportedly stayed intact, but third-party ecosystem exposure is a chronic, compounding issue for a bank holding ~67M consumer clients.
2. **BSA/AML & sanctions compliance remediation under live OCC C&D;** SAR-filing failures already drew a $7.5M SEC fine. Compliance-manual deficiencies in a $3T+ asset bank invite both regulatory escalation and an AML-enforcement targeting risk.
3. **Key-person / market-influence concentration on the CEO:** 16-year incumbent whose conference comments alone moved the stock ~5% in September; succession planning is a perennial board focus. Low broad key-person risk given 211K employees, but CEO-outcome sensitivity is outsized.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment

- **Live goodwill positives:** $10M Boston Children's behavioral-health grant, $100B+ personal retirement platform milestone, 15th annual tech summit, strong digital-banking brand.
- **Persistent reputational liabilities:**
  - **Epstein association** — settled with final approval, but "ignored red flags" allegations are a durable reputational scar and will resurface in every lawsuit compendium.
  - **Consumer-abuse history** — double-dipping fees, phantom accounts, illegal garnishments ($250M+ in fines/refunds across 2022–23) keep the "fee-abusive megabank" narrative alive.
  - **ESG political whiplash:** Exited NZBA (Jan 2025) under conservative pressure while Democratic lawmakers investigate the exits — BAC is now exposed to criticism from **both** flanks simultaneously, a classic politically-tainted positioning risk.
  - Entry into the **stablecoin consortium** (21 banks, 2027 launch) introduces new regulatory and consumer-protection exposure.
- Net: no active brand crisis comparable to Wells Fargo's scandals, but the compounding of Epstein, fee abuses, and ESG flip-flopping keeps reputational risk non-trivial.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **Moderate**

**Rationale:** No active DOJ/SEC investigation, no accounting-fraud indicators, and the largest recent regulatory overhang (CFPB + Zelle + Epstein) has *resolved or settled*. The offsetting live items — the OCC AML/Sanctions C&D, third-party data-breach pattern, CEO duality with rising shareholder dissent, and two-front political ESG exposure — are genuine but manageable, and trajectory is generally improving (early exit from CFPB monitoring, DOJ declination).

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Live OCC cease-and-desist (BSA/AML/sanctions)** — escalate to monitor remediation milestones and any move to impose monetary penalties; ties directly to the Merrill SAR-filing fine.
2. **Recurring third-party data breaches** — systemic vendor-risk-management gap; one large breach could trigger state AG/CFPB action and class litigation.
3. **Governance concentration:** Chairman+CEO duality with 32.6% independent-chair dissent and a SEC-mandated breakup vote — watch 2027 proxy season and any activist build-up.
4. **ESG political crossfire** — NZBA-exit investigation by Democratic lawmakers + conservative withdrawal pressure; monitor for state-level actions or federal financial regulator retaliation.
5. **Persistent CEO insider selling** (~33M shares/24 months, no buys) — currently routine; escalate if clusters appear near announcements or if 10b5-1 plans are amended.
