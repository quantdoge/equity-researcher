# Macroeconomic Environment Assessment — Bank of America (BAC)

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Risk-on/transitional.** The US is in a constructive-but-fragile configuration: the Fed has eased the funds rate ~108bps from 4.83% (Oct 2024) to **3.75% (Sep 2026)**, yet the long end has *backed up* — the 10Y UST sits at **5.24% (Oct 1, 2026)**, up from ~4.3% in April 2026. The curve is **positively sloped and steepening** (2Y 4.65% → 10Y 4.99% → 30Y 5.37%; 2Y-10Y spread +34bps, no inversion signal). Growth is solid (~2.2% YoY), unemployment is 4.2% (Sep 2026), but CPI has re-accelerated to **~3.1% YoY (Aug 2026)** from ~2% in late 2024. That sticky inflation is exactly why short-end easing has not translated into long-end relief — term premium and fiscal angst are doing the work.

This is a **"steepening curve" regime**: the Fed cuts at the short end while the market resists at the 10-30Y. For a money-center bank that funds short (deposits) and earns off long loans and capital markets, that skew is *net-interest-margin constructive* — BAC itself guides **NII growth of 5–7% FTE for 2026**.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **FAVOURABLE**

The regime — Fed easing at the short end, a steep positive curve, ~5% long yields, 2%+ real growth with ~3% inflation — is arguably the *best* macroeconomic configuration available to a diversified US money-center bank in this cycle: it maximizes net interest margin while keeping credit losses and funding costs in check. The rating is **Favourable, not Neutral**, because the NII machine is pointed in the right direction and BAC has guided to it. Offsets (multiple compression, credit normalization) are real but second-order.

## 5. Material-Surprise Scenario (Bear)

**"Stagflationary re-acceleration: the 10Y breaks 5.5%."** Suppose energy/tariff pass-through pushes CPI toward **4%** by H1 2027, forcing the Fed to *halt* easing just as deposit betas finish repricing down — while the 10Y UST climbs through **5.5%** and the 30Y approaches 6%. The curve steepens further (great for NIM on paper) but the economy pays: mortgage/CRE refinancing freezes, card delinquencies accelerate off their normalized base, and unemployment drifts toward 5%. With beta 1.16, BAC draws down harder than the sector; the equity story shifts from "NII growth" to "provisioning and CRE marks," compressing the multiple despite good net interest income. Trigger to watch: **CPI prints >3.5% for two consecutive months or a 10Y auction tail**. The bull case — long rates melting toward 4% on a soft landing — is partially priced in and would re-rate BAC sharply upward, but it is not the surprise; the 5.5% 10Y is.
