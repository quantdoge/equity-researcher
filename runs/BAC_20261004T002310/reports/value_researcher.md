# BAC — Bank of America Corporation
## Value Investment Analysis

*Price at analysis: ~$53.75–54.14 | Market cap: ~$382–406B (share count ~7.1–7.55B) | Sector: Diversified Banks (US "G-SIB" money-center)*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Wide (franchise-based), but not a pricing-power moat**

BAC is the second-largest US deposit franchise (~$2.0T deposits inside a $3.4T balance sheet), serving ~68M consumers/SMBs, with Merrill Lynch wealth and a top-tier global markets/corporate bank. The moat is real but is a **scale-and-switching-cost moat, not a pricing-power moat**:

- **Deposit franchise & funding advantage:** a sticky, low-cost core deposit base is the single most durable asset in US banking; it funds the balance sheet below market cost and is extraordinarily hard to replicate. This is the moat that matters, and it has **widened** over 5 years as BAC consolidated branches into a high-efficiency digital network (40M+ digital users).
- **Intangible assets:** brand, 240+ years of history, regulatory charters, and the fed/prime dealer + G-SIB franchise rights — valuable but shared with 7 peers; nothing proprietary or patented.
- **No network effect worth the name** in consumer banking; global-markets client relationships have moderate switching costs.
- **Test of moat — ROIC vs WACC:** bank-levered ROIC is ~5.6% and ROE ~10%, **below best-in-class peers (JPM ~15%+)**. The moat keeps BAC *in* the game at scale but has not produced excess returns above the cost of capital of a better-run competitor. The moat is **moderately wide, moderately durable**, and has neither widened nor narrowed materially.

Quality grade: **B+ business, held back by rate sensitivity (NII ≈ 45% of revenue) and sub-par fee income mix vs. JPM/MS.**

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **7 / 10**

- **Earnings trajectory (reported):** Net income $30.5B in FY2025 — an all-time record — vs. $17.9B (2020) → $32.0B (2021) → $27.5B (2022) → $26.5B (2023) → $27.1B (2024) → $30.5B (2025). Growing but **highly rate-cyclical**; Q1'26 EPS $1.11 (vs. $0.96 est.) and Q2'26 $1.21 (vs. $1.13 est.) show momentum above consensus.
- **Margin stability:** operating income margin 19.7% (FY25) vs. 15–27% in prior years — volatile, and the revenue/margin series is **not comparable across 2022→2023** (accounting/interest-presentation change). Read the series on operating income: $38.0B (2025) vs. $34.0B (2021) — steady, not explosive.
- **Cash conversion:** FCF/EBITDA-style ratios are **meaningless for a bank** (loan growth consumes operating cash; the tool's "FCF" swings from −$8.8B to +$45.0B purely from balance-sheet growth). Correct measure — owner earnings = net income + D&A − maintenance capex (near zero for a bank): **~$32.8B for 2025**, ≈108% of net income. Excellent conversion at the equity level.
- **Balance sheet:** $3.41T assets, $303B total equity, tangible book value **$40.18/share**, strong CET1 (est. ~11.5–12%) and liquidity. A **fortress**, post-GFC.
- **Capital return:** 2025 = $9.6B dividends + **$24.1B buybacks ≈ $33.7B = ~110% of net income**. Few large caps return more of their earnings.
- Deductions: earnings volatility, ROE below best-in-class, and a persistent valuation discount that reflects real returns quality.

**Score: 7/10** — strong balance sheet and capital generation; earnings quality marred by rate dependence and sub-consensus returns on equity capital.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Reject the tool's DCF input:** the FCF-based DCF ($240B intrinsic, i.e. ~$32/share vs. $53.75 price) undervalues BAC because it treats loan growth as a cash burn. Equity value in a bank must be measured on **normalized earnings power**.

**Owner-earnings valuation (this analyst's model):**
- Normalized through-cycle earnings power: **$29–31B** (5-yr avg of $28.7B; FY25 $30.5B; 2026 quarterly run-rate implies ~$4.4/share ≈ $31–32B, ahead of FY25's $3.82).
- At a 10% discount rate, 4% earnings growth for 10 years, 2.5% terminal growth → central intrinsic value **≈ $460–470B, ≈ $60–65/share**.
- Conservative case (3% growth, $28B base, 10.5% discount) → ≈ $380–390B, ≈ **$50–52/share**.
- **Central intrinsic value: ~$58–62/share.** Current price **$53.75–54** sits **~10–15% below** the central estimate — inside the fair-value band but **well short of my required 20–30% margin of safety**.

**Market-relative check:** trailing P/E **14.1x** (≈12x forward on 2026 consensus); P/B **1.34x**; dividend yield **2.2%**; earnings yield ≈ **7.5%+**. That is cheaper than JPM (~19x forward), roughly in line with WFC, richer than Citi — appropriate for a bank with BAC's ROE (10% vs JPM ~15%). Analyst targets cluster **$62–70** (recent Evercore cut to $62 from $67).

**Implied growth at $53.75:** the price capitalizes ~7.5% current owner earnings — you get paid to wait even at ~0% real growth. The question is not "is it a growth stock" but "is 12–15% off intrinsic enough for the cycle risk."

**Verdict on price:** approximately fair, modestly cheap on generous rate/credit assumptions. **No margin of safety to speak of.**

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good (conservative stewards)**

- **Brian Moynihan (CEO since 2010):** one of the better capital allocators in large-cap banking — post-2008 clean-up, relentless expense discipline (efficiency ratio ~56–58%), **no empire building** (divested/shrunk non-core global markets franchises), and returned **>$100B** to shareholders. Buyback track record: $25.1B (2021), $5.1B (2023), $13.1B (2024), **$24.1B (2025)** — countercyclical and value-aware.
- **Succession:** Dean Atkinson (30-year insider, head of Global Banking) named next CEO for 2026 — continuity risk is low, unlike an external hire.
- **Caveats:** insider ownership is nominal (standard for a mega-bank; management does not eat its own cooking in the Buffett sense); prolonged sub-15% ROE is itself a capital-allocation critique — retained earnings would compound faster at better-returning peers.

**Management: 7.5/10.**

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

This is a well-run, boring, capital-returning giant at **roughly fair value**. It fails the Buffett tests on two counts:
- **No margin of safety** — ~10–15% under intrinsic, not the required 20–30%.
- **Not a compounding moat machine** — 10% ROE and heavy rate sensitivity mean the owner earns a *yield*, not an *edge*.

It is a sensible **income/yield holding** (7%+ owner-earnings yield, 2.2% dividend growing ~8–9%/yr, fortress capital) for a 5–10 year horizon — and the recent 2026 quarterly beats plus a $4.4ish EPS run-rate argue the intrinsic side is closer to $60+. But **Strong Buy / Buy requires the price to break toward the $40s** or a demonstrable ROE inflector. Long-term investors holding BAC should hold; new money should wait for a cycle dip or the post-Basel resolution.

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Rate-cycle peak:** BAC is among the *most* rate-sensitive majors; every Fed cut compresses NII (~45% of revenue). The 2024–26 earnings surge is partly a rate gift, partly real.
2. **Credit cycle:** card net charge-offs are rising; office-CRE exposure is above several peers as a % of the book; consumer leverage is at post-2010 highs.
3. **Regulatory capital (Basel III Endgame)** still unresolved — a punitive outcome taxes the very capital-return program that underpins the thesis.
4. **CEO transition execution risk** (Moynihan → Atkinson) into a softening rate environment.
5. **Structural multiple cap:** BAC has traded at a persistent 15–40% discount to JPM for 15 years because of ROE; do not underwrite a re-rating.
6. **Data caveats:** revenue/margin series discontinuity (2022→2023 accounting change) and cash-flow artifacts render headline "FCF" metrics unusable for discipline; all conclusions rest on normalized earnings power, which itself is rate-dependent.
