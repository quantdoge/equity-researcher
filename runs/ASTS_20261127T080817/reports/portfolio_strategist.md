## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT** (bearish consensus near-term; no consensus at all on the horizon that matters)

**Where agents agree (High agreement):**
1. **Launch cadence — not demand — is the binding constraint**, and it is outside the company's control. Sector, Alt Data, Launch and ESG all converge on this independently. It is the single most important variable and the least observable.
2. **The near-term is bad.** 0/7 EPS beat record, a Q4 bar requiring +78% QoQ, a March 2027 print, dilution, and a securities suit. **Not one agent has a constructive 1–2 quarter view.**
3. **Dilution is the primary per-share value leak**, not ESG, not demand. ESG, Sector, Credit and Macro all name it.

**Where agents disagree (genuine Split):**
- **Sector vs. Company.** The Sector Researcher rates the *sector* Attractive but explicitly warns "do not conflate this with rating ASTS attractive." The sector's attractiveness accrues to the self-launching incumbent. This is the most important disagreement in the pack and it resolves *against* ASTS.
- **Sentiment vs. Fundamentals.** The Alt Data + PT cluster (upgrade, insider buy, "rocket risk priced in") is a **capitulation marker** — but as the Alt Data Analyst says plainly, "it is a *sentiment* signal, not a fundamental one." The fundamental agents all say the same thing: no evidence of a beat.
- **Terminal value vs. survivability.** The bulls ($45–98 PTs) underwrite 2031 fleet economics. The bears underwrite the next four quarters. **Both are right.** The question for a portfolio is which one binds first.

**Conflict resolution:** The Geo/Legal fraud-litigation flag and the Launch/Execution slip take precedence over the value/sentiment case. A thesis that requires flawless 2027 execution from a company that has missed every EPS estimate for seven quarters, is being sued over capital-adequacy disclosure, and does not control its own launch, cannot be underwritten on a 12-month view — only as an option.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **0% now — PASS to initiate**

**Base case: 0.0% of portfolio. Do not initiate.**

**Kelly check (why this is not a "small buy"):** Bull $50 (40%), base $26 (35%), bear $11 (25%) → expected value **$31.9 (+23%)**. Win +92% / loss −58%, b = 1.586. Full Kelly = 0.40 − (0.60/1.586) = **2.2%**. That is the *theoretical* ceiling for a perfectly calibrated forecaster. Given (a) a 0/7 beat record, (b) binary launch dependency, (c) a March catalyst that is more likely negative than positive, and (d) an 82% realised drawdown, I haircut to **half-Kelly at most, and further discount for estimation error → 0.5%**.

**Why the base case is still zero, not 0.5%:** there is **no cost to waiting**. The stock is in a −38%/four-week downtrend, sitting 16% above its 52-week low, with its next hard catalyst (Q4/FY26 print, ~1 Mar 2027) likely to be a miss. A company that must raise equity at an 80% drawdown will probably give you a better entry *after* the print or *after* the next raise. Paying up for optionality you can buy later at the same or lower price is a negative-expectation trade.

**If the mandate requires an expression (venture/optionality sleeve only):**
- **Initial: 0.5%** of portfolio — and only *after* the Q4 print clears or a launch-cadence datapoint confirms. **Hard cap 1.0%** including all adds.
- **Risk contribution at 0.5%:** ~0.53% portfolio vol and ~1.65% portfolio beta. At 1.0%: ~1.06% vol and ~3.3% beta — i.e. **1% of capital buys you the beta of a 3.3% index position.** That is the real cost and it is why the cap is 1.0%.

## 6. Risk Management Rules

**Stop-loss:** For a 0.5% position, a conventional 15% stop is **2.2 daily sigma** (daily vol ≈ 6.7%) and will be triggered by noise — it is not a real stop. Use either:
- **Hard price stop at $17.50** (−33% from current, ~1.5x the November range) — limits the loss to ~0.17% of portfolio; **or**
- **No price stop at all** — size the position so a total loss is acceptable (0.5% of capital is within most risk budgets) and manage on fundamental triggers only. **This is the cleaner approach for a binary asset.** A 33% stop on a 106%-vol name is cosmetic.

**Thesis invalidation (exit on any one — these are the real stop):**
1. 45-satellite deployment slips **beyond Q3 2027** (currently guided early/mid-2027).
2. **No commercial service activation** in the first-wave countries by end-H1 2027 — this is the pattern the class action litigates.
3. Any equity raise **>20% of shares outstanding** at a discount, or a convertible issued on worse terms than the 1.625% 2034s.
4. **Loss of an anchor MNO** (AT&T, Verizon or Vodafone) or evidence of MNO dual-sourcing away from ASTS.
5. **Adverse FCC action** on the 248-satellite conditional grant, or the Senate sole-source >$100M restriction surviving conference and impairing SB-AMTI.
6. Cash below **$1.0B** without committed financing, or a covenant breach.
7. Adverse class-action outcome or any restatement of capital/liquidity disclosures.

**Trim trigger:**
- **Sell 1/3 at $45** — the fresh consensus cluster ($43–48) and ~73% above current. This is also where the sector's "75x EV/Sales" premium begins to be indefensible on sub-scale revenue.
- **Sell 1/3 at $65** — B. Riley's prior target, ~90% of the *lowest* defensible intrinsic value in the pack.
- **Trailing stop on the remainder** after the first trim.
- **Hard rule:** if a further raise is announced, **do not average down.** Dilution is the identified value leak; adding to it is the error the specialists uniformly warn against.
