## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Not a carbon story.** No disclosed Scope 1/2/3 inventory, no science-based targets, no CDP submission located. Absence of disclosure is a reporting gap, not a large emissions exposure — launch cadence of ~45–100 satellites is a trivial emissions base versus revenue.
- **Orbital debris is the real E-risk.** The BlueBird 7 New Glenn mission (April 2026) placed the satellite in an off-nominal orbit; it powered on but was unrecoverable and deorbited. This is a live demonstration that launch-partner failure creates both asset loss and debris-management obligations. Company states satellites deorbit within 5 years of retirement, are designed to burn up completely, and have a 10-year design life — compliant on its face with the FCC 5-year rule. It also filed passive-deorbit collision-probability analysis with the FCC (June 2025).
- **Brightness.** ASTS is among the higher-profile bright-satellite operators; it has an NSF coordination agreement, array coatings, attitude steering, altitude ≤700km, and radio-astronomy beam avoidance. Credible programme, but this is a genuine licence-to-operate tail risk with academic pressure building ("limits of orbital regulation" literature).
- **Physical climate risk.** Cape Canaveral launch dependence (hurricane season) and Midland TX manufacturing (hail/wind). Moderate, insurable, and already partly manifest as schedule slippage.
- **Controversies:** no spills, pollution fines, or remediation liabilities identified.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Strong positive product impact**, and it is commercially load-bearing: AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Rakuten, Taiwan Mobile partnerships plus a US Government contract. Impact is not a marketing overlay here.
- **Rapid scaling risk.** Headcount ~1,126 against a manufacturing ramp from single-digit to a targeted 6 satellites/month by December 2026. Manufacturing and launch-integration work is inherently hazardous; no fatality or serious-injury event was identified in our searches, but safety management systems are being stress-tested at speed.
- **Supply chain / geopolitical.** Launch dependency is the dominant social-operational risk: the campaign slipped to early 2027, and vertical integration ("Blacklift," late-2028 target) is a high-capex, high-execution-risk response. Component sourcing and any China-linked content would attract CFIUS/DoD scrutiny given government contracts — no adverse finding located, but disclosure is thin.
- **Weak S metrics disclosure.** No EEO-1/D&I data, no turnover, no union status disclosure, no supply-chain labour code published. Median employee pay implies a ~103:1 CEO ratio.
- **Governance-adjacent S:** heavy workforce churn risk in a −65% stock environment where equity is a large share of comp (non-CEO base salaries only $250k).

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10**

**Negatives (dominant):**
- **71.6% voting control** in founder Chairman & CEO Abel Avellan via super-voting Class C — CEO/Chairman duality plus control. Nominating and Compensation Committees need not be independent under controlled-company exemptions.
- **Board:** 10 directors in an 11-seat structure (one vacancy); strategic investors (AT&T Ventures, Rakuten/Mikitani) sit on the board under a Stockholders' Agreement, entangling commercial partners with oversight.
- **Disclosure credibility:** securities fraud class action, class period Mar 4 2025 – Jul 15 2026, alleging material misstatements/omissions on **capital and liquidity position** and on the frequency of debt load and dilution; stock fell 17.04% ($11.30/share) on July 16, 2026. Lead-plaintiff deadline Nov 13, 2026. Say-on-pay's 99.2% approval is largely an artefact of super-voting control and carries little signalling value.
- **Capital markets behaviour:** serial equity issuance plus $1.0B converts in nine months; repeated downward revisions to launch timing.

**Positives (partial offset):**
- Independent audit committee (Torres chair, Rubin, Wibergh); KPMG auditor.
- Clawback policy adopted per Nasdaq rules; ~50% of equity in PSUs tied to operational/financial/capital-raising goals — reasonable alignment, though "capital-raising" metrics arguably incentivise the very dilution at issue.
- Director open-market purchase of ~$500k (Nov 2026) — genuine skin-in-the-game signal at multi-year lows.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **FCC (highest):** SCS commercial authorization (248 satellites, Apr 2026), 5-year deorbit rule, orbital debris and conjunction reporting, RF-interference and MPE limits, leased MNO low-band spectrum (700/800 MHz from AT&T/Verizon). Licence withdrawal or condition tightening is the single largest regulatory tail.
- **ITU / NSF / NASA:** orbital filings, brightness coordination, Space Act Agreement and CARA conjunction procedures.
- **National security:** US Government contracts imply ITAR/EAR export controls and CFIUS sensitivity to foreign strategic shareholders (Rakuten, Vodafone).
- **Non-US telecom licensing:** Japan (Rakuten JV, Nov 2026), Taiwan (NCC via Taiwan Mobile), EU member states via Vodafone — country-by-country market-access risk.
- **EU CSRD / SEC climate rule:** currently **immaterial**. De minimis EU revenue today; SEC climate rule stayed. If EU revenue scales via Vodafone, CSRD value-chain (Scope 3) pull-through becomes relevant in FY2028+, requiring an emissions inventory the company does not yet publish.
- **CBAM:** not applicable.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** **none** — converts at 1.625% carry no sustainability margin ratchets, so no ESG covenant or step-up risk to the capital structure. A genuine positive.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD**
*(Governance-driven; environmental and social factors are not the binding constraint.)*

ASTS is not a sustainability laggard in impact terms — its core product is one of the more credible digital-inclusion stories in telecom. It is a laggard in **stewardship**: 71.6% founder voting control, CEO/Chairman duality, controlled-company committee exemptions, no formal sustainability report or emissions inventory, and an active securities fraud class action specifically about capital and liquidity disclosure. The governance discount is real and it is being priced.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL for the thesis (negative tilt on cost of capital)**

- **Not the swing factor.** The thesis is launch cadence, FCC-authorized constellation build-out, and MNO economics. ESG neither invalidates nor validates it.
- **Governance suppresses the multiple.** Founder control enables fast, decisive capex (arguably right for a first-mover build-out) but removes minority checks on dilution — and dilution, not ESG, is the main per-share risk here. Expect a structural 10–20% governance discount and a higher cost of equity than peers.
- **Capital exclusion is real.** Controlled-company structure plus no quantified ESG disclosure screens ASTS out of many ESG-mandated and EU SFDR Article 8/9 mandates — a persistent marginal-buyer headwind, not a thesis-breaker.
- **What would improve the rating:** (i) majority-independent board and an independent chair, or Avellan sunset of Class C super-voting; (ii) published SASB-indexed sustainability report with Scope 1/2/3 and orbital-debris/conjunction metrics; (iii) clean resolution of the class action; (iv) sustained launch cadence to early 2027 target, which restores disclosure credibility faster than any ESG initiative.
