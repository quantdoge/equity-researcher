# AST SpaceMobile, Inc. (ASTS) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price: $26.02 | Market cap: ~$10.7B (reported) / ~$7.8B on 300M diluted shares | EV: ~$9–12B | Sector: Communication Services**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat Rating: NONE established (aspirational "Narrow")**

ASTS is building a space-based cellular broadband network designed to connect directly to unmodified smartphones — no dish, no terminal. Revenue is real but tiny ($142.9M TTM) against a ~$12B enterprise value. The business is a capital-intensive infrastructure build-out, not yet an operating business.

What could become a moat:
- **Intangible assets:** A large patent portfolio around large-aperture phased-array direct-to-device technology, plus S-band/nearby spectrum access and regulatory filings.
- **Carrier relationships:** Commercial agreements with AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Google, Samsung and others — these are genuine distribution advantages and hard to replicate quickly.
- **Efficient scale (theoretical):** Orbital slots and usable spectrum are finite, and a working D2D network would be a natural near-monopoly in unserved geographies.

Why I cannot call it a moat today:
- **The moat is being contested before it exists.** SpaceX's Starlink Direct-to-Cell is already operating commercially with T-Mobile on a constellation orders of magnitude larger. ASTS is attempting to build the same thing with a fraction of the capital and launch cadence.
- **No pricing power evidence.** Gross margin swung from −1,334% (2024) to +53% (2025) to −68% in Q4'25 and back to +40% in Q3'26. That is not the margin stability of a moated business; it is the lumpiness of a company still learning to operate its own hardware.
- **No returns on capital.** ROIC has been negative every year: −39.1% (2022), −77.6% (2023), −28.8% (2024), −6.2% (2025). The 2025 "improvement" is denominator-driven — capital employed ballooned — not a business improvement. **The single defining test of a moat, ROIC > WACC, is failed by a wide margin.**

**Conclusion:** The technological ambition is real and the addressable market is enormous. But a moat is demonstrated by durable excess returns, and ASTS has never earned a positive return on capital. I rate it **no moat** — an option on a moat, not a moat.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Intrinsic value: not reliably estimable. The current price embeds assumptions more aggressive than the risk profile supports.**

Traditional multiples are meaningless or negative: P/E −19.4x, EV/EBITDA −17.8x, P/FCF −5.9x, FCF yield −17.1%. A DCF on negative FCF produces a nonsensical output, so I will not pretend otherwise. The honest method is to ask **what the price implies**, then judge whether that is reasonable.

**What $12B of EV requires.** At a 12% WACC and 3% terminal growth, a perpetuity needs roughly **$1.05B of steady-state free cash flow** to justify $12B. At a generous 25% FCF margin, that is **~$4.2B of revenue** — roughly 30x today's TTM revenue. Even at a 40% FCF margin, you need ~$2.6B of revenue.

**The Iridium reality check.** Iridium (IRDM) operates a fully built, profitable 66-satellite LEO constellation. It trades at **$6.3B EV, 14.3x EV/EBITDA, 15.5x P/FCF, 40.5x P/E, with a 1.4% dividend.** For ASTS to be worth today's ~$12B EV at Iridium's 14.3x EBITDA multiple, it needs **~$840M of EBITDA** — at a 40% margin, about **$2.1B of revenue**, or 15x current levels. At a more generous 20x EBITDA, it needs ~$600M EBITDA / ~$1.5B revenue. And that is *before* further dilution, which is certain.

Globalstar (GSAT) at ~$11.0B EV and 129.8x EV/EBITDA and Viasat (VSAT) at 7.3x EV/EBITDA bracket the range: ASTS is priced at the **optimistic end of a sector where the profitable operator trades at 14x**.

**Peer comparison summary:** The reported "sector peer median P/E" of 23.5x is drawn from Alphabet, Meta, Netflix and AT&T — not remotely comparable businesses. The relevant comps are IRDM, GSAT and VSAT above. On revenue, ASTS trades at **~63–84x TTM sales**; on EBITDA it has no multiple at all. **No margin of safety exists, and none can be computed.**

**Analyst targets are a lagging indicator, not a value anchor.** Recent targets: B.Riley $48 (upgraded to Buy, Nov 19), BofA $45, UBS $43, Scotiabank $55.70, Piper $54 (Neutral), Berenberg $92, Deutsche Bank $93. Note the trajectory: DB went $106 → $93, UBS $80 → $78 → $43, Piper $100 → $98 → $54. The targets are falling faster than the stock. Every one of them is a price-target on a story, not a valuation of cash flows.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Founder-led and aligned, but with a credibility deficit on execution and a heavy dilution record.**

- **Alignment:** Abel Avellan is founder, Chairman and CEO, with control via Class B super-voting shares. He is an owner-operator in the literal sense — genuinely invested, technically credible, and building something he has pursued for over a decade. This is a positive.
- **Capital allocation:** The record is "raise and spend." Diluted shares went from 54.4M (2022) to 300.1M (Q3'26) — **5.5x dilution in under four years**, and total shares outstanding now appear to be ~410M including Class B/C. The company has moved from equity to **$4.1B of convertible debt**, adding ~$121M of annual interest to a business with $143M of revenue. No dividends, no buybacks. For a build-out this is defensible in principle, but the cost of capital is rising while the revenue ramp keeps slipping.
- **Execution:** Revenue missed consensus in three of the last four quarters — Q1'26 by 62%. EPS missed badly each time (−$0.66 vs −$0.255; −$0.77 vs −$0.315; −$0.44 vs −$0.404). Cost overruns, not demand, are driving the misses. Schedule credibility is the single most important management attribute for a company like this, and it is currently impaired.
- **Governance:** Super-voting control means shareholders cannot meaningfully check capital allocation. That is a structural negative for a company requiring repeated external financing.

**Verdict on management: honest intent, strong alignment, poor recent execution and a dilutive funding model.** I would not call it a red flag on integrity; I would call it a yellow flag on discipline and forecasting.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

This is not a "bad company" call — it is a "not an investment at this price" call. ASTS fails every gate in my framework:

| Gate | Result |
|---|---|
| Within circle of competence | **No** — binary technology/regulatory outcome |
| Durable moat (ROIC > WACC) | **No** — ROIC negative every year |
| Predictable revenue | **No** — 0 → $4.4M → $70.9M → lumpy quarterly misses |
| Positive free cash flow | **No** — −$1.14B in 2025, ~−$1.4B annualized |
| Conservative balance sheet | **No** — $4.1B debt, $1.3B net debt, rising interest |
| Margin of safety | **Not computable** — no positive cash flow to discount |

The stock is down ~80% from its May 2026 peak near $133, and the temptation is to call that a bargain. **It is not.** A 80% decline from a speculative peak is not a margin of safety; it is a repricing toward reality. At $26 the company still carries ~$12B of enterprise value against $143M of revenue and negative $655M of EBITDA. Even a *successful* outcome — a fully built, profitable constellation earning Iridium-like multiples — may not support today's price without further dilution.

**What would change my mind:** several consecutive quarters of revenue at or above guidance, positive gross margin sustained above 40%, capex visibly peaking, and a clear path to positive operating cash flow — with a price that discounts that future at a 20–30% margin of safety. None of those conditions exist today. I would revisit after the capex peak, not before.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to my bearish/avoid stance (what would make me wrong):**
1. **Launch execution.** Successful BlueBird launches on schedule could drive a rapid revenue inflection toward $500M+ and re-rate the equity violently.
2. **Strategic capital.** A hyperscaler or Tier-1 carrier taking a large stake at a premium (Google is already an investor) would validate the technology and fund the build without public dilution.
3. **Competitive failure by SpaceX in D2C**, or a spectrum/regulatory outcome that favors ASTS's architecture.
4. **Short-squeeze dynamics.** Beta of 2.85 and a heavily shorted, retail-owned float mean price can detach from fundamentals in either direction. I would not short this.

**Risks to owning it (the bear case):**
1. **Starlink Direct-to-Cell** achieving scale and carrier lock-in first, permanently capping ASTS's addressable market.
2. **Continued dilution at depressed prices** — the most probable outcome given ~$1.4B annual burn and $2.8B of cash, which funds roughly two years before another raise.
3. **Debt service and refinancing risk** on $4.1B of converts with the equity down 80%.
4. **Launch or manufacturing failure** — a single catastrophic launch event is an existential, not merely financial, risk.
5. **Regulatory and national-security risk** across dozens of jurisdictions.
