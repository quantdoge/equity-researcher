## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US regulatory/licensing and spectrum-policy dependency (Probability: Medium-High | Impact: Very High)
ASTS's commercial existence rests on FCC action. In April 2026 the FCC granted, **with conditions**, authority for the full 248-satellite constellation and SCS commercial authorization using partner MNO spectrum (700/800 MHz low-band with Verizon/AT&T). That is a major de-risking, but three live vectors remain:
- **Competitive re-regulation.** On July 10, 2026, FCC Chairman Brendan Carr publicly emphasized "intensifying multi-player competition" in direct-to-device — citing SpaceX/T-Mobile and **Amazon's absorption of Globalstar's MSS spectrum into Project Leo**. ASTS shares fell sharply on the remarks (SatNews, 7/11/26). Regulatory framing that favours larger incumbents is a genuine, recurring headline risk.
- **WRC-27 spectrum diplomacy.** The US has finalized its 20 agenda-item positions ahead of WRC-27; international S-band/IMT allocations are contested, and China is actively pushing spectrum and orbital positions (CSIS). Outcomes could raise ASTS's cost of international market entry.
- **SB-AMTI sole-source politics.** Senate appropriators have moved to **bar sole-source awards over $100M for Space Data Network and SB-AMTI launch** — directly touching ASTS's ≈$100M Space Force SB-AMTI award.

### Risk 2 — US–China direct-to-device / LEO competition and militarised space (Probability: Medium | Impact: High)
China's **Qianfan/SpaceSail** constellation completed China's first direct-to-cell demonstration in June 2026 and is scaling toward Starlink-scale deployment. ASTS's differentiation is its large ~2,400 sq ft phased arrays and MNO spectrum partnerships — but it competes for the same scarce resources: **orbital slots, spectrum, and launch capacity**. Secondary effects: export-control tightening on space tech, and pressure on US/allied procurement to favour "sovereign" or trusted-vendor networks. Japan's J-LEO decision was explicitly framed as building **sovereign** D2D infrastructure against Starlink — favourable to ASTS, but the same logic cuts both ways if allies choose national champions.

### Risk 3 — Foreign-partner and foreign-investment screening exposure (Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium-High)
ASTS's growth model is a web of foreign MNO and state-linked JVs: **Rakuten** (Japan), **Vodafone** (UK), plus strategic equity from Vodafone and Google. As a US defence supplier holding a **DPAS priority rating** on a dual-use satellite programme, foreign shareholding and JV structures invite CFIUS/EAR/ITAR scrutiny, technology-transfer conditions in the Rakuten JV, and potential restrictions on non-US persons accessing US government mission payload data. No adverse action is currently disclosed, but the combination — DPAS-rated defence work + foreign JV partners + large rocket-engine development (Blacklift) — is precisely the profile that attracts mitigation agreements and licensing delays.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active securities class action (material, ongoing).**
- **Court:** W.D. Texas. **Class period:** March 4, 2025 – July 15, 2026. **Lead plaintiff deadline:** November 13, 2026 (now passed). Multiple firms competing for lead counsel (Rosen, Kessler Topaz, Pomerantz, Kaplan Fox, Robbins, Zeldis).
- **Allegations:** materially false/misleading statements regarding AST's capital and liquidity position — specifically that growing capital needs would require more debt and more frequent, larger equity raises; the complaint also alleges exaggeration of cash sufficiency. The corrective disclosure event is **July 15, 2026**, tied to an announced securities offering and associated disclosures (Robbins LLP, PR Newswire 11/12–11/13/26).
- **Assessment:** Standard post-offering securities litigation. With a ~79% drawdown from October 2026 highs and a $10.7B market cap, claimed damages could be large, though these cases typically settle for a fraction. Expect 2–4 years of overhang and rising D&O cost. **Not yet accrued at a material level as far as disclosed.**

**Regulatory authorisations secured (positive):**
- FCC: 248-satellite constellation + SCS commercial authorization (April 2026, conditional).
- Brazil: commercial service authorization with combined 50 MHz S-band/low-band.
- Japan: MIC J-LEO selection; ~$1.0B non-dilutive funding, disbursed over ~3 years via CIAJ.
- US: **DPAS priority rating** granted for the dual-use programme — a powerful but double-edged government lever.

**Litigation/IP:** I found no disclosed patent litigation or government investigation (DOJ/SEC/FTC). Note the **$125.9M "loss on involuntary conversion"** recorded in Q2 2026 — an insurance/asset-loss event (likely satellite or facility related) that warrants verification in the full 10-Q; it is not a legal contingency but signals operational risk concentration.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating

# **HIGH**

**Rationale.** On a pure jurisdictional basis ASTS looks benign — US-domiciled, near-100% US-sourced revenue, no sanctioned-market exposure. The rating is driven instead by **concentration and permission-dependency**:

1. **Revenue is one customer class in one country** (US government + gateway deliveries) against a $10.7B valuation that prices multi-continent commercial success.
2. **Every revenue dollar forward requires a sovereign permission** — FCC conditions, MIC/CIAJ subsidy milestones, Brazilian authorisation, WRC-27 spectrum outcomes. This is a licence-to-operate business before it is a telecom business.
3. **The regulatory referee is also the competition regulator** for SpaceX, Amazon/Globalstar and T-Mobile — and has recently talked up competitive parity.
4. **Live securities litigation** over capital-adequacy disclosure, in a company that raised equity in July 2026 and carries $3.2B cash with a $150–200M revenue guide.
5. **Dual-use defence posture** (DPAS, SB-AMTI, SDA) brings export-control, foreign-ownership and congressional-appropriations risk that most satellite telecom peers do not carry.

**Key swing factors to monitor:** (a) whether year-end 2026 "intermittent nationwide coverage" actually activates in the US, Canada, Japan, Saudi Arabia and UK — a slip reconfirms the pattern litigated in the class action; (b) the Rakuten/AST J-LEO JV's technology-transfer terms; (c) whether the Senate sole-source restriction on >$100M SB-AMTI awards survives conference; (d) FCC treatment of the Amazon/Globalstar and SpaceX spectrum positions; (e) WRC-27 S-band outcomes.
