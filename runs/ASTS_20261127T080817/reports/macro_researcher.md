## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-off / late-cycle reflation, hostile to long-duration growth**

The US economy as of late November 2026 is in an unusual and, for ASTS, toxic configuration: **growth is re-accelerating while inflation is re-accelerating, and the Fed has flipped from cutting to hiking.** Real GDP rose to $24,665bn in Q3 2026 (+2.3% YoY, ~4.3% QoQ annualized) while headline PCE re-accelerated to 3.8% (core 3.3%) and CPI to ~3.8% YoY — up from ~2.65% in December 2025. The FOMC held at 3.75–4.00% on 28 October, and markets now price a ~97% probability of a 25bp **hike** at the December meeting. The long end has repriced violently on term premium/fiscal supply rather than Fed expectations: the 10Y has moved from 4.44% (30 June) to 5.13% today, having peaked at 5.31% on 5 October, with the 30Y at 5.47% and 2s10s at +39bp (bear steepening).

Credit is the one genuinely supportive pillar: HY OAS at 3.72% is still near the tight end of history, though it has widened ~107bp from the 2.65% cycle tights of 11 September. In short: **narrow, expensive equity leadership; the discount rate is rising faster than earnings; and speculative, cash-consuming growth stories are the funding source of choice for the market's de-risking.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact (ASTS)

ASTS is the purest **long-duration asset** in the market: $10.7bn market cap, $42.3M of Q3 2026 revenue, beta 2.85, and a business plan that only reaches scale in 2031+. Roughly 85–90% of intrinsic value sits in post-2031 cash flows. It behaves like a levered, 18–20-year zero-coupon bond with an equity option attached.

**A. Discount rate — strongly negative.** The +69bp move in the 10Y since 30 June mechanically cuts a ~18-year-duration DCF by roughly 12–18%. ASTS fell 70.7% over the same window ($88.86 → $26.02; −80.5% from the $133.09 peak on 28 May). **Macro explains perhaps a third of that**; the balance is idiosyncratic — launch cadence slippage at Cape Canaveral, the 9 October −19% single-day break on 54m shares, SpaceX competitive overhang, and dilution fear. Crucially, the correlation runs one way: the stock does not rally when yields fall (10Y fell 47bp between 5–20 October and ASTS still lost 55%), but it is defenseless when yields rise.

**B. Financing / refinancing — a genuine mitigant, but dilutive marginal capital.** Long-term debt is $4.08bn, including $1.15bn of **1.625% converts due 2034** — cheap, fixed, long-dated, low near-term refinancing risk — against $3.2bn of cash. That is a real buffer. The problem is *forward* capex: 45 satellites by mid-2027 and 100+ thereafter must be funded. With a 5.13% risk-free rate and elevated equity risk premium, the cost of equity is ~11–12%; every dollar raised at $26 (versus $100+ eight months ago) is roughly 4x as dilutive per dollar. Widening HY spreads (3.72% and rising) directly raise the price of the convert issuance that funds the constellation — ASTS's most important macro transmission channel.

**C. Inflation on cost structure — negative, no offsetting pricing power.** Satellite manufacturing in Midland, TX is labour- and component-intensive (1,126 employees); 4%+ wage inflation plus semiconductor/phased-array input costs flow straight to cash burn. With $42M revenue and no pricing power, ASTS cannot pass through input inflation. Tariff exposure on imported components adds a further unhedged cost line.

**D. Currency — modestly negative.** Revenue is contracted with international MNOs (Vodafone, Rakuten, Google alongside AT&T/Verizon); a broad dollar at 123.6, +4.8% in ten weeks, is a translation headwind, and a strong dollar tightens offshore-currency liquidity for partners' capital commitments, which delays revenue recognition at the margin.

**E. Policy — the one potential positive.** ASTS has no cyclical GDP sensitivity, but it is levered to *policy*, not the cycle: FCC spectrum/SCS authorisation, DoD and government contract awards, and industrial policy. In a fiscal-expansion, defence-heavy budget environment, government demand is a genuine tailwind that is orthogonal to the rate shock.

**Variable scorecard:** GDP growth **neutral** (no earnings linkage); CPI **negative** (costs, not price); Fed funds **negative** (hikes priced, high cost of equity); 10Y **strongly negative**; HY spreads **negative**; USD **mildly negative**; credit availability **neutral-positive** (markets still functioning); fiscal/defence policy **positive**.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

This is close to the worst macro regime obtainable for this specific business model: rising growth is irrelevant to a company with no cyclical revenue; rising inflation raises costs it cannot pass on; and a rising term-premium-driven long end attacks 85%+ of its valuation directly while simultaneously inflating the cost of the capital it is structurally forced to raise. The mitigants — $3.2bn cash, $1.15bn of 1.625% 2034 converts, functioning credit markets, policy-driven government demand — prevent this from being an existential macro problem. But the beta of 2.85 is not decorative: in a 5%+ long-bond world, ASTS is the first asset sold and the last bought, and it needs a *macro regime change*, not merely good launch news, to re-rate durably.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario

**Base case is bearish macro with a near-certain December hike (97% priced) — the surprise is skewed to the downside on a 10Y break above 5.5%.** If December's hike is accompanied by hawkish dots and Q4 CPI prints above 4%, the 30Y pushing through 5.75% and HY OAS through 450bp would shut the convertible window that funds the constellation. ASTS would then face a forced equity raise into a $20–22 stock, and with implied duration of ~18 years the equity could lose another 30–40% *with no change in launch cadence*. The tell: the 10Y already tested 5.31% on 5 October, and the stock's $23.27 low on 20 November was set while the 10Y was actually *falling* — technical, not fundamental, tolerance for this name is nearly exhausted.

**Bull tail (less likely, but high-magnitude):** The Hill notes October's PCE was partly energy-driven; if Q4 CPI rolls toward 3.0% and the December hike proves terminal, term premium compresses and the 10Y returns to 4.4%. On ~18-year duration that is a mechanical +15–18% from rates alone; layered on 2.85 beta and short interest, ASTS could retrace $45–60 within a quarter on macro alone.
