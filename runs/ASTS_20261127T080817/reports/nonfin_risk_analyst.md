# AST SpaceMobile, Inc. (NASDAQ: ASTS) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment

**Assessment date:** late November 2026 | **Price:** ~$23–26 | **Market cap:** ~$10.7B | **Sector:** Communication Services / Telecom | **Employees:** 1,126 | **Beta:** 2.85

**Context:** Pre-revenue-at-scale D2D satellite operator, down ~64% YTD 2026 and ~78–79% from its 2026 high, following launch-timeline slippage, a third $1.0B convertible raise, an in-house rocket program (Blacklift), and a securities class action. Non-financial risk is materially elevated and *is* the investment case here.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (but with two structural flaws)**

**Evidence supporting Adequate:**
- **10 director nominees** (2026 proxy, meeting June 12, 2026); ~8 of 10 independent (Cisneros, Ibbetson, Knapp, Larson, Rubin, Sarnoff, Torres, Wibergh); non-independent: Avellan (CEO) and Johnson (CFO/CLO).
- **Lead Independent Director** exists and is functioning: Julio A. Torres, who also chairs the Audit Committee and qualifies as an audit committee financial expert (as does Ronald Rubin, CPA/telecom CFO).
- **Genuine sector expertise on the board:** Knapp (ex-CTO, American Tower), Wibergh (ex-CTO, Vodafone), Ibbetson (Vodafone R&D), Sarnoff (KKR media/telecom) — relevant, not ornamental.
- **Compensation aligned:** CEO total comp $14.2M for 2025, **100% equity, zero base salary**, split 50% time-based RSU / 50% performance-based PSU. 99.2% say-on-pay support. This is unusually shareholder-aligned for a founder-led company.
- No director received a "against" recommendation on attendance/overboarding grounds.

**Flaws (evidence of concentration of power):**
- **CEO/Chairman duality:** Abel Avellan is Founder, Chairman **and** CEO — confirmed in his signed SOX 302 certification on the 10-Q dated Nov 9, 2026. Mitigated only by a Lead Independent Director, not eliminated.
- **CFO also sits on the board** (Andrew M. Johnson, CFO *and* Chief Legal Officer *and* director). Combined CFO+CLO+director is an unusual triple concentration of control over financial reporting, legal disclosure and board oversight — precisely the function the class action targets.
- **Founder ownership:** Avellan holds ~235.0M shares (10% owner), plus a 2.5M-share prepaid forward (below). Founder control plus dual role limits the board's practical ability to overrule him.
- **Immaterial but notable:** one board vacancy; smaller board than prior year (12 with one vacancy → 10 nominees).

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**Securities litigation — ACTIVE, the single largest legal exposure.**
- Consolidated **securities fraud class action** against the Company, **Abel Avellan (Chairman/CEO)** and **Andrew M. Johnson (CFO/CLO)**. Class period **March 4, 2025 – July 15, 2026**. Lead-plaintiff deadline **Nov 13, 2026** (now passed — expect a lead plaintiff appointed and litigation to proceed into discovery in 2027).
- Firms involved: Kessler Topaz, Levi & Korsinsky, Rosen, Schall Brown & Schwartz (SBS) — i.e., a competitive, well-resourced plaintiffs' bar.
- **Core allegations:** (i) capital requirements were likely to drive **more frequent and larger debt raises and dilution** than signaled; (ii) the company **overstated the sufficiency of its cash/liquidity** to fund operations for 12 months while completing **three ~$1.0B convertible raises within nine months** (Oct 2025, Feb 2026, Jul 2026); (iii) overstated durability of its D2C competitive position **even after the EchoStar transaction**; (iv) **slow user adoption in the U.S. and Japan**.
- **Catalyst dates underpinning the claim:** −17.0% on Jul 16, 2026 ($1.0B convertible announced); −15.2% on Feb 12, 2026 (another $1.0B converts priced); −12.1% on Jan 7, 2026 (Scotiabank downgrade to Sell); −9.5% Sep 9, 2025 (UBS downgrade on EchoStar competition).
- **No SEC enforcement action or DOJ matter identified** (EDGAR full-text search returned zero regulatory actions for ASTS). This is a private civil matter, not a government enforcement matter — an important distinction.

**FCC / spectrum — NET POSITIVE, largely de-risked.**
- FCC **granted** the AST / AT&T / Verizon / FirstNet applications for AST's **Supplemental Coverage from Space (SCS) leases** of AT&T's and Verizon's spectrum (DA-26-391).
- FCC orders **FCC 26-70 / 26-71** granted **waiver relief** on 800 MHz Cellular rules underpinning the AST–Verizon–AT&T leasing arrangement (Oct 8, 2026 fact sheet).
- FCC has an open NPRM to **modernize D2D leasing rules**, opening ~500 MHz of licensed spectrum for satellite — a tailwind if finalized, but an **unfinished proceeding** whose terms could shift.
- **Residual regulatory risk:** AST does not own U.S. spectrum; its service is structurally dependent on **leased MNO spectrum and MNO goodwill**, and international service requires country-by-country licensing (Japan adoption already lagging). SpaceX's EchoStar spectrum purchase creates a competitor with owned spectrum.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Launch bottleneck is the existential constraint — and management's answer is a bet-the-company vertical-integration gamble.** On **Nov 6, 2026** AST announced **Blacklift**, an in-house 20-ton partly-reusable medium-lift rocket carrying up to five BlueBird satellites, with **first flight targeted late 2028** — against an industry norm of ~5 years for a new orbital rocket, i.e., the schedule itself is a red flag. Near-term launches remain hostage to **New Glenn's performance** (100+ launches targeted 2027–28). The **45-satellite continuous-coverage milestone has slipped to mid-2027** (from early 2027); management concedes **too few satellites for beta service this year** and calls the 2027 outlook uncertain. Sell-side now models mass adoption around **2031**. Any further slip compounds revenue pushout, and the milestone is the single largest driver of equity value.
- **Counterparty/supplier concentration in the Blacklift economics.** The **Firefly Aerospace** transaction (424B5, Nov 17, 2026): perpetual license to manufacture Miranda and vacuum-variant Vira engines for **$400M cash plus an estimated 10–14M shares** (10M issued immediately, **no cash proceeds** to AST), plus a **$250M services fee** and **$250M hardware fee** over three years — roughly **$900M+ of committed economics** for a rocket that has never flown. Critical propulsion IP and the first three Blacklift rockets depend on a single vendor. Separately, the **Rakuten AST joint venture (Rakuten 51% / AST 49%)** cedes control of the Japan business to an MNO partner in exchange for a grant of up to **JPY 148B (~$938M)** — asymmetric economics and a related-party relationship to monitor.
- **Capital intensity and dilution as an operational risk, not just a financial one.** ~$2.288B cash at Jun 30, 2026 (~$2.7B including restricted) sounds ample, but against a program requiring **100+ satellites**, three **$1.0B convertible raises in nine months**, and now equity-for-engines, the funding gap is a *pace-setting* constraint: any capital-markets closure forces a slower build and a further milestone slip, which in turn raises cost of capital — a reflexive loop. Convertible overhang also creates structural selling pressure on rallies.

*Additional:* single satellite production facility (Midland, TX) ramping toward ~6 satellites/month; a **single-point launch-site bottleneck at Cape Canaveral**; and severe **key-person dependence** on Avellan (founder, Chairman, CEO, ~10% owner, technical and commercial figurehead).

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Narrative has inverted.** From a 2026 high, the stock is down **~78–79%** (down ~64% YTD; ~55% in a single month, touching a 52-week low on Nov 18, then falling to ~$23). Retail-heavy, high-beta (2.85) ownership makes the shareholder base volatile and loud.
- **"Securities fraud" is now the dominant search result for the ticker.** Sustained paid PR from four+ plaintiffs' firms through the Nov 13, 2026 deadline has embedded the words *ASTS* + *fraud* + *deadline* into news flow — a durable brand irritant with retail investors and a recurring talking point on calls.
- **Credibility damage from guidance cadence.** Independent analysts have publicly criticized "weak management communication" as a reason to downgrade; the launch milestone has slipped repeatedly. Mainstream skepticism is now high-profile (Jim Cramer: "a very hard stock to own," Nov 18).
- **Competitive narrative risk:** Starlink/SpaceX's EchoStar spectrum purchase and its own D2C push frame AST as the slower, less-capitalized follower — reinforced by alleged slow U.S. and Japan adoption.
- **Partial offsets:** B. Riley upgraded to Buy (Nov 19), arguing launch/rocket risk is priced in; a director bought ~$1.0M of stock at ~$23.54; the FCC approvals are a genuine, verifiable win.
- **Low traditional ESG controversy exposure:** no product recalls, safety incidents, environmental enforcement, or consumer-harm controversies identified. Latent (not current) issue: space-debris/orbital-congestion criticism as the constellation scales.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Not Critical — there is **no restatement, no identified material weakness, no SEC enforcement action, no auditor change, and no whistleblower complaint** on record; the FCC has granted the core spectrum leases and waivers; the board is majority-independent with real telecom expertise; and CEO pay is 100% equity with PSUs. But High is warranted because the *combination* of (a) active securities-fraud litigation naming the Chairman/CEO and CFO over liquidity and dilution disclosures, (b) an unprecedented concentration of executive selling into the 2026 highs including a $146.7M CEO forward, (c) a serial pattern of missed launch milestones now dependent on a rocket that does not yet exist, and (d) demonstrated willingness to dilute heavily (three $1B converts plus 10–14M shares for engines) creates a self-reinforcing risk loop in which operational slippage → capital raise → dilution → litigation → reputational damage → higher cost of capital → further slippage.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **CEO's $146.7M variable prepaid forward (June 22, 2026, AA Gables 2 LLC, 2.5M shares, floor $59.58 / cap $111.72, settling Mar 2028).** Escalate: the founder economically monetized ~1% of the company at ~4x the current share price while retaining voting rights, immediately ahead of a period of guidance deterioration. Ask: was this disclosed in a 10b5-1 plan adopted before the class period closed? Is there additional undisclosed monetization (pledges, margin loans) against the remaining ~235M shares? The same units are **pledged as collateral** — a forced-liquidation risk if the stock falls further.
2. **Class action names the CFO/CLO *and* the CEO, and the CFO sits on the board.** This is an internal-control and disclosure-governance issue, not just litigation. Escalate to the Audit Committee: confirm the **status of ICFR and disclosure controls for FY2026**, whether any material weakness was identified, whether the "cash sufficient for 12 months" language was vetted by counsel, and whether the CFO's combined CFO/CLO/board role is under review.
3. **Blacklift + Firefly commitment (~$900M+, 10–14M shares) approved by a board chaired by the CEO and disclosed alongside a milestone slip.** Escalate: full terms of the Firefly license and Supply & Services Agreement, related-party status of any counterparty principals, and the board's approval process. Confirm the **ultimate cost/schedule cap** and the dilution mechanics of the VWAP-based share true-up.
4. **Rakuten AST JV (51% Rakuten / 49% AST) alongside a JPY 148B (~$938M) grant.** Related-party-adjacent: an MNO strategic partner and former board-level counterparty takes **control** of the Japan subsidiary. Escalate for an explicit related-party-transaction review and disclosure of governance rights, transfer-pricing and exit terms — particularly given the class action's allegation of **slow Japan adoption**.
5. **Sell/buy ratio ~9:1 among officers with sales clustered at $59–127.** Escalate: verify whether any sale occurred outside a 10b5-1 plan or during a blackout window, and whether the CEO's forward was struck during an open window. Note Yao's CTO exercise-and-sell pattern (three tranches) as a recurring supply signal.
