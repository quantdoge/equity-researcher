## 1. Geographic Risk Map

Charter is a **single-country operator**. It has no material non-US revenue, no foreign subsidiaries of consequence, and no FX translation or repatriation exposure. The entire geographic risk stack is domestic US.

| Geography | Revenue exposure | Risk level | Drivers |
|---|---|---|---|
| United States — 41+ states (legacy Charter footprint + Cox: CA, AZ, NV, GA, FL, OH, TX, VA, etc.) | ~100% | **Medium** (High on regulatory intensity) | FCC + 50-state PUC oversight, franchise authorities, AG enforcement, price regulation pressure, litigation-prone plaintiff bar |
| International | ~0% | **Very Low** | No operations; but indirect exposure via imported CPE/network gear and vendor national-security rules |

**Why "Medium" not "Low":** concentration is the risk. Charter has zero geographic diversification to offset an adverse federal rule, a hostile state commission, or a single adverse verdict. Compare to a diversified peer where a bad outcome in one jurisdiction is absorbed elsewhere.

**Cox integration materially changed the state mix.** The Cox transaction closed in August 2026, adding ~11M passings and ~5M customers and deepening California exposure — the most adversarial regulatory jurisdiction in Charter's footprint.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — Federal/state regulatory intervention in the broadband franchise model
**Probability: High | Impact: Medium-High**

Charter's asset base is legally constructed from franchise rights and spectrum licences — both are grants from government, revocable and re-priceable. The current FCC under Chairman Carr is deregulatory (net neutrality proceeding terminated; a broad "streamlining/modernizing agency operations" docket; legacy all-IP rule reform in Nov 2026), which is net favourable. But that cuts both ways: the same commission is asserting authority on **digital discrimination/DEI** grounds — the FCC's own release approving Charter-Cox was titled to "protect against DEI discrimination" — and has run a **data-cap inquiry** that providers argue lacks legal basis. The risk is not a stable deregulatory regime; it is **policy volatility across administrations**, with Charter's pricing power as the target.

State layer is the sharper edge: the **CPUC approved the Cox transfer only on 12 August 2026, subject to two settlement agreements and a "comprehensive set of conditions"** including expanded affordable-broadband options — and Charter and CETF publicly argued the proposed decision **exceeded CPUC authority**. Federal merger clearance was set to expire 15 September 2026, meaning Charter was initially warning California delay could kill the deal. This is a live demonstration that a single state commission can hold a $34.5B transaction hostage.

### Risk 2 — ACP subsidy compliance / False Claims Act exposure
**Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium-High**

Charter was the largest participant in the FCC's Affordable Connectivity Program, receiving federal subsidies for low-income broadband from December 2021 until the programme's June 2024 exhaustion. The FCC has **sent letters to Charter Communications** warning that failure to use the National Verifier "may result in improper enrollments" — a direct, named compliance flag.

The template for outcomes already exists: **FCC-OIG announced a major ACP provider voluntarily repaid $49.4 million** for claims improperly made between June 2021 and July 2022, after OIG sent a warning letter. Charter's revenue base is orders of magnitude larger, so a proportionate finding would be materially bigger. Timing is adverse: **DOJ's Civil Division issued a May 2026 memorandum accelerating review of FCA qui tam actions** against federally funded benefits programmes, and relators filed 1,297 qui tams last year (up 32%). The whistleblower pipeline into telecom subsidy programmes is widening, not narrowing.

### Risk 3 — Trade policy, supply chain and spectrum national-security friction
**Probability: Medium | Impact: Low-Medium**

Two channels. First, **capex input costs**: Charter's network build and CPE supply chain run through Asian manufacturers, exposed to tariff regimes and Secure Equipment Act restrictions on Chinese vendors. Second, **spectrum**: Charter's mobile strategy (Spectrum Mobile, an MVNO riding Verizon plus owned CBRS/3.5GHz spectrum) depends on FCC auction authority, which has lapsed and repeatedly required congressional action — a political bottleneck entirely outside management control. Neither is existential, but both inject cost and timing noise into a business already cutting $2B of opex.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Resolved favourably — copyright (material de-risking).** In **March 2026 the Supreme Court ruled unanimously for Cox Communications** in *Cox v. Sony Music*, holding an ISP bears no liability for copyright violations of its customers, reversing the jury verdict; the Fourth Circuit had already thrown out the **$1B+ damages award**. This effectively closes the ISP-secondary-liability theory that had produced billion-dollar exposure across the cable sector, including against Charter.

**Active — securities class action over ACP guidance.** A shareholder class action (*Sandoval*) was filed **14 August 2025** against Charter, **CEO Chris Winfrey and CFO Jessica Fischer**, alleging securities-law violations tied to guidance about the financial impact of the ACP shutdown. Lead-plaintiff deadline was 14 October 2025.

**Active — fresh investigation notices (Nov 2026).** **Kahn Swick & Foti commenced an investigation** into Charter's officers and directors in November 2026, again ACP-centred. Separately, **Levin Law is investigating claims on behalf of former Liberty Broadband shareholders** who received Charter shares in the 2026 all-stock merger. These are investigations, not filed complaints against the company — low direct financial cost, but they signal a persistent plaintiff-bar campaign and raise D&O/disclosure-controls concerns.

**Regulatory settlements — pattern of recurring FCC/AG actions.** FCC settled a **911 and network outage reporting** investigation with Charter for **$15M**; a separate **Emergency Alert System** investigation settled at **$1.1M** with a mandated compliance plan. Historically, the **New York AG secured a $174.2M settlement** (2018) over misleading internet-speed claims. The through-line: Charter is a repeat target for state AGs and FCC enforcement bureaus, and compliance-plan obligations accumulate.

**Merger clearances — obtained, but with conditions.** FCC approved Charter-Cox in **February 2026 via delegated authority** (WCB/WTB/OIA) rather than a full Commission vote; **DOJ cleared under HSR**; **CPUC approved 12 August 2026** with conditions. The Cox deal added ~**$12 billion of debt and finance leases**, and the concurrent Liberty Broadband merger cut Charter's share count by ~4.7M.

**Balance sheet is the amplifier on all legal risk.** Total principal debt was **$108.6 billion at 30 September 2026**, against a market cap of roughly **$19.8 billion**. Management targets **3.5x net debt/adjusted EBITDA within three years**. Charter launched **$1.5B of tender offers across eight note series** in November 2026. A company at ~5x leverage with $108.6B of debt has limited absorptive capacity for a large adverse judgment or FCA settlement — this is why legal risk translates into equity risk here far more than at an unlevered peer.

**$6.33B franchise impairment (Q3 2026).** Triggered by a **sustained decline in Charter's share price** and lower long-run customer estimates, driving a **$2.4B net loss** and a **37% drawdown** from ~$250 in April 2026 to ~$110 in September. Technically an accounting event, but substantively it is the market repricing the durability of the regulated franchise itself — and it makes future impairment testing sensitive to further price weakness.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating

# MEDIUM-HIGH

Split explicitly, because the two axes diverge sharply:

- **Classic geopolitical risk: LOW.** A 100%-US revenue base means no Russia sanctions, no China revenue, no emerging-market currency controls, no expropriation or nationalisation risk, and no cross-border data-transfer exposure. On the standard country-risk framework Charter screens near-clean.
- **Legal & regulatory risk: HIGH.** Single-jurisdiction concentration, active securities litigation naming the CEO and CFO, a named FCC compliance inquiry with a $49.4M peer precedent, a $6.33B franchise write-down that reveals franchise-valuation fragility, and **$108.6B of debt against a ~$19.8B equity value** that leaves almost no room for an adverse outcome.

**The single most important thing for the investment memo:** Charter's risk profile is not "foreign" risk — it is the risk that the *US regulatory compact that underpins its franchise value* is repriced. The $6.33B impairment is the market's verdict that this has already begun. The thing to monitor is not geopolitics but (a) whether FCC OIG converts the ACP National Verifier letters into a demand, and (b) whether the CPUC-style conditional-approval playbook spreads to other states as Charter seeks franchise renewals and any future approvals.
