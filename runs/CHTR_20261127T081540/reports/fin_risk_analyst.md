# Charter Communications (CHTR) — Financial Risk Assessment
**As of Q3 2026 (period ended 9/30/2026) | Latest audited FY: 2025**

## Executive Summary
Charter is a **structurally levered, sub-investment-grade cable operator whose core business has tipped into secular decline**. FY2025 was the last "clean" year: $54.8B revenue, $21.2B EBITDA, but FCF compressed to $4.4B (capex now 21% of revenue). On **August 19, 2026 Charter closed the Cox acquisition**, pushing total debt from $96.7B to **$109.9B** and adding ~11M passings. Q3 2026 delivered a **$2.4B net loss** driven by an estimated ~$6.5–7.0B non-cash impairment — the market telling us the cable asset base is carried above its recoverable value. The stock is down ~43% YTD and ~76% from its peak.

The critical insight: **this is not a solvency crisis, it is a capital-allocation and business-model risk.** Over FY2021–2025 Charter spent **$35.3B on buybacks against $25.9B of cumulative FCF** — funding $9.4B of buybacks with debt while its competitive position eroded. That is a reversible policy error, not a fixed cost. Leverage is manageable *if* management stops the buyback; it is dangerous if it doesn't.

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment: **SUB-INVESTMENT GRADE**
**Ratings (confirmed Aug 2026):** S&P **BB+** (stable, affirmed on post-deal deleveraging at ~4.2x), Moody's **Ba1**, Fitch **BB+**. Solidly in the "crossover/upper-junk" band — one notch below investment grade at all three agencies, with essentially no upgrade path until leverage sustainably clears ~3.5x.

**The problem is coverage, not absolute debt.** Debt/EBITDA of 4.2–4.6x is high but survivable for a subscription business with stable, contractual revenue. The binding constraint is **interest coverage of 2.5–2.9x on EBIT** — thin for a business with declining revenue, where a 20% revenue shock pushes coverage toward 1x. Weighted average cost of debt is **5.2%**, and with the 10Y Treasury at 5.13% there is **no refinancing tailwind** — refinancing is accretive to cost only if credit spreads hold.

**Altman Z-Score of 0.602 (Distress zone)** is a legitimate red flag here, not just a cable-sector artifact (goodwill-heavy balance sheets depress x4 mechanically, but x4 on book equity, 0.154, is nearly identical to x4 on market cap, ~0.13, because the equity is so thin relative to liabilities). **Piotroski F-Score of 7** is the offsetting positive — leverage fell, operating cash flow is robust, no dilution, gross margin improved.

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue −20%

Base case pro forma: Revenue ~$67B, EBITDA $26.0B (38.8%), D&A ~$10.4B, EBIT ~$15.6B, interest $5.4B, net debt $109.4B. Assume ~65% contribution margin (content/customer costs variable).

| Scenario | Revenue | EBITDA | Net Debt/EBITDA | EBIT Coverage | FCF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Base** | $67.0B | $26.0B | 4.2x | 2.9x | ~$5.5B |
| **−10%** (−$6.7B) | $60.3B | $21.6B | **5.1x** | **2.1x** | ~$3.2B |
| **−20%** (−$13.4B) | $53.6B | $17.3B | **6.3x** | **1.3x** | ~$1.0–1.5B |

**At a 20% revenue decline, Charter's financial model breaks in a specific, identifiable way:**
- **Coverage collapses to ~1.3x EBIT/interest.** Even on an EBITDA basis (~3.2x), fixed charges consume the overwhelming majority of operating profit. Any further deterioration turns coverage sub-1.0x on EBIT.
- **Leverage jumps to ~6.3x**, triggering likely multi-notch downgrades (BB+ → B+/B territory) and materially higher spreads on a $110B debt stack — a self-reinforcing spiral given constant refinancing needs.
- **FCF falls to roughly zero.** With capex at 21% of revenue, there is no discretionary cushion left except network investment — and cutting capex below maintenance levels sacrifices the only growth asset Charter has (its network).
- **Further impairments near-certain.** Enterprise value is already ~$123B (using ~117M shares × $118 + $109.4B net debt) against $178B of total assets — the equity market is implying ~$55B of book value is unrealizable. Q3's ~$6.5–7.0B charge is likely the first of several.
- **Mitigants:** $2B of new annualized opex savings plus $1B+ of Cox synergies (~12% of pro forma EBITDA) provide real offset; and the buyback can be turned off to preserve ~$5B/yr.

**Is −20% plausible?** KeyBanc projects >3M internet subscriber losses by 2028 (~11% of the base) — 973k in 2026, 1.15M in 2027, 1.04M in 2028. Layer fixed-wireless and fiber share gains on top of video cord-cutting, and a 20% multi-year revenue decline is a credible, not extreme, scenario. **Q3 2026 alone lost 233k internet customers.**

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**
Not **Critical** — no maturity wall, debt is overwhelmingly long-dated ($106.8B long-term vs $3.2B short-term) and fixed-rate, revenue is contractual and recession-resistant, EBITDA is $26B, and the business generates positive FCF. But **High** is warranted on: sub-investment-grade ratings at all three agencies, 4.2–4.6x leverage with deteriorating EBITDA, 2.5–2.9x EBIT coverage, negative tangible equity of −$74B, a 0.37 current ratio, and a demonstrated willingness to fund buybacks with debt into a declining business.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Buybacks exceeded FCF by $9.4B cumulatively (2021–2025).** $35.3B repurchased vs. $25.9B of FCF, while the stock fell 76%. Value-destructive capital allocation at scale — and still running ($5.1B in 2025 vs $4.4B FCF).
2. **FCF halved structurally.** $8.7B (2021) → $3.2B (2024) → $4.4B (2025) as capex rose from 14.6% to 21.3% of revenue. The "high FCF yield" (22%+) prices a melting ice cube, not a durable cash machine.
3. **Q3 2026 impairment (~$6.5–7.0B) and $2.4B net loss.** Book value is being written down toward market reality; more likely.
4. **Interest coverage of 2.5–2.9x** is the single tightest constraint — no cushion for a revenue shock.
5. **Liquidity is revolver-dependent.** $573M cash against $3.2B of near-term maturities; 0.04x cash ratio. Charter lives on continuous bond-market access.
6. **Debt-funded M&A into a declining core.** Cox added $12.8B of debt for scale in a business losing subscribers to fixed wireless and fiber.
7. **Negative tangible book equity of −$74B** — no asset backing for creditors beyond the network itself.
