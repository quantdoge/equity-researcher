## 1. Macro Environment Summary — Stance: **Transitional / Hawkish re-tightening (risk-off tilt for long-duration equities)**

As of 27 November 2026 the US economy is in a **"no-landing / inflation re-acceleration" regime**, not a recession and not a cutting cycle. Growth is running above trend (Q3'26 real GDP 24,665.2bn chained dollars, **+2.3% YoY**, and **+4.2% annualised QoQ**), unemployment has *fallen* to **4.1%** from 4.5% a year ago, and yet CPI has re-accelerated to **3.82% YoY** from a 3.30% trough in July 2026. The Fed's response has been to stop easing and turn hawkish: the effective funds rate bottomed at **3.63% (Jun–Aug 2026)**, has already drifted back up to **3.88% (Oct)**, the FOMC **paused on 28 October**, and the Reuters poll of 10 November puts a **+25bp December hike to 4.00–4.25%** as the consensus path — with the FOMC median dots (per Fidelity) sitting at 4.00–4.25% through end-2027.

The critical feature for Charter is the **shape and direction of the curve**. The long end is bear-steepening violently: the 10Y has gone from **4.38% (26 Jun 2026) to 5.13% (27 Nov)** — **+75bp in five months**, with **+27bp in the last three weeks alone**. The 30Y is at **5.47%** and the 2s10s is **+39bp**. This is precisely the wrong regime for a highly levered, long-duration, low-growth cash-flow stream. Credit is still benign but deteriorating at the margin: the Baa–10Y spread has widened from **1.39% (24 Sep) to 1.61% (24 Nov)**, implying an all-in Baa yield of ~6.74%. The broad dollar has firmed **+4.6% since 11 September (118.21 → 123.60)**, consistent with tighter global financial conditions.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates — the dominant variable (strongly negative).** Charter is the most rate-sensitive structure in Communication Services. Post-Cox the balance sheet carries roughly **$100bn+ of debt (est.; Cox contributed ~$10.8bn per the 3Q26 debt schedule)** against a **$19.9bn market cap** — equity is only ~15–16% of enterprise value, so it behaves as a **levered 6x call option on enterprise value**. Two channels:
- *Discount rate:* with ~85–90% of a cable DCF in terminal value, a 100bp WACC increase cuts EV roughly 18–22%; on a 6x levered stack that is a **50–70% equity hit**. The tape confirms it: the 10Y rose 75bp from June to November and CHTR fell **39%** (from $241.78 on 23 Apr to $147.27) even as fundamentals (Cox close, $2bn cost plan) improved.
- *Refinancing:* the November **tender offers for eight series of notes (up to $1.5bn)** plus a Q3 debt exchange that cut principal ~$1.5bn show active liability management. Every 100bp on refinanced paper costs roughly **$1bn pre-tax ≈ $5.6/share after tax ≈ 3.8% of the current share price** — before any multiple effect. Deleveraging to the **3.5x net-debt/EBITDA target "within three years"** of the August 2026 Cox close is now materially harder in a 5%+ long-bond world.

**Inflation — negative on both sides of the P&L.** 3.82% CPI with 4.1% unemployment means wage and vendor inflation persists in a **91,900-head** cost base and in programming/retransmission costs that historically run above CPI. Charter has limited pricing power against fixed-wireless competition, so the ~6.4% YoY nominal PCE backdrop is not translating into ARPU. The **$2bn run-rate opex savings plan over 18 months** (plus $1bn Cox synergies) is management's answer — but it is a *response* to the macro margin squeeze, not a macro tailwind.

**Growth/employment — the one genuine offset.** 4.1% unemployment and +2.3% real GDP mean the subscriber base does not face a jobless shock; household formation and small-business demand hold up. This caps downside to churn and bad debt. However, high long rates suppress housing turnover and move-related connects, and the **June 2024 end of the ACP subsidy** removed a ~$30/month support from Charter's most price-sensitive cohort — a structural, not cyclical, drag.

**Currency & trade — effectively neutral.** Charter is a domestic US operator (~zero FX-translated revenue). Dollar strength is mildly helpful on imported network CPE/set-top and DOCSIS 4.0 equipment; **tariff exposure on imported network gear** is the relevant trade risk, and it lands in a capex line that is already being funded at a higher cost of capital.

**Credit conditions — benign now, wrong direction.** At 6.74% all-in Baa, Charter's unsecured curve is issuing into a market where spreads have widened 22bp in two months. A further 60–80bp of spread widening closes the refinancing window precisely when the deleveraging clock is running.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Three reasons: (i) inflation at 3.82% and rising forces the Fed into a hike the market has only partly priced, keeping the long end under pressure; (ii) a **bear-steepening 30Y at 5.47%** is the single worst curve shape for a levered, long-duration, terminal-value-heavy equity; (iii) the company's own 3.5x leverage target requires ~$10bn+ of cumulative debt reduction into a 5%+ market while its low-income customer cohort absorbs 3.8% inflation. The only mitigants — resilient employment, the $2bn cost programme, and a stock already down 65% from its peak — temper but do not reverse the rating.

## 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise: **Bear — a second Fed hike breaks the refinancing window**

Consensus treats October's pause as the peak and assumes cuts resume in 2027. The surprise: October CPI at 3.82% is not a base-effect blip but shelter/services pass-through, the **December 25bp hike lands and is followed by another in March 2027** (target 4.25–4.50%), the **10Y clears 5.75%** and the **Baa–10Y spread widens to 250bp** as the front end inverts the curve again. Charter then refinances $10–15bn of annual maturities at 6.5–7.0% rather than 5%, interest expense rises ~$400–600m, free cash flow available for deleveraging shrinks, the 3.5x target slips beyond 2029, and the equity de-rates from ~6x to ~5x EV/EBITDA on flat EBITDA — implying **$85–95 per share, a further −35% to −42%**.

*Symmetric upside risk (lower probability, higher convexity):* if shelter disinflation bites in H1 2027 and the 10Y retreats to ~4.25%, Charter's deleveraging becomes credible, the multiple re-rates toward 6.5x, and the same 6x levered equity delivers **$220–240 (+50% to +65%)**. Both tails are wide because the equity is a levered claim — which is itself the macro conclusion: **own CHTR as a rates trade, not as a cable trade.**
