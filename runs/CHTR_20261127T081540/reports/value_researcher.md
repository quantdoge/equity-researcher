## Charter Communications (CHTR) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price analyzed: $147.27 (close 25 Nov 2026) | Market cap ~$19.9B | Net debt $109.4B | EV ~$129B*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW (and narrowing)**

Charter is the largest U.S. cable/broadband operator post-Cox: ~34.3M internet customers, 36.7M customer relationships, 13.8M video, 13.5M mobile lines, ~56M+ passings. The product is genuinely essential — residential broadband is the last subscription households cut.

**Sources of moat, and their condition:**

| Moat source | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Efficient scale / local franchise** | Historically wide. Duplicating a network costs ~$1,000+/home passed. But fiber overbuilders and FWA have made duplication economically rational at scale — the barrier has fallen. |
| **Switching costs** | Weak. Bundling (Spectrum One) creates some inertia, but churn is rising and FWA is a zero-install substitute. |
| **Intangible assets** | Spectrum brand is decent but not pricing-power-grade. No patent or license protection of consequence. |
| **Cost advantage** | Real but not decisive — 40% adj. EBITDA margin on pro forma Q3 2026 revenue of $16.5B. Scale helps, but so does Comcast's. |
| **Network effects** | None. |

**The decisive evidence the moat has narrowed — not widened — over five years:**
- Internet subscribers have now declined for multiple consecutive quarters: −120k (Q1 2026), −172k (Q2 2026, vs −116k a year earlier), −233k pro forma (Q3 2026; −156k legacy Charter, −77k legacy Cox).
- Management itself took a **$6.3B franchise impairment charge** in Q3 2026 — a formal accounting admission that the asset base is worth less than carried. You do not impair a widening moat.
- Revenue has stalled: FY2025 revenue **−0.56%** to $54.77B; 5-yr CAGR only 2.63%. Pro forma Q3 2026 revenue **−1.5%** and pro forma adj. EBITDA **−4.0%** YoY — the *combined* entity is shrinking, even after $1B+ of cost actions.
- CFO attributes losses to "expanded fixed wireless competition… including lower sales from low-income customers" — i.e., the low end of the market is being taken by T-Mobile/Verizon FWA, which is a structural, not cyclical, loss.
- KeyBanc models ~973k internet losses in 2026, 1.15M in 2027, 1.04M in 2028 — roughly 3M subs, ~9% of the base, over three years.

**ROIC vs. WACC:** ROIC has been remarkably *flat* at 11.1–11.5% for 2022–2025 (11.34% in 2025). It clears an estimated ~7.5% WACC, so capital is being employed profitably — but there is no upward trajectory. A moated business should show widening returns. This one shows a plateau at the moment competition arrived. Note ROE of 24–31% is leverage-and-buyback-inflated, not a moat signal.

**Verdict: this was a Wide-moat business five years ago. It is now Narrow, and the trend is negative.** That is the single most important fact in this analysis, and it is why the multiple is low.

**What's good:** ~40% adj. EBITDA margin; a genuinely essential product; FCF conversion of 89% of net income in FY2025 (4.42/4.99) is in the "excellent" band of my framework; no dividend obligation; incremental margins on any revenue stabilization are very high.

**What's bad, and it dominates:**
- **Owner earnings have collapsed** from $5.14B (2022) to $2.04B (2025) — a 60% decline — as capex intensity rose from 16% to 21% of revenue. Buffett owner earnings, not "adjusted EBITDA," is the honest measure here, and it is shrinking.
- **Leverage is the killer.** Post-Cox, net debt is $109.4B against ~$26B pro forma EBITDA = **~4.2x**. Interest expense runs ~$5.4B/yr — pro forma EBITDA/interest coverage is only ~4.8x. For a business with declining revenue, that is thin.
- **Capex is a treadmill, not a choice.** ~$12B+/yr is required to keep the network competitive with fiber. Management promises it will come down, but it has not.
- **Accumulated deficit** of −$5.3B in retained earnings; equity of $29.8B sits against $178B of assets, of which $104B is goodwill and intangibles. Tangible book is deeply negative.

A 4/10 reflects a business with real cash-generating assets and a broken growth engine, financed with an aggressive amount of debt.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples (at $147.27):** P/E 4.1x on trailing GAAP EPS of $36.21 (a figure that is now stale and will be meaningless once the Q3 impairment flows through); P/B 0.99x; **EV/EBITDA ~5.0x pro forma**; **FCF yield ~22.6%** ($4.5B / $19.9B).

The reported "82.7% discount to peer median P/E" is **not** a valid signal — the sector peer set is dominated by Alphabet, Meta and Netflix. On the metric that matters for a cable operator, EV/EBITDA, CHTR at ~5.0x sits at a discount to large-cap telecom typically trading at 6–7x. That discount is the market's price for structural decline and 4.2x leverage, and it is not obviously wrong.

**Equity DCF (my own, since the tool's WACC parameter mis-parsed):** Because interest and capex are largely fixed, I model FCF to equity directly and discount at a leveraged 10.5% cost of equity. Note the per-share figures use the provider's ~135M shares; the Q3 2026 diluted count was ~117M, which would raise per-share values ~15% — I use the more conservative figure.

| Scenario | Assumptions | Equity value | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear (40%)** | FCF $2.8B, −5%/yr perpetual, 11.5% discount | ~$16B | ~$119 |
| **Base (40%)** | FCF $4.0B, −2%/yr perpetual, 10.5% discount | ~$31B | ~$233 |
| **Bull (20%)** | FCF $5.5B, −0.5%/yr (cost savings land), 10% discount | ~$52B | ~$386 |
| **Probability-weighted** | | **~$29B** | **~$218** |

**What the price implies:** At a 22.6% FCF yield, the market is capitalizing Charter's cash flow as if it will decline **~12% per year in perpetuity**. That is a severe expectation — but not an absurd one for a business losing 2.6% of subscribers annually at 4.2x leverage.

**My honest read:** the central intrinsic value of ~$215–225/share implies ~32% upside, which technically clears a 20–30% margin-of-safety hurdle. But the *range* is $119 to $386 — a ±60% error band. **A discount that is smaller than your own estimation error is not a margin of safety.** Buffett's margin of safety exists precisely to absorb error; here the error band swallows the discount whole. In a levered business, that is disqualifying: a 10% EBITDA miss removes ~$2.6B, which is roughly half of equity FCF, because interest and capex do not flex.

**And the cheapness has partly evaporated.** The shares traded at **$102.48 on 13 Oct 2026** and closed at **$147.27 on 25 Nov 2026** — a 44% rally in six weeks, apparently driven by Comcast-merger speculation rather than fundamentals. *That* was the price at which a 33% FCF yield and ~50% discount offered a real margin of safety. At $147.27, buying into a rumor-driven bounce is not value investing.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Below average on capital allocation, adequate on operations**

**Operations:** CEO Chris Winfrey (since Dec 2022, formerly COO) is a credible cable operator. The Q3 2026 "new operating model" — $2B annualized opex savings over 18 months plus $1B+ of Cox synergies — is a real, quantifiable plan, and mobile is genuinely working (+1.8M lines LTM to 13.5M). He is not asleep at the wheel.

**Capital allocation — this is the problem:**
- **Buybacks bought at the top.** Charter repurchased **$15.4B in 2021, $10.3B in 2022, $3.2B in 2023, $1.2B in 2024, $5.1B in 2025** — roughly $35B+ at prices mostly between $400 and $700 — then effectively stopped in Q3 2026 ($21M repurchased) with the stock near $120. Share count fell from 203M (2020) to ~117M (Q3 2026), a 42% reduction funded with debt. This is the *inverse* of Buffett's discipline: aggressive at high prices, absent at low prices. It destroyed tens of billions of dollars of shareholder value and is a large part of why net debt is $109B today.
- **The Cox acquisition looks like empire-building.** A **$34.5B** deal (FCC approved Feb 2026, closed 19 Aug 2026) adding 11M+ passings and 5M+ relationships — closed precisely as industry subscriber losses were accelerating. It added ~$13B of debt in one quarter (total debt $96.7B → $109.9B) and diluted returns. The claimed $1B+ of synergies is small relative to the leverage added. Buying scale in a shrinking market is not obviously value-accretive.
- **No dividend, ever.** With buybacks now paused, shareholders receive nothing while waiting for the turnaround.
- **Governance:** Charter's dual-class structure gives Advance/Newhouse supervoting Class B shares effective control; minority holders cannot influence strategy. (I could not retrieve the 10-K — the filing tool returned "No 10-K found" — so I flag this as a structural feature rather than a freshly verified one.) Insider alignment is real; shareholder influence is not.

**Assessment:** honest, operationally competent management with a genuinely poor long-term record of allocating capital. I would not pay a premium for this team.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate at $147.27)

This is not a bad business — it is a good business in structural decline, financed with too much debt, run by a team with a poor capital-allocation record, trading at a price that has already rallied 44% off its panic low.

**Why not Buy:**
1. The moat is Narrow and narrowing — internet subscribers are falling at an accelerating rate, and management's own $6.3B impairment concedes it.
2. Pro forma revenue (−1.5%) and pro forma adj. EBITDA (−4.0%) are both declining. The trend is negative and getting worse, not better.
3. 4.2x leverage on $109.4B of net debt makes the equity a levered call option on a shrinking asset. That is the opposite of a margin of safety.
4. My ~$218 central intrinsic value sits inside a $119–$386 error band; a ~32% discount does not compensate for ±60% uncertainty.
5. The margin of safety that existed at $102 in October has been given away.

**Why not Sell:** At 5.0x EV/EBITDA and a 22.6% FCF yield, the cash flows are real and the asset base has value. Selling into a 22%+ FCF yield on an essential utility-like product is likely to be the wrong side of the trade. The $2B cost-savings program plus $1B of Cox synergies against a ~$26B EBITDA base could add ~$3B — meaningful against a $20B market cap.

**What would change my mind to Buy:**
- Price at or below ~$105 (near the October low), where the FCF yield exceeds 33% and the discount to base-case value exceeds 50%; **or**
- Two consecutive quarters of pro forma internet subscriber stabilization, **and** net leverage trending toward 3.5x; **or**
- A credible, value-accretive consolidation at a premium that compensates for the leverage.

**What would make me Sell:** leverage above 4.5x, a dividend/equity-linked financing, or a further acceleration in internet losses beyond ~300k/quarter.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to the bearish (Hold) view — i.e., what could make me wrong to the upside:**
- **Cost savings overshoot.** $2B + $1B synergies is ~11% of pro forma EBITDA. If delivered in full against flat revenue, FCF could reach $7–8B and the equity would be worth $300+.
- **Mobile becomes the growth engine.** 13.5M lines and +1.8M LTM is real; convergence bundling could re-anchor churn and stabilize ARPU.
- **Consolidation.** Comcast/Charter speculation is live (analysts have floated it; Comcast publicly denied exploring cable mergers in April 2026). A strategic or infrastructure buyer could pay a premium to EV/EBITDA.
- **Capex rolls off.** Management has repeatedly promised lower capital intensity; if capex falls to ~$9B, FCF rises ~$3B and the yield doubles.

**Risks to the investment thesis (the downside):**
- **Accelerating cord-cutting to FWA and fiber.** FWA is a cheap, zero-install substitute; fiber overbuilds are hitting Charter's footprint directly. Broadband losses could exceed 1.2M/yr — eroding the single cash engine.
- **Refinancing risk.** $109.4B of debt at ~5% average cost. A 100bp increase in refinancing costs is ~$1.1B of annual FCF — a fifth of the entire equity FCF base. Loss of investment-grade ratings would compound this.
- **Value trap.** Cable multiples have historically looked "cheap" all the way down. A 5x EV/EBITDA multiple on declining EBITDA is not cheap; it is a melting ice cube priced accordingly.
- **Cox integration execution.** Absorbing 11M passings, 5M customers and a new operating model simultaneously, while cutting $2B of costs, is a lot of change at once — and the impairment charge suggests the acquired assets are already worth less than paid.
- **Governance.** Supervoting Class B control means minority shareholders cannot force a change in strategy if the decline continues.
- **Data gaps.** My FY2026 figures are 9-month actuals plus pro forma Q3 disclosures; the full-year picture post-Cox is not yet observable, and the share-count/consideration mechanics of the Cox transaction could not be fully verified from the filing set available to me. Per-share figures should be treated as ranges, not point estimates.

---

**Bottom line:** Charter at $147.27 is a levered, structurally challenged business at a price that is no longer distressed. The moat has narrowed, the balance sheet is stretched, and the capital allocators have already spent the margin of safety. I would hold if I owned it, and I would not buy it here — my buy price is ~$105, or evidence that broadband losses have stopped.
