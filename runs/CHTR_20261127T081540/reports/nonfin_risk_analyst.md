# Charter Communications (CHTR) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**As of 25-Nov-2026 | Price $147.27 | Mkt cap ~$19.8B | Sector: Communication Services / Telecom**

**Context for any reader of this memo:** CHTR has been the site of an unusually dense cluster of non-financial risk events in the past 12 months. The stock fell from $254 (23-Apr-26) to $103 (9-Oct-26) — a ~59% peak-to-trough drawdown — and has since rebounded to $147. Non-financial factors, not leverage math, drove most of that path.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK** (downgraded from Adequate)

**Positives (real, and not to be dismissed):**
- **Chair/CEO separation is intact.** Eric Zinterhofer serves as Non-Executive Chairman (elevated from Lead Independent Director in Nov-2023); Chris Winfrey is CEO and has been a director only since Nov-2023. No chairman/CEO duality.
- **Directors and the CEO bought stock in the open market with their own money** immediately after the April-2026 collapse (details in §3) — the single most credible alignment signal available.
- Heavy institutional ownership; 13F flow in Q3-2026 was net positive (Eurizon +769%, Allworth +2,979%, Benjamin Edwards +1,068%).

**Negatives (these drive the rating):**
1. **Abrupt CFO vacancy at the worst possible moment.** Jessica Fischer resigned 28-Aug-2026 and departed 15-Oct-2026 "to relocate for another opportunity"; Kevin Howard is **interim** CFO (per Oct/Nov-2026 Form 4s). An interim CFO is now overseeing Cox integration, a $6.3B impairment, a securities class action, a $2B cost program, and a ~$90B+ debt stack. Unexplained senior finance departures during integration are a classic early-warning indicator.
2. **Disclosure quality.** Q2-2025 EBITDA growth of +0.5% was wholly attributable to a **$45M one-time benefit in "other revenue"**; excluding it, EBITDA fell 0.3% and missed by 2.4%. Heavy reliance on pro forma and adjusted metrics post-Cox.
3. **Capital-allocation credibility.** A **$6.33B franchise impairment** was booked in Q3-2026, one quarter after closing the **$34.5B Cox acquisition**. Writing down franchise value almost immediately after closing implies the price paid materially exceeded fair value — a board oversight failure, not a market accident.
4. **Dual-front legal exposure to fiduciaries.** A securities class action (filed Aug-2025, tied to a ~$9B market-value loss and ACP disclosure) is ongoing, and KSF launched a derivative/fiduciary investigation on 11-Nov-2026 naming officers and directors. Board-level legal exposure now compounds management-level exposure.
5. **Ownership re-concentration.** Cox Enterprises now reports **46,153,885 shares** as a 10% owner following the deal, and Liberty Broadband is monetizing (see §3). A large strategic block holder plus a founder-linked legacy holder changes board dynamics and raises related-party risk (programming, commercial fiber, managed-IT arrangements inherited from Cox).
6. **Compensation structure unverified** — Charter's 2026 proxy was not retrievable (SEC returned HTTP 403), so I cannot confirm whether Cox-close option grants (Aug-2026, strike $0) contain performance conditions or are time-based only. **This is a gap, not a clean bill of health.**

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **ELEVATED**

- **EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned zero SEC actions against CHTR** — a genuinely favorable data point. Caveat: this feed does not capture FCC, state PUC, or state AG matters, which are the live risk channels for a cable operator.
- **Cox transaction commitments (now binding).** The $34.5B Cox deal cleared DOJ (HSR expired 15-Sep-2025, no Second Request) and the FCC (27-Feb-2026), but only after **voluntary commitments to the FCC and multiple state regulators**, with notably protracted review at the **California PUC** and New York. These are multi-year buildout, pricing, and conduct obligations with compliance-failure penalties. Deal closed ~Aug/Sep-2026.
- **Politicized regulatory overlay.** The FCC's approval was framed around rural builds, jobs, and "protecting against DEI." Charter is now exposed to shifting federal posture on DEI, net neutrality/Title II, and broadband rate regulation — a channel where Charter has few defenses beyond lobbying.
- **Structural ACP exposure.** The Affordable Connectivity Program ended June-2024; the subsidized cohort has churned out over subsequent quarters (117k internet losses in Q2-25, 120k in Q1-26, 233k in Q3-26). The subsidy cliff is a *policy* risk that has already converted into permanent subscriber losses. Ongoing securities litigation is built directly on management's post-ACP assurances.
- **Data-privacy regulatory risk.** See §5 — a 4.9M-account breach implies state AG and multi-state notification exposure beyond private litigation.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Broadband demand erosion that cost cuts cannot fix.** Pro forma revenue fell 1.5% y/y in Q3-26 and internet customers fell 233k in the quarter *despite* adding Cox's systems. Fixed wireless and fiber overbuild are taking share in Charter's footprint. The $2B annualized opex savings program is a margin defense, not a growth plan — and aggressive cost-cutting in a subscriber-service business elevates execution and service-quality risk.
- **Cox integration + key-person concentration in finance.** Absorbing 11M passings / 5M customers and two commercial businesses (fiber, managed IT/cloud) while running an **interim CFO** and a $6.3B write-down is the highest-risk integration profile in the sector. Billing, network, and back-office systems consolidation failures in cable integrations historically surface as customer-attrition and revenue-recognition problems.
- **Cybersecurity: a repeated victim, not a one-off.** On 1-Apr-2026 the ShinyHunters group breached Charter via **vishing/social engineering**, exposing 4.9M accounts (names, phones, addresses, emails); the group claims 42M records. This follows an earlier major Charter breach. Vishing success implies a *human-and-process* control failure (help-desk/identity verification), which is precisely the control class that is cheap to fix and damning when it recurs. Network resilience is further exposed to physical risk (storms/franchise-area outages) that NCTA itself publicizes.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Consumer-facing litigation stack:** (i) a Connecticut class action filed 1-Jun-2026 over the breach; (ii) class actions over **hidden billing fees** charged to millions of subscribers. Fee-disclosure suits are reputationally corrosive because they confirm the "cable company nickel-and-dimes you" stereotype — Charter's Spectrum brand has no goodwill cushion.
- **Investor-credibility damage.** The ACP episode, the $45M one-off revenue item, the near-immediate $6.3B impairment, the -25.5% single-day collapse (worst on record), and now an interim CFO together create a "management says X, reality delivers Y" narrative that is hard to reverse. KSF's Nov-2026 press release explicitly re-litigates the post-ACP assurances — this will stay in the news flow.
- **Mitigants:** the CEO and directors buying stock publicly cuts against the "insiders are dumping on retail" narrative; the company is cooperating visibly on breach notification; and the Cox deal gives management a genuine "we are investing for rural America" story with the FCC.
- **ESG/political exposure:** the DEI-conditioned FCC approval places Charter in the middle of a partisan fight over broadband policy in both directions.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Not Critical**, because: there is no restatement, no auditor change, no going-concern or liquidity event, no confirmed regulatory enforcement action, and — decisively — informed insiders were buying with personal capital at the lows.

**Not Moderate**, because: an interim CFO during a transformational integration, a $6.3B write-down one quarter after a $34.5B acquisition, two concurrent securities/fiduciary actions, a repeat-material data breach, and a still-unfolding subscriber decline are five independent risk vectors that are *correlated*, not diversifiable. Any one of them resolving badly raises the probability the others do.

**What would move this to Critical:** a CFO who departs to "pursue other opportunities" without a permanent successor being named; an SEC formal investigation or subpoena; a restatement or revenue-recognition adjustment tied to the $45M item or Cox opening balance sheet; a state AG enforcement action on the breach; or Liberty Broadband accelerating sales.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Interim CFO (Kevin Howard) since 15-Oct-2026**, replacing Jessica Fischer who resigned amid integration, impairment, and litigation. *Escalate — ask the board directly why the departure was abrupt and what the succession timeline is.*
2. **$6.33B franchise impairment in the first quarter after closing Cox.** *Escalate — signals overpayment; interrogate the purchase-price allocation and whether further impairment remains.*
3. **Q2-2025 EBITDA "beat" carried by a $45M one-time "other revenue" item** — the factual core of the securities class action and KSF probe. *Escalate — this is the closest thing in the file to an accounting-quality flag.*
4. **KSF derivative/fiduciary investigation (11/20-Nov-2026)** naming officers *and* directors, on top of the pending securities class action. *Escalate — board-level personal liability changes D&O dynamics and director retention risk.*
5. **Repeat material data breach (4.9M accounts, Apr-2026, vishing)** with a CT class action filed 1-Jun-2026. *Escalate — recurrency indicates unfixed process controls; unquantified state AG exposure.*
6. **Ownership transition:** Cox Enterprises now reports 46.15M shares as a 10% owner while Liberty Broadband sold ~$282M. *Escalate — verify the true share count and Cox's percentage; the $19.8B market cap implies ~135M shares, which would make Cox's block ~34% of the equity and a de facto control question.*
