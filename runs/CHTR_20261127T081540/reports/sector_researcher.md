## SECTOR BRIEF — Charter Communications (CHTR)
**Sector:** Communication Services › Telecommunications Services (US cable / broadband)
**Note on data:** FMP's sector-aggregate endpoint returned unusable single-company period rows, so all peer aggregates below are hand-computed from the five largest listed comps. Fiscal 2025 peer financials are complete; operating metrics are as of Q3 2026 (Charter's latest, post-Cox close 19-Aug-2026).

---

### 1. Sector Overview
US fixed broadband is a ~$64bn-revenue market (2025) that has flipped from growth to share-shift: total connections are still rising slowly, but cable — which holds roughly 60% of US broadband subscriptions — is now a net subscriber loser, having ceded an estimated 280k broadband subs in Q1 2026 alone (Comcast, Charter, Altice combined; Parks Associates), with New Street Research concluding cable is unlikely to return to broadband subscriber growth *this decade*. Competition has shifted from intra-cable to cross-technology: fixed wireless access (T-Mobile, Verizon) attacks the low-end on price, while fibre overbuilders (AT&T, plus private capital) attack the high-end on speed, in a product with near-zero switching costs. The sector is high-fixed-cost, capital-intensive (Charter alone spends ~$13bn/yr on capex) and consolidating as the only remaining lever — Charter/Cox, Charter/Liberty Broadband, and smaller cable tie-ups all closed in 2026. Concentration is moderate (top-3 cable + top-2 telco hold the majority of connections), but the relevant competitive set is widening, not narrowing.

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Consolidation-driven cost extraction is the sector's main earnings lever.** Charter targets **$2bn annualised run-rate opex savings over 18 months on top of >$1bn of Cox synergies** — against a ~$26bn pro forma EBITDA base. Comcast and Verizon are pursuing the same playbook (divestitures, headcount, network sharing). In a no-growth revenue sector, margin self-help is the P&L.
- **Convergence/mobile is genuinely growing.** Charter's Spectrum Mobile reached **13.5m lines, +15.4% y/y (+1.8m LTM, +508k in Q3)**; bundling mobile into broadband raises revenue per household and lowers churn. Mobile is the only line item in the cable P&L compounding at a double-digit rate and is the sector's clearest volume offset.
- **Capex has peaked, so FCF should inflect.** Charter's pro forma Q3 capex fell to **$3.2bn from $3.6bn** y/y on a footprint of **70.7m passings (+2.2%)**; the HFC/DOCSIS upgrade path lets operators sell faster upstream tiers from existing plant. Lower build intensity plus the $2bn cost programme is the bull case for free cash flow; Charter also deleveraged ~$1.5bn in the quarter via note exchanges.

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Structural subscriber loss in the core product.** Charter's pro forma internet customers fell **233k in Q3 2026 (−2.5% y/y to 34.3m)**, with penetration down 260bp to **51.9%** of passings; pro forma customer relationships −2.6% and video −1.8%. Cable is losing the unit-growth battle to FWA and fibre, and no pricing action fixes a share loss.
- **Pricing power has broken down.** Pro forma revenue **−1.5% y/y to $16.5bn** and pro forma Adjusted EBITDA **−4.0%**; residential revenue per customer fell **1.6% to $117.85/month**. Operators are answering FWA with no-data-cap plans and free mobile for a year — i.e., giving away price to defend units.
- **Balance sheet and cost inflation in a levered, capex-heavy model.** Charter carries ~$100bn of debt and a **3.5x net-debt/EBITDA target "within three years"** while funding ~$13bn of annual capex; the expiry of the ACP subsidy (2024) removed a demand prop for low-income connects, programming costs escalate, and Q3 included a **$6.3bn franchise impairment** — an accounting admission that the installed base is worth materially less.

### 5. Charter's Competitive Position: **AVERAGE**
**For:** Unmatched US cable scale post-Cox — 36.7m customer relationships, 34.3m internet customers, 70.7m passings, national mobile MVNO; highest operating margin in the peer set (24.4%) and a *rising* margin trend (21.0% → 24.4% over five years); a credible, quantified $3bn+ combined cost/synergy programme; and improving video trends (−77k vs −121k prior year), with early improvement in legacy Cox markets since the 16-Sep Spectrum launch.
**Against:** Charter sits on the wrong side of the sector's subscriber flow — penetration −260bp y/y in one year is a share-loss signature, not a temporary wobble; ARPU is deflating; and the balance sheet (~$100bn debt, 3.5x leverage target deferred three years) removes the buyback/price-investment flexibility that historically defined the cable equity story. Scale in a structurally shrinking product is a moat around a receding pond — it wins the cost war without solving the demand problem. Positioning: **scale/cost leader, structural share loser** — net average, with the self-help story being idiosyncratic rather than sector-supported.

### 6. Sector Rating: **UNATTRACTIVE**
Broadband/telecom-cable scores poorly on every framework dimension: **Porter's** — high rivalry (three technologies competing for one connection), high buyer power (zero switching costs, price-led churn), high substitution threat (FWA, satellite), and high fixed costs that prevent rational capacity exit; **cycle** — late-mature/declining in video and voice, with the growth engine (broadband) now in structural share erosion, so the sector's revenue line is flat-to-down and earnings growth must come from cost cuts; **financials** — capex ~20–25% of sales, leverage elevated, and the peer median posting just **+2.5% revenue growth**. The sector's best operators (TMUS) are gaining share from *outside* the wireline model, which is where the attractive economics sit. Charter-specific upside is a leveraged turnaround/cost-story bet against a deteriorating sector tape, not a sector tailwind — and the reported 30% rebound off the November low suggests that trade is already partly priced. **Individual names (TMUS, and CHTR only as an event-driven self-help case) can be owned; the sub-industry should be underweight.**
