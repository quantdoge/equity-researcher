- **Brand/demand interest has inflected.** "Spectrum internet" bottomed at 62–68 through Sept–Oct and has climbed to 78, +11% vs the same weeks a year ago. This is the first sustained up-sequence since the July collapse, and it lines up with the **Sept 16 rollout of Spectrum pricing/packaging across the former Cox footprint** (11M passings) — management called early demand "encouraging," and Trends corroborates that households are actively shopping the brand.
- **The churn-intent signal is the cleanest positive in the dataset.** "cancel Spectrum" has fallen 75 → 73 → 67 → 62 over four straight weeks, a 17% sequential decline heading into the Q4 print. Retention is improving.
- **But there is a real caveat: the outage series is deteriorating.** "Spectrum outage" spiked from 59 to 81 in two weeks — near the highest reading of the year. The ratio of cancel-intent to outage-intent has fallen from ~1.02 a year ago to 0.77 now. Translation: **recent search activity looks driven by a service-reliability event rather than competitive defection.** Outage events fade, but they raise churn and truck-roll opex in the quarter they occur — i.e., a November-dated headwind into a January report.
- **Web traffic: unavailable.** The SimilarWeb proxy returned only competitive-position color (spectrum.com ranks #1–2 vs. xfinity.com as of Oct '26) with no visit counts. I could not compute MoM traffic, so I have no independent read on digital order volume.

### 3. Earnings Preview Signal: **In-Line, tilting modestly ABOVE on subscriber KPIs, BELOW on opex/quality**

Consensus for Jan 29, 2027: **EPS $9.34, revenue $16.50B** (first clean combined-entity quarter).

- **Supporting the above-consensus read:** churn intent -17% sequentially, brand search +11% YoY, and the Cox-footprint relaunch broadening the addressable funnel. Legacy Charter internet losses were -156k in Q3; the alt data is consistent with that loss rate **narrowing** in Q4.
- **Against it:** the November outage spike argues for elevated churn and higher service cost in the exact quarter being reported, on top of a **$674M FCF quarter (down from $1.62B)** and a `$6.3B` franchise impairment already taken.
- **Estimate revision direction is DOWN, and the stock has outrun it.** Post-Q3 cuts: Citi to **$140** (Buy), Morgan Stanley to **$140**, JPMorgan to **$150**, UBS to **$140**. At $147.27, CHTR trades **at or above the freshly-cut targets from three of its four largest coverage houses.** A mean PT of ~$209 is stale (pre-collapse prints of $257/$302 still circulate). This is the single most important asymmetry: alt data says "modestly better operations," price says "recovery already discounted."
- **Historical surprise quality is useless as a guide:** 28.6% beat rate (2 of 7), average surprise -48% — entirely distorted by the -$20.50 impairment quarter. Revenue has beaten in 7 of 8 quarters; EPS quality is erratic. Weight revenue/KPIs, not EPS.

### 6. Overall Alt Data Signal: **NEUTRAL** (slight positive tilt on demand, offset by service-quality decay and stretched positioning)

The demand/retention sub-signals are the best they've been since mid-2025: churn intent down four weeks running, brand interest +11% YoY, Cox relaunch generating real shopping activity, mobile still compounding (+508k lines pro forma, 13.5M total, +1.8M YoY). But the core franchise is **still shrinking** — internet customers -233k pro forma (-156k legacy Charter, -77k legacy Cox), pro forma revenue -1.5%, pro forma adj EBITDA -4%. And the November outage spike tells me the current quarter carries a service-cost and churn penalty. With the stock **above freshly-cut Street targets** after a 43% six-week melt-up, alt data justifies "operations are stabilizing," not "operations are inflecting."

### 7. Confidence Level: **MEDIUM**

**Why not High:** Google Trends is an intent proxy, not a subscriber count, and the series is volatile/normalization-sensitive (see the Xfinity series, which broke from a 20→100→14 pattern and is unusable as a competitive benchmark). Three feeds failed outright — **web traffic (no visit counts), patents (API error), job-postings trend (level only, no time series)** — so I have no quantitative churn, traffic, or hiring-acceleration data. News sentiment is diluted by 13F auto-generated filler. There is also **no satellite/geospatial read** available for retail storefront or install activity, which would be the highest-value dataset here.

**Why not Low:** The `cancel Spectrum` sequential decline is a clean, directionally consistent, multi-week signal, and it cross-validates with improved video retention (-77k vs -121k) and the Sept 16 pricing relaunch. Two independent sources agree.

**Lag warning:** Google Trends is weekly and re-normalized retroactively; SimilarWeb/13F data lag 30–45 days. The Nov outage spike is ~2 weeks old and fully relevant to the Jan 29 print.

**Positioning implication:** Alt data supports a **constructive-through-earnings but not chasing** stance — operationally stable, valuation-stretched. Given price sits above Citi/MS/UBS's $140 targets, I'd want the January print to (a) show legacy Charter internet losses narrowing toward -100k, and (b) show November outage search interest mean-reverting below 65, before treating the 43% rally as a durable re-rating rather than an oversold squeeze. A short-dated premium-selling / put-spread structure into Jan 29 is the cleaner expression of "in-line operations, stretched price."
