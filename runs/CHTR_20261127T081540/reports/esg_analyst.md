# Charter Communications (CHTR) — Full ESG Analysis

**Snapshot:** US cable/broadband (Spectrum), ~91,900 employees, ~32M customers pre-Cox; 41 states. Market cap ≈ $19.8B at $147.27; ~$34.5B Cox acquisition closed Aug 2026 (FCC approval Feb 27, 2026; final CA approval Aug 2026), materially enlarging the balance sheet and the regulatory footprint.

**Data-quality caveat up front:** The 10-K and DEF 14A retrievals failed on this feed ("No 10-K/DEF 14A found"), and Charter's FY24 ESG Report PDF returned unparseable binary. Findings below rest on SEC/agency documents, regulatory dockets, litigation filings, and third-party assessments retrieved via search — not on Charter's self-reported ESG scores. Emission tonnages and safety rates are therefore **estimates/gaps, flagged as such**.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Carbon intensity:** Charter's footprint is dominated by purchased grid electricity for the HFC/network plant, plus fleet fuel (Scope 1) and set-top/customer-premises equipment and content in Scope 3. Estimated Scope 1+2 in the ~1–1.5 MtCO₂e range (unverified estimate from peer disclosure patterns; Charter does not report to CDP), implying roughly $20–30 of CO₂e per $1,000 revenue — low in absolute intensity terms for a $55B-revenue company, and *falling* per dollar as revenue grows.
- **Targets:** Committed to **carbon neutrality in operations (Scope 1+2) by 2035**, with purchased EACs reported as equal to or exceeding non-renewable electricity use, by region (FY24 ESG Report). Liberty Media publicly tracks Charter's 2035 carbon-neutrality goal. **Not SBTi-validated**, no Scope 3 target — weaker than AT&T/Verizon/Comcast on target credibility.
- **Disclosure grade:** Green America rates Charter **"D"** on climate/energy policy; it does not respond to CDP. SASB material issues flagged by Ceres: energy consumed by infrastructure, data privacy/security, product end-of-life (set-top boxes).
- **Transition risk:** Low. No meaningful carbon-tax, stranded-asset, or CBAM exposure. Real exposure is *cost* of electricity and renewable procurement, not compliance penalty.
- **Physical risk:** Medium. Concentrated aerial plant in FL/TX/Gulf/Southeast and CA wildfire zones → storm restoration capex and outage-driven regulatory risk (see §5).

**Verdict:** Environment is the least thesis-relevant pillar. It matters through operating cost and reputational/disclosure optics, not through stranded assets.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

This is where Charter's ESG risk concentrates, and where it is *cash-flow* material.

- **Customer / product safety & fairness:** ACSI 2026 has Spectrum at **71**, flat YoY, versus a fiber average of **76** — i.e., structurally below the technology that is taking its subscribers. Consumer Reports' 2026 member survey places Spectrum among the weakest on **value**. Historical anchor: **NY AG $174.2M consumer-fraud settlement (Dec 2018)** for advertising internet speeds it did not deliver (legacy TWC plant) — the largest single consumer-protection penalty in Charter's history and evidence the "speed claims" risk is recurring, not one-off.
- **Regulatory service quality:** FCC **$15M civil penalty** to settle an investigation into **911 and network-outage reporting** compliance — small in dollars, but a 911-failure category of risk is a license-to-operate issue for a regulated network operator.
- **Data privacy/security:** Confirmed breach via a third-party vendor after **ShinyHunters extortion threats**, reported at **~4.9M accounts**. Charter says no sensitive personal or network data was exfiltrated; that claim is untested. Expect notification/monitoring costs (est. $10–30M), state AG inquiry risk, and follow-on class actions.
- **Labour:** Workforce is overwhelmingly non-union. **~1,800 IBEW Local 3 members struck** over a contract dispute, with subsequent decertification/replacement-worker disputes — a labour-relations flashpoint. **CWA and public-interest allies petitioned the FCC to deny the Charter/Cox merger**; Cox brings a partially unionised workforce, raising integration friction.
- **DEI retrenchment:** Charter — historically a relatively strong DEI profile — **told the FCC it was ending its diversity and inclusion policies** and curtailed workforce DEI programmes in 2025. The **NYC Comptroller plus finance officers from six jurisdictions sent a joint letter** objecting. No direct cash cost, but it removes Charter from a slice of ESG-mandated and public-pension AUM and invites proxy/voting pressure.
- **Digital equity:** Now a contractual obligation rather than a choice: CPUC conditions include a **$20/month low-income broadband plan**, statewide promotional **price caps for three years ($30/100Mbps, $50/500Mbps, $70/1Gbps)**, and broadband buildout commitments; FCC conditions include **rural builds and job commitments**.

**Verdict:** High. Social factors here are not "nice-to-have" — they are churn, ARPU caps, and merger conditions.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10**

**Positives:**
- Chairman must be independent of both Liberty and Advance/Newhouse under the Stockholders' Agreement; a 2026 board overhaul rebalanced major-shareholder influence.
- CEO Christopher Winfrey (since Dec 2021) is an operator with a clear disclosure cadence; no evidence of fraud findings to date.
- No sustainability-linked debt with coupon step-ups identified — no direct ESG-covenant cash penalty.

**Negatives (why 4, not 6):**
1. **Control structure:** Advance/Newhouse holds **Class B common units with super-voting rights, exchangeable into Class A** — the Newhouse family retains outsized influence relative to economic ownership. The Liberty/Advance-Newhouse Stockholders' Agreement constrains board composition by contract, not by shareholder vote. Effectively a quasi-controlled company.
2. **Compensation & pay equity:** CEO pay ratio reported by labour-affiliated analysts at an extreme **~1,635:1** (Policy Matters Ohio, 2025), with 2025 CEO total comp ~$6.5M (third-party tabulation; treat as approximate). Metrics are heavily weighted to adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow — the same metrics that justify leverage-funded buybacks.
3. **Capital allocation as a governance failure:** Tens of billions of buybacks executed at $400–700/share, with the stock now ~$147 and the balance sheet re-levered by a $34.5B acquisition. This is the single largest destroyer of shareholder value in the story and is a governance (incentive-design) problem, not an operating one.
4. **Disclosure posture:** Charter fought investor ESG disclosure requests (2019 proxy contest by Ceres/Trillium) and only began reporting energy/GHG data under pressure; still no CDP response, no SBTi target, no Scope 3.
5. **Litigation overhang:** Securities fraud class action **SDNY 25-cv-06747** (class period 26 Jul 2024 – 24 Jul 2025) alleging misleading statements about the **ACP wind-down's impact on internet subscriber losses and EBITDA trajectory**; motion to dismiss filed 31 Mar 2026, still briefing. Parallel fiduciary-duty investigations by Kahn Swick & Foti and Johnson Fistel.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US federal (dominant):** FCC — 911/outage rules (already enforced), cybersecurity labelling programme, digital-discrimination rules, net-neutrality oscillation, USF/Lifeline, BEAD subsidy terms. Charter is US-only, so FCC and state PUCs *are* its ESG regulator.
- **State PUCs:** CPUC (California) is the binding constraint post-Cox — price caps, $20 low-income plan, buildout. NY PSC historically aggressive (2018 settlement, 2021 franchise scrutiny).
- **EU CSRD / CBAM:** **Minimal/none** — no material EU operations or EU-manufactured imports. Charter is effectively out of scope, a relative advantage vs European telcos.
- **SEC climate disclosure rule:** Stayed; Charter's Scope 1+2 would be reportable only if the rule survives in some form. No Scope 3 obligation.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** None identified. Charter's IG/HY notes carry no sustainability-linked margin ratchets — **no ESG-driven coupon risk**, unlike European peers.
- **Affordability regulation:** ACP expiry already drove measurable subscriber losses (the core of the securities suit); successor subsidy policy is a live revenue variable.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD**

Not "ESG Risk" — Charter's controversies are chronic and costly at the margin but not existential, and its environmental profile is benign. But it is a laggard on the three things ESG capital actually screens for: **disclosure depth (no CDP, no SBTi, thin Scope 3), social/product trust (below-fiber ACSI, repeated consumer-protection actions), and governance (super-voting family control, extreme pay ratio, value-destructive buybacks, active securities litigation).** Third-party scores corroborate: Green America **D**; S&P Global ESG and Sustainalytics coverage exists but at scores consistently in the bottom half of Communication Services. Environment alone would rate Average; the composite is dragged to Laggard by S and G.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL (with a negative tilt for ESG-constrained mandates)**

**Why neutral for the core thesis:** Charter's valuation debate is broadband competition, post-Cox deleveraging (~4.5x), and FCF durability. ESG factors are not the swing variable. There are three genuine ESG-to-cash-flow transmission channels, and all are already visible to the market:

1. **Regulatory conditions as a tax on the merger** — CA price caps and low-income mandates cost real NPV, but they were disclosed and are now sunk into the deal terms.
2. **Customer satisfaction → churn** — the 71 vs 76 fiber gap is arguably the best single forward indicator of subscriber trajectory; treat ACSI and Consumer Reports rankings as leading operating indicators, not as reputational noise.
3. **Litigation tail** — the ACP securities class action is the only ESG-adjacent item that could produce a genuine negative surprise; monitor the MTD ruling.

**Why a negative tilt for ESG-mandated capital:** Charter will be excluded from many Article 8/9 funds, many European ESG indices, and increasingly from US public-pension ESG screens following the DEI rollback and the six-jurisdiction letter. It offers no green-bond/SLL story and no credible 1.5°C-aligned target. That structurally caps the ESG-influenced bid and raises the marginal cost of equity at the margin.

**Bottom line for the memo:** ESG does not change the investment case, but it *removes a category of buyer* and adds a small, persistent litigation/regulatory drag. Score it as a **non-thesis, mild-negative overlay** — the correct action is to underwrite the ACSI/churn relationship and the securities-litigation outcome, and to ignore the environmental pillar except as an electricity-cost line.
