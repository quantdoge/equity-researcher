# Charter Communications (CHTR) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $147.27 | Market cap ~$17–20B | Net debt ~$105B | EV ~$125B | Data as of Nov 25, 2026*

---

## 1. Growth Story

Charter is **not a growth company — it is the disrupted incumbent in its own core market.** The 2026 combination with Cox and Liberty Broadband tripled down on a fixed-line broadband asset that is now *losing* subscribers to fixed wireless (T-Mobile/Verizon), fiber overbuilds, and satellite, while management's answer — a new operating model plus $3B of cost cuts — is a margin-defense story, not a TAM-capture story. The stock has correctly de-rated from ~$420 (May 2025) to $147, compressing from ~20x earnings to ~4x, and the burden of proof is now on whether cash flow can be harvested faster than the subscriber base erodes.

That is textbook capital misallocation at the top of the multiple.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

- **US residential broadband (~$85–90B revenue pool):** Charter serves 34.3M internet customers (~28% national share) post-Cox. **Penetration is the problem, not the opportunity** — footprint penetration is already high, and the incremental household is being won by FWA at $40–50/month. Charter's own footprint lost 233k internet customers in Q3 (156k legacy Charter + 77k legacy Cox) during the *first* quarter of ownership.
- **Cox's 11M added passings / 5M customers** is a legitimate under-penetration asset — but legacy Cox is *contracting faster* than legacy Charter, so integration starts from a shrinking base.
- **US wireless (~$200B service revenue):** Spectrum Mobile is the only genuine grower, but it is an **MVNO riding Verizon's network** — no spectrum, no RAN, no proprietary tech. Structurally a low-margin reseller, not a moat.
- **Innovation moat: essentially none.** There is no data network effect, no platform dynamic, no proprietary silicon or model. Charter's "AI investment" is back-office cost reduction (onshoring service calls), not product differentiation. In Cathie Wood terms: **CHTR is on the wrong side of the disruption curve** — the disruptors (FWA, LEO, fiber altnets) are taking its units.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4 2026 results — Jan 29, 2027** (consensus EPS $9.34, rev $16.5B). First full quarter with Cox; the binary event is the 2027 FCF/EBITDA framework and pro-forma subscriber disclosure. *<3 months.*
- **$2B opex program + $1B Cox synergies tracking** (18-month runway). Quantification/credibility check through 2027; most savings are headcount layers, not frontline.
- **CFO appointment** — Jessica Fischer departed Oct 15, 2026; a new CFO is an explicit statement on capital allocation (buyback vs. deleveraging). *Timing unknown.*
- **"Internet-first" net-add inflection** — new operating model with COO Nick Jeffery (ex-Frontier/Vodafone UK); first measurable evidence of churn stabilization likely H2 2027.
- **Deleveraging / asset monetization** — spectrum, tower, or non-core divestitures; any equity issuance is the key downside re-rate risk. 11.1-year weighted average debt life buys time.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **SELL**

Against a growth mandate, CHTR fails nearly every gate: negative revenue growth, Rule of 40 of **7.5**, flat EBITDA margins across five years, a shrinking core subscriber base, no innovation moat, and decelerating FCF ($6.1B in 2022 → $4.4B in 2025 → ~$2.7B annualized in Q3'26). This is a **leveraged restructuring / liquidation-candidate thesis masquerading as a value opportunity**, and it does not belong in a portfolio built on durable compounding.

To be explicit about the bull case I am rejecting: at ~4.8–5.5x EV/EBITDA on ~$26B pro-forma EBITDA, the equity is a cheap option. Rough scenario math: **bear** (EBITDA −8%/yr to $17B, 4.5x) = equity roughly worthless; **base** (EBITDA flat $26B, 4.75x, net debt $100B) ≈ $205/share, +39%; **bull** ($3B savings land, EBITDA $29B, 5.5x, deleveraging) ≈ $500+/share. The 1:5 skew is real — but it is a **credit-and-execution trade on a melting ice cube**, and paying up after a +44% off-the-low squeeze, above the median analyst target, with a first-quarter Cox subscriber loss of 77k, is the wrong entry for a growth portfolio. **Prefer the disruptors (TMUS at 9.7x EV/EBITDA, or fiber/LEO exposure) over the disrupted.** Revisit only below ~$100 (≈3.5x EV/EBITDA) or on evidence of two consecutive quarters of positive pro-forma internet net adds.

## 7. Key Risks

**To the downside (thesis-supporting):**
- **Secular share loss:** FWA + fiber + LEO are structural, not cyclical; price/volume trade-offs are deteriorating. A 2027 "doom loop" (fewer subs → higher unit costs → worse service → more churn) is the base-case risk.
- **Leverage:** $109B debt principal, ~$6B+ annual interest against ~$2.7B current FCF — interest coverage is now under 1x FCF. Equity is deeply levered to a 50bp move in EBITDA assumptions.
- **Execution:** $3B of cuts with a first-year Cox asset already shrinking; aggressive cost reduction risks NPS/service quality, accelerating churn.
- **Management churn:** CFO out, new COO in, CEO publicly conceding "we're not satisfied." Turnaround credibility is unproven.
- **Impairment already taken:** the $6.33B franchise write-down is management's own admission that the asset base is worth materially less.

**To my Sell call (what would make me wrong):** a credible 2027 guide showing FCF >$6B, pro-forma internet net adds turning positive, a takeover/merger approach (Comcast-style consolidation), or a large accelerated buyback at these prices. Any of those triggers a violent re-rate in a heavily shorted stock — which is precisely why this is a special-situations trade, not a growth investment.

**Data-quality caveats:** (1) The reported EV/market-cap multiples rely on the YE2025 balance sheet and are **stale pre-Cox** — true EV is materially higher. (2) Charter's gross-margin series (39% → 46%) is distorted by cost-of-revenue/SGA reclassification, not operating leverage; use operating margin. (3) Consensus EPS estimates are near-unusable — 2027 range of $16.43–$67.99 across 8 analysts, and the 2026 figure mixes the impairment. (4) The revision-score tool flagged a +27.6% "upgrade," but it is comparing the 2030 estimate against the 2029 period, not a true revision — **disregard it.** (5) Google Trends returned 12 months, not the requested 5 years.
