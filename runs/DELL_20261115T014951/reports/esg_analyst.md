**FY26 (ended 30-Jan-2026):** Revenue $113.5B, operating income $8.1B (non-GAAP $10.0B), diluted EPS $8.68 (non-GAAP $10.30), $7.5B returned to shareholders.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Strengths:**
- **Net-zero Scope 1, 2 and 3 by 2050** with quantified interim 2030 targets: −50% Scope 1+2 (vs FY20), **−45% Scope 3 purchased goods & services**, **−30% absolute Scope 3 use-of-sold-products**, 100% renewable electricity by 2030.
- Disclosure quality is genuinely above-peer: standalone GHG report, third-party assurance underway for Scope 1/2/3, energy, renewable electricity, packaging and water.
- Circularity leadership: **14.1% of product content from recycled/renewable/reduced-carbon material** (FY24) toward a >50% goal — best-in-class among US OEMs alongside HP.
- Product efficiency is a *revenue* argument: liquid-cooled PowerEdge claims up to 77% rack-footprint reduction and 4.5× GPU density vs air-cooled — this is how Dell monetises customer Scope 2.

**The central tension (underappreciated by the market):** Dell's growth engine is **structurally emissions-accretive**. A ~$95B AI-server backlog and record AI orders mean rapidly rising Scope 3 *use-phase* emissions and rising supplier-manufacturing emissions. The 2030 target of a **30% absolute reduction in use-of-sold-product emissions** was set against a pre-AI demand curve. Either the target gets restructured (a greenwashing/credibility headline risk) or Dell must lean heavily on customer renewable procurement and efficiency claims it does not control. **This is the single most likely source of a future ESG controversy for DELL.**

**Also:** CBAM direct exposure is minimal (Dell is not a primary steel/aluminium/cement importer), though aluminium in chassis/heat sinks creates indirect pass-through cost. Physical risk is concentrated upstream — Taiwan semiconductor/component concentration and water stress at Asian fabs.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Workforce — the dominant issue.** Dell cut roughly **11,000 roles in FY26 (−10.2%)**, taking headcount from ~108,000 to ~97,000, with **~$569M of exit costs** booked and a further **$261M of severance in Q1 FY27** per the 10-Q — i.e. **cuts are continuing, not complete**. Roughly **36,000 roles eliminated over three years**. Two-sided read: near-term margin support and a legitimate cost-efficiency narrative, but it is a *repeated* restructuring rather than a one-off, which erodes employer brand precisely when AI-infrastructure talent is scarcest. Retention risk in ISG/AI engineering is the real cost. Reported return-to-office pressure adds attrition risk (single low-quality source — **unverified**).

**Human rights / supply chain.** Dell was a defendant in *Doe v. Apple, Alphabet, Dell, Microsoft, Tesla* (DRC cobalt child labour, TVPRA) — the Ninth Circuit **affirmed dismissal in March 2024**, removing litigation overhang. However, cobalt/conflict-mineral traceability remains a live **UFLPA and EU Conflict Minerals Regulation** exposure, and Dell's own supply-chain traceability work (with Columbia SIPA) implicitly concedes the gap. No material product-safety or consumer-protection event identified.

**DEI.** The 2025–26 US corporate DEI retrenchment is a sector-wide reputational factor; Dell's specific program changes could not be independently verified — **treat as a gap.** Low financial materiality, moderate reputational/stakeholder materiality.

**Positives:** low-hazard assembly/light manufacturing profile (structurally better safety record than heavy industry), no unionised-manufacturing flashpoints of note, strong community/education franchise (HBCU and STEM partnerships) supporting US social licence and public-sector procurement eligibility.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10**

**What earns points:**
- Board has a **majority of independent directors and a Lead Independent Director** *despite* controlled-company status.
- Compensation and Nominating/Governance committees are **fully independent**, and Dell **voluntarily complies with NYSE independence standards** it is legally exempt from.
- Pay is genuinely performance-linked: multi-year PSUs weighted to **non-GAAP revenue, operating income and relative TSR**, with an independent consultant and a **recoupment (clawback) policy**. FY26 1-yr TSR of **+203%** means pay outcomes are defensible to shareholders.
- Two-tier ESG governance (ESG Steering Committee + ESG Interlock Team) with board oversight — functional, not decorative.

**What caps the score at 4:**
1. **Control trumps ownership.** Michael Dell beneficially owns ~265.7M Class C shares (~45.7% on an as-converted basis) carrying **~10 votes per share**, giving him **effective majority voting control** — a structure inherited from the 2013 LBO. Minority holders cannot elect directors, block a related-party transaction, or force strategic change. There is no realistic activist path on a $219B company.
2. **CEO/Chairman duality** — permanent, by virtue of control.
3. **Staggered/classified board** (FY26 ballot: seven Group I + one Group IV nominees) — entrenches management further.
4. **Delaware → Texas redomestication, approved 25-Jun-2026** (Board unanimously recommended after a six-meeting Evaluation Committee process). Management's stated rationale — codified business-judgment presumption, statutory D&O protections, Texas Business Court — is transparently **litigation-risk reduction**. Management itself disclosed the countervailing costs: **loss of Delaware precedent, potential 3% ownership thresholds for derivative suits, and higher thresholds for stockholder proposals.** Read plainly: this is a **reduction in shareholder rights**, and Bloomberg Law/Texas Tribune coverage questions the payoff. For a controlled company already insulated from shareholder pressure, this further widens the gap between ownership and control.
5. **Audit fees $33.9M** with PwC — no flag, but scale warrants monitoring.

**Verdict:** Above-average *committee hygiene* wrapped around *structurally deficient* shareholder rights. Score 4/10 is a governance-discount score, not an accounting-integrity score.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD / ESRS** — In scope (EU revenue far above thresholds); full E1–E5, S1–S4, G1 reporting. High data burden but Dell's assurance program puts it ahead of most peers. EU "Omnibus" simplification raises thresholds/delays some waves — monitor Dell's final wave.
- **EU CSDDD** — Supply-chain due diligence; timeline softened by the 2025 Omnibus but direction unchanged. Directly targets Dell's cobalt/minerals chain.
- **EU ESPR / Ecodesign, Batteries Regulation, WEEE/RoHS, right-to-repair, Digital Product Passport** — The most *operationally* binding ruleset: product design, repairability, recycled-content mandates. Material to unit economics in CSG.
- **EU Conflict Minerals Regulation** and **US Dodd-Frank §1502** — In scope; established compliance infrastructure.
- **US UFLPA** — Import-detention risk on China-linked inputs; the highest-probability supply-chain interruption channel.
- **US BIS/EAR export controls, Entity List, AI-diffusion rules** — **The binding constraint.** Dell is simultaneously expanding AI-server volume and holding China manufacturing/supplier exposure. Not classified as "ESG" but functionally a governance/license-to-operate risk with the largest cash-flow sensitivity.
- **California SB 253/261** — Scope 1–3 disclosure and climate-risk reporting; in scope by revenue. Enforcement posture remains contested.
- **SEC climate rule** — Effectively stayed/withdrawn; **low** current exposure, but this is precisely why Dell's voluntary GHG reporting is a competitive asset rather than a compliance cost.
- **CBAM** — Indirect/immaterial (aluminium pass-through only).
- **EU AI Act** — Limited provider obligations for AI hardware/infrastructure; emerging, low near-term cost.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

A genuinely bifurcated profile that nets out to the middle:

- **Environmental: above-peer / Leader-adjacent** — quantified net-zero pathway, assured Scope 1/2/3, circular-materials leadership, and efficiency-as-product.
- **Social: Average** — repeated double-digit workforce reductions and continuing severance are the weak point; human-rights litigation overhang has cleared.
- **Governance: Laggard** — majority voting control in the founder, CEO/Chairman duality, classified board, and a 2026 redomestication explicitly designed to reduce shareholder litigation and proposal rights.

The governance deficit is what prevents a "Leader" designation. ESG *disclosure* quality at Dell is high; ESG *accountability* to shareholders is low. Those are different things and the market frequently conflates them.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**Why neutral rather than negative:**
- No material environmental or social liability is embedded in the P&L. Dell is not a stranded-asset story, has no superfund-scale remediation exposure, and its Scope 3 problem is a *growth* problem, not a *fine* problem.
- Environmental positioning is a commercial **asset** — enterprise and public-sector procurement increasingly screens vendors on product carbon and circularity, and Dell is on the right side of that screen versus smaller ODMs.
- Social issues are cost-efficiency decisions management has deliberately made; they are already in consensus estimates.

**Why neutral rather than positive:**
- **Governance is a permanent, structural discount.** With Michael Dell holding effective majority voting control via 10-vote Class C stock, plus CEO/Chairman duality, a classified board, and a Texas charter that raises derivative-suit and shareholder-proposal thresholds, there is **no mechanism for shareholders to influence capital allocation, succession, or strategy.** For a cyclical, capital-return-heavy hardware business, that is a real cost-of-equity item, and it forecloses the activist/M&A catalyst that often re-rates hardware names.
- **The most financially material ESG-adjacent risk is mislabelled by the market.** Export-control compliance (China shipments, AI-diffusion rules) is not in most ESG screens but is the one factor here capable of moving hundreds of millions of dollars. The Nov-2026 sector probe and Dell's 8% single-day drawdown demonstrate how quickly that tail risk prices.
- **Scope 3 target integrity is a live watch item.** If AI-server volumes keep compounding, the 2030 use-phase reduction target becomes arithmetically implausible and a restatement would damage the credibility that currently differentiates Dell's reporting.

**Bottom line for the memo:** ESG is **not** a thesis driver for DELL and should not be sized as one. It is a **modest cost-of-capital drag via governance** and a **monitoring flag on trade-compliance and Scope 3 target credibility**. Treat the controlled-company structure as a permanent feature, not a fixable defect — no engagement campaign changes it. Position sizing and catalyst timing on DELL should be driven by AI-server backlog conversion and ISG margins, with governance as a discount applied to the multiple rather than a reason to own or avoid.

**What would change the rating:** (a) a disclosed BIS penalty or license restriction on AI-server shipments → downgrade to **ESG Risk**; (b) formal restatement of the 2030 Scope 3 target → downgrade to **ESG Risk** on credibility; (c) any dilution of Michael Dell's voting control or de-staggering of the board → upgrade toward **Average+/Leader**.
