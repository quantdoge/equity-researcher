## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (NASDAQ: SPCX)

**As of:** November 17, 2026 | **Price:** $141.70 | **Market cap:** ~$1.87T | **IPO:** June 12, 2026 at $135/share
**Note on record:** SPCX has no 10-K and no DEF 14A on file yet (IPO'd June 2026; first 10-K due ~Q1 2027). Governance conclusions below draw on the IPO prospectus/424(b)(4), 10-Q certifications, and third-party analysis. This is disclosed as a data gap, not papered over.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

This is the single most important finding in this report, and it is structural rather than fixable.

- **Perpetual minority control by design.** Class B carries 10 votes/share, Class A one. Musk holds ~40–42% of equity but ~80–82.4% of votes. Critically, Class B converts to low-vote Class A on transfer to anyone *other than Musk*, and new Class B may only be issued to Musk and Musk-related entities. Per Bebchuk/Kastiel (Harvard Program on Corporate Governance, May 2026), Musk can therefore sell down to **~9% of equity while retaining an absolute control lock** — and could add non-voting share classes to go lower still. Alignment between controller and public holders is engineered to decay.
- **"Controlled company" status.** Per the 424(b)(4), SpaceX qualifies as a controlled company under exchange rules — exempting it from majority-independent board, independent compensation committee, and independent nominating requirements. Board independence, comp structure, and related-party review are **unverifiable** absent a proxy. Morningstar characterised the IPO as carrying "a long list of unfriendly shareholder policies"; multiple pension funds reportedly declined on governance grounds.
- **No shareholder remedy.** Dual-class is permanent — no sunset, no time-based or ownership-based conversion. Minority holders have no path to correct inefficiency.
- **Musk is CEO and sole named principal executive officer**, personally signing the SOX 302 certification on the Oct 22 10-Q. Chairman/CEO duality is unconfirmed but the voting structure makes board capture moot either way.
- **Conflicts to watch:** floated Tesla–SpaceX merger (which Democrats say they may oppose), Musk's DOGE/government role concurrent with >$700M of federal funding, and Starlink consumer data being redirected to xAI AI-training (Jan 2026 privacy policy change). Related-party flow is large and unaudited by any public proxy.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**SEC:** Clean. EDGAR full-text search returns **no SEC enforcement actions** naming SPCX. First-year reporting appears uncontested.

**FAA (highest-frequency exposure):**
- May 22, 2026 Starship Flight 12: Booster 19 aborted boostback / hard splashdown → FAA required a formal mishap investigation (confirmed May 27) before Flight 13.
- Flight 13 cleared with no mishap investigation.
- **Nov 13, 2026 flight: booster catch attempt scrapped** after an early in-flight issue; booster lost to the Gulf. Root cause still open (reported low-fuel-mass alarm). Licensing gates couple every technical anomaly directly to launch cadence and therefore Starlink V3 deployment timing.
- Historical precedent: FAA proposed **$633,009** in civil penalties (disputed by SpaceX); OSHA issued a **$115,850** fine for seven "serious" violations.

**Environmental/NEPA:** Active litigation track (Surfrider Foundation and others vs. FAA over Boca Chica review); FAA draft tiered Environmental Assessment comment period extended July 2026 — meaning **launch-expansion approvals are pending, not secured**. A Memphis data-center pollution suit is also outstanding.

**FCC / spectrum:** SpaceX has won approvals to acquire spectrum without auction (e.g., 15 MHz in 1695–1710 MHz from Tristar); incumbent carriers are actively contesting a ~290 MHz satellite reallocation. This is a live, well-funded lobbying war against the largest carriers — adversarial regulatory risk, not a rubber stamp.

**Congress (most escalated):** After the Democratic midterm sweep, oversight is turning adversarial: subpoenas on SpaceX contracts, Starshield/Ukraine access restrictions, the DOGE role, and >$700M in taxpayer funding. Tesla + SpaceX shed a combined **$420B in market value in two days** on this fear before recovering. Expect Q4'26–2027 headline risk and possible legislative pressure on contract awards.

**Data privacy:** Starlink's revised policy permitting customer data for AI training creates GDPR (Ireland is a named market) and state-AG exposure, plus Senate/regulatory attention on satellite data retention.

### 3. Insider Activity Analysis

- **Only three Form 4 filings since the June 2026 IPO:** 6/17/26, 9/15/26, 9/24/26. Extraordinarily sparse for a company this size.
- **No evidence of open-market insider selling** — notable given the stock is ~37% below its June peak. Read as neutral-to-positive (also consistent with lock-ups binding).
- **Lock-up is staggered, and the first major unlock is imminent: December 8, 2026** — up to **~797.6M shares** (or ~328.4M if an August price-triggered early release fired). One reported structure bars sales before day 368 unless the stock is ≥30% above IPO price — a meaningful cushion that should blunt the first unlock.

- **Gap:** the filings feed does not give transaction direction/size per Form 4. Direction is inferred, not verified. Do not treat this as confirmed accumulation.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Key-person concentration at an extreme.** One individual is CEO, controlling shareholder (80%+ vote), chief technical decision-maker, and simultaneously runs Tesla, xAI and X, while engaged in national politics. There is no disclosed succession plan and no board able to constrain him. A health, legal, or attention event is unhedgeable and would be violent for a $1.87T equity.
- **Starship execution risk now on public display.** Consecutive booster anomalies (Flight 12 mishap, aborted Nov 13 catch) mean reusability — the core of the next-gen Starlink unit-economics case — remains unproven, while every failure triggers an FAA review cycle. Technical risk and regulatory risk are the *same* risk here.
- **Infrastructure, customer and capital concentration.** Launch depends on a small number of pads (Starbase/Boca Chica — itself under NEPA challenge — and Cape Canaveral); revenue leans on U.S. government (NASA, NSSL, Starshield) plus Starlink; and the reported **$4B bond sale to fund Nvidia AI compute** adds leverage and capital intensity to a company with no public-company track record. Add Starlink's status as a state-actor cyber target, with no disclosed incident history either way.

---

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment

**Elevated and consumer-facing.** The brand is fused to a politically polarised founder — an asset in raising capital, a liability in selling broadband. Concrete manifestations: pension funds publicly declining the stock on governance/ESG grounds; European and Canadian consumer markets (both material to Starlink) carrying higher Musk-backlash and GDPR sensitivity; the Ukraine/Starshield episode producing congressional and international accusations of politically motivated service restriction; and environmental-justice litigation at Boca Chica and the Memphis data center. The Jan 2026 privacy-policy change (customer data → AI training) is a reputational landmine with regulators and subscribers simultaneously. Expect sentiment to move on Musk statements, not on SpaceX fundamentals.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical — there is no fraud indicator, no SEC enforcement, no going-concern-style governance failure, and the operational safety record has not yet produced a casualty event. But the combination of (a) permanently disenfranchised public holders, (b) an unconstrained and over-committed key person, (c) a live adversarial Congress, (d) repeated FAA mishap cycles, and (e) an imminent lock-up unlock is a materially worse non-financial profile than the average mega-cap. This risk is **not** priced by standard factor models, and it cannot be diversified away within a large-cap industrials allocation (there is no comparable at $1.87T; the closest peers — GE Aerospace, RTX, Lockheed, Boeing, all $130–340B — carry conventional governance).

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Controller can cut equity to ~9% and keep control.** Alignment decays mechanically over time; no sunset. → Escalate to voting/policy committee; flag for any ESG or governance-screened mandate.
2. **Congressional subpoena cycle beginning now** (contracts, Starshield/Ukraine, DOGE, funding). Binary headline risk on government revenue. → Monitor committee dockets weekly.
3. **December 8, 2026 lock-up unlock** — up to ~797.6M shares. Near-term supply event, three weeks out.
4. **Repeated FAA mishap events** — a crewed or fatal failure would be a step-change in legal and reputational exposure, and the safety record is being established in real time as a public company.
5. **Un-vetted related-party complex** — potential Tesla merger, xAI data sharing, Musk's government role. No proxy exists to test these. Escalate as a conflict-of-interest watch item.
6. **ICFR untested at public scale.** No 10-K yet; first annual audit cycle under SOX 404(b) lands in 2027. Combined with an 8-month reporting history, control failures would surface late.
