## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Carbon intensity — low, and largely a red herring.** Launch emissions (kerosene soot from Falcon; methane from Starship) plus satellite reentry are immaterial against group revenue. Musk's rebuttal — *"unfortunately, electric rockets are impossible"* — is glib but directionally correct: an emissions-intensity screen is the wrong lens for this business. Rough order of magnitude: sub-0.5 Mt CO₂e/yr against $15bn+ revenue.

**The genuine environmental exposures are three, and none is a carbon-intensity problem:**

1. **Non-CO₂ atmospheric effects.** Starlink satellites now constitute ~40% of all reentering satellite debris, with reentries occurring near-daily. Alumina and other metal oxides injected into the stratosphere/mesosphere, plus black carbon from RP-1 at 20–40km altitude, have outsized radiative-forcing potency per gram. SpaceX publishes a "Commitment to Space Sustainability" and demisability standards — **self-reported, unaudited, no third-party verification.**
2. **Launch-site permitting and litigation.** TCEQ found four unpermitted industrial wastewater discharges (Mar–Jul 2024); EPA entered a Clean Water Act consent agreement and final order ($148,378), with combined 2024 state/federal environmental penalties ~$152,128. The FAA is preparing a **programmatic EIS covering up to 456 annual launches and 912 landings** at Starbase, and has proposed a rule waiving several environmental requirements for commercial launch — drawing Earthjustice-led opposition. **This is the material item:** a cadence restriction or injunction delays Starlink replenishment and Starship ramp, which is a multi-billion-dollar revenue-timing risk, not a fine.
3. **Physical climate risk.** Starbase is a low-lying coastal Texas site exposed to hurricane and storm surge; launch cadence is already weather-dependent.

**No climate targets, no TCFD disclosure, no Scope 3 inventory, no internal carbon price.** For a company of this size seeking index inclusion and a reported ~$20bn bond sale, that absence is itself a disclosure gap.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Safety record is the standout issue.** A Reuters investigation ("At SpaceX, worker injuries soar in Elon Musk's rush to Mars") documented injury rates well above aerospace peers. OSHA cited SpaceX for **7–8 serious violations** after a crane collapse at Starbase ($115,850 in penalties at maximum rates, being contested), and is now investigating the **first workplace fatality at Starbase — a fall on 15 May 2026**. This is a genuine licence-to-operate and talent-retention issue: 22,000 employees doing high-hazard work under schedule pressure.
- **Labour relations.** The NLRB's long-running unfair-labour-practice complaint was **dismissed on 9 February 2026** after the Board relinquished jurisdiction — a procedural win for SpaceX (which had challenged the agency's constitutionality) rather than an exoneration on merits. Separately, a suit by eight engineers alleging retaliatory dismissal after raising sexual-harassment and racial-discrimination concerns is proceeding.
- **Diversity:** no EEO-1 or workforce-composition disclosure. Leadership is male-dominated; Musk's public anti-DEI posture creates polarisation risk with EU/UK institutional capital and constrains the hiring pool.
- **Community/social licence:** Boca Chica/Starbase beach closures, sonic booms, wildlife (plover) litigation, and Carrizo/Comecrudo tribal opposition constitute a persistent local permitting friction.
- **Product safety** is arguably the most under-appreciated social factor: human-rated Dragon flights and ground-casualty risk from reentry fragmentation (the ESA's 2026 Space Environment Report adds a new ground-casualty-risk metric). A crew-loss event would be catastrophic for the Starshield/government franchise.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **2 / 10**

This is among the weakest governance profiles of any mega-cap US issuer. The five New York City pension systems, the NY State Common Retirement Fund and CalPERS (combined AUM >$1T) wrote to SpaceX on 13 May 2026 describing it as *"the most management-favorable governance structure ever brought to the U.S. public markets at this scale."*

| Provision | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Dual-class** | Class A = 1 vote; Class B = 10 votes. Musk ~42% of equity but **~79–85% of votes** |
| **Perpetual control** | **No sunset.** B shares auto-convert to A if sold to non-Musk holders; *new* B shares may be issued only to Musk/affiliates → his voting share ratchets *up* over time. Modelling suggests he could fall to ~9% of equity while retaining absolute control |
| **CEO removal** | Removal requires a Class B vote — i.e. **Musk's own consent**. Effectively unfireable |
| **Board** | Controlled-company status → exempt from majority-independent board requirements. Musk holds CEO + CTO + Chair simultaneously while running Tesla, xAI, Neuralink, Boring Co. |
| **Shareholder rights** | **Mandatory arbitration** of shareholder claims; Texas-law provisions limiting derivative litigation |
| **Related party** | xAI absorbed in an all-stock deal (Feb 2026, terms opaque); Tesla–SpaceX special committees formed Oct 2026 to explore combination; documented Tesla/SpaceX/xAI transactions 2017–2025 |
| **Compensation alignment** | No disclosure yet — no DEF 14A. Given control, expect Musk-favourable terms |

**Why 2 and not 1:** Gwynne Shotwell as President/COO is a genuinely credible operator and a partial mitigant to key-man risk; the company does have institutional investors engaged; and the structure is disclosed transparently in the S-1 rather than buried. **Why 2 and not 4:** Bebchuk & Kastiel's small-minority-controller framework applies almost perfectly — the theoretical agency cost scales as Musk's *equity* stake falls while his *control* persists, and the structure is explicitly designed to make that divergence permanent. MSCI's own governance score is **3.2/10**; my score is lower because MSCI under-weights the arbitration and derivative-litigation bars.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US — FAA:** strongest lever. Programmatic EIS (456 launches/yr) plus proposed environmental-waiver rule; any adverse outcome directly throttles Starship cadence.
- **US — EPA/TCEQ:** Clean Water Act consent order imposes ongoing compliance obligations and future permitting scrutiny.
- **US — OSHA / NLRB / EEOC:** active. Politically salient given Musk's public profile — enforcement intensity may be administration-dependent in both directions.
- **US — SEC:** climate-disclosure rule currently neutered, so **no mandatory Scope 1/2/3 for now**. Risk is litigation/risk-factor adequacy, not climate reporting.
- **EU:** SpaceX's EU prospectus was BaFin-approved (5 June 2026) and it operates Starlink in Ireland → **CSRD scope is live** (22,000 employees, EU turnover above the €450m threshold post-Omnibus). Expect first mandatory sustainability statement within ~1–2 years, including Scope 3. Also subject to the **EU ESG Rating Regulation** (transparency/conflict-of-interest rules on the raters themselves) and **SFDR**-driven exclusion from Article 8/9 funds at CCC.
- **CBAM:** negligible — SpaceX is not a material importer of covered goods.
- **ESG-linked debt:** none disclosed. The mooted **~$20bn bond sale** is the transmission channel — a CCC rating on a large new issue could add roughly 10–25bp, i.e. **~$20–50m/yr** on $20bn. Small, but a real and growing drag if leverage rises.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **ESG RISK**

Not merely a laggard — this screens into exclusion territory. A **CCC** MSCI rating, **1/10** controversies score, **3.2/10** governance, an active fatality investigation, and governance provisions expressly engineered to foreclose shareholder accountability place SPCX in the bottom decile of large-cap US issuers. It is simultaneously one of the most important companies in the world, which is precisely the discomfort the rating exposes: MSCI measures *risk management*, not *social utility*, and on that measure SpaceX is genuinely poor.

**One legitimate counterpoint:** the rating agencies' own 2022 *Review of Finance* divergence findings mean a single CCC is not a verdict. And Jefferies' ESG head has argued investors overstate the governance discount given Musk's demonstrated value creation. I weight that at maybe 20%.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE**

**But be precise about *why* — this is a governance/valuation issue, not an environmental one.**

- **Environmental and social risks are not thesis-breaking.** Sub-$300k of total penalties against a $1.87T market cap. Do not sell SPCX on carbon intensity or OSHA fines; that is noise.
- **Governance is value-relevant and unpriced by consensus.** A perpetual, unfireable, sub-10%-equity controller with mandatory arbitration and a documented pattern of related-party absorption (xAI; Tesla talks) warrants a **5–10% valuation haircut** on agency-cost grounds — roughly **$90–190bn**. The Bebchuk/Kastiel mechanism is not theoretical here: the charter is designed to let Musk's economic stake fall while control persists, which is exactly when agency costs peak.
- **Cost of capital:** CCC status plus EU SFDR exclusion narrows the buyer base precisely as the company seeks ~$20bn of new debt and eventual index inclusion.
- **The real tail risk is key-man, not climate.** One individual is CEO, CTO and Chair while running several other companies, cannot be removed without his own consent, and has no disclosed succession plan. That is a governance risk with a genuine fat-tail payoff profile.
- **Watch items that would change the call:** (1) independent special-committee terms on the Tesla combination; (2) whether the charter includes *any* sunset in the final S-1 versus pre-IPO press reports; (3) first CSRD sustainability statement — the first real Scope 1/2/3 data; (4) Starbase fatality findings; (5) FAA programmatic EIS outcome for 456 launches/yr.

**Bottom line for the memo:** own SPCX for the launch monopoly and Starlink cash generation, but size it as a controlled-company position, apply a governance discount to your target multiple, and do not expect ESG-constrained or European Article 8/9 capital to be a marginal buyer.
