# Macroeconomic Assessment — Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SPCX)

**Analyst:** Macroeconomy Researcher | **Data as of:** 2026-11-17 (last close $141.70) | **Sources:** FRED, FMP, press

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: RISK-OFF / Transitional — a re-tightening cycle in progress.**

The critical finding is that the US is **not** in the easing regime the 2024–25 data implies. The Fed cut from 5.33% (Aug 2024) to 3.63% by June 2026, then **reversed**: a 25bp hike on **2026-09-16** to 3.75–4.00% — the first hike in nearly three years — and market-implied odds of another 25bp on **2026-12-09 sit at 91–100%**, with a live 50bp tail (Waller, Musalem). FOMC median dots put fed funds at **4.00–4.25% through end-2027**; Musalem has flagged "several hikes before spring 2027."

The driver is a **reflation** in which inflation has re-accelerated while growth *re-accelerated too*: CPI went from +2.65% y/y (Dec 2025) to **+3.82% y/y (Oct 2026)**, and real GDP growth rose from +2.6% y/y (Q1 2026) to **+2.1% y/y / ~+3.4% annualised (Q3 2026)**. Unemployment has *fallen* to **4.1%** from 4.5% (Nov 2025). This is a textbook hawkish mix — too hot to cut, too strong to break.

The single most important nuance for a long-duration equity: **10Y breakevens are falling** (2.38% on Oct 28 → **2.21%** on Nov 17) even as headline CPI runs at 3.8%. The entire +65bp rise in the 10Y since SPCX's IPO (**4.48% on 2026-06-12 → 5.13%**) is **real yield**, not inflation compensation. Implied real 10Y = **2.92%**, up from **2.17%** at IPO. That is the mechanism squeezing this stock.

Meanwhile credit is **wide open** — HY OAS 334bp, near cycle tights despite widening 69bp off the Sept 11 low of 265bp. Easy credit + high real yields = a market that will fund capex but will not pay for distant cash flows.

A material institutional wildcard: a **new Fed Chair** took office during this period with documented political pressure for lower rates, *while* CPI sits at 3.8%. Credibility risk at the Fed is a live, unquantifiable tail.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

SPCX is arguably the **purest long-duration asset in the US market**: a $1.87T market cap with essentially all value in a Starship/Starlink terminal value 10–25 years out, funded by enormous front-loaded capex. Macro transmission is therefore dominated by the **discount rate**, not the demand cycle.

| Macro variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Real 10Y +75bp since IPO** | **Strongly negative** | Dominant variable. At ~25-yr duration, +75bp ≈ **−14 to −19%** mechanical fair-value hit. Explains the de-rating. |
| **Fed hiking into 2027** | **Negative** | Compresses multiples on zero-near-term-FCF growth; raises the hurdle rate on Starship capex and Starlink V3 buildout. |
| **CPI +3.82%, sticky** | **Mixed / mildly positive** | *Positive:* genuine pricing power — Starlink subscription pricing in underserved markets, and near-monopoly US launch pricing passes through costs. Reusability delivers structural unit-cost deflation that beats input inflation. *Negative:* 22,000 employees at 4.1% unemployment = wage pressure; aerospace supply chain (titanium, aluminum, avionics) inflating. |
| **GDP +3.4% SAAR** | **Positive** | Commercial satellite operators' capex and launch cadence are pro-cyclical; a strong economy underwrites rideshare demand and enterprise Starlink. |
| **Unemployment 4.1% (falling)** | **Positive (demand) / negative (cost)** | Low churn on consumer broadband; offset by wage inflation. |
| **HY OAS 334bp — easy credit** | **Positive** | Underappreciated tailwind: satellite-fleet capex is debt-funded, and SpaceX can raise cheaply. Easy credit expands the customer pool of launch buyers. |
| **USD +3.8% in 2 months** | **Mildly negative** | Launch contracts are USD-denominated — a stronger dollar makes them dearer for Ireland/Canada/international buyers and hurts Starlink affordability in EM local-currency markets. Small share of revenue; a translation, not a solvency, issue. |
| **Bear-steepening curve (+39bp)** | **Negative** | Long-end-led rise is the worst shape for equity duration; 30Y at 5.47% is the discount-rate anchor for infrastructure-like cash flows. |
| **Defense fiscal impulse** | **Strongly positive, GDP-insensitive** | ~$8bn Golden Dome + ~$6bn Starshield contracts. Funded by geopolitics and the deficit, not the cycle — a genuine diversifier from macro beta. |
| **Commodity inputs** | **Unquantified gap** | No FRED commodity series pulled within budget; propellant/energy/metals are a real exposure left unmeasured. |

**Cross-check on price data:** SPCX fell **−53.5%** from its June 16 intraday high of **$225.64** to **$104.83** (Aug 3), then rallied **+68%** to **$176.42** (Oct 6) — that rally was *idiosyncratic* (Starship 14 test, Golden Dome, Nasdaq-100 weight up from 1.28% to 2.25–2.82%). The subsequent **−30%** slide to **$123.62** (Nov 5) is the one that coincides cleanly with macro: Fed hike, real yields to 5.31%, HY OAS 303→348bp, USD +1.5%. **SPCX is now trading macro, not story.**

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Not because the business is cyclically exposed — end-market demand is the best part of the setup (3.4% growth, easy credit, a shooting-gallery defense budget). It is **Unfavourable purely on the discount rate**: real 10Y at 2.92% and rising, a Fed that will hike on Dec 9 and likely again into 2027, and a 30Y at 5.47%, against an asset whose entire value is in 2035+ cash flows.

Add two dated hazards converging on **December 9, 2026**: the FOMC decision *and* the approximate expiry of the 180-day post-IPO lockup ("SpaceX Unlocks"). A hawkish 50bp surprise plus fresh share supply is the single worst combination this security can face.

One honest limitation: **no balance sheet or debt-maturity data was available in this dataset**, so refinancing risk cannot be assessed. Beta is reported as 0 (no history) — with only ~5 months of trading, no reliable macro beta exists; the −53% drawdown implies realised vol near ~50–60% annualised.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**Bear (non-consensus, high severity): the breakeven market is wrong.**

The 10Y breakeven of **2.21%** is pricing a confident return to target. That is inconsistent with CPI at **3.82%**, growth at **~3.4% SAAR**, and unemployment at **4.1% and falling** — a combination that historically does not resolve itself benignly. My base case is the Fed does *not* stop at 4.00–4.25%.

If the December 9 meeting delivers **50bp** (a live tail) and dots move to 4.50–4.75% for 2027, the real 10Y goes from 2.92% toward **3.4–3.5%**. On a ~25-year duration asset that is a further **−20 to −25%** of fair value *on top of* what has already occurred. Combined with lockup supply, SPCX breaks the **$104.83** August low and trades toward **$85–95** — a −35% move from $141.70 — with the irony that **the underlying operating news (Starship, Starlink V3, Golden Dome) could be excellent throughout.** The stock falls on arithmetic, not on fundamentals.

**Bull mirror case** (for symmetry): a H1 2027 growth scare as the 69bp of HY widening since September extends — flight to quality takes the 10Y to 4.3%, real yields to ~2.0%, and SPCX re-rates **+35–45%** to $190–205 with zero change in business fundamentals.

The asymmetry is entirely a function of one number: **the real 10Y**. That is the variable to monitor, and it is currently moving the wrong way.
