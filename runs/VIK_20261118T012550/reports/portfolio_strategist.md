## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT** (high agreement on quality, split on price)

**Consensus FOR (high agreement, ~9 of 11 agents):** This is a genuinely high-quality business. F-Score 8/9, ROIC 21.9%, net leverage 1.07x, gross margin 34.1%→39.0% in two years, negligible goodwill ($89M on $13.9B assets), and a demand book — 68% of 2027 core sold, 77% ocean — that is objectively de-risked. **There is no accounting fraud.** The Short analyst, tasked with finding one, explicitly rejected it.

**Consensus AGAINST (high agreement, ~8 of 11):** Two things are broken. (a) **Valuation** — 24.3x EV/EBITDA and 36.7x earnings against a 15x cruise-peer norm, with a 2–3.6% FCF yield. (b) **Recurring climate impairment** — every agent from ESG to Alt Data to Sector independently flagged the European low-water issue as structural, not weather, with the voucher drag capping 2027 net yield at low-to-mid single digits and Mizuho modelling the drag through 2028.

**The split:** *Growth* and *Alt Data* underwrite 2027 forward numbers (21.9x 2027E, 0.88 PEG) and see a recovery the market hasn't re-rated. *Value*, *Quant* and *Short* underwrite the same facts and conclude the price already discounts that recovery — and more. **This is not a factual dispute; it is a discount-rate dispute.** When the disagreement is purely about price, the answer is sizing, not conviction.

**Conflict resolution — three things the bulls under-model:**
1. **The tax cliff.** The 1.7% effective rate on $1.17B pre-tax is NOL shielding against a −$4.16B retained deficit. Normalising to Bermuda's 15% Pillar Two rate removes **13–15% of EPS** with no operational change. Neither Growth nor Value models this. It is the single most under-priced item in the dataset.
2. **The 2027 bridge.** Consensus needs +21.5% revenue and +50% EPS on 9.2% trailing growth. Growth's own report flags $8.90B of 2027 revenue as "2–4 points optimistic."
3. **The near-term catalyst is negative.** Alt Data expects a Q4 miss on Mar 2, 2027. Buying three months ahead of a likely miss at 11% above fair value is a poor entry, not a contrarian one.

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector concentration.** The vendor taxonomy is broken (it groups VIK with AMZN/TSLA/HD); true classification is **Travel Services / Leisure**. Viking's demand driver — affluent, 55+, discretionary travel — is *shared* with airlines, hotels, casinos and the other cruise lines even though the correlation is imperfect. **Cap aggregate travel/leisure at 5% of the book**, and treat VIK as consuming the same risk budget as an airline or lodging position.

**Factor exposure — the binding constraint.** VIK is **long high-beta (1.61), long quality (ROIC 21.9%, F-Score 8/9), short value (1.0/5, last in its own industry), long momentum.** It is therefore a poor fit for a book already tilted to long-duration growth, and it is a *natural casualty* of the macro regime the Macro analyst describes — a bear-steepening curve at a 5.13% 10Y mechanically compresses a 20x+ multiple. That is not a thesis; it already happened (29% de-rating Aug–Sep). **If the portfolio already carries high-multiple, high-beta growth, VIK is redundant beta and should be skipped regardless of price.**
