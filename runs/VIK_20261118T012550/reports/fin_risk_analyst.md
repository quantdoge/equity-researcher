## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: SUB-INVESTMENT GRADE (upper-tier high yield / crossover candidate)**

| Source | Rating | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Moody's | **Ba2** CFR, affirmed | Projects debt/EBITDA <3.0x by 2027; FFO+interest/interest → ~6.0x |
| S&P | **BB-** issuer (upgraded) | $1.7B unsecured notes due 2030 rated **BB**, recovery rating '4' |

The credit story is one of rapid post-COVID repair. Net debt has fallen from $4,035M (2023) → $3,083M (2024) → **$1,937M (2025)**, a 52% reduction in two years, while EBITDA roughly doubled. Book equity flipped from **–$219M to +$1,121M**. Interest expense has declined three consecutive years ($480M → $380M → $363M) despite flat gross debt, indicating refinancing into lower coupons.

The single most important credit fact: **net leverage is 1.07x** — comfortably inside BB territory and approaching investment-grade metrics. The constraint on a rating upgrade is not leverage but the **thin book equity base (equity/assets = 9%)** and the still-negative $4.16B accumulated deficit, which will take years of retained earnings to clear.

**Verdict: no near-term default risk. Upgrade path to BB/BB+ is credible if FCF holds.**

### Critical caveat on the Altman Z-Score
The 0.41 "Distress" reading is **structurally misleading for this company** and should not be taken at face value:

- **x2 = –0.34** (retained earnings / assets) is negative purely because of the COVID-era accumulated deficit, not current losses. The company earned $1,148M in 2025.
- **x1 = –0.10** (working capital / assets) is negative because **customer deposits are booked as current liabilities**. This is a *funding source*, not a liquidity problem — Viking collects cash from passengers a year before sailing. Every cruise operator shows this.
- **x4 = 0.10** uses *book* equity. Substituting market cap ($42.1B / $11.1B liabilities = 3.79) lifts the Z-score to **~2.62 — grey zone, bordering safe**.

The Piotroski F-Score of **8/9** is the more reliable read here, and it is emphatically strong. The only miss is F7 (no dilution) — share count rose from 364M to 443M, which is the May 2024 IPO, a one-time event, not ongoing dilution.

**Interpretation:** VIK is a high-beta, high-volatility discretionary travel name. A 1.56 beta means a 20% market drawdown plausibly produces a **~31% drawdown** in VIK. The stock has already demonstrated this: it fell 35% in two months during the spring 2025 tariff/consumer-scare episode, and again ~14% in the last two weeks (from $79.71 on Feb 26 to $68.91 on Mar 6) before recovering to $72.30.

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis

### Scenario A: Revenue –20% (moderate recession / demand shock)
Revenue $6,501M → **$5,201M**. Assuming ~40% of COGS variable and ~15% of SG&A variable:

| Line | Base | –20% Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $6,501M | $5,201M |
| Gross profit | $2,533M (39.0%) | ~$1,550M (29.8%) |
| SG&A | $1,031M | ~$876M |
| **Operating income** | $1,530M | **~$674M (–56%)** |
| EBITDA | $1,815M | **~$959M** |
| EBIT / interest | 4.22x | **1.86x** |
| EBITDA / interest | 5.01x | **2.64x** |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 1.07x | **2.02x** |
| Gross debt / EBITDA | 3.16x | **5.99x** |
| FCF (incl. WC reversal) | $1,533M | **~–$830M** |

**Assessment: survivable without external funding.** Net leverage at 2.0x remains inside the Ba2/BB band. Interest coverage of 1.86x is thin but positive. The FCF swing to roughly –$830M is driven by (a) committed newbuild capex of ~$1.0B and (b) reversal of the $697M working-capital benefit as deposits shrink. Against $3.8B of cash, that is **~4.6 years of coverage** — no liquidity event. Covenant risk is low: maintenance covenants on the revolver are typically set near 4.0–4.5x net leverage, and 2.0x is far inside.

### Scenario B: Revenue –35% (severe, 2020-style shock)
Revenue → $4,226M. Fixed cost base (~$3.26B) dominates:

- Gross profit ~$813M; SG&A ~$876M → **operating loss ~–$63M**
- EBITDA ~$222M; **interest coverage ≈ 0x**
- Gross debt / EBITDA ~26x; net leverage ~8.7x
- FCF deeply negative, likely **–$1.5B to –$2.0B**

**Assessment: this is the genuine tail risk, and it is a liquidity event, not a solvency event — provided management acts early.** Viking has lived through exactly this: in 2020–21 revenue went to near zero and the company survived via equity issuance, secured debt, and a $3.8B liquidity build. Today's starting point is far stronger (positive FCF, $3.8B cash, 1.07x net leverage vs. 2020's leveraged pre-COVID balance sheet). But note the structural vulnerability: **a cruise line cannot flex its cost base quickly** — ships, crew, and debt service are fixed. A 35% revenue decline would force a dividend/capex halt, a revolver draw, and likely an equity raise. Dilution risk to equity holders in this scenario is material.

### Scenario C: Rate/refinancing stress
$5.74B gross debt, $401M due within 12 months, covered **9.5x by cash**. The 2030 notes push the bulk of maturities out. A rates shock raises future refinancing cost but does not create a near-term wall. **Low risk.** (Detailed maturity ladder by year is a data gap — not disclosed in the toolset; the 20-F would have it.)

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating

# MODERATE

**Rationale:** The balance sheet has been transformed in 24 months. Net leverage of 1.07x, interest coverage of 4.2x, $3.8B cash, FCF of $1.5B and a Piotroski score of 8/9 describe a company in good — not fragile — financial health. The "Moderate" rather than "Low" rating reflects three things: (1) **high operating leverage** — a 20% revenue decline cuts EBIT by more than half; (2) **high market risk** — 40% vol and 1.56 beta mean equity holders bear amplified drawdowns; (3) **thin book equity (9% of assets)** leaves little cushion on a book basis, even though market-based leverage is trivial.

The Altman Z-score's "Distress" label is a false signal here and should be explicitly discounted in the investment memo.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **⚠️ Altman Z-Score of 0.41 reads "Distress" — but is an artifact.** Negative retained earnings (COVID deficit) and negative working capital (customer deposits as current liabilities) mechanically depress x1 and x2. Market-cap-adjusted Z is ~2.62. **Do not use the raw Z-score as a risk input for this name.**

2. **⚠️ Current ratio of 0.79 and negative working capital of –$1,214M.** Structurally normal for cruise (deposit-funded), but it means the company has **no traditional liquidity buffer** — it relies on continuous booking flow. If bookings stall, the deposit float unwinds and liquidity deteriorates faster than the current ratio implies.

3. **⚠️ High operating leverage.** Fixed cost base ~$3.3B against $6.5B revenue. A 20% revenue decline cuts operating income 56%. This is the core risk in any consumer recession.

4. **⚠️ Equity market risk is elevated.** 40.4% annualised vol, beta 1.56, and an observed –35.4% drawdown in under two months. Position sizing should reflect a name that can lose a third of its value on sentiment alone.

5. **⚠️ Valuation leaves no room for error.** FCF yield of 3.64% on a 42x P/E means the equity is priced for continued execution. A growth stall would compress the multiple *and* the earnings simultaneously.

6. **⚠️ Data quality issues in the historical series (flagged, not resolved):**
   - FY2023 EBITDA is reported as **–$1,111M** while operating income was **+$816M** and D&A $254M — internally inconsistent (should be ~+$1,070M).
   - FY2022 EBIT reported as **$860M** vs. operating income of **$62.7M** — inconsistent.
   - FY2023 net loss of –$1,851M vs. FY2022 net income of +$415M, despite improving operations — implies a large one-off charge (likely IPO-related preferred/warrant remeasurement or debt extinguishment) that is **not itemized in the available data**. This distorts all 2023-based trend metrics.
   - **Recommend verifying against the FY2025 20-F before publishing any 2022–2023 trend claim.**

7. **Data gaps:** No detailed debt maturity ladder by year; no CDS spread; no covenant terms disclosed; no 3-year price history (IPO May 2024) so full-cycle drawdown is unobservable; peer set returned by the sector tool (AMZN, TSLA, HD, MCD) is **not comparable** — no cruise-line peers (RCL, CCL, NCLH) were available, so relative credit benchmarking could not be performed.

---

**Bottom line for the memo:** VIK is a **fundamentally sound, high-beta discretionary name with a repaired balance sheet and a genuine but manageable cyclical risk profile.** The credit is fine; the equity is the volatile instrument. The key monitorable is **net bookings / deposit growth** — that is the leading indicator that would signal the operating-leverage risk in red flag #3 turning live.
