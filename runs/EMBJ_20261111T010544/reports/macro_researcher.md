### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **"Reflation with a fiscal fault line" (risk-on, but fragile)**

The global regime in late 2026 is **reflationary, not disinflationary**. US real GDP accelerated to **~3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026** (from 2.2% in Q2), unemployment is **4.1%** (Oct 2026), and CPI has *re-accelerated* to **3.82% y/y** (Oct 2026) from a 3.30% trough in July. With the effective fed funds rate at **3.88%**, the **real policy rate is ≈0%** — policy is no longer restrictive against a 3.8% inflation rate. The Fed's 2025 easing cycle (4.33% → 3.63% by Jan 2026) has stalled, and the effective rate has **backed up 25bp since August 2026**, consistent with a hawkish hold or an outright resumption of tightening.

The bond market has already voted: the 10Y traded up to **5.31% on 5 Oct 2026** before easing to **4.88% (9 Nov)**, with the 30Y at **5.47%** and a positively-sloped 2s10s at **+39bp**. This is a **term-premium / fiscal-risk-driven steepening**, not a growth signal — the most punishing environment for long-duration, capital-intensive manufacturers.

Layered on top is a **Brazil-specific shock**. President Lula's surprise re-election on **26 October 2026** triggered a violent repricing: the real fell **~11% intraday**, the Ibovespa hit a historic low with trading halted, and the central bank is now **seen freezing its rate-cut cycle** (SELIC/call money ~**13.94%**, Sep 2026) as inflation expectations deteriorate. USD/BRL has moved from **4.979 (9 Oct)** to **5.4895 (6 Nov)** — a **10.3% depreciation in under four weeks**.

**Regime classification: risk-on in US growth terms, risk-off in EM/rate-volatility terms.** For an EM-domiciled, USD-revenue, long-duration industrial, those two forces point in opposite directions.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Embraer's structure is the crux: **revenue and guidance are denominated in USD** (FY26 guidance $8.4–8.6bn), while a **large share of the cost base, engineering and manufacturing footprint is in BRL**. That makes EMBJ a *structural beneficiary of BRL weakness* and *structurally exposed to US long rates*.

| **US GDP +3.4%, unemployment 4.1%** | **POSITIVE** | Supports global flight hours → Services & Support ($638M, +30% y/y, 17% margin) and Executive Aviation demand. |

| **Fed tightening bias / real rate ≈0%** | **NEGATIVE** | Caps multiple expansion; EMBJ is a high-beta-duration equity despite a 0.72 market beta. |

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL** (with a negative skew on duration, positive on FX)

The company is enjoying the best operating environment in its history — record backlog, expanding margins, and a currency tailwind that flatters USD-reported profitability. Against that, the **cost of capital is the binding constraint**: US 30Y at 5.47% and a Brazilian risk premium re-widening post-election directly attack both the discount rate applied to a back-ended FCF stream and the financing capacity of the customers who must fund a $35.7bn backlog. The verdict is **Neutral, not Favourable**, precisely because the strongest positive (BRL weakness) is a symptom of the same fiscal distress that drives the negative (Brazil risk premium and frozen/possibly-reversing SELIC).

Note this is a company where **FX help and rate harm come from the same source** — a genuine hedge, but an unstable equilibrium.

---

### 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **Bear: a Brazilian fiscal accident**

**The non-consensus risk is not aerospace, it is Brasília.** The market is currently treating the post-election BRL selloff as a containable, 2018-style episode that will fade (as it partly did on 27 Oct, when the real recovered ~2%). The scenario that would break EMBJ's thesis:

1. Fiscal credibility continues to erode and **USD/BRL breaks 6.00**;
2. The BCB is forced to **reverse course and hike from ~14% back toward 16%+**, inverting the market's "easing cycle" assumption baked into Brazilian customer financing;
3. A **sovereign rating downgrade** follows, widening the spread on Embraer's own BRL and USD debt;
4. Embraer's **FX hedges flip from tailwind to a large mark-to-market loss** in the financial line — the exact line that already cut adjusted net income margin from 4.2% to 3.1% in Q3.

In that world, EUR/USD-style translation gains on the cost base are overwhelmed by **hedging losses, a higher discount rate, and deferred deliveries** as LatAm customers lose financing. A stock at ~$82.50 that rallied 13% in two sessions on results (10 Nov) is carrying a lot of optimism into that risk. Probability: **~20–25%**.

*Upside mirror case:* if US CPI rolls back toward 2.8% through H1 2027 and the Fed resumes cuts, the 10Y returns to ~4.4% while a Brazilian fiscal truce restores the real to ~5.10 — a **double multiple-expansion** scenario (lower discount rate + sustained FX margin benefit) worth materially more than the bull case currently discounted.
