# ESG Analysis — Embraer S.A. (EMBJ / B3: EMBJ3)

**Sector:** Industrials — Aerospace & Defense | **HQ:** São José dos Campos, Brazil | **Market cap:** ~US$15.1bn | **Employees:** 21,122 | **FY2025 revenue:** US$7.6bn, record backlog US$31.6bn

**Framing note:** For an airframer, the materiality centre of gravity is *not* factory emissions — it is (a) product-in-use carbon (Scope 3 cat. 11) and the regulatory trajectory hitting the airline customer, (b) certification/safety events, (c) labour and supply-chain execution in a Brazilian industrial footprint, and (d) a governance structure in which the Brazilian state holds a veto share. I weight the analysis accordingly.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Own operations (low):** Embraer reported **direct (Scope 1) emissions up ~4% in 2025** versus 2024, attributed to production volume growth and natural-gas use in manufacturing; natural-gas efficiency became a formal environmental-management-system target for 2025. Absolute Scope 1+2 is small (manufacturing/assembly footprint, 21k employees) — immaterial versus a US$7.6bn revenue base. Targets: **net zero by 2050**, signatory to UN Global Compact since 2008, reports to GRI + SASB + TCFD.

**Product-in-use (high, structural):** The economically relevant emissions are the ~20–25 year in-service CO2 of a delivered aircraft. This is a *demand tailwind* for Embraer: the E195-E2 is marketed at ~25% lower fuel burn/CO2 versus the prior generation, and the E2 family is the primary compliance lever for airlines facing ReFuelEU, EU ETS phase-out of free allowances, and CORSIA mandatory participation from 2027. Embraer also has SAF ecosystem activity (2026 LOI with Razen) and a green-concept aircraft family, plus the Eve Air Mobility eVTOL subsidiary.

**Disclosure gap (negative):** Embraer is **not committed to SBTi**, does **not disclose to CDP Climate**, and is **not covered by MSCI ESG Ratings** (covered only by Sustainalytics and LSEG). For a US$15bn industrial with CSRD-adjacent EU operations and Brazilian ISSB-based reporting coming, this is a lagging disclosure posture.

**Verdict:** Medium — no stranded-asset or carbon-tax exposure of consequence to Embraer itself; the E-pillar risk is transmitted through customer regulation, where Embraer is a relative beneficiary.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Safety (the single largest S risk):** Escalating airworthiness activity.
- **April 2026:** FAA and ANAC issued a joint **Emergency Airworthiness Directive** on Legacy 450/500 and Praetor 500/600 business jets over the **pitch trim actuator (PTA)** — potential uncommanded pitch movement; immediate inspection and, in cases, replacement before further flight; several hundred aircraft affected. No confirmed accident, but emergency ADs are the highest-severity regulatory instrument and directly touch the Executive Jets revenue line.
- **2026:** Further FAA ADs adopted for **ERJ-190-300/-400 (E2)** and **EMB-505 (Phenom 300)**. Elevated AD cadence at a manufacturer scaling a new-generation fleet is a monitoring item, not yet a thesis-breaking one.

**Labour:** September 2025 — **indefinite strike by ~10,000 metalworkers at São José dos Campos** during the wage campaign; initial 5.05% offer rejected against an 11% demand; strike subsequently suspended after a **4.06% increase**. The union has escalated beyond the plant, issuing a release to **Embraer's airline customers** alleging labour-rights rollbacks — a reputational/customer-relationship risk channel. Management concurrently flags **labour shortages** as a delivery constraint. Unionised, high-skill, concentrated workforce = recurring annual wage-campaign margin and delivery risk.

**Supply chain:** Management has repeatedly identified **supply-chain constraints** as the binding factor on ramp-up. Social-labour standards in the tier-2/3 Brazilian and global supply base are the latent risk (no specific incident identified in this review).

**Defence/end-use:** Embraer Defense & Security (KC-390, A-29 Super Tucano) sells to governments with mixed human-rights records; A-29 airframes have been used in active counter-insurgency operations. This is the dominant *exclusion-screen* issue for ESG-constrained investors and creates headline/reputational optionality.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.5 / 10**

**Positives:**
- Board of **9–11 members, 8 independent** — strong independence ratio by Brazilian standards; board meets ~8x/year.
- Listed on **B3 Novo Mercado**, the highest-governance Brazilian segment (single-class ordinary-share structure, 100% tag-along in principle).
- **2026 proposed long-term incentive plan caps share-settled awards at 5% of fully diluted capital**, grants within 10 years — disciplined dilution control and a genuine alignment mechanism with long-horizon shareholders.
- Active IR disclosure cadence (quarterly earnings, governance report, detailed sustainability indicators annex).
- CEO (Francisco Gomes Neto) and board leadership are separate roles; no CEO/chairman duality identified.

**Negatives:**
- **Golden Share** held by the Federative Republic of Brazil confers **veto rights** over defined matters (notably military programmes and certain structural/governance decisions) and entitles the government to **elect one sitting board member** (a golden-share board representative changed effective 1 September 2026). This is a structural minority-shareholder-rights impairment: it forecloses most change-of-control outcomes and embeds a political principal inside the boardroom. In practice it caps any takeover premium and is a permanent, un-diversifiable governance discount.
- **Legacy FCPA record:** 2016 global settlement — **US$205m total** (US$107m to DOJ, US$98m SEC disgorgement/interest/prejudgment, ~US$20m to Brazilian authorities) over bribery in the Dominican Republic, Saudi Arabia and Mozambique and books-and-records failures. Remediation and a compliance monitorship followed; no new anti-corruption action identified in this review, but the offset/defence-sales model that produced it remains structurally intact.
- **Defence-segment opacity:** order intake, offset obligations and end-user vetting in Defence & Security are less transparent than commercial programmes.
- Governance disclosure on executive pay quantum and ESG-linked comp linkage is thin relative to US/EU peers.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **Brazil — CVM Resolution 193 (ISSB/IFRS S1–S2 basis):** mandatory sustainability reporting phased from **fiscal 2026**. Embraer is on GRI/SASB/TCFD today; migration to ISSB is required and SBTi/CDP gaps will become visible.
- **Brazil — SBCE regulated carbon market:** large-emitter obligations phasing in 2026–2030; Embraer's ~4% Scope 1 growth puts it closer to thresholds, though absolute volumes likely below the >10,000 tCO2e/yr regulated tier.
- **EU CSRD — non-EU parent wave (FY2028, reported 2029):** applies to non-EU groups with EU turnover above the threshold. Embraer's **Portugal (Évora) and EU commercial subsidiaries** create realistic scope. This is the biggest *new* compliance cost and will force Scope 3 cat. 11 (use-phase) quantification — the number that matters.
- **EU ETS (aviation) / ReFuelEU Aviation / CORSIA:** do **not** bind Embraer directly; they bind its customers. Full auctioning of EU aviation allowances from 2026, SAF blending mandates (post-2025, ~90% supplier compliance per EASA), and mandatory CORSIA from 2027 raise airline operating cost — which **accelerates replacement of older regional jets**, a net positive for E2 order intake.
- **CBAM:** indirect, minor — aluminium/steel/composite inputs into EU-based metal-structure production.
- **No ESG-linked debt covenants identified.**

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE (leaning Laggard on disclosure, Leader on product decarbonisation)**

Embraer is a **two-speed ESG profile**. On the dimension the market increasingly prices — *product* decarbonisation — it is genuinely well positioned: an efficient new-generation narrowbody/regional family, SAF and eVTOL optionality, and net-zero-2050 alignment with IATA. On *governance architecture and disclosure discipline* it is a laggard: a state golden share that permanently subordinates minority rights, a 2016 FCPA settlement of scale, no SBTi target, no CDP response, and no MSCI coverage.

Third-party scores: **no MSCI rating**; covered by **Sustainalytics** and **LSEG** (values behind paywalls — not obtained). Absence of MSCI coverage is itself a flow-of-passive-ESG-capital negative, and the absence of a CDP score is a red flag for European institutional mandates.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL to modestly NEGATIVE** (from an ESG-mandate lens) — **POSITIVE on climate-transition fundamentals**

**Three points for the thesis:**

1. **The E pillar is a tailwind, not a cost.** Embraer's decarbonisation exposure runs through customers, and that exposure *increases demand for its product*. Do not let an ESG screen treat this as a carbon-risk name; the correct classification is **climate-transition enabler**. This argues for a small *positive* adjustment to terminal growth assumptions for Commercial Aviation, not a discount.

2. **The real ESG discount is governance, and it is permanent.** The Golden Share establishes a hard ceiling on any strategic/control outcome and places a political appointee in the boardroom. This should be modelled as an evergreen **cost-of-equity add-on** (I would apply roughly 50–100bp) rather than a discrete event risk. It is the single reason Embraer should trade at a discount to a comparable US/EU airframer on identical cash flows. Note also that a September 2026 golden-share board-seat change is a reminder that this channel is live, not vestigial.

3. **Safety and labour are the two watch-items that can convert to near-term cash-flow events.** The April 2026 emergency AD (PTA) on the Praetor/Legacy mid-size family and the elevated AD cadence on the E2 arrive exactly as management raises guidance on record backlog and Executive Jets mix. A second emergency AD, or a repeat of the September 2025 strike in the next wage campaign, would hit the delivery schedule that the entire bull case now rests on. Neither is currently in consensus.

**Recommendation to the investment committee:** Do not exclude on ESG grounds — the climate-transition positioning is a competitive advantage. But flag EMBJ as **non-compliant for Article 8/9 funds with defence exclusions or strict governance screens**, and treat the Golden Share plus the disclosure deficit (no SBTi, no CDP, no MSCI) as the source of an ESG-driven valuation discount rather than an operational risk.
