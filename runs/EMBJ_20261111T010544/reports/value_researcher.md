## Embraer S.A. (EMBJ / NYSE ADR) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price $82.50 (Nov 10, 2026) | Market cap $15.1B | USD/BRL 5.60 | 183.3M ADRs (1 ADR = 4 shares)**

> **Critical data note:** the standard screener outputs (P/E 7.84, P/B 0.72, EV/EBITDA 3.93) are currency-mangled — they divide a **USD** price by **BRL** earnings and book value. Rebased at spot FX, the true multiples are ~6x higher. All figures below are currency-consistent.

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW** (widening modestly)

| Segment | Position | Moat source |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial (E-Jets) | Effective duopoly with Airbus A220 in 70–150 seat jets; dominant in US regional (scope-clause constrained) | Certification barriers, 20-yr product cycles, pilot/spares switching costs |
| Executive (Phenom/Praetor) | #3 in business jets; record $8.2B backlog, 16% EBIT margin | Brand + fractional/commonality, but 5 credible rivals |
| Defense (C-390) | Genuine competitive win vs C-130 (Portugal, Hungary, Netherlands, Austria, Czech, Sweden, Lithuania) | Sole non-US medium airlifter; political/lumpy |
| Services & Support | ~Annuity on installed base — highest-margin, recurring | **Best asset in the company**; grows with fleet, not cycle |

- **Backlog $35.7B, +14% y/y, 8th consecutive record** = ~4.2x revenue. This is real, verifiable visibility and the single strongest argument in the bull case.
- **But the moat does not convert into high returns.** Gross margin 17.5%, net margin 4.6%, **ROIC 9.3% (2025), 11.2% (2024), 5.2% (2023), −1.8% (2022)**. ROIC has straddled the cost of capital (I estimate 10.5–11% USD, higher in BRL) for the entire period. A narrow moat, not a fortress.
- The moat *has* widened since 2021 (services mix, C-390 traction, Boeing's self-immolation ceding the regional space), but the economics are still those of a capital-intensive assembler, not a pricing-power franchise.

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Earned marks:** record backlog → revenue predictability better than typical cyclical. Deleveraged hard: net debt R$12.7B → R$4.2B ($745M), ~0.6x adj. EBITDA. Cumulative 5-yr FCF R$7.7B vs cumulative net income R$4.6B (>100% conversion over the cycle).

**Deductions:** FCF is **wildly** volatile (R$0.19B in 2023, R$2.65B in 2022) because working capital — inventory R$17.4B, 42% of revenue — swings violently; owner earnings (NI + D&A − capex) were only **R$0.70B in 2025 and R$0.80B in 2024** because capex (R$2.7B, 6.4% of sales) runs nearly 2x D&A. Goodwill + intangibles R$14.9B = **71% of equity**, so the "cheap" book value is largely capitalized development cost. Capex intensity plus thin margins means this business consumes most of what it earns.

**DCF (FCF $700M base per 2026 guidance, WACC 11%, terminal 3.0%):** FCF growing 12%/yr for 6 years → EV $12.4B − net debt $0.75B = **~$63/ADR**.
**Bull case** (15% FCF CAGR, 10% WACC, 3.5% terminal): **~$88/ADR**.
**Base-blended intrinsic value: ~$68–72.** Current $82.50 = **15–20% premium to fair value**, i.e. a *negative* margin of safety. Even the bull case offers only ~7% upside.

Corroboration: the stock is now **above every published analyst target** (Scotiabank $81, UBS $65, Morgan Stanley $67). The 20–30% discount I require implies a buy price of **$48–55**, roughly where the shares traded in May 2026.

*(Peer context: aerospace/defense median P/E 37x — EMBJ at 35x TTM is in line with a re-rated sector, not discounted to it. That median is flattered by loss-making Boeing and defense re-rating.)*

### 4. Management Quality — **Above average**

CEO Francisco Gomes Neto (since 2019) has delivered a genuine turnaround: R$992M loss (2022) → R$1.93B profit (2025), net debt cut R$8.5B, record backlog, margin expansion, and the C-390 export wins. Capital allocation has been disciplined: R$1.02B buyback in 2025 with share count down ~3% (183.7M → 178.0M ADRs), plus R$354M declared remuneration across 2Q/3Q26. No empire-building M&A.

Two reservations: (a) the **Brazilian government holds a golden share** with veto rights over strategic decisions — a structural governance discount and a constraint on any value-maximizing transaction; (b) Eve (eVTOL) remains a consolidated cash drag that flatters "adjusted" figures by exclusion.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (trim / avoid)

Embraer is a **better business than it was in 2022** — better managed, better backlogged, better balanced sheet. It is also, at $82.50, a **materially worse investment**. After a ~30% move since June and an 11% post-Q3 pop, the shares discount a flawless multi-year ramp (revenue $7.4B → ~$11B, margins to 12%+) while the company has never sustainably earned returns above its cost of capital. ROIC ~9–11% against an ~11% WACC is not a moat worth paying 35x earnings and 4x tangible-light book for.

Existing holders who bought the turnaround at $30–55 should ring the register into strength; the thesis is now consensus and fully priced. New money: pass. Revisit at **$55–62** (a 20–25% discount to base intrinsic value), or if FCF guidance converts into a durable >$1B run-rate with ROIC sustainably above 13%.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (i.e. what would prove me wrong)

1. **Backlog conversion & margin surprise** — if 10.6% adj. EBIT margin plus services mix pushes FCF to $1.0–1.2B by 2028, the bull case ($88+) becomes the base case and I'm too conservative.
2. **US tariffs on Brazilian-built aircraft** — the explicit reason guidance is quoted "ex-tariffs." A punitive outcome hits the largest end market; a benign one is upside.
3. **Cyclicality of executive aviation** — 160–170 deliveries guided; business jet demand is the most rate- and sentiment-sensitive part of the mix.
4. **Supply chain / engine availability** — GTF and narrowbody engine constraints are the industry's binding constraint on delivery ramps.
5. **FX** — BRL at 5.60 (near 52-week weak extreme) flatters translated results; a stronger real compresses BRL earnings and ADR EPS.
6. **Working capital reversal** — R$17.4B inventory and customer advances are a large, undated liability; a delivery slowdown could flip FCF negative, as in 2023.
7. **Eve / program execution** — E2 and eVTOL capital calls would extend the capex-over-D&A problem.
8. **Governance** — golden-share intervention or a dilutive state-prioritized decision.
