# EMBJ (Embraer S.A.) — Forensic Short-Seller Review
**Price:** ~$82.50 (post-3Q26 pop, Nov 2026) | **Mkt cap:** $15.14bn | **ADRs:** ~183M | **Reporting currency:** BRL | **Latest reported period:** 3Q26 (Nov 10, 2026)

---

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment — **MEDIUM** (not fraudulent; but flattered by non-operating and customer-cash items)

The headline forensics come out **clean**. This is an operations/valuation story, not an accounting fraud. But three specific mechanisms make reported profit and cash less durable than the multi-year highs suggest:

| Test | Result | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Beneish M-Score | **−2.888** (low risk; thresholds −1.78 / −2.22) | No manipulation signal |
| Accruals ratio (NI−OCF)/assets | **−4.01%** FY25 (−4.39% FY24) | Negative accruals = cash-backed earnings |
| DSRI (days sales in receivables) | **0.676** | Receivables *falling* vs sales — anti-channel-stuffing |
| Net receivables | R$6.79bn → R$4.81bn (−29%) on +5% revenue | Cash collections ahead of revenue |
| Inventory days | 230.9 → 218.6 → 201.7 → **186.0** | Improving, not deteriorating |
| Cash conversion (OCF/NI) | **2.47x** LTM | Superficially excellent — see caveat |
| Piotroski F-Score | **7 / 9** | F3 (ROA improvement) and F8 (gross margin) are the misses |
| Altman Z | **1.18** ("distress") | Distorted (x2=0 from a data artefact, book-equity x4); indicative only, but leverage/working-capital tightness is real |
| Composite quality score | **59/100** | Middling |

**The caveat that matters:** the 2.47x cash conversion is *not* operational. Q4 2025 operating cash flow was **R$4.69bn — 99% of the entire FY2025 OCF of R$4.76bn**, of which **R$3.98bn was working-capital release**. Q1 2026 OCF was **−R$2.03bn** (WC −R$2.17bn). Q3 2026 *adjusted* FCF of **$627.7m** was explicitly "helped by pre-delivery payment inflows" — against full-year FCF guidance of only "$700m+". One quarter ≈ 90% of the year's cash. That is customer deposit timing, not earning power.

**Rating: Medium.** Clean books, fragile cash rhythm.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

1. **Cash guidance raised 75% with zero change to profit or delivery guidance.** On Nov 10, 2026 Embraer lifted 2026 adjusted FCF to "$700m+" from "$400m+" while **leaving adj. EBIT margin guidance unchanged at 10.0–10.6%** and delivery guidance unchanged. The increment is balance-sheet (pre-delivery payments = a liability), not earnings. A short seller should never pay a multiple for cash raised by collecting customers' deposits early.
2. **Adjusted net income collapsed sequentially.** 3Q26 adj. net income **$73.6m vs $218.6m in 2Q26** (−66%) on *higher* revenue ($2,347m vs $2,235m) and a healthy 10.9% adj. EBIT margin. The entire swing is below the EBIT line — FX on USD debt / interest / tax. For a company with ~85% USD-linked revenue and a large BRL cost base, **reported EPS is largely an FX derivative**, and the stock still rose 7% on the print.
3. **An unexplained ~R$1.5bn Q4 2025 below-the-line charge.** Q4 2025: operating income **+R$1.163bn** but reported EBIT **−R$328m** and EBITDA **R$98m**; FY2025 EBIT (R$2.54bn) sits R$0.79bn below operating income (R$3.33bn). Net income was still positive (+R$456m) courtesy of below-the-line gains. Translation: Q4 is the quarter that makes or breaks the year, and it is where the one-offs live.
4. **Gross margin has never broken out: 17.3% → 18.0% → 17.5% (FY23–25).** Q4 2025 GM compressed to 16.7% from 18.6%. Q2 2026 rebounded to 19.9% — good, but this is a structurally low-margin assembler (vs Airbus ~15% but 4x the scale, and a services/aftermarket mix Embraer has under-monetised).
5. **FY2025 headline numbers were poor while the stock doubled.** FY2025: revenue +4.95% (BRL), **EBIT −46%** (R$4.76bn → R$2.54bn), **net income −11%**, **EPS R$10.52 vs R$11.88 (−11%)**. Yet the shares went from ~$36 to ~$64 during 2025 (+78%). The 2025 move was **pure re-rating**, not earnings.
6. **Balance sheet is thin beneath the surface.** Goodwill + intangibles **R$14.56bn = 74% of equity** and 21% of assets. Tangible book ≈ **R$5.2bn (~$1bn) against a $15.1bn market cap → ~15x tangible book.** Net debt has risen from R$4.17bn (Dec-25) to R$6.17bn (Jun-26); current ratio 1.44; interest expense R$1.08bn vs reported EBIT R$2.54bn in FY25 = **~2.4x cover**.
7. **Inventory is 28% of total assets and re-inflating in 2026.** R$17.44bn → R$18.90bn → **R$19.14bn** (Dec-25 → Mar-26 → Jun-26), ~188 days of COGS, ~46% of LTM revenue. Undelivered E2s and supply-chain buffers. Every delivery slip converts this into an impairment or a discount-financing event.
8. **Buybacks executed at peak prices, then stopped.** Share repurchases of **R$1.02bn in FY2025** and **R$940m in Q1 2026** (price range roughly $55–79) while net debt rose and inventory built; Q2 2026 repurchases fell to R$26m. Buying back stock at 30–40x earnings with a levered, working-capital-hungry balance sheet is a capital-allocation flag, not a signal of confidence.
9. **"Adjusted free cash flow *excluding Eve*."** The cash-burning eVTOL subsidiary (Eve) is stripped out of the FCF metric management now guides on. Classic perimeter engineering.
10. **ADS ratio change (Oct 30, 2026; stock −3.3% next session)** — worth monitoring as an optics/liquidity action rather than a fundamental one.
11. **Insider disclosure is thin.** Only six Form 4s on file; a cluster of three on **2026-04-27** plus one 2025-10-01, with no purchased shares visible. As a foreign private issuer, disclosure is sparse — treat as an *information gap*, not proof of selling.
12. **Revenue deceleration confirmed by the model:** YoY +47.2% (4Q24) → +42.1% → +19.4% → +17.5% → **+1.6% (4Q25)** → +15.5% → +16.3% (2Q26) → +17% (3Q26). Growth is lumpy and delivery-schedule dependent; the 4Q25 stall is the template for what a bad quarter looks like.

**Data caveats (for transparency):** FY2025 capex (R$2.65bn) does not reconcile to the sum of quarterly capex (R$1.27bn); retained earnings show as zero in some FY rows; Q4 2025 EBITDA/EBIT prints are internally inconsistent. I have not relied on the consensus estimate file — its revenue (USD) and EBITDA (apparently BRL) fields are mixed-currency and unusable.

## 3. Beneish & Accruals Detail

- **M-Score −2.888** — comfortably inside "low risk." The only index above 1.0 is **GMI 1.028** (gross margin deterioration), and **TATA −0.0401** (income-decreasing accruals). **There is no earnings-manipulation case here.**
- **Accruals: −4.01% of assets** (FY25), −4.39% (FY24), −9.24% (FY22). Consistently negative across the cycle — a genuinely cash-generative working-capital model.
- **Receivables vs revenue: no red flag.** AR −29.1% vs revenue +4.95% in FY25, −0.31% vs +6.61% in FY23. This is the *opposite* of channel stuffing. (Caveat: Embraer sells/-securitises receivables in the normal course; a decline of this size could partly be factoring rather than collection. Not evidenced either way in the data available.)
- **Cash conversion 2.47x, FCF conversion 1.10x** — but as shown above, the multiple is Q4/PDP timing, and FY2025 FCF fell to R$2.11bn from R$2.50bn even as revenue grew.

**Conclusion: the accounting is clean. Anyone shorting EMBJ on "fraud" or "fake earnings" is wrong.**

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment — **Moderate-to-High (structural, slow-burn)**

**Genuine moats (state them fairly):**
- **E175 is the only in-production regional jet compliant with US scope clauses** — a regulated near-monopoly in the 70–76 seat US feeder market. This is the crown jewel and the highest-margin product line.
- **E2 family passed 500 firm orders**; SAS (45) and Avelo (50 firm + 50 options) broke the US E2 deadlock. The E195-E2 is ~30% cheaper than the A220 per Simple Flying's reporting, with comparable per-seat economics.
- **C-390 Millennium** — 13 operator nations, UAE order for up to 20, Defense backlog +42%, "the West's most successful new military airlifter." This is real, and it's the fastest-growing segment.
- Backlog **$34.5bn (7th consecutive record)** ≈ 4x annual revenue. That is the single strongest argument against a short.

**Erosion vectors:**
- **Airbus A220: ~1,119 firm orders vs ~540 for the E190/195-E2.** Airbus can price, finance and slot-manage Embraer out of any contested campaign; Embraer cannot match Airbus's export-credit and leasing-company support.
- **The scope clause is a regulatory moat, and regulatory moats can be legislated away.** If US pilot contracts (ALPA's next cycle) relax the 76-seat/weight limits, two things happen at once: E175 demand peaks and the A220-100 becomes legal in the same segment. Embraer's single best franchise has a policy expiry date it does not control.
- **Regional aviation demand is structurally capped** by pilot shortages in the US and by mainline up-gauging.
- **Business jets** (45 deliveries in 3Q26) face post-2023 normalisation and heavy competition (Gulfstream G400, Cessna, Bombardier's clean balance sheet).
- **No new narrowbody decision** ("deliberately unanswered" per AviationOutlook). Either Embraer commits (capex ramp, FCF destruction 2028+, financing risk) or it doesn't (share loss to A220/COMAC after 2030). Both branches are bad for the equity at 25x+ earnings.
- **GTF engine availability** (Pratt & Whitney) remains the binding constraint on E2 ramp — an issue entirely outside management's control.

## 5. Management & Governance Red Flags

- **Promotional framing vs delivery:** FY2025 EBIT −46%, EPS −11% — yet the narrative was uniformly "record." Adjusted EBIT, adjusted FCF, and "excluding Eve" perimeters now do the heavy lifting. 3Q26 adj. net income fell 66% and the stock rose 7%: management has trained the market to look at revenue, backlog and cash.
- **One-off charges inside the most important quarter** (Q4 2025: ~R$1.5bn between operating income and EBIT). No visible auditor changes or related-party issues in the data.
- **Capital allocation:** ~R$1.96bn of buybacks in FY25–1H26 at cycle-high prices, while net debt rose R$2bn and inventory built. That is the wrong use of cash at this point in an aerospace cycle.
- **Disclosure quality is acceptable but thin** (foreign private issuer, 6-K/20-F regime); insider signalling is effectively unobservable. I could not retrieve the 20-F for risk-factor detail (no 20-F returned), and Form 4 contents were not retrievable — a genuine gap, not a negative finding.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, but it is a valuation/cyclicality short, not a fraud short.** The mechanism:

> Embraer is being priced as a structural grower (record backlog, defense optionality, E2 momentum) at **~18–19x EV/adj. EBIT, ~23x EV/FCF, ~26–30x forward earnings and ~15x tangible book**, for a business with **17–18% gross margins, 10–11% EBIT margins, 28% of assets in inventory, 74% of equity in goodwill, and 100% of annual free cash flow generated in a single quarter.** Any of four ordinary events — Q4 delivery slippage, a GTF/Pratt supply interruption, a Brazilian-tariff or BRL shock (BRL-reported revenue grew 4.95% in FY25 vs +18% in USD, i.e. FX is doing a lot of work), or a below-the-line FX/interest swing — takes 25–35% out of the equity with no change to the franchise. Q4 2025 was a live rehearsal: revenue +1.6%, GM −190bp, EBIT −46%.
>
> **Downside case: $55–60 (−25% to −33%)** on a de-rating to ~15x EV/EBIT plus a delivery/working-capital miss. Not a zero. Not a bankruptcy. And no dated catalyst until the 4Q26 print.

**The other side is stronger than the bear case right now, and I'll say so plainly:** guidance raised three times in 2026, backlog at seven consecutive records ($34.5bn), 3Q26 revenue +17% with adj. EBIT margin expanding to 10.9%, adj. FCF guidance up 75%, defense backlog +42% with C-390 winning NATO-adjacent campaigns, E2 finally cracking the US with SAS and Avelo, ~$1.0bn of net debt on $7.6bn+ revenue (≈1.6x EBITDA), and a forensic audit that finds **nothing** — negative accruals, DSRI 0.68, inventory days improving four years running, Piotroski 7. Fighting that tape on a valuation argument, in a sector where Airbus trades at similar levels and Boeing's distress has re-rated the entire duopoly-adjacent complex, is a poor risk/reward with expensive borrow and squeeze risk on every order announcement.

**Triggers that would convert this into a Short (watch list):**
1. Any 4Q26 delivery shortfall vs the unchanged commercial/executive guidance.
2. FY2027 FCF guidance that again requires "excluding Eve" and relies on PDPs.
3. A formal new narrowbody launch (capex/capex-partner risk) — or, conversely, a scope-clause relaxation in the 2027 US pilot contract cycle.
4. A tangible-book/goodwill impairment (Eve, defense programmes) or another unexplained Q4 below-the-line charge.
5. Net debt/EBITDA through 2.5x with inventory still building.

**Bottom line:** EMBJ is an expensive, high-quality-*enough* cyclical with a clean set of accounts and genuinely improving commercial momentum. The bear case is real but unprovable on today's numbers; the honest answer is **no edge at this price**. I would not put this in the book as a short. I would hold it as a **watch-list name with a written trigger sheet** (Q4-26 deliveries, FY27 FCF bridge, scope-clause headlines, BRL) — and I would size any short only after the first evidence that the Q4 cash/delivery machine has stuttered, because that is when a 25–33% de-rating becomes a probability rather than a hope.
