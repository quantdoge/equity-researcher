## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1) US–Brazil tariff/political cycle (Probability: High of recurrence; Impact: Medium-High)**
The 2025 US 50% IEEPA tariff on Brazil — explicitly linked to the Bolsonaro trial — hit Embraer before aircraft were carved out. Civil aircraft are now exempt: Reuters (24-Feb-2026) "Brazil hails zero US tariff on aircraft exports" and Aerotime ("Embraer ducks Trump's 50% US tariff hike, aircraft exempt"); the July-2026 Section 301 action (25% on Brazil, effective 22-Jul-2026, Federal Register 2026-14542) also exempts civil aircraft (Airlift tariff tracker). But this is an executive-branch discretionary carve-out, not a treaty right: Embraer previously quantified a 10% tariff at US$70–80m cost per year (WSJ) and ~US$9m per aircraft in worst case, and sell-side work suggested a full 50% rate could cut 2026 earnings 55–60% (Invezz/TradingView). With Lula winning a fourth term in the Nov-2026 runoff (Reuters/The Hill), the political dispute — including US sanctions on Brazilian Supreme Court justices (CRS R46236) and Brazil's two WTO complaints — persists. Watch for: revocation of the aircraft exemption, retaliatory Brazilian measures, or OFAC action creep (Steptoe notes "risk of increased US sanctions on Brazil is high").
*Revenue at risk:* ~59% of sales touch the US market indirectly through tariffs, scope clause, and the FAA.

**2) US export-control / ITAR leverage over the defense ramp (Probability: Medium; Impact: High)**
Defense is the fastest-growing leg (Q3-26 revenue US$341m, +22%; backlog +56%) and is being exported into NATO and Middle East markets where US approval of US-origin content (engines, avionics, systems) is required. Embraer's own FY2025 20-F flags: *"Developments related to geopolitical conflicts, sanctions, export controls and instability in certain jurisdictions may adversely affect us"* (SEC 20-F FY2025). This gives Washington a second lever beyond tariffs — e.g., if Brazil's BRICS/China alignment or Middle East customers become politically contentious, ITAR/end-use scrutiny can delay or block C-390 campaigns (UAE 10 aircraft, Sweden/Netherlands/Austria joint programme, India/Mahindra Nagpur line — the latter explicitly conditional on FID, per FlightGlobal, Oct-2026). Any US restriction here would hit exactly the segment the equity story is now pricing.

**3) Brazil sovereign/political & FX/currency-control backdrop (Probability: Medium; Impact: Medium)**
Revenue is largely USD-denominated while the cost base is BRL, so BRL swings are a direct margin variable; Brazilian tax and labour contingencies are a recurring drag. Embraer also depends on state development bank BNDES for customer export credit (e.g., R$4.5bn for E175s to Canada, US$312m for 13 aircraft to SkyWest) — i.e., public financing is a competitive input that can be politicised, and Brazil's fiscal path/FX controls are a structural (if low-probability) tail risk. Nationalisation/expropriation risk is negligible (Embraer was privatised in 1994), but golden-share/strategic-asset considerations have surfaced historically.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Legacy FCPA/anti-corruption (resolved, but the reputational template):** In 2016 Embraer paid >US$205m globally — US$107m DOJ DPA (admitted conspiracy to violate FCPA anti-bribery/books-and-records) plus SEC disgorgement of US$83.8m + US$14.4m interest (SEC Press Release 2016-224; DOJ OPA; Harvard Forum on Corp Governance). The conduct involved third-party agents and a US-based subsidiary — precisely the channel now being re-used for defense sales into new jurisdictions (UAE, India, Middle East).
- **Current investigations:** No new DOJ/SEC/CVM enforcement action against Embraer surfaced in current searches. **Gap flagged:** the 20-F "Legal Proceedings" section could not be retrieved directly (the filing tool returned no 20-F; SEC full-text extract was not accessible within budget), so I cannot rule out disclosed-but-immaterial matters. The 20-F does carry recurring provisions for tax, civil and labour claims (Note 19, "Tax, Civil and Labor Claims and Contingent Liabilities"), which are a normal, non-trivial cost of doing business in Brazil.
- **Boeing JV termination arbitration (legacy):** the 2020 collapse of the Boeing commercial JV and C-390/defense carve-out partnership generated arbitration; it is now a historical item, not a live contingency, but it demonstrated the strategic fragility of Embraer's largest bilateral relationship.
- **Certification/regulatory gating:** E175-E2 programme paused a second time for four years because US regional **scope clauses** (seat/MTOW caps) remain unresolved (Aerospace Global News). This is the single most important *regulatory* constraint on the commercial franchise: the US regional market is Embraer's core, and its next-generation narrowbody is effectively blocked by airline-union contracts rather than by regulators. FAA rulemaking and airworthiness directives continue (FAA DRS-2026-03-05 on EMB-505; FAA-2026-10627 special conditions for ERJ 190-400). EASA certification obtained for ROAAS on E2 (2026) — positive for European ops.
- **Securities/market plumbing:** ADS ratio change from 1 ADS : 4 shares to 1:1 via forward split (PRNewswire, 30-Oct-2026) — no legal issue, but a governance/liquidity item to note for holders.
- **Related-party/listed affiliate:** Eve Holding (eVTOL) is separately NYSE-listed; Embraer retains an interest and funding exposure — a discrete disclosure and capital-allocation risk rather than a legal one.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Embraer's geographic mix is genuinely diversified across US/Europe/Brazil, its largest historical legal overhang (FCPA) is resolved, and the aircraft tariff carve-out plus the EU/NATO defense ramp materially improved the political backdrop in 2026 (record US$35.7bn backlog supports this). What keeps the rating at Medium rather than Low is (i) the **~59% US revenue concentration sitting on discretionary executive-branch tariff exemptions and US scope-clause/union politics**, (ii) **ITAR/export-control leverage over the fastest-growing defense segment**, and (iii) a **Brazilian political relationship with Washington that has already been weaponised once (50% tariffs, sanctions on judges)** and remains unresolved post-election. No Very-High-risk jurisdiction concentration (no Russia, no Iran, de minimis China) and no live material litigation were identified.

**Key monitors for the next 12 months:** (1) any revision to the US aircraft exemption under Section 301/232 or IEEPA successor actions; (2) ITAR approvals for UAE/India C-390 contracts; (3) FAA/EASA certification milestones and any movement on US scope clause relief; (4) new FCPA/DOJ or CVM disclosure in the FY2026 20-F; (5) Brazilian FX/fiscal developments affecting BNDES export-credit availability.
