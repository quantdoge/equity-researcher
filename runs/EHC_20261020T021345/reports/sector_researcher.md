## SECTOR BRIEF — ENCOMPASS HEALTH (EHC)
**Sector:** Healthcare — Medical Care Facilities (Post-Acute / Inpatient Rehabilitation)
**Note on timing:** latest audited annuals are FY2025 (filed Feb-2026); the company has since reported Q1–Q2 2026 and raised FY2026 guidance.

---

### 1. Sector Overview
EHC competes in U.S. **post-acute care**, specifically freestanding **inpatient rehabilitation facilities (IRFs)** — the highest-acuity, highest-margin PAC setting, reimbursed by Medicare under the IRF PPS (~$9–10bn of Medicare fee-for-service spend, plus Medicare Advantage and commercial). Demand is structurally driven by an ageing population, rising stroke/neuro/ortho-acuity case mix, and hospital discharge pressure to move patients downstream quickly. Supply is constrained by certificate-of-need laws in roughly half the states, the Medicare "60% rule" compliance requirement (a fixed share of patients must have qualifying rehab diagnoses), and multi-year construction/ramp economics — which favours incumbents with capital and regulatory expertise. The industry is **moderately concentrated**: EHC is the largest freestanding pure-play (roughly one-fifth to one-quarter of freestanding IRF beds nationally), followed by Select Medical's rehab hospital JV, Vibra/Kindred-type operators, and the hundreds of not-for-profit hospital systems that run their own IRF units. The adjacent **home health/hospice** market (post-2022 Enhabit spin, EHAB) is far more fragmented, labour-intensive and reimbursement-constrained — a relevant contrast.

**Cycle position: mid-cycle expansion.** Volumes are growing above GDP, pricing is positive but below input-cost inflation, and the builders (EHC, SEM) are adding beds. This is the "good but maturing" phase — not early-stage hypergrowth, not decline.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Reimbursement reset is positive but thin:** CMS finalised a **+2.6% IRF PPS update for FY2026** (market basket 3.4% less productivity cut; ~+$340m of additional IRF payments) and **+2.3% for FY2027** (3.2% market basket less 0.9%). Predictable, non-negative rate updates with no disruptive case-mix model overhaul.
- **Demographic + acuity mix:** EHC's Q2-2026 **discharges +5.6% (same-store +2.8%)** with **net patient revenue per discharge $22,521, +3.9%**, driven partly by higher patient acuity — i.e., growth in both volume *and* price/mix. Stroke and neuro-rehab demand is a multi-decade runway.
- **Capacity scarcity economics:** CON regimes and the 60% rule throttle new entrants; de novo IRFs typically ramp to mature margins over 3–5 years, so incumbents earn outsized returns on incremental beds. EHC opened Bangor, ME (50 beds) in Oct-2026 and is piloting a **small-format hospital** in Montgomery County, TX — a capital-light expansion template that extends the runway into smaller markets.

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Cost inflation outrunning rate updates:** hospital expense growth ran ~7.5% in 2025 against a ~2–3% Medicare update; labour is ~60% of provider costs and clinical/nursing wage pressure persists. PwC puts the 2026 medical cost trend at **8.5%** (group market) — payers will push back on providers.
- **Medicare Advantage / payer mix risk:** MA now covers roughly half of Medicare beneficiaries; MA plans apply tighter prior-authorisation and length-of-stay management to IRF admissions. MedPAC and the AHA are actively scrutinising MA's effect on post-acute provider finances — a live threat to both volume conversion and days per discharge.
- **Policy and site-neutrality overhang:** site-neutral payment expansions and hospital-outpatient rate reforms (CMS +2.6% for 2026) compress the rate differential that makes freestanding IRFs economically attractive; any future unified PAC-PPS (MedPAC's long-running prototype) is a structural tail risk for the whole setting.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**
- **Scale + density:** largest freestanding IRF operator; local cluster density drives referral relationships with acute hospitals, therapist recruitment, and fixed-cost leverage — the source of the 43% gross margin, ~900bp above SEM's rehab-hospital segment economics.
- **Growth algorithm is repeatable and self-funded:** 10.5% CAGR-type revenue growth over FY2021–25 (5-yr revenue CAGR **10.7%**) from a mix of de novo beds, same-store discharge growth, and ~3–4% annual revenue-per-discharge — not M&A-dependent.
- **Balance sheet / cash conversion:** EBITDA $1.40bn (23.7% margin) against $123m interest expense (~1.1x cover on net income basis) and a 0.61 beta — a defensive, low-cyclicality profile versus acute-care peers exposed to payer mix and ACA subsidies.
- **Risks to the position:** concentration in a single Medicare payment bucket; construction-driven capex means FCF lags earnings; MA utilisation management and labour inflation are the two variables most able to compress the 17.7% operating margin.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**
Demand is demographically underwritten, supply is regulated-constrained, and pricing is at least nominally positive — a combination that supports mid-single-to-low-double-digit revenue growth with stable-to-rising margins for scaled operators. The rating is not higher because the **rate update (+2.3–2.6%) sits structurally below input-cost inflation**, meaning real margin expansion must come from operating leverage, acuity mix and MA contracting rather than price. Within the sector, EHC is the highest-quality expression: growth at ~2x peer median, margins ~600bp ahead, and a genuine capacity pipeline. **Neutral-to-negative for sub-scale or leveraged post-acute names (EHAB, ACHC) where the same reimbursement math bites without the offsetting density.**
