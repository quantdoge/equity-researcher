### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- **Emissions profile:** Facility-based estate (~168 hospitals, 248k admissions) — Scope 1 from natural gas boilers/backup generators and fleet, Scope 2 from purchased electricity. Structurally low carbon intensity relative to revenue (~$5.4B). No disclosed Scope 1/2/3 inventory or science-based target was locatable; **no CDP climate response** found — investors are actively pressing on this (Boston Partners' 2026 engagement report names EHC on GHG disclosure).
- **Transition risk:** Nil-to-minimal. No EU operations → no CSRD, no CBAM. US SEC climate rule remains stayed. No internal carbon price exposure.
- **Physical risk:** Concentrated hurricane/storm exposure in FL, TX, Gulf Coast, Puerto Rico; incremental remediation and business-interruption cost, insurable.
- **Controversies:** None material identified (no spills, EPA enforcement, or remediation liabilities surfaced in search).
- **Verdict:** Financially immaterial today; the reputational gap is *non-disclosure*, which is a screening irritant for EU-domiciled ESG-mandated holders, not a valuation issue.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

This is the material pillar, and it is worse than typical self-reported ESG would suggest:

- **Quality/safety outliers:** A July 2025 *New York Times* / KFF Health News investigation reported that EHC owned **34 of the 41 inpatient rehab facilities** Medicare rated as having statistically significantly **worse rates of potentially preventable readmissions** among discharged patients, and **28 of 87** with worse in-stay preventable readmissions — against a ~1-in-7 national facility share. Documented serious events include a carbon-monoxide death during construction (Huntington, WV), a patient found dead after a bed-exit alarm was disabled (Jackson, TN), and a wrong-drug death among 26 medication errors in six months (Sioux Falls, SD).
- **Offsetting strength:** EHC performs *above* the national rate on "discharge to community" (79 of 233 top-rated facilities), the metric most correlated with payer value.
- **Regulatory blind spot:** CMS does **not** fine IRFs or publish consumer-facing star ratings as it does nursing homes and acute hospitals. This suppresses near-term financial impact but creates latent headline/legislative risk and weakens the feedback loop that normally forces quality remediation.
- **Labour:** ~29,700 employees; therapy/nursing scarcity and contract-labour cost are the primary margin variables (MedPAC documents sharp swings in EHC contract-labour usage). Union density is low; no material strike or OSHA action surfaced.
- **Verdict:** High social risk, but the transmission channel is **volume/referral and litigation**, not fines — slow-burn rather than acute.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Positives**
- **Separate Chairman and CEO** — Greg Carmichael (independent; former Fifth Third Bancorp CEO) as Chairman, Mark Tarr as President & CEO. No CEO/Chair duality.
- **Single class of stock** — no dual-class structure, no poison pill identified; shareholders vote annually on directors (10 elected at the May 2026 annual meeting) and on pay.
- Substantial independent board with public-company CEO/CFO experience; established committee structure and a stated ethics/compliance programme built on the "Seven Elements of an Effective Compliance Program."

**Deductions**
- **Legacy integrity overhang:** the company *is* the former HealthSouth, which collapsed in a ~$1.4B accounting fraud (2003). Board and management are fully reconstituted, but the institutional memory keeps a compliance discount attached.
- **Repeat FCA/whistleblower exposure** — the 2019 $48M settlement covered conduct alleged from **2007 onward**, and FCA litigation continues (*Gentry v. Encompass Health Rehabilitation Hospital*, 5th Cir. No. 25-20093, argued 2025). Recurrence after a landmark fraud settlement is the single most damaging governance fact here.
- **Compensation alignment:** pay is weighted to adjusted EBITDA and growth/development metrics — incentives that reward volume growth in a business where volume growth is precisely what generated the FCA allegations. Weak explicit linkage to quality, safety or compliance outcomes in disclosed design.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **Highest: CMS / Medicare IRF regime** — the **60% Rule** (≥60% of admissions from 13 qualifying conditions) is the binding licence-to-operate constraint and the direct motivation behind the settled fraud allegations. FY payment updates and site-neutral/readmission policy are the dominant ESG-adjacent cash-flow levers. State CON (certificate-of-need) laws govern new-hospital expansion.
- **DOJ/OIG healthcare fraud enforcement** — currently the most active enforcement regime touching EHC (reverse false claims and MA risk-adjustment theories are the 2026 priority).
- **HIPAA / state privacy** — routine.
- **EU CSRD: not applicable.** **CBAM: not applicable.** **SEC climate disclosure rule: stayed.** No ESG-linked debt covenants identified.
- **Net:** regulatory ESG exposure is **concentrated in US healthcare payment/compliance**, not in climate or disclosure regimes — which is why generic E/S/G scores mis-rank this name.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Not a Leader (no GHG disclosure, no quality-linked compensation, repeat compliance failures). Not a Laggard or "ESG Risk" designation on balance — governance structure is genuinely sound (independent chair, single share class, no control abuses), environmental risk is trivial, and the company leads the industry on community-discharge outcomes. Third-party ESG risk ratings place EHC in the elevated-risk band (multiple mandate-driven engagement funds list EHC among holdings with Sustainalytics ESG Risk scores ≥30, focused on **Product Governance and Emissions/Effluents/Waste** — note this is inferred from engagement disclosures, not a primary score retrieval).

**The rating is held back almost entirely by one thing: measured quality-of-care dispersion within its own portfolio.**

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**Does not change the thesis, but should widen the risk premium modestly.**

- **What is priced:** the 2019 DOJ settlement and legacy fraud are historical and well understood; environmental exposure is de minimis; governance mechanics are clean. None of this justifies an ESG-driven discount.
- **What is not priced:** (a) the concentration of Medicare's worst preventable-readmission outliers inside EHC's own portfolio, now nationally publicised via NYT; (b) continued FCA litigation after a landmark settlement; (c) compensation that rewards volume in a business where the enforcement risk *is* volume-driven.
- **Why it stays Neutral:** CMS cannot currently fine IRFs the way it fines nursing homes, so there is no mechanical earnings hit; and EHC's superior discharge-to-community performance gives it a genuine payer-value defence. The risk is tail-shaped (licensure, whistleblower, legislative) rather than recurring.
- **Monitoring triggers that would move this to Negative:** any CMS rule extending penalties/star ratings to IRFs; a new DOJ intervention or >$50M resolution; adverse 5th Circuit ruling materially expanding FCA exposure; a state licensure action following a patient death; or a proxy that again omits quality/safety metrics from executive incentive design.
- **Triggers that would move it to Positive:** publication of a Scope 1–3 inventory with targets and third-party assurance; adding readmission/adverse-event metrics to the annual incentive plan; board-level quality committee with disclosed oversight of outlier facilities.

**Bottom line for the memo:** EHC's ESG profile is a governance-and-quality story, not a climate story. Treat it as a **litigation/concentration risk overlay on a high-margin growth compounder** — size positions for tail risk in Medicare enforcement and referral relationships, not for carbon transition.
