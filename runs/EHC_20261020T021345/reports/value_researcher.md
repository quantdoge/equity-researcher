## Encompass Health Corporation (EHC) — Value Investment Analysis

**Business:** Largest U.S. owner/operator of inpatient rehabilitation hospitals (IRFs) — ~165 hospitals, ~30,000 employees. Post-2022 Enhabit spinoff, EHC is a pure-play IRF operator. Price used: ~$122; market cap ~$12.4B; net debt ~$2.7B; EV ~$15.1B.

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow (widening)**

**Sources of advantage:**
- **Regulatory/efficient-scale moat:** The IRF "60% rule" and state certificate-of-need regimes cap new capacity. Encompass is roughly 2x the size of its nearest competitor with ~30% share of a fragmented ~1,200-hospital industry. Scale drives superior payer contracting, fixed-cost leverage, and the deepest de novo pipeline in the sector.
- **Referral-network switching costs:** IRF placement is driven by hospital discharge planners and physician relationships; density of local hospitals creates a flywheel competitors cannot easily replicate, and the largest systems increasingly joint-venture *with* EHC rather than build.
- **Demonstrated pricing power:** Revenue per discharge has compounded mid-single-digits despite CMS rate updates of only ~2.5–2.8%/yr (FY26 +2.6%; FY27 proposed +2.8%) — evidence of mix, acuity, and commercial/MA pricing power.

**Moat trajectory over 5 years:** Widened. Revenue CAGR 10.7% (FY21–25) with gross margin up ~80bp and operating margin up ~230bp (14.5% → 17.7%), while ROIC rose from 13.0% to 17.6%. A widening spread between ROIC and WACC through a reimbursement-constrained period is the clearest proof of moat.

**Moat caveat:** The customer is effectively the federal government (Medicare and Medicare Advantage). This is a regulated-utility-like moat — durable, but with a political ceiling on pricing. It is not a brand or network-effects moat; it should not be underwritten at a consumer-staples multiple.

**Strengths:** Revenue and margin are unusually predictable (same-store discharge growth 3–4% + rate +2–3% + new capacity 2–3%). Interest coverage ~11x; leverage ~1.9x and falling; interest expense down from $176M (FY22) to $123M (FY25). ROIC of 17.6% vs. an estimated ~7.5% WACC is a genuine value creator.

**Deductions:** (a) FCF conversion is structurally ~75–80%, not 100%, because growth capex ($736M in FY25, guided higher in FY26) is continuous — reported FCF of $439M vs. $720M of estimated owner earnings shows how much of the economics is being reinvested; (b) the balance sheet carries real, permanent leverage; (c) GAAP effective tax/non-controlling-interest presentation is messy, so adjusted figures must be tracked.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current state (FY2026E):** Revenue guidance $6.41–6.49B; adjusted EBITDA $1.365–1.395B; adjusted EPS trending ~$6.00–6.10 (H1 actual $3.15).

| Measure | Current |
|---|---|
| P/E (FY26E) | ~20x |
| EV/adj. EBITDA | ~11x |
| EV/EBIT | ~15x |
| P/FCF (trailing, capex-heavy) | ~28x |
| Owner-earnings yield (equity) | ~5.8% |
| Dividend yield | 0.6% (payout ~13%) |

**Intrinsic value — three methods:**

1. *DCF on reported FCF* (base $439M, FCF inflecting to ~$500M in FY26 as capex growth moderates, then 12–14% for five years fading to 4%; WACC 7.5–8.0%, terminal 2.5%): **$126–$142/share**.
2. *Owner earnings capitalized* (net income ~$615M + D&A ~$350M − maintenance capex ~$245M ≈ **$720M**, grown 3.5–4% in perpetuity at 8–9%): **$120–$150/share**, midpoint ~$140.
3. *EPS compounding* ($6.05 base, 12% CAGR, exit 17–18x in 2036, discounted 8%): **$139–$157/share**.

**Intrinsic value estimate: ~$140/share** (range $125–$155). **Current price $122 ≈ 13% discount to IV.**

**What the price implies:** ~11x EV/EBITDA and 20x earnings for a business compounding revenue ~9% and EPS ~12–15% with 17%+ ROIC is not demanding — roughly 12–14% expected annual return if multiples hold. But it is also not the 25–30% discount my discipline requires. Consensus targets ($144–$155) corroborate that the stock is modestly, not grossly, undervalued.

**Buy price for a margin of safety:** ~$105 (25% below IV). At that level, EV/EBITDA ~9.5x and owner-earnings yield ~7%.

---

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good/High**

- **CEO Mark Tarr** (long-tenured, operational background) has executed a coherent strategy: exit non-core (home health/hospice spinoff as Enhabit in 2022), redeploy capital into the highest-return asset (de novo IRFs at 6–10/year plus 150–200 bed additions), and shrink the share count modestly. EPS CAGR since FY22 of ~22% is largely operational, not financial engineering.
- **Capital allocation discipline:** Growth capex is large ($736M FY25) but targets de novo hospitals with mid-20s% cash-on-cash returns and 3–5 year ramp — far above WACC. Debt reduced from $2.99B (FY22) to $2.71B (FY25). Buyback authorization recently *increased* and repurchases rose to $158M in FY25 from $31M. Dividend raised ~10%/yr with a very conservative ~13% payout.
- **Alignment:** Management has historically given conservative guidance and beaten it (2026 guidance raised twice; Q1/Q2 2026 EPS beats of $0.09 and $0.07). That is the hallmark of a sandbagging, owner-minded culture.
- **Gap:** Insider ownership percentages and detailed compensation metrics were not available in this data set — a genuine gap in the assessment, though the operating record is strong.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (accumulate below ~$105)

This is a high-quality, moated, predictable compounder with a widening ROIC spread, a clean balance sheet, and shareholder-aligned management — exactly the kind of business I want to own for a decade. But at ~20x forward earnings, ~11x EV/EBITDA and a 3.5% reported FCF yield (5.8% owner-earnings yield), the price already reflects most of the good news. **A fair price is not a buy.** Existing holders should keep the position (the compounding engine is intact and there is no thesis break); new capital should wait for either a reimbursement-driven selloff (the stock fell ~10% on a single day in July 2025 and ~7% in October 2025 on such fears) or a price near $105, which would offer a true 25% margin of safety.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Medicare reimbursement (the dominant risk).** Essentially all IRF revenue is government-linked. FY26 gave +2.6%; FY27 proposed +2.8%. A cut, a market-basket methodology change, or a bad 60% rule revision would hit both margins *and* the multiple simultaneously — the classic regulatory-moat failure mode.
2. **Site-neutral / post-acute payment reform.** Policy pressure to equalize payments across post-acute settings would erode the IRF pricing premium. This is the bear case that periodically compresses the stock by 10–15% in a day.
3. **Medicare Advantage penetration.** MA now covers roughly half of beneficiaries; plans deny and down-code more aggressively than fee-for-service. Rising MA mix is a slow margin leak that is hard to see quarter to quarter.
4. **Labor cost inflation.** Nursing/therapy wage pressure (~55% of revenue is labor) can outrun the 2.6% rate update; EHC has managed this so far but the buffer is thin.
5. **Capex execution / FCF dependence on growth.** With capex guided higher in 2026, reported FCF may stay in the $350–500M range. If de novo returns disappoint (new markets, health-system JV partners taking economics), the compounding math breaks and the "owner earnings" case collapses toward the reported-FCF case (~$126/share).
6. **Leverage + rates.** ~1.9x net debt/EBITDA is manageable, but refinancing $2.7B of debt in a higher-rate world would eat into the EPS trajectory that the multiple depends on.
7. **Data caveats:** price history available through Feb-2026 (last close $107) while the quote/profile implies ~$122 in late 2026; peer multiples for true IRF comparables (Alden-type private operators, Select Medical) were not available — the sector-peer feed returned only mega-cap pharma/device names and was not usable. Valuation is therefore triangulated on EHC's own history and absolute cash-flow math rather than a peer table.
