## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: SUB-INVESTMENT GRADE (BB / Ba3-equivalent) — but high-quality, deleveraging, positive outlook**

EHC is a ~$12.0B market-cap post-acute operator (149 inpatient rehab hospitals, 29,699 FTEs) rated **S&P BB issuer / BB- issue-level on the $500M 2034 senior unsecured notes**, with **Moody's outlook revised to positive**. It sits at the top of the high-yield band, one notch from crossover.

The rating understates the balance-sheet quality. At FY2025:
- Net debt/EBITDA of **1.83x** (my calc 1.86x on balance-sheet net debt of $2,611M / EBITDA $1,404.1M) — down from **2.76x in 2023**. LTM through Q2 2026 is **1.74x** ($2,611M / $1,500.0M).
- EBIT/interest of **8.55x** (EBITDA/interest **11.4x**), versus a **3.00x minimum coverage covenant**.
- **$1.0B senior secured revolver maturing 2031**, with a **max leverage covenant of 4.50x**. Current ~1.9x gross leaves enormous headroom.
- **No meaningful debt maturities until 2028** (4.50% notes due 2028, 4.75% due 2030, 4.625% due 2031, new $500M 2034s). No refinancing cliff.

This is a structurally sound credit whose rating is a function of payer concentration, not leverage.

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis

Base cost structure: cash operating costs (ex-D&A) = $4,531.1M (76.3% of revenue), D&A $327.9M fixed, interest $123.2M.

I model **35% cost flexibility** — deliberately conservative, because post-acute labor is semi-fixed and staffing ratios are regulated.

| Scenario | Revenue | EBITDA | EBITDA Δ | EBIT/Int | Net Debt/EBITDA | Covenant (4.50x) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base FY2025 | 5,935.2 | 1,404.1 | — | 8.7x | 1.86x | ✅ 2.4x cushion |
| −5% (rate cut) | 5,638.4 | 1,211.2 | −14% | 7.2x | 2.16x | ✅ |
| −10% | 5,341.7 | 1,018.3 | −27% | 5.6x | 2.56x | ✅ |
| **−20%** | **4,748.2** | **534.3** | **−62%** | **1.68x** | **4.89x** | ❌ **BREACH** |
| Covenant breach point | −21.4% | 580.2 | −59% | — | 4.50x | — |

**Findings:**
- **Covenant cushion ≈ 21% revenue decline.** Leverage (4.50x max) binds before coverage (3.0x minimum), which would require a ~27% decline on an EBITDA basis.
- **At −20% revenue, the company remains operationally solvent** but breaches the leverage covenant: EBIT $206.4M covers interest 1.68x, net loss reported. An **equity cure** provision and revolver availability would bridge it.
- **Liquidity survives the −20% shock.** FCF turns roughly breakeven-to-+$110M (EBITDA $534M − interest $123M − ~$50M tax − ~$250M maintenance capex, with growth capex deferred). Cash of $145M plus **~$900M+ undrawn on the $1.0B revolver (2031)** means **no liquidity crisis and no near-term refinancing need**.
- **The realistic stress is not −20% volume but −3% to −8% reimbursement.** At −5%, leverage is 2.16x and coverage 7.2x — entirely manageable. EHC's Medicare/Medicare Advantage concentration means **rate is the risk, not demand** (demographics are an 85+ population tailwind).

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating

# **LOW** (credit) / **MODERATE** (equity & policy)

The balance sheet is not the risk at EHC. A 9/9 F-Score, 1.74x LTM net leverage, 9.2x interest coverage, expanding margins, a 2031 revolver, and a seven-year maturity runway constitute a genuinely low-risk financial profile. FCF has compounded from $268M → $360M → $439M.

The risk sits **outside** the financial statements: 60%+ of revenue comes from government payers, and the equity's 27% volatility is a direct function of that. FCF yield of 3.53% is modest for a sub-investment-grade credit and reflects the ~$736M annual capex program (12.4% of revenue) on de novo hospitals — genuine growth investment, but it caps distributable cash.

**Caveat:** Q2 2026 showed the first sequential margin slip (EBITDA margin 24.1% vs. 25.7% in Q1 2026; net income $153.9M vs. $194.5M). One quarter is not a trend, but de novo start-up drag and payer mix bear monitoring.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Payer concentration is the dominant risk** — Medicare/MA rate policy, not leverage, drives the equity. Three >7% single-day moves in eight months confirm this. Unhedgeable at the balance sheet.
2. **Thin reported liquidity:** $145.1M cash, current ratio 1.08, cash-to-liabilities 0.04. Day-to-day liquidity depends entirely on the revolver. Fine given 2031 maturity, but there is no cash buffer.
3. **Sub-investment-grade rating** (BB/BB-) — higher cost of capital and index/liquidity constraints. **Refinancing economics are moving against them:** existing coupons are 4.50–4.75%, while new 2034 notes price in a ~6% BB market; refinancing the ~$2.07B unsecured stack at +125bp adds roughly $26M/yr of interest. Not a cliff — a slow cost headwind.
4. **Goodwill & intangibles of $1,626M (23% of assets)** leave tangible equity of ~$1,650M against $2,714M of debt (1.64x tangible leverage). A reimbursement reset that impaired de novo or acquired hospital values would hit book equity directly.
5. **Capex intensity (12.4% of revenue)** leaves FCF margin at just 7.4%. Capital returns ($229.1M dividends + buybacks) are 52% of FCF — sustainable, but with little slack if growth capex accelerates.
6. **Data gaps:** Altman Z uses book equity (understates); no CDS spread available; no peer benchmarking performed; **yield-curve tool failed** (schema error, not retried); Sortino ratio unavailable; $464.7M debt reconciliation gap and FY2024 net-income inconsistency noted in §2.

**Bottom line:** Financially robust, ratings-constrained. A 20%+ revenue decline is required to threaten covenants — a low-probability tail. The risk-adjusted case should focus on payer policy and capex returns, not solvency.
