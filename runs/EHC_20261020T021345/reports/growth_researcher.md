# Encompass Health Corporation (EHC) — Growth Investment Analysis

*Price $121.71 | Market cap ~$12.0B | EV ~$15.0B | Sector: Healthcare / Medical Care Facilities*

---

## 1. Growth Story

Encompass Health is the largest owner-operator of inpatient rehabilitation hospitals (IRFs) in the U.S. — 176 hospitals as of Q2 2026, up from 149 at the 2022 Enhabit spin — and it is executing a repeatable, capital-backed capacity-compounding machine: 6–10 de novo hospitals plus 80–120 beds per year into a demographically underwritten demand pool (stroke, neuro, cardiac, complex ortho recovery for an aging population). This is not a "new technology" disruptor; it is a **share-taker in post-acute care**, where its IRF model delivers measurably better functional outcomes than skilled nursing for the highest-acuity rehab patients, in an industry protected by certificate-of-need barriers and scarce clinical labor. The growth algorithm is durable rather than explosive: ~5–6% discharge growth, ~3–4% net pricing, plus margin leverage from scale — netting a consistent **~10% revenue and ~12% EPS compounding** with unusual visibility.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

- **Core TAM:** U.S. inpatient rehabilitation is roughly a **$16–18B revenue pool** (≈$10–11B Medicare IRF spend, ~1,200 facilities). EHC at ~$6.45B is already **~35–40% of the IRF industry** — so the incremental story is not IRF share alone.
- **The bigger prize is post-acute share shift:** Medicare post-acute spend exceeds **$200B**, of which IRFs are only ~5–6% (SNFs ~$170B, home health ~$100B+). Every policy nudge toward higher-acuity, outcome-based rehab (where IRF wins on functional gain and readmission avoidance for stroke/neuro/ortho) moves dollars into EHC's lane.
- **Demographics underwrite volume:** the 65+ cohort grows ~3%/yr and the 85+ cohort faster; stroke prevalence and joint-replacement volumes rise with it. Same-store discharge growth of 2.8% (Q2'26) plus net bed additions yields 5–6% total discharge growth.
- **Moat:** certificate-of-need regimes in ~two-thirds of states cap new IRF beds; scale in clinical recruiting, payer contracting, and referral relationships with health systems (many via JV) is not replicable by a de novo entrant. Land-and-build in CON states is the durable edge.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts (next 6–12 months)

1. **Q3 2026 results — Oct 28, 2026** (consensus $1.36 EPS / $1.60B revenue). Seven straight beats and a freshly raised guide make another beat-and-raise the base case.
2. **5 additional de novo hospitals + >100 beds to open before year-end 2026** (after 3 hospitals/139 beds and 54 beds in H1'26) — direct 2027 revenue and a visible pipeline for the 6–10/yr cadence.
3. **FY2027 IRF PPS final rule (effective Oct 1, 2026):** market basket +3.2% less 0.9% productivity = **2.3% net rate update**, with CMS estimating ~**+2.8% total IRF payments (+$340M)**. In line with, not better than, history — the "no negative surprise" outcome removes an overhang.
4. **Capital return acceleration:** dividend raised 10.5% ($0.19 → $0.21/qtr, July 2026) plus an **expanded buyback authorization** (Aug 2026) — modest but signals confidence and a floor under the multiple.
5. **2027 guidance at the Q4'26 report (Feb 2027)** — the key test of whether double-digit EPS growth extends into a third year; also any M&A/JV announcements in the de novo pipeline.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY**

EHC is the highest-quality compounder in post-acute care: 10%+ revenue growth, expanding margins, a 7-for-7 beat record, positive estimate revisions, and a de novo pipeline that makes 8–9% forward revenue and ~12% EPS growth highly credible. It trades at **19.9x 2026E and 10.7x EV/EBITDA** — a premium to UHS (7.9x P/E, 6.2x EV/EBITDA) and THC (17.2x P/E, 7.7x EV/EBITDA) that is *earned* by roughly double the growth rate, yet the stock is **flat over the last 12 months while estimates rose**, leaving it ~23% below the $144–155 price-target cluster (median ~$150). That combination — rising estimates, falling multiple, visible capacity growth — is the classic setup for 20%+ total return without needing multiple expansion. It is not a Cathie Wood-style hyper-growth disrupter (Rule of 40 of 18 and 28x P/FCF cap the "premium multiple" argument), which is why this is a **Buy, not a Strong Buy**: expect ~12–15% EPS-led compounding plus modest re-rating, not a triple.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Reimbursement concentration (highest risk).** ~75–80% of revenue is Medicare/Medicaid. The FY2027 update is only **2.3% net** vs. EHC's ~3% cost inflation; **site-neutral payment reform** or any IRF rate compression would hit both margin and multiple simultaneously. This is the primary reason the stock de-rated in 2025.
2. **Labor cost inflation.** ~29,700 employees; nursing/therapist shortages drove the sector's contract-labor pain in 2022–23. Salary and benefit inflation is the largest controllable-uncontrollable in the P&L; watch SWB per FTE each quarter.
3. **Capex intensity and de novo dilution.** $736M of 2025 capex against $1.176B operating cash flow leaves only **7.4% FCF margin** and 28x P/FCF. Every new hospital carries 12–24 months of pre-opening losses; a slower ramp (labor, CON delays, referral build) converts "growth" into ROIC drag.
4. **Same-store volume deceleration.** Total discharge growth (5.6%) is nearly double same-store (2.8%); if new-bed additions slip or per-discharge pricing normalizes below ~3%, the 10% revenue algorithm breaks.
5. **Leverage/refinancing.** ~$3.0B net debt, ~$123M annual interest; refinancing at higher coupons would consume a meaningful slice of the ~$640M annual EBITDA growth. Also exposes EHC to credit-spread shocks.
6. **Competition and execution.** UHS, THC, Acadia (currently distressed, EV/EBITDA negative — a potential share-donor or an acquirer), plus health-system JVs. EHC's premium multiple leaves no cushion for a single disappointing quarter.
7. **Multiple risk.** At 10.7x EV/EBITDA vs. 6–8x for peers, all return must come from EBITDA growth; a sector de-rating (as seen in late 2025) can offset two years of fundamental progress.

---

**Bottom line:** Own it for the 8–10% revenue / ~12% EPS compounding at ~20x forward with 23% upside to consensus targets. Monitor quarterly on three dials: **same-store discharge growth, net revenue per discharge, and salary/benefit per FTE.** A break in any two would move this to Hold.
