# EHC — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Bottom line:** Encompass Health is a **pure-play US operator** — 176 inpatient rehabilitation hospitals in 39 states and Puerto Rico, with no disclosed non-US revenue. Classic geopolitical risk (sanctions, FX, expropriation, cross-border trade) is effectively **nil**. The real exposure is **domestic payment-policy risk**: a single-payer-dependent business model facing a below-cost-trend rate update, an active CMS reform RFI, and a persistent False Claims Act / Medicare Advantage enforcement backdrop.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**Risk 1 — Reimbursement compression via the IRF PPS (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High)**
CMS finalized a **net +2.3%** FY2027 IRF update (3.2% market basket less 0.9pt productivity), ~$340m aggregate industry increase (Source: [CMS-1845-F fact sheet, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/fy-2027-inpatient-rehabilitation-facilities-irf-prospective-payment-system-pps-final-rule-cms-1845-f)). That is below EHC's own ~3.9% net revenue-per-discharge growth and below wage inflation — a real-terms squeeze on a business where Medicare is the dominant payer. Compounding it, **MedPAC reported the IRF Medicare margin rose >2pts to 17.1% in FY2024 and projects further increases** (Source: [Applied Policy summary of MedPAC March 2026 Report](https://www.appliedpolicy.com/medpac-releases-march-2026-report-to-congress/)) — elevated margins are precisely the condition under which MedPAC recommends constraining updates. Watch item: CMS's **RFI on IRF case-mix redesign** (moving toward a PDPM-style model) embedded in the FY2027 rule — a multi-year repricing risk.

**Risk 2 — Medicare Advantage friction and payer-mix drift (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
MedPAC's June 2026 chapter specifically models the **association between MA penetration and post-acute provider finances** ([MedPAC June 2026, Ch.4](https://www.medpac.gov/document/chapter-4-estimated-association-between-medicare-advantage-enrollment-and-hospitals-and-post-acute-care-providers-financesjune-2026-report/)), and HHS OIG has documented MA prior-authorization denial rates of 23%–0.4% for post-acute admissions with near-universal reversal on appeal ([OIG, 2026](https://oig.hhs.gov/reports/all/2026/medicare-advantage-organizations-overturned-nearly-all-appealed-prior-authorization-denials-for-skilled-nursing-facility-admission-raising-concerns-about-initial-denials/)). Higher MA mix = higher denial/appeal cost and slower cash conversion for EHC's ~68,900 quarterly discharges. Partially offset by CMS's 2026 prior-authorization turnaround rules.

**Risk 3 — Trade policy and labour-immigration friction raising the cost of growth (Probability: Medium / Impact: Low-Medium)**
EHC's growth engine is physical construction — 6–10 new hospitals plus 80–120 beds annually (Management target; 3 hospitals/139 beds opened in H1'26, 5 more planned for 2H'26 — Q2'26 release). Tariffs on steel, aluminium, and imported medical equipment feed directly into per-bed capex, and restrictive immigration policy tightens the therapist/nurse labour pool underlying the 29,699-person workforce. This is the only material channel through which US trade/geopolitical tension reaches EHC's P&L. No export-control, sanctions, or China/Taiwan supply-chain exposure is disclosed.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **FY2027 IRF PPS (CMS-1845-F), 30 Jul 2026** — Favourable clarifications: all therapies must now be initiated within the **36-hour rule** (CMS expects this to "significantly reduce inquiries"); IDT meeting required by day 4 and weekly thereafter (defined as 7 days from the initial meeting). Unfavourable: IRF QRP data submission window cut from ~4.5 months to ~45 days from FY2029, with a **2-percentage-point Annual Increase Factor penalty** for non-reporting. ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/fy-2027-inpatient-rehabilitation-facilities-irf-prospective-payment-system-pps-final-rule-cms-1845-f))
- **FCA / qui tam — favourable appellate outcome:** *United States ex rel. Gentry v. Encompass Health Rehabilitation Hospital of Pearland, L.L.C.*, No. 25-20093 (5th Cir., 3 Nov 2025) — the Fifth Circuit **affirmed dismissal** of the whistleblower suit against EHC; Judge Ho concurred separately questioning the constitutionality of qui tam. ([Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/ca5/25-20093/25-20093-2025-11-03.html); [Reuters, 4 Nov 2025](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/conservative-appellate-judge-faults-us-whistleblower-laws-constitutional-2025-11-04/))
- **Legacy FCA settlement (2019):** **$48m** to resolve allegations EHC submitted inaccurate information to Medicare to maintain IRF compliance — i.e. the "60% rule"/preadmission-screening perimeter. Notably **no Corporate Integrity Agreement** was imposed, so no ongoing monitor. ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/encompass-health-agrees-pay-48-million-resolve-false-claims-act-allegations-relating-its); [EHC statement](https://investor.encompasshealth.com/news/news-details/2019/Encompass-Health-Settles-DOJ-Investigation-and-Related-Qui-Tam-Lawsuits/default.aspx))
- **New (2026) ERISA class action:** filed **30 March 2026** by Miller Shah LLP on behalf of participants in the Encompass Health Retirement plan (401(k) fee/prudence theory). Standardised, low-severity claims; watch for consolidated-plan settlement in the single-digit-millions range ([Miller Shah](https://millershah.com/blog/erisa-class-action-encompass-health/)).
- **Quality/regulatory reputational overhang:** KFF Health News reporting documents that serious patient-safety failures at for-profit rehab hospitals — including at an Encompass facility — go largely unpenalised and undisclosed by CMS, which is a live catalyst for tighter IRF quality reporting (consistent with the QRP changes above) ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/health-industry/for-profit-rehab-hospitals-errors-unpenalized-undisclosed-cms/)).
- **Sector enforcement direction:** DOJ's FCA posture toward **Medicare Advantage** providers is intensifying (e.g., Complete Health Partners, **$14.1m**, 3 Aug 2026), with law-firm commentary flagging MA risk-adjustment and medical-necessity theories as the growth area in FCA ([WilmerHale, 31 Aug 2026](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260831-recent-doj-settlements-highlight-false-claims-act-scrutiny-of-medicare-advantage-providers); [DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/medicare-advantage-provider-complete-health-pay-14100000-settle-false-claims-act-suit)).
- **Long-run structural:** MedPAC notes the Medicare Hospital Insurance trust fund is projected **insolvent by 2033** — the tail risk behind any 2030s-era IRF rate reform.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

> ### **Geopolitical risk: Very Low. Regulatory/Legal risk: Medium. Composite: Medium.**

**Rationale.** For a global-risk mandate, EHC is close to a best case: no foreign revenue, no sanctions, FX, or expropriation exposure, and a favourable recent appellate ruling on its principal litigation archetype. The composite lands at **Medium** entirely because of **concentrated US federal payment dependency** — a +2.3% FY2027 rate update against a 17.1% and rising Medicare margin, an open CMS case-mix-reform RFI, and a structurally active FCA/MA enforcement environment. None of these are existential in the next 24 months; the realistic sensitivity is 100–200bps of EBITDA margin from rate and MA-denial dynamics, plus episodic legal accruals that have historically been absorbable ($48m in 2019 vs. ~$1.4bn EBITDA today).

**Monitoring triggers:** (1) MedPAC's FY2028 IRF update recommendation and any congressional site-neutral/post-acute package; (2) CMS's case-mix modernisation proposal emerging from the FY2027 RFI; (3) MA prior-authorisation denial trend in EHC's disclosures; (4) any new sealed qui tam surfacing in 10-Q legal proceedings.
