## EHC (Encompass Health) — Forensic Short-Seller Analysis

---

### 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **HIGH**

I went looking for fraud and did not find it. EHC's reported numbers are backed by cash to a degree that is unusual for a capital-intensive provider:

| Metric | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OCF / Net income | 138% | 193% | 235% | 167% | **207%** |
| Accruals ratio | — | -7.7% | -8.2% | -8.4% | **-8.6%** |
| Gross margin | 42.4% | 40.3% | 41.3% | 41.6% | **43.2%** |
| Operating margin | 16.4% | 14.5% | 15.2% | 16.1% | **17.7%** |
| DSO (days) | — | 45.1 | 46.5 | 40.7 | **38.1** |

Accruals are *negative* in every year and getting more negative — the opposite of manipulation. Cash conversion is 2x earnings. Receivables are growing **3.4% vs revenue +10.5%**, and DSO has fallen ~700bp in two years. There is no channel stuffing, no revenue-recognition stretch, no capitalisation games visible (capex/revenue is flat-to-down at 12.4%).

The one blemish: the composite quality score is only **52/100**, driven entirely by **FCF conversion of 78%** — not by accounting. That is a *capital allocation* issue, not an integrity issue (see §2).

### 2. Key Red Flags Found (ranked by materiality)

**A. Growth capex is eating the cash — the "quality" of the growth is unproven.** Capex rose from $551M (2021) to $736M (2025), or **2.25x D&A**. Growth capex ≈ $408M/yr, ~6.9% of revenue, going into de novo IRF beds. Reported FCF is just $439M → **3.7% FCF yield**. On maintenance-capex-adjusted (≈D&A) owner earnings of ~$848M, the yield is ~7% — acceptable, not cheap. The bear lever is that EHC is a serial deployer whose incremental returns are only visible years out, and the depreciation on those beds (D&A already +49% since 2021) will arrive before the returns do.

**B. Revenue growth is decelerating on a 2-year view.** Quarterly YoY has drifted from 12.69% → 9.58%:

- Q4'24 12.69% | Q1'25 10.59% | Q2'25 12.03% | Q3'25 9.36% | Q4'25 9.94% | Q1'26 9.01% | Q2'26 9.58%
- **Average first four: 11.8%. Average last four: 9.5%. That is a 230bp fade.**

The deceleration tool labels this "stable," which I think is too generous. Q2'26 volume detail (discharges **+5.6%**, revenue/discharge **+3.9%**) shows the mix is healthy, but 5.6% discharge growth is inflated by new hospital openings — same-store is lower.

**C. Q1 2026 looks like it contained a one-off, and guidance was raised off it.** Net margin by quarter: 9.8%, 9.8%, 8.6%, 9.5% (2025) → **12.3%** (Q1'26) → **9.6%** (Q2'26). Q1'26 is a ~260bp outlier vs every other period, worth roughly **$40M / $0.40 per share**. It produced a "+28% YoY" Q1 EPS print. Q2 EPS then fell 21% sequentially (1.96 → 1.55) and grew only 8.3% YoY. The raise to $5.89–$6.11 adjusted EPS came off that base; **consensus of $6.11 sits at the very top of management's range**, leaving zero cushion.

**D. Denial/underpayment leakage is worsening.** EHC's own Q1 2026 release cites risk from Medicare reimbursement claims and **"lengthening delays in its ability to recover improperly denied claims."** Denials and RAC activity on IRF admissions are a slow-burn margin leak that DSO cannot capture (they show up as write-offs, not receivables).

**E. Thin tangible book.** Goodwill + intangibles $1.63B = 49.6% of equity, 23% of assets. Tangible book ~$16.7/share against a $121.71 price = **7.3x tangible book**. There is no asset floor.

**F. Data caveat / reconciliation gap.** The cash-flow statement shows 2024 net income of $599.4M vs $455.7M on the income statement — a **$143.7M unexplained gap** (2022 shows a similar $94.9M gap, plausibly Enhabit spin-related). I could not access the 10-K to resolve this (no 10-K retrievable via the filing tool). It is most likely a gain-on-sale or continuing/discontinued-ops presentation artifact rather than fabrication — but it should be reconciled before anyone relies on the cash-flow net income line.

**G. Altman Z = 2.12 (grey zone).** Misleading here: the model uses book equity. Substituting market cap lifts Z to ~**2.62** — still grey, but gross debt/EBITDA is only **1.9x** and net debt has fallen from $3.43B (2021) to $2.61B. **Solvency is not a risk.**

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

The moat is **regulatory and certificate-of-need-based, not technological** — which is exactly why it is durable *and* why the tail risk is binary.

- **Structural protections:** 38 states have CON/need laws limiting new IRF beds. EHC is the largest operator (~170+ hospitals), giving it referral relationships, scale purchasing and payer contracting leverage that de novos cannot replicate quickly. This is why gross margin is *expanding* (43.2% → 44.5% in Q2'26) despite the industry's Medicare Advantage mix shift. **The obvious bear thesis — MA plans squeezing post-acute — is currently being falsified by the P&L.**
- **Real threats:** (i) CMS IRF PPS rate updates and any move toward **site-neutral payment**; (ii) post-acute bundles/MA prior-authorization creep; (iii) CON liberalisation in growth states; (iv) substitution of inpatient rehab by SNFs and home health for lower-acuity cases. Any of (i)–(iii) is a step-function hit to a business that trades at ~10.5x EV/EBITDA on ~1.9x leverage.

I could not obtain valid operating comparables — the peer tool returned mega-cap pharma (LLY, JNJ, ABBV), not post-acute operators. **Peer multiple benchmarking is a gap in this report**; I anchor on absolute multiples and EHC's own history instead.

- Judgement: **a yellow flag, not a red one.** A long-tenured CEO of a $12B company selling 1.7% of his stake at a high is routine diversification and likely 10b5-1. I cannot see transaction codes, so **net insider direction is unverified** — do not over-weight this.
- Positives: no auditor change flagged, no related-party issues surfaced, guidance has been *raised*, and the buyback was expanded. Management language is promotional but has been **matched by delivery** (F-Score 9, guidance beats).
- Governance is clean by the standards that normally precede collapse.

### 6. Short Thesis Summary: Is there a credible bear case?

**Yes — but it is a valuation/deceleration short, not a fraud short.** The strongest version:

> EHC trades at ~20x 2026 adjusted EPS and ~10.5x EV/EBITDA for a business whose revenue growth has faded from ~12% to ~9%, whose reported FCF yield is 3.7%, and which must reinvest ~7% of revenue annually into new beds simply to sustain high-single-digit growth. The Q1 2026 margin outlier ($0.40/share) flattered the H1 comparison and the subsequent guidance raise. Consensus ($6.11) sits at the *top* of management's own ($5.89–6.11) range. Any adverse CMS IRF PPS or site-neutral ruling, or further MA denial escalation, compresses a 10.5x multiple toward 8x — a 20-25% drawdown with no tangible book support. Management is selling into the strength.

**Why I would not put this on:** the accounting is pristine (M-score -2.87, accruals -8.6%, F-score 9), the cash backs every dollar of earnings, receivables are *improving*, gross margins are *expanding*, leverage is 1.9x, and beta is 0.61 with a defensive non-elective demand base. The stock has already had two -7% to -10% air pockets (Jul 2025, Oct 2025) and both were bought. The bull case — CON-protected capacity, 5.6% discharge growth, 3.9% pricing, deleveraging, buyback, defensive demand — is currently *winning on the data*.

### 7. Contrarian Rating: **NO EDGE — Reject Short Thesis**

There is no forensic short here. The bear case is a legitimate *valuation-and-regulatory* debate that the fundamentals are presently winning. A short at $121.71 with the stock at highs post-raise, improving margins, and clean accounting is a bet on a CMS ruling and a sentiment reversal, not on a business deteriorating. That is macro/event risk, not analytical edge.

**What would flip me to "Short":** (1) two consecutive quarters of *same-store* discharge growth below 2%; (2) gross margin turning down YoY (currently the single best refutation of the bear case); (3) FY2027 IRF PPS final rule with a net update below ~2% or any site-neutral expansion for IRF; (4) DSO rising above 45 days or receivables growth outpacing revenue for two quarters; (5) Q3/Q4 2026 EPS implying the Q1 2026 margin was indeed non-recurring and guidance must be cut.

**Honest disclosure of gaps:** 10-K risk factors were not retrievable (no 10-K available via the filing tool); the $143.7M 2024 cash-flow/income-statement reconciliation gap is unexplained; insider buy/sell *direction* is unverified; and valid post-acute peer multiples were unavailable. None of these gaps change the rating, but the first two should be closed before any position.
