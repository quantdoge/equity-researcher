# EHC — Encompass Health Corporation
## Non-Financial Risk Assessment

**Snapshot:** Largest U.S. owner/operator of inpatient rehabilitation hospitals (IRFs). ~168–170 hospitals, 38 states, 29,699 employees, market cap ~$12.0B, price $121.71, beta 0.61. Note: the vendor profile still lists Home Health & Hospice — that business was spun off as Enhabit (July 2022); EHC today is effectively a pure-play IRF operator, which concentrates regulatory risk in a single Medicare payment system.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate (positive tilt)**

**Evidence for:**
- **CEO/Chair separation:** Greg D. Carmichael has served as *independent chairman* since Jan 2020; Mark J. Tarr is President/CEO and a director but not chair. This is the single most important structural check and it is in place.
- **Board size/structure:** 10-member board, all 10 nominees elected at the May 7, 2026 annual meeting; PwC ratified as independent auditor; recent refresh (Kevin O'Connor appointment) shows ongoing renewal.
- **Shareholder alignment:** Say-on-pay passed with **92.5% support** (May 2026) — a strong result with no visible proxy fight or activist campaign.
- No SEC enforcement actions returned in EDGAR full-text search; no restatement, no auditor change disclosed.

**Evidence against / watch items:**
- **CEO tenure ~10 years** (since Dec 2016) with no disclosed public succession plan — key-person and "status quo" risk, though the bench appears deep.
- **One-way insider selling** (see §3) without corresponding purchases is the main governance soft spot.
- Legacy overhang: the HealthSouth accounting fraud era (renamed 2018) is long remediated, but it means the compliance function gets no benefit of the doubt from regulators or short-sellers — schema matters if a new billing issue surfaces.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — **the dominant risk for this name**

| Item | Status | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **DOJ/OIG False Claims Act — $48M settlement** (IRF qualification) | Resolved (2019) | Resolved three *qui tam* suits alleging EHC gave Medicare inaccurate information to keep facilities qualified as IRFs and obtain higher reimbursement. Directly strikes at the core revenue mechanic (the 60% rule / intensity-of-therapy criteria). Establishes a template for future relators. |
| **FY2027 IRF PPS final rule** (finalized July 30, 2026) | Final | Rate update ~**2.4%** (market basket 3.2% less 0.8% productivity); ~$355M aggregate industry increase. CMS tightened **"all therapies initiated within 36 hours"** and mandated the initial interdisciplinary team meeting by **day 4** — operationally demanding and auditable. |
| **Review Choice Demonstration (RCD)** | Active | CMS' FY27 rule explicitly aligns *enforcement* with the Center for Program Integrity RCD (pre-claim review, denial + recoupment rights). EHC's core states (AL HQ, TX, PA, FL, CA) are RCD-relevant. Industry (AMRPA) petitioned CMS to sunset it in July 2025 and **lost** — the RCD is being hardened, not retired. |
| **Site-neutral payment** | Legislative/policy risk | CBO scored a full site-neutral policy at ~$157B deficit reduction over 10 years. This is the structural bear case on IRF economics and would compress the exact premium EHC earns versus hospital-based units. |
| **Case-mix modernization RFI** | Pending | CMS is exploring a PDPM-style redesign of IRF case-mix classification. Medium-term reweighting risk; unknown direction, nonzero magnitude. |
| **ERISA class action** (filed Mar 30, 2026, Miller Shah) | Active | 401(k) plan (~27,000 participants) alleged excessive fees / imprudent investment. Low direct financial materiality; governance/fiduciary signal. |
| **SEC/DOJ current investigations** | None found | EDGAR FTS clean; no open enforcement. |

**Net:** no acute enforcement event, but EHC operates inside the most audit-intensive post-acute payment corridor, with a documented FCA history and a demonstration program explicitly designed to deny claims in its home markets.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Revenue-integrity / documentation risk (highest severity).** IRF reimbursement depends on documented medical necessity, therapy intensity, and the 60% rule. The $48M FCA settlement, the RCD pre-claim review regime, and CMS' tightened 36-hour therapy/IDT timing rules all attack the same control point. A documentation failure is not a tail event here — it is a recurring, quantifiable leakage risk.
2. **Quality-of-care and clinical-outcome concentration.** Medicare data show EHC owned **34 of the 41 IRFs rated as having statistically significantly worse potentially preventable readmissions**, and 28 of 87 worse on in-stay readmissions, despite representing only ~1 in 7 facilities nationally. Documented serious incidents include a fatal carbon-monoxide leak (Huntington, WV), a death after a bed-exit alarm was disabled (Jackson, TN), and a fatal wrong-drug error among 26 medication errors in six months (Sioux Falls, SD). Because CMS does not fine or star-rate rehab hospitals the way it does nursing homes, the current exposure is regulatory-latency risk: the gap is publicly documented and invites rulemaking.
3. **Labor supply and capacity execution.** ~30k employees; therapy/nursing shortages and wage inflation hit a fixed-rate business directly. Simultaneously EHC is executing one of the sector's most aggressive builds (6–10 hospitals and 80–120 beds annually, plus new small-format and replacement facilities — Bangor ME, Lower Providence PA, Montgomery County TX). Rapid de novo openings are when staffing, licensure, and clinical-protocol failures cluster.

*Adjacent:* no EHC-specific breach disclosed, but a HIPAA-covered entity of this size carries standing cyber exposure (sector ransomware, vendor/clearinghouse contagion).

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Elevated**

- The **July 15, 2025 KFF Health News / New York Times "Broken Rehab" investigation** is the defining reputational event. It is national, co-published with the NYT, built on named deceased patients, and frames EHC as the *dominant* for-profit consolidator of a lightly-policed niche. That framing ("one company, one third of admissions, the worst outcome ratings") is the exact narrative that attracts plaintiff firms, congressional staff, and reimbursement-focused journalists.
- **Response quality is a risk in itself:** general counsel Patrick Darby dismissed Medicare's readmission ratings as "a crude scoring measure" and violations as "rare occurrences." A defensive posture from legal — rather than a clinical-quality narrative from the C-suite — tends to extend the story's life.
- **Countervailing positives (real, and material):** Newsweek "America's Most Awarded Leader in Inpatient Rehabilitation" **seventh consecutive year** (Oct 2026); named to Modern Healthcare's Best Places to Work 2026; consistently better-than-national "discharge to community" performance (79 top-rated facilities) — which is arguably the more outcome-relevant metric.
- **ESG/consumer sentiment:** no boycott-level consumer exposure (patients are referred, not self-selecting), so reputational damage transmits through regulators, referral sources, and litigation rather than customers — which makes it slower-burning but more durable.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

**Rationale:** Governance is genuinely sound (independent chair, 92.5% say-on-pay, no enforcement history, clean auditor). Financial reporting/integrity risk is low — no fraud indicators, no restatements, no whistleblower disclosures currently public. What keeps this out of "Low" is a *structural, not episodic* regulatory exposure: a single-payer-dominated revenue model, a prior FCA settlement on the exact billing mechanic that generates revenue, an actively hardening audit demonstration in its home states, a below-cost-trend rate update, and a live site-neutral legislative threat. Add a documented quality-of-care pattern now in the national press, and the risk is better characterized as **Moderate with a High-risk regulatory sub-score**.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Verify the 10b5-1 status of the CEO's 2026 sales.** ~$21.7M sold in August 2026 at the highs plus 150k shares in February, with zero insider purchases, is the single most checkable item on this list. If the August sale was *not* under a pre-existing plan, escalate to High. (I could not confirm the Rule 10b5-1 checkbox from the filing footnotes — data gap.)
2. **Confirm whether a Corporate Integrity Agreement (or successor compliance obligation) from the 2019 FCA settlement remains in force**, and whether RCD denial rates at EHC facilities are trending worse than the demonstration average. Both are leading indicators of the next enforcement cycle.
3. **Quality-metric deterioration is now public and quantified.** Track Care Compare readmission ratings and any CMS/state survey "immediate jeopardy" citations by facility. A single additional NYT-tier story, or a CMS decision to extend nursing-home-style enforcement to IRFs, is a direct multiple-compression event.
4. **ERISA suit — check whether the 401(k) plan holds EHC company stock.** If it does, this converts from a fee case into a stock-drop case with a much larger claimed loss, and it implicates the fiduciary committee's monitoring record.
5. **Reimbursement policy calendar:** FY2028 IRF PPS proposed rule (spring 2027) and any site-neutral markup. Both are binary, dateable catalysts against a ~$12B cap and a pure-play IRF revenue base.
