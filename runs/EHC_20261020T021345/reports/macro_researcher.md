## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: TRANSITIONAL — late-cycle with a hawkish skew. Risk-on in equities, risk-off beginning in rates and credit.**

The defining feature of this cycle is a **policy divergence**: the Fed delivered ~170bp of easing between September 2024 and mid-2026 (EFFR 5.33% → 3.63%), but the long end never cooperated. The 10Y has risen **~74bp since late May 2026** (4.45% → 5.09%), and the September CPI print re-accelerated to **3.72%** from a 3.30% July trough. The result is a **bear steepener**: the market has stopped pricing cuts (2Y at 4.65% sits *above* the 3.75% policy rate) while charging a rising term premium at the 30Y (5.37%).

Growth is fine — real GDP +2.2% YoY in Q2'26, unemployment 4.2% — so this is **not a recession signal**. The 2s10s is positively sloped (+34bp, not inverted). This is an **inflation-and-fiscal-dominance regime, not a growth scare.** The tell: HY OAS has widened **71bp in seven weeks** (2.60% → 3.31%) even as equities print highs — early-stage credit vigilance, though absolute spreads remain tight.

The policy rate read deserves a flag: EFFR printed **3.75% in September versus a 3.63–3.64% plateau from January through August 2026**. Consistent with a 25bp tightening move or range technicals — either way, **the easing cycle has stalled.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**A. Interest rates — NEGATIVE (the most quantifiable channel).**
EHC carries **$2.84bn gross / $2.73bn net debt** on a $14.74bn EV; Debt/EBITDA is only **1.87x** and interest coverage **8.89x** — the balance sheet is not stressed. But two channels bite:
- **Valuation duration.** At EV/EBITDA 10.07x on a ~6.77% WACC, a 50bp WACC increase compresses fair value ~7%. EHC's 2026 recovery (from $92.85 on 30 Jan to $129.35 on 16 Oct, +39%) occurred squarely inside the easing window; when the 10Y broke 5.0% in October, the stock stalled and fell **-4.82% on 19 Oct** on 2.27m shares (2x normal volume). **No company-specific news explains that move** — the most plausible driver is rate-driven de-rating plus pre-earnings de-risking ahead of Q3 on 28 Oct.
- **Refinancing.** At $2.84bn, each 100bp on refinanced notes is ~$28m pretax ≈ **$0.28/sh, ~4.6% of $6.13 EPS**. Manageable, but it absorbs roughly two years of ordinary EPS growth if the whole stack reprices at 5%+.

**B. Federal reimbursement — NEGATIVE, and the dominant macro lever (bigger than the Fed).**
EHC's revenue is overwhelmingly Medicare/Medicaid. The **FY2027 IRF PPS final rule (issued 30 Jul 2026) sets the update at just +2.3%** (3.2% market basket less 0.9pp productivity). Against **3.72% CPI**, that is a **~1.4pp real rate cut** on the core business. EHC must close the gap with discharge growth, acuity mix and cost control — its usual playbook, but the cushion is thinner than in the 2.8–3.2% update years. Two forward risks from the same rule: a revised 36-hour rule, and CMS's **RFI on payment reform** — the seed of a future site-neutral or unified post-acute payment model, which is the single largest long-term threat to the IRF economics.

**C. Labor inflation — NEGATIVE but decelerating.**
~42,300 employees; salaries and benefits are the largest cost line. With unemployment at 4.2% and CPI at 3.7%, wage pressure persists (the 3.2% market basket is essentially a wage index). Because EHC's price is administratively set at +2.3%, **this is a direct margin squeeze** — partially offset by its documented productivity and turnover improvements.

**D. Growth and demand — POSITIVE / defensive.**
IRF demand is demographic (65+ population growth) and non-discretionary post-acute recovery, not GDP-cyclical. Beta of **0.60** confirms it. A 2.2% growth, 4.2%-unemployment economy supports commercial payer volumes and limits bad debt. If growth cracks, EHC's volumes are among the most insulated in healthcare.

**E. Currency and trade — NEUTRAL (a genuine differentiator).**
Revenue is **~100% domestic** (42 states plus Puerto Rico). There is effectively **zero FX translation risk** — a real advantage versus MedTech/pharma peers navigating a firming dollar. The +2.9% dollar rally *helps* at the margin by cheapening imported medical equipment. The offsetting exposure is **tariffs on construction materials** (steel, HVAC, electrical) against a **$799m annual capex program** for de novo hospitals — a modest drag on development ROIC, not on the P&L.

**F. Medicaid — NEGATIVE, second-order.**
Medicaid eligibility restrictions and reduced federal funding taking effect in October 2026 raise uncompensated care and bad-debt risk, concentrated in the Home Health & Hospice segment (Southeast/Texas).

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL (unfavourable skew into 2027)**

The offsets are real and unusually strong: **1.87x leverage, 8.89x interest coverage, $413m FCF, 14.5% ROIC, ~100% domestic revenue, 0.60 beta, and demographic demand**. EHC is structurally one of the least macro-fragile names in healthcare.

But three forces all point the same direction: (i) a **~1.4pp real reimbursement cut** from the FY2027 rule, (ii) a **74bp rise in the 10Y** compressing a 10x EBITDA multiple, and (iii) **wage inflation** that the administratively-set price cannot pass through. The macro is not hostile — it is *grindingly* unhelpful. The company must earn its return through volume growth and operating leverage, not through multiple expansion or pricing.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BEAR (hawkish regime break)**

Consensus assumes inflation drifts back to target and the Fed resumes cutting. **The surprise is the opposite:** CPI holds above 3.5% through H1'27 on tariffs and services stickiness, the Fed **hikes twice**, and the 10Y reaches **5.75%** with HY OAS at 4.5%. Simultaneously, FY2028 IRF update lands near 2.0% and Medicaid uncompensated care spikes.

Mechanically: WACC 6.77% → ~7.6%, compressing EHC from **10.1x to 8.5x EV/EBITDA** on ~$1.55bn 2027E EBITDA (~$13.2bn EV, less $2.7bn net debt) → equity **~$10.5bn, ~$106/share, roughly -13%**, with a dividend-yield cushion of only 0.69% offering little support. Note the stock already fell **25% in four months** (Oct'25–Jan'26) in a comparable de-rating episode — this is a demonstrated, not hypothetical, risk.

**The bull mirror image:** CPI returns to 2.5%, the Fed cuts to 3.0%, the 10Y falls to 4.3%, and EHC re-rates to 11.5x → **~$159/share (+31%)**. Given equities are at highs with credit spreads already widening, the asymmetry favours hedging the bear case.
