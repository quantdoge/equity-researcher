# SECTOR BRIEF — CoreWeave (CRWV)
### AI "Neocloud" / GPU Cloud Infrastructure | Data as of 16 Oct 2026 (CRWV $61.39, mkt cap $33.9B)

---

## 1. Sector Overview

CoreWeave competes in the **neocloud** segment — purpose-built GPU/AI cloud infrastructure sold as rentable compute, distinct from general-purpose hyperscalers (AWS/Azure/GCP) and from GPU hardware vendors. The segment is small but compounding at extraordinary rates: neocloud industry revenue exceeded **$25B in 2025**, with Q4-2025 alone at **$9B (+223% YoY)**, and is projected at **~$20B+ in 2026** with sell-side forecasts approaching **$400B by 2031** (Synergy Research). Growth is a direct derivative of hyperscaler capex — **~$630B planned for 2026, +62% YoY**, with ~75% AI-related. The sector is **early-cycle / hyper-growth** but is now entering a **credit-gated shakeout phase**: after two years of unrestricted debt-funded capacity build, capital markets re-priced AI-infrastructure credit in October 2026, splitting winners (owned power, contracted revenue, investment-grade funding) from losers (lease-heavy, floating-rate, concentrated customers). CoreWeave sits at the centre of that fault line despite being the segment's revenue leader (~20% of 2025 neocloud revenue).

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Contract backlog has become the sector's earnings annuity.** CRWV reportedly holds **>$100B in revenue backlog** (up from ~$55B mid-2026) anchored by Microsoft, a **~$14B Meta deal**, and a large OpenAI contract. Multi-year take-or-pay structures convert a volatile spot-rental business into quasi-contracted cash flow and underwrite debt capacity.
- **GPU rental pricing is firming, not deflating — for now.** H100 one-year contract rates rose **~40% from $1.70/hr (Oct-25) to ~$2.35/hr (Mar-26)**, reflecting genuine compute scarcity. Simultaneously, **30–50% of planned 2026 AI data-centre capacity may slip to 2028** on electrical/interconnection constraints — artificially tightening supply and supporting price.
- **Power and land are the binding constraint, and that protects incumbents.** AI's share of global data-centre power rises from **~14% today to ~27% by 2027**. Firms with energised capacity and signed interconnection agreements hold a scarce, non-replicable asset for 3–5 years — a genuine barrier to entry for new neoclouds.

---

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Depreciation is arriving faster than contracts amortise.** CRWV's D&A reached **$1.39B in Q2-26 = 54% of revenue**, and **$2.45B / 48% of revenue** for FY25. Sector TCO analyses (McKinsey) show GPU rental pricing declining **~50% over five years**, with **post-depreciation gross margins of only 14–16%** — implying today's 66–72% reported gross margins are substantially a pre-D&A artefact.
- **AI-infrastructure credit is repricing violently.** CRWV's bonds traded to **~15% yields**, CDS widened sharply, and its **current ratio of 0.46** forces near-term refinancing. Press estimates put gross leverage at **~6.3x by year-end**, with TTM interest expense of **$1.87B (~25% of revenue and roughly doubling YoY)**. The stock fell **27.5% in one week** in mid-October before a 17% snap-back — a sector-wide de-rating, not a company-specific one.
- **Customer concentration and obsolescence risk are structural.** Neocloud demand is concentrated in a handful of model developers/hyperscalers who are simultaneously building owned capacity and can renegotiate. Combined with 5-year asset lives against 2–3 year contract terms, the sector bears residual value risk on every GPU generation.

## 5. CRWV's Competitive Position: **AVERAGE (structurally advantaged commercially, structurally disadvantaged financially)**

**Strengths**
- Largest, most proven pure-play GPU cloud; ~20% of 2025 neocloud industry revenue; revenue per employee ~$3.5M (2,189 staff) — extreme operating leverage.
- Best-in-class pure-play operating discipline: **+$2.47B EBITDA (48% margin FY25)**, **57% EBITDA margin in Q2-26**, and the only cohort member near operating breakeven.
- Nvidia holds **~10%** and is a de facto strategic backer — preferential GPU allocation is a durable supply-side edge; backlog >$100B provides multi-year visibility.

**Weaknesses**
- **It does not own most of its real estate or power** (unlike IREN); it is a lessee of others' campuses (e.g. APLD's Polaris Forge) — thinner asset backing and landlord/power-price exposure at renewal.
- **Balance sheet is the tightest in the cohort**: 6.3x gross leverage, 15% marginal bond cost, 0.46 current ratio, $1.87B TTM interest. Its cost of capital, not its product, now sets the growth ceiling.
- **Gross margin erosion of ~700bps in three quarters** (73.0% Q3-25 → 65.9% Q2-26) signals the mix is shifting toward lower-margin, larger, more price-sensitive contracts — normal for scale, but it undermines the "premium platform" narrative.
- Insider selling **>$5B in 12 months** ($3.3B by founders since IPO) is a governance/governance-perception drag with equity near all-time lows (-57% from the Oct-25 high of $143).

**Net:** CRWV is the **commercial leader of a structurally attractive but financially fragile sub-sector**, and its equity is behaving as the sector's high-beta credit proxy (a 7.3 beta is unreliable but directionally revealing). Its competitive position is strong on demand and weaker on durability: it wins the compute contract but finances it at the worst cost of capital in the group.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL** (demand: Attractive | financing conditions: Unattractive)

**Rationale.** The demand side of the neocloud sector is as attractive as anything in industrials/tech: +200%+ industry growth, firming GPU rental pricing, a hard power bottleneck, and trillion-dollar multi-year capex commitments from creditworthy buyers ($7.6T of AI capex 2026–31 per Goldman). But the sector's **returns on invested capital, not its growth, are now the question**, and the marginal cost of funding that growth has roughly doubled in a quarter. With D&A at ~50% of revenue, interest at ~25%, and post-depreciation gross margins plausibly in the mid-teens, the sector earns its cost of capital only if utilisation stays at ~100% through the depreciation cycle — an assumption the October 2026 credit repricing has begun to challenge. We would upgrade to **Attractive** on evidence of (a) investment-grade financing access for tier-1 neoclouds, (b) H100/H200 rental pricing holding >$2.00/hr, or (c) CRWV converting backlog without further gross-margin slippage. We would downgrade to **Unattractive** on a failed refinancing, an order cancellation at a top-2 customer, or GPU rental rates breaking the Oct-2025 lows.
