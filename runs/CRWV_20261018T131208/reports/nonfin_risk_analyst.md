# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — CoreWeave, Inc. (NASDAQ: CRWV)

**Price $61.39 | Market cap ~$33.9B | IPO 3/28/2025 at $40 | Down ~67% from June-2025 high (~$187) | Beta 7.32 | 2,189 employees**

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

| Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **CEO/Chair duality** | Michael Intrator is co-founder, **Chairman, CEO and President** — no separation of roles. His own proxy language defends the combination on "CoreWeave-specific experience" grounds. |
| **Dual-class control** | As of 7/31/2026: 458.9M Class A vs. 92.7M **super-voting Class B** (converts 1:1 to Class A on transfer). Class B sits with founders/insiders, entrenching control well beyond economic ownership. |
| **Founder pedigree** | Intrator, Brannin McBee and Brian Venturo are **former natural-gas futures traders** (Hudson Ridge Asset Management), pivoting crypto-mining → AI cloud. Thin public-company and AI-infrastructure governance track record. |
| **Board quality (positive)** | Genuine independents exist: **Glenn Hutchins** (Lead Independent Director, chairs Compensation *and* Nominating/Governance), **Meg Whitman**, **Karen Boone** (Audit Committee chair), **Jack Cogen**. This is better than the peer neocloud cohort. |
| **Capital allocation** | $9B **Core Scientific merger terminated 10/30/2025** after CORZ shareholders rejected it; proxy advisers called the price inadequate. CoreWeave lost the owned power/datacenter pipeline it was strategically dependent on — then had to re-contract third-party capacity (the root of the current litigation). |
| **Structural conflicts** | **Magnetar Financial** is simultaneously CoreWeave's earliest/largest backer and an active seller ($61.7M in Aug-2025 at $142–151, plus $94.4M and $25M tranches). **Nvidia** holds ~9% (~47M shares) *and* is sole GPU supplier *and* a customer — textbook circular-financing optics, now under DOJ antitrust scrutiny at the Nvidia level. |

**Verdict:** Board composition is adequate on paper, but control concentration, role combination, and the founder-controlled Class B make shareholder accountability structurally weak. Insiders can sell into strength while retaining voting control.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **ELEVATED AND RISING**

**Active litigation (confirmed):**
- **Securities class action**, D.N.J., filed **12 Jan 2026**; class period **3/28/2025 – 12/15/2025**; lead-plaintiff deadline 13 Mar 2026; multiple firms competing (Rosen, Berger Montague, Kessler Topaz, ZLK). Allegations: (i) overstated ability to meet demand; (ii) **materially understated reliance on a single third-party data-center developer**; (iii) foreseeable revenue impact. Trigger: 11/10/2025 guidance cut citing a "third-party data center developer behind schedule," then a **12/15/2025 WSJ report that the developer had flagged delays ~9 months earlier**. That documentary timeline is the most dangerous fact in the file.
- **Fresh investigation solicitations** (SueWallSt, 10/14/2026) following the bond rout — new claims likely.

**Enforcement:** EDGAR full-text search returns **no SEC enforcement action** naming CRWV. No known DOJ or SEC formal investigation of the company. *(Gap: absence of an action is not evidence of absence of an inquiry.)*

**Regulatory/political:**
- **27 states** advancing data-center bills (energy cost allocation, moratoriums). **New Jersey — CoreWeave's HQ state — clawed back $250M** of a $500M AI data-center tax credit program (Sept 2026).
- **Senate investigation** into data-center cost/benefit claims (Oct 2026); White House pressuring utilities on AI power costs. Data-center oversight is now a **bipartisan election-year issue**.
- **Energy/interconnection**: 850MW active, ~3.1GW contracted. Permitting and grid-queue slippage is a direct revenue-recognition risk given CoreWeave books revenue only on completed installation.
- **Nvidia DOJ antitrust probe** creates indirect read-through risk to supply terms.

**Interpretation:** 10b5-1 plans provide a legal defense, but the *pattern* is damning: plans were adopted at the top, and selling continued mechanically through a 67% drawdown. Founders retain voting control via Class B while converting economics to cash — the single clearest signal of misaligned incentives in this file. Notably, **Nvidia has not been reported as adding** to its ~47M-share stake.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Single-developer / single-supplier concentration in the build-out.** The company's ability to convert a **$66.8B (YE25) → $99.4B (Q1'26) → $104.2B (mid-2026) backlog** into revenue depends on a small number of third-party data-center developers and a **single GPU vendor (Nvidia) for essentially 100% of compute**. Revenue is recognized only on completed installation, so one developer's slip moves the P&L. This is precisely what already happened in Nov-2025 and is now the subject of securities litigation.
2. **Leverage and refinancing risk — now acute.** ~**$50B of debt**; unsecured 2032 notes trading at **~14.5–15% yield**; **CDS near 1,000bps**; FY2025 **interest expense $1,229M** against a **$1,167M net loss**. Add $2.6B convertibles and a $2.5B revolver. A refinancing window that closes converts a liquidity problem into a solvency one, and creditors are already described as "nervous."
3. **Key-person dependency and thin management bench.** A 2,189-person company executing a $100B+ backlog, run by a three-founder team with no prior at-scale infrastructure or public-company operating experience. Intrator is simultaneously Chair, CEO and President — no succession disclosure and no independent executive chair as a backstop.

*(Also material: physical/power concentration in a limited number of campuses; cybersecurity posture is asserted via the CrowdStrike partnership but no breach history or incident-response disclosure was retrievable — treated as an information gap, not a clean bill of health.)*

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

CoreWeave has become **the market's proxy for the AI-bubble and AI-debt debate** — a position no issuer wants:

- **Oct 13–15, 2026**: unsecured 2032 notes hit a record ~14.5–15% yield; stock fell **16% in one session** and **~14%** on the day Elon Musk replied "Yikes" to a viral post about the bond yield. Cramer: *"People are terrified."*
- **Bloomberg, Reuters, Motley Fool, GuruFocus** all running "neocloud economics / AI infrastructure spending" scrutiny pieces in a single week — narrative is compounding, not fading.
- **Kerrisdale Capital short report (Sept 2025)**: alleges **aggressive depreciation schedules that "grossly flatter GAAP financials,"** extreme customer concentration, and a **$10 fair-value estimate** (−90%+). The GAAP-to-adjusted gap is real and large: **FY25 adjusted EBITDA $3,093M (60% margin) vs. GAAP operating loss of $46M**. Whether or not Kerrisdale is right on fair value, the depreciation debate is now the lens through which every quarter will be read.
- **WSJ investigation (12/15/2025)** into data-center delivery failures — the rare case where reporting, not disclosure, drove the stock.
- **Political/ESG**: rising electricity bills, water use, and state moratoriums make data centers a local-approval lightning rod. Being HQ'd in a state that just **clawed back $250M** in AI tax credits is an unhelpful symbol.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH** (top of band; one notch below Critical)

**Rationale for not rating Critical:** no restatement, no SEC/DOJ formal action against the company, credible independent directors including a properly constituted audit committee, and a customer base genuinely diversifying beyond Microsoft (from **~67% of 2025 revenue** toward OpenAI/Meta backlog concentration). **Rationale for not rating Moderate:** a securities-fraud class action with a documentary paper trail, $2.3B of one-way insider selling, $50B of debt pricing at distressed yields, and CEO/Chair duality under a super-voting dual-class structure.

**What would move this to Critical:** an SEC formal order or subpoena disclosure; an auditor change, restatement, or depreciation-life revision; any covenant breach or failed refinancing; a second WSJ/DOJ-style disclosure showing management knew of delays earlier than represented; or insider selling continuing into the current price collapse without any offsetting purchase.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🚩 Disclosure-timing gap (highest severity).** WSJ reported the developer flagged delivery delays **~9 months** before the 11/10/2025 guidance cut. If discovery confirms internal awareness, this is a scienter case, not a nuisance suit. Request: board/Audit Committee investigation status, D&O reserve disclosures, and whether any 8-K was considered earlier. *(Deadline pressure: lead plaintiff already appointed, March 2026.)*
2. **🚩 $2.3B of insider sales with zero purchases**, including 10b5-1 plans adopted within days of the all-time high and a director sale of ~$106M at $108. Direct misalignment with public shareholders who bought the AI narrative.
3. **🚩 Control asymmetry.** Founder Class B super-voting stock + combined Chair/CEO/President means public holders cannot effect change even with 100% of the Class A vote — while insiders monetize.
4. **🚩 Credit market repricing as a leading indicator.** 15% unsecured yield and ~1,000bp CDS is the bond market pricing a materially higher probability of impairment than the equity at $61 implies. Bond/equity divergence is a classic escalation trigger.
5. **🚩 Circular financing web.** Nvidia equity stake + sole-supplier status + customer relationship, with Magnetar as both capital provider and largest seller. Demand quality and revenue durability are harder to verify than headline backlog suggests.
6. **🚩 Backlog quality is unverifiable.** $104B backlog against $5.1B FY25 revenue, with only ~36% expected to convert near-term and revenue recognized only on completed installation. Escalate for footnote-level review of cancellation/termination rights and counterparty credit.
