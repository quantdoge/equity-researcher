## Sector Brief — Bloom Energy (BE)
**Sector:** Industrials → Electrical Equipment & Parts (sub-segment: on-site / behind-the-meter power generation & fuel cells) | Price $206.77, mkt cap ~$61.1B, beta 3.64

### 1. Sector Overview
BE competes in distributed/on-site power generation — solid-oxide fuel cells displacing grid interconnection and gas turbines for data centres, hospitals and industrials. The demand driver has shifted decisively from "green energy" to **time-to-power**: US data-centre capacity is projected to grow from ~80 GW (2025) to ~150 GW by 2028 (Bloom's own 2026 Data Center Power Report — self-published, treat as directional), while heavy-duty gas turbine slots at GEV/Siemens/Mitsubishi are effectively sold out into 2029–2030. That supply-demand gap has repriced the entire electrical-equipment complex. The segment is **capacity-constrained, not demand-constrained**, and is in an early-to-mid-stage investment super-cycle rather than a mature replacement cycle. Barriers are high: 15+ years of SOFC stack IP, a multi-year field-installed reliability record, and now increasingly scarce factory capacity.

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Turbine/grid scarcity = structural pricing power.** With 3 vendors controlling ~85% of heavy-duty turbine slots and grid interconnection queues multi-year, fuel cells sell on speed-to-power at premium economics. BE's Q3'26 revenue +138% YoY ($1,236M) and the $2.65B / up-to-1 GW AEP agreement show it is converting scarcity into volume.
- **Margin inflection is real, not aspirational.** Gross margin 14.8% (FY23) → 27.5% (FY24) → 29.0% (FY25) → **33.8% (Q3'26)**; operating margin -15.7% → 3.6% → **18.2%**. Operating leverage is arriving as manufacturing absorbs fixed cost — the core bull case.
- **Electrification/AI capex is sector-wide, not single-name.** GEV carries a record $197B backlog and raised guidance; Vertiv posted Q3'26 revenue +61% with orders up >100%. The whole power chain is being pulled, which de-risks BE's backlog conversion versus a single-customer story.

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Customer concentration in AI/neocloud capex.** BE traded ±15–25% on Oracle and CoreWeave newsflow alone in Oct–Nov 2026, and fell 26.6% in October — the equity is now a leveraged proxy on hyperscaler/neocloud spending decisions, not on fuel-cell fundamentals.
- **Input/feedstock exposure.** SOFC systems run primarily on natural gas, so economics are tied to gas spreads vs. grid tariffs, plus rising political scrutiny of gas-fired generation in some states (Texas permitting politics flagged as a risk).
- **Valuation and volatility, not demand, are the binding constraint.** At ~16x TTM revenue (~12x annualised Q3'26 run-rate) and ~61x annualised Q3 net income, with beta 3.64 and a 55% drawdown from the June 2026 peak ($345.85 → $156), the sector's capital is priced for flawless execution. Industry capacity additions (BE's own fab expansion, plus PLUG/FCEL restarts) risk compressing pricing by 2028.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (within a structurally attractive niche)**
- **Scale and profitability gap is decisive:** 33.8% gross / 18.2% operating margin vs PLUG (-34% / -96%) and FCEL (-17% / -77%). BE is the only scaled SOFC platform with a viable unit economic model.
- **Outgrowing every comparable:** +138% quarterly growth vs peer set's +9% to +61%. On revenue growth and margin trajectory combined, BE screens #1 of six.
- **Caveats:** BE remains **below** the electrical-equipment margin leaders (VRT 36% GM/18.5% OM on far larger revenue) and is far more cyclical/concentrated than GEV. Its advantage is speed-to-power and IP, not cost position — and it is a single-technology, largely single-facility manufacturer.

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE** (with a valuation caveat)
Demand visibility to 2028+ from AI power scarcity, high entry barriers, and a genuine margin inflection justify an Attractive sector call — this is an early-cycle, capacity-constrained build-out, not a mature market. The distinction that matters for BE: the *sector* is attractive, but the *fuel-cell pure-play sub-segment* is bifurcating, with capital concentrating in the one scaled winner (BE) and away from sub-scale peers. The risk is priced: at ~16x TTM sales with beta 3.6 and 55% peak-to-trough drawdowns, sector attractiveness is already substantially embedded in BE's multiple.
