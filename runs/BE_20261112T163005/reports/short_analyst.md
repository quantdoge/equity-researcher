# Bloom Energy (BE) — Forensic Short-Seller Workup

**Price $206.77 | Market cap $61.1B | Beta 3.64 | Net debt $295M | Price ~12.5x the Apr-2025 low of $16.60**

**Headline verdict up front: this is not a fraud short. The accounting is clean; the valuation is not.** Beneish and accruals both clear the company. The bear case is an expectations/cash-conversion short, not a Hindenburg-style forensic short — and that materially lowers the conviction of any short here.

---

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment — **Medium** (was Low in FY25; improving, with one serious cash caveat)

The FY25 mechanical score of **0/100** is an artifact: the model divides by a negative net income. Rebuilt on TTM data, quality is *mediocre-to-good*, not fraudulent:

| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | 9M26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 14.8% | 27.5% | 29.0% | 33.4%* |
| Net income ($M) | -302 | -29 | -88 | **+517** |
| Operating CF ($M) | -373 | +92 | +114 | +273 |
| FCF ($M) | -456 | +33 | +57 | **+42** |
| **CFO / NI** | n/m | n/m | n/m | **53%** |
| **FCF / NI** | n/m | n/m | n/m | **8%** |

*\*Q3-26 GM 33.8%. Nine-month revenue $3.05B, up 130%+.*

Margin expansion is real and sequential (20.1% → 12.4% → 14.8% → 27.5% → 29.0% → 33.8%), which is the signature of genuine manufacturing leverage, not accounting. I credit that.

But the quality problem is unambiguous: **Bloom is earning record GAAP profits and converting almost none of it to cash.** Q3-26 alone: net income **+$250M**, operating cash flow **–$27M**, FCF **–$181M**. Nine-month FCF of $42M against $517M of net income is an **8% conversion rate**. Against my >100% quality bar, that is a fail.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

1. **Contract assets exploded +343% YoY — $178.9M → $792.8M** while revenue grew 138%. Revenue is being recognised *ahead of the unconditional right to bill*. This is the single most important line item in the file, and the standard receivables screen **misses it entirely** because it only reads "net receivables" (which grew just 14.5% in 2025). AR + contract assets now total ~$1.43B against $3.83B TTM revenue (~136 days). Contract assets are exactly the bucket that gets written down when a customer renegotiates scope or a project slips.
2. **Customer concentration: one customer ≈73% of H1-2026 revenue** (per the 10-Q summary), described as falling below ~40% later in the year. Either way, in a business with $1.43B of receivables/contract assets, a single counterparty's payment behaviour or credit event is a company-level risk. The filing itself flags "high customer and credit concentration."
3. **Almost all FY25 cash arrived in one quarter.** Q4-25 OCF was **+$421M** with a **+$234M working-capital release**; the first nine months of 2025 were **–$307M**. Annual cash generation is a Q4 working-capital unwind, not a steady drip.
4. **Working capital is absorbing the growth.** 9M26 WC drag of **–$435M** (inventory 643→928M; contract assets +$614M). Growth is being financed by the balance sheet, not by customers paying.
5. **Persistent dilution: +70% basic shares since 2021** (173.4M → 294.8M), diluted 324.3M (+10% overhang). Q3-26 stock comp of $57M is ~23% of net income — earnings partly paid in paper. Convertible debt of $2.78B sits on top.
6. **Altman Z ≈ 1.48 on my rebuild** (FY25 reported 0.148, now stale) — still grey/distress, driven by a **$3.47B accumulated deficit** and $4.6B of liabilities against $1.96B equity. Cash of $2.49B keeps liquidity risk low, but the balance sheet is levered, not fortress-like.
7. **Inventory days structurally higher**: 93 (2022) → 161 (2023) → 186 (2024) → 163 (2025) → ~103 annualised (Q3-26). The direction is now *improving*, so I will not press this one.
8. **Feed/data integrity gaps**: FY25 and Q2-26 cash-flow rows report stock-based comp as **zero** (actual was $82M in FY24 and $57M in Q3-26); FY25 operating income ($72.8M) and EBIT (–$30.5M) are mutually inconsistent. Goodwill of $215M appears in Q2-26 and vanishes in Q3-26. I am flagging these as data artifacts, not accusations — but it means the FY25 cash bridge in my dataset is incomplete.

## 3. Beneish M-Score and Accruals Analysis

- **Beneish M-Score: –2.523** → "Low risk." Below every threshold. Sub-indices corroborate: DSRI 0.834 (receivables *lagging* sales — no channel-stuffing signal), GMI 0.946 (margins improving), TATA –0.046 (income-backed by cash), SGAI 1.027. SGI of 1.373 correctly flags the growth, but nothing else does.
- **Accruals ratio: –4.6% in FY25** (negative accruals = higher quality), and –4.6% in FY24, +2.9% in FY23, –5.6% in FY22. Consistently unremarkable.
- **Verdict: exonerated on manipulation models.** The bear cannot argue earnings are manufactured — not while margins expand sequentially and accruals run negative. My contract-asset concern is a *timing/counterparty* risk, not evidence of manipulation. Historically Bloom's AR did outgrow revenue in 2023 (+28.3% vs +11.2%) and 2024 (+25.9% vs +10.5%), which is why this company deserves a permanent receivables discount — but that pattern reversed in 2025.
- **Piotroski F-Score: 4/9 (weak)** — fails on positive ROA, ROA improvement, leverage, dilution, and asset turnover.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

Bloom's product is real and its moat right now is **speed-to-power, not cost**. That is the most fragile kind of moat. Hyperscalers bought Bloom because gas turbine slots (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy) are sold out into 2028-29 and grid interconnection queues run 3-5 years. As turbine capacity expands and interconnection reforms land, the scarcity premium that justifies Bloom's LCOE premium erodes.

The demand is also **policy-levered**. Fuel cells qualified for the 30% investment tax credit (48E) entering 2026 — a major reason JPMorgan upgraded the stock. That same credit is time-limited post-OBBBA (construction-start deadlines in 2026, placed-in-service termination after 2027). Much of the 130–165% growth is therefore **credit-driven pull-forward** into 2026-27, which structurally raises the probability of a 2028 air pocket. If Bloom has to eat ~30 points of credit to hold volume, a 34% gross margin becomes a mid-single-digit one. That is the credible margin-erosion path — and it is a *policy* variable Bloom does not control.

I will not overclaim the statutory detail; the directional risk (subsidy cliff coinciding with the peak of the current order cycle) is the point.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — **the bear case is valuation, not fraud**

The bull case here is genuinely strong, and I will state it plainly: revenue is accelerating (Q1 +130%, Q2 +166%, Q3 +138%), gross margin is up ~670bps YoY, GAAP profitability has arrived, guidance was raised to $4.4–4.6B, net debt is only $295M, and the sector tailwind (AI data-centre power) is the strongest in a decade. Bears have been wrong, repeatedly and expensively, on this name.

The bear case is arithmetic, not forensics:

1. **$61.1B market cap on ~$4.5B of 2026 revenue = 13.6x sales and ~75x 2026 earnings**, for a company with a 3.64 beta. Consensus needs **$7.3B (2027), $10.7B (2028), $13.2B (2029), $15.6B (2030)** — i.e. ~50% compound growth for four more years — just to arrive at 15x 2030 EPS in 2030. The market is paying today for flawless execution through the decade. Even on *delivered* consensus the stock only earns a market return at best; on any shortfall, the multiple compresses violently against EPS that is already discounted.
2. **The 8% FCF conversion is the tell.** Accelerating revenue, expanding margins, and *negative* quarterly operating cash flow cannot coexist for many more quarters without either capex/working-capital funding (more converts, more dilution) or a growth slowdown that reveals the cash hole. Q3-26: +$250M net income, –$181M FCF.
3. **Single-customer 73% (H1-26) concentration** in a company with $1.43B of receivables and contract assets. One counterparty's capex pause — and hyperscaler capex *always* pauses — converts a growth story into a receivables problem.
4. **The subsidy cliff meets the order peak in 2028.** Pull-forward demand + expiring credit + turbine supply normalising = a margin and volume air pocket the consensus 2028-30 revenue line does not contain.
5. **Insiders are selling at $277–$298** while the sell-side raises targets.

What would break the bear case: FCF conversion above 80% for two consecutive quarters, contract assets growing slower than revenue, and a disclosed second/third customer above 10%. Watch those three, not the narrative.

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **Short — but sized small and defined-risk**

**Not "Strong Short."** Two of my three strongest forensic tools (Beneish –2.52; accruals –4.6%) *clear* this company, and momentum plus a genuine demand inflection is the most dangerous environment in which to be short. The fraud/manipulation thesis is **rejected**: gross margins expanding 670bps on scale, negative accruals, and receivables now lagging sales are the opposite of the accounting signature I look for.

The defensible bear case is that **BE is a $61B bet on four consecutive years of ~50% growth, with 73% customer concentration, an 8% FCF conversion rate, a policy cliff in 2028, and insiders distributing at $277–$298** — against a 3.64 beta. That is a valuation-and-cash-conversion short with a large but *timing-uncertain* payoff. I'd express it with long-dated put spreads rather than a naked short, cap it at a small position size, and treat a run above $300 or a sub-40% concentration disclosure with 80%+ FCF conversion as a hard stop-out.
