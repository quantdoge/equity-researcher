## 1. Governance Quality Rating: WEAK

**Structural concentration of power**
- **Founder–Chairman–CEO duality**: KR Sridhar has held all three roles for ~25 years. No independent chair.
- **Dual-class control**: Class B common carries **ten votes per share** vs. one for Class A, and Sridhar holds **irrevocable proxies** over other holders' Class B shares (per prior proxy disclosures), giving management effective voting control despite holding ~4.86M shares (~1.6% of 294.8M shares out). Minority holders cannot meaningfully contest board action.

**Genuine strengths (why not worse)**
- Audit, Compensation and Nominating committees are **all-independent**; a named **Lead Independent Director** (Jim Snabe) and an Audit Committee Financial Expert exist.
- Board includes credible independents (John Chambers, Jeff Immelt, Michael Boskin, Gary Pinkus). Boardroom Alpha grades the board **B‑**.
- CEO 2025 total comp was modest at **$3.5M** (only ~27% salary, no new equity grant in 2025), which limits the "pay without performance" critique.

**Disqualifying evidence — financial-reporting governance**
- **CFO churn: four finance chiefs since the start of 2024**, including **~11 months with no permanent CFO** between Dan Berenbaum's abrupt departure (effective May 1, 2025, after ~one year) and **Simon Edwards (effective April 13, 2026)**. That vacuum overlaps precisely with the alleged misstatement period and the build-out of the Brookfield JV financing structure.
- **Revenue-recognition precedent**: a 2020 restatement (8-K, Feb 12, 2020) reversed **$165–180M** of revenue after the audit committee and PwC determined Managed Services Agreements should have been treated as **financings, not sales**. The current controversy again centers on revenue recognized through financing partners rather than end users.
- **10-Q/A amendment** filed for Q2 2026 to correct transposed "three months/six months" figures — clerical, but evidence of strained disclosure controls at a company guiding to $4.4–4.6B revenue.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: ELEVATED

- **No SEC enforcement action or DOJ matter identified** in EDGAR full-text search for BE — a genuine clean result on formal enforcement.
- **Securities class action active** (Rosen, Levi & Korsinsky, Kessler Topaz, Kaplan Fox, Pomerantz, et al.): class period **Feb 27, 2025 – Jul 8, 2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline **Sept 28, 2026**. Allegations: Bloom obtained scandium through intermediaries sourcing from China, **understated China reliance**, and made materially misleading statements about operations and prospects. **Derivative suit** investigations are running in parallel (pre-Feb 27, 2026 buyers), creating D&O exposure.
- **Environmental permitting — Project Jupiter (Oracle, New Mexico)**: the switch to Bloom fuel cells requires an **air-quality permit that is not granted**. The **New Mexico Supreme Court unanimously stayed** permit proceedings and water drilling (Aug 24, 2026); hearings have been delayed repeatedly, with the state unable to give a review timeline (Oct 27, 2026). Risk of multi-year delay or cancellation of the anchor project.
- **China export controls**: Beijing now requires an **export licence for every scandium shipment**. If Bloom's supply is China-linked (see §4), a foreign government holds an operational off-switch on a US AI-infrastructure supplier — a live geopolitical/regulatory tail risk.
- **Customs/tariff exposure**: Chinese supplier Three Circle's own 2021 Shenzhen Stock Exchange filing states it created **Glory Winner (Thailand) in July 2019 "to mitigate the impact of the additional tariffs on fuel cell separator plates."** Bills of lading for Sep–Oct 2026 shipments (~20 t of ceramic electrolyte membranes) arrive with **shipper and consignee names blanked** but carry "BE PALLET" markings. Unredacted transshipment to evade duties would create CBP penalty, False Statements Act and reputational exposure.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Single-point materials dependency on Chinese scandium (existential).** Scandium is "an important role" in Bloom's electrolyte (per its own COO's July 7, 2026 blog). Trade data traces four China-linked routes: direct scandium oxide to the Delaware plant, plus ceramics/powders via Thailand, Japan and Korea. Hunan Oriental Scandium (claims >50% of fuel-cell-grade scandium) reportedly called itself "BE's largest supplier" and said material is "not exported directly." Hunterbrook's model: Bloom needs ~220t of Sc₂O₃ for Wall Street's 5GW ramp against ~240t of global supply and ~310t of total global demand (including Lockheed's F-35 needs) — and Bloom's own claim of 25GW/yr support would imply ~620t/yr, **~10x all 2025 world consumption**. Ex-China processing capability is negligible. The Sep–Oct 2026 **reopening of a dormant Thailand route** suggests Bloom has *fewer* alternatives than publicly stated.
2. **Counterparty and demand circularity in the AI data-center pipeline.** **74% of Q4 2025 revenue ($574M of $778M)** and **$373M of $751M in Q1 2026** came from **Brookfield JVs Bloom part-owns**; Bloom's footnotes concede the customer "may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user." Two buyers reportedly took ~two-thirds of sales. **Oracle is seeking force majeure** on the 2.45GW Project Jupiter (payment relief if it isn't online in 2028); **AEP's $2.65B order slipped** from end-2028 to "no later than 2030." Reported $20B "backlog" is unaudited and includes non-obligatory service revenue and unagreed tax credits; audited **RPO was only $492.6M** (~2.5% of backlog; ~40x gap vs. ~2x for the widest of 10 peers studied).
3. **Key-person and scale-up execution risk.** A 25-year founder-CEO with no succession disclosure, a first-year CFO, and a manufacturing/service footprint that must scale from ~1GW to a claimed 5GW/yr — while honouring 10–15-year service contracts whose economics depend on scandium content that Bloom's own patents link to stack longevity. Any reduction in scandium loading to stretch supply directly undermines the service-contract liability.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: HIGH AND PERSISTENT

- **Two short-report campaigns with documentary evidence**: Hunterbrook Media ("Bloom's Big Lie," **July 8, 2026**; "Chinese Scandium Supply Route Restarts," **Oct 30, 2026**) and **Crossroads Capital**, both short BE and derivatives. Bloom has responded via an 8-K ("categorically rejects... false") and a COO blog post, and shares rose ~6% on the rebuttal — but the second report directly tests the credibility of the first rebuttal.
- **Disclosure asymmetry allegation**: Bloom's July 7 scandium blog post carried a legal disclaimer that, per Hunterbrook, appears on no other company blog post — a tell that counsel was engaged and the company was managing liability rather than informing.
- **Sentiment is now hostage to AI-datacenter narrative**: −26.6% in October 2026, −16% in a single session (Nov 5, 2026, $200.40 → $167.52 on heavy volume), and +15% on a *peer's* (CoreWeave) earnings. A beta of 3.64 with a ~60x 2026 adjusted EPS multiple means reputational shocks transmit violently to the share price.
- **Legacy credibility overhang**: the 2020 restatement, the Delaware jobs shortfall (~600 jobs below projections, costs >5x original estimates), and the SK Ecoplant volume-relief episode form a pattern short sellers are explicitly exploiting ("when end demand doesn't materialize, find an intermediary willing to bet on Bloom, then book the bet as growth").
- Offsetting: visible community/ESG activity (e.g., Oxford Fire Department donation, Nov 6, 2026) and a genuine "no water, no noise, faster than grid" product narrative that retains political tailwinds after the Democratic midterm sweep.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: the company combines (a) a live securities-fraud class action and derivative suits over disclosure of its most critical input, (b) documentary third-party evidence of a disguised China supply chain and possible tariff transshipment, (c) a related-party-financed revenue base with an unaudited backlog ~40x audited RPO, (d) four CFOs in under three years during the exact period under scrutiny, (e) unresolved environmental permitting on its anchor project, and (f) $149.6M of insider sales with zero purchases. Formal regulatory enforcement has **not** materialized (EDGAR FTS clean), and the board retains credible independent voices — which is why this stops short of Critical.

**Upgrade to CRITICAL if any of:** an SEC/DOJ subpoena or formal investigation; an Item 4.02 non-reliance/restatement covering 2025–2026 revenue; a CBP penalty or customs inquiry; Project Jupiter cancellation; or a further documented China shipment after an explicit on-record denial.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Securities class action + derivative suits** (class period Feb 27, 2025 – Jul 8, 2026) alleging understated China/scandium reliance — D&O, indemnification and disclosure-control exposure. *Escalate to GC/legal.*
2. **Revenue circularity**: 74% of Q4 2025 revenue from part-owned Brookfield JVs; footnote admits customer "may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user." *Audit-committee-level issue.*
3. **Backlog integrity**: $20B unaudited backlog vs. $492.6M audited RPO; includes non-obligatory service revenue and unagreed tax credits. *Would not survive a disclosure-quality stress test.*
4. **Finance leadership instability**: 4 CFOs since 2024, ~11 months interim, spanning the alleged misstatement window. Combined with the 2020 revenue-recognition restatement, this is a **pattern**, not an event.
5. **Possible customs/tariff evasion** via Thailand (Glory Winner; supplier's own filing cites US tariff mitigation) with **redacted shipper/consignee** on Sep–Oct 2026 bills of lading. *Potential penalties, import disruption.*
6. **Oracle force majeure + New Mexico air-permit stays** — keystone 2.45–2.8GW contract and the AEP order are both slipping; concentration means one adverse ruling hits a large share of backlog.
7. **Insider selling**: $149.6M sold / $0 bought over 12 months by 10 insiders, including the CEO ($47M) and a director at $297.69.
