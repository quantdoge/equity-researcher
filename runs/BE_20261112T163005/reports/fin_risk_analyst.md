## Financial Risk Assessment — Bloom Energy Corporation (BE)

**Data note:** FY2026 Q3 (period ended 2026-09-30) is the latest period available, and the financial trajectory has changed radically over the last four quarters. Where the pre-built ratio tools (leverage, liquidity, Z-score, F-score, FCF yield) rely on FY2025 annual data, I have recomputed them on a trailing-twelve-month (TTM through Q3'26) basis and report both. **The FY2025-based tool outputs materially understate current financial health.**

### 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **Investment Grade (lower tier) / improving; unrated**

Bloom has transformed from a loss-making, cash-burning fuel-cell developer into a profitable, cash-generative manufacturer in four quarters. There is **no public credit rating or CDS data available (data gap)**, so this is a synthetic assessment.

| Debt / equity | 3.77x (book) | **1.42x** | Improving |
| Debt / assets | 0.68x | **0.42x** | Improving |

**Debt structure is the single strongest part of the credit story.** Per filings, the stack is dominated by **$2,500M of 0% convertible senior notes due Nov 2030** (conversion rate 5.1290 sh/$1,000 ≈ **$194.97 conversion price**), plus smaller **3.0% Green Convertible Notes due 2028 (~$448.7M) and 2029**. Cash interest expense is only **$35.7M TTM (~1.3% effective cost of debt)** on $2.78B of debt. Short-term debt at Q3'26 is just **$45.7M** — there is **no near-term refinancing cliff**; the first real wall is 2028, with the bulk in 2030.

**Covenant/refinancing risk is low but not zero:** the 2030 notes carry a 0% coupon that will not be re-financeable at 0% — the 5Y UST is at 4.93% and 10Y at 5.13% (curve upward-sloping, 2s10s +39bp, no inversion). Pro-forma: $2.5B refinanced at ~5% adds ~$125M annual cash interest, taking coverage from ~15.8x to **~3.5x**. That is still serviceable, but it is the key 2030 event.

The loss lands on the equity multiple, not the balance sheet: at 100x EBITDA, a de-rating to 40x on the stress-case EBITDA of $182M implies ~$7B EV — **an ~88% equity decline**. That is the true downside scenario for a shareholder.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Split verdict: **credit/liquidity risk = LOW** (net debt/EBITDA 0.48x, coverage ~16x, current ratio 3.7x, $2.49B cash, no maturities before 2028, 0% coupons). **Market/equity risk = HIGH** (108% vol, beta 2.4–3.6, –53% max drawdown, ~100x EV/EBITDA). The former has been fixed; the latter has been created by the re-rating. Overall Moderate reflects a genuinely de-risked balance sheet offset by a valuation that has no room for execution error.

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Working-capital-driven cash flow divergence (highest-priority flag).** Q3'26 net income was +$250M but **operating cash flow was –$27.1M**, a swing of ~–$277M QoQ, driven by a **–$356M working capital change**. Earnings are running well ahead of cash. TTM OCF of $694M vs net income of $518M reconciles, but the quarterly volatility is severe and the trend is deteriorating into the growth ramp.
2. **Dilution.** Share count went from 240.4M (FY25) to **294.8M (Q3'26), +22.6% in nine months** — the sole failed Piotroski test. Diluted count 324.3M. The 2030 converts add a further **12.8M shares** at $194.97 (currently only 4% in-the-money), so convertible dilution is modest — but equity-comp-driven dilution is not.
3. **Capex step-up.** Q3'26 capex $153.5M vs $26.1M in Q1'26 — a ~6x increase in two quarters. If the demand ramp slips, this becomes stranded capacity and FCF turns negative.
4. **Valuation/reflexivity.** At ~100x EV/EBITDA and beta ~2.4–3.6, the stock is a leveraged bet on continued hyper-growth; a single guidance miss historically produces –11% to –27% days.
5. **2030 refinancing.** $2.5B of 0% paper must be replaced at ~5% rates, adding ~$125M of cash interest (coverage 15.8x → ~3.5x).
6. **Negative retained earnings of –$3.47B** and thin book equity ($1.96B) — the balance sheet's equity cushion is still small relative to the enterprise; this is what drives the book-equity Altman reading.
