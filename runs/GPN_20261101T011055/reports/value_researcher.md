## Global Payments Inc. (GPN) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $96.84 (Oct 30, 2026 close) | Market cap ~$25.6B | Diluted shares ~259M | Net debt $17.0B | EV ~$42B*

**Situation summary:** GPN closed two transformational deals in early 2026 — acquiring Worldpay ($24.25B) and divesting its Issuer Solutions business to FIS. It is now a pure-play merchant acquirer/commerce-solutions company. FY2026 guidance: adjusted EPS $13.60–13.80, normalized cc adjusted net revenue growth 4–5%, ~150bp margin expansion, >$2B capital return. The stock rose 13% on Oct 30 on a report that CD&R and GTCR (GPN's largest shareholder, and the seller of Worldpay) are pursuing a take-private.

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW (and I would not argue it has widened)**

What GPN genuinely has:
- **Scale/cost advantage in a scale business.** One of the two or three largest merchant acquirers globally — trillions in payment volume, 175+ countries, dual acquiring relationships with the card networks. Processing is a fixed-cost-heavy business, so unit costs fall with volume.
- **Switching costs in vertical software.** In restaurant, healthcare, education, and B2B verticals, GPN's POS/software is embedded in the merchant's daily operations. Ripping out a payments-plus-software stack is painful. This is the best part of the franchise.
- **Distribution intangibles.** Bank referral relationships, ISV channels, and enterprise contracts are real but contractually finite.

What it does *not* have:
- **No network effects.** Those accrue to Visa/Mastercard. Acquirers sit between the merchant and the network and compete on price and service.
- **No real pricing power.** Merchant acquiring has persistent structural price deflation (~1–2%/yr), offset by volume growth and attach of value-added services. Worldpay in particular has been a share loser under prior ownership.
- **ROIC that clears the cost of capital only barely.** GAAP ROIC was 3.2% in 2025, 5.4–5.7% in 2022–24. On *adjusted* numbers (stripping acquired-intangible amortization), NOPAT of ~$4.3B on ~$41B of invested capital is ~10% versus a ~9% WACC. That is a business earning roughly its cost of capital — the definition of no durable moat.
- **Evidence of moat erosion in the capital-allocation record.** Adjusted EPS went from ~$8.00 (2021) to ~$13.70 (2026E), up ~70%, while the share price went from a ~$220 peak (2021) to $97 — a ~55% decline. The market has re-rated the multiple from ~28x to ~7x because the growth was purchased, not earned. TSYS ($21.5B, 2019) and now Worldpay ($24.25B, 2026) were both large, full-price deals. Goodwill and intangibles now stand at **$46.4B, or 73% of total assets.**

**Conclusion:** a scale-based narrow moat in a commoditizing industry, with the strongest sub-franchises in vertical software and the weakest in generic enterprise acquiring.

The core problem is **earnings quality**: adjusted EPS excludes ~$3.0B/yr of acquired-intangible amortization against $46B of intangibles. I treat the amortization as largely a sunk-cost artifact of past premiums rather than a current cash cost — but it is *not* zero, because GPN must keep buying technology and vertical assets to defend its position. Capitalized tuck-in M&A is a real, recurring cost that does not appear in either capex or adjusted EPS. That is why I haircut FCF in my valuation.

The second problem is **leverage plus a messy transition year**. Reported H1 2026 free cash flow was negative; management's "adjusted free cash flow" adds back deal and integration costs. Until GPN prints a clean full year of cash flow as a pure-play, FCF is an estimate, not a fact.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Multiples at $96.84:**
- P/E on FY26E adjusted EPS: **7.1x**
- P/B: 1.05x
- EV/adjusted EBITDA (~$6.1B): **~6.9x**
- EV/adjusted net revenue: ~3.4x
- FCF yield (on ~$3.0B normalized FCF): **~12%**
- Dividend yield: 1.03% (payout ~7% of adjusted EPS — trivially covered)

**Peer context:** the whole large-cap processor complex is de-rated. PayPal trades at ~6.6x 2026 EV/FCF; Fiserv's EV is ~$60B against a similarly compressed multiple. GPN at 7x adjusted EPS is **cheap but not uniquely cheap** — it is priced in line with a group the market believes is structurally challenged. (Note: my automated peer-multiple tool returned index funds rather than processors; peer figures are from secondary sources and should be treated as approximate.)

**DCF (owner-earnings based):** normalized FCF $2.75–3.00B, growth 6% yrs 1–5 fading to 4% yrs 6–10, terminal growth 2.5%.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear (Worldpay shrinks, integration slips) | $2.2B, 2% growth | 10% | ~$21B | **~$80** |
| Base | $2.75B, 6%→4% | 10% | ~$28.6B | **~$108** |
| Base, lower discount rate | $3.00B, 6%→4% | 9% | ~$40B | **~$135** |
| Take-private report ($40B equity) | — | — | $40B | ~$150 |

**Intrinsic value estimate: ~$110 (base, conservative), range $80–$140.**

At $96.84 the stock trades at roughly a **12–15% discount to base-case intrinsic value**. That is a discount — but it is short of the 20–30% margin of safety I require, particularly for a narrow-moat, levered, serial-acquirer business with an unproven cash-flow year.

**What the price implies:** ~7x earnings and a 12% FCF yield imply the market expects little or no real growth and assigns meaningful probability to value destruction. If GPN merely compounds adjusted EPS at 7–8% and delevers, the stock should earn 12–15%/yr. That is a reasonable bet — but it requires trusting the adjusted numbers.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Grade: C+/B–**

Positives:
- **Strategic simplification is correct.** Exiting Issuer Solutions (a slower-growth, capital-intensive issuer-processing business) to become a pure-play commerce solutions provider removes a genuine conglomerate discount and sharpens capital allocation.
- **Capital return is now substantial and explicit:** >$2B in 2026, ~$7.5B planned for 2025–2027, funded by FCF. Dividends plus buybacks are retiring ~2%/yr of shares. This is a real improvement over the empire-building era.
- **Deleveraging is a stated priority**, with leverage around 2.8x and a path lower.

Negatives:
- **Price discipline is questionable.** Paying $24.25B (~10x EBITDA) for Worldpay — a low-growth, share-losing asset — with debt and stock, immediately after a decade in which two mega-deals (TSYS, Worldpay) coincided with a 55% decline in the share price, is not the record of a disciplined acquirer.
- **The adjusted-vs-GAAP gap is enormous**, and the Q1 2026 $1.80B GAAP loss (with a loss on the Issuer disposal) is a reminder that the "real" economics have repeatedly disappointed the accounting.
- **Insider ownership appears low** for a company of this size (I could not verify a precise figure within budget — treat as an open item). The largest shareholder is GTCR, a private-equity seller-turned-holder, not an owner-operator.
- **The CEO is a promotion from within** (Cameron Bready, since 2023) with a solid operating reputation but a short record as capital allocator.

The reported CD&R/GTCR take-private interest is itself informative: the people who know Worldpay best — its former PE owner, now GPN's biggest holder — reportedly see more value in GPN's shares than $97.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (accumulate below ~$82–85)

I cannot call this a Buy at $96.84, and I would not sell it either.

**Why not Buy:** The business earns roughly its cost of capital, its moat is narrow and arguably narrowing, 73% of assets are goodwill and intangibles, leverage is ~2.8x, and the current year's free cash flow is unproven (reported H1 2026 FCF was negative). A 12–15% discount to intrinsic value does not compensate for that combination. And the 13% one-day pop on takeover speculation means a meaningful slice of today's price is an event premium, not a fundamental one — if the CD&R/GTCR report fades, the stock returns to the low $80s.

**Why not Sell:** At 7x adjusted EPS and a ~12% FCF yield with 42% adjusted operating margins, $2B+/yr of capital return, and a credible deleveraging path, the downside is largely priced. A base-case DCF supports $110+, and the deleveraging-plus-buyback math can generate low-to-mid-teens annual returns if execution merely meets guidance.

**Actionable framing:**
- **Below $82** (≈25% discount to base-case IV): **Buy**, with position sizing appropriate to a levered narrow-moat name.
- **$82–105:** **Hold.** This is where we are.
- **Above ~$120:** trim; the fundamental case is exhausted and you are being paid only for deal hope.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **The adjusted-earnings illusion.** If acquired-intangible amortization and recurring tuck-in M&A are treated as real economic costs, GPN's true owner earnings are materially below $13.70/share. The gap between GAAP and adjusted results has been widening, not narrowing.
2. **Worldpay integration and share loss.** Worldpay was a structurally challenged asset. If normalized 4–5% growth proves to be flattered by pricing actions rather than volume, the deleveraging math breaks.
3. **Leverage in a higher-for-longer rate world.** $22.4B of debt at ~4.9% costs ~$1.1B/yr. Refinancing risk and the call on FCF are real; a downturn that hits travel/ discretionary spend squeezes both EBITDA and the deleveraging timeline.
4. **Middle East conflict / travel exposure.** Management explicitly cut its 2026 revenue outlook for this reason — evidence that the portfolio is more cyclical than the "recurring revenue" label suggests.
5. **Structural disintermediation.** Stripe, Adyen, Toast, Square, and embedded-fintech platforms continue to take share at the software-integrated end of the market, precisely where GPN's switching costs are supposed to be highest.
6. **Deal risk in both directions.** If a take-private at a rich price materializes, holders are capped near the offer. If it does not, the stock gives back the 13% speculation premium. Neither outcome is under an owner's control.
7. **Data gaps in this analysis:** I could not verify insider ownership, obtain clean post-deal adjusted EBITDA/FCF reconciliations, or get a reliable peer-multiple set (the tool returned index funds). The FCF base of $2.75–3.0B is my own estimate and the single largest source of valuation error.

**Bottom line:** GPN is a statistically cheap, narrowly moated, heavily levered serial acquirer that is finally doing the right things — simplifying, deleveraging, and returning capital — at a price that already reflects a good deal of skepticism. It is a reasonable holding and a poor place to demand a large margin of safety today. Patience, and a lower entry, is the better trade.
