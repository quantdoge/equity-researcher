# GPN — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**Global Payments Inc. | Financial Services / Payment Processing | Atlanta, GA | Mkt cap ~$25.6bn | Price $96.84 | 26,000 FTE**
*Assessment basis: FY2025 10-K (filed 20-Feb-2026), Q1/Q2 2026 10-Q, Q2 2026 earnings disclosures, SEC Form 4 metadata, EDGAR full-text enforcement search, news/insider feeds. Current date context: early Nov 2026.*

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (deteriorating on capital-allocation/tax discipline)**

**Positives**
- **CEO/Chair separation is genuine**: M. Troy Woods (ex-TSYS CEO) serves as **Independent Chair**; Cameron Bready is CEO/President and a director. No power concentration on this axis.
- Internal control over financial reporting carried an **unqualified ICFR opinion** in the FY2025 10-K; no material weakness disclosed in any 2025–2026 10-Q reviewed ("no changes in our internal control").
- CEO continuity: Bready was CFO 2014–2019 and COO 2019–2023, i.e., internally groomed, and the forward disclosure program (guidance, adjusted metrics, provisional purchase accounting) has been transparent about complexity.

**Negatives**
- **Capital allocation and tax structuring failed at the board level.** The 17-Apr-2025 transaction (Worldpay acquired for ~$17.0bn of consideration incl. **42.8–43.3m GPN shares** + ~$6.2bn cash; Issuer Solutions sold to FIS) closed 9-Jan-2026 and produced a **$1,586m (Q1) / $1,688m (1H) loss from discontinued operations, "mainly tax on non-deductible goodwill."** A ~$1.7bn cash-tax consequence of deal structuring is a board-level diligence miss, not a market event.
- **Rising GAAP/non-GAAP divergence**: Q1-26 GAAP EPS **$(6.59)** vs. "adjusted" revenue growth of ~29%; Q2-26 net income attributable to GPN of just **$13.0m ($0.05/diluted share)** on $3.32bn revenue. Numbers are moving further, not closer, to GAAP.
- **Reporting churn**: reorganized again in 2026 into **Enterprise / Platforms / SMB** (third structure since the TSYS merger), with Issuer Solutions recast as discontinued operations. Comparability is impaired — a standard earnings-quality watch item.
- **Litigation history on disclosure**: *In re Global Payments Securities Litigation* (class period 31-Oct-2019 to 18-Oct-2022) — final judgment Dec-2024, **settlement checks mailed 27-May-2026**. That case centred on reported/adjusted revenue and merchant-partner disclosure. A settled case over the same non-GAAP metric family now central to the Worldpay story is directly relevant.
- **Board refresh/independence and pay-for-performance could not be verified** — the DEF 14A was not retrievable (see Gaps). No proxy contest or activist campaign surfaced in search.
- 2024–2026 headcount reductions/restructuring (US, Canada, UK, India) are running concurrently with integration; incentive metrics under a transformation program are a classic risk of paying for cost-out that is really deferred investment.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **ELEVATED (M&A-completion risk retired; conduct/tax/antitrust-survivor risk now dominant)**

- **No SEC enforcement actions or DOJ matters** returned by EDGAR full-text search for GPN. That is a clean screen result and the strongest single regulatory data point.
- **Deal approvals: cleared.** Worldpay/Irrevocable Issuer Solutions transaction obtained UK and EU competition clearances and closed 9-Jan-2026. Completion risk is now zero; **survivor risk is not** — GPN is materially larger in merchant acquiring and will face heightened scrutiny of any further consolidation, most-favoured-nation/pricing conduct, and interchange-related rules.
- **Tax structuring exposure** (see Red Flag #1): non-deductible goodwill on the divestiture implies tax-authority review of the structure and valuation is a live possibility; $1.7bn of tax expense is booked, so further downside would be penalties/interest rather than principal.
- **Payments-conduct regulation** — the perennial sector-wide channel: CFPB prepaid/consumer-fee rulemaking (Netspend/BCS heritage), FTC and state AG attention to merchant-processing sales practices, junk-fee/surcharge rules, and state money-transmitter licensing across 50 states plus EU/UK/APAC regimes. Integration of Worldpay's sponsor/ISO and high-risk-merchant books into GPN's compliance stack is the single most likely source of a future consent order.
- **Legal proceedings text was not retrievable** from the filing tool (returned only the SOX certification exhibit), so ordinary-course merchant/consumer litigation volume cannot be quantified — treat as an unquantified but normal-course exposure.
- **Card-network/compliance history**: the 2012 card-data breach cost ~$94m and remains the reference precedent for GPN's PCI/card-network penalty exposure.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Worldpay integration + Issuer Solutions carve-out executed simultaneously.** Cutover of legacy Worldpay processing/acquiring stacks into GPN's platforms, a three-segment reorganisation, **$600m of run-rate Worldpay expense synergies targeted by YE2028** and **$650m of transformation benefits by 1H27** — all while providing transition services to FIS on the divested Issuer platform GPN once ran. Synergy shortfalls, merchant attrition, or a migration outage would hit revenue, brand and (given leverage) the equity simultaneously.
2. **Leverage and refinancing dependency.** Total debt **$22.4bn** at 30-Jun-2026, with a **$1.0bn term loan fully drawn**, **$1.6bn revolver drawn**, only ~$5.0bn of revolver commitments left, and interest expense of **$239m in a single quarter**. Covenant compliance is affirmed, but the deleveraging path now depends on delivering guidance *after* management cut FY2026 revenue and EPS on 5-Aug-2026. Operating cash flow collapsed to **$374m for 1H26 vs. $1,373m a year earlier** — a genuine early-warning indicator.
3. **Cybersecurity and third-party concentration.** A materially larger merchant and cardholder data estate (Worldpay + TSYS + legacy GPN stacks, plus tokenisation/embedded-payments partners like Lightspeed DMS and CKE deployments) on a post-breach history. Elevated data volume, three overlapping security architectures, and heavy reliance on owned first-party processing = the highest-severity tail risk on the sheet.
4. **Key-person and bench risk.** Bready is CEO, President, principal executive officer and the public face of a multi-year transformation; the finance/accounting function has absorbed two years of deal and re-segmentation work (the CAO was selling stock in Dec-2025). Low-quality third-party commentary refers to "executive exits" alongside the August guidance cut — **I could not verify this**; treat as unconfirmed but worth a phone call.
5. **Vertical/travel-mix concentration.** Management explicitly tied the FY2026 guidance cut to **travel-related volume weakness from the Middle East conflict** — evidence that a meaningful share of revenue is discretionary, cross-border and macro/geopolitically sensitive.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **MODERATE-ELEVATED**

- Principal live issue is **credibility, not scandal**: a guidance cut roughly three months after closing the largest deal in company history, a $1.79bn reported 1H loss, and a narrative centred on "adjusted" revenue (+29%) that differs wildly from GAAP. Financial media and sell-side coverage have already begun contrasting adjusted and GAAP results.
- **Merchant-conduct/pricing perception** is a chronic, low-grade brand issue (merchant-review sites criticise non-uniform rate cards and lack of transparency) — a category-level reputational drag that becomes acute if regulators or a journalist pick up a specific ISO/partner's sales practice.
- **Employee/brand sentiment** around successive layoff rounds (US, Canada, UK, India) and a large Worldpay integration adds talent-retention and Glassdoor-grade reputational friction.
- Positives: no consumer boycott, no ESG controversy cluster, no short-seller report identified, high-stakes institutional ownership stable-to-improving (BNY Mellon new ~$99.7m stake in Q2; HSBC +40% in Q3) alongside some 13F reductions (CalPERS, Sterling Capital −88.9%) — mixed but not a confidence crisis.
- Residual legal-marketing noise: at least one plaintiffs' firm is advertising an investigation tied to the August guidance cut. **Unverified and typical post-drawdown behaviour**, but it is the mechanism by which a reputation problem becomes a securities-fraud problem.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not because of fraud or a live enforcement matter (both screens are clean), but because the company has, in one move, **tripled its integration complexity, doubled its debt, accumulated $27.0bn of goodwill against $23.1bn of equity, and generated a $1.7bn tax error** — all while guiding down. Governance is structurally sound (independent chair, clean ICFR) but has demonstrably weak capital-allocation and tax-diligence capability at the board level. Non-financial risks here are highly financially consequential: equity is thin enough relative to intangibles that one impairment or one stalled synergy program re-prices the stock.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🚩 $1.7bn tax hit on non-deductible goodwill (Issuer Solutions sale).** Q1-26 $1,586m and 1H26 $1,688m losses from discontinued operations, "mainly tax on non-deductible goodwill." **Escalate:** this is a structuring/diligence failure that consumed ~7% of market cap, was not the disclosed expectation when the deal was signed, and implies tax-authority engagement on valuation. Ask management directly: was this a change in structure, a valuation outcome, or a known cost under-disclosed at signing? Any hint of the third is a disclosure-controls issue.
2. **🚩 Goodwill $26.98bn vs. total equity $23.11bn (117%), plus $19.4bn of intangibles and *provisional* purchase accounting.** Interim measurement-period adjustments were still being recorded in Q2-26 for ~$10.0bn of provisional Worldpay goodwill and $16.4bn of intangibles. Equity is effectively goodwill-and-intangibles only; a $5–8bn write-down is arithmetically absorbable but would push book equity toward the leverage-covenant conversation.
3. **🚩 Adjusted-revenue dependence layered on a settled securities class action about adjusted revenue.** The prior case (2019–2022 class period, final judgment Dec-2024, settlement paid May-2026) concerned exactly the metric family now being used to sell the Worldpay story. Any restatement, re-segmentation error, or adjusted-to-GAAP reconciliation change should be treated as elevated-probability and high-impact.
4. **🚩 Guidance cut 3 months post-close + operating cash flow down 73% (1H26 $374m vs $1,373m).** Cash conversion, not just optics, is deteriorating during the heaviest integration period. Bridge this before underwriting the deleveraging thesis.
5. **🚩 Reporting-structure instability**: three segment structures in three years, discontinued-operations recasts, and "Financial Reporting Considerations for the Issuer Solutions Transaction" guidance issued alongside FY2025 results. Comparability risk is being manufactured faster than analysts can normalise it.
6. **⚠️ Insider tape offers no offsetting signal** — no open-market CEO/CFO/COO buying through a guidance cut and a $22bn leverage event; officer/former-affiliate sales dominate.
