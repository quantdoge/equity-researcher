## GPN — Global Payments Inc. | Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $96.84 (Oct 30, 2026 close) · Market cap ~$24–25B · EV ~$37–38B · Sector: Financials / Credit Services*

---

### 1. Growth Story

Global Payments has just completed a transformational portfolio swap — acquiring **Worldpay for $24.25B** and divesting **Issuer Solutions to FIS for $13.5B**, both closing January 9, 2026 — converting itself into a **pure-play merchant commerce solutions provider** with ~$3.5–4T of annual payment volume, ~42% adjusted operating margins, and a $600M cost + $200M revenue synergy program over three years. The reported −23.8% FY25 revenue decline is entirely a deconsolidation artifact, not deterioration: normalized constant-currency adjusted net revenue is growing **4–5%** with **~150bps of margin expansion** and **~12% adjusted EPS growth**, while management returns **>$7.5B of capital (2025–27)** — over 30% of market cap. The thesis is **not** disruptive innovation; it is a cash-flow re-rating story at 7x forward earnings, now turbocharged by an Oct 30 report that **CD&R and GTCR are preparing a ~$40B take-private bid**.

### 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **Global payments revenue pool**: ~$2.5–2.7T and growing ~7–9% annually per McKinsey's Global Payments Report — a TAM no single player can dominate, which favors scaled multi-vertical acquirers.
- **GPN's position**: ~$3.5–4T of combined processed volume against ~$35–40T of global card purchase volume → **~10% share**, leaving room to consolidate share via Worldpay's enterprise/e-commerce book (historically underpenetrated in SMB) plus GPN's **Genius POS/software** stack.
- **Adjacency layers**: SMB vertical software, embedded finance, payfac, and AI-driven commerce tooling — these convert volume-based (basis-point) economics into per-seat SaaS multiples, which is the real re-rating lever.
- **Honest read**: this is a *share-shift and cross-sell* TAM story, not a new-market-creation story. TAM capture is bounded at the low end of the payments value chain, which is why the multiple is 7x, not 25x.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q3 2026 earnings — Nov 5 (results just ahead)**: first clean read on synergy capture and whether the Middle East travel drag stabilizes; consensus $3.58 adj EPS / $3.18B revenue.
- **CD&R / GTCR take-private process (live, reported Oct 30)**: ~$40B EV bid. Net debt ~$13.5B implies ~$110/share equity value (~14% above current). A confirmed bid or a competing bidder (Advent, Silver Lake, strategic) is the dominant near-term swing factor; a dead deal gives back the +13% day.
- **Synergy milestones**: $600M run-rate cost synergies over 3 years — each $100M ≈ $0.30–0.35 of EPS at scale.
- **Deleveraging path**: S&P flagged possible downgrade if leverage stays >3.5x. Getting to ~3.0x by 2027 unlocks buyback acceleration and multiple expansion.
- **Genius platform adoption + AI commercialization** — the only genuine "innovation multiple" argument available.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (moderate conviction — value/catalyst, not innovation)

**This is not a Cathie Wood compounder.** Organic growth of 4–5% and a normalized Rule of 40 of ~31 fail any disruptive-innovation screen, and growth is majority acquisition-driven. But the risk/reward is unusually asymmetric:

- **Base case ($115–120)**: 8–9x FY27E EPS of $16.11 as synergies land and leverage falls — still a steep discount to FIS (13x) and Fiserv (17x). Plus ~13% FCF yield and $7.5B of buyback support.
- **Take-private case (~$110–125/share)**: $40B EV report implies ~$110 at current net debt; a competitive auction on one of the largest LBOs on record could push toward $120–125. Note **GTCR is bidding for an asset it partly sold** — it knows the book intimately.
- **Bear case (~$70–78)**: deal collapses *and* travel/Middle East drag persists *and* organic growth decelerates below 4%.

Probability-weighted upside comfortably exceeds downside at 7x earnings. The main reason this isn't a Strong Buy is that ~13% of the current price is a binary M&A premium, and GAAP EPS of $0.05 in Q2 shows how thin economic earnings are beneath the adjusted numbers.

---

### 7. Key Risks

1. **Balance sheet / leverage (highest severity)**: ~$13.5B+ net debt post-Worldpay; S&P explicitly threatens a downgrade if leverage stays above 3.5x. Rising rates hit both interest cost ($650M+ pre-deal, now materially higher) and LBO financing feasibility — an LBO is highly rate-sensitive.
2. **Deal-break risk**: The Oct 30 +13% move is unconfirmed reporting. Failure to launch a formal bid likely returns the stock to the $80s; elevated financing markets make a $40B LBO far from certain.
3. **Execution/integration risk**: Worldpay is a large, previously PE-owned, multi-platform asset. Merchant attrition, platform consolidation costs, and culture friction could erode the $600M synergy target and the 4–5% organic base.
4. **GAAP vs. adjusted gap**: ~$1.4B+ annual D&A from acquired intangibles means reported EPS is near zero; the 7x multiple is on adjusted numbers. Any shift in how investors credit adjusted EPS is a de-rating risk.
5. **Secular/regulatory pressure**: Interchange caps (EU, US CCCA debate), card-not-present fraud liability, and disintermediation by Stripe/Adyen/Toast/Shift4 in the fastest-growing verticals; a 25bp rate rise scenario also pressures consumer spend.
6. **Geopolitical**: Management explicitly cited Middle East conflict hitting the travel portfolio — a reminder that ~a fifth of merchant volume is discretionary/Cross-border.

**Bottom line:** Own it for the cash flow, the multiple gap, and the catalyst stack (Nov 5 print + live PE bid + $7.5B buyback) — not for growth leadership. Size modestly; the M&A premium means the position is part event-driven trade, part value compounder.
