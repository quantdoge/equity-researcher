# GPN — Forensic Short-Seller Analysis

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **MEDIUM**

Notably, the forensic toolkit comes back *cleaner than I expected*. This is not a fraud short. But "clean" here is partly an artifact of a balance sheet that was torn apart and reassembled by two simultaneous mega-deals — so the cleanliness deserves scrutiny rather than credit.

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Read |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accruals ratio | -2.50% | -4.18% | -2.36% | Negative = high quality |
| Cash conversion (OCF/NI) | 2.28x | 2.25x | 1.90x | >100%, strong |
| FCF conversion | — | 1.82x | 1.46x | Strong |
| Composite quality score | — | — | **48/100** | Middling |
| Piotroski F-Score | — | — | **6/9** | Moderate |

The 48/100 composite is the honest number: cash generation is unimpeachable, but the score is dragged down by the FCF-conversion decline (1.82x → 1.46x) and rising capital intensity as Worldpay consolidated. F-Score fails on leverage (F5) and asset turnover (F9) — both structural consequences of serial M&A.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**RF-1 — GAAP earnings have effectively gone to zero while "adjusted" EPS hits records.** This is the single most important fact in the file.
- Q1 2026: GAAP net loss **-$1,799.9M**, GAAP EPS **-$7.49** (-$6.59 as reported attributable) vs. **adjusted EPS +$2.96**
- Q2 2026: GAAP EPS **+$0.05** vs. **adjusted EPS +$3.46** (42.0% "adjusted" operating margin vs. **10.2% GAAP operating margin** — $337M GAAP operating income on $3.32B revenue)
- FY2026 guidance: **adjusted EPS $13.60–13.80** → stock trades at ~6.6x a number that has no GAAP analogue
- Q2 2026 D&A annualises to ~$3.5B (vs. $1.4B in FY2025); the ~$2.5B/yr wedge is acquisition-intangible amortisation. Bears should be precise: this is non-cash and GAAP-standard, *but* it is the price of buying growth GPN could not generate organically. Every dollar of "adjusted" EPS assumes that spending was free.

**RF-2 — Buyback capital destruction is quantifiable and severe.** Diluted shares fell 293.7M (2021) → 240.2M (2025), a 53.5M reduction, at a cumulative repurchase cost of **$8.88B** across 2021–2025. That is an average of **~$166/share** — and it understates the true cost, since part of each year's buyback merely offset stock-based comp. At ~$90 today, **~$4.1B (~17% of market cap) has been incinerated on buybacks alone.**

**RF-3 — Buy high, issue low.** In Q2 2026 diluted shares jumped 240.2M → **259.4M (+8%)** as Worldpay consideration stock was issued — roughly $1.7B of equity at ~$90, immediately after spending $8.9B on the same equity at $166. This is the opposite of disciplined capital allocation.

**RF-4 — Deleveraging is subordinated to price support.** Management plans **~$7.5B of capital returns over 2025–2027** (>$2B in 2026 alone; $1.2B already done YTD) while net leverage sits above the "3x by end-2027" target on ~$5.6B 2026E EBITDA. My rough FCF bridge (adjusted net revenue ~$12.4B × 42% margin = ~$5.2B, less ~$1.1B interest, ~$0.9B capex, ~$0.8B tax) gets to **~$2.4B FCF**. Returning $2B+ implies an ~85% payout while levered. Deleveraging therefore depends on EBITDA growth, not debt paydown. Interest expense has already run from $650M (FY2025) to **~$1.04B annualised** (Q1+Q2 2026).

**RF-5 — Guidance cut with a convenient excuse.** Normalized constant-currency adjusted net revenue growth guided down to **~4–5%** from ~5%, blamed on "the ongoing conflict in the Middle East and its impact on our travel portfolio." Organic growth of 4% is roughly half the nominal growth of the global payments revenue pool — i.e., **share loss**, not a geopolitical accident.

**RF-6 — Altman Z-Score of 1.024 (Distress zone).** I will not over-sell this. X5 (asset turnover 0.145) and X2 (11.1%) are structurally depressed for any goodwill-heavy processor, so the score is largely a business-model artefact, not a solvency signal. But X4 (book equity/liabilities = 0.80) is genuinely thin, and FY2025 tangible book equity is only **~$2.5B against $23.8B of book equity** — a ~90% goodwill-and-intangible equity base.

**RF-7 — Impairment is a serial event, not a one-off.** FY2022 net income collapsed to $111M (EPS $0.41 from $3.29) on a >$680M EBIT decline — a write-down. FY2025 booked a $33.2M goodwill impairment in discontinued operations. Q1 2026 carried a **$1,586.2M loss on discontinued operations** (Issuer Solutions divestiture). A company that has written off pieces of Total System, TSYS, Active Network, EVO and now Issuer Solutions should not be granted a permanently pristine multiple on its remaining $21B+ of goodwill.

## 3. Beneish M-Score and Accruals Analysis

**M-Score: -3.056 → "Low risk." Well below both the -2.22 and -1.78 thresholds.** Accruals are negative (-2.36% of assets), and TATA of **-0.0236** is a genuinely strong reading. There is no cash-versus-earnings divergence here, and no evidence of revenue-recognition games:

| Index | Value | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| DSRI | 0.951 | Receivables *lagging* revenue — no channel stuffing |
| GMI | 0.865 | Gross margin "improved" — **but this is divestiture mix, not pricing power** |
| AQI | 0.894 | Asset quality "improved" — **also an artefact (intangibles moved to held-for-sale)** |
| SGI | 0.763 | Sales index <1 because revenue *shrank* 23.75% |
| DEPI | 0.927 | Depreciation rate up modestly |
| **SGAI** | **1.331** | ⚠️ SG&A/revenue jumped 40.2% → 53.5% — the one index pointing the wrong way |
| **LVGI** | **1.088** | ⚠️ Leverage index up — consistent with the debt-funded deal |
| TATA | -0.0236 | Strong negative accruals |

**My honest caveat:** three of the four indices that make the score look clean (GMI, AQI, SGI) are mechanical products of moving a whole segment into discontinued operations, not evidence of improving economics. Strip those out and the two forward-looking indices — SGAI at 1.331 and LVGI at 1.088 — both read as deterioration. The M-score is clean; the *reason* it is clean is not reassuring.

**Receivables check:** AR grew faster than revenue only once (+12.19% vs. +7.56% in FY2023), then reversed hard (-3.4% in FY2024, -27.5% in FY2025). No pattern. **Inventory:** not applicable (services). **Capitalisation:** capex $493M (2021) → $618M (2025), a smooth ~7–8% of revenue with no suspicious step-up. **No evidence of expense capitalisation abuse.**

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment: **HIGH and worsening**

- GPN normalized organic growth of **~4%** versus **Shift4 at +49% gross revenue less network fees (Q1 2026)**, and **Adyen expanding its Toast partnership into the US (June 2026)**. Adyen was named a Forrester Wave Leader for Merchant Payment Providers in 2026.
- Worldpay — acquired for ~$24B from FIS — is a *share loser* being bolted onto a *share loser*. FIS could not grow it; GPN is buying a business whose historic value was scale in a commoditising rail, not software differentiation.
- The Genius platform is the bull's hope, but it is being launched into a market where Toast, Square and Shift4 already own the integrated-software channel.
- The travel portfolio, now explicitly called out as a drag, is GPN's most differentiated vertical — and it's the one exposed.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**The bear case is real, but it is not the case I was hired to find.**

There is **no forensic smoking gun**. Cash conversion of 1.9x, negative accruals, an M-score of -3.06, receivables lagging revenue, no capitalisation games, no auditor churn. Anyone shorting GPN on "accounting fraud" is going to get run over.

The credible short thesis is a **capital-allocation and structural-decline thesis**:
1. GPN earns ~$0 GAAP per share while marketing $13.70. The gap is amortisation of businesses bought because organic growth stalled at 4%.
2. That M&A engine is now debt-funded at >3x net leverage, with interest expense up ~60% and $7.5B of buybacks prioritised over deleveraging.
3. The equity story's historical EPS engine — a 18% share-count reduction — is now running in reverse (+8% shares in Q2 2026). **Adjusted EPS growth must now come from real operations, and management just cut the only operational metric that matters.**
4. Tangible equity is ~$2.5B against $23.8B book; another Worldpay-sized impairment is entirely plausible and would be the third in five years.
5. Market-share maths is unforgiving: 4% organic in a market compounding high-single-digits.

**Now the other side, stated fairly — and it is strong:**
- **6.6x adjusted EPS and an ~8.7% FCF yield** on a business with 42% adjusted operating margins and an S&P 500 index membership. That is deep-value territory, not bubble territory.
- Worldpay was bought at roughly **7x EBITDA** — a genuinely low entry multiple with obvious cost-synergy runway. Synergy capture is the highest-probability source of upside.
- Deleveraging from ~3.5–4x to 3x on growing EBITDA is achievable without equity issuance.
- Beta of 0.81, a 47-year operating history, ~$1.1% dividend yield — this is a defensive cash generator, not a melting ice cube.
- The stock has already de-rated from $116 (Nov 2024) to $90 — much of the damage is in the price.

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **NO EDGE — Reject Short Thesis**

I am declining to recommend a short. Three reasons, in order of weight:

1. **The forensics are clean.** Accruals, cash conversion, receivables, capitalisation and the Beneish model all read high quality. Shorting a 1.9x cash-converting, negatively-accruing business on accounting grounds is a losing trade.
2. **The valuation already discounts the bear case.** At 6.6x adjusted EPS with an ~8.7% FCF yield, the market has priced in the leverage, the 4% growth and the integration risk. The stock is up ~45% off the June 2026 low ($62.06 → $94.56 on 13 Aug) — my short would be entered *after* the crowd already capitulated and reversed. Bad entry, bad risk/reward.
3. **No catalyst with a dated horizon.** Impairment risk is real but management controls the timing and can lean on adjusted metrics indefinitely. "Eventually the buybacks look stupid" is not a short thesis; it's an observation.

**What would turn me short — the trigger set to monitor:**
- Normalized organic growth breaking below **3%** on a like-for-like basis (would confirm genuine share loss rather than travel softness)
- Net leverage failing to fall in FY2027 (would prove the buyback is cannibalising the balance sheet)
- A goodwill impairment in **continuing** operations at Worldpay — the moment the market stops believing the adjusted-EPS construct
- Any further equity issuance, or a dividend cut
- Adjusted EPS guidance cut with buybacks maintained

**Positioning conclusion:** This is a **value trap with a leveraged balance sheet and a genuinely poor capital allocator**, not a fraud. I would neither short it nor own it. The single best forensic takeaway for the portfolio is not "short GPN" — it is that **GPN's ~$4.1B of buyback value destruction at a $166 average cost is a live case study in why management capital-allocation records should be scored as rigorously as income statements.** Apply that lens to the rest of the financials sleeve.
