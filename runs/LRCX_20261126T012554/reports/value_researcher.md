# Lam Research Corporation (LRCX) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $185.01 | Market Cap: $233B | EV: $232B | Diluted shares: 1.261B | Net cash: $1.46B**
*Data as of Nov 25, 2026 (FY ends late June)*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **WIDE** (cyclical)

Lam is one of three companies on earth (with Tokyo Electron and Applied Materials) that can supply leading-edge deposition and etch. In conductor etch (Kiyo) and tungsten ALD (ALTUS) it is effectively an incumbent standard — every leading-edge logic and memory node is qualified on Lam tools, and requalifying a new vendor costs a fab 12–18 months of yield risk. That is a genuine **switching-cost + intangible-asset moat** (installed base of ~75,000 chambers also drives a high-margin recurring service/upgrade annuity).

**Evidence the moat is real and widening:**
- Gross margin has expanded **+550bps over 5 years** (45.2% FY19 → 50.5% FY26) *through* a full cycle — pricing power, not commodity behavior.
- Operating margin 23.7% (FY17) → 35.3% (FY26).
- ROIC has never dropped below ~31% in the last four years, and reached **49.4% in FY26** — 3–4x cost of capital, sustained.

**Evidence the moat is narrower than it looks:** WFE spending is set by ~5 customers (TSMC, Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron, Intel) and by memory capex, which is itself wildly cyclical. China has been ~30%+ of revenue — a policy variable, not a competitive one. No moat protects a business whose customers stop building fabs.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — price embeds optimism, not margin of safety

**Current multiples:** 32.1x trailing FY26 EPS ($5.76); ~27.6x TTM EPS (~$6.70, rolling in the Sept-26 quarter); 18.7x book; 26.6x EV/EBITDA; **FCF yield 2.1%**; owner-earnings yield 2.9%; dividend yield 0.64%. vs. semiconductor peer median P/E of 27.3x → **17.7% premium**.

**Normalized earnings power.** This matters more than any trailing multiple. Annualizing Q1 FY27 puts revenue near $33B — roughly **2.2x the FY24 trough** and probably at or beyond a cycle peak. A "through-cycle" Lam earns something closer to $6.5–7.5B of owner earnings (FY24–FY26 average OE is $5.2B; FY26 alone $6.7B). I model from a **$7.0B normal base** and test growth/discount:

| Intrinsic value/share (WACC 9%, terminal 3%, +$1.46B net cash) | 8% growth | 10% growth | 12% growth | 14% growth |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base $6.0B OE | $121 | $141 | $164 | $191 |
| Base $7.0B OE | $142 | $166 | $197 | $230 |

**Intrinsic value estimate: ~$150/share (range $125–190).** At $185, LRCX trades at a **~20% premium to my central estimate**. To justify $185, you must believe Lam compounds owner earnings at **~13–14% for a decade** off an already-inflated base — roughly 2x nominal GDP, in a business that has historically produced mid-teens revenue CAGR (14.7% over 10 years) *with two 15–25% drawdowns inside that window*.

**The bear case the market is now pricing:** the stock is **−57% from its June 30, 2026 peak of $432.87**, driven by the AI/tech derating, China export-control fears, and expectations of Street estimate cuts. Note the sell-side targets ($290–$435, set Oct 22 when the stock was $230–260) are stale and lagging — they are not a valuation anchor. A 57% decline is not the same thing as cheap: at the old peak Lam traded at ~45x peak-cycle earnings. Even after the crash it is at 19x *forward-peak* FY27 EPS (~$9.8) and 27x *mid-cycle* EPS. **The multiple compressed; the earnings base did not.**

## 4. Management Quality — **Good, with one mark against**

- **CEO Tim Archer** — 25+ year Lam veteran, internal promotion, deep process-technology credibility. Operationally excellent: margins expanded in both up- and down-cycles, and R&D has been defended through troughs (which is why the current cycle's share gains happened).
- **Capital allocation:** disciplined and shareholder-aligned in structure — FY26 returned $5.12B ($3.85B buybacks + $1.27B dividends) against $4.89B FCF, i.e. slightly over 100% of FCF. Dividend raised every year (quarterly now $0.33, +27% YoY). Net cash maintained throughout. No empire-building M&A — goodwill/intangibles are just 8% of assets.
- **The blemish:** buybacks ran heavily through FY26 as the stock rose from $96 to $433 — repurchasing at 30–45x earnings. Lam is a superb business but an indifferent buyer of its own shares. I'd rather they had bought aggressively in FY24 at $75.
- **Gap:** I could not retrieve current insider ownership or the DEF 14A. Archer's track record and the absence of value-destructive deals support a positive read, but I flag this as unverified.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate; trim into strength)

Lam Research is a **wonderful business at a full price**. The moat is wide, the returns on capital are among the best in American industry, and the balance sheet is pristine — this is exactly the kind of franchise Buffett would want on a watchlist. But two things disqualify it as a purchase today:

1. **No margin of safety.** $185 vs. ~$150 central intrinsic value. My framework demands a 20–30% *discount*; I am being asked to pay a 20% *premium*. A 2.9% owner-earnings yield is not compensation for cyclical risk.
2. **Earnings are at/near a cyclical peak.** Buying a WFE name at 27x TTM when revenue has doubled in 24 months is the classic value trap: the P/E looks reasonable precisely because the "E" is about to be questioned. WFE has never avoided a downturn, and China policy is an unhedgeable tail risk.

**Actionable levels:**
- **Buy zone: $105–125** (22–30% discount to intrinsic value; ~2x normalized book-plus, 11–13x peak EPS). This is where the risk/reward inverts.
- **Hold / trim: $160–200** (today).
- **Avoid new money above $200.**

If you already own it, the franchise quality argues for holding through the cycle rather than trading it — but the position should be sized for a 30–40% drawdown, not a straight line.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to owning it (why the price may fall further):**
1. **AI capex digestion.** Lam's revenue is levered ~3–4x to WFE. A 25–30% WFE correction (historically routine) takes FY27-style earnings of ~$9.8 down to $6–6.5 → the stock at $185 would be 28x trough-cycle EPS.
2. **China export controls.** China has been ~30%+ of revenue; further restrictions or a domestic-substitution push (NAURA, AMEC) is a permanent, non-cyclical haircut.
3. **Customer concentration in memory.** SK Hynix/Micron/Samsung are ~45%+ of revenue; a memory price correction would hit Lam before any other equipment maker.
4. **Multiple compression is not finished** if the market re-rates semis from "AI growth" to "cyclical."
5. **Buyback timing** — repurchases at 30–45x earnings destroy value if the cycle turns.

**Risks to my cautious view (what would make me wrong):**
1. If leading-edge logic *and* HBM/DRAM capacity additions run at current levels for 5+ years (i.e., this is a secular step-up, not a cycle), 12–14% OE growth is achievable and $185 is fair — even cheap versus $300+ sell-side targets.
2. Gross margin at 50.5% and rising suggests a structurally larger service/installed-base mix than prior cycles, which would raise the sustainable base above my $7.0B assumption.
3. A $10–15B annual buyback capacity in a downturn could compound per-share value faster than my model assumes.

**Bottom line:** A 9/10 business at a 5/10 price. Great company, wrong entry point. Wait for Mr. Market's cyclical despair rather than his current anxiety.
