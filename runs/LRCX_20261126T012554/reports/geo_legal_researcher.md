## Lam Research (LRCX) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Prepared from SEC filings, company results releases, and news/web sources; latest data as of late November 2026.*

---

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

Lam operates in seven reported regions. Revenue recognition is by customer fab location, so "China" captures domestic Chinese fabs (incl. restricted buyers), while Taiwan/Korea reflect the AI-driven leading-edge buildout.

**FY2026 (ended 6/28/26, total revenue $23.24B)** — source: Bullfincher compilation of FY2024–FY2026 10-K geographic disclosures

| Region | FY2026 Revenue | % of Total | FY2025 % | Risk Level | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **China** | $7.86B | **33.8%** | 33.7% | **Very High** | Direct target of US EAR export controls (14nm logic equipment); Affiliates/50% Rule exposure; MATCH Act overhang |
| **Taiwan** | $5.22B | **22.5%** | 18.7% | **High** | Cross-strait military/blockade risk; TSMC concentration; single-point-of-failure geography |
| **Korea** | $4.51B | **19.4%** | 22.4% | **Medium-High** | Samsung/SK Hynix concentration; active patent litigation vs. Korean suppliers; allied export-control alignment pressure |
| **Japan** | $2.17B | 9.3% | 10.2% | Medium | Stable rule of law; but TEL is a direct competitor and Japanese METI alignment is a MATCH Act lever |
| **United States** | $1.53B | 6.6% | 7.5% | Low | Home market, but only 6.6% of revenue — a striking underweight |
| **SE Asia** | $1.25B | 5.4% | 4.5% | Medium | Singapore/Malaysia backend fabs; moderate |
| **Europe** | $0.70B | 3.0% | 3.1% | Low-Medium | Small; GDPR/data-privacy exposure limited by revenue size |

**Latest quarter (Q1 FY2027, ended 9/27/26):** Revenue **$8.22B (+54% Y/Y)**, gross margin 52.3% — but China **rebounded to 35%** (from 26% a year earlier), with Korea 20% and Taiwan 18% (**73% combined**) per the company's results release (PRNewswire, 10/21/26).

> **Key insight:** China concentration has *not* structurally declined despite two years of tightening controls (42.2% FY2024 → 33.7% FY2025 → 33.8% FY2026 → 35% in Q1 FY27). Lam's stated guidance that China would fall "below 30% of revenue in 2026" is, on the latest print, not being achieved. Geographic risk is therefore rising, not falling.

**Customer concentration:** four customers represented **55% of FY2026 revenue** (Olympus Intelligence brief citing FY2026 10-K). This amplifies geography risk — a single Chinese or Korean customer restriction moves the model.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk #1 — China export-control escalation / Affiliates ("50%") Rule snap-back**
- *Probability: High (~70% of some tightening in next 12 months). Impact: High.*
- The US–China trade truce that suspended the BIS **Affiliates Rule** — which extends Entity List prohibitions to unlisted firms ≥50%-owned by blacklisted entities — was extended **through January 10, 2027** (Legal500/BIS reporting). Without a further Federal Register notice, the rule "is written to return automatically."
- Commerce has already converted a year of private warning letters into **binding EAR text**, restricting China's access to equipment for **14nm logic** and tightening AI-processor licensing — explicitly targeting **Lam, Applied Materials and KLA** (Finks, 10/12/26).
- Quantified damage already disclosed: Lam guided to **~$200M** hit to China sales in one quarter and **~$600M** to full-year 2026 revenue. AMAT guided ~$600M; KLA $300–350M.
- Market validation: on **11/25/26**, "fresh China chip curbs" drove LRCX **-7%** and AMAT -6% (24/7 Wall Street).

**Risk #2 — Taiwan Strait / cross-strait disruption**
- *Probability: Low near-term (<10% annually). Impact: Severe (existential).*
- Taiwan is 22.5% of FY2026 revenue and 18% of the latest quarter, concentrated in leading-edge logic where Lam's etch/deposition share is highest. Taiwan is also tightening its own AI-chip export controls toward China (CommonWealth, 10/20/26), which adds regulatory layering but also signals alignment with Washington.
- A blockade or conflict would simultaneously hit revenue, supply chain, and the entire AI capex thesis. Not modelled in sell-side numbers.

**Risk #3 — MATCH Act and allied-alignment legislation**
- *Probability: Medium-High (TS Lombard: passage is "when," not "if," most likely via FY2027 NDAA). Impact: Medium — and directionally ambiguous.*
- **MATCH Act (H.R.8170**, introduced by Rep. Baumgartner, 4/2/26) would restrict SME exports and **pressure the Netherlands and Japan to match US curbs on China**, with the Senate version directing Commerce to impose controls itself if allies don't comply. TS Lombard judges **ASML and Tokyo Electron more exposed than US toolmakers** — i.e., MATCH is a relative *share-shift positive* for Lam even as it is an absolute volume negative.
- Timing risk: a government-shutdown fight around the **Dec 11, 2026** funding deadline could push enactment into early 2027.
- Compounding: China has issued **"blocking rules"** to shield Chinese entities from extraterritorial US law (Taipei Times, 11/25/26) — creating genuine legal-conflict risk for a US company with 35% China revenue.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Delaware Chancery — *Turner v. Lam Research Corp.*, C.A. No. 2024-1308-KSJM (Mar. 20, 2026):** derivative/stock-award claim (reported as a "~$25 million lesson" on inquiry notice) **dismissed** in Lam's favor. Resolved, no material accrual indicated.
- **Patent litigation — offensive campaign in Korea:** Lam has filed **12 patent lawsuits against domestic (Korean) companies between 2020 and 2026, nine of them after [2022]**, prompting Korean press coverage of strain on domestic semiconductor suppliers (Chosun Ilbo, 1/14/26). This creates **counterclaim and antitrust/abuse-of-IP exposure** in a jurisdiction worth ~19% of revenue. *Note: I could not retrieve the article body — headline and search snippet only.*
- **Federal Circuit activity:** *In re Lam Research Corp.*, No. 25-1534 (nonprecedential order, Dec. 11, 2025); *Lam Research Corp. v. Inpria Corp.*, No. 25-1872 (Fed. Cir., filed 6/18/25). IP disputes with photoresist/adjacent-material suppliers — routine for the sector, immaterial individually.
- **Regulatory posture:** Lam's primary regulator is **BIS (Commerce)** — not the FTC/DOJ. Export licensing, deemed-export rules, and the new **remote-access rule** on advanced chips are the operative constraints. No evidence found of an active DOJ/SEC/FCPA investigation.

---

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **China revenue re-acceleration is the core red flag.** China at 35% of revenue *while* the US writes explicit 14nm tool restrictions into the EAR raises the question of **which Chinese customers** are driving the 9-point Y/Y mix increase. Any shift toward restricted or Affiliates-Rule-covered buyers would be a serious disclosure/enforcement issue.
2. **Distributor/intermediary and smuggling-adjacent risk.** The November 2026 Nvidia smuggling scandal has re-energized enforcement attention on diversion of advanced technology into China. Lam's systems are not chips, but "know-your-customer" and diversion diligence for Chinese fabs is an active enforcement theme.
3. **Legal-conflict exposure (US EAR vs. China blocking rules).** A US person complying with US export controls may be exposed under Chinese blocking statutes — a genuine, if low-probability, penalty scenario.
4. **Customer concentration (55% from four customers)** is a disclosure and negotiating-leverage risk as much as a compliance one.
5. **No FCPA/AML/data-privacy flags identified.** Europe is only 3% of revenue, limiting GDPR materiality. I found no bribery or sanctions-evasion matter in the public record — an absence of evidence, not proof of absence.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Justification:** 73% of revenue sits in China + Korea + Taiwan; the single largest region (China, 33.8–35%) is the direct object of a binding, actively tightening US export-control regime that names Lam explicitly in its scope; and roughly **$600M of FY2026 revenue has already been lost** to those rules with a further legislated escalation (MATCH Act) likely in the FY2027 NDAA. Countervailing factors that keep this from "Very High": Lam is a **US person** with policy influence and is *relatively* advantaged versus ASML/Tokyo Electron under MATCH; the litigation docket is modest and the one Delaware matter resolved favorably; and end-demand (AI capex, WFE guided to mid-$150B in 2026, >$200B in 2027) is powerful enough that Lam grew 54% despite the restrictions.

**What would move the rating:** to *Very High* — Affiliates Rule snapping back on Jan. 10, 2027 without replacement, or Chinese revenue moving >40%. To *Medium* — a durable bilateral truce, MATCH imposing allied parity (leveling the field against ASML/TEL), and China mix falling durably below 25%.
