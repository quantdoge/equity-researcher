# Sector Brief — Goldman Sachs (GS)

## 1. Sector Overview
Goldman Sachs operates in **Financial Services — Capital Markets** (FMP sector: Financial Services; industry: Financial – Capital Markets). The sector is dominated by a small oligopoly of G-SIBs — JPMorgan, Bank of America, Citi, Morgan Stanley and Goldman — that together control the majority of the global investment-banking (M&A, ECM/DCM underwriting) fee wallet and institutional trading flows. After a two-year fee trough (2022–2023), the sector is in the **early-recovery phase of its cycle**: advisory backlogs and IPO pipelines are rebuilding, equity and credit trading remain structurally strong, and GS itself guides to a **record M&A year in 2026**. Sector-aggregate revenue (top-3 banks) grew only ~0.9% in FY25 to $1.02T — top-line growth is muted because falling rates compress interest income, but *fee-based* businesses (IB, wealth/asset management) are inflecting higher, which is where Goldman earns its premium.

## 2. Key Tailwinds
- **M&A/ECM renaissance:** GS's advisory backlog is at record highs and management projects 2026 could be the biggest M&A year in history; its #1 global IB fee-wallet position and leadership in equity underwriting (record equity franchise in Q4'25) are direct revenue levers.
- **Steady-fee diversification:** Record assets under supervision of **$3.45T** in Q3'25; Asset & Wealth Management is scaling toward a more repeatable, less cyclical earnings mix.
- **Capital-relief regulation:** The 2026 Basel III Endgame re-proposal cuts the incremental RWA hit by an estimated **9–19%** vs the 2023 draft — easing the capital drag on GS's balance-sheet-intensive businesses and supporting buybacks (share count fell ~6% in FY25).

## 3. Key Headwinds
- **Top-line flatness:** Gross revenue fell **-1.4% YoY in FY25** (to $125.1B) as net interest income compressed; growth now depends on volatile markets/fee flows rather than a rising rate tailwind.
- **Compensation cost inflation:** SGA rose **+13%** YoY in FY25 ($19.6B) — talent competition and comp-to-net-revenue pressure cap operating leverage even as revenues normalize.
- **Cyclicality & valuation risk:** Trading/IB results are inherently lumpy (2021's record levels will not repeat), and at ~$902/share with market cap ~$266B, the stock trades at/near all-time-high multiples — limited margin of safety if the 2026 M&A "renaissance" disappoints.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Strong**
Goldman was the **fastest-growing of the big-5 banks in FY24 (+17.0%)** and in FY25 delivered the **best bottom-line inflection at scale**: net income +20.3% to a record $17.2B and diluted EPS +26.6% to $51.32, with operating margin improving to 17.5% (from 14.5%). It is structurally **#1–2 in global IB fees** (M&A + equity underwriting share gains) and its fee-heavy model carries higher earnings sensitivity to the 2026 M&A cycle than universal-bank peers. The offset is a **weak FY25 revenue print (-1.4%, worst-tied with Citi)** on interest-income compression and gross margins below JPM/MS on a revenue-mix basis — reflected in its premium but cyclical valuation. On balance, GS is the highest-quality *pure-play* capital-markets franchise among the comparables.

## 6. Sector Rating: **Attractive**
The capital-markets sector is in an early-cycle fee recovery — record advisory backlog, rising IPO pipeline, and a more benign Basel III regime argue for multiple years of double-digit IB/wallet growth off the 2023 trough, with GS the best-positioned pure play. The offsetting factors (flat gross revenue ex-fees, elevated valuations, comp-cost stickiness) justify a selective, quality-biased exposure rather than broad sector beta.
