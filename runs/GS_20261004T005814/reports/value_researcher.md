# Investment Memo — The Goldman Sachs Group, Inc. (GS)
**Price: ~$902.56 | Market cap: ~$266B | Analysis date: early October 2026**

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Wide (with cyclical caveats)**

Goldman Sachs is one of the four or five most consequential financial institutions on earth, and it earns a wide moat in its two core franchises:

- **Investment Banking (#1–2 advisor globally).** Persistent top-tier share in M&A and underwriting is the definition of franchise value: Fortune-500 CFOs and private-equity sponsors pay for Goldman's brand, relationships, and execution precisely because the fee premium is a rounding error against the cost of a failed transaction. Switching costs are real. This is the clearest "intangible asset + switching cost" moat in the firm.
- **Global Markets (top-tier market maker).** Scale begets liquidity, liquidity begets flows, flows beget market share — a genuine network effect. GS is a designated primary dealer and a top-3 rates/equities derivatives house. Post-2008, capital requirements and balance-sheet capacity became a *barrier to entry* that favors the incumbents (GS, MS, JPM) and starves challengers — the moat has actually widened versus 2008-era peers.
- **Asset & Wealth Management (growing fee engine).** AWM revenue reached $4.6B in Q2 2026, +20% YoY, and private-markets / wealth platforms (including the consumer wind-down redirect) are shifting the mix toward durable, recurring fee income — the single most important strategic development of the last five years. This narrows the earnings volatility that historically defined the stock.

**Moat trend:** Widening. Five years ago GS was a trading firm with a consumer detour (Marcus/GreenSky) bleeding capital and credibility. Today it has exited consumer lending, rebuilt around IB + Global Markets + AWM/platform solutions, and set an industry-leading return-on-tangible-equity target (~17%). The residual caveat: a large share of profits still comes from trading P&L that is inherently cyclical and, in a severe downturn, vulnerable — this is a wide-moat firm, not a toll bridge.

- **Earnings predictability: the core weakness.** 2021 peak ($21.6B) → 2023 trough ($8.5B) → 2025 ($17.2B) → 2026 likely $20B+. This is a 300% earnings swing inside five years. Trailing GAAP multiples on peak-year earnings flatter valuation, which is the central trap for value investors here.
- **FCF is not usable for this company** (trailing FCF −$47.2B; FCF yield −16.5%): as a bank, operating cash flow is dominated by trading-account working capital. The proper cash metric is **owner earnings ≈ $17.3B (2025) ≈ $54/share** — comfortably above the 80% conversion benchmark.
- **Balance sheet / capital:** total equity $125B, CET1 well above regulatory minimums, leverage ~14.5x assets/equity (normal for a G-SIB and down from pre-2008). Book value per share $393.49, tangible book ≈ $372.
- **Capital return:** 2025 buybacks $12.4B + dividends $5.3B ≈ 100%+ of net income; dividend raised 4 straight years ($2.00→$5.00/quarter by Sept 2026, ~2.0% yield); share count cut ~12% since 2021. Rank-and-file discipline is excellent.
- **Score rationale:** high quality (7.5/10) — exceptional franchise returns on tangible book (~14% FY25, ~17–19% run-rate), fortress capital, generous and sensible capital return; docked points for inherent earnings cyclicality and a leverage profile that demands the equity of a careful owner.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **Fairly valued; no margin of safety**

- **Multiples:** P/E 17.6x on FY2025 EPS; **~13.9x on TTM EPS of ~$64.78**; P/B **2.29x** on $393 book (≈2.2x on inflated 2026 book); EV/EBITDA 11.3x; dividend yield ~2.0%.
- **Peer context:** GS's premium to JPM on book value is not justified by ROE (JPM earns ~18–19% ROE at a similar ~2.2x book; GS earns ~14% FY25). It trades in line with Morgan Stanley on both P/B and P/E. The low/incomplete peer data from the feed precluded a rigorous median comparison, so this uses known sector references.
- **Intrinsic value (owner-earnings / P-Bv-RoE cross-check):**
  - *Base case:* normalized through-cycle owner earnings ~$45–50/share (blending the 2025 real of $54 with the 2023 trough of $32), a 12–14x mid-cycle multiple, or equivalently 1.8–2.1x tangible book on a ~15% ROTE → **intrinsic value ≈ $750–880, midpoint ~$820.**
  - *Bull case* (durable fee mix lifts mid-cycle ROE to 17–18%): ~$950–1,000.
  - *Bear case* (market/trading downturn normalizes EPS toward $40–45): ~$600–650.
- **Current price vs. intrinsic:** At ~$902, GS sits **at or modestly above** my base-case intrinsic value — *no margin of safety*, and a 20–30% discount to value is nowhere in sight. The July 2026 spike to $1,150+ (after the Q2 EPS blowout of $20.98) was a euphoric print; the ~22% pullback since has merely restored fair value, not created opportunity. At 2.2–2.3x book for a 14%-ROE, cyclically-peaked bank, the market is paying full freight for a perfect execution story.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good, improving (B+)**

- **CEO David Solomon (since 2018):** disciplined, strategically coherent owner-operator. Under his tenure GS exited the consumer experiment (Marcus/GreenSky wind-down 2023–24), reallocated toward fee-based Asset & Wealth Management and Platform Solutions, and committed to a 15–17% ROTE target. Execution is visible in the numbers: ROE 7.3%→~14% and rising; an investment-grade balance-sheet rebuild.
- **Capital allocation is genuinely shareholder-friendly:** ~100% payout of net income via dividends + buybacks at a *measured* pace, buybacks done at book-value-adjacent prices rather than chased — this is the behavior of disciplined allocators, not empire builders.
- **Caveats:** compensation consumes ~1/3 of net revenue (industry norm, but a constant tax on owners); key-person and talent-retention risk is structural; and at G-SIB scale, management has limited control over regulatory and macro forces that move the P&L more than any strategy decision.

## 5. Investment Verdict — **HOLD**

This is a wonderful business whose moat has demonstrably widened, run by a credible steward, delivering record capital returns. But the price is the problem: ~14x TTM EPS earned at a cyclical peak, 2.2–2.3x tangible book, ~0% margin of safety versus my ~$800 base-case intrinsic value, and ahead of a Q3 print (Oct 13) where consensus ($14.44) looks conservative after two blowout quarters — i.e., expectations are already high. **Discipline says: a fair price is not a buy.** I would become a buyer at ~$700–750 (≈1.7x book, ~11x normalized earnings, ≥15% discount to value); on any deep-cycle derating toward $600, it becomes a strong buy.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Earnings cyclicality is the load-bearing risk** — investment-banking fees, trading revenue, and AWM performance fees are all pro-cyclical. A macro downturn could halve earnings from the 2026 run-rate, and the high multiple on peak earnings would compress simultaneously (2018–2023 showed exactly this: EPS ranged $23–60).
2. **Leverage / systemic tail:** ~14.5x assets-to-equity and a $1.8T balance sheet mean a 2008-style credit shock — however unlikely under today's capital regime — would hit equity holders hard. Counterparty and derivatives concentration risk remains in Global Markets.
3. **Regulatory overhang:** G-SIB surcharges, Basel "endgame" rules, and CCAR stress-test outcomes can raise capital requirements and sap ROE exactly when the cycle turns.
4. **Valuation normalization:** the 2024–26 re-rating from ~1.2x to ~2.3x book presumes the new, higher fee-mix ROE is durable. If the AWM/platform growth story underwhelms or trading revenues revert, the multiple has a long way to fall.
5. **Competition at the margin:** in digital/retail-adjacent wealth and transaction banking, Goldman is a later mover; fee compression in traditional asset management is secular.
6. **Reputation/execution risk:** as a top advisor to everyone, GS is a magnet for headline risk (conflicts, regulatory settlements) that can tarnish the franchise premium.
