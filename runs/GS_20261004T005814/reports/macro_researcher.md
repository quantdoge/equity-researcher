# Macroeconomic Assessment — Goldman Sachs (GS)
**Sector:** Financial Services — Capital Markets | **Global Macro Analyst view · 4 Oct 2026**

## 1. Macro Environment Summary (Current Stance: **TRANSITIONAL — tilting risk-off**)

The US macro regime has pivoted mid-2026. Growth remains solid (real GDP $24.41T in Q2'26, ~2.2–2.5% annualized sequential), the labor market is still tight (unemployment 4.2% in Sep, but drifting up from 4.0% in early 2025), yet **inflation is sticky at ~3.0% and reaccelerating at the margin (headline CPI YoY 3.05% Aug-26), with an oil-price risk factor apparent**. The decisive development: after easing from 4.83% (Oct-24 peak) to ~3.63% in mid-2026, the Fed **turned hawkish in September 2026** (effective funds rate 3.75%), and the October hike was "pulled off the table only by a weak jobs report, with December still very much in play." Long-end Treasury yields are at **24-year highs** (10Y ≈ 5.0–5.3%, 30Y 5.37%) on a global bond selloff and rising term premium — yields have risen ~100bp since April 2026 even as policy rates were cut. The result is a positively sloped (2s10s +34bp, not inverted), steepening curve — no imminent recession signal, but a **higher-for-longer repricing regime** that is precisely the environment in which capital-markets equities (GS included) de-rate first. GS itself confirms the transmission: after peaking at $1,146 (14-Jul-26) on record Q2 earnings, the stock is down ~21% to **$902.56 (2-Oct-26)**, roughly flat YTD after a full round trip.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL (leaning cautious)**

The macro regime is a genuine two-sided coin for GS in Oct-2026, and the market is already pricing the headwind (−21% from the July peak). **Bull side:** record M&A pipeline, ECM/DCM fee momentum, an October hike now off the table, and volatility that is structurally bullish for Global Markets — roughly half the P&L. **Bear side:** higher-for-longer at 24-year high long yields, policy-rate re-tightening risk in December, sticky 3% inflation, a softening labor market, and heavy GS exposure to credit extension and long-duration marks. On balance: **Neutral**, with the duration/rate channel the swing factor.

## 5. One Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (Bear)

**The term-premium spiral / credit event.** Suppose the global bond selloff (Treasury supply, foreign official selling, oil spike) pushes the 10Y through **5.5–6.0%** while the Fed, facing two more 3%+ CPI prints, **hikes in December 2026 and again in H1-2027**. The 2s10s curve steepens further, credit spreads on leveraged finance, private credit and CRE widen sharply, and merger-financing costs spike — stalling the conversion of GS's record advisory/underwriting backlog (fees are earned at close, not announcement). GS's own long-duration marks (alternatives/PE/real estate) get marked down, and its financing book — expanded meaningfully into private credit — faces spread and tenor stress. GS equity, cyclically levered to volatile-markets revenue and already off its highs, retests the **$750–800** zone. The upside corollary: an inflation reprieve that rolls yields back from ~5.3% toward ~4.5% would re-rate GS back toward the $1,100+ zone — the speed of the two-sided reaction is the watch item into the December FOMC.
