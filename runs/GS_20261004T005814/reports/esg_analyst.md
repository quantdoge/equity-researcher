# ESG Analysis — The Goldman Sachs Group, Inc. (GS)
*Scope: Investment banking / capital markets; sector peer group: MS (direct comp), JPM, BAC, HSBC. Prepared from SEC/regulatory records, settlement disclosure, and news evidence.*

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **Medium (with negative trajectory)**

- **Operational:** Strong disclosure (CDP participation, supplier-engagement leaderboard); GS targeted net-zero operational emissions and 100% renewable electricity by 2025. Direct physical risk is low.
- **Financed emissions (the real exposure):** GS is among the largest financiers of fossil-fuel expansion in the US banking complex. It had NZBA-aligned 2030 sector targets for oil & gas, power, and autos, and faced As You Sow shareholder proposals demanding a credible climate-transition plan.
- **Commitment regression:** GS **exited the Net Zero Banking Alliance** (late-2025) — criticised by BankTrack and peers as "backsliding" — and **closed its Paris-Aligned Climate ETF (GPAL)** as ESG demand fell. This reduces transition-risk governance at exactly the moment financed emissions remain large; carbon-tax/Basel concentration risk is unhedged by public commitment.
- Carbon price/CBAM exposure: immaterial operationally (no industrial production); relevant indirectly through client underwriting.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **Medium**

- **Workplace culture:** GS is emblematic of the investment-banking hours problem; post-2021 scrutiny (analyst surveys documenting 100+ hour weeks) forced an ~80-hour cap and weekend-use reforms. The Leo Lukenas tragedy struck a peer (BofA) but intensified sector-wide scrutiny GS is in scope of. Reputation/attrition risk is real but GS has tightened controls.
- **Diversity:** GS had sector-leading diversity targets and a history of vocal DEI positioning — but the **Apple diversity-scorecard dispute** (2022, ending with the card partnership's termination and ~$10m arbitration) showed DEI demands can damage commercial relationships. Like peers, GS has since tempered ESG/DEI rhetoric under political pressure.
- **Community/license to operate:** Secondary to its wholesale business; consumer banking contraction (Card platform wind-down) is the main consumer-facing sore spot. No product-safety or supply-chain-labour issues of note.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**Strengths:** Single class of common equity (no dual-class, no poison pill); majority independent board that has been refreshed and expanded (post-2018 additions) with strong risk/audit committees; modern clawback policies; robust post-1MDB compliance remediation and FCPA monitorship; framework that passes annual Fed CCAR/DFAST since the 2011 failure.
**Weaknesses:** **CEO/Chairman duality (David Solomon holds both since Oct 2018)** — repeatedly flagged by proxy advisors; historically small board relative to universal-bank peers; CEO pay ($30m+ yr) with high internal pay ratio and substantial discretion in fairness reviews; legacy conduct record (1MDB) keeps GS on enhanced regulatory watch; abrupt retreat from climate coalitions introduces credibility risk with clients and regulators alike.
**Net:** Strong plumbing, weak cage-rattlers — an above-median but not best-in-class governance profile.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US:** SEC climate-disclosure rule (stayed/re-scoped under current administration — reporting burden low but uncertain); Fed G-SIB stress tests (CCAR/DFAST) and 2023 Fed climate scenario-analysis pilot — GS participates; Basel III endgame and living-wills keep compliance spend elevated.
- **EU/UK:** CSRD reporting obligations for GS's EU entities (indirect); **SFDR/SDR and FCA anti-greenwashing rules** apply to GSAM funds — GS has published SFDR disclosures but the GPAL closure trims EU ESG-fund footprint; **CBAM: immaterial** (no industrial production).
- **US anti-ESG state laws:** TX/FL-style exclusion lists — GS partially excluded from some state mandates; minor AUM impact, compliance drag.
- **Key watch item:** NZBA exit leaves GS without a coordinated financed-emissions commitment ahead of any CBAM/Fed climate-capital tightening — the gap between climate rhetoric and book composition is the single biggest forward ESG risk.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE (recently trending toward Laggard on E-commitment)**

- **Governance:** above-average controls, tarnished by 1MDB legacy and duality.
- **Social:** average-to-strong with culture stains.
- **Environmental:** disclosure-strong but commitment-weakening (NZBA exit, climate ETF closure) while financed emissions stay high — the weakest pillar and turning worse relative to MS/JPM.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **Neutral** for the thesis

ESG factors do not break the GS investment case: conduct risk is priced and contained post-1MDB (fines are sunk, compliance remediated), human-capital risk is managed, and operational E-footprint is immaterial. The negative watch-items — governance duality, NZBA exit, financed-emissions exposure to future carbon policy — are real but slow-burning and second-order to the firm's earnings power. **Monitor** (a) any new conduct investigations, (b) rollback-driven AUM outflows in Asset Management, and (c) carbon-regulation tightening on the wholesale book; none currently justify a negative override.
