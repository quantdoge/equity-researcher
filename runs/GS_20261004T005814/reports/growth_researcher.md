# GS — The Goldman Sachs Group, Inc. — Growth Equity Analysis

**Price $902.56 | Mkt Cap ~$266B | Ticker: GS | Sector: Financial Services (Capital Markets) | CEO: D. Solomon**

## 1. Growth Story

Goldman Sachs is a cyclical capital-markets franchise executing a deliberate repositioning: exiting low-ROE consumer lending (Apple Card transfer to JPMorgan, $20B of balances), doubling down on fee-rich asset/wealth management and transaction banking, and layering an AI productivity program ("GS 3.0") on top of a recovering M&A/ECM supercycle. The result is an earnings engine compounding at ~50% EPS CAGR over the past two years (2023→2025) with operating margin expanding from 9.9% to 17.5% — growth that is organic, cyclical-recovery-accelerated, and increasingly durable as platform/AuM revenue compounds and capital return accelerates.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **Investment Banking:** Global M&A fee pool is roughly $50–60B (2025 global M&A activity +41%) plus a ~$60–70B debt/equity capital-markets origination pool. GS holds a #1/#2 league-table position in global IB — penetration of the *recovering* part of the cycle is high; the opportunity is the cycle, not share gain (share leaders: Evercore/Centerview pressure on advisory).
- **Asset & Wealth Management:** ~$140T global wealth/AuM pool, projected to $200T+ by 2030. GS manages >$3T in AuM/AUS with a mid-single-digit share of the *addressable high-net-worth* segment — the largest penetration runway in the franchise.
- **Transaction banking / platform:** Small today relative to the $10T+ B2B payments market; a multi-year share-gain story.
- **Bottom line:** TAM is huge but mostly cyclical for IB, structural for wealth/AuM. The growth thesis rests on shifting revenue mix toward the recurring, fee-based 40% while harvesting the IB cycle while it is open.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY**

At $902.56 the stock trades at **13.2x 2026E consensus EPS** ($68.35) and ~12.5x 2027E — versus median analyst targets of ~$1,195 (~33% upside). The inconsistency is striking: the market is pricing a decelerating, mid-teens-multiple bank while delivering 20%+ EPS compounding (1H26 +54% YoY), a 100% beat history, and margin leverage still expanding. After the −22% pullback from the July peak, much of the froth is out. PEG ≈ 0.7–0.9 on consensus growth — cheap for the category; richer if 2027 EPS lands in the $75–80 range as the beat pattern suggests. I would not go **Strong Buy**: revenue remains hard-levered to capital-markets cyclicality, forward consensus already embeds deceleration, and a 51% twelve-month run (Jan'26) shows how quickly this stock can re-rate in both directions.

## 7. Key Risks

- **Revenue cyclicality (the big one):** ~60% of revenue is market-dependent (FICC, IB, equity trading). A 2022-style ECM/issuance freeze would knock revenue down 30%+ and crater EPS; the 2023–26 recovery is mid-cycle, not late-cycle, but the crash risk is structural.
- **Valuation re-rating risk:** At P/E ~17.6x trailing EPS, GS sits at the high end of large-bank multiples; if the beat stream normalizes, multiple compression alone is −20% downside (the July→Oct 2026 move previewed this).
- **Concentration of surprises in trading:** The +45% Q2'26 beat was FICC/trading-heavy — less durable and less controllable than recurring platform/AuM revenue.
- **Execution risk on consumer exit:** ~$1B discount + 24-month wind-down; legacy green-sky/litigation provisions could linger.
- **Competitive threats:** Independent advisory shops (Evercore, Centerview, PJT) keep taking M&A share on the highest-margin mandates; JPMorgan's balance-sheet scale looms in banking/lending cross-sell.
- **Regulatory/Basel:** Endgame capital rules continue to pressure ROE and cap balance-sheet leverage; any rate-shock episode (persistent Treasury-yield selling pressure flagged by BofA) hits trading revenue first.
- **Talent:** Rent for front-office talent is rising again in a hot M&A market; comp ratio creep would mute the AI-driven margin story.
