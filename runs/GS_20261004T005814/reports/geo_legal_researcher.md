# Goldman Sachs (GS) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

*Prepared for the investment memo. Sources: FMP company data, SEC/EDGAR references, Reuters, Financial Times, CFPB releases, Global Investigations Review, Bloomberg Law.*

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1. US–China decoupling / counter-sanctions — Probability: High | Impact: Medium**
China's 2026 counter-sanctions package (expanded anti-foreign-sanctions law, restrictions on extraterritorial US legal actions per Morrison Foerster analysis) plus US tariff/export-control escalation raises the risk profile of GS's China franchise and cross-border deal flow (revenge M&A bans, tech listings). GS is deliberately staying ("JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs stay in China but businesses shift," Reuters 10/2025) — this is a deliberate call that monetises China now, accepting contingency risk.

**2. Middle East escalation (Iran–Israel, Gulf) — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium**
Fragmentation of Gulf energy/security architecture directly hits GS's FICC commodity, FX and equities trading books, its $3.65Tn AUM across sovereign-wealth mandates, and EMEA revenues (~20%). Oil spikes compress risk appetite and widen funding spreads — volatility is net-positive for trading but net-negative for IB/AM.

**3. EU–UK–US regulatory fragmentation — Probability: High | Impact: Low-Medium**
Brexit-era divergence (EU re-proposes structural-separation-in-rings-fencing rules; UK's new senior-manager/surveillance regimes) plus a US administration softening Basel III Endgame while EU/UK hold the line creates compliance drag and capital asymmetry across EMEA. FCA/PRA already fined Goldman Sachs International £96.6M (2022) for 1MDB-related risk-management failures — regional regulators remain activist.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Enforcement in the last 12–24 months:**
- **CFPB — Apple Card (Oct 2025):** CFPB ordered GS to pay a **$45M penalty + ~$19.8M redress** (Apple separately $25M) for deceptive dispute handling — first targeted strike at GS's shrinking consumer platform.
- **SEC — GSAM ESG failures:** ~$4M fine for ESG research policies/document-retention failures (2024).
- **SEC — securities lending:** $15M settlement re: failed Regulation SHO locates.
- **US Fed:** public concerns over GS's FinTech partnership risk-management (GreenSky, Apple Card era) — written-agreement territory; monitorship likely to persist through the consumer wind-down.

**Legacy litigation (mostly de-risked):**
- **1MDB:** global $5.6B resolution (2020); US criminal case formally closed (2023); Roger Ng convicted/sentenced. **New wrinkle:** GS filed an LCIA arbitration claim against Malaysia alleging breach of the $3.9B settlement — a live but small contingent claim.
- **Aluminum price-fixing class action:** dismissed with prejudice on standing (2nd Cir., 2024).
- **Subprime-era actions:** fresh legal action surfaced in UK press — immaterial, cost-free provision status.
- **SVB:** DOJ/SEC scrutiny of GS's role in SVB's late capital raise remains open — an unresolved OCIO/underwriting conduct matter; no charges public.

**Capital/regulatory posture:** Final Basel III Endgame rules (July 2025) delivered ~4% lower CET1 impact than proposed; GS paired the result with a large buyback — this is the single biggest tail-risk item management has been de-risking.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **FCPA/AML track record:** 1MDB = the largest global corruption penalty ever applied to a bank ($5.6B). Residual monitorship, Roger Ng conviction, and the new Malaysia arbitration mean the bribery-theme tail is not fully closed.
- **Consumer regulatory cascade:** CFPB Apple Card order + Fed FinTech concerns + Marcus/GreenSky wind-down all point to fragmented consumer-compliance infrastructure being dismantled at speed — integration risk during exit.
- **ESG-labelling gap:** SEC fined GSAM for policies/procedures around ESG research — greenwashing-adjacent risk as SEC re-prioritises fund-labelling enforcement.
- **China legal arbitrage:** operating a 100%-owned onshore securities JV with direct PII/capital flows into a jurisdiction actively building an anti-foreign-sanctions and extraterritorial-liability regime (2026 regulations) — compliance and contingency exposure is concentrated in the highest-risk region.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **Medium**

**Rationale:**
- *Geopolitical:* LOW–MEDIUM — ~70% of revenue is US-domiciled; Russia fully exited; China exposure deliberately capped and managed.
- *Legal/Regulatory:* HIGH frequency of enforcement (CFPB, SEC, FCA/PRA, Fed) but **modest quantum** (recent fines ~$85–140M cumulative vs. 2025 net earnings of ~$14–16B) and declining legacy exposure (1MDB closed, aluminum dismissed, Basel tail de-risked).
- The dominant near-term risk is **domestic regulatory and litigation cost** (consumer wind-down, SVB/SEC conduct probes), not sovereign risk.

**Key watch items:** (1) resolution of SVB-related DOJ/SEC inquiry; (2) outcome of the Malaysia LCIA arbitration; (3) any expansion of China counter-sanctions to financial-services JVs; (4) Fed consent-order scope on FinTech partnerships.
