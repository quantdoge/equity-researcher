## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT** — but with high-conviction agreement *around* the edges

**Where agents genuinely agree (high confidence):**
- **Do not short.** Short, Quant, Value and Sector all independently land on "not a fraud, bad news largely priced."
- **Quality has deteriorated structurally, not cyclically.** Value, Fin Risk, Short, Quant and ESG converge on ROIC decay (20.3%→9.6%), inverted operating leverage, and a dividend no longer cash-covered.
- **The Q1 2027 separation is the whole thesis.** Growth, Sector, Non-Fin Risk, Macro and ESG all identify it as the dominant catalyst — and all five flag it as the dominant *risk*.

**Where they conflict (the actual debate):**

| Debate | Bulls | Bears | My resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cheap or value trap? | 16x adj. EPS, 0.70x sales, SOTP $145 | Price > IV midpoint $120; 2.45% FCF yield vs 5.15% 10Y | **Both true.** Cheap vs. peers, not cheap vs. its own cash flows or the risk-free rate. This is a **catalyst trade, not a value investment.** |
| Near-term direction | Separation + bid optionality | Alt data bearish into Feb 16; consensus 6% above guide midpoint | **Bears win the next 3–4 months.** Bulls win the 12–18 month horizon *if* the spin executes. |
| Governance | Board responded to Elliott | CEO conflict + ISS decile 8 | **Conflict is real and unmitigated.** It is a sizing input, not a pass/fail. |

**Fraud/forensic overlay:** the Short Analyst's forensic toolkit (Beneish, accruals, adjusted gross margin) **fails to confirm** the bear case. Per my conflict-resolution rule, the fraud flag is absent — so the bear case reduces to a leverage/cash-flow/capital-allocation case, which is a *sizing* problem, not an *exclusion* problem.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **1.0% of portfolio to initiate; 3.0% hard cap**

| Separation closes cleanly; Motion re-rates; NAPA bid firm | 25% | $158 | +28% | +7.0% |
| Separation closes; modest re-rating, dis-synergies absorb upside | 40% | $131 | +6% | +2.4% |

| Taxable spin / second impairment / covenant event | 10% | $82 | −34% | −3.4% |
| **Expected return** | | | | **+2.5%** |

**Kelly check:** E = +2.5%, σ ≈ 20% → full Kelly ≈ 0.6x leverage; frankly the edge is too thin to size aggressively. **Quarter-Kelly ≈ 5–6%.** Then apply explicit haircuts for the risk flags that Kelly cannot see:

- **−50% for separation execution + tax-free qualification risk** (binary, uninsurable, non-diversifiable within the name)
- **−50% for balance-sheet fragility + governance conflict** (CEO negotiating against his own future employer → adverse allocation of debt/pension is a live, unpriceable risk)

**→ 1.0–1.5% initial. I recommend 1.0%.**

This is deliberately below my own framework's "medium conviction / elevated risk = 1–3%" lower bound, because **four** independent risk flags (leverage, FCF coverage, governance conflict, spin-tax) all sit on the same event. Correlated risks of that type do not get a mid-range size.

**Factor exposure — the tension.** Beta is genuinely low (0.48 tool / 0.68 profile) but **realised volatility is 36.4%** with a −37.5% max drawdown and a **Sharpe of 0.12 (and −0.094 on the Sharpe/Sortino tool)**. So:
- ✅ *Reduces* portfolio market beta — defensive in a drawdown
- ❌ *Increases* portfolio volatility and adds a "quality-laggard / deep-value" tilt
- ❌ Adds to an **event-driven / special-situations** bucket — cap total bucket exposure at ~10%.

**Book it as event-driven, not as a defensive core holding.** Sizing it as a low-beta staples substitute would misprice its risk.

## 6. Risk Management Rules

**Hard stop (price):** weekly close below **$105** (−15%). That sits just under the July 21 intraday low of $108.65 and brackets the short analyst's $107 downside case. Cost at 1.0% weight: **−15bp of portfolio.** Do not use a tighter stop — 36% annualised vol will whipsaw it.

**Thesis invalidation (exit on any one — these override price):**
1. **Separation slips past mid-2027**, or the Form 10 misses its SEC review timeline.
2. **Taxable spin** — §355 opinion/IRS ruling fails or is qualified. Binary, company-changing.
3. **FY2026 adjusted EPS ≤ $7.50** (low end) or Q4 2026 GAAP EPS < $2.00 — confirms consensus ~$7.74 is unattainable.
4. **FY2026 FCF < $350M with the dividend held** — second consecutive debt-funded payout.
5. **Second goodwill impairment**, specifically at Motion (of the $4.95B, 106% of book equity).
6. **Net debt/EBITDA > 4.25x**, or **current ratio < 1.0** (currently 1.22).
7. **Debt allocation places >$5B on the ~$1.1B-EBITDA Motion stub** (>4.5x leverage on the lower-quality asset) — the specific governance-conflict outcome to watch.
8. **Annualised interest expense > $250M** (10Y at 5.15% makes refinancing the legacy low-coupon paper materially dearer than the 2.0% current effective rate implies).

**Trim triggers:**
- **$132** (+7%) — 90% of blended IV ($120–145). *Stop adding, hold.*
- **$140** (+13%) — trim **one-third**. The de-rating has happened; banking some catalyst premium is correct.
- **$152–$158** (+23–28%) — trim to a **runt position** or exit. At that level you are paid for the decision, not the execution.
- **On separation completion:** exit the pair rather than hold either stub, *unless* the Motion stub trades below 11x standalone EBITDA at the time of distribution.

**Position-level governance escalation (from Non-Fin Risk):** before T2, request from the Lead Independent Director written confirmation of (a) whether CEO Stengel recused from the debt/pension/overhead allocation and (b) whether his separation comp is tied to Motion's standalone valuation rather than total shareholder value. **If the answer is adverse, cancel T2 and reduce T1 to 0.5%.**
