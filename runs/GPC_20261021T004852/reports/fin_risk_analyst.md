## GENUINE PARTS COMPANY (GPC) — Financial Risk Assessment

**Data note up front:** GPC's FY2025 GAAP results contain a ~$1.0B non-cash charge (Q4'25: net income **–$609M**, EBIT **–$790M**, vs. operating income of +$48M). Every tool-computed ratio built off the feed's FY2025 EBITDA ($754M) is therefore **materially distorted** — reported EBITDA embeds the write-down while the company's own adjusted EBITDA is ~$2.0B (8.3% of sales). I present both and lead with adjusted.

**Altman Z correction:** the tool uses *book* equity (x4 = 0.27). Substituting market cap (~$17.0B / $16.84B liabilities = 1.01) lifts Z to **~2.16**, and normalising x3 back to operating income (+0.16) and x1 to the stronger current quarter (+0.07) gives **~2.4 — grey zone, not distress.** The "Distress" label is an artefact of the impairment and book-value x4.

**Ratings evidence gap:** no rating-agency data was retrievable from the tools; I could not verify the specific Moody's/S&P level, CDS spreads, or the debt maturity schedule. The IG call above is inferred from leverage, coverage and cash-flow metrics, not from a confirmed rating.

### 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue –20%

Base: TTM revenue $25,333M; TTM gross margin 37.7%; annualised Q3'26 operating income ~$1,351M; annualised interest ~$184M; D&A ~$550M.

| Line item | –10% revenue | –20% revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $22,800M | $20,266M |
| Gross profit lost @ 37.7% | –$955M | –$1,910M |
| SGA flex (15% variable) | +$114M | +$228M |
| **Operating income** | **~$510M** | **~(–$331M)** |
| EBITDA (add D&A) | ~$1,060M | ~$220M |
| Net debt / EBITDA | **~5.7x** | **~28x** |
| Interest coverage | 2.8x | negative |

**Why so brutal:** a 5% operating margin against a 37.7% gross margin means **each 1% of lost revenue destroys ~7% of operating profit**. Distributors carry high fixed occupancy/logistics costs; the flex is not there in year one.

**Three mitigants that genuinely matter:**
1. **Working-capital release.** NWC (~36% of revenue) unwinds in a downturn; a 20% revenue decline releases roughly **$1.5–1.8B** of cash over 4–6 quarters. Inventory is $6.4B and fell in the last downturn cycle. This is the primary survival mechanism and it is real.
2. **Defensive demand mix.** ~74% North America; the NAPA DIFM repair business is need-based, and Motion is MRO/consumable. A full 20% revenue decline is a severe assumption — FY2023–25 revenue growth of +4.8%/+1.7%/+3.5% shows low cyclicality in the observable window (though that window contains **no recession year**, a material data limitation for calibrating downside).
3. **Deleveraging already underway.** Net debt down $1.74B in nine months; if that pace holds, entry leverage into a downturn would be ~2.5x rather than 3.0x.

**Verdict on the scenario:** at –20% revenue, EBITDA of ~$220M against $6.06B net debt would **breach any conventional 3.5x leverage covenant** (which requires EBITDA ≥ ~$1.73B) and cut off the revolver exactly when needed. Survival would depend on the working-capital release and covenant waivers — not on earnings. At –10%, the company stays cash-generative but leverage of ~5.7x breaches investment-grade thresholds and likely triggers a downgrade. **A ~15% revenue decline is roughly the break-point for covenant comfort.**

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE (trending High into the Q1 2027 separation)**

This is a solvent, cash-generative, low-beta business with a 70-year dividend record and leverage that is genuinely ~3.0x — not the 10.35x the raw data implies. But it is a **thin-margin, high-operating-leverage distributor** carrying $6.8B of debt, $4.95B of goodwill, and sub-100% FCF dividend coverage, with a major structural event (two-way split) 1–2 quarters away. It survives a normal downturn; it does not survive a severe one without the balance sheet shrinking first.

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **~$1.0B non-cash charge in FY2025** (Q4'25 EBIT –$790M; FY EPS collapsed from $6.49 to $0.47). A write-down of this size is a verdict on past acquisition pricing, not a one-off nuisance.
2. **Goodwill + intangibles $4,950M = 23% of assets and 106% of book equity.** More impairment risk remains, particularly in the cyclical Industrial/Motion segment being carved out.
3. **Dividend not covered by free cash flow** — 134% FCF payout in 2025, funded with debt. The 70th consecutive raise (to $4.25) prioritises the streak over deleveraging.
4. **Debt up $2.5B in 2025** to $8.27B, driving interest expense +69%. Refinancing now occurs with the 10Y at **4.99%** and 30Y at **5.37%** — a structurally higher coupon than the maturing paper.
5. **~$200M per quarter of "other" operating charges in 2026** (~$615M annualised) sitting between gross profit and operating income, alongside the $100–150M incremental separation cost. Persistent "one-offs" mask true earning power.
6. **Quick ratio 0.46–0.54 and cash ratio 0.05–0.08** — liquidity depends on inventory turnover and revolver access, not on cash.
7. **Separation execution risk (Q1 2027):** two smaller, less-diversified entities will each carry a weaker credit profile than the consolidated whole, with dis-synergies and split-rating risk. The $3B market-value loss on announcement suggests the market is pricing cost, not value.
8. **Working capital drain** of –$380M in 2025; inventory +10% to $6.44B adds tariff/obsolescence exposure.
9. **Altman Z in the distress zone** (1.72) on book equity — relieved only by market-value and normalisation adjustments.
10. **Three-year Sharpe of 0.065** — no compensation for 32.5% volatility and a –37.5% drawdown.
