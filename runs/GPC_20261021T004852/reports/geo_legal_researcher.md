## GPC — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company:** Genuine Parts Company (NYSE: GPC) — NAPA automotive aftermarket + Motion industrial distribution. FY2025 net sales **$24.3B**; market cap ~$17.0B; ~65,000 employees. Two segments being separated: **Global Automotive (GPC)** and **Global Industrial (Motion)**, targeted **Q1 2027**.

---

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

FY2025 disclosed geographic mix: **~74% North America / ~16% Europe / ~10% Australasia** (10-K, filed 2026-02-20, per SEC 10-K summary). Segment mix: North America Automotive ~39%, International Automotive ~24%, Industrial ~37%. Country splits below are my decomposition of those disclosed regional buckets (company does not publish country-level revenue; treat as estimates).

| Geography | Est. revenue exposure | Basis | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | ~64% | NA Automotive + US Motion | **Low** (but tariff/import-cost sensitive) |

**Concentration read:** ~26% of sales are outside North America, and ~74% depend on the US consumer, US import policy, and USMCA. This is a *trade-policy* risk profile, not a classic EM/confiscation profile — no Russia, no China operations, no meaningful expropriation or currency-repatriation exposure.

---

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**#1 — US tariff policy on China, Mexico and Canada (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High)**
The 10-K explicitly states increased tariffs on imports from **China, Mexico and Canada have already affected costs and margins**. GPC is a distributor — it cannot fully reprice imported hardparts (brakes, filters, rotating electrical) without losing share to peers. Risk is asymmetric: tariff escalation hits gross margin (Q3 2026 gross profit $2.48B on $6.52B sales) while the company is simultaneously guiding down (2026 adj EPS narrowed to **$7.50–$7.75 from $7.50–$8.00**, 2026-10-20).

**#2 — European operations: Ukraine spillover, energy, FX (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium)**
The 10-K names "instability in the geopolitical environment, such as the conflict between Russia and Ukraine," as a supply-chain and European-operations disruptor, plus inflation, cyberattack and trade-policy risk. Europe (~16% of sales, AAG) has been the chronic margin underperformer and a driver of the multi-year restructuring ($231M charges in 2024; $193.4M in 9M 2026). A EUR/USD move is a direct EPS translation risk.

**#3 — Separation execution + leverage (Probability: Medium / Impact: High)**
The Q1 2027 split into GPC (auto) and Motion (industrial) is the single largest idiosyncratic event. Documented risks: tax-free treatment may not be obtained/upheld, one-time separation costs, dis-synergies, and **S&P's BBB- rating with negative outlook** on elevated leverage. A failed or taxable separation would be a materially negative event and would likely dominate any tariff-driven move.

*Honorable mention:* cyber risk — GPC runs 6,864 NA Auto + 3,199 International Auto locations on integrated ERP/DC systems; the 10-K flags a serious disruption to business processes and reputation.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Asbestos-related product liability — the key legal contingency.** FY2025 net income fell 92.7% to **$66M** (EPS $0.47 vs $6.47) on ~**$1.1B of pre-tax one-time charges**, including a **$742M pension settlement**, **credit losses**, and **asbestos-related product liability**. Asbestos exposure (legacy friction/brake and industrial gasket lines within Motion) is a recurring, long-tail liability requiring ongoing reserve adequacy assessment; it is the largest single legal item on the balance sheet narrative.
- **Restructuring** — $231M (2024), $150–180M guided (2025), $193.4M actual (9M 2026), $64.5M in Q3 2026 alone. Multiple tranches raise disclosure-control and accrual-quality questions, and have already drawn rating-agency scrutiny.
- **Pension settlement** — $742M annuity/termination charge; a funded-status and fiduciary-governance matter rather than litigation, but it materially reduced book equity.
- **Tax-free spin tax opinion** — an IRS private letter ruling / tax opinion is the gating regulatory step for Q1 2027; failure converts a non-event into a shareholder-level tax liability.
- **Antitrust/competition** — ~**50+ acquisitions in 2025** adding 250+ locations, heavily NAPA independent-store buy-ins in the US and Canada. Cumulative small-deal consolidation into a ~39%-of-sales NA auto network invites FTC/state AG and ACCC (Australia) scrutiny; none disclosed as active, but this is the live regulatory vector.
- **No disclosed DOJ/SEC/FCPA enforcement action** in the filings reviewed. Note this reflects the sources available to me — I could not retrieve the full 10-K risk-factor text (EDGAR browse/direct 10-K endpoints returned 403/no-result), so absence of disclosure is a gap, not a clean bill of health.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **FCPA / anti-bribery (Medium):** Operations in **Indonesia, Singapore, Mexico, Poland** plus 50+ annual acquisitions — third-party distributor and newly-acquired-entity integration is the classic FCPA failure mode. No violation disclosed; monitor.
2. **Asbestos reserve adequacy (Medium-High):** a fresh charge in FY2025 after years of "contained" messaging suggests reserve true-ups remain likely.
3. **Independent-store owner network / channel practices (Medium):** NAPA's owner-operated model plus aggressive buy-ins creates related-party and revenue-recognition/receivables quality questions (FY2025 credit-loss charge is a corroborating signal).
4. **Data privacy (Medium):** GDPR (EU/UK) and CCPA exposure across four continents of retail/repair-shop customer data; no breach disclosed.
5. **Sanctions (Low):** no Russia/Iran/China operations disclosed; a Russia exit from European auto distribution would have been immaterial.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Jurisdictions are almost entirely rule-of-law, high-income markets (US, Canada, Western/Central Europe, Australia/NZ) — no nationalisation, currency-control or sanctions exposure, which anchors the rating at Medium rather than High. What pushes it off "Low" is a real, already-realised **tariff cost channel** (China/Mexico/Canada), a structurally challenged **European** business sitting in a war-adjacent region, an unsettled **asbestos** tail, and the **Q1 2027 separation** concentrated into a BBB- credit profile.

**What would move it to High:** (a) a taxable/failed spin, (b) a step-change in US tariffs on Mexico or Canada auto parts (USMCA breakdown), or (c) a large adverse asbestos verdict/reserve charge.
