## Revolution Medicines (RVMD) — Value Analysis
*Price $196.55 (Oct 20, 2026) | Market cap ~$42.1B | EV ~$38.8B | ~214M shares*

**Framing note:** This is not a conventional value analysis. RVMD is a single-asset commercial-stage oncology company that began reporting product revenue only weeks ago. A trailing-FCF DCF is meaningless here (2025 FCF: −$914M; no revenue). What follows is a probability-weighted rNPV/scenario analysis, which is the only honest way to value a binary clinical asset.

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow (emerging)**

**What it owns:** Rasonque (daraxonrasib), an oral, pan-RAS(ON) "molecular glue" inhibitor — first-in-class mechanism. FDA-approved **Aug 26, 2026** for 2L metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma in patients ineligible for multiagent therapy, just ~5 weeks after a July 22 filing, enabled by a Commissioner's National Priority Voucher (plus Breakthrough and Orphan designations). EU is under phased review.

**Evidence the asset is real, not promotional:** Phase 3 RASolute 302 showed median OS of **13.2 vs 6.7 months** — the first time any experimental therapy has pushed median survival past one year in pancreatic cancer, presented in an ASCO plenary and described by clinicians as "practice changing." Benefit was seen in both RAS G12-mutant and broader populations, which widens the addressable pool beyond a single mutation. >2,000 patients were already on compassionate use before approval, evidence of genuine physician pull.

**Moat sources and durability:**
- *Intangible assets:* Composition-of-matter patents on the tri-complex RAS(ON) platform, first-mover position in pan-RAS, and a mechanism competitors cannot simply copy. This is a real, if time-limited, moat — 8–12 years of exclusivity, not decades.
- *Efficient scale / switching costs:* None in oncology. Physicians switch on data. There is no network effect.
- *Direction:* **Widening** — from preclinical in 2020 to approved standard-of-care candidate in 2026, with 1L PDAC, adjuvant PDAC and a Phase 3 NSCLC program behind it.

**Verdict:** Narrow moat. A durable pharma franchise requires multiple approved agents and label expansion over a decade; RVMD today is one molecule with one label. ROIC is not yet measurable (deeply negative). This sits at the edge of my circle of competence — I can underwrite the *science*, but not the *binary outcomes* that drive the price.

### 3. Valuation Analysis — Intrinsic value ≈ **$125/share** (range $50–$222)

**Anchors:** List price $39,800 per 30-day supply (~$484K/yr; ~$350–390K net of gross-to-net). Launch trajectory per RBC: **$28M (Q3'26), $148M (Q4'26), ~$1.1B by end-2027**; consensus ~$1.2B in 2028. Peak sales estimates: **PDAC alone $5–7.6B** (Evercore $7.4B, Leerink $7.6B, UBS $6–7B, RBC $5B+ US only); **RBC total worldwide $11.5B** across PDAC/NSCLC; Stifel sees $3.2–3.3B ex-US. My independent bottom-up on 2L PDAC (29K US-eligible patients, 55% peak share, $350K net, ~8-month real-world duration) lands at **$3B US / ~$5B global** — squarely mid-consensus for PDAC alone.

**Scenario model (discounted at 10%, net cash $3.3B added, future dilution subtracted):**

| Scenario | Prob. | Peak sales | NOPAT margin | Exit multiple | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear** — 2L PDAC only, tolerability/label friction, share caps ~35% | 30% | $3.5B (2031) | 30% | 13x | **$50** |
| **Base** — 2L+1L PDAC, modest NSCLC | 45% | $8.5B (2033) | 38% | 15x | **$121** |
| **Bull** — pan-tumor franchise incl. 1L PDAC, NSCLC, CRC | 25% | $14.0B (2034) | 42% | 17x | **$222** |

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $125/share.** Current $196.55 implies the market is paying for something between base and bull — approximately the **70th percentile outcome**. To justify today's price you need ~$13B+ peak sales, 40%+ NOPAT margins *and* a 17x exit multiple simultaneously. That is top-decile execution priced as the central case.

**Multiple reality check:** EV of ~$38.8B is **~7x base-case peak sales** and **~32x 2027E revenue (~$1.2B)**. Mature, diversified pharma trades at 3–5x sales. A 7x peak-sales multiple is only defensible for a durable, multi-indication franchise — which this is not yet.

**Peer comparison is uninformative:** RVMD's P/E is −33 (no earnings); the healthcare peer median of 31.7x is a category error. Book value multiple is 22.9x — reflecting zero tangible earnings power, not cheapness.

**Required entry for a 25–30% margin of safety: ~$88–$95.** Notably, that is roughly where the stock traded in the months before the approval re-rating.

### 4. Management Quality — **Above average (execution), unproven (capital allocation)**

- **Mark Goldsmith (CEO)** has run the company since 2014/2018-era formation and delivered a genuinely rare thing: a first-in-class mechanism taken from discovery to FDA approval in ~6 years, with a filing-to-approval interval of five weeks.
- **Regulatory and clinical execution is elite.** BTD, Orphan, National Priority Voucher, EU phased review — the company has extracted every available acceleration mechanism. Compassionate-use demand pre-approval shows strong KOL relationships.
- **Capital allocation is the weak link.** Serial equity issuance (share count +30% CAGR), ~$270M/yr SBC, and a $642M debt/royalty facility — rational for a biotech racing a binary catalyst, but it means per-share value creation lags enterprise value creation. No buybacks or dividends (appropriate), no evidence yet of disciplined M&A.
- **Solo commercialization in Europe** is a costly, unproven decision for a company with no commercial infrastructure; the alternative (partnering ex-US) would preserve capital and de-risk the model. Rumor of a takeout bid earlier this year suggests management is weighing strategic alternatives — a positive.
- Insider ownership data was not retrievable — **gap noted**; I would want to confirm insider alignment before owning this.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **AVOID / SELL (Trim if held)**

RVMD is a scientifically outstanding company and a mediocre investment at $196.55. The RASolute 302 result is a genuine breakthrough that will change pancreatic cancer treatment — and the market has already paid for it, twice over. My probability-weighted intrinsic value of **~$125/share** sits ~36% below the current price, and the *entire bull case* ($222) offers barely 13% upside. That is a deeply asymmetric risk/reward in the wrong direction: you are underwriting binary clinical, competitive and reimbursement outcomes with no margin of safety and no FCF to anchor you.

Applying my discipline: a business trading *above* fair value, with a narrow, single-asset moat and no predictable cash flows, is not a buy — it is a candidate to own only at a substantial discount. **I would be a buyer near $90–$100**, where the stock would trade at a 25–30% discount to base-case intrinsic value and the optionality on 1L PDAC and NSCLC becomes free.

I concede the two things that could make me wrong quickly: a **strategic acquisition** at a premium (a $10B+ peak oncology asset is exactly what large pharma buys, and the stock was already a rumored target), and **1L PDAC data** (RASolute 303) that would roughly double the addressable market. Neither is a value thesis — both are event risk.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Concentration risk / binary dependence.** Effectively one molecule, one approved label. Any safety signal in the real-world setting (rash, diarrhea, mucositis, fatigue are already common) would impair both revenue and the pipeline's read-through value. A single bad quarter of tolerability data can halve the equity.
2. **Narrow label limits the near-term TAM.** The approval is restricted to 2L patients *ineligible for multiagent therapy* — a subset of an already-small pool. Broadening depends on label expansion, not on launch execution alone.
3. **Real-world treatment duration is the silent killer of the model.** The $39,800/month price only matters if patients stay on drug. Median OS of 13.2 months in a trial population rarely translates to 13 months of commercial therapy in frail 2L PDAC patients; if average duration is 5–6 months, revenue per patient halves versus consensus.
4. **Competition in RAS is intense.** Every major oncology player and a dozen biotechs are pursuing KRAS G12D/G12C/pan-RAS. First-mover advantage is real but not permanent; a better-tolerated follow-on could compress peak share from 55% to 35% within one product cycle.
5. **Gross-to-net and reimbursement pressure.** A ~$484K list price invites payer pushback, prior authorization, and eventual IRA/negotiation exposure. Net price realization, not list price, drives the model.
6. **Dilution and burn.** $2.1–2.2B of 2026 opex against ~$3.9B of cash means at least one more capital raise is probable, and SBC continues to transfer value from owners to employees.
7. **Sentiment risk.** After a >5x twelve-month move, expectations are maximal. The Q3 print (Nov 4) is the first hard look at actual Rasonque revenue — a miss against a $28M bar would be punished severely.

**Bottom line:** Superb science, superb execution, narrow moat, no margin of safety. Own it at $90–$100, not $197.
