### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical/Political Risks

**Risk 1 — US drug-pricing and MFN policy colliding with a ~$480k/yr launch price (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High)**
RASONQUE reportedly launched at a **~$39,800/month list price (~$480,000/year)** (secondary press/social reports — low-confidence, company has not been confirmed to disclose WAC). That price point is politically exposed in the current US environment, where the administration has tied tariff relief to **Most-Favored-Nation pricing agreements** running through Jan 20, 2029 (Section 232 pharma framework; MedCityNews, Green Worldwide). A single-asset, single-indication company with a first-in-class drug and no diversified book has little ability to absorb mandatory MFN-style price concessions. IRA Medicare negotiation eligibility for a small molecule approved in 2026 is years out, but **MFN is the nearer-term vector**.
*Watch item:* the Oct 13, 2026 8-K (content not retrievable by my tools) — confirm it is not pricing/policy-related.

**Risk 2 — Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs on the supply chain (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium)**
Per the April 2, 2026 proclamation, a **100% tariff applies to patented branded pharmaceutical imports without US production**, with **15% reduced rates for EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland** and further relief for companies holding MFN agreements; effective dates began July 31, 2026 (AFS Law, Mohawk Global, GHY, TT News). RVMD is a virtual manufacturer relying on third-party CMOs — **I could not verify where daraxonrasib API and finished tablets are made** (no 10-K manufacturing section available; searches for CMO disclosures returned nothing). Two asymmetric exposures:
- If US commercial supply is ex-US (EU/India/China), RVMD faces a **100% duty or must re-shore**, materially hitting COGS on a drug with no margin cushion yet.
- EU-manufactured product for EU sale is unaffected by US tariffs but exposed to EU-US trade-deal terms.
*This is the single largest unverified item in my analysis — I flag it as a gap, not a conclusion.*

**Risk 3 — BIOSECURE Act / US–China biotech decoupling (Probability: Medium / Impact: Low-Medium)**
The **BIOSECURE Act was enacted inside the FY26 NDAA signed Dec 18, 2025**, barring federally funded biotech entities from contracting with "biotechnology companies of concern" tied to China, Russia, North Korea, Iran (Mercury News/San Diego Union-Tribune, Jan 2, 2026). RVMD's exposure is (a) CRO/CDMO and comparator-sourcing contracts that may sit downstream of a BCC, and (b) competitive: China now runs more pivotal oncology trials than the US and hosts multiple KRAS G12D/G12C programs that could reach the market faster/cheaper. RVMD's global Phase 3 trials also depend on ex-US site networks that this regime can disrupt. No confirmed RVMD violation.

*Also monitor (lower materiality):* EU joint clinical assessment/HTA and EU pharma-package pricing pressure; French/EU social unrest (Oct 2026 protests) as a minor distribution/access irritant; Sanofi (France) as counterparty on the legacy SHP2 collaboration (RMC-4630) — royalty/milestone and termination risk, not geopolitical per se.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **No active material litigation, DOJ/FTC/SEC investigation, or sanction finding surfaced** in searches across news, filing metadata and web. **Caveat: this is a negative search result, not a verified "no legal proceedings" statement** — the legal-proceedings note in the 10-K/10-Q could not be retrieved. For a company that just launched its first product, this absence is plausible but should be re-checked against the next 10-Q.
- **Regulatory posture is strongly favorable — an underappreciated positive:**
  - FDA **Breakthrough Therapy Designation** (Sept 14, 2026) for 1L metastatic PDAC in combination with gemcitabine/nab-paclitaxel, on top of the existing approval in previously treated disease.
  - Selected for the **FDA Commissioner's National Priority Voucher** pilot — an accelerated-review channel that is itself a policy-dependent benefit (could be unwound by a change in FDA leadership priorities).
  - EMA **Cancer Medicines Pathfinder** phased review → CHMP positive opinion Oct 15, 2026; EC marketing authorization is the remaining step and is normally routine.
  - Expanded Access Program reached >2,000 patients across nearly all 50 states + Puerto Rico within ~3 weeks — evidence of clean REMS/distribution execution and a strong safety-tracking burden.
- **IP risk is the real legal exposure, and it rises with commercial success.** The RAS inhibitor field is crowded (Amgen, BMS/Mirati legacy, Lilly, plus Chinese entrants). A $42B valuation on a first-in-class oral RAS(ON) multi-selective inhibitor is precisely the profile that attracts **IPR/PGR challenges, patent-infringement suits and trade-secret claims** (ex-employee risk is endemic in Bay Area oncology). None are pending that I can find; probability of a challenge emerging within 24 months is **moderate**.
- **Compliance build-out risk:** as RVMD self-commercializes in Europe and runs global trials, it takes on **FCPA/UK Bribery Act exposure for HCP interactions in government-run health systems**, plus EU transparency (EFPIA) reporting, GDPR/clinical-data obligations, and Sunshine Act reporting in the US. A company going from 883 employees to a multi-country commercial footprint typically has its first compliance incidents in years 1–2 of launch.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **LOW–MEDIUM**

**Breakdown:**
- **Geopolitical (country/political stability): LOW.** US-incorporated, US-headquartered, no EM manufacturing or revenue base, no sanctions exposure.
- **Regulatory/policy: MEDIUM.** The company's upside now depends on government payers in the US and 27 EU systems, and on tariff/origin rules it does not control. Policy is currently a *tailwind* (Breakthrough, Priority Voucher, Orbis, Pathfinder) — that is itself a concentration risk: the same discretion that accelerated approval can slow a follow-on indication or a competitor's.
- **Legal/litigation: LOW today, rising.** No pending matters identified; IP-challenge probability increases with revenue.

**For the investment memo — three things that would change this rating:**
1. Disclosure that daraxonrasib API/finished dose is imported without a US production footprint → upgrade tariff risk to **High**.
2. Any MFN pricing commitment demanded as a condition of tariff relief → direct revenue-model hit.
3. A patent challenge or an EU pricing rejection in Germany/France → reframes the solo-Europe thesis (Reuters notes three analysts think solo launch is "doable"; that is the key legal/commercial assumption to test).

**Explicit gaps in this report:** (i) 10-K/10-Q text unavailable, so revenue-by-geography, legal proceedings and manufacturing disclosures are unverified; (ii) the Oct 13, 2026 8-K content was not retrievable; (iii) US list price is from secondary press, not confirmed by the company; (iv) no peer-level comparison of tariff/geography disclosure was possible within budget.
