# Amer Sports, Inc. (NYSE: AS) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

*Prepared as of the latest available data (Q3 2026 results reported 17-Nov-2026). Company: Cayman-incorporated (ISIN KYG0260P1028), HQ Helsinki, ~15,400 employees, operations in 40 countries, products sold in 100+, FY2025 revenue $6.6bn. Controlled by the ANTA Sports-led consortium (China).*

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1) Tariff-regime volatility and the new Section 301 forced-labour action — Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High**
The Supreme Court's **20-Feb-2026** decision invalidating IEEPA tariffs (affirming *V.O.S. Selections*) forced a rescission of those duties and opened refund claims; Amer booked a **$50.1m net tariff refund benefit** and **390bps of gross-margin benefit in Q2-26** — a single-quarter swing that demonstrates how tariff policy, not brand performance, can move reported margins. Guidance explicitly *assumes the most recently announced Section 301 rates remain in place*. On **2-Jun-2026** USTR announced new Section 301 proposals arising from a forced-labour investigation covering as many as 16 countries including China (potentially textiles/apparel) — directly aimed at Amer's sourcing base (~95% Asia). With ~95% of COGS sourced in Asia and ~36% of revenue in the Americas, a renewed escalation (or country-specific action on Vietnam) is the most probable adverse policy event. Mitigant: pricing power at Arc'teryx/Salomon and demonstrated ability to pass through (GM still 60.5–61.0% guided for FY26).

**2) China concentration + political/nationalist risk — Probability: Medium / Impact: High**
Greater China is ~25–28% of revenue and the fastest-growing region (group FY26 growth guided ~24%, China compounding at 40%+), making Arc'teryx's China franchise the single largest driver of the equity story. The **Sept-2025 Arc'teryx "Rising" fireworks display in Tibet** triggered a ~600m-view boycott, an official Chinese investigation and the dismissal of four local officials — a live demonstration that a single marketing decision can convert into a state-adjacent and consumer-driven revenue shock. Additional layers: (i) **ANTA Sports indirectly holds ~53–58%** of Amer (with FountainVest, Anamered/Tencent), so China policy and ANTA's own Xinjiang-cotton positioning (Nikkei: the cotton controversy has divided the group) transmit to Amer; (ii) any future US action against Chinese-controlled US-listed issuers (HFCAA-style disclosure/audit or outbound-investment rules) is a tail risk to the listing and index inclusion; (iii) a March-2026 follow-on of 20.6m shares at $36.40 vs. a current ~$30 share price signals that supply overhang/liquidity events around the sponsor stake remain a recurring feature.

**3) US–China strategic rupture (Taiwan, export controls, summit-level diplomacy) — Probability: Medium / Impact: High**
Amer sits precisely at the intersection — American/European brands, Asian manufacturing, Chinese ownership and Chinese growth. The November-2026 Trump–Xi–Putin summit and ongoing Commerce Department export-control tightening (WSJ, 17-Nov-2026) show the relationship remains transactional and headline-driven. A deterioration would hit all three legs simultaneously: Chinese consumer sentiment toward Western premium brands, tariff treatment of Asian sourcing, and the valuation of a China-owned US-listed equity.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action (active).** Filed after the Sept-2025 Tibet incident; firm investigations (Pomerantz, Levi & Korsinsky) allege materially misleading statements/disclosures; AS fell >5% on 22-Sep-2025. Amer already excludes "expenses related to certain legal proceedings" from adjusted EPS — i.e., litigation is recurring and material enough to be a standing adjustment. Dismissal/settlement outcome not confirmed in available sources (**gap**).
- **Employment litigation:** *EEOC v. Amer Sports Winter & Outdoor Co.* — $350,000 settlement and consent decree entered in D. Colo. on **6-Mar-2026** (age discrimination/retaliation). Small dollar value; relevant as a pattern of US employment-process exposure.
- **Product/PFAS regulation (under-appreciated, near-term):** EU REACH restriction on **PFHxA applies to apparel from 10-Oct-2026** (25ppb); California **AB 1817** PFAS ban in effect since 1-Jan-2025 (severe-wet-conditions outerwear exempt until 1-Jan-2028) plus New York. This hits the highest-margin waterproof/technical outerwear (Arc'teryx, Salomon) — a materials-reformulation cost and compliance-failure risk.
- **Supply-chain due diligence:** US **UFLPA** (Xinjiang) import detentions, **EU Forced Labour Regulation** (applicable 2027) and CSDDD create traceability obligations across the 27% China sourcing base; brands in this peer set have already been named in NGO/reporting lists alongside Amer.
- **Data privacy/consumer:** DTC reached ~49% of revenue in 2025 → GDPR, China PIPL, CCPA, plus advertising/marketing claims scrutiny (greenwashing and "performance" claims are an active regulatory theme in the EU).
- **IP:** Counterfeiting of Arc'teryx/Salomon in China is an ongoing brand-equity and enforcement cost; no material disclosed IP verdict.
- **Governance:** Controlled-company status (ANTA consortium) limits minority influence; related-party distribution in Greater China and sponsor sell-downs are the key conflict vectors.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Supply-chain traceability (elevated):** ~27% China sourcing with UFLPA/EU FLR exposure and a publicly documented internal split over Xinjiang cotton — detention or forced-labour findings would be a direct revenue and reputational hit.
2. **Litigation-adjusted earnings:** recurring "certain legal proceedings" add-backs mean adjusted EPS quality warrants scrutiny.
3. **China growth + state-adjacent sensitivity:** the Tibet episode shows non-financial risk converting to revenue risk quickly; ESG/permitting failures in China are a live compliance channel.
4. No evidence of sanctions (OFAC/Russia), FCPA/DOJ or AML exposure identified — consumer branded goods, limited high-risk-jurisdiction footprint beyond China sourcing.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: ~95% of sourcing concentrated in Asia (27% China) with an active new Section 301 forced-labour track; ~25–28% of revenue in a politically sensitive, boycott-prone Greater China market that is also the primary growth engine; a Chinese state-linked controlling shareholder; an active securities class action; and a dense 2026–27 regulatory calendar (PFAS, EU Forced Labour Regulation, CSDDD). Offsets: no sanctions exposure, strong pricing power (60.5–61.0% guided gross margin), demonstrated tariff pass-through, and a geographically diversified revenue base (Americas 36% / EMEA 29%). This is a *high* but not *very high* rating — the risks are policy-conjunctural rather than structural (no nationalisation, currency-control or expropriation exposure, and no direct Russia/OFAC nexus).

**Gaps:** No 10-K/10-Q text was retrievable through the filing tool (only Form 4 metadata returned), so exact legal-proceedings language, committed tariff dollar exposure and the current ANTA ownership percentage could not be confirmed from primary filings; figures above rely on company releases, IR pages and press reporting.
