## AMER SPORTS, INC. (NYSE: AS) — Value Investment Analysis

*Price $30.24 | Market cap ~$17.8bn (589m diluted sh.) | Net debt ~$0.65bn | EV ~$18.5bn | No dividend*

**Circle of competence:** Yes — branded consumer durables with identifiable pricing power. I can underwrite Arc'teryx and Salomon; I cannot confidently underwrite the Chinese consumer or a consortium-controlled board. Both matter here.

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: NARROW (and widening)

Three segments: Technical Apparel (Arc'teryx, Peak Performance), Outdoor Performance (Salomon, Atomic), Ball & Racquet (Wilson, Louisville Slugger, DeMarini).

**Evidence of a genuine moat:**
- **Intangible assets / brand pricing power.** Gross margin has gone from 49.1% (2021) to 60.8% guided for 2026 — roughly **1,170bp of expansion in five years**. Very few apparel/hardgoods companies expand gross margin at that rate while growing revenue ~22% annually. That is the signature of a brand gaining pricing power, not discounting to buy volume. Arc'teryx in particular functions as a luxury-adjacent technical brand (Q2'26 gross margin ~66%).
- **Efficient scale in niche technical categories.** Salomon owns trail running; Wilson is #1 in racquet sports with MLB licensing. Small, defensible categories where shelf space and athlete endorsement are winner-take-most.
- **Returns on capital are inflecting the right way.** ROIC (incl. goodwill) 0.8% (2022) → 4.7% (2023) → 7.3% (2024) → 9.0% (2025) → **~12%+ estimated FY2026**. Excluding the ~$5.1bn of acquisition goodwill/intangibles, returns on tangible invested capital are very high (30%+). Since goodwill is a sunk cost, *incremental* returns are what matter, and they are good.

**Why not Wide:** Hardgoods (skis, bikes, baseball) are cyclical and replaceable; the company is only 2.5 years into public life with no through-cycle proof; and the moat is concentrated in **one brand** (Arc'teryx) whose growth has been disproportionately China-driven. A Wide moat requires a decade of evidence, not five years.

**Strengths:** FCF conversion is excellent — TTM FCF (~$700m) exceeds TTM net income (~$643m), i.e. **>100% conversion**. The balance sheet transformation is the real story: net debt cut from **$6.2bn to under $0.7bn in three years** (~0.4x EBITDA). Interest expense fell from $98m to a ~$50m run-rate, so earnings growth is now operational, not financial engineering.

**Deductions:** (a) Goodwill + intangibles of $5.1bn = 88% of book equity and 29% of market cap — book value is largely accounting residue; (b) retained earnings still negative (−$214m); (c) inventory $1.62bn, up 33% — ahead of revenue growth, a working-capital item to watch; (d) highly seasonal, with Q2 a near-breakeven quarter (10.4% op margin vs 15.8% in Q3).

### 3. Valuation Analysis — Fair value ~$28–34; price is *at* fair value

*Note: the headline screening multiples are stale. The 39.8x P/E is FY2025 GAAP EPS of $0.76. On the actual forward numbers — FY2026 adjusted EPS guided to $1.32–1.34 and TTM GAAP EPS of $1.11 — the stock trades at **22.7x forward adjusted** and **27x TTM GAAP**.*

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Forward adjusted P/E (FY26E $1.33) | 22.7x |
| EV/EBITDA (FY26E ~$1.6bn) | ~11.5x |
| FCF yield (FY26E ~$700m) | 3.9% |
| Owner earnings yield (TTM ~$850m) | ~4.8% |
| P/B | 2.9x (against mostly goodwill) |
| 52-month range | ~$20 (Apr'25) to ~$42.8 (Aug'25/Feb'26); now ~29% off the high |

I built three explicit scenarios (10-year DCF, FCF margins expanding from ~8.5% toward 11–13%, terminal growth 2.75%, WACC 10%):

- **Base** (revenue CAGR ~9% tapering from 14%, FCF margin →11%): **~$30/share**
- **Bull** (Arc'teryx compounds, revenue CAGR ~12%, margin →13%): **~$42/share**
- **Bear** (China stalls, margin plateaus ~10%, growth →3%): **~$21/share**
- **Probability-weighted (50/30/20): ~$31/share**

At 11% WACC — defensible given a **beta of 1.97** — the base case falls to ~$26 and the blended value to ~$28. *Sensitivity to discount rate is the whole ballgame here.*

**What the price implies:** ~15% revenue CAGR fading to 4%, with FCF margins reaching ~11%. That is a reasonable, non-heroic forecast — but it leaves **zero margin of safety**. I require a 25–30% discount to intrinsic value; at $30.24 we are ~5% *below* a blended fair value of ~$31. A business trading at intrinsic value is not a buy.

---

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — 7 / 10

**Positives:** CEO Jie Zheng is an Anta veteran who has executed a coherent premiumization strategy — kill discounting, lift gross margin, deleverage, expand DTC. No empire-building M&A since IPO. And in Q3'26 the board authorized a **$1bn buyback** (~5.6% of shares, ~1.5x annual FCF) — the first meaningful return of capital, and a signal management views ~$30 as reasonable value. That is disciplined, owner-like behavior.

**Concerns:** This is a **controlled company**. Anta Sports holds roughly 44–46% (via the Mascot JVCo consortium with FountainVest, Tencent ~5.6% of the JV, and Chip Wilson's Anamered); the Anta-led consortium took the company private at €40/share in 2019 and re-IPO'd it at $13 in Feb 2024. Alignment with the controlling shareholder is strong; alignment with *minority* holders is not structurally assured. The China business runs partly through Anta-related distribution — a related-party channel that deserves ongoing scrutiny. Insider ownership is very high (good), but the "owner-operator" here is a state-scale Chinese sportswear company, not a founder.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (Buy below ~$24)

This is a genuinely good business — improving moat, exceptional margin trajectory, fortress balance sheet, high FCF conversion, and a fresh buyback. But at $30.24 the stock already capitalizes a successful outcome, and my base-case intrinsic value is approximately the current price.

- **Strong Buy** below ~$21 (bear-case value — you'd be paid for the China risk)
- **Buy** below ~$24 (25% discount to blended fair value)
- **Hold** $24–$34 ← **we are here ($30.24)**
- **Sell** above ~$42

If you already own it, hold — the compounding is intact and the buyback supports the floor. If you don't, patience costs you nothing but opportunity; wait for Mr. Market to re-price the China scare.

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Greater China deceleration — the most important risk.** Q3'26 Greater China revenue was **$601.6m versus $604.9m a year earlier — flat**, while total revenue grew 24%. China's share of revenue fell from ~34% to ~28% in one year. The growth engine of the last three years has stalled; if that is consumer weakness rather than channel timing, the bull case loses its axle.
2. **Multiple compression.** At 22.7x forward with a 1.97 beta, a shift to a 16–18x multiple on unchanged earnings is a 25% drawdown. High-beta, high-multiple consumer names de-rate violently.
3. **Tariffs and supply chain.** Production is concentrated in China/Vietnam; US tariff changes hit gross margin directly for a company that has been expanding it — the reverse of the current trend.
4. **Governance / related-party risk.** Anta control means minority holders have limited recourse if capital is allocated to serve group interests (e.g., China distribution arrangements).
5. **Goodwill impairment.** $5.1bn sits against brands whose value depends on margin durability; a stumble in Arc'teryx puts a write-down in play and breaks the "clean compounding" narrative.
6. **Buyback execution vs. price.** The $1bn authorization is accretive at $30; it becomes value-destructive if pursued aggressively above $40.
7. **Growth-quality check on inventory.** Inventory +33% vs revenue +27% — if sell-through slows, markdowns reverse the gross-margin story quickly.

**What would change my mind to Buy:** the stock at $24 (a 20% decline on no fundamental change), or Greater China returning to double-digit growth while operating margin holds above 15% — either would let me raise the base case toward $35+.
