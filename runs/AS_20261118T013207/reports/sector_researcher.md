## SECTOR BRIEF — Amer Sports, Inc. (NYSE: AS)
**Sector:** Consumer Cyclical / Leisure — premium technical apparel, outdoor equipment & racquet sports
**Data as of:** Q3 2026 results (reported 17 Nov 2026); market cap $17.6bn, price $30.24, beta 1.97

---

### 1. Sector Overview

AS competes in the ~$175bn global outdoor apparel market (2026E, growing ~5.4% CAGR to ~$239bn by 2032) and the broader ~$400–500bn sporting goods market. The sector is **highly fragmented at the aggregate level** — Nike (~$46bn revenue), adidas, VF Corp ($9.6bn), Lululemon ($11.1bn) and AS ($6.6bn) together hold well under 25% of a global market, implying a low HHI (<500) — but **sharply concentrated within niches**: Arc'teryx in premium technical outerwear, Salomon in trail running and ski, Wilson in racquet sports. The critical structural feature in 2026 is a **bifurcation**: mass athletic is in a tariff-and-destocking trough (Nike revenue +0.2%, operating margin collapsed 336bps to 8.2%; VF −5.1% four-year CAGR), while **premium/technical brands are compounding 25–30%** at 57–63% gross margins. AS sits entirely on the winning side of that split.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Premiumisation and mix shift are visibly pricing through.** AS group gross margin has risen from 49.1% (FY21) to 57.6% (FY25) — +853bps in four years — with FY26 adjusted gross margin guided to ~60.8%. This is not cost-driven; it is Arc'teryx-weighted mix and full-price DTC (Arc'teryx DTC +32% in Q3'26).
- **Greater China premium outdoor remains the sector's best growth market.** China's outdoor sports industry exceeds $422bn with explicit policy support, and premium outdoor is pulling in younger, female and luxury-adjacent consumers. AS grew Greater China revenue 30.4% to $601.6m in Q3'26 *against* a soft Chinese consumer — and its Anta-led ownership gives it distribution no Western peer can replicate.
- **Incumbent retrenchment is releasing share and shelf space.** Nike's restructuring (op. margin 12.3%→8.2%) and VF's contraction have opened wholesale doors and consumer mindshare. AS, DECK and ON are the share takers — the three fastest growers in the table are also the three with the strongest margin trajectory.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Tariffs are the number-one industry hurdle and AS is directly exposed.** Section 301 forced-labour tariffs added 12.5% on China and Vietnam-sourced goods from 24 July 2026, on top of existing layers; apparel/footwear sourcing is concentrated in exactly those countries. Tellingly, AS's Q2'26 operating margin got a **390bp one-off lift from net tariff refunds** — proof of the exposure — and FY26 guidance leans on benefits that do not repeat. Industry commentary expects flat-to-negative H1 comps on tariffs.
- **China concentration cuts both ways.** Greater China is now ~$600m/quarter and the fastest-growing region; it is also the region most exposed to stimulus withdrawal, nationalist sentiment toward foreign brands, and geopolitical escalation. Anta's majority stake adds governance and political-sensitivity overhang.
- **"Gorpcore" fashion-cycle risk in the highest-multiple franchise.** Arc'teryx's growth is partly fashion-led, and Lululemon is the cautionary template in this very table: revenue growth decelerated 18.6% → 10.1% → 4.9% and operating margin fell 23.7% → 19.9% in three years. ON Holding (30% growth, 62.8% GM) is also attacking premium footwear directly.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **Growth:** fastest in the peer set bar ON, at 6–9x the median. Revenue has more than doubled from $3.07bn (FY21) to $6.57bn (FY25), with FY26E ~$8.2–8.3bn — no year below +16%.
- **Margins:** gross margin above peer median and expanding faster than any comparable except ON (+853bps). GAAP operating margin is now exactly at peer median (10.4%) but from a far weaker base (1.4% in FY22) and still ~5–9pp below DECK/LULU, leaving visible self-help runway as DTC mix and SG&A leverage (47.3% of sales in FY25 vs 52.6% in FY22) continue.
- **Balance sheet/cash flow:** the post-IPO deleveraging is the under-appreciated driver — interest expense fell from $401.7m (FY23) to $97.7m (FY25), swinging net income from −$252.7m to +$427.4m, with $1.11bn EBITDA and a $1bn buyback authorised.
- **Caveats:** sub-scale versus Nike ($46bn) at $6.6bn; brand concentration in Arc'teryx for both growth and margin; only ~2.5 years of public reporting (IPO Feb-24 at $13 vs $30.24 now); beta 1.97 means the equity amplifies any sector de-rating.

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE** (for AS's premium-technical niche) — **NEUTRAL** for mass athletic

The addressable sub-sector offers ~5–7% structural volume growth, genuine pricing power, and a clear share-shift tailwind from wounded incumbents; AS's own guide of 25–26% growth on a $6.6bn base implies years of runway before it meets category growth. The offset is that premium technical looks **late-cycle on margin** — 57–63% gross margins attract entrants (ON is proof), tariffs are a real and rising cost line, and fashion-driven demand can decelerate as violently as Lululemon's did. Rating is Attractive *conditional on* Arc'teryx brand heat persisting; the single largest risk in this thesis is not sector demand but brand-cycle mortality.
