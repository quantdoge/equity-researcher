### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives (verifiable):** SBTi **validated** near- and long-term targets in January 2025 — **−90% absolute Scope 1&2** and **−90% absolute Scope 3** (net zero 2050), which is best-in-class ambition for the sector rather than a vague pledge. Execution is tracking: renewable electricity **86% in 2025 vs 58% in 2024**; recycled content in apparel/footwear **19.3% vs 13%**; LCA coverage of **62%** of strategic product categories; TCFD-aligned reporting already published. Arc'teryx launched a circular-design framework (System 0™, Sept 2026). Estimated carbon intensity is modest (~200 tCO₂e per $1m revenue, dominated by Scope 3 purchased goods) — typical for asset-light apparel, well below vertically-integrated peers.

**Risks:** Almost the entire footprint sits in Scope 3, i.e. outside the company's direct control and dependent on supplier decarbonisation in China/Vietnam — the hardest 90% reduction to deliver. **PFAS** is the sharpest environmental edge: France banned PFAS in textiles *and ski wax* from 1 Jan 2026 (directly hits Salomon/Atomic wax lines), EU PFHxA limits bite from 10 Oct 2026 (25ppb), and ECHA's RAC adopted its final opinion in March 2026 on a near-universal PFAS restriction. Mitigant: the shift to **GORE-TEX ePE** (PFAS-free membrane) was largely completed ahead of regulation; cost was absorbed and the brand absorbed the performance trade-off.

**Physical climate:** low direct asset exposure, but snow-dependent product lines (Atomic, Salomon winter sports) carry structural demand risk in a warming climate — a multi-decade, not near-term, earnings issue.

---

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

- **Labour/supply chain:** Reasonably strong architecture — Supplier Code of Conduct, FLA-aligned third-party audits, **99% of Tier 1 suppliers audited and trained**, published Modern Slavery/Human Trafficking statements, Human Rights Policy, whistleblowing channel. No UFLPA entity-list or CBP detention event found for AS in my search. Residual risk: geographic concentration in China and Vietnam and limited Tier 2/raw-material traceability disclosure (cotton, down, leather).
- **The Tibet "Rising Dragon" incident (Sept 2025)** is the dominant social controversy: Arc'teryx sponsored a Cai Guo-Qiang fireworks display on the Tibetan plateau, triggering ~**600 million WeChat mentions**, accusations of greenwashing, an apology widely criticised as shifting blame to the China team, resignation of the Greater China GM, and a Shigatse government ruling that the brand must pay **ecological compensation** (amount undisclosed). This is a values-credibility failure at the core of the outdoor brand proposition — the single most material ESG event in the company's short public history.
- **Product safety/consumer:** PFAS-adjacent exposure; no AS-named consumer class action surfaced (litigation has targeted supplier W.L. Gore), but greenwashing suits against Gore-Tex are a template that could extend downstream to brands marketing "environmentally friendly" technical wear.
- **Workforce:** 15,400 employees; no material safety or industrial-action controversies found. Named #84 on the 100 Best Corporate Citizens list (Sept 2026).

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Strengths:** Chair/CEO roles separated; a designated **Lead Independent Director (Bruno Sälzer, ex-CEO Hugo Boss/Escada)**; board committees constituted; double-materiality assessment, ethics/code and whistleblowing infrastructure in place; sustainability owned at SVP level (Anne Larilahti) and reported with TCFD structure — better than most 2024-vintage IPOs.

**Deductions:**
- **Controlled company status.** AS discloses that it is a **foreign private issuer** relying on the **NYSE "controlled company" exemption** — i.e. it is not required to have a majority-independent board or fully independent nomination/compensation committees. Effective control sits with the **ANTA Sports-led consortium** (~40%+ economic interest), and a **Business Cooperation Agreement (Feb 2024)** grants ANTA board-nomination rights while it holds ≥10%.
- **Board and management overlap with the controlling shareholder.** The Chairman is **Shizhong Ding, co-founder/Chairman of ANTA**; another director (**Mingwei Bi**) is ANTA's Executive Director & CFO; the CEO (**James/Jie Zheng**) is an ANTA alumnus. The chair is therefore not independent of the party that controls the shareholder vote.
- **Related-party footprint.** Warehousing and logistics agreements with **Fujian ANTA Logistics**, plus China distribution arrangements — recurring RPTs that require vigilant audit-committee scrutiny and are a structural minority-interest leakage risk.
- **FPI perimeter:** exempt from US proxy rules, Section 16 insider reporting and quarterly 10-Q disclosure, and Cayman-incorporated with Finnish operations — a thinner disclosure and accountability regime than a US domestic issuer of comparable size.
- No disclosed ESG-linked executive compensation metric; no dual-class share structure (a positive).

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU:** CSRD (post-Omnibus I, in force 18 Mar 2026) — AS's EU turnover comfortably exceeds the revised thresholds and it already reports DMA/SBTi/TCFD, so compliance risk is *low*; scope timing is the only uncertainty. **EU PFAS restrictions** (PFHxA limits from Oct 2026; universal restriction advancing) — *high relevance*. **EU Forced Labour Regulation** (applies Dec 2027) — requires supply-chain tracing capability. **ESPR/Ecodesign textiles + national EPR schemes** — medium. **CSDDD** as amended by Omnibus I — reduced but non-zero.
- **France:** PFAS ban in textiles and **ski wax** from 1 Jan 2026 — most concrete, dated product-compliance cost (Salomon, Atomic).
- **US:** California AB 1817 / state PFAS apparel bans — low cost (already ePE); **UFLPA** — supply-chain documentation burden; SEC climate-disclosure rule effectively stayed/no longer a driver.
- **China:** tightening environmental-compliance expectations for foreign brands post-Tibet — reputational, not formal.
- **Not applicable / minimal:** CBAM (textiles out of scope), EU taxonomy alignment reporting (voluntary).

---

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (high-quality disclosure, structurally weak control)

Genuine environmental leadership for its size (validated −90% absolute Scope 1–3 targets, 86% renewable electricity, FLA-grade supplier auditing) offsets a governance profile that is *below* US-listed norms because of controlled-company status and heavy ANTA overlap. This is not an ESG laggard — it is a well-disclosed company with concentration risk in exactly the two places that matter: **supply-chain geography** and **China brand trust**.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL (with a specific, non-consensus risk to monitor)**

ESG factors are **not** the primary driver of the AS thesis — growth, Arc'teryx brand heat, DTC mix and margin expansion are. Carbon and PFAS are manageable, largely pre-invested costs that will not move the valuation. The material ESG contribution to the investment case is **tail risk, not cash-flow drag**:

1. **China brand-trust concentration is the real ESG risk.** A brand whose licence to operate rests on environmental authenticity, deriving ~40% of its highest-multiple brand's sales from one socially reactive market, has demonstrated it can generate a 600m-impression backlash in 48 hours. That is a low-probability/high-severity event worth explicit scenario weighting, not an ESG score.
2. **Governance deserves a discount, small but real.** ANTA-derived control, related-party logistics, FPI disclosure perimeter and a non-independent chair cap the multiple a governance-sensitive holder should pay; watch for RPT expansion or any move to increase consortium influence.
3. **Positives already priced/underappreciated:** SBTi-validated absolute Scope 3 targets and FLA-aligned auditing make AS a credible supplier to sustainability-gated institutional capital and reduce future CSRD/CSDDD remediation cost.

**Net:** Neutral for the thesis — no ESG-driven adjustment to fair value is warranted on current evidence, but position sizing should acknowledge the China social-licence tail and the controlled-company governance discount.
