## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Amer Sports, Inc. (NYSE: AS)
**Price $30.24 | Mkt cap ~$17.6B | Sector: Consumer Cyclical / Leisure | Inc.: Cayman | HQ: Helsinki | IPO: Feb 2024**
*Assessment window: Nov 2025 – Nov 2026*

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate — with a Weak shareholder-alignment overlay**

**Positives (verified):**
- **Chairman/CEO separation.** Board chaired by Shizhong Ding (ANTA co-founder); James (Jie) Zheng is CEO and an executive director.
- **Single class of shares, one vote per share** — no dual-class super-voting structure.
- **Board: 11 members, ~54% independent (6/11)** per company sustainability disclosure (31-Dec-2025).
- **Balance sheet deleveraged:** March 2026 $750M **primary** equity raise (20.6M shares at $36.40) used to redeem the 6.750% senior secured notes due 2031; S&P affirmed **BBB-** following debt repayment.
- **No SEC enforcement or DOJ action** found via EDGAR full-text search.

**Concerns (material):**
- **Controlling-shareholder overlay.** The 2019 buyout consortium — ANTA Sports (~56% at IPO), FountainVest (~16%), Anamered/Chip Wilson (~20.6%), Tencent (~5.6%) — retains effective control. Per S&P, **ANTA has the contractual right to nominate 5 of 11 directors, including the chairman, for as long as it holds ≥30%**. Board independence is therefore structurally qualified: a majority-independent board can still be outvoted on any ordinary resolution.
- **Foreign private issuer status (20-F / 6-K).** AS does **not** file 10-Ks, 10-Qs, or a DEF 14A. This means no quarterly financial statements on the US domestic schedule, materially lighter executive-compensation disclosure, and no proxy-level related-party transparency. For a US-listed name with a controlling foreign shareholder, this is a **real monitoring handicap** — I could not retrieve risk-factor text or compensation tables through available channels. Treat disclosure cadence as a standing governance discount.
- **Board diversity is thin:** 2 of 11 directors are women (18%).
- **Related-party dependence:** ANTA is simultaneously controlling shareholder, board controller, and the key Greater China distribution/JV counterparty — i.e., the counterparty to the company's fastest-growing and highest-margin geography is also its controller. *Gap: I could not verify the exact Greater China JV economics or related-party revenue quantum within budget.*
- **Compensation optics are weak:** insider activity is unidirectionally monetizing (see §3), which suggests equity awards are being treated as liquidity events rather than alignment.

**Trade-policy and product-compliance detail:**
- **Tariffs are the dominant regulatory variable.** Multi-country Asian sourcing (Vietnam, China) into the US, with ASEAN tariff regimes in flux through 2026. Note that Q2 2026 adjusted gross margin of 65.8% (+710bps) included a **390bp benefit from net tariff refunds** — the company has been a *beneficiary* of tariff-litigation refunds, meaning the current margin trajectory embeds a policy-dependent, potentially reversible item.
- **Forced-labour / UFLPA pincer (structural).** Arc'teryx and the group sit awkwardly between Western Xinjiang-cotton scrutiny (UFLPA detentions, supply-chain tracing) and Chinese nationalist expectations that ANTA-affiliated brands endorse Xinjiang cotton — a conflict previously reported as dividing ANTA from its own international subsidiary. This is a **two-sided regulatory/reputational trap** that cannot be fully hedged.
- **Chemical/PFAS compliance** (EU PFAS restriction, California AB 1817-type textile bans) is a genuine, sector-wide re-engineering cost for Gore-Tex-based technical apparel — the highest-margin product line. *No AS-specific citation; flagged as industry-level exposure, not a confirmed company action.*
- **Ski-binding product liability** (Atomic/Salomon) is a structurally litigious category with long-tail indemnity claims; no specific suit identified.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Greater China concentration plus geopolitical single-point-of-failure.** Greater China is the primary growth and margin engine (Arc'teryx omni-comp, Salomon softgoods) yet is exposed simultaneously to Chinese consumer-nationalist backlash, Tibet-related regulatory scrutiny, US-China trade escalation, and ANTA's own strategic priorities. This is a single geography that can compress both the multiple and the earnings base at once.
- **Tariff-refund-dependent margin optics.** With 390bps of a 710bps gross-margin gain coming from net tariff refunds, underlying margin expansion is materially smaller than headline. If refunds lapse or are clawed back, the "beat-and-raise" cadence inverts — an operational *and* credibility risk.
- **Key-person concentration and a thin bench.** CEO Zheng has additionally assumed **interim leadership of Salomon and the Outdoor Performance segment following the Salomon CEO's departure**, on top of group CEO duties. A CEO simultaneously running the group and its most important softgoods growth engine is a governance and continuity exposure, particularly with the CFO (Andrew Page) only a few years into the seat and prior senior-management churn at the executive-director level.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Elevated and already materialized**

- **Arc'teryx Tibet fireworks ("Rising"), Sept 2025:** a high-altitude fireworks display staged in a fragile Tibetan environment triggered a **~600M-view boycott movement in China**, accusations of greenwashing/hypocrisy against the brand's environmental identity, and an **official government probe**. Sell-side framed it as "short-lived" and used it as an upgrade catalyst — a complacent read given that the affected brand is the group's **premium margin engine and its China growth story simultaneously**.
- **Brand-values fragility is structural, not episodic.** Arc'teryx's pricing power rests on authenticity with a highly vocal outdoor community; the event converted a values asset into a liability in one weekend. Repeat probability is non-trivial given heavy DTC event marketing.
- **Dual-audience exposure:** satisfying Western ESG/forced-labour expectations alienates Chinese consumers; satisfying Chinese nationalist sentiment invites UFLPA/EU scrutiny. Wholesale partners (REI-type retailers, EU specialty) add a second veto point.
- **Sentiment is already priced in:** the stock fell from **$41.96 (20-Feb-26) to $30.24**, ~**-28%**, with a 52-week low of $26.27 (17-Sep-26) — **despite** consecutive beats, raises, and upward guidance revisions (FY26 revenue growth guided from 16–18% → 20–22% → **25–26%**). Fundamentals and share price are diverging, which usually means the market is discounting a risk the P&L does not yet show: China/tariff/brand durability.
- **Offsetting positive:** no active organized boycott registered by Ethical Consumer; the China event appears contained, not systemic.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH** (borderline Moderate)

**Rationale:** No fraud indicators, no solvency stress, no SEC/DOJ action, and a BBB- balance sheet post-deleveraging argue against anything worse. But two categories carry genuine tail risk: (i) a **materialized reputational event plus regulatory probe** at the highest-multiple brand in the most important growth geography, layered over an unresolvable forced-labour/trade-policy pincer; and (ii) **consistent, unopposed insider selling** combined with **tariff-refund-inflated margins** and **reduced FPI disclosure**. Upgrade to Moderate would require: ≥2 quarters of clean China brand momentum, disclosed tariff-refund quantification, and at least one credible insider purchase.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **CEO sold 500,000 shares (~$17.2M) and CSO exited ~78% of direct holdings; zero insider buys in 12 months.** Escalate to PM — check whether CEO sales were 10b5-1 pre-scheduled and the adoption dates.
2. **Tariff-refund contribution to gross margin (390bps of 710bps in Q2 2026) is not visible in headline "beat-and-raise" framing.** Request refund quantum, timing, and clawback risk; stress-test margins ex-refunds.
3. **Tibet fireworks display is under active government investigation** with a 600M-view China boycott precedent — unquantified contingent liability plus brand-equity impairment at the most valuable asset.
4. **ANTA deploying ~$1.8B into a 29% Puma stake** while AS trades ~28% off its high — potential capital-allocation conflict and a signal that controlling-shareholder monetization of AS could resume.
5. **FPI disclosure regime (20-F/6-K, no 10-Q, no proxy)** materially limits monitoring of related-party dealings with ANTA, which is both controller and China JV/distribution counterparty. Highest-priority diligence gap.
6. **CEO also serving as interim Salomon/Outdoor Performance head** after that unit's CEO departure — key-person and succession risk to the second growth engine.
7. **Tencent 13D/A reduction to 2.7%** — corroborates the pattern of pre-IPO holders exiting into strength.
