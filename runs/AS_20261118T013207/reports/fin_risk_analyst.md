# Financial Risk Assessment — Amer Sports, Inc. (NYSE: AS)

**Price $30.24 | Market Cap $17.59B | Sector: Consumer Cyclical / Leisure | Domicile: Finland | IPO Feb 2024**

> **Framing note:** AS is not a conventional credit-risk story. It entered public markets (Feb 2024) as a heavily levered LBO with $6.66B of debt and negative book equity. Eighteen months later it has retired most of that debt and holds investment-grade ratings. The **residual risk is almost entirely market/equity risk and intangible-asset concentration — not solvency risk.**

| Line | Value |
|---|---|

| SG&A | 2,950.8 |

**Conclusion: the balance sheet survives both scenarios without external financing.** Cash of $668.1M alone exceeds net debt in the base case. Even in Scenario B, with EBITDA down 80%, leverage stays inside the rating agencies' stated tolerance and the company remains roughly FCF-neutral. The constraint is not liquidity — it is that **at −35% revenue the income statement goes to an EBIT loss and the equity would likely de-rate by 40–60%**.

**The real downside transmission channel is the $5.0B of goodwill and intangibles (46.4% of total assets).** A severe, sustained revenue decline — particularly in Arc'teryx China, the highest-multiple growth engine — would very plausibly trigger a non-cash impairment that destroys book equity and, more importantly, resets the market's view of brand value. This is the tail risk, and it is an *equity* risk, not a creditor risk.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Split assessment, because a single label obscures the picture:

- **Credit / solvency risk: LOW.** Net leverage 0.31x, ICR 12.9x, net cash position on a financial-debt basis, investment-grade rated, FCF positive through a severe downside, no near-term refinancing cliff.
- **Market / equity risk: HIGH.** 47% vol, beta ~1.5–2.0, −37.9% max drawdown, short public history, intangible-heavy book, concentrated growth exposure.
- **Liquidity risk: LOW.** Current ratio 1.79, $668M cash against $444.6M net debt, positive FCF, no dividend obligation (dividends paid = $0), no buyback commitment.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Goodwill & intangibles = $5,005.1M, or 46.4% of total assets.** This exceeds reported total equity of $7,174M in relative terms and represents ~28% of market cap. Brand-value impairment is the primary mechanism by which a demand shock becomes an equity event.
2. **Inventory at $1,975.9M, ~232 days of COGS.** Up 19% YoY vs. revenue +24% — inventory is growing *slower* than sales (a positive), but the absolute level is high for a fashion/technical-apparel business and carries obsolescence risk into a downturn.
3. **Very short operating history as a public company (IPO Feb 2024).** No through-cycle credit record; no recession test of the Arc'teryx-led growth model. All market-risk statistics cover ~2.8 years, an unusually benign period.
4. **China / tariff concentration.** Management explicitly guides on the assumption that "current tariff rates remain in place," and Greater China is the margin-accretive growth engine. A single-market policy shock is the most plausible route to Scenario B.
5. **Negative book retained earnings until Q3'26.** Retained earnings were −$1,057.5M (2023), −$984.9M (2024), −$213.6M (2025), and only turned positive at **+$203.6M in Q3'26**. Thin accumulated cushion; book equity is a recent construct, not a long-earned buffer.
6. **Dilution.** Share count rose 11.6% in FY2025 to fund deleveraging. Further equity issuance is unlikely given current leverage, but the precedent matters.
7. **Equity-beta amplification.** At ~1.5–2.0 beta, a market correction translates into a magnified drawdown in a name that already showed a −31% move in six weeks this autumn.
