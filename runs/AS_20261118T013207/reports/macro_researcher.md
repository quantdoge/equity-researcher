---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional, risk-off tilt**

The US is in a **reflation-with-a-hawkish-Fed** regime, not a landing. Real GDP re-accelerated to ~3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026 (GDPC1 $24,611B vs $24,408B), unemployment fell to **4.1%**, and nominal PCE spending is running **+6.3% y/y** — but CPI has re-accelerated to **3.82% y/y** (+4.5% annualized over the last four months). The Fed cut ~70bp off the 2023–24 peak, then **paused on 28 October 2026 at 3.75–4.00% with an explicit tightening bias**; the easing cycle is effectively over.

The problem is the **long end**. The 10Y sits at **5.13%** and the 30Y at **5.47%**, with the 2s10s at **+39bp** — a bear-steepening driven by term premium, not growth optimism. Equities are now visibly cracking: the Dow fell 848 points (-1.6%) on 17 November and the Nasdaq-100 is >10% off its high. Meanwhile **UMich consumer sentiment has collapsed to 48.1** (Sept 2026) from 71.7 in Jan 2025 — a record-low zone — even as aggregate spending holds up. That divergence (strong nominal GDP, record-weak sentiment, squeezed real incomes) is the defining feature of this cycle for a consumer discretionary name.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**FX — the sharpest near-term swing (Negative).** AS reports in USD but earns >50% of revenue outside it, and FY26 guidance of +25–26% already embeds a **~200bp currency tailwind** built on the H1 2026 weak-dollar period. The dollar has since rallied 4% in eight weeks (broad index) and 5.4% vs the euro. If sustained through Q4/FY27, that 200bp tailwind flips to a **150–200bp revenue translation headwind**, concentrated in Salomon/Atomic's European revenue and costs. Partial natural hedge: EUR-denominated manufacturing costs translate down in USD, cushioning gross margin. Risk is a **guidance/consensus reset in the revenue line**, not the margin line.

**Rates & valuation — the dominant transmission channel (Negative).** This is a long-duration equity: ~28x forward earnings, beta 1.97, no dividend. At 10Y 5.13% and 30Y 5.47%, the discount rate is doing the damage, not the P&L. Note the tell: AS is **down ~29% from its $42.76 February high and ~19% YTD** while growing revenue 24–25% — pure **multiple compression**. Solvency risk is low: 9M 2026 operating cash flow of **$514M**, deleveraging since the 2024 IPO, and a new **$1B buyback** all signal balance-sheet comfort. Post-IPO debt is largely amortizing term debt; the Fed's 70bp of cuts have lowered the floating-rate component. *Gap: exact net debt/leverage and maturity ladder not available from my data feeds — I'd flag this for the credit workstream, though it is not the primary macro risk.*

**Tariffs — policy has flipped to a tailwind, temporarily (Positive, non-recurring).** Q2 2026 gross margin jumped 710bp to 65.6%, including **390bp from net tariff refunds**. Q3 gross margin still rose 250bp to **59.3%** with adjusted operating margin at 16.4%. FY26 adjusted gross margin guided to **~60.8%**. This is legal/policy relief on Vietnam/China-sourced footwear and apparel into the US — it flatters YoY comps and is **not repeatable**; the underlying tariff regime remains volatile and is a structural cost risk for a company with Asian sourcing.

**US/China consumer — a genuine two-speed picture (Mixed, leaning Negative).** US: nominal spending is fine but sentiment at 48.1 argues the marginal, entry-price Salomon sneaker buyer is under pressure, while the high-income Arc'teryx customer (DTC +32%, Arc'teryx omni-comp +16%) is far more resilient — AS's mix is a natural hedge. China: retail sales grew just **+0.4% y/y in October**, yet AS grew **Greater China +30%**. That is share gain and brand heat (Jisoo campaigns, Beijing flagship, Salomon Sportstyle), not macro beta — impressive, but a deceleration is the single highest-probability negative surprise in the model, precisely because the macro backdrop there is deteriorating.

**Growth (Positive).** 4.1% unemployment and 3.4% real GDP underpin volumes; premium outdoor is outgrowing mass apparel. Guidance raised to **+25–26% revenue**, adjusted operating margin 14.5–14.7%, with all segments and geographies double-digit.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL (negative skew)**

The **operating** backdrop is favourable — resilient US employment, reflation, tariff refunds, and brand momentum strong enough to outgrow a stagnant Chinese consumer. The **financial** backdrop is hostile — 5.13% 10Y, 5.47% 30Y, a Fed with a tightening bias, CPI at 3.8%, a 4%-in-eight-weeks dollar rally, and record-weak consumer sentiment. For a 1.97-beta, ~28x, USD-reporting, China-levered discretionary name, **the macro is a multiple problem, not an earnings problem**. Net: Neutral, with the distribution of outcomes skewed to the downside because three of four transmission channels (FX, discount rate, consumer sentiment) are currently moving against it simultaneously.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario (Bear)

**A long-end funding shock that forces the Fed back into hikes.** If term premium keeps building and the 10Y clears **5.75–6.0%** while CPI prints >4% (energy + tariff pass-through), the Fed's "tightening bias" becomes action in H1 2027. Three things hit AS at once: (1) the equity derates from ~28x to ~20x regardless of earnings; (2) the dollar breaks to new highs (broad index >128), wiping out the FX tailwind and then some; (3) China, unable to ease into a strong dollar, keeps retail sales sub-1%, and Greater China growth decelerates from +30% toward +10%. A 25%-growth company with a collapsing multiple is a $22–24 stock (down ~25%) even on in-line fundamentals. Beta 1.97 means it takes roughly double the index hit.

The mirror-image bull case — dollar rollover plus Chinese consumption stimulus plus the Fed's next move being a cut — would restore the FX tailwind and re-rate the shares back toward $40. I weight the bear scenario the higher probability over a 6–12 month horizon.
