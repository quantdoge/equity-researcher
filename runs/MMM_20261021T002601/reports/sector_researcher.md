## SECTOR BRIEF — 3M Company (MMM)

**Note on data:** the sector-aggregate feed mixes fiscal year-ends (34 contributors for FY2025 totalling $1.046T revenue at a 33.4% blended gross margin, plus stray single-company periods), so I anchor benchmarking on company-level peers rather than the aggregate.

### 1. Sector Overview
MMM competes in the **diversified industrial / multi-industry** space — a $1T+ US-listed revenue pool spanning short-cycle industrial consumables (abrasives, adhesives, tapes, PPE), electrical/thermal management, filtration and consumer staples. It is a mature, low-growth, high-fragmentation sector: EBITDA margins in the high-teens to mid-20s, R&D intensities of 2–5%, and consolidation driven more by portfolio pruning (spins/sales) than by organic share gains. The cycle is in **mid-recovery**: US manufacturing PMI reached 55.6 in July 2026 (highest since May 2022), after a two-year destocking trough. Crucially, the recovery is uneven — electrical equipment, power and data-centre-exposed names are booming, while short-cycle general industrial and consumer-facing volumes are only now stabilising. MMM sits closer to the slow lane.

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Manufacturing cycle inflection:** ISM/S&P Global PMI back above 55 with new orders and production surging; distributor destocking has ended, restoring volume leverage on incremental revenue (MMM's Q3'26 operating margin 23.7% vs 18.3% FY25).
- **Data centre / AI capex spillover:** demand for thermal management, tapes, filtration and electrical components; MMM issued Amazon warrants tied to **~$6B of data-centre-related sales**, and new-product sales rose **34% YTD to ~$3B**.
- **Reshoring/nearshoring capex and pricing discipline:** ~81% of CEOs plan to reshore/nearshore (up from 63% in 2022), supporting NA industrial demand; peer set has delivered structurally rising gross margins (ETN 33.3%→37.6%, TT 31.1%→36.2% since FY22).

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Tariffs and input-cost volatility:** tariff pass-through and stubborn above-target inflation are compressing real volumes and forcing price/volume trade-offs; MMM's own gross margin is still below FY22 levels (39.6% vs 43.7%).
- **Short-cycle/consumer softness and China exposure:** automotive, electronics and consumer DIY remain sluggish; MMM's reported growth of ~1–2% implies it is lagging the cycle rebound that electrification peers are capturing.
- **Litigation and portfolio overhang:** PFAS/earplug liabilities, the retained ~19.9% Solventum stake and the resulting GAAP noise (FY23 –$7.0B net loss) keep a structural discount on the equity and raise cost of capital vs cleaner peers.

### 5. MMM's Competitive Position: **Average (defensible franchise, sub-par momentum)**
- **Positives:** gross margin of 39.6% sits ~190bp *above* the peer median — evidence of genuine pricing power in adhesives/abrasives and a proprietary, specification-driven product base. Operating margin (18.3%) is in line, and Q3'26 showed real self-help (op. margin 23.7%, 4.2% EPS beat, guidance raised).
- **Negatives:** organic growth of ~1–2% is roughly **600bp below the peer median**, and MMM is not participating in the two fastest end-markets (electrification, aerospace) where ETN/PH/TT are compounding at 8–10%. Margin recovery is catch-up, not leadership (ITW earns 26% operating margin at similar growth). Capital allocation is defensive — buybacks shrinking the share count (539M→523M diluted) rather than growth investment.
- Net: strong assets, strong brand, strong gross margin, but a **share-loser in the current upcycle** with a litigation-discounted multiple (~19x FY26 adj. EPS of ~$9, price $171 vs 52-wk range $139–185).

### 6. Sector Rating: **Attractive**
Cyclical recovery is confirmed (PMI 55.6, four-year high), capacity utilisation and pricing power are rebuilding, and the data-centre/electrification/reshoring capex super-cycle gives multi-year visibility. The caveat is dispersion: **Attractive for electrification, thermal and aero-exposed industrials; Neutral-to-Attractive for short-cycle diversified names like MMM**, where the tailwind is real but arriving late and with tariff-driven cost friction. Within that, MMM's sector is a better place to be long than short — but MMM is currently a beta participant in the recovery rather than a leader of it.
