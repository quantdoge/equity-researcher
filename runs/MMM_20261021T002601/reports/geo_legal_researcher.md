Revenue is *legally* concentrated in the US (~44%) even though ~56% is earned abroad — meaning 3M's largest single risk jurisdiction is also its home jurisdiction, where PFAS/earplug claims sit.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Macro-Political Risks

**Risk 1 — PFAS legal-regulatory cascade across jurisdictions (Probability: ~95% of further charges; Impact: High, $1–3B incremental over 5 yrs)**
This is the dominant risk on the name and is now multi-jurisdictional rather than purely US. The $10.5–12.5B public water system (PWS) settlement runs **2024–2036**; the AFFF/PFAS MDL 2873 docket stands at **~23,573 cases with ~15,240 personal-injury cases unresolved as of June 2026**, and the court has pushed parties to meet-and-confer on the PI track. New Jersey settled for up to **$450M** (payments to 2034+). Internationally, 3M paid **€571M to the Belgian government (2022)** and went on trial in Belgium in **February 2026** facing ~1,400 Zwijndrecht residents claiming ~**€30M**. EU REACH entry 79 restricts PFHxA from **10 October 2026**, and the universal PFAS restriction could enter force as early as **2027**. 3M completed its PFAS manufacturing exit by end-2025 — that caps *future* production liability but does nothing for legacy remediation, which is where the tail sits.

**Risk 2 — Transatlantic tariff shock / US-EU political rupture (Probability: Medium; Impact: Low-Moderate, ~$30–40M)**
Unusually for an industrial, 3M has *quantified* a geopolitical scenario: management said Trump's threatened tariffs on eight European nations over Greenland could be a **"$30 million, $40 million impact"** on 2026 earnings. Broader tariff drag was ~**$0.20 of EPS** in the most recent guidance (down from ~$0.60 earlier), with tariff refunds helping recent quarters. The risk is asymmetric: a Greenland/Arctic-driven rupture would hit EMEA profitability (17% of sales) and could invite EU retaliation against US-headquartered manufacturers.

**Risk 3 — US-China escalation and export-control choke points (Probability: Medium-High; Impact: Moderate, ~2–4% of sales at risk)**
China + HK is ~**12% of sales** (~$2.95B), and 3M is simultaneously a supplier into Chinese electronics/auto supply chains and a consumer of Chinese rare earths, magnets and components. Beijing's post-October-2025 tightening of rare-earth and technology export controls, plus reciprocal US measures (e.g. October 2026 sanctions on five Chinese firms under the Protect American IP Act), create two-way risk: input cost/availability and retaliatory squeeze-out of US brands. Management flagged high-single-digit China growth in 2025, which makes the 2026–27 comparison exposed to policy rather than demand.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Combat Arms Earplugs:** $6.01B total ($5.01B cash + $1B stock), payable **2023–2029**; >99% claimant participation, but residual risk from non-participants and future claims. Included as a defined term in the August 2026 credit agreement, i.e. it is a covenant-relevant liability.
- **PFAS PWS settlement:** $10.5–12.5B, 2024–2036, final approval April 2024; ~$840M (8%) in class counsel fees.
- **AFFF/PFAS personal injury MDL:** unresolved; the largest single source of *unquantified* tail risk.
- **State AG actions:** New Jersey up to $450M; other states pursuing natural-resource-damages theories (Connecticut identified in MDL filings).
- **Belgium:** criminal probe into the Zwijndrecht PFOS contamination (reported by Bloomberg Law), parliamentary inquiry, €571M government settlement, and a live 2026 civil trial.
- **Regulators:** US EPA (CERCLA PFAS designation, MCLs), state AGs, EU ECHA/REACH, Belgian regional authorities, plus EU antitrust/state-aid exposure typical of a diversified industrial.
- **Governance/structure:** April 2024 Solventum spin-off left 3M with **retained PFAS liabilities** and ongoing transition/supply agreements — a known source of indemnity disputes.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

No new FCPA/AML/sanctions enforcement action surfaced in this review, and the August 2026 credit agreement shows standard Anti-Corruption Laws, UK Bribery Act and OFAC/Patriot Act representations — i.e. lenders are pricing compliance risk as ordinary-course. Watch items rather than red flags: (a) legacy PFAS disclosure quality and the risk of restated remediation accruals; (b) export-control exposure via China-sourced rare earths and electronics inputs, and via defence/aviation end-uses of 3M tapes and adhesives; (c) data-privacy/AI governance risk flagged in the FY2025 10-K (board-level cyber oversight, third-party assessments) as 3M pushes into data-centre and optical-connectivity products with Amazon.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Geopolitical/trade exposure alone would justify *Medium* — no Russia revenue of consequence post-exit, no nationalisation risk, ~44% US revenue in the safest jurisdiction, and tariff drag quantified at a manageable ~$0.20 EPS. The uplift to **High** comes entirely from the legal/regulatory stack: **~$16.5–18.5B of contracted legal outflows through 2036** against ~$24.95B of annual sales, an *unresolved* 15,000+ case personal-injury docket, and a PFAS regulatory ratchet in both the EU and US that keeps adding new claim cohorts faster than settlements retire them. In an investment-memo frame: **PFAS is 3M's cost of capital, not a one-off charge** — the risk is not existential but it is perpetual, and it caps re-rating independent of the operating turnaround (which, per Q3 2026 results, is genuinely working: EPS $2.50, guidance raised to $9.10–9.15).
