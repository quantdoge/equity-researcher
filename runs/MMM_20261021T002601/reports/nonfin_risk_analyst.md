## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — 3M Company (MMM)

**Prepared:** Oct 2026 | Price $171.37 | Mkt cap ~$88.4B | 60,500 employees | CEO: William Brown

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (trending better, with two structural flags)**

**Evidence:**
- **Board refresh is real.** Neil G. Mitchill, Jr. elected Feb 6, 2026; Jennifer Rumsey (Cummins CEO) elected June 5, 2026 — an outsider CEO, a sitting industrial CEO, and new blood on a board long criticized as insular. PwC ratified as auditor for 2026.
- **CEO/Chair duality — confirmed.** Form 4 titles list Brown as "COB, CEO." Brown succeeded Michael Roman (who held an executive-chairman role post-CEO). Duality is common in Industrials but concentrates power at exactly the moment the company is reshaping portfolio and litigation strategy. *No independent lead director could be confirmed from available disclosures — see data gaps.*
- **Pay scale is large:** Brown's 2025 reported compensation ~$21M. Not disqualifying, but note the company is guiding to EPS on a **"new basis"** in 2026.
- **Transparency flag (minor but watchable):** Q3-26 guidance raise explicitly "incorporates new basis" treatment of certain costs (GAAP EPS $2.63 vs. adjusted $2.50). The guidance raise is the third consecutive this year. Legitimate, but a changed cost-reporting basis alongside raised guidance and a ~$21M CEO package is the sort of combination that deserves a look at the reconciliation table, not just the headline.
- **No accounting-irregularity or restatement signals found.** No SEC enforcement actions surfaced in EDGAR full-text search for MMM — a genuinely clean result for a name this litigious.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary
**No active SEC/DOJ enforcement actions found attributable to 3M.** Regulatory exposure is instead **product-liability and environmental**, and it is large, long-dated, and mostly *quantified*:

| Matter | Status (2026) |
|---|---|
| PFAS / public drinking water (U.S.) | $10.3B tax-adjusted present-value settlement (up to $12.5B), **13-year payment schedule**, final court approval received. Directly comparable to a bond: it is a known, scheduled drain, not a surprise risk. |
| Combat Arms earplugs | ~**$6.0B** settlement, paid through ~2029; Aearo subsidiary bankruptcies resolved. |
| New Jersey PFAS | ~$450M: $43.45M year one, then $275–325M/yr 2026–2034. |
| **Australia (Commonwealth), A$2B / ~US$1.4B** | Filed May 2026 over PFAS at defence bases; **contested hearing Oct 2026**. 3M's defense: never manufactured PFAS in Australia and ceased the relevant sales ~two decades ago. Unreserved. |
| State AG "deceptive marketing" suits (DC, Massachusetts, Texas) | Evolving theory (concealment of health effects, blood monitoring of employees). Low dollar value, **high discovery/reputational value** — this is the vector that generates document-driven headlines. |
| EU/global PFAS restriction | 3M **exited PFAS manufacturing by end-2025**, removing forward regulatory exposure but not legacy cleanup liability. |

**Positive:** manufacturing exit de-risks the go-forward regulatory perimeter. **Negative:** non-U.S. litigation (Australia) is now the live, unpriced frontier.

### 3. Insider Activity Analysis — **Net selling; zero buying. Bearish-leaning, but mostly benign in character**

- **Open-market purchases, trailing 24 months: ZERO.** 0 purchases by 0 insiders. Not a single discretionary buy.
- **Sales, 24 months:** 27 sales by 9 insiders, **~$44.57M** total.
- **Last 180 days:** 0 purchases, 5 sale lines (~$4.31M). Sales: Chavez Rodriguez $663K @ $182.51 (Aug 5), Banovetz $1.34M @ $170.44, Rhodes $1.31M @ $171.20, Goralski $836K @ $170.46 (all Jul 22–23), Reinseth $157K.
- **Character of selling matters:** nearly every sale was a **same-day option-exercise-and-sell (M→S)**, i.e., routine compensation monetization, several clustered immediately post-Q2 earnings. CFO Maheshwari exercised 10,944 and withheld 4,991 for tax — **no open-market sale**.
- **Reading:** this is not a conviction-exit signal, but the complete absence of insider buying at a $88B cap with a "turnaround" narrative and a 52-week-low-to-high swing of $138→$185 is a soft negative. Insiders sold into strength in July at ~$171–182; the stock is now $171.
- **Short interest:** 9.68M shares, ~3.1 days to cover — not elevated, no crowding signal.

### 4. Top Operational Risks
- **Customer/platform concentration is being *created* by the growth strategy.** The Amazon data center/optical-connectivity agreement carries a **warrant for up to ~3.16M shares (~0.6% dilution), 85% vesting only when cumulative payments reach $6B over ~7 years.** The fastest-growing part of the story is now tied to one hyperscaler counterparty, on a vesting schedule that pays Amazon in equity for buying. Watch for revenue-recognition and channel-stuffing questions as the $6B threshold approaches.
- **Litigation-payment-funded balance sheet.** Roughly $16B+ of scheduled legacy payments through the mid-2030s must be serviced from operating cash flow amid a turnaround. 3M refreshed its revolver via a new credit agreement dated **Aug 17, 2026** (JPMorgan as agent) — prudent, but it signals the company is managing to a multi-year cash-commitment calendar, which constrains buybacks, M&A, and crisis response capacity simultaneously.
- **Key-person risk concentrated in Brown.** The turnaround, the portfolio pruning, and the litigation narrative are all personally identified with a CEO less than three years into the job. His prior role was outside 3M; institutional depth was thinned by years of restructuring and the Solventum (health care) spin.
- *(Additional)* **Third-party data exposure:** the Pentagon DMDC personnel-system breach (unauthorized access from ~Oct 2025) exposed 3M personnel records including unencrypted SSNs. Not a 3M-controlled network, but 3M is the employer of record for the affected defense-contractor workforce — a privacy remediation and notification burden with class-action potential.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Improving operating narrative, structurally impaired reputational floor**
- **Positive:** Q3-26 beat, third consecutive guidance raise, 3.3% organic growth, 26.2% adjusted operating margin, new-product sales up 34% YTD to ~$3B. Media tone has flipped from litigation-focused to turnaround-focused (Cramer, Barron's, WSJ all constructive). The Amazon deal reframed 3M as an AI-infrastructure play.
- **Negative:** 3M remains a named defendant in "forever chemicals" coverage globally. Any Australia trial testimony, state AG discovery, or new PFAS health study re-opens the reputational wound regardless of settlement progress. ESG screening exclusion persists, capping the marginal institutional buyer.
- **Sentiment:** stock jumped ~5.8% on earnings — high beta *to good news*, which cuts both ways: a litigation headline will be equally well transmitted.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**
Sub-ratings: Governance **Adequate** · Regulatory/Legal **High** · Operational **Moderate** · Reputational **Moderate-High**.

Rationale: the largest liabilities are **settled, scheduled, and court-approved** — the rare case where a "high legal risk" name has actually de-risked. Offsetting that: zero insider buying, CEO/Chair duality, an unpriced A$2B Australian action, a growth engine dependent on one counterparty's purchasing behavior, and a "new basis" reporting change layered onto raised guidance.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation
1. **Australia PFAS (~US$1.4B), Oct 2026 hearing.** The largest *unreserved* claim and the one most likely to set an adverse precedent for non-U.S. PFAS liability. Escalate on any ruling that pierces the "we never manufactured there" defense.
2. **Zero insider purchases in 24 months** while insiders sold ~$44.6M and the CEO was paid ~$21M. Ask IR to confirm the 10b5-1 status and whether any director has bought on the open market since 2024.
3. **"New basis" cost reporting in 2026 guidance** — request the full GAAP-to-adjusted reconciliation for Q3-26 and confirm the change is disclosure-driven, not a definitional loosening timed to a guidance raise.
4. **Amazon warrant structure (up to 3.16M shares, vesting at $6B of purchases)** — dilutive, and creates incentive for 3M to book data-center revenue aggressively. Request segment-level disclosure on the optical/EBO ramp.
5. **Chairman/CEO duality with no confirmed independent lead director** post-Roman. Confirm governance structure in the 2027 proxy.
