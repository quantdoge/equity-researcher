# 3M Company (MMM) — Financial Risk Assessment

**Data as of:** FY2025 annual (period end 2025-12-31, filed 2026-02-03) and latest quarter Q3 FY2026 (period end 2026-09-30). Price $171.37, market cap ~$88.4bn.

---

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: INVESTMENT GRADE (lower tier, BBB-equivalent)**

Leverage and coverage are unambiguously investment grade: net debt/EBITDA of **1.11x** (FY2025) and interest coverage of **6.34x**. S&P reportedly revised its outlook to **stable** on reduced leverage, affirming ratings and expecting S&P-adjusted leverage to hold near **2.5x** over 12–24 months (secondary source; exact Moody's rating not confirmed — **data gap**).

However, this is investment grade with an unusually fragile equity base. Book equity has collapsed from **$14.77bn (FY2022) to $4.75bn (FY2025)** to **$5.14bn** at Q3'26 — only **13.0% of assets**. Goodwill and intangibles of **$9.40bn** now exceed total equity: **tangible book equity is roughly –$4.3bn**. That is the consequence of ~$10.3bn in litigation charges (2023 net loss of –$7.0bn). This is a balance sheet that withstands stress because of cash flow, not because of its capital base.

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue –20%

Applied to LTM revenue (~$25.5bn) with a conservative 40–45% incremental contribution margin, reflecting fixed-cost absorption on lost volume:

| Line item | Base (LTM) | Stress (–20% rev) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$25.5bn | $20.4bn |
| EBIT | ~$4.73bn | **~$2.5–2.7bn** (–45%) |
| EBITDA | ~$6.0bn | ~$3.9bn |
| Interest expense | ~$0.85bn | $0.85bn |
| **Interest coverage** | ~5.6x | **~2.9–3.2x** |
| **Net debt / EBITDA** | ~1.7x | **~2.6x** |
| Altman Z (book) | 2.82 | **<1.81 — distress zone** |
| S&P-adj. leverage | ~2.5x | **~3.5–4.0x** |

**Verdict: survivable, but the rating and the dividend come under real pressure.** Coverage of ~3x clears covenants comfortably and liquidity remains adequate: **$5.15bn cash** plus an unconfirmed revolver (**data gap**) against ~$2.4bn of near-term debt. The binding constraints are:

1. **Cumulative litigation cash takes:** ~$5bn of earplug settlement cash payable **through 2029**, plus PFAS payments extending 13–25 years. These are non-discretionary and continue through the downturn.
2. **Post-litigation FCF turns thin to negative:** stressed FCF of ~$1.2bn less ~$1.0–1.5bn of litigation disbursements leaves **~$0 to $0.5bn against a ~$1.3bn dividend.** The dividend would not be covered.
3. **The offset is discretion:** suspending buybacks (which ran at $4.8bn in FY2025) instantly restores ~$3bn+ of annual flexibility. Management has already shown willingness to flex. This is why the scenario is *stress*, not *default*.

**Refinancing cliff: not a near-term issue.** Sept 2026 issuance of 3.5% 2028, 3.9% 2031 and 4.1% 2034 notes lengthened the ladder materially. Debt is well-staggered to 2034 at coupons of 3.5–4.1% — far below current market rates at the long end. **No 2026–2028 maturity wall.** Full year-by-year maturity schedule not obtained from the primary 10-K — **data gap**.

⚠️ One flag on the Q3'26 balance sheet: total debt rose **$2.44bn** in a single quarter ($12.55bn → $15.00bn) while goodwill/intangibles rose **$1.97bn** ($7.43bn → $9.40bn) — consistent with a debt-funded acquisition. This raises net debt to $10.16bn and pushes intangibles to 23.8% of assets, adding **goodwill impairment risk** to an already thin equity base.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

**Rationale.** 3M clears every quantitative solvency test on current cash flow (1.1–1.7x net leverage, ~6x coverage, $5bn+ cash, no refinancing wall through 2028) and margins are inflecting hard in the right direction — FY2026 nine-month operating margin of **20.6%**, with Q3'26 at **23.7%** versus 18.3% for FY2025. That trajectory, if sustained, mechanically de-levers the balance sheet and easily covers the dividend.

But the balance sheet is structurally fragile (13% equity/assets, negative tangible equity), cash conversion is poor (CFO/NI 0.71x), leverage is rising again after two years of deleveraging, and there is a multi-year non-discretionary litigation cash drain that management cannot cut in a downturn. The rating is MODERATE rather than HIGH only because the discretionary buyback provides ~$3bn+ of annual downside protection and maturities are laddered through 2034.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Negative tangible equity (~–$4.3bn).** $9.40bn of goodwill/intangibles against $5.14bn of equity. Impairment would be a direct hit to an already thin capital base, and it limits unsecured borrowing capacity in a stress event.
2. **Poor accrual quality.** FY2025 CFO/NI of 0.71x; cumulative –$8.4bn working-capital/other outflow across FY2024–FY2025. Reported earnings are not converting to cash.
3. **Capital returns funded from the balance sheet.** FY2025 distributions of $5.99bn against FCF of $1.40bn (4.3x). Cash fell $1.8bn. Not repeatable.
4. **Re-leveraging in 2026.** Net debt +45% to $10.16bn in nine months; D/E now 2.92x. Direction of travel has reversed.
5. **Litigation overhang is non-discretionary.** ~$5bn earplug cash through 2029; PFAS extending decades. Cannot be deferred in a downturn. Reserve adequacy on the balance sheet not isolated — **data gap**.
6. **Liquidity ratios softening.** Current ratio 1.71x → 1.41x; working capital $6.79bn → $4.85bn.
7. **Volatility (27.45%) is idiosyncratic, not market-driven** (beta 0.94). Litigation headlines produce risk that sector diversification cannot offset.
8. **Altman Z in grey zone at 2.82** on book equity; falls into distress territory (<1.81) under the –20% revenue scenario.
