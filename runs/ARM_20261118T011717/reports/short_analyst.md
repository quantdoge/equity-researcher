## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment

**Rating: MEDIUM — acceptable cash quality, but structurally overstated profitability**

The good news, stated honestly up front: this is **not** a fraud. Cash backs the earnings over a full cycle.

| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Net income ($M) | 524 | 306 | 792 | 904 |
| Operating CF ($M) | 739 | 1,090 | 397 | 1,524 |

| Cash conversion (OCF/NI) | 141% | 356% | **50%** | 169% |
| Cumulative OCF / cumulative NI (4yr) | **148%** | | | |

Cumulative 4-year OCF/NI of 148% clears the quality bar. The FY26 cash conversion of 169% and accruals of -5.8% are genuinely good numbers.

**But three things spoil the story:**

- **Operating cash flow is violently unstable** — $739M → $1,090M → **$397M** → $1,524M. FY25 OCF *fell 64% while net income rose 159%*. Working capital swung -$1,465M in FY25 and -$602M in FY26. The FY26 cash "recovery" is largely the reversal of the FY25 drain, not a durable quality improvement. Do not annualise the 169% conversion.
- **Stock-based compensation of $1,052M exceeds free cash flow of $979M.** SBC-adjusted owner earnings in FY26 were **negative $73M**. SBC is 21.4% of revenue (FY23: 3.0%; FY24: 32.1%) and grew 28% YoY. Q2 FY27 non-GAAP EPS was $0.52 versus GAAP $0.25 — *half of headline profitability is stock comp*.
- **Share count is climbing**: 1.026B (FY23) → 1.061B (FY26) → 1.079B diluted (Q2 FY27). Piotroski F7 = 0.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**🚩 RED FLAG #1 — Receivables are absorbing nearly all incremental revenue (the strongest accounting signal I have)**

| | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 2,679 | 3,233 | 4,007 | 4,920 |
| Net receivables ($M) | 1,165 | 1,129 | 1,898 | 2,466 |
| DSO (days) | 159 | **127** | **173** | **183** |
| AR as % of annual revenue | 43.5% | 34.9% | 47.4% | **50.1%** |

- FY25: receivables rose **+$769M** against a revenue increase of **+$774M** — *effectively 100% of the incremental revenue landed in receivables*.
- FY26: receivables rose +$568M on +$913M revenue — 62% of growth went to AR.
- AR now equals **50% of annual revenue** and 40% of total current assets.
- DSO expanded 55 days in two years.

For a business that typically bills licences annually in advance, 183 days DSO is abnormal. Either ARM is financing customers (margin-dilutive) or recognition is running ahead of collection. Flagged as a red flag for two consecutive years by the detector; I concur, though I'd characterise this as *aggressive commercial terms* rather than proof of fictitious revenue.

**🚩 RED FLAG #2 — Revenue grows 23%, operating income grows 9.8%.** Margin compression is measurable and ongoing:
- Gross margin: 94.86% (FY25) → **92.48%** (FY26), -238bps. Cost of revenue **+80%** vs revenue +23%.
- Operating margin: 20.64% (FY25) → **18.46%** (FY26).
- R&D: $2,009M → $2,776M, **+38%**, now 56% of revenue.
- Capex: $143M → $219M → **$545M** (+149%).
- Latest evidence, not stale: Q1 FY27 op margin **7.6%** vs 10.2% prior year; Q2 FY27 **13.9%** vs 14.4%. Margins are *still* compressing at +27% revenue growth.

**🚩 RED FLAG #3 — The consensus model requires margin expansion that has never occurred.** Analyst estimates embed FY31 revenue of **$28.9B** (5.9x FY26) *and* net income of $11.45B — a **39.7% net margin** versus 18.4% today (+2,130bps). Every dollar of recent growth has *cost* margin. The model assumes a different company.

**🚩 RED FLAG #4 — Valuation has no anchor.** P/S 39.7x (FY26) / 35.7x TTM. P/E 216x GAAP. EV/EBITDA 137x. EV/FCF 197x. P/FCF 199x. On SBC-adjusted owner earnings: **negative**.

**🚩 RED FLAG #5 — Concentration and related parties.** Top 5 customers = **57% of FY26 revenue**, and that group explicitly includes **Arm China and SoftBank Group** — i.e., related parties are inside the concentration disclosure. PRC revenue 18% of total.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

**Threat level: HIGH and rising — and the most dangerous threat is self-inflicted.**

**(a) Arm is now at war with its own customers — this is the core moat problem.** The CSS/own-chiplet strategy requires ARM to compete with Qualcomm, MediaTek, Nvidia, Apple, Samsung, Amazon and Microsoft. No supplier can sustainably compete with its licensees. The financial fingerprint is already visible: cost of revenue +80% (gross margin 94.9%→92.5%) is what happens when a ~95%-margin IP licensor starts shipping physical deliverables. **That 92.5% gross margin is a peak, not a floor**, and the 39.7x sales multiple was never meant for a semi-finished-goods business.

**(b) Qualcomm litigation — quantified, dated, and adjudicated against ARM.** ARM disclosed Qualcomm at **9% of FY26 revenue**. On **13 October 2026** a Delaware federal jury found that **ARM breached its Architecture License Agreement** with Qualcomm. Qualcomm is seeking a royalty suspension of up to five years; remedy estimates run to **~$300M/year, ~$1.5B cumulative**. The remedy ruling is pending. Precedent risk is worse than the dollars: every ALA licensee now knows ARM's licence terms are contestable.

**(c) RISC-V — real but long-dated, and I will not overstate it.** Published estimates are irreconcilable (one report claims 25% overall share in 2026; another shows industrial share moving only 1.6%→2.6% by 2031). Qualcomm acquired Ventana. This is a 2028+ earnings item, not a 2027 catalyst. Treat any RISC-V-driven short thesis with suspicion.

**(d) Arm China governance — ARM consolidates a business it does not control.** A SoftBank-controlled vehicle (Acetone, in which ARM holds a *10% non-voting* interest) owns ~48% of Arm China. ARM books the revenue but cannot direct the entity. Arm China revenue **declined YoY in Q1 FY27**. You are paying 40x sales for a business where 18% of revenue sits behind a structure you don't control and a government you don't influence.

That is a structural, multi-year supply overhang — and simultaneously the reason the float is so thin that the stock can go from $104 to $439 in five months (beta **3.94**).

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — Is There a Credible Bear Case?

**Yes — a valuation bear case of unusual severity, partially de-risked by the 58% drawdown but nowhere near resolved.**

**The one-line thesis:** the market is paying $195B for $979M of free cash flow that is entirely consumed — and then some — by $1,052M of stock compensation, on a business whose margins are compressing while it attacks its own customers, and whose largest architecture licensee just beat it in court.

**Valuation math.** To earn a 10% return over five years you need ~$314B of market cap in 2031; at 25x FCF that requires ~$12.6B of FCF, or roughly **$42B of revenue at a 30% margin**. Consensus FY31 revenue is **$28.9B** — i.e., *even if consensus is exactly right, you under-earn your cost of capital.* The market is already discounting 6.7x EV/sales on 2031 numbers.

**Bear case (FY29, my base):** revenue $9–11B (haircutting consensus $11.1B for the Qualcomm remedy and mix shift), SBC-adjusted FCF margin 25% → $2.3–2.8B. At 30x → **$70–84/share (-55% to -62%)**. At a generous 45x → $105–118 (-36% to -43%).

**What the bull would say, and it deserves respect:** (i) data-centre royalties are *more than doubling* and Q2 FY27 was a record $1.44B, +27%, with non-GAAP EPS of $0.52 beating $0.42; (ii) royalty models have enormous operating leverage once the IP is amortised, so 92%+ gross margins can return; (iii) the AI inference build-out is the single largest incremental silicon TAM in a decade, and ARM takes a royalty on essentially every non-x86 core; (iv) the balance sheet is pristine ($2.29B net cash, Altman Z 4.04) so there is no forced-financing clock; (v) short interest is ~12–14% of free float and the float is tiny — this stock has already demonstrated a 319% five-month advance from $104 to $439. **The bull case on fundamentals is legitimate. The bull case on the price is not.**

Why I stop short of "Strong Short":

- **The fraud thesis is dead** — M-Score -2.48, TATA -5.8%, Z-score 4.04, cumulative OCF/NI 148%. Don't short it as a fraud.
- **Squeeze risk is severe and structural**: beta 3.94, thin float, and a stock that has already run 4x in five months. Being right on valuation and wrong on timing is a total loss here.
- **Momentum has already broken** (peak $439 → $183, -58%), which reduces but does not eliminate the remaining downside, and the psychology of a broken AI-momentum name can overshoot far below fair value — which cuts against a short as much as for it.
- **Elevated short interest (12%+ of float)** means the easy money has been made. The crowd is already here.
