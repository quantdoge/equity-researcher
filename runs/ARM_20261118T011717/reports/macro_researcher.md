# Macroeconomic Assessment — ARM Holdings plc (ARM)

**As of 18 November 2026** | Price $183.39 | Mkt cap ~$195bn | Beta 3.94 | Sector: Semiconductors (IP licensing, UK-domiciled, USD-reporting)

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — tilting risk-off for long-duration growth assets.**

The regime is best described as **reflation with a hawkish Fed and a bear steepener**. US real GDP re-accelerated to **+3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026** (Q2: +2.2%, Q1: +2.5%), unemployment is a low **4.1%**, yet headline CPI has pushed back up to **3.82% y/y**, with the last three months running at a **~4.1% annualised** pace. That combination has ended the easing cycle: the effective fed funds rate bottomed at 3.63% (Apr–Aug 2026) and has already ticked **up to 3.88%** in October, with **~87% odds of a further hike at the 9 December FOMC** (CME FedWatch) and the September SEP median pointing to **4.00–4.25%** through 2027.

Crucially, the market is pricing a *hiking* cycle, not a pause: the **2Y yield (4.74%) sits ~86bp above the effective funds rate**, and the 10Y has backed up to **5.13%** from 4.44% at end-June — a **+69bp** move in under five months. With 10Y breakevens still contained at **2.21%**, the sell-off is almost entirely **real-rate and term-premium driven** (implied 10Y real yield **~2.9%**), not an inflation-expectations shock. That is the most punishing possible configuration for an equity whose value sits almost entirely in 2030+ cash flows.

Importantly, this is **not a credit event**: HY OAS at **334bp** remains historically tight (vs. ~500bp long-run average), having widened only ~70bp off the September low of 265bp. Financial conditions are tight at the *risk-free* curve but still permissive for corporate credit — which is why the AI capex cycle is wobbling on *valuation* and *estimate revisions* rather than on *financing*.

Evidence of that directly in ARM's tape: the stock is **down 58% from its 18 June peak close of $439.46** and **48% since 30 June** — a move that decomposes into roughly *half* duration/discount-rate compression and *half* AI estimate cuts (a major research house trimmed 2030 AI-chip growth in mid-November; the VanEck Semi ETF fell 5%+ and semis now trade at a ~25% discount to the S&P 500).

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

ARM is the **purest long-duration asset in the semiconductor complex**: ~$195bn market cap on FY26 (to 31 Mar 26) revenue of $4.92bn (+22.8%), i.e. **~36x TTM sales** (derived TTM ≈ $5.5bn), with **depressed earnings** ($130m net profit in Q2 FY27 despite $1.443bn revenue, +27% y/y, and licensing +50% to $773m). Beta is **3.94**. There is no meaningful net debt, so credit/refinancing risk is a non-issue — but that also means **100% of the macro transmission runs through the discount rate and through customer capex**, not through the interest line.

| Macro variable | Direction for ARM | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **10Y / real yields (+69bp/6mo)** | **Strongly NEGATIVE** | At ~36x sales with cash flows back-loaded past 2030, equity duration is ~25–30 years. Each 100bp of discount rate ≈ 25–30% of value. This alone explains a large part of the −48% move since June. |
| **Fed hiking to 4.00–4.25%** | **NEGATIVE** | Removes the multiple-expansion tailwind that carried ARM from $105 (Feb) to $439 (June); caps re-rating optionality. |
| **GDP +3.4%, unemployment 4.1%** | **POSITIVE** | Royalty units track chip shipments: smartphones, autos, IoT, and now data centre. A 3%+ nominal growth economy underpins end-demand. |
| **Inflation 3.8% / wages** | **Neutral-to-positive** | ARM's royalty is a % of chip ASP; ASP inflation mechanically lifts royalty dollars, and ARM v9 mix plus rate increases are showing pricing power (royalties +20.5% in FY26). Offset: ~9,600 mostly UK/US salaried staff, opex wage pressure. |
| **Broad USD +3.9% off Sept low** | **NEGATIVE (modest)** | Revenue is geographically dispersed (US, China, Taiwan, Korea, Europe); a firming dollar is a translation headwind. Blunted because most licensing/royalty contracts are USD-denominated. |
| **Tight HY OAS (334bp)** | **POSITIVE / key tell** | No direct ARM leverage, but tight spreads are what enable debt-funded hyperscaler AI capex — ARM's demand driver. Watch this series: sustained widening past ~450bp would be the leading indicator that AI capex gets cut. |
| **Export controls / China** | **NEGATIVE** | November Commerce Department action on chip exports; Arm China has ~48% external (SoftBank-controlled Acetone) ownership and the region is a high-teens/20%+ revenue block. Structural RISC-V substitution risk in China rises with every control. |
| **Tariffs (Section 232 semis)** | **Indirect negative** | ARM licenses designs rather than shipping product, so direct tariff cost is near zero — but tariffs tax device ASPs and volumes, which is ARM's royalty base. |

**Net: the macro is transmitting to ARM almost exclusively as a valuation event, not an earnings event — and it is a headwind.**

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The combination is close to a worst case for this specific business model: policy re-tightening, a ~2.9% *real* 10Y, a firming dollar, and escalating export controls — all bearing on an asset priced at ~36x sales with a 3.94 beta and decelerating profit. The mitigants are real but secondary: nominal GDP growth of 3%+ supports unit volume, credit spreads confirm the AI capex cycle has not broken, and ARM's Q2 FY27 licensing surge (+50%) shows the demand side is intact. **This is a de-rating, not a downgrade of the business.** For a holder, the macro is a multiple problem; for a buyer, macro has now done a large part of the work.

## 5. Surprise Scenario (Bear, with a bull trigger)

**The bear surprise: a December hike plus a "5.5% 10Y".** Consensus treats December as the *last* hike. If CPI's Aug–Sep momentum (~4.1% 3-mo annualised) persists into the January print, the Fed would have to signal 4.50%+, the 2Y pushes to ~5.1%, and the 30Y tests **5.75–6.00%** on term-premium/fiscal-supply concerns. On ARM's sensitivity (~25–30% of value per 100bp), a further +50bp on the long end combined with one more round of AI-capex estimate cuts would compress the multiple toward 20–22x sales — **a $110–135 stock, another 25–40% downside** from $183. The tell would be HY OAS widening simultaneously through 400bp, which would confirm capex cuts rather than mere sentiment.

**The bull trigger to monitor:** October CPI cooled to +0.35% m/m and breakevens are *falling* (2.21%) — if the November/December prints confirm the summer inflation pop was tariff- and energy-driven noise, the Fed hikes once and stops, the 10Y retreats to 4.50–4.60%, and a 3.94-beta name re-rates violently: **$270–300** on unchanged estimates. The asymmetry is genuinely two-sided; the macro is the swing factor, not the fundamentals.
