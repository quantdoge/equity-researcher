## 2. Consensus Assessment: **HIGH agreement on direction, SPLIT on magnitude**

**Consensus (rare and therefore informative):**
- **Business quality is not the debate.** Even the Short Analyst concedes a wide-moat franchise. 92.5% gross margin, ~99% smartphone CPU share, $3.1B net cash. Unanimous.
- **The price is the debate.** **Zero of eleven substantive agents recommend buying at $183.** The most constructive view in the entire book is Growth's HOLD.
- **The recurring engine is decelerating.** Value, Growth, Short, Alt Data and Sector all independently flag the same fact: royalties +8% ($670M, a 7% consensus *miss*) vs licensing +50% ($773M). You are paying a royalty-annuity multiple for a lumpy spot-market mix.
- **Cash earnings are lower than headline.** Value and Fin Risk arrive at the same number independently: **FCF ex-SBC is negative (−$73M)** with SBC at 21% of revenue and ~1.3%/yr dilution with zero buybacks.

**Contested:** whether the 58% drawdown has "done the work" (Macro says half; Alt Data says trade it) or not nearly enough (Value, Quant, Short). This is the only genuine disagreement, and it is a matter of degree, not direction.

**Conflict resolution per framework:** Where Growth's optionality argument (AGI CPU, data-centre share) meets Geo-Legal/Non-Fin's business-model and regulatory flags, **the risk flags take precedence.** The own-silicon pivot is not adjacent to the antitrust exposure — it *is* the antitrust exposure. Any optionality value must be haircut for behavioural-remedy risk, exactly as the Non-Fin agent instructs. I underwrite $0 of AGI CPU optionality in the base case.

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector concentration — additive, not diversifying.** The IP-licensing sub-sector is new, but the *exposure* is not. At **0.70 correlation to QQQ**, a 1% ARM position is functionally a ~1.7% QQQ proxy position (beta 3.85 × 0.70). For any book already long semis/AI infrastructure, this is a duplicate bet with worse downside convexity.

**Correlation matrix (252d) — the key finding:**

| | ARM | NVDA | AVGO | AMD | MU | QQQ | SPY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ARM** | 1.00 | **0.47** | 0.56 | **0.71** | 0.55 | **0.70** | 0.60 |

Two portfolio-relevant readings:
1. **ARM is NOT an NVDA substitute or hedge.** At 0.47, it is *less* correlated to NVDA than to QQQ. Owning both does not diversify an AI-CPU view; it just adds a second, more volatile expression of the same factor.
2. **ARM ≈ AMD correlation (0.71) is the highest peer link** — ARM trades as "high-beta long-duration semis," not as a unique royalty annuity. **If the book already holds AMD or QQQ overweight, ARM is the most expensive way to add nothing new.**

**Factor exposure — worsens every tilt the book already has.** ARM sits in the **worst quintile on both Value and Low-Vol**, with **negative 12-1 momentum (−17.5%)** and top-quintile duration. Adding it increases: high-beta tilt, long-duration tilt, negative-momentum tilt, and AI-theme crowding — simultaneously. The only offsetting factor (GP/A 0.425, accruals −0.058) is mid-pack and already available far cheaper in MU (9.6x P/E, 61% ROE) or AVGO.

**Idiosyncratic risk is the real constraint.** R² vs SPY is just **0.37** — 63% of ARM's variance is stock-specific. A 1% position contributes ~0.9% of standalone portfolio volatility for ~0% incremental expected return. That is an unhedgeable, uncompensated risk budget.
