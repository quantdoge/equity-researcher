### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US–China export controls and PRC retaliation against Arm IP (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High)**
Arm licenses architecture IP that flows into Chinese SoCs; the US has repeatedly expanded BIS controls over advanced computing and semiconductor end-uses, including the foreign-produced direct product rule (CRS R48642; BIS press releases). China has responded by building its own export-control apparatus — the Dual-Use Items Regulation (effective 1 Dec 2024) and, as of 13 Oct 2026, draft MOFCOM measures replacing individual licensing with an "export certificate" system for dual-use goods (Akin Gump, 2026), plus proposed controls on AI technology (White & Case, 26 Oct 2026). Exposure: ~$874M PRC revenue plus PRC-domiciled customers billing elsewhere. Watch item: whether Arm-licensed designs reach Huawei or other Entity List parties, and whether Beijing treats Arm as a "reliable" supplier or restricts Chinese licensees from using it.

**Risk 2 — Licensing-model integrity: Qualcomm litigation remedy and global antitrust (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
The Oct 13, 2026 Delaware verdict is the single largest legal contingency. A federal jury found Arm breached Qualcomm's Architecture License Agreement and intentionally interfered with Qualcomm's Meta business, awarding $1 on each of two claims; Arm won four Technology License Agreement pricing claims (Futurum, 27 Oct 2026; Reuters, 13 Oct 2026). Judge Maryellen Noreika — not the jury — decides Qualcomm's requested remedy: **suspension of royalty payments for up to five years, ~$300M/year, ~$1.5B total** (Futurum; EE Times). The judge has signaled reluctance to set the Armv10 royalty rate herself and will issue a written ruling (Briefs.co). Separately, Round 1 went against Arm: Dec 2024 jury verdict for Qualcomm, Judge Noreika dismissed Arm's remaining claims 30 Sep 2025, and Arm's appeal is pending at the Third Circuit (Paul Weiss; Qualcomm IR).
Compounding this: Qualcomm has filed antitrust complaints against Arm with the **European Commission, US FTC, and Korea Fair Trade Commission** (eeNews Europe; MLex), and the KFTC physically inspected Arm's Seoul office in Nov 2025 (Reuters). The theory — that Arm now competes with its own licensees — is given credibility by Arm's March 2026 launch of its own AGI CPU with Meta as first customer (Bloomberg; NYT, 24 Mar 2026), which "risks angering some of its biggest licensees." A five-year royalty suspension would remove ~6% of annual revenue and, more importantly, would validate a contract-interpretation precedent for every other licensee.

**Risk 3 — Arm China structure, related-party dependence, and PRC regulatory leverage (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium-High)**
Arm no longer controls its China business: the Group disposed of its 48.2% shareholding in Arm Technology (China) Co. Ltd to **Acetone Limited, another SoftBank subsidiary**, retaining a 10% non-voting interest in a vehicle whose primary asset is that 48.2% stake (FY2026 Annual Report; FY2025 10-Q). Arm China revenue is nonetheless reported as Arm revenue and is growing — "total revenue derived from Arm China increased during FY2026" (20-F). Consequences: (a) Arm earns royalties through a party it neither controls nor can audit; (b) the arrangement is a recurring related-party transaction with its controlling shareholder; (c) PRC authorities gain leverage over an entity Arm cannot direct; (d) governance/minority-shareholder optics are poor. A 2022-era leadership dispute at Arm China (Allen Wu) is the precedent for how quickly this structure can become ungovernable.

**Secondary watch items:** SoftBank control (post-IPO ~90% retained) and key-man risk — Nvidia reportedly hired a search firm and approached CEO Rene Haas about its own CEO role (WSJ via 24/7 Wall St, 17 Nov 2026), a live succession overhang for a company whose strategy pivot is CEO-driven. UK political risk is low; no nationalisation exposure in the UK or US.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **Related-party opacity (highest flag):** China revenue is generated through a SoftBank-controlled entity in which Arm holds only a 10% non-voting interest. Royalty flows, transfer pricing, and revenue recognition across this boundary warrant scrutiny; this is precisely the structure that invites both audit-quality and FCPA/books-and-records questions.
- **Export-control dual exposure:** Arm must simultaneously satisfy US BIS rules (as a US-listed, US-technology-dependent licensor) and PRC export-control and anti-foreign-sanctions rules — obligations that can conflict directly. No disclosed violation, but the risk is structural, not incidental.
- **Antitrust/competition:** Multi-jurisdiction complaints plus a vertical-integration pivot (licensor becoming a chip vendor) is the classic fact pattern for abuse-of-dominance findings; the KFTC inspection is a formal escalation step.
- **Data privacy:** GDPR/UK GDPR exposure is modest (B2B licensing), but Arm's developer tools/software business carries standard data-processing obligations. Not a material flag.
- **Sanctions:** No Russia/OFAC-designated-customer exposure disclosed; Entity List screening of Chinese licensees is the operative control.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH** (lower end of High)

**Rationale:** Four factors push ARM above Medium: (1) an adverse jury liability finding with a pending remedy worth up to ~$1.5B and precedent value far larger than the dollar amount; (2) active antitrust complaints in three major jurisdictions plus a regulator site inspection; (3) ~18% China revenue sitting inside a structure Arm does not control; (4) ~14% Taiwan revenue concentrated in a geopolitical flashpoint. It stops short of Very High because the company faces no sanctions or anti-corruption enforcement action, no expropriation risk in its domicile, and the largest single legal contingency is bounded at roughly 0.8% of market cap — material to earnings, not to solvency.
