# SiTime Corporation (SITM) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price (pre-split): $378.29 | Market cap: ~$11.4B (30.1M sh) / ~$13.0B fully diluted (34.5M sh incl. in-the-money converts) | Net debt: $995M | EV: ~$12.4B**
*Note: a 3-for-1 forward split distributes after the close Dec 4, 2026. All per-share figures below are pre-split; divide by 3 for post-split equivalents.*

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow, but currently widening**

SiTime is the leading merchant supplier of **MEMS silicon timing devices** (oscillators, clock ICs, resonators) displacing legacy quartz. Structural advantages are real:

- **Technology/intangible assets:** 20+ years and billions of devices of MEMS resonator + analog IC co-design; timing is a "spec-in" component where jitter, stability and reliability are hard to match. 70.7% non-GAAP gross margin (Q3'26) is direct evidence of pricing power.
- **Switching costs:** design-in cycles in automotive, industrial and datacenter run 2–5 years. Once designed in, displacement is expensive. This is the single best part of the franchise.
- **Efficient scale / consolidation:** the July 2026 acquisition of **Renesas' timing business (TPD)** for ~$1.5B cash + ~4.1M shares (~$3B total) adds VersaClock, FemtoClock and PCIe clocking — turning SiTime from an oscillator vendor into a full timing-platform supplier and removing a competitor. Strategically sensible.

**But the moat is not "wide":**
- The market is cyclical and SiTime's own history proves it: revenue went **$284M (2022) → $144M (2023), a 49% collapse**, then $203M → $327M → ~$906M (FY26E). A wide-moat business does not halve.
- Current growth is **concentrated in AI datacenter/optical networking** (core CED revenue +202% y/y). That is a capex-cycle dependency, not a durable annuity.
- Gross margin has structurally stepped down: 64.5% GAAP (2022) → 51.6% GAAP (Q3'26, vs 70.7% non-GAAP). Acquired clocking products carry lower margins than MEMS oscillators, and the mix shift is permanent.

This is a financially lower-quality company than it was 12 months ago.

The reported DCF tool output ($2.6B) is not meaningful — it extrapolates a depressed trailing FCF of $34.7M, distorted by acquisition working capital. My own scenario work, discounting at 10% and treating SBC as a real cost:

- **Base case** (revenue $906M FY26 → 25% CAGR to 2031, non-GAAP operating margin 42%, SBC 8% of revenue, 21% tax, capex 5%): **EV ~$7.8B → equity ~$6.8B → ~$220/share.**
- **Bull case** (30% CAGR to 2029, 38–40% margins, SBC included): **~$280–300/share.**
- **Super-bull / "everything works"** (40% growth four years, then 30%, 45% margins): **EV ~$13.7B → ~$410/share** — roughly today's price.

Midpoint intrinsic value: **~$260/share (range $180 – $410)**, i.e., the stock embeds a *heroic* outcome and trades at a **~40% premium** to my base-case value. The market's implied expectation: five years of 30%+ growth, margins holding near 43% non-GAAP, and no cyclical interruption — in an industry that cut SiTime's revenue in half three years ago. Analyst targets ($370–$900, most recently $425 after a fall from ~$800) reflect the same momentum logic, not a margin of safety.

**Check on the acquisition:** ~$3B paid for a business doing ~$345M/yr of revenue (+67%) = ~8.7x sales. At current run-rate margins that capital earns ~6–8% after tax versus a WACC of ~10–11% (beta 2.9). Value-creating only if growth and margins both compound — i.e., the market is paying today for integration that hasn't happened yet.
