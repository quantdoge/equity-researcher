## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US data-centre permitting and political backlash — Probability: HIGH | Impact: HIGH**
The Nov 2026 US midterms produced "dozens of ballot initiatives to restrict the development of such facilities, while Democrats won sweeping victories in both houses of Congress" (CNBC, 12 Nov 2026). Separately, "regulators and local authorities are reviewing operational permits for several Nebius AI facilities, with some approvals still pending as of early November 2026," with analysts explicitly warning that "heightened regulatory scrutiny regarding data sovereignty and localization, especially in core markets such as the U.S., Europe, and Israel, may significantly increase compliance costs and limit expansion potential" (Simply Wall St, 9 Nov 2026). This bites directly on guidance: FY26 capex raised to **$27B** (from $20–25B) and FY27 capex guided to **$40–45B** vs. $28.8B consensus (CNBC). Capital deployed ahead of permits = stranded-cost risk. Nebius also lowered FY26 revenue to the low end of $3.0–3.4B, citing delayed third-party capacity (WSJ, 12 Nov 2026).

**② US export controls / single-supplier chip dependency — Probability: MEDIUM-HIGH | Impact: VERY HIGH**
Nebius's entire product is re-exported US advanced compute. Live vectors: (a) the **Chip Security Act (2026)** progressing through Congress, which would mandate location-verification chip-security mechanisms; (b) BIS guidance of 31 May 2026 confirming the November 2023 licensing architecture "remains fully in effect"; (c) Nvidia reportedly instructing data-centre operators to cap Chinese customers at 49% of a site's capacity ("AI Overwatch" measures in the FY2027 NDAA). Because Nebius operates in Finland, France and the UK — *not* US soil — its non-US capacity is squarely in the re-export/licence crosshairs, and European "sovereignty" positioning does not exempt it from US jurisdiction. Compounding: **Nvidia is simultaneously supplier, shareholder (~$329M stake at 30 Jun 2026; a 9.3% stake was reported in Jul 2026) and counterparty** — a single point of failure with related-party optics.

**③ Russia legacy + Israel conflict exposure — Probability: MEDIUM | Impact: MEDIUM-HIGH**
Volozh was EU-sanctioned in 2022 and **delisted in March 2024**; the group exited Russia in Feb 2024 and publicly certified the split. Residual risk is therefore *reputational and enforcement-tail*, not operational: secondary-sanctions screening of counterparties, potential future EU packages reaching former affiliates, and headline risk ("Is Nebius a Russian company?", Moscow Times-sourced coverage) that can trigger index/ESG exclusion and retail de-rating. Separately, the 20-F risk factors explicitly name **Israel and Iran** as operational instability jurisdictions — a live, non-hypothetical security risk to R&D and any Israeli capacity.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **No material active litigation identified.** Targeted searches for securities class actions, arbitrations and enforcement actions against Nebius returned nothing company-specific. Given the 14–20% single-day drop on 12 Nov 2026 after a guidance cut, *securities class action filing risk is elevated prospectively* (AI-related federal filings ran ~118–121 in H1 2026, +30% h/h — NERA/Cornerstone).
- **Permitting/administrative proceedings** are the live legal matter: pending operational approvals for several AI facilities (Simply Wall St, 9 Nov 2026); Hüüru, Estonia plant has drawn local opposition (ERR).
- **Sanctions/trade-compliance infrastructure is unusually well-developed** for a company this size — a public *Sanctions Compliance Policy* (Aug 2024) and *Trade Controls Policy* (Nov 2025), the latter expressly addressing US secondary sanctions on Iran, North Korea, Russia and Venezuela. Read as: management treats sanctions as a principal, not incidental, risk.
- **EU/French/Dutch regulatory perimeter:** EU AI Act high-risk obligations effective 2 Aug 2026; proposed **Cloud and AI Development Act (CAIDA)** with sovereignty requirements; **Dutch FDI screening expanding to six new sensitive-technology categories on 1 Jan 2027** — relevant to any Bolt-on M&A as a Dutch-domiciled acquirer/target.
- **Related-party/insider signal:** dense cluster of Form 4 filings (13 in Oct–Nov 2026) — insider selling into volatility, not a legal issue but a governance watch item.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags
1. **Re-export control** — non-US data centres serving multinational/AI customers: highest-probability enforcement interface.
2. **Customer concentration under sanctions screening** — Microsoft, Meta and sovereign/enterprise buyers; Chinese-linked end-use at overseas sites is the specific BIS theory of the case in 2026.
3. **Russia-nexus residual** — Toloka (spun out 2025, Nebius retained significant stake) has Russian-rooted data-annotation operations; secondary-sanctions and reputational screening exposure.
4. **Data sovereignty/localisation** — EU customers demanding jurisdictional guarantees that conflict with US CLOUD Act/export-control reach; a conflict with no clean resolution.
5. **Middle East operations** — Israel/Iran-adjacent personnel and physical security.

## 5. Overall Rating

**GEOPOLITICAL/LEGAL RISK: MEDIUM-HIGH** *(policy/geopolitical: **HIGH**; traditional legal/litigation: **LOW-MEDIUM**)*

Nebius is not a sanctions-tainted or litigation-burdened issuer — it is a **policy-dependent** one. Its growth case (capex $27B → $40–45B against $12–13B of 2027 revenue guidance) requires permits, power, and US-licensed silicon to arrive on schedule in four jurisdictions simultaneously, while the US political cycle has turned against data-centre buildouts and Washington is tightening control over exactly the non-US compute capacity Nebius is selling as "European sovereign AI." Diversification into Finland/France/UK lowers single-country risk but *concentrates* export-control risk. **Trend: deteriorating** (post-midterm permitting regime + Chip Security Act pipeline). Recommend a 50–100bp policy-risk premium and explicit diligence on facility-level permit status before underwriting 2027 capacity.
