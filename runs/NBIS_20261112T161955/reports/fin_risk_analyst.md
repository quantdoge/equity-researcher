### 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: SUB-INVESTMENT GRADE (≈BB–/B+), with a Low near-term default probability but High structural funding dependence.**

Nebius is a paradox of balance-sheet strength and cash-flow weakness. It holds **$8.04B cash** against **$10.06B total debt** — net debt of only **$2.01B**, ~4% of enterprise value. Critically, **there is no refinancing cliff**: the debt stack is almost entirely long-dated, low-coupon convertible notes (2.00% due 2029, 3.00% due 2031, plus newly issued 2030 and 2034 series from the Aug 19, 2026 $4.50B offering). Short-term debt is just **$46.7M** against $8.0B of cash — a 172x coverage. Convertible indentures of this type typically carry no maintenance financial covenants. Liquidity-based default risk over the next 12 months is negligible.

The credit problem is not solvency, it is **funding dependency**. Trailing-twelve-month (TTM) operating income is **–$684M** and TTM free cash flow is **–$5.88B** after **$11.14B of capex**. Management's FY2026 capex guide is **$20–25B** against an ARR exit target of only **$7–9B**. The company is therefore a serial capital-markets borrower: debt has gone from ~$30M (Sept 2024) to $10.06B (June 2026) to **~$14.5B pro forma** for the August raise. Credit quality is entirely a function of continued access to convertible and secured-debt markets — which in turn is a function of the share price.

### 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue –20%

**Scenario construction:** Capacity is currently the binding constraint, not demand, and a meaningful share of forward revenue is contracted (the Microsoft agreement is the anchor, alongside multi-year enterprise AI contracts). So a 20% revenue shock is most plausibly a *2027–28 AI-compute pricing glut* rather than a 2026 event — a scenario where GPU rental rates deflate and take-or-pay contracts reprice at renewal.

Base case 2027 revenue ≈ $6.0B (conservative vs $7–9B ARR exit). **Shocked: $4.8B.**

| Line item | Base ($6.0B rev) | –20% shock ($4.8B rev) |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit (77% GM) | $4.62B | $3.70B |
| Adj. EBITDA (41% margin) | $2.46B | **$1.97B** |
| Interest expense (~$14.5B @ ~1.6%) | $232M | $232M |
| **Adj. EBITDA / interest** | 10.6x | **8.5x** ✓ |
| Growth capex | $20–25B | cut to ~$4B maintenance |
| **FCF** | –$18B to –$23B | **~–$2.9B** |
| Cash available | ~$12.4B pro forma | ~$12.4B |
| **Runway at shocked FCF** | <3 quarters (forced raises) | **~4 years** ✓ |
| **GAAP net income** | D&A ~$6–7B → **–$3.5B** | **–$4.5B** |

**The counter-intuitive finding: a 20% revenue decline is survivable and arguably *improves* the balance sheet.** The company stops the $20–25B capex treadmill, EBITDA of ~$2.0B covers interest 8.5x, and $12.4B of cash buys ~4 years. **The real risk is not demand falling — it is that the current capex trajectory requires raising another $15–25B, and that rising D&A (from $807.9M TTM toward an estimated $6–7B annually as $20–25B/yr of capex capitalises) drives persistent multi-billion-dollar GAAP losses and book-equity erosion regardless of revenue.**

**The genuinely severe scenario** is a 20% compute-price shock *combined with* PP&E impairment. PP&E of **$14.9B is 53% of total assets** and consists overwhelmingly of GPUs with 5–6 year useful lives. If AI compute pricing resets 20–30%, residual values and payback periods break (the company currently cites ~1 yr 10 mo payback at $20–25M ACV/MW), and a write-down of even 20% of PP&E ($3.0B) against $10.3B of equity would consume ~29% of book equity in one stroke. **Asset-quality risk on the GPU fleet is the single largest unhedged exposure in this capital structure.**

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Capex vastly exceeds the revenue base.** FY2026 capex guide of $20–25B vs $3.0B ARR and $11.1B TTM capex already spent. Every dollar of ARR requires ~$2.5–3 of capital — a capital intensity with no precedent in software, and only workable while capital markets stay open.
2. **Optical cash flow quality.** TTM operating cash flow of **+$5,258M** alongside TTM net income of **+$42.4M** and a net loss in three of four quarters. That gap is driven by working-capital inflows of **+$2.12B (Q1'26)** and **+$0.81B (Q2'26)** — consistent with large customer prepayments/deferred revenue. This is real cash, but it is **financed by future service obligations**, not earnings, and it will reverse if bookings slow.
3. **Net income is not operating income.** Q1 2026 net income of +$621.2M and Q2 2025's +$584.4M are dominated by non-operating gains (fair-value/revaluation items), not the AI cloud business. Do not read the positive EPS prints as profitability.
4. **Rising interest burden.** Interest expense compounded from $4.8M (Q2'25) to $95.0M (Q2'26) — ~20x in four quarters. At ~$14.5B pro forma debt this annualises above $380M.
5. **Dilution is already material and continuing.** Shares outstanding +18% in twelve months, plus the August 2026 exchange of existing converts for equity. "Limited dilution" is the stated intent; the record shows otherwise.
6. **Customer concentration.** The Microsoft contract is the anchor of the backlog and the collateral logic for planned secured financing. A single counterparty's re-sizing of AI capacity would hit both revenue *and* the financing plan simultaneously.
7. **Valuation leaves no cushion.** ~20x EV/ARR and ~44x TTM revenue on a business with negative EBIT. Any growth deceleration is amplified into equity downside through the 2.9 beta.
