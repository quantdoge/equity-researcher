# ESG Analysis — Nebius Group N.V. (NASDAQ: NBIS)

**Price $130.30 | Market cap $31.3bn | Netherlands-domiciled, Dutch one-tier board | 1,543 employees | Data as of 13 Nov 2026**

**Critical framing note:** NBIS is not a normal software company. It is a **1.2 GW-plus AI data centre developer with 2026 capex guidance of $20–25bn and a 5 GW contracted-power target**. Its ESG profile is therefore dominated by *physical infrastructure externalities* (energy, water, land, permitting) and by a *governance legacy* inherited from Yandex N.V. ESG here is not a scoring exercise — it is a proxy for **permitting risk, dilution risk, and cost-of-capital risk**.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM**

**Strengths (genuinely above peer set):**
- **PUE 1.25 portfolio average in 2025** vs. reported global industry average **1.54**; best monthly PUE **1.15** at an own facility. In-house server design cut ~**102 GWh** of consumption in 2025.
- **Water: WUE 0.018 L/kWh** at a liquid-cooled site vs. a projected US average of **0.45–0.49 L/kWh** — roughly **25x better**. Closed-loop direct-to-chip cooling; "geographically agnostic, no operational water intake" design.
- **Energy mix 2025: 80% low-carbon, 62% renewable** of data centre consumption; **100% renewable in Iceland, France and the UK**; Finland transitioned from 95% low-carbon to fully renewable.
- **Heat recovery: 19.5 GWh exported** to Mäntsälä (Finland) district heating in 2025, cutting municipal heat emissions ~65% (~4,000 tCO₂e avoided); ~20 GWh total exported.
- First **formal double-materiality and climate-risk assessment** disclosed in the 2025 report (published 30 Jul 2026); ISO 14001; ESRS used as voluntary reference since 2024.

**Risks:**
- **Absolute emissions are set to rise sharply regardless of intensity.** Contracted power >4 GW today, target 5 GW by YE2026, on $20–25bn of 2026 capex (~$8.1bn spent in H1). Even at modest utilisation this is a multi-TWh ramp; renewable procurement must scale faster than load or market-based Scope 2 rises.
- **Behind-the-meter natural gas:** fuel-cell capacity being developed with **Bloom Energy** in the US adds Scope 1 combustion emissions — a carbon-cost and reputational exposure in a jurisdiction (Missouri) with no carbon price but rising utility/ratepayer scrutiny.
- **Grid strain / ratepayer backlash** is now the single fastest-moving environmental issue for US data centres, and Nebius is building in exactly the states (MO, AL, PA) where utility-cost politics is hottest.
- **Data gap:** absolute Scope 1/2/3 figures and a net-zero target were not retrievable in this review (PDF extraction failed; SEC 20-F text blocked). Absence of a stated science-based net-zero target is itself a screen-level negative.

**Verdict: Medium.** Best-in-class operational efficiency and water performance offset by a very steep absolute energy ramp and no verified absolute decarbonisation target.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**The defining issue is social licence, and it is deteriorating:**

- **Independence, Missouri (1.2 GW AI Factory):** a **16-count lawsuit** in Jackson County Circuit Court alleging violations of Missouri's Sunshine Law, zoning law and the city charter, plus failure to enforce city laws; a temporary restraining order to halt construction was sought (hearing set 22 Oct 2026). Reporting indicates a judge allowed the project to advance **without an environmental review**. As of 31 Oct 2026 plaintiffs dropped the TRO request but the suit continues; local reporting (10 Nov 2026) shows ongoing complaints about **construction traffic nuisance**.
- **Birmingham, Alabama (BHM01, 300 MW):** two lawsuits by Oxmoor Valley residents seeking injunctions — first **dismissed 29 May 2026**, second **filed 29 Jun 2026**.
- **Vineland, New Jersey (300 MW, operating):** contested; suit filed 26 May 2026, **voluntarily dismissed without prejudice** 19 Jun 2026.
- Third-party registry (updated 5 Nov 2026): **"3 of 3 sites carry documented local opposition or litigation."** For a company adding gigawatts annually, a 100% site-contestation rate is a structural, not incidental, cost.

**Other social factors:**
- **Workforce:** small direct headcount (1,543) against an enormous contractor buildout — safety and contractor-labour standards are the material tail risk; no incident data disclosed publicly.
- **Community benefit:** heat recovery in Finland with ~10% lower household heating bills is a genuine, quantifiable positive and a template for the US buildout.
- **Data-labour exposure:** legacy Toloka (crowd-based data annotation) and TripleTen (edtech) businesses carry inherent worker-classification and consumer-protection risk, though both are now non-core.
- **Positives:** SOC 2/3, ISO 27001 family, HIPAA, NIS2, DORA certifications — relevant to enterprise/regulated-sector trust.

**Verdict: Medium-High.** Not a reputational issue of Nebius's own making, but litigation-driven permitting delay is a direct threat to contracted revenue timing.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **4.5 / 10**

**Positives:**
- **5 of 8 directors independent** (62.5%): independent non-executive Chair (John Boynton) plus Arne Grimme, Kira Radinsky, Charles Ryan, Matthew Weigand. **No CEO/Chair duality** — Volozh is a non-independent executive director.
- Dutch one-tier board, SEC reporting company, Big Four auditor, annual AGM with all resolutions carried.
- **Russia exit completed**: full divestment of Russia-based businesses closed July 2024 ($5.4bn; $2.8bn received); Big Four certification of no residual Russia ties commissioned; a formal **Trade Controls Policy** (Nov 2025) covering sanctions and export controls.

**Negatives (why the score is below average):**
1. **Dual-class control.** Class A = 1 vote; **Class B = 10 votes** (33.46m Class B vs. 238.40m Class A). Class B holders — the founder group — control roughly **58% of votes on ~12% of the economics**. Takeover is structurally impossible; minority votes are largely advisory in practice.
2. **Dilution authority.** Board designated for **five years** to issue up to **20% of issued share capital** with **pre-emption rights excludable**, plus 18-month authority to repurchase up to 20%. For a company funding a $20–25bn capex programme largely in equity-linked instruments, this is a standing minority-value risk — and sell-side bears already cite "high capex and dilution" as core to a ~$100 fair value.
3. **Key-person concentration.** CEO/founder Volozh holds ~13%; he is the architect of the post-Yandex strategy and the counterparty face to Microsoft/Meta. No succession disclosure retrieved.
4. **Legacy disclosure failure.** Class A shares were **suspended from Nasdaq trading for roughly two years** (2022–2024) amid the restructuring, with delayed/non-current filings. Recent history, not ancient history.
5. **Sanctions history on the CEO.** Volozh was EU-sanctioned and **removed from the EU sanctions list in March 2024** after condemning the war — a rare relief, but the fact pattern keeps headline risk alive and complicates some institutional mandates.
6. **Related-party opacity.** The Russia sale involved a buyer consortium with Russian industrial ownership; the structure and any residual economic linkages were not ascertainable from available sources (gap).

**Score: 4.5/10** — insulated control, credible board independence and a completed Russia separation are real, but dual-class voting, a 20% pre-emption-free issuance authority and unresolved disclosure history put NBIS in the bottom half of large-cap tech governance.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL to modestly NEGATIVE**

ESG is **not the thesis driver** for NBIS, and it should not be — but it supplies three concrete, quantifiable risk channels:

1. **Permitting/litigation is a live revenue-timing risk, not a reputational abstraction.** With 3 of 3 named US sites contested and the 1.2 GW Independence campus in active litigation *while under construction*, the probability distribution of 2027 revenue has a fatter left tail. This is the ESG factor most likely to become a cash-flow event. **Recommendation: demand site-by-site permitting status disclosure and the Q4 2026 contracted-power bridge before underwriting the 5 GW target.**
2. **Governance is a cost-of-capital penalty.** Dual-class control plus a 5-year, 20% pre-emption-free issuance authority means **minority holders bear dilution without voting recourse** — precisely when the company needs repeated equity-linked raises for a $20–25bn capex programme. This warrants a valuation discount, not a premium, and is the strongest ESG-linked argument on the bear side.
3. **The environmental profile is a competitive asset worth defending.** Sub-1.0-PUE design, 25x-better water intensity and district-heat exports are exactly what municipal planners and Scope-3-conscious enterprise customers (Microsoft, Meta) now demand at the permit stage. **This is the one ESG dimension that should be treated as a moat, and its erosion — via gas fuel cells or a coal-heavy US grid mix — would be an early warning signal.**

**Bottom line:** ESG does not argue for exclusion, but it does **not** support a governance or sustainability premium. Treat NBIS as an **execution-risk asset with below-market governance standards**: the green light for the thesis must come from contracting, power delivery and financing — not from the sustainability report. Key-person risk (Volozh, ~13% + supervoting) and permitting litigation are the two ESG factors capable of permanently impairing capital.
