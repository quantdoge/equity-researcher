# Macroeconomic Assessment — DaVita Inc. (DVA)

**As of 21 October 2026 | Price $175.35 | Market cap $11.19B | Beta 0.795 | Sector: Healthcare / Medical Care Facilities**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **TRANSITIONAL (late-cycle reflation, hawkish policy pivot)**

The US economy is running at trend with full employment, but inflation has re-accelerated and the Fed has **reversed course**. Real GDP grew at a ~2.2-2.5% annualised pace in H1-2026, rebounding from a near-stall in Q4-2025 (+0.2% annualised) — a soft patch that now looks like a shutdown-related air pocket rather than the start of a downturn. Unemployment is 4.2%, essentially unchanged over twelve months. There is no cyclical growth problem.

The problem is at the price level. Headline CPI has re-accelerated from 3.30% y/y in July to **3.72% y/y in September** — a 4.6% annualised run-rate over the last three months. The FOMC responded by **raising the target range 25bp to 3.75-4.00% on 17 September 2026**, ending a 75bp easing cycle executed in H2-2025. The long end has repriced violently: the 10Y went from 4.36% (early May) to 5.15% (19 Oct), touching 5.31% on 5 October, while the 30Y sits at 5.37%. The 2s10s curve is **+34bp and bear-steepening** — this is a term-premium/fiscal-supply move, not a growth signal.

Credit is the tell. High-yield OAS at **3.29%** is still historically tight (long-run median ~4.5%), so refinancing markets are open — but spreads have widened **63bp in a month** (2.66% on 21 Sep → 3.29% on 19 Oct). Liquidity is complacent but no longer improving.

**Regime read:** reflationary growth + hawkish Fed + steep long end + tight-but-widening credit is a **risk-off regime for long-duration, leveraged, administered-price equities** — which is precisely DVA's profile. The market has begun to agree: DVA is **-27% from its 3-August high of $247.49**, including **-6.4% on 20 October alone** on 2.1M shares, coincident with the 5%+ 10Y and widening spreads.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**The core transmission mechanism is not demand — it is the gap between administered price updates and actual cost inflation.**

| Macro variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Inflation (CPI 3.7% & rising)** | **NEGATIVE — high conviction** | DVA cannot price to CPI. The CY2026 ESRD PPS base rate is **$281.06 vs $273.82 (+2.64%)** — a *fixed* CMS update. With CPI at 3.72%, real revenue per treatment is falling ~1% and widening. TTM operating expenses grew **+7.2% y/y to $11.88B** against revenue growth of **+5.2% (Q2-26 revenue $3.55B)** — SG&A alone +8.6%. The cost-price scissors is already visible in reported numbers. |
| **Labor market (U 4.2%, full employment)** | **NEGATIVE — high conviction** | ~78,000 employees; dialysis is labour-intensive (nurses/technicians ≈ two-thirds of the cost base). Full employment plus healthcare wage inflation of 4-6% plus tighter immigration enforcement keeps wage costs above the 2.6% rate update. This is DVA's single largest macro headwind. |
| **Fed policy (hike to 4.00%)** | **NEGATIVE** | Refinancing and floating-rate cost step up; discount rate rises on a leveraged equity. |
| **Long-end yields (10Y 5.0-5.3%)** | **NEGATIVE** | DVA carries roughly **$11-12B gross debt** (total debt +4.4% y/y at June-2026) against an $11.2B market cap — ~50% debt in the capital structure, EV ≈ $22-23B. It has been actively terming out in 2026: an amended credit agreement ($2B 5-yr term loan, $1.5B revolver) plus $750M and $1B senior notes due 2032 — **all executed into a 5%+ Treasury market**. Rule of thumb: every 100bp on the stack is ~$110-120M pre-tax, ~5% of the $2.2B adjusted operating income guidance midpoint. Exact maturity ladder and coupons not verified in this window — flag as an estimate. |
| **Credit spreads (HY OAS 3.29%)** | **NEUTRAL / watch item** | Still tight enough that DVA can refinance — the crucial offset. But the +63bp widening in a month is the leading indicator to monitor; a move to 5%+ would turn a manageable leverage story into a genuine solvency narrative and force buybacks to stop. |
| **USD (+2.3% since August)** | **NEUTRAL / mildly negative** | Post-divestiture the international footprint is a small share of revenue (subsidiaries across ~10-12 countries incl. Brazil and Saudi Arabia per the FY25 EX-21.1), so translation exposure is modest. Dollar strength is a low-single-digit-percent headwind at the margin, not a thesis driver. |
| **Tariffs / trade policy** | **NEGATIVE at the margin** | Dialyzers, tubing, and bicarbonate are heavily imported; medical-device and pharma tariffs raise per-treatment COGS. Compounding this is drug-channel policy risk (EPO/biosimilar pricing). Not quantified in this window. |
| **Fiscal policy (deficits, ACA subsidies, Medicaid)** | **NEGATIVE / mixed** | Large deficits are the proximate cause of the 5%+ long end (term premium, not growth). Payer-mix risk: expiry of enhanced exchange subsidies and state Medicaid pressure shift patients toward lower-reimbursing government payers — and government already funds **~65%+** of DVA's revenue. |
| **Cyclical demand (GDP)** | **POSITIVE / defensive** | ESRD incidence is driven by diabetes/obesity prevalence, not the business cycle. Treatment volumes are among the least cyclical revenue streams in healthcare — flat-to-low-single-digit growth, but it will not fall in a recession. This is the structural offset to everything above. |

**The critical nuance:** DVA is a *defensive revenue, cyclical cost* business. A slowdown does not cut its volume — but a rise in unemployment erodes **commercial payer mix** (commercial rates run ~3-4x Medicare for the same treatment), which is the actual recession channel. DVA's beta of 0.795 understates its true sensitivity, which now runs through rates and payer mix rather than the economic cycle.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Two independent macro variables are moving against DVA at the same time:

1. **Cost-price scissors.** CPI at 3.72% and re-accelerating against a legislated 2.64% CMS update compresses real margins in a business with no pricing power. At 12.0x FY26 EPS guidance midpoint of $14.65, there is no valuation cushion for a margin reset.
2. **Rate/duration regime.** A Fed hike, a 5%+ 10Y, and a bear steepener raise the cost of a $11-12B debt stack that is being refinanced now, and compress the multiple on a business whose EPS growth is substantially **buyback-driven** — buybacks that become more expensive and eventually less fundable as interest costs crowd out free cash flow.

The offsets — non-cyclical volumes, an open credit market at 3.29% OAS, and ~45% Berkshire ownership providing a valuation floor — prevent this from being outright bearish. But the macro wind has clearly shifted from tailwind (2024-25 disinflation vs. ~3% CMS updates) to headwind. Recent price action (-27% from the August high, -6.4% on 20 October alone) is the market repricing exactly this.

---

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BEAR: payer-mix recession**

**The setup:** The Q4-2025 GDP stall (+0.2% annualised) and the September 2026 hike may together prove to be a policy error — a Fed tightening into a slowing labour market. If unemployment breaks from 4.2% to 5.0%+ over the next two to three quarters, the consensus "recession-proof dialysis" view gets tested in the wrong place.

**The mechanism the market will miss:** DVA's *volume* holds up in a recession — but its *revenue per treatment* does not. Job loss converts commercially insured patients to Medicaid/Medicare/charity care. A 300-400bp shift in commercial mix is worth several hundred million dollars of revenue at a fraction of the margin, and it arrives at the same moment that (a) interest expense is resetting higher from 2026 refinancings and (b) the CY2027 ESRD PPS rule folds phosphate binders into the base rate at **+$15.96 per treatment** — a figure providers have argued sits below true acquisition cost, ending the transitional add-on payment that currently supports that drug margin.

**Downside:** EPS could reset from the $14.65 midpoint toward $12 and the multiple from 12x toward 9x — a **$108-125 stock (-30% to -38%)** — while credit spreads at 5%+ shut the refinancing window and force DVA to suspend the buyback, breaking the very mechanism that has generated most of its per-share growth.

**Mirror case (bull):** If Q4 CPI prints below 3% as energy and shelter roll over, the Fed's hike is repudiated, the 10Y retreats to 4.3%, and DVA re-rates to 15x on $14.65 (~$220, +26%) with the refinancing overhang removed. I weight the bear case as more probable given the September CPI momentum (4.6% annualised) and the bear-steepening term premium.
