## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — DaVita Inc. (NYSE: DVA)

**Business context:** ~$11.2B market cap, ~78,000 employees, U.S. dialysis + international + lab + value-based kidney care. Structural feature dominating this assessment: DaVita and Fresenius together control roughly 80% of U.S. dialysis clinics and ~90% of industry revenue, and DaVita is a **repeat defendant in False Claims Act matters**. This is a company whose non-financial risk is concentrated in *government-payer conduct*, not in ordinary operating matters.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate** (with watch items)

**Evidence supporting:**
- CEO/board separation: Javier Rodriguez has been CEO since June 2019 (joined board at that time); no data surfaced indicating he also holds the chair, and leadership transition from founder-CEO Kent Thiry was completed in an orderly, multi-year succession rather than a founder-entrenchment pattern.
- Board refresh: appointment of Dennis Pullin as a new independent director (2025) signals ongoing refresh rather than a stale board.
- Shareholder alignment mechanics: Berkshire's periodic sales are tied to a **planned quarterly repurchase arrangement designed to hold its stake near 45%** — this is mechanical, not a signal of board conflict.
- Disclosure hygiene: 10-Q carries standard Sarbanes-Oxley 302 certifications with no disclosed material weakness or fraud exception in the most recent quarter.

**Watch items (evidence gaps / concerns):**
- **Ultra-concentrated register:** Berkshire ~45% is an extraordinary single-shareholder dependency; it effectively caps takeover optionality and creates an ownership overhang if Berkshire's disposition trend continues.
- **Governance follow-through on compliance:** A board that has overseen four FCA settlements in ~a decade has a demonstrable compliance-oversight problem regardless of its independence profile. I could not retrieve the proxy (DEF 14A) or full 10-K risk factors from the filing tool — **board independence ratio, chair identity, and comp metrics are unverified and should be checked manually before publication.**
- Historically weak shareholder rights (limited proxy access) per older governance commentary; current status unverified.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — **High**

**Active/ongoing enforcement and litigation:**
- **FTC antitrust investigation (Big Dialysis):** Opened ~early 2024 into DaVita and Fresenius over alleged anti-competitive use of **nephrologist non-compete / restrictive covenants** to block physician mobility and limit home-dialysis competition. The FTC's *federal* noncompete rule was subsequently withdrawn/vacated, but the agency has explicitly pivoted to **case-by-case enforcement** — meaning the DaVita probe survives the rule's death. DaVita also operates under a prior FTC order (University of Utah nephrology matter) that already restricts noncompetes in that context — precedent for conduct remedies.
- **Private antitrust class action:** Complaint alleges DaVita and Fresenius engaged in a **multi-prong conspiracy to allocate markets and protect supracompetitive profits**. Treble damages plus potential structural/injunctive relief make this the largest single tail risk in the file.
- **FCA recidivism (the core issue):** Settlements of **$350M (2014)**, **$450M (2015, drug-wastage/Vainer)**, **$495M (2023, alleged kickbacks to physicians to induce "switching")**, and **$34.5M (July 2024)** — explicitly reported as the *third FCA case in ten years*. DaVita has repeatedly paid without exclusion, the widely noted "too big to exclude" dynamic; that protects the franchise but **removes the deterrent that would otherwise force remediation**.
- **HHS/DOJ Medicare Advantage billing scrutiny:** Prior HHS subpoena and a ~$270M MA risk-adjustment settlement (HealthCare Partners coding, 2008–2014). MA coding remains a live enforcement theme sector-wide.
- **HIPAA/data-privacy:** April 2025 ransomware breach (see §4) plus a **$3.8M settlement** over third-party web-tracking disclosure of patient data. State AG and HHS OCR exposure not fully resolved by the private class settlement.
- **Reimbursement policy (the near-term catalyst):** A **CY2027 ESRD PPS / kidney-care policy update emphasizing more competition and innovation incentives** was the proximate driver of the ~25% drawdown from the $247.49 high to ~$175. MedPAC commented on the CY2027 ESRD rule in Aug 2026. Compounding: **expiration of ACA enhanced subsidies** is pushing patients out of commercial/Exchange plans, compressing revenue per treatment (flagged in Q2 2026 results).

*Data gap:* EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned zero hits for DVA, which is inconsistent with the documented settlement history — treat that tool as unreliable here rather than as evidence of a clean record.

### 3. Insider Activity Analysis — **Net Selling (negative signal)**

- **Direction:** Aggregated Form 4 data indicate roughly **14 sales by ~7 insiders totaling ~$580M, with no open-market purchases**. Filing cadence shows large clusters on **2026-05-18** and **2026-08-18** (9 filings each) — the classic signature of option exercises/RSU vesting plus tax-withholding dispositions, which are low-signal.
- **Higher-signal items:** CEO **Javier Rodriguez sold ~39,407 shares for ~$8.2M at ~$209.28** — near the 52-week high ($247.49) rather than into the drawdown. Directors/officers selling into strength with zero offsetting buys is a mild caution.
- **Berkshire:** Sold ~1.66M shares (Jan 29, 2026 @ ~$120.56), ~1.22M shares (May 1, 2026, ~$182.8M), and ~$230M ahead of Q2 earnings, with reports of the stake drifting toward the mid-30s percent range. **Context matters:** these sales are largely governed by a repurchase arrangement intended to keep Berkshire at a target percentage — so interpret as *portfolio/mechanical*, not as an insider verdict on fundamentals. Directionally, however, the marginal buyer of size is stepping back while the CEO sells at highs.
- **Verdict:** No open-market insider buying in the past twelve months, meaningful executive sales near highs, and a dominant holder reducing. Net negative, though not alarming on its own.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Cybersecurity / patient-safety continuity:** The **April 12, 2025 ransomware incident** encrypted parts of the network and disrupted operations at a provider delivering *life-sustaining* therapy; reported exposure reaches roughly **2.7 million individuals** with **SSNs and health data** implicated. Direct costs include a **$15M class-action settlement** (settlement site: DVACyberattackSettlement.com) plus the $3.8M tracking-tool settlement. This is the clearest example of a risk where the financial penalty understates the harm: regulatory (OCR/state AG), reputational, and clinical-continuity dimensions all remain open.
- **Single-payer, single-regulator dependency:** Revenue is overwhelmingly tied to Medicare/Medicaid and Medicare Advantage rate-setting and quality-program (QIP) scoring, with no contractual hedge against rule changes. The 2026 policy update demonstrated how quickly a Washington publication can erase ~25% of market value.
- **Key-person concentration:** Rodriguez is the sole public face of strategy and payor relations; the compliance function has now cycled through repeated settlement-driven overhauls. Loss of the CEO, or a Chief Compliance Officer departure amid an active FTC/DOJ posture, would be materially destabilizing.
- **Labor and clinical staffing:** ~78,000 employees in a chronic-nurse-shortage modality; wage inflation and any unionization/NLRB activity at facility level are unreimbursed cost shocks. *(Watch item — no specific 2026 labor action surfaced in this sweep.)*

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **High**

- DaVita sits at the center of the "Big Dialysis" narrative: two firms, ~80% of clinics, serial kickback settlements, and non-compete allegations that critics frame as patient lock-in. This is a *persistent structural* reputational discount, not an episodic one.
- The $495M physician-kickback settlement (reported as among the largest whistleblower-driven resolutions without government intervention) and the $34.5M 2024 follow-on generated durable headline risk and reinforce the "recidivist" frame with journalists, plaintiffs' counsel, and advocacy groups.
- A ransomware breach touching millions of dialysis patients' SSNs and health records is precisely the category of event that attracts congressional letter-writing and state AG attention, extending the news cycle well past remediation.
- Countervailing positives carry real but limited weight: extensive clinical-research presence at ASN Kidney Week 2026, community disaster-response programs (hurricane preparedness activations), value-based care expansion with Humana, and Berkshire's continued large holding all provide reputational ballast with clinicians and institutional investors.
- **Asymmetry:** reputational damage here has historically not impaired the franchise because patients have no practical alternative. That protection erodes precisely if the FTC/antitrust matters force network or contracting changes.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

The financial profile (9–10x forward earnings, heavy buyback-driven EPS growth per sell-side commentary) already prices in a degree of regulatory pessimism — but it does not price in a structurally adverse antitrust remedy or an FCA resolution that arrives *with* exclusionary or integrity-agreement conditions. Given serial fraud settlements, an active FTC probe, a private antitrust conspiracy suit, 2.7M-person data breach exposure, an actively reform-driven payor, and net insider selling into a 25% drawdown, a **High** rating is warranted. It is not **Critical** only because the historical pattern shows the company reliably converting legal exposure into finite cash settlements while preserving operations.

---

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **FCA recidivism without a publicly confirmed corporate integrity agreement.** Four settlements ≥$34.5M in ten years ($350M / $450M / $495M / $34.5M) and no exclusion. *Escalate:* obtain the 2023 and 2024 settlement agreements and confirm whether a CIA or enhanced compliance-monitoring obligation is in force — this materially changes forward legal-reserve cadence. **Verification gap: unconfirmed.**
2. **FTC antitrust probe + private market-allocation class action.** Potential remedies extend beyond fines to **contracting/non-compete bans or network divestiture**. Treble-damages exposure is not reserved in a way visible from the surface. *Escalate* to legal counsel for a remedy-scenario memo.
3. **April 2025 ransomware affecting up to ~2.7M patients with SSN/PHI exposure.** Private settlement ($15M) does not close OCR/state AG files. *Escalate:* request status of regulatory investigations and remediation attestations post-incident.
4. **Insider pattern:** CEO sale of ~$8.2M at ~$209 near the 52-week high, ~$580M aggregate insider sales, **zero open-market purchases**, alongside Berkshire's steady reduction. *Escalate* for a 10b5-1 plan verification (were the sales pre-scheduled?) before drawing a strong inference.
5. **Payor-mix deterioration from ACA subsidy expiration plus the CY2027 ESRD policy shift.** This is the most probable *timing* trigger for the next leg of de-rating, and it interacts with the antitrust narrative (home-dialysis competition incentives are the policy's stated intent).
6. **Governance data gap:** DEF 14A and full 10-K risk factors were **not retrievable** with the available filing tools (only an exhibit-level 10-Q certification was returned). Board independence, chair/CEO separation, and compensation metric design remain **unverified** and should be closed out manually before this assessment is relied upon.
