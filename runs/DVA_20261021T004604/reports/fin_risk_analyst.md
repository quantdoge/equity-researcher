## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: Sub-Investment Grade (indicative BB/Ba-range, high end)**

DaVita is a structurally levered, cash-generative but shareholder-return-aggressive credit. The profile is quintessential **"stretched BB"**: solid, recurring EBITDA and strong free cash flow, offset by ~5.7x gross leverage, thin book equity, and deeply negative tangible equity.

The defining credit event of FY2025 was **debt-funded shareholder returns**. Total debt rose from $12.07B → $15.05B (+$3.0B, +24.7%) while the company repurchased **$1.79B** of stock. Net debt climbed to $14.29B. In parallel, total equity collapsed from $2.09B → $1.16B. Interest expense rose **+16.8% YoY** to $550M — faster than revenue (+6.4%) or EBITDA (−3.0%). Management is choosing to lever the balance sheet at the top of the cycle rather than de-lever.

Mitigants: interest is comfortably covered by EBITDA (4.8x), FCF ($1.31B) covers interest 2.4x, and the 2025 refinancing (Term Loan B-1 refinanced; **6.75% senior notes due 2033** issued) pushed maturities well out. Current maturities are only **$109M** — there is **no near-term refinancing cliff**. But the 8.00% coupon on the 2030 notes and 6.75% on 2033 confirm the market prices DVA as a sub-investment-grade borrower, and every refinancing resets interest higher.

Multi-decade-low beta paired with single-day 20% gaps is a classic signature of **regulatory/policy event risk** — the stock trades on CMS decisions, not the macro cycle. Sharpe of 0.34 over three years is weak compensation for that tail.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

DaVita is not a near-term liquidity problem — it is a **leverage and resilience problem**. The business generates $1.3B of FCF and interest is covered 4.8x on EBITDA, so default in the next 12 months is not the risk. The risk is that the company has spent its balance-sheet headroom on buybacks precisely when its gross margin is compressing 590bp, its FCF is declining 11%, and its cost of debt is rising. At 5.4x net leverage with $1.2B of book equity and negative tangible equity, DVA has **very little capacity to absorb a 20% revenue shock** without breaching covenants or needing external capital.

Specialized dialysis is also among the most policy-exposed sub-sectors in healthcare — a single CMS rate decision can move EBITDA double digits. That is why a 0.33-beta stock carries 39% volatility and a 41% drawdown.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Debt-funded buybacks at cycle peak.** $1.79B repurchased while adding ~$3.0B of debt; equity halved. Debt/equity 13.0x. This is the single largest credit deterioration signal.
2. **Gross margin collapse of 590bp** (32.9% → 27.0%). Cost of revenue grew +15.8% vs revenue +6.4% — labor/contract cost inflation is not being passed through. Watch closely; if this is structural, EBITDA declines accelerate.
3. **Retained earnings now negative (−$328M)** and **tangible equity −$6.6B**, with goodwill at 44% of assets. Impairment risk directly threatens the already-thin equity cushion.
4. **Interest expense +16.8% YoY**, rising faster than EBITDA. 8.00% coupon on 2030 notes and 6.75% on 2033 = sub-IG cost of capital, and every refinancing resets higher.
5. **FCF declining three straight years** ($1,491M → $1,467M → $1,311M) despite 6%+ revenue growth — conversion (FCF/EBITDA) fell from 64% to 50%.
6. **Altman Z 1.22 (distress) and Piotroski 4/9** — both confirm deteriorating financial quality, even allowing for buyback distortion of the Z-score.
7. **Piotroski leverage and equity-change tests both fail** — the two components most directly tied to default risk.
