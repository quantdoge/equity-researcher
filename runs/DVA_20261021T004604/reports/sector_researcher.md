# Sector Brief — Healthcare Providers: Kidney Dialysis / Renal Care
**Target: DaVita Inc. (NYSE: DVA)** | Price $175.35, market cap $11.2bn | Data as of ~20 Oct 2026

---

## 1. Sector Overview

Dialysis is a **need-driven, administratively-priced oligopoly**. Two large dialysis organisations (LDOs) — DaVita and Fresenius Medical Care (FMS) — control roughly **70%+ of US outpatient dialysis**, and LDOs collectively treat **>85% of US ESRD patients** (NIH/PMC). US treatment volumes are structurally near-flat (ESRD incidence has plateaued, mortality improvement and home-dialysis mix shift reduce centre-based treatment growth), so revenue growth is essentially **price (~2%) + mix/acquisitions (~3–4%)**, not volume. Pricing is set by CMS: the CY2026 ESRD PPS final rule lifted the base rate to **$281.71/treatment (~2.2% net increase)** — payment stability, but no real pricing power. The US dialysis services market is growing ~**3.2% CAGR** (global dialysis ~5–6% on emerging-market demand). The sector sits in **late mature / low-growth** phase: a consolidated, cash-generative, capital-intensive business whose single biggest swing factor is federal reimbursement and payer mix, not the business cycle.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Administrative price stability:** CY2026 ESRD PPS base rate $281.71 (+~2.2%), with market-basket updates effectively guaranteed; AKI dialysis payment aligned to the same rate. Removes the deflationary pricing risk that hurt 2022–23.
- **Oligopoly economics + scale:** Top-2 share >70% and LDO share >85% of patients; new entry requires a de-novo centre network, nephrologist relationships and certificate-of-need/state approvals. DaVita's operating margin (14.7%) runs ~540bps above FMS (9.3%) — direct evidence of scale advantage.
- **Chronic disease pipeline & value-based care:** ~7,000–16,000 patients in risk-based/integrated care models; CKD prevalence growth (diabetes/obesity) guarantees a large pre-ESRD funnel, and Integrated Kidney Care (IKC) plus home-dialysis expansion (FMS investing €300m in home) offer above-BU-growth adjacencies.
- **Cash-flow/deleveraging optionality:** DVA generated $256m FCF in Q2 2026 alone, funding aggressive buybacks (share count down from 90.8m in 2023 to 86.1m in 2025) — a per-share EPS engine independent of sector growth.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Flat-to-negative organic volume:** Industry treatments per day are essentially flat; DVA's 5-yr revenue CAGR is only **3.4%** (peer median 3.9%), and 2022 revenue actually fell (-0.1%). Growth requires share gain or M&A, not the tide.
- **Payer-mix erosion / policy risk:** Medicare Advantage and exchange-plan penetration (with premium-assistance pressure) compresses revenue per treatment; the Aug-2026 (-17% in one session) and 20-Oct-2026 (-6.4%) selloffs were driven by **regulatory/policy fear, not volume** — the sector carries a persistent policy discount.
- **Cost inflation with no price offset:** Labour (78,000 FTEs — nursing/technician wage inflation) and dialysis drugs/devices inflate cost of revenue against a ~2% administratively capped rate. DVA's cost of revenue rose **15.8% in FY2025 vs 6.5% revenue growth**, cutting gross margin from 32.9% to 27.0% and net income 20% to $747m.
- **Leverage:** DVA interest expense rose from $399m (2023) to $550m (2025) — 40% of operating income consumed by debt service, leaving little cushion if reimbursement disappoints.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **#1/#2 duopoly position** in US dialysis with unmatched density (~2,800 US centres) and ~78,000 employees; scale drives procurement, lab, and fixed-cost leverage.
- **Best-in-class margin vs direct peer:** 14.7% operating margin vs FMS 9.3%, despite FMS being larger by revenue — a durable ~5pt structural gap sustained through 2021–25.
- **Growth above peers:** +6.5% FY25 vs +5.7% peer median and FMS +1.5%; Q2 2026 revenue $3.42bn (+6% YoY) with adjusted operating income $579m and adjusted EPS $4.02 — margin recovery is intact.
- **Capital allocation:** ~45% Berkshire ownership, aggressive buyback (FY25 diluted EPS $9.51 on $747m net income), giving EPS growth well above revenue growth. Trading ~9–10x forward earnings after a **27% drawdown from the July 2026 high of $240.96**.
- **Caveats:** the FY25 gross-margin step-down (32.9%→27.0%) alongside only 160bps of operating-margin erosion implies a cost-classification shift as well as real labour/drug inflation — verify in the 10-K. Leverage (interest = 40% of EBIT) and policy exposure cap the multiple.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

**Rationale:** Demand is non-cyclical and recession-proof, the market is a legally protected duopoly, and 2026 reimbursement offers pricing stability. But the sector's economics are structurally capped: **~2% administratively set price × flat volume**, rising labour/drug costs, and a persistent regulatory overhang that has repeatedly produced 15–25% drawdowns. Peer operating margins cluster at 6–16% with only the two LDOs earning true scale returns.

The **sector is Neutral; DaVita is the best-positioned operator inside it.** Alpha here comes from company-specific margin execution and capital allocation (buybacks), not from sector tailwinds — which is exactly why DVA outperformed FMS by ~48 points over 12 months.
