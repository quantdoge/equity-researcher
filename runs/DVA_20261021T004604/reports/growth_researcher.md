## DaVita Inc. (DVA) — Equity Growth Analysis
*Data as of latest available (fiscal 2025 annuals; Q2 2026 reported Aug 4, 2026; shares last observed ~$188 mid-Oct 2026, +65% YTD; Sept 30 close $174.92)*

### 1. Growth Story

DaVita is the largest US kidney-care pure-play (~2,600 US centers, ~88M diluted shares, ~$13.6B revenue), and the growth story has quietly changed character: from a mature, rate-driven dialysis utility into a **cash-flow compounding machine with an emerging value-based-care option**. The FY26 setup is an earnings inflection — adjusted EPS guided to a $14.65 midpoint (+~40% YoY) on only ~3-5% revenue growth — driven by cost-per-treatment leverage, Integrated Kidney Care (IKC) swinging to positive adjusted operating income ($40M in Q2'26), a new Humana CKD value-based partnership (Aug 25, 2026), and an aggressive buyback that has retired 28% of shares since 2020. This is **not** a Cathie Wood-style disruptive innovator; it is a high-single-digit-to-teens EPS compounder whose multiple (12.8x FY26E) already discounts a lot of reimbursement pessimism.

**Critical caveat on quality of growth:** with revenue +5% and operating income roughly flat in 2025 (op margin down 160bps), the +40% FY26 EPS guide is substantially below-the-line — margin rebound plus buybacks — not organic operating leverage. And StockStory notes **treatments were flat over two years** (~7.23M/quarter), i.e., revenue growth is price/mix, not volume.

### 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

- **Core dialysis SAM is mature and largely penetrated.** ~550–600k US ESRD patients on dialysis; Medicare spends roughly $50B+/yr on ESRD (<1% of beneficiaries, ~7% of the Medicare budget). DaVita's ~29M treatments/yr at ~$400 blended revenue/treatment implies a US dialysis pool of ~$30–35B with DaVita holding roughly a third-plus share alongside Fresenius. **Penetration headroom in the core is minimal** — DaVita already treats roughly one in three US dialysis patients. Growth = incidence (+1–2%) + rate updates (~+2%) + payer mix.
- **The real TAM expansion is CKD, not ESRD.** Total Medicare CKD spend is estimated at $120–130B+. DaVita's IKC segment (22 value-based networks, KCC model participation, Humana partnership) attacks pre-dialysis CKD 4–5 populations. This is the only lever that can move DaVita from a 3% grower to a 6–8% grower, and management just proved the model can post positive segment profit. Treat IKC's ~$40M quarterly contribution as the first evidence, not yet a trend.
- **Competitive differentiation:** two-player oligopoly in US dialysis (DaVita/Fresenius), dense center density driving local referral lock-in with nephrologists, and scale-driven lab/administrative integration. This is a moat of *density and workflow*, not proprietary technology.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **CY2027 ESRD PPS final rule (~late Oct/early Nov 2026):** proposed base rate **$299.55**; CMS expects a net payment increase. The single largest near-term re-rating event — a constructive final rule plus market-basket update de-risks 2027.
- **Q3 2026 results — Oct 29, 2026:** consensus EPS **$3.57** on $3.52B revenue; watch cost-per-treatment and whether the $50–100M sequential op-income step-up materializes.
- **Initial 2027 guidance (Feb 2027):** first full year with a profitable IKC and Humana ramp; also the first year absorbing ~$70M of ACA-subsidy-expiration headwind (vs ~$40M in 2026).
- **Home dialysis / expanded HD rollout:** management flagged expanded home HD; mix accretive but capex- and training-intensive.
- **Buyback cadence:** $1.5B+/yr at 11–13x earnings is highly accretive — the quietest catalyst in the story.

### 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Google Trends ("DaVita", US, 5y):** flat 17–26 band with no structural trend; recent readings **18–19 (Sept–Oct 2026) at the low end of the range**. No demand acceleration; mildly consistent with flat treatment volumes.
- **Job postings (careers portal, nurse roles):** pagination indicates **~1,430+ open nurse/clinical listings** — steady staffing churn in a labor-intensive model. Proxy only; no time-series available, so I cannot claim a hiring inflection.

- **Revision score caveat:** the 2029 EPS estimate was cut **−7.3%** (1 analyst only) — thin and long-dated, but directionally worth monitoring.
- **Price action as signal:** +112% from Jan 2026 ($114) to Jul 31 ($240), then **−17% on Aug 5** on 3.8M shares (3x normal) despite an EPS/revenue beat and *maintained* guidance. This was a valuation/momentum unwind, not a fundamental break — a caution about crowding, not about the business.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

DaVita is a genuinely improving business — accelerating revenue, an EPS inflection, an IKC option turning real, and an 85% beat rate — but it is **not a growth investment on this framework**. Rule of 40 of 16, 3% forward revenue growth, flat treatment volumes, and EPS growth manufactured primarily by buybacks and a margin rebound rather than durable top-line expansion disqualify it from a "high-growth premium multiple" thesis. The multiple is undemanding (12.8x FY26E, ~11x FY27E, ~8% FCF yield, ~20% upside to consensus targets), so the risk/reward is decent — but the return driver is **multiple normalization and capital return**, not TAM capture. **Upgrade to Buy** if (a) the CY2027 final rule lands at or above the proposed $299.55 base rate, (b) IKC sustains positive segment profit for 3–4 quarters, and (c) treatment volume growth holds ≥2%.

### 7. Key Risks

- **Reimbursement/policy concentration:** essentially all economics flow through CMS. A weak CY2027 final rule, site-neutral or MA risk-adjustment changes, or payer-mix shifts can compress the whole thesis in a single afternoon.
- **Volume stagnation:** flat treatments over two years in a business with high fixed costs — negative operating leverage is the fastest way to break the FY26 EPS bridge.
- **Long-term disruption (the real innovation risk):** GLP-1/SGLT2-driven slowing of CKD progression reduces future ESRD incidence, and gene-edited xenotransplantation is a credible 10-year threat to the dialysis TAM. Both shrink the terminal pool — the opposite of a Cathie Wood curve.
- **Leverage and rates:** ~$14B net debt incl. leases (~5x EBITDA), $550M interest expense (+93% since 2021); buybacks exceed FCF ($1.79B vs $1.31B in 2025), so shareholder returns are partly debt-funded.
- **Labor and margin:** 78,000 employees; wage inflation and the 2025 gross-margin collapse to 27.0% from 32.9% show how quickly cost-per-treatment can overshoot.
- **Momentum/crowding:** after a +65% YTD run and a −17% single-day air pocket, expect high volatility around each print; competitive threat from Fresenius and nonprofit chains caps pricing.
