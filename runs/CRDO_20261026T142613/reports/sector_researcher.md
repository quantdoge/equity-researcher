## SECTOR BRIEF — CRDO (Credo Technology Group)
*Sector: Technology / Semiconductors — sub-segment: AI data-centre connectivity silicon (AEC, SerDes/retimers, optical DSP). Data through Q1 FY27 (quarter ended 1 Aug 2026).*

---

### 1. Sector Overview

CRDO competes in the highest-velocity niche of semiconductors: the connectivity layer of AI clusters — active electrical cables (AEC), SerDes chiplets/IP, retimers, Ethernet PHY and optical DSPs. Demand is a direct derivative of hyperscaler capex, which the four largest buyers are guiding to ~$700–725bn in 2026, with the top-5 cohort forecast to exceed $1trn annually from 2027. The architecture cycle is favourable: 800G Ethernet switching plus NICs is projected at >$50bn across 2026–27 (~200M ports), with 1.6T becoming the primary AI back-end port speed around 2027 and Ethernet continuing to take share from InfiniBand. AI-focused optical transceivers are estimated at ~$26bn in 2026 (up from $16.5bn in 2025), while the narrower AEC market itself is only ~$4.5bn in 2025 growing ~12.5% CAGR — meaning CRDO's ~200% growth is *share gain and content expansion*, not market growth. The sub-sector is in an **early-to-mid growth phase of an unusually steep cycle**, not a mature one.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Hyperscaler capex super-cycle with visibility into 2027.** $700bn+ of 2026 capex, scaling toward $1trn+/yr; connectivity content per rack rises with every speed node (800G → 1.6T), so CRDO's addressable content per AI cluster compounds faster than capex itself.
- **Copper AEC is structurally winning inside the rack.** AECs consume ~30% less power than optical modules at equal speed and win on cost, latency and failure rate over 1–3m reaches. Power budgets at multi-hundred-MW AI halls make this a hard economic constraint, not a preference — and CRDO pioneered the category with the deepest deployment history.
- **Customer-base widening beyond the anchor account.** Three hyperscalers each contributed >10% of revenue in FY26, with a fourth ramping past that threshold in FY27 — reducing (though not eliminating) single-customer risk and extending the runway of AEC + optical DSP + IP licensing attach.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Sequential growth is decelerating sharply.** QoQ revenue: +20.1% → +51.9% → +7.4% → +9.6% (Q1 FY27). Annualised, the business is now compounding ~40–45% versus 206% in FY26 — a classic "law of large numbers" reset that compressed the stock ~38% even through a beat-and-raise.
- **Margin inflection has turned negative.** Q1 FY27 gross margin fell to 64.5% (from 68.2% in Q4 FY26) and operating margin to 25.2% (from 35.7%): opex rose to $188.4M with SG&A +43% QoQ and implied R&D up ~26% QoQ (~24% of revenue) to fund 1.6T/optical DSP. Competitive pricing in AEC is also visible in the 370bp GM step-down.
- **Technology-substitution and competitor risk.** Co-packaged optics (CPO) and linear pluggable optics threaten longer-reach copper; Marvell has arguably leapfrogged on 1.6T AEC (TSMC 5nm), while Astera Labs, Broadcom, MACOM and Amphenol attack adjacently. Meanwhile TSMC is a sole-source foundry dependency (supplier power = high).

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position — **STRONG**

- **Technology/incumbency:** Category creator in AEC with proprietary low-power SerDes/DSP IP, the most deployed install base, and a broadening portfolio (AEC → optical DSP → retimers/PHY → SerDes IP licensing) that raises content per system.
- **Economics:** 68% gross margin and 33% operating margin are top-quartile in the comp set — only Altera-class IP models and Broadcom do better on mix, and CRDO achieves it at $1.6bn revenue with a debt-free, cash-generative balance sheet.
- **Caveats that cap the rating at "strong, narrowing":** (i) extreme buyer concentration against three-to-four hyperscalers with full pricing leverage — Porter's *buyer power: high*; (ii) gross margin is now trending the wrong way; (iii) Marvell's 1.6T lead and CPO/LPO substitution cap long-run ASP durability; (iv) beta of 3.25 and a 38% drawdown demonstrate the fragility of the multiple. *Barriers to entry are genuinely high* (mixed-signal design talent, 3–5yr hyperscaler qualification cycles, TSMC 5nm access), which is why the ROIC structure persists — but they protect a niche, not a fortress.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

AI data-centre connectivity is the fastest-growing, highest-margin pool in semiconductors today, with 2027 demand visibility underwritten by >$1trn of hyperscaler capex and a 800G→1.6T architecture refresh that *raises* content per port. The rating is tempered by late-cycle characteristics: sequential growth has decelerated to single digits, margins are rolling over, and competitive substitution (CPO, Marvell, Astera) is intensifying — so this is an attractive sector in the **volume-expansion, pricing-normalisation** stage, where share gainers with the best cost/power curve (CRDO, ALAB) still win, but multiple compression is a live risk.
