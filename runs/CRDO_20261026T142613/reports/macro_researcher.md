## Macro Assessment: Credo Technology Group (CRDO)
**As of 26 October 2026 | Price $148.73 | Market cap $27.7B | Beta 3.25**

---

### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **TRANSITIONAL, TILTING RISK-OFF**

The US is in a **late-cycle inflation re-acceleration with a term-premium shock at the long end** — the worst possible combination for a high-beta, long-duration growth equity. Three facts define the regime:

- **The Fed is hiking, not cutting.** After holding at 3.63% effective through most of 2026, the FOMC raised 25bp in September to a 3.75–4.00% target range — with a median dot near 4.1% for end-2026, i.e. more tightening priced. The 2Y at 4.65% sits ~90bp *above* effective fed funds, confirming the market believes the hiking cycle is unfinished. (Consensus forecasts published earlier in 2026 for two cuts to 3.0–3.5% have been decisively wrong — an important lesson for positioning.)
- **Inflation is re-accelerating.** CPI rose from $332.568 (Jun) to $336.298 (Sep): a **3-month annualized rate of 4.55%** versus 3.35% YoY in August. Headline CPI YoY is now **+3.72%**. September alone was +0.65% m/m. Nominal PCE is +6.1% YoY, implying real consumer spending near +2.5% — demand is not the problem; the deflator is.
- **The long end has broken out.** 10Y peaked at **5.31% on 5 October** (levels not seen in over 20 years) and sits at 4.99%; the 30Y is 5.37%. Critically, this is **bear steepening, not inversion**: 2s10s is +34bp. That is a *term-premium* repricing, and there is a structural driver — hyperscalers have moved from 2% of USD IG issuance (2022–24) to an expected ~9% in 2026, with **41% of that issuance beyond 15 years**. The AI buildout is now partly financing itself into higher long yields.

**Credit is the tell.** HY OAS has widened from 265bp (1 Sep) to **338bp** — still historically tight (~8.4% all-in yield) but +73bp in six weeks. Spreads are cheap optionality on a policy error. Equity volatility has already arrived: CRDO is **-51% from its 22 June peak ($302.52)**, despite delivering Q1 FY27 revenue of $479M (+115% YoY) and 50% gross margins. That is pure multiple compression, not an earnings miss.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**NEGATIVE — Discount rate (highest-severity channel).** CRDO is the archetype of a long-duration asset: 115% revenue growth, terminal-value-heavy valuation, beta 3.25. Using June's peak (~$51B cap) versus today's $27.7B, roughly **45% of market value has been erased on essentially unchanged fundamentals**. A 20-year-duration asset loses ~12% of value per 60bp of rate increase in isolation; CRDO has absorbed a 60bp rise in the 10Y *plus* a widening equity risk premium. No debt, no refinancing risk (a genuine strength — self-funded, strong net cash), but **the valuation is the liability**.

**NEGATIVE — AI capex financing channel.** ~90% of revenue comes from the top 10 customers, with **three hyperscalers each >10%**. Those customers financed ~one-third of 2026 capex with debt, and their 15yr+ paper is precisely what is clearing at 5%+. Higher long yields raise the hurdle rate on the marginal datacenter, and widening HY spreads raise it further for neoclouds. This is the direct macro-to-revenue link: **CRDO's order book is levered to the financing cost of its customers' capex.**

**NEGATIVE — Trade / tariffs.** FOMC minutes explicitly attribute projected inflation decline to fading "tariffs, geopolitical developments, and the AI buildout" effects — meaning tariffs are a *live, present* cost. CRDO manufactures in mainland China and Mexico and sells into US hyperscalers, leaving it exposed to Section 232 semiconductor/tariff action and export-control changes. **Greater China (mainland + HK) is a disclosed, meaningful revenue region; exact % requires FY26 10-K verification** — flagging as a data gap rather than guessing.

**NEUTRAL-to-NEGATIVE — USD.** The broad dollar has firmed 3.0% in six weeks to 121.40. Mild tailwind on China/Mexico cost base, mild headwind translating Greater China revenue, and a modest competitive negative versus Asian peers.

**POSITIVE — Demand backdrop.** Real GDP +2.2%, unemployment 4.2%, real consumer spending +2.5%: no recession in the base case. AI infrastructure remains the strongest secular capex line in the economy. **CRDO's fundamental macro is fine; its valuation macro is hostile.** Do not confuse the two.

---

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Driven overwhelmingly by the rates/term-premium channel, not by demand. In a world of 3.7% CPI, a Fed still hiking, and a 10Y near 5% with an upward-sloping curve, the market will not pay a premium multiple for 2032 earnings. The path to a rating upgrade is narrow but mechanical: **10Y back below 4.5% and HY spreads inside 300bp would re-rate this name far more than any earnings beat.** Watch the long end, not the income statement.

---

### 5. The Surprise Scenario — **AI Credit Accident (Bear)**

*Consensus assumes AI capex is self-funded by cash-rich hyperscalers. It increasingly is not.* With one-third of 2026 capex debt-financed, 41% of hyperscaler issuance at 15yr+, the 30Y at 5.37%, and spreads already +73bp in six weeks, the fragile link is a leveraged datacenter/neocloud borrower facing a 2027 refinancing at 8%+ all-in. A single high-profile failure would gap HY OAS from 338bp toward 600bp, force hyperscalers to publicly trim 2027 capex guidance to defend their own credit, and hit CRDO twice — through the discount rate *and* through the order book, at 3.25 beta. Plausible downside: **$90–100 (-35%)**, retesting the March 2026 low of $86.49.

**The mirror-image bull case is equally violent:** if the Fed signals the September hike was the last and the Treasury shifts issuance shorter, the 10Y falling to 4.3% would compress term premium and could re-rate CRDO +50–70% on *unchanged* estimates — the Seeking Alpha upgrade and $300+ price targets show how much multiple is coiled in this name. Either way, **the macro variable that matters for CRDO is the 30-year yield, not the revenue line.**
