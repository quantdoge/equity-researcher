## 1. Growth Story

RB Global is the largest omnichannel marketplace for used commercial equipment (Ritchie Bros.) and total-loss/salvage vehicles (IAA) — a platform that digitizes two enormous, historically offline, fragmented asset classes and monetizes them through transaction fees, marketplace services (Rouse, SmartEquip, Veritread) and, increasingly, principal inventory sales. The post-IAA (2023, ~$7.3B) transformation doubled the revenue base and created a ~$18–19B annualized GTV platform with genuine data/network effects in asset pricing and buyer liquidity. **However, the 2025–26 evidence shows a maturing growth profile rather than an accelerating one:** organic GTV is +7% (total +11% aided by BigIron and other M&A), service revenue is only +5%, and take rate is compressing — this is a high-quality marketplace compounder, not a frontier disruptor.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

- **TAM:** Global used construction/agricultural/heavy equipment transactions ~$150–200B annually; salvage & total-loss vehicles ~$30–40B; adjacent remarketed vehicles and consumer/real-estate auctions add more. Blended **serviceable TAM ~$200B+**.
- **Penetration:** ~$18.5B annualized GTV ≈ **9–10% of SAM** — real headroom, but the easy digitization gains (online auction conversion) are already captured. Incremental share now requires taking consignment volume from dealers and OEMs and growing salvage share against Copart.
- **Differentiation:** Two-sided liquidity, proprietary pricing/valuation data (Rouse, SmartEquip), global buyer base, and a genuinely narrow moat (Morningstar concurs). The **BigIron** deal adds U.S. ag — a structurally under-digitized vertical.
- **Why not a peer:** No listed pure comp at scale except **Copart (CPRT)**.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q3'26 results — Nov 9, 2026** (consensus $0.99 / $1.17B): 7/7 beat history plus a raised FY26 GTV guide (9–11%) sets up a likely beat; the swing factor is take rate.
- **Leadership clarity — Oct 8, 2026 announcement** of executive appointments/organizational changes (Steve Steinberg elevated to CFO, per release headline). A CFO transition is the most plausible explanation for the unexplained **-14% on Aug 5** despite a beat-and-raise; resolution de-risks the overhang.
- **Take-rate stabilization** as automotive pricing incentives lapse and HE&T inventory mix normalizes — the single biggest re-rating lever.
- **Deleveraging:** ~3.2x net debt/adj EBITDA toward <2.5x, alongside **$150M/qtr buybacks** (~3%/yr) and the dividend raised to $0.33/qtr (+6.5%, ~1.6% yield).
- **HE&T volume recovery** tied to dealer capex and infrastructure/ag spending; BigIron cross-sell into the Ritchie network.

## 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Copart's revenue growth collapsed to +0.4% in FY2026** (from +9.7%), and its multiple has compressed to ~10.6x EV/EBITDA. Read-through is **two-sided**: the salvage market is soft (bearish tape), but RBA's Automotive GTV +13% with unit growth of 11% implies **RBA is taking share**.
- **GTV units +11% in Automotive with higher average price per vehicle** — volume-led, not price-led growth, which is the healthier signal.
- **-1% transactional seller revenue** in Q2'26 (consignments) is the clearest warning that heavy-equipment sellers are holding back.
- **7 consecutive beats with +9.5% average surprise** — guidance is being managed conservatively, a classic under-promise pattern.
- *Gap acknowledged:* Google Trends / job-postings / app-download checks were not run within budget; the organic-GTV and unit disclosures above serve as the primary operating proxy.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY (moderate conviction) — a quality-growth marketplace, not a hyper-growth disruptor**

At $81.39 the stock is **-31% from its Feb-2026 high of $118.49** and 2% off its 52-week low, yet fundamentals have *improved* (FY26 GTV guide raised to 9–11%; adj. EBITDA guidance maintained at the top half). Valuation: **13.2x 2026E adj. EBITDA, 18.8x 2026E adj. EPS, 17.1x 2027E, 21.7x FCF (4.6% FCF yield), PEG ~1.8**. Consensus PTs are **$124 (Barclays), $124 (National Bank), $145 (Raymond James)** — ~52% above spot. A 10–12% EPS grower with buyback plus dividend and multiple stability supports low-to-mid-teens annual returns; the drawdown looks sentiment- and leadership-driven rather than fundamental.

**Why not higher:** Rule of 40 of 25, organic growth of 7%, falling take rate, flat operating margins, and a cheaper, higher-margin peer in Copart (10.6x EBITDA, 27% FCF margin). This does not merit a premium growth multiple until take rate inflects.

**Why not lower:** Narrow-moat duopoly assets, share gains in Automotive, 100% beat rate, positive revisions, and a $200B TAM at ~10% penetration.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Take-rate erosion is structural, not cyclical** — mix shift toward low-margin inventory sales and lower-take-rate acquisitions inflates revenue while diluting marketplace economics. Watch for a third consecutive quarter of >100bps decline.
2. **Pro-cyclical end markets** — HE&T volumes are already declining; a construction/ag/freight downturn hits consignment GTV directly.
3. **Leverage and capital-return mismatch** — $4.8B net debt (~3.2x); ~$845M of buybacks plus dividends versus ~$700M FCF means capital returns exceed free cash flow, limiting flexibility for M&A or a downturn.
4. **Leadership turnover / integration execution** — third CEO/CFO transition cycle since 2023, with IAA and BigIron integration still incomplete. This is the main execution risk.
5. **Earnings quality** — ~$315M/yr of adjustments (largely IAA intangible amortization) separate GAAP EPS (~$2.60 TTM) from adjusted (~$4.34); 31x GAAP P/E leaves little room for disappointment.
6. **Competitive intensity** — Copart's scale and margin structure let it defend salvage share aggressively; IAA's historical service issues remain a vulnerability.

**Upgrade trigger:** two quarters of take-rate stabilization + organic GTV >8%. **Downgrade trigger:** take rate below 20% or leverage above 3.5x.
