# RB Global, Inc. (NYSE/TSX: RBA) — ESG Analysis

## 1. ESG Materiality Map

RBA is a **transaction-fee business** (take rate on GTV) with an average ~$4B+ revenue base, not a manufacturer. This inverts the usual industrials materiality ranking — carbon is near-immaterial, while **conduct, controls and product integrity** carry the financial weight.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- **Carbon intensity is structurally low.** Revenue is commission/fee income, so emissions-per-dollar-of-revenue is flattering by construction. Scope 1/2 is limited to yards, facilities and a modest fleet. No disclosed carbon-intensive operations, no stranded-asset exposure, negligible CBAM relevance.
- **Scope 3 is large but the sign is positive.** Reselling used construction, agricultural and commercial equipment *extends asset life* and displaces new-build manufacturing emissions — a genuine circular-economy thesis, and the company's core ESG narrative.
- **The real E risk is site-level, not atmospheric.** IAA salvage yards handle total-loss vehicles containing fuel, oil, coolant and refrigerant — stormwater runoff, soil/groundwater contamination and Clean Water Act permit exposure are the live liabilities. No material EPA/state enforcement action against RBA surfaced in this review, and typical salvage-yard penalties are small ($5K–$35K band for comparable operators). Risk is a portfolio of small remediation events, not a single large liability.
- **Physical climate risk is partially inverted:** hurricane/hail/flood events *increase* total-loss vehicle volume and therefore IAA revenue. The exposure is to yard damage and business interruption, not demand destruction.

---

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Product/consumer safety is the sharp edge.** IAA remarkets total-loss, damaged and low-value vehicles. The structural risk is unsafe or recall-open vehicles re-entering the road, plus title-washing. This sits under 50-state DMV/title regimes and NMVTIS, and is the most plausible source of an attorney-general or regulator action. It also touches insurer relationships, which are the demand engine (a nationwide insurer relationship is a cited growth driver).
- **Workplace safety:** auction yards move heavy equipment, forklifts and vehicles around public bidders. There is a documented history of **fatalities at Ritchie Bros. sites** — including a forklift-related death of a part-time employee and a fall fatality at the Davenport, FL yard. These are episodic rather than systemic, and no pattern of sustained OSHA egregious-citation activity was found — but yard safety is a permanent operational risk given the business model.
- **Labour:** 8,350 employees with a meaningful part-time/seasonal auction workforce plus contracted towing and haulage (Veritread). Union density is low; turnover in yard and driver roles is a cost and safety vector.
- **Community:** positive — auction sites function as local small-business liquidity engines; minimal social-licence friction.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Formal architecture is sound; demonstrated execution is poor.**

*Positives:*
- Separate Board Chair role exists (published Board Chair Role Description), i.e. Chair/CEO separation.
- Majority voting policy, shareholder engagement policy, standalone Nominating & Governance and Audit Committee charters, Code of Business Conduct in five languages.
- Dual-listed NYSE/TSX with a US domestic filer profile since the 2023 continuance — no evidence of a poison pill.

*Material deductions:*
- **The 2023 C-suite rupture is the defining governance event.** CEO Ann Fandozzi and CFO Eric Jacobs departed *simultaneously and abruptly* in August 2023 following a reported breakdown over her compensation package; the stock fell ~4% on the day and Fandozzi subsequently resigned her board seat (February 2024). A board that loses a CEO on a pay dispute, and then a director, has signalled that **compensation-setting and board–management dynamics are dysfunctional** — a direct hit to governance quality and a classic source of a governance discount.
- **Persistent internal control weakness.** Post-IAA, the company has carried a disclosed material weakness in internal control over financial reporting (explicitly risk-factored in the FY2024 10-K). Multi-period material weaknesses elevate audit cost, invite restatement tail risk, and undermine confidence in the acquired business's controls.
- **Contested transformative M&A.** The IAA acquisition was a bet-the-company deal that narrowed shareholder approval and loaded the balance sheet; the architect of the deal was then removed. Strategic accountability is unclear.
- **Conduct exposure at the data crown jewel** (see §5) — a governance failure of the "how we compete" variety, not merely a legal contingency.
- **Not confirmed:** current multiple-voting-share structure and exact board independence ratio could not be verified from primary filings (EDGAR blocked). Historically Ritchie Bros. carried multiple voting shares; this should be confirmed via the current proxy before relying on shareholder-rights parity.

**Score: 5/10** — average-to-weak. Recovering under Kessler (internal COO succession, Oct 2026 executive reshuffle), but the board's credibility on compensation and controls is not yet re-established.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **California SB 253/261** — RBA's revenue (>$1B) puts it **in scope** for Scope 1/2 (and eventually Scope 3) GHG reporting and climate-risk disclosure. Scope 3 is the hard part: the emissions of every machine it auctions are enormous and poorly measured. Compliance cost and greenwashing (AB 1305) liability are real; the underlying emissions are not financially material to RBA itself.
- **EU CSRD** — RBA has European operations (Ritchie Bros. Europe, IAA UK). If EU turnover exceeds the €150M threshold it falls into reporting scope under ESRS with **double materiality**, requiring a Scope 3 inventory it does not presently publish at that granularity.
- **Canada** — TSX listing and Canadian origin bring *Fighting Against Forced Labour in Supply Chains Act* reporting; low risk given asset categories, but the towing/haulage contractor chain is the weak link.
- **SEC climate rule** — currently stayed/uncertain; if revived, Scope 1/2 only, immaterial.
- **CBAM** — not applicable as a principal activity.
- **Most material regulatory regime is not "ESG" at all:** 50-state vehicle title/registration law, NMVTIS, state insurance-salvage rules, and federal/state **antitrust**. RBA's licence to operate runs through these, not through climate disclosure.
- **No ESG-linked debt covenants identified.**

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Genuine circular-economy substance and a low-carbon, capital-light model are offset by a governance record that includes a compensation-driven CEO/CFO exit, a multi-period material weakness, and an antitrust action aimed at the company's own data business. Not a Laggard — the disclosure framework is real and improving — but nowhere near Leader.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

ESG is **not** a valuation driver for RBA. There is no stranded-asset risk, no carbon-cost pass-through risk, no meaningful environmental remediation overhang, and no ESG-linked financing. On the pure E and S axes, RBA screens better than most Industrials peers simply because it burns almost nothing and manufactures nothing.

The thesis-relevant issue is **Governance**, and specifically the **Rouse antitrust litigation** — the one place where an ESG-classified factor can move cash flows. Rouse is small in revenue but high-margin and central to the "insights and services" re-rating narrative. A settlement in the $50–250M range is absorbable against a $14.8B market cap; a behavioural injunction on RRI's data practices is not, because it would impair the moat rather than just the P&L.

Second-order: the 2023–24 governance turbulence and the ~17% de-rating through 2026 (from ~$95 to ~$79.81, alongside take-rate compression) suggest the market is already applying a governance/execution discount. Kessler's tenure and the October 2026 executive reshuffle are the test of whether that discount closes.

**Position:** No ESG-driven exclusion or overweight warranted. Treat the Rouse litigation as a **legal/governance watch item**, not an ESG screen output, and verify the multiple-voting-share structure and board independence ratio via the current proxy (primary-source gap in this review).
