### Investment Memo: RBA (RB Global, Inc.) — 20 October 2026

**Verdict**: HOLD | Conviction: Medium-High
**Price Target**: $90 (~13% upside from $79.81, 19 Oct close) | Bear $60 | Bull $110
**Time Horizon**: 12–24 months

**Investment Thesis**
RBA is a genuine narrow-moat marketplace — #1 globally in heavy-equipment auctions, #2 in the US salvage duopoly — with clean accounting and real free cash flow ($732M FY25), but it is a business earning ~7% ROIC (roughly its cost of capital), watching its take rate erode 110–140bp/yr, and carrying 3.45x and rising net leverage into a hostile rate regime. The stock's 31% de-rating from the February high has brought it to *fair* value ($80–95 base case), not *cheap* value — and the near-term skew is negative heading into the 9 Nov print. This is a hold-and-wait, not an initiate and not a short: the forensic work is unambiguous that there is no accounting catalyst, and the downside case is a second-tier de-rating story already half-played out.

**Key Findings by Domain**
- **Macro & Sector**: Macro is the single largest de-rating force — a bear steepener (10Y ~5.1%, +93bp since June), a resumed Fed hike cycle (Sep 2026), and USD strength hitting a 14x EV/EBITDA, 2.95–3.45x-levered equity. Sector structure remains attractive (salvage duopoly, record 23.1% total-loss frequency, equipment OEM demand at a 2027-recovery trough) but used-asset price deflation is compressing take rates even as volumes grow. Sector rating: Neutral.
- **Geopolitical & Legal**: Medium risk. ~85–90% of revenue in US/Canada/Australia (low-risk core), but live tariff sensitivity, a litigation-dense state-regulated salvage business (>50% of GTV), and a genuine disclosure gap (10-K not retrievable; geographic mix unverified). No DOJ/FTC/SEC enforcement identified; HSR cleared BigIron early.
- **Value**: HOLD, high conviction. Fair value $78–107, base $80–85. At $79.81 the discount is 0–15% — below the required margin of safety; entry ≤$64–68. Negative tangible book (−$1.06B), 59% of assets in goodwill/intangibles, growth bought via M&A (organic ~7%).
- **Growth**: The only bull (moderate conviction): 7/7 beats, FY26 GTV guide raised to 9–11%, automotive share gains vs. a stalling Copart (+0.4% growth). But Rule of 40 = 25, service revenue +5% vs. GTV +11%, flat operating margins — growth quality is deteriorating. Consensus PTs of $124–145 predate the selloff and are discounted heavily.
- **Quantitative Signals**: Neutral-negative (~40/100). Quality fails outright (ROE 7.2%, ROIC 7.1% — bottom of peer set); realised vol 35.5% with R² of only 7% vs. SPY (idiosyncratic, unhedgeable risk); momentum signal internally conflicting in the data feed and given zero weight.
- **Financial Risk**: Moderate. Cash-resilient but covenant-fragile: a −20% revenue scenario pushes net leverage to 5–6x (likely covenant breach) even while FCF of ~$390–450M still covers cash interest and the dividend. Leverage rose +19% in FY25 into flat EBITDA — the wrong direction. Debt maturity schedule and revolver terms are unverified — the highest-priority data gap.
- **Non-Financial Risk**: Moderate, with one HIGH flag: simultaneous CFO and COO separations *with severance* (Oct 2026), disclosed alongside a guidance raise, mid-integration and mid-$1B buyback — second pay-related senior-exit episode after the 2023 CEO rupture. CEO sold at $101–115; only non-employee directors have bought near $80–84.
- **ESG**: Average; not a valuation driver. Low-carbon circular-economy model; the thesis-relevant item is governance — the Rouse antitrust class action ($50–250M settlement range; injunctive risk to a high-margin data asset is the real exposure).
- **Alternative Data**: Mildly bearish. Brand search interest at 12-month lows; IAA traffic −2.1% MoM vs. Copart +1.05%; beat magnitude compressing (+13% → +4%); fresh 52-week low on heavy volume three weeks before the print. Expect a ~3–5% beat, not the historical ~9.5%.
- **Short / Contrarian View**: Explicitly rejects a fraud or distress short (Beneish −2.50, accruals −4.6%, OCF/NI 228%). The legitimate bear case is economics: manufactured growth (gross-basis inventory sales +28% at a 5.9% rate), take-rate concessions buying share from a structurally superior Copart, 18.4x forward adjusted EPS / ~30x true EV/FCF. Best expression: long CPRT / short RBA pair, not a naked short.

**Bull Case**
- Duopoly + structural volume tailwind: record total-loss frequency (23.1% of claims) mechanically feeds IAA, and Copart's growth stall (+0.4%) evidences share rotation toward RBA's automotive segment (GTV +13%, units +11%).
- Real cash, real capital return: $732M FCF, 228% OCF/NI conversion, buyback doubled to $1B, dividend +6.5% — against 8.9% short interest.
- Macro convexity: if oil normalises and the Sep hike proves one-and-done, the discount-rate channel alone implies ~8–12% multiple re-rating, with a +30% move toward the 200DMA (~$101) plausible; equipment OEM demand 29% below year-ago sets up a 2027 consignment-supply recovery.

**Bear Case**
- Take-rate erosion is structural: mix shift to low-take acquired businesses and "pricing incentives" mean volume growth doesn't convert to profit — the moat is being shared with customers. Third consecutive quarter of >100bp decline on 9 Nov confirms the thesis.
- Leverage into tightening: 3.45x net debt/EBITDA and rising, negative tangible equity, $7.1B goodwill — impairment risk with zero tangible headroom if automotive softens.
- Late-cycle multiple regime: 10Y at ~5.1%, Fed hiking, 19x adjusted earnings for ~10% EPS growth — fair, not mispriced, with alt data pointing to a smaller beat and take-rate downside at the 9 Nov print.

**Key Risks**
1. Take rate below 20% or >150bp/yr erosion (the single most important variable).
2. Net leverage >4x or a covenant-relevant downturn; unverified debt maturity schedule.
3. Goodwill impairment on IAA share loss to Copart.
4. Governance: further executive exits or evidence the CFO/COO departures were controls-related.
5. Macro: 10Y through 6% / December hike; the mirror-image bear takes the stock through the $78 low toward $60–65.
