## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **C-suite discontinuity during peak execution load.** New CFO effective **Oct 7, 2026** — one day before the 8-K — while the company simultaneously absorbs **BigIron**, rebuilds IAA's auto workflows, expands the GSA relationship (~35,000 vehicles annualized), and executes an upsized **$1B buyback** (from $500M, Sept 15, 2026; NCIB for up to 10% of float). Institutional knowledge loss in the CFO/COO seats at this moment is the single largest operational risk.
- **Consignor concentration and insurer behavior risk.** The auto segment's economics hinge on a handful of insurer consignors; **Progressive's shifting behavior** is explicitly cited as reshaping the salvage market. IAA's salvage share has fallen to ~35% from ~50% pre-acquisition; further carrier-driven volume diversion (to Copart or in-house) would hit high-operating-leverage earnings hard. Take-rate/fee increases intended to lift revenue per unit raise customer-friction and churn risk — the market's ~22% July–August 2026 selloff on "take-rate concerns" shows how quickly this is penalized.
- **Platform, cyber and physical-infrastructure dependency.** A digital-auction, title-processing and yard-network business cannot tolerate platform outages: RB discloses a CISO-led incident-response and breach-management program under Item 1C, but third-party cyber scoring places Ritchie Bros. around **741/1000** (elevated, not critical), and no material breach has been disclosed. Physical concentration (yards, storage, heavy-haul via Veritread) adds catastrophe/continuity exposure across 8,350 employees and multiple countries.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: MODERATE

- **Customer-satisfaction drag at IAA**: active Better Business Bureau complaint volume at the IAA profile indicates persistent buyer/seller friction (fees, condition disputes, title delays) — a slow-burn brand issue in a relationship-driven, duopoly market.
- **Industry controversy exposure**: salvage/total-loss remarketing is under scrutiny for reselling unsafe or emissions-tampered vehicles (NJ AG action vs. Manheim). Any comparable finding at IAA would be a headline and political risk, not just a legal one.
- **Integration/brand confusion**: the Ritchie Bros. → RB Global rebrand and multi-brand portfolio (IAA, Rouse, SmartEquip, Xcira, Veritread) still reads as a house of brands rather than a unified platform — complicates trust-building with insurers.
- **Market-confidence hit**: the July–August 2026 drawdown on take-rate worries, layered on a rare CEO ouster over pay in 2023, feeds a narrative of a company that repeatedly surprises investors on execution.
- **ESG**: no first-order controversy flagged, but the core business *is* monetizing end-of-life and damaged assets — structural ESG-screen exclusion risk rather than a controversy-score problem.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Strong governance architecture (independent chair, clean enforcement record, 95% say-on-pay, HSR clearance) is offset by unusually high executive turnover, a concentrated insurer-consignor base undergoing behavioral change, and a still-unfinished integration. Nothing here is fraud-adjacent: no restatements, no whistleblower allegations, no SEC/DOJ matters, and the FY2025 10-K asserts no material litigation beyond routine matters.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **[HIGH] Simultaneous CFO and COO separations with severance (Oct 2026), disclosed alongside a guidance raise and a reorg.** Two of the three most senior operating officers leaving within days of each other, one day after a new CFO's start date, during an active integration and a $1B buyback. Escalate to the PM: request the separation agreements/8-K exhibits and press management on whether departures relate to controls, disclosure, or integration disputes.
2. **[MEDIUM] Executive selling above current price with zero open-market executive buying** while the stock sits ~40% below its 2026 highs — only non-employee directors have bought. Monitor next 10b5-1 adoption and any acceleration in sale pace.
3. **[MEDIUM] Director overlap with the largest customer.** Director Michael Sieger is a former **Progressive Claims Group President** (also named to ISG's advisory board in June 2026). Deep carrier ties are strategically useful but warrant related-party/conflict disclosure review given Progressive's outsized influence on IAA volumes.
4. **[MEDIUM] Board-management friction precedent.** The 2023 CEO replacement "after a dispute over compensation" was never followed by a clear public explanation. Combined with the 2026 departures, the pattern suggests recurring compensation-driven executive exits — a retention and key-person risk the market may be under-pricing.
5. **[LOW/MONITOR] Sector regulatory contagion** from state AG/environmental enforcement against salvage auctioneers (NJ vs. Manheim) and AI-based valuation tooling — no action against RBA, but the compliance perimeter is widening.

**Recommended diligence before sizing a position:** obtain the FY2025 10-K Item 1C and legal-proceedings sections, the 2026 DEF 14A compensation discussion (to test whether 2023's pay dispute structural issue was fixed), and the Oct 8, 2026 8-K separation exhibits.
