## 2. Consensus Assessment

**Agreement level: SPLIT — with a negative near-term skew.**

There is **no consensus buy**. The only outright bullish report is Growth. Value explicitly says “fair, not cheap” and refuses to initiate. The Short analyst concedes the accounting is clean and the near-term bull case is “probably stronger right now,” but still calls the risk/reward mediocre. Macro is outright unfavourable. Quant is neutral-negative. Alt Data is mildly bearish. Sector is neutral.

**What the agents agree on:**
- RBA is a genuine narrow-moat marketplace with a durable duopoly position in US salvage and a leading global equipment auction franchise.
- The accounting is clean; this is **not** a fraud or accounting short.
- The core problem is **take-rate compression** — service revenue grew only 5% against 11% GTV growth, with the service take rate down 110–140bp.
- Leverage is elevated and rising: 3.45x net debt/EBITDA, negative tangible equity, 59% of assets in goodwill/intangibles.
- The stock is down ~31–33% from its February 2026 high, but still not statistically cheap on adjusted earnings.

**How I resolve the conflicts:**
- **Growth vs. Value:** Growth’s bullish case relies on multiple stability and consensus price targets of $124–145 that largely predate the selloff and the September Fed hike. Value’s DCF, built on normalized owner earnings and a realistic 8.0–8.5% WACC, says fair value is $80–95. I weight Value higher because the growth report itself admits organic GTV is ~7%, take rate is falling, and operating margins are flat. A 10–12% EPS grower at 19x adjusted earnings is not mispriced enough to compensate for 35% volatility.
- **Short vs. Growth:** The Short analyst’s forensic work is the highest-quality evidence in the packet: RBA is not cooking the books. That removes the tail risk of a fraud catalyst.
