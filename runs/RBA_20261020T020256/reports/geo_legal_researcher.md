### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US tariff & trade-policy shock to used-equipment values (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
Management explicitly names "global trade tensions and … current, proposed or future tariffs, including retaliatory tariffs" among principal forward-looking risks (Q2 2026 release, 4 Aug 2026). The 2026 regime is live and changing: 50% Section 232 steel/aluminium tariffs with 2026 carve-downs/relief negotiations for Canadian and Mexican producers (Supply Chain Dive; PwC Tax Insights, 2026; S&P Global, 12 Jun 2026), plus Canadian counter-tariffs. Transmission channel: tariffs raise new-equipment prices → lift used-equipment residuals (a *tailwind to GTV*), but also compress cross-border consignment and raise the risk on owned inventory, which grew 28% y/y in Q2'26 to $383.7M and now carries a 5.9% inventory rate.

**Risk 2 — US auto-salvage regulatory & litigation regime (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium)**
Automotive is now the largest sector (Q2'26 GTV $2.45B of $4.67B, 52%). The salvage/remarketing business is regulated at state level (dealer licensing, title/odometer compliance, environmental/hazmat handling) and is structurally litigious — evidenced by the *Leon v. IAA* California PAGA class settlement (fully executed settlement agreement, published). Adverse insurer total-loss-frequency trends and state fee-disclosure rules are the recurring pressure points.

**Risk 3 — USMCA / Canada–US cross-border dependency (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium)**
The Canadian-incorporated/US-HQ duality is a real exposure: the auction network sources and sells across the US–Canada border, and 2026 tariff/counter-tariff volatility sits directly on that flow. Secondary Canadian-soil risk: TSX/NCIB and CSA disclosure obligations run in parallel with SEC obligations, doubling regulator count for the same disclosure events.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Cross-border KYC / sanctions screening** — a global marketplace selling high-value, dual-use-adjacent machinery (construction, mining, energy, transport) through UAE and Asian channels requires robust end-buyer screening against Russia/Belarus/Iran-linked parties. No disclosed OFAC/sanctions matter, but the residual risk is structural, and the 10-K sanctions language could not be reviewed (data gap).
2. **FCPA/UK Bribery Act** — no disclosed matter; risk concentrated in Middle East and emerging-market government-surplus auctions, where government entities are counterparties. Elevated-scrutiny category, unverified.
3. **Title/provenance & AML** — salvage and rebuilt-vehicle remarketing carries "title washing" and stolen-vehicle-laundering exposure; requires per-state title controls.
4. **Tariff-driven inventory valuation** — inventory sales revenue +28% y/y means balance-sheet exposure to a tariff/recession reversal; impairment and valuation-allowance risk is real.
5. **Deconsolidation complexity** — the SYNETIQ (UK) deconsolidation in June 2025 is a non-routine accounting/control event that merits auditor-scrutiny attention.
6. **Disclosure granularity** — the shift away from geographic reporting and the recurring "other legal, advisory and non-income tax" line make external legal-risk quantification harder; a legitimate diligence ask on the next call.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Revenue is ~85–90% concentrated in the US, Canada and Australia — three of the lowest-geopolitical-risk jurisdictions globally — with negligible disclosed exposure to sanctioned states and no identified DOJ/FTC/SEC enforcement action. That argues for Low. It is pushed to Medium by (a) acute, fast-moving US tariff and retaliatory-tariff sensitivity that management itself flags, (b) a litigation-dense, state-regulated US salvage-vehicle core representing over half of GTV, (c) the collapse in international diversification post-SYNETIQ, and (d) a genuine disclosure gap — I could not verify geographic revenue or the Legal Proceedings footnote from primary filings within this run, which is itself a reason not to underwrite the risk as Low.

*Recommendation for the memo:* treat geopolitical risk as a **second-order** factor for RBA versus tariff/cycle risk and used-equipment residual values; the legal overhang is manageable in size (single known California PAGA settlement) but the disclosure opacity warrants a direct management question.
