# SECTOR BRIEF — RB Global, Inc. (NYSE/TSX: RBA)
**Sector:** Industrials → Specialty Business Services (asset-light auction/remarketing marketplaces)
**Analysis date:** 16 Oct 2026 | Price $84.30 | Mkt cap ~$15.6B | Beta 0.53

---

## 1. Sector Overview

RBA does not really compete as a diversified industrial — it sits in a narrow, highly concentrated sub-sector: **third-party auction and remarketing of used commercial assets and total-loss vehicles**. The relevant addressable markets are the global used construction/heavy equipment trade (~$133B in 2026, forecast to grow at ~5.6% CAGR to $174B by 2031, Mordor) and the US online salvage/total-loss vehicle auction market, which is effectively a **duopoly** (Copart ~60–62% share, IAA ~35–38% — an HHI well above 3,000). Barriers to entry are high and physical, not technological: ~200+ permitted salvage yards with environmental zoning, insurer contracts awarded on multi-year terms, and two-sided buyer liquidity that is self-reinforcing (more sellers → more bidders → higher recovery values → more sellers). The sector is **asset-light on the technology side but capital-heavy on the yard/logistics side**, and is currently mid-cycle-to-late in a *volume* upswing (record total-loss frequency) but in a *price* downswing (deflating used equipment values). Industrials as a whole — the 25 largest names, $901.7B of FY2025 revenue — earned a 25.0% aggregate gross margin and 10.2% net margin; the marketplace sub-sector earns structurally higher gross margins (40–62%) but, for RBA, lower net margins than the industrial aggregate.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Record total-loss frequency is a structural volume tailwind for IAA.** CCC's Crash Course 2026 puts total-loss frequency at **23.1% of claims — a new industry high**, with bodily-injury severity +10.3% YoY. Rising repair costs (parts tariffs, ADAS recalibration, labour) mechanically push more vehicles from "repair" to "total loss," which is pure volume into the auction channel regardless of vehicle price. Overall claims frequency fell only 3.5% through September 2026 vs 8.3% the prior year — i.e., the volume headwind is *fading*.
- **Share rotation away from Copart is visible in the peer data.** Copart's FY2026 (July) revenue grew just **+0.4%** vs +9.7% and +9.5% in the prior two years — a hard stall — while ACV grew +19.2% and OPENLANE +8.2%. BofA notes IAA's salvage share has fallen to ~35% from ~50% historically, and Morningstar expects **IAA to regain share and lift take rate**. RBA's Q2 2026 automotive GTV "continued to gain share," aided by a new nationwide insurer relationship that validates the physical-yard moat.
- **Used-equipment pricing has stabilised and OEM new-equipment demand is at a trough.** Ritchie Bros.' own Q2 2026 read: **US pricing flat YoY, Canada surging, most earthmoving categories improving**; meanwhile AEM reports US high-horsepower equipment sales **26% below the 5-year average and 29% below year-ago** as of September — a classic late-cycle setup where depressed new sales, an ageing fleet and deferred replacement drive *future* consignment supply into the used channel. RBA is also expanding flagship capacity (Orlando auction extended to six days for Feb 2027) and buying into adjacencies (BigIron, agriculture).

---

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Take-rate compression and stalled operating leverage.** Q2 2026 revenue rose **+11.1%** (to $1,317M) but adjusted EBITDA rose only **+6%** — explicitly attributed to *lower take rates and acquisition mix*. Full-year evidence is the same: FY2025 revenue +9.0% while EBITDA margin fell **240bp to 29.9%** (from 32.3%) and operating margin was flat at 17.7%. Commission-based models cannot outrun falling asset values; auction values in North America were **−6.72% YoY in March 2026** (Sandhills), with used inventory building on dealer lots.
- **A broad de-rating of the marketplace business model.** Over the trailing twelve months RBA is **−20.6%** ($106.14 → $84.30, 52-week range $79.75–$118.49) and Copart is **−38.2%**. This is multiple compression, not just earnings: RBA still trades at roughly **28–36x** annualised FY2026 net income versus ~10% sector net margins, leaving little room if organic growth converges to the high-single digits. Rate sensitivity matters too — RBA's own chair-equivalent (Reserve Bank of Australia's Bullock) warned in September 2026 that oil, Middle East conflict and AI-capex are **re-igniting inflation risk**; higher funding costs directly suppress equipment purchases, dealer floorplan and insurer severity economics.
- **Structural margin disadvantage versus the salvage pure-play.** RBA's 17.7% operating margin is roughly **half Copart's 35.4%**, reflecting a heavier cost base (D&A alone is **$655M, 14% of revenue**) and the lower-margin, GTV-pass-through nature of IAA's transport/titling services. Any loss of salvage share to Copart compounds this, and the IAA integration has already cost ~15 points of share. Combined with **$195M of annual interest expense** and $655M D&A, RBA has limited cushion if equipment values roll over again.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (but second-best economics)**

**Rationale — the moat is real.** RBA is the **#1 global heavy-equipment auctioneer** (Ritchie Bros.) and the **#2 US total-loss vehicle remarketer** (IAA), with a genuinely two-sided network, ~8,350 employees, yards in 190+ countries of buyer reach, and proprietary data assets (Rouse benchmarking, SmartEquip, Xcira simulcast tech) that raise switching costs for insurers and OEMs. The duopoly structure of US salvage and the fragmented, brand-driven nature of global equipment auctions give it pricing power that a normal industrial lacks — evidenced by a 17.7% operating margin on an asset-light, commission-based revenue base.

**Rationale — but it is not Copart.** It earns ~half Copart's operating margin, has ceded ~15 points of salvage share, carries $195M of interest expense and 14%-of-revenue D&A, and is now delivering sub-10% growth with flat margins. Management is responding rationally (buyback doubled from $500M to **$1B**, deleveraging, BigIron/ag expansion, executive reshuffle in October 2026), and the balance sheet gives it room — but the equity story has shifted from "growth compounder" to "self-help and capital return at a cyclical trough." **Verdict: Strong franchise, average-to-strong execution, premium multiple.**

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Structurally this is one of the better sub-sectors in Industrials — oligopolistic, network-effect protected, with a genuine secular volume driver (23.1% and rising total-loss frequency). But three things cap the rating today: (i) **price deflation** in used equipment is compressing take rates even as volumes grow, so revenue growth is not converting to margin; (ii) the **marketplace multiple regime has broken** (RBA −21%, CPRT −38% in twelve months) and RBA still trades at ~28–36x earnings against high-single-digit growth; and (iii) the **cycle position is late-stage in salvage volume but trough-stage in equipment OEM demand**, meaning the next leg of the equipment recovery (AEM: off-highway sales 29% below year-ago, "hope builds for 2026 recovery") is a 2027 event. I would become constructive on evidence of a stabilising take rate (watch Q3 2026, due 9 Nov 2026), IAA share recapture, and a flattening of used-equipment auction values.
