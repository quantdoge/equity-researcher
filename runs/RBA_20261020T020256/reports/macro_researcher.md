## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: RISK-OFF / late-cycle tightening — "stagflation-lite."**

The regime has changed materially in the last 90 days. The Fed **cut** through 2025 (effective funds 5.33% Aug-2024 → 3.63% Feb-2026), then held for seven months, and **hiked 25bp in September 2026** (effective funds 3.63% → 3.75%, implying a 3.75–4.00% target range) — the first increase since 2023. The trigger is an **oil supply shock**: WTI has gone from **$69.60 (6 Jul) to $101.91 (14 Oct)**, +46%, on the Iran/Middle East conflict and Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure. September CPI re-accelerated to **3.7% y/y** (from 2.65% in Dec-2025), and September import prices rose more than expected on energy pass-through.

Critically, the long end is **not** selling off on inflation expectations — 10Y breakevens are stable at **2.35%**. The 10Y has risen **93bp since 26 June (4.38% → 5.31% on 5 Oct, 5.09% now)** on *real* yields/term premium: fiscal supply, foreign official selling, and midterm fiscal risk. That is a **bear steepener** (2s10s now +34bp, no inversion) — the worst possible backdrop for long-duration, leveraged cyclicals. Growth is decelerating (real GDP +2.19% y/y in Q2'26 vs +2.64% in Q1'26) while inflation rises. The dollar is strengthening alongside it (broad index 117.9 on 9 Sep → 121.4), a classic tightening-of-conditions signature.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

RBA is a **transaction-volume business with a levered balance sheet** — net debt **$4.17B**, debt/EBITDA **2.95x**, interest coverage **4.51x** (TTM revenue $4.85B, EBITDA $1.32B), and **~23% of revenue outside the US** (est. US ~77%, Canada ~15%, Europe ~7%, plus Australia). Its GTV depends on *equipment supply*, *buyer financing capacity*, and *used-asset price realisation* — all three are macro-determined.

| Macro driver | Mechanism for RBA | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Fed back to hiking; 10Y 5.1%** | Buyer financing cost for used equipment rises → lower conversion on lots, softer GTV; also ~$25–45M extra pre-tax interest per 100bp on $4.7B debt (4–8% of $606M pretax) | **NEGATIVE** |
| **Term premium / bear steepener** | RBA trades at 14.3x EV/EBITDA and 17x forward PE — a long-duration multiple. ~12% EV de-rating per 100bp of discount rate; +93bp since June explains most of the meltdown | **NEGATIVE (largest single factor)** |
| **Growth decelerating (2.2%)** | Construction/ag fleet capex slows → less *voluntary* consignment supply; but involuntary supply (repos, dealer destocking, insolvencies) rises with a lag — partially offsets | **NEUTRAL / mixed** |
| **Oil $102 (+46%)** | Positive: energy-sector asset turnover, Western Canada/Australia/permian equipment churn, stronger used values for oilfield and earthmoving assets. Negative: transport/fuel costs (Veritread haulage is buyer-paid, so it eats into net bid), and it drives the inflation/rate response | **NET MILD POSITIVE on GTV, NEGATIVE on margin** |
| **Tariffs / import prices +3.7% CPI** | Higher new-equipment prices support used valuations and GTV per lot (good); raises cost base and repair/parts inputs (bad) | **NEUTRAL-to-POSITIVE on revenue, NEGATIVE on cost** |
| **USD strength (+3% since 9 Sep)** | ~23% of revenue translated from CAD/EUR/AUD; a 3% USD move is a ~0.7% EPS translation hit; also makes US equipment relatively dearer to foreign bidders | **MILDLY NEGATIVE** |
| **Q2'26 mix: revenue +11% but services only +5%, inventory sales +18%** | Growth is coming from lower-margin principal inventory sales (RBA takes balance-sheet risk on owned lots) — precisely the wrong mix entering a used-value downturn | **NEGATIVE (quality of growth)** |
| **Buyback expanded $500M → $1B (15 Sep)** | Supportive of EPS/floor valuation, but debt-funded buybacks at 5.1% rates are value-neutral at best | **NEUTRAL** |

**What's working in RBA's favour:** beta of 0.53, a non-discretionary salvage/insurance franchise (IAA) that generates volume in downturns, a counter-cyclical supply of distressed assets, and a short interest of **8.9% of shares out** — the bear case is now well-owned. **What's not:** the market has de-rated the multiple, not the earnings; front-end and belly of the curve still price *more* tightening; and RBA's 19 Oct close came on 4.27M shares (−5.3%) the same week the 10Y held near 5.1%.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

*(was Neutral through H1'26; downgraded on the September Fed hike, the 90bp+ bear steepening, and the re-acceleration of inflation.)*

The economy is not in recession — unemployment is 4.2% and nominal activity is decent — but the *policy mix hitting RBA is the wrong one*: rising real discount rates compress a 14x EBITDA multiple, higher financing costs hit a 2.95x-levered balance sheet, a stronger dollar erodes a fifth of revenue, and a mix shift into owned-inventory sales raises the downside if used values roll over. This is a "rates-first" problem, not a volume problem: the fundamental franchise is intact (Q2 revenue beat consensus by 5.5%), but the valuation is rate-duration.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (BULL)

**Oil normalises → the September hike proves one-and-done → violent re-rating.**

Evidence it's already forming: on 19 Oct, oil *fell* as traders weighed a **US waiver of Russian energy sanctions** and Middle East supply fears eased after Houthi strikes failed to disrupt Saudi output. If WTI retraces from ~$102 to $75–80 by Q1'27, headline CPI rolls back toward 2.5%, the Fed's September hike is validated as a one-off, and the 10Y falls from 5.1% back to ~4.4%. Effect on RBA: **~8–12% pure multiple re-rating from the discount-rate channel alone**, plus lower buyer financing costs re-opening GTV conversion and lower interest expense (~$25–40M). Add a 9% short interest and a $1B buyback, and a +30–35% move toward the 200DMA ($100.83) is plausible. **Catalysts to watch: FOMC 27–28 Oct 2026, Q3 results 9 Nov 2026, and any ceasefire headline.**

*(The mirror-image bear: foreign official selling pushes the 10Y through 6%, the Fed hikes again in December, credit widens, and RBA — now carrying more owned inventory after +18% inventory sales — takes used-equipment writedowns. In that world the stock breaks the August low of $81.79 decisively.)*
