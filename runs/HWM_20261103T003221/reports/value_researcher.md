# Howmet Aerospace Inc. (NYSE: HWM) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price:** $212.97 | **Market cap:** ~$85.6B | **Net debt:** ~$2.3B | **EV:** ~$88B | **Shares:** ~402M
*Basis: FY2025 10-K data plus reported Q3 2026 (quarter ended 9/30/26) and TTM figures. Latest full year is FY2025; Q3 2026 is the most recent period.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **WIDE** (with a fresh crack)

Howmet makes the parts that are hardest to make and hardest to re-source: investment-cast turbine airfoils and seamless rolled rings for jet engines, aerospace fasteners, titanium and nickel superalloy structures, and forged aluminum wheels. Roughly 60–65% of profit is tied to **aftermarket spares** — engine shop visits — rather than new-build aircraft.

Evidence of a genuine moat:
- **Qualification barriers as switching costs.** Every part is designed into an engine or airframe and certified by the OEM and regulator. Re-qualifying a casting supplier takes years and risks the program. This is why Howmet holds sole- or dual-source positions on many high-temperature airfoils.
- **Pricing power is now revealed in the numbers.** Operating margin went from **16.1% (2020) → 17.4% (2021) → 18.3% (2022) → 18.8% (2023) → 22.5% (2024) → 24.8% (2025)**, and 9-month 2026 GAAP operating margin is **29.6%** with adjusted EBITDA margin at **32.2%**, up 320bps YoY. Gross margin expanded from 21.7% to ~37% over six years. Volume leverage plus contractual escalators — not one-off pricing — is doing the work.
- **Returns on capital confirm it.** ROIC: **13.1% (2022) → 15.8% → 20.8% → 24.4% (2025)**, versus a WACC I'd estimate at 8–8.5% (beta 1.2, investment-grade balance sheet). A ~1,600bps spread sustained and widening is the single best moat test there is.
- **Demand visibility.** Record Q3 2026 revenue of $2.70B, **+29% YoY (+25% organic)** — the strongest quarterly growth in company history — driven by engine spares, defense, and **+45% gas turbine revenue** (data-center power demand is a second, independent growth engine). Management guides FY2027 revenue to **~$11.5B, +12%**, on the aircraft order backlog.

**The crack:** in September 2026 the stock fell ~11% in a day when **GE Aerospace (its largest customer) acquired a rival casting supplier** — the "customer becomes competitor" risk. I do not think GE can re-source qualified airfoils quickly or cheaply, but it signals that the largest buyer intends to erode single-source economics at the margin. Combined with Boeing's 737 delivery miss, this is why the shares are ~30% off their $310 high. Moat trend: **widening on economics, narrowing slightly on customer structure.**

## 4. Management Quality — **Excellent (one caveat)**

- **John Plant** has run the business since the 2020 Arconic separation (Executive Chairman & CEO). Capital allocation has been textbook: divested non-core assets, cut net debt from $4.35B (2021) to $2.31B, retired 7.6% of shares, initiated and then **raised the dividend 17%** this quarter, and just authorized an additional **$5B buyback** ($5.4B total authorization, ~6% of market cap) while funding organic capacity.
- **Disclosure and execution:** guidance has been raised repeatedly; Q3 2026 beat the high end of guidance on revenue, EBITDA and EPS. Q3 repurchases of $500M were done at prices well above today's — a reminder that buybacks at 40x earnings are less value-accretive than they look.
- **Caveat:** Plant is in his late 70s and succession is the key governance question; insider ownership is modest and management is not a large owner-operator. Watch the transition.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** — a wonderful business at a full price

This is exactly the kind of company I want to own: a wide-moat, sole-source supplier with 24% ROIC, expanding margins, a fortress balance sheet, and a management team that returns capital rationally. **But price is what you pay.** At 28x EV/EBITDA and 44x FCF, the market has already capitalized several years of the spares up-cycle, the gas-turbine boom, and continued margin expansion. My base-case intrinsic value of ~$175 sits **~20% below** the current quote, and even my bull case is only ~30% above it.

- **If you own it:** hold. Quality this durable is worth deferring taxes for; momentum and the 2027 backlog are real.
- **If you don't:** do not initiate here. Set a buy trigger at **$150–165** (25–30% discount to base-case value), which would also put the stock near 20x forward EBITDA — a price that assumes nothing heroic.
- **Revisit upward if:** HWM grows into the multiple via a 2027–28 beat-and-raise cycle with FCF above $2.7B, or the multiple compresses to ~30x forward earnings while revenue guidance holds at double digits.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Customer vertical integration (the biggest one).** GE Aerospace buying a rival casting supplier is a direct attack on single-source economics. If GE shifts meaningful airfoil or fastener volume in-house, both growth and the 30%+ margin structure come under pressure.
2. **Valuation risk / multiple compression.** At 44x FCF, a de-rating to 30x — even with earnings intact — is a 30%+ loss. The Sept–Nov 2026 drawdown from $310 to ~$200 is a live demonstration that sentiment, not fundamentals, sets the price near-term.
3. **Aerospace cycle.** 2020 proved revenue can fall 26% in a year. A Boeing or Airbus build-rate cut, or a deferral of engine shop visits, hits the high-margin spares line first.
4. **Spares over-earning.** Spares pricing after a decade of pent-up shop visits may be at a cyclical peak; the "Middle East conflict" commentary shows how sensitive demand assumptions are.
5. **Capex intensity.** Growth investment is rising; if capacity additions coincide with a demand pause, FCF conversion deteriorates just as the market expects it to inflect up.
6. **Succession.** The strategy is unusually identified with one executive.

**Bottom line:** Wide moat, 9/10 financial quality, excellent stewards — and roughly 20% too expensive for a disciplined buyer. My verdict is **Hold**, with a standing order to buy at $150 or below.
