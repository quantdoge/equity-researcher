## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Howmet Aerospace Inc. (NYSE: HWM)

**Snapshot:** Aerospace & Defense (engine products, fasteners, engineered structures, forged wheels); ~$84.7bn market cap; 25,430 employees; CEO & Executive Chairman John C. Plant. Data as of the most recent filings available (Q3 2026 10-Q filed 2026-10-29; FY2025 10-K filed 2026-02-12).

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate (trending strong on structure, weak on power concentration)**

**Evidence supporting strength:**
- 9-member board, 8 independent; independent **Lead Director James F. Albaugh** with authority to preside over executive sessions; Chairman/Lead Director roles evaluated and appointed annually by independent directors.
- Company states Chairman and Lead Director positions are reviewed annually by the independent directors — a real, not nominal, check.
- Director pay is conventional and capped: $130k cash retainer, $200k RSU grant, total director comp capped at $750k/yr, **$750k stock ownership guideline** with a 6-year clock. No exotic perks disclosed.
- No SEC enforcement actions or DOJ matters surfaced via EDGAR full-text search (zero hits) — a clean enforcement record.

**Evidence of concern:**
- **Combined Executive Chairman + CEO role** (John C. Plant). This is the single most substantive governance criticism: the Lead Director mitigates but does not eliminate power concentration, and the board has not moved to separate the roles despite the CEO's large realized sales.
- **CFO-level churn signal:** Form 4s show Patrick Winterlich first receiving an EVP, CFO equity award in Feb 2026, implying a recent CFO transition. CFO turnover at a company executing a $1.8bn acquisition is worth monitoring (inference from filings; not independently confirmed).
- CAO/Controller-level selling (see §3) — senior financial-officer selling is a governance-sensitive signal.

### 2. Regulatory & Legal Risk Summary — **Low, with two live labor/employment irritants**

- **No active SEC/DOJ enforcement.** EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned no actions. Legacy **UK litigation is now "discontinued and closed"** per the Q1 2026 10-Q legal-proceedings update — a genuine de-risking.
- **NLRB charge 08-CA-381672** (filed 2026-02-23, Region 8): unfair labor practice charge alleging 8(a)(3) discrimination/discipline. Single-site, low financial materiality, but it is a formal federal charge.
- **Wage-and-hour litigation cluster:** California state-court class action by a quality inspector alleging bonus/labor-code violations; separate unpaid pre-shift/overtime suit. Typical for industrial manufacturing; immaterial individually.
- **Environmental/safety enforcement history:** aggregated penalty records include roughly **$360k workplace-safety and $170k aviation-safety** penalties historically, plus small OSHA citations (including ~$10k in 2026). De minimis versus scale; no consent decree identified.
- **Antitrust/M&A:** the $1.8bn CAM acquisition closed 2026-04-06 with no disclosed Phase II review or consent order — clean clearance.
- **Gap noted:** FY2025 10-K risk-factor text and the 2026 proxy were not machine-retrievable (tool returned "no 10-K found"; SEC/OSHA pages 403'd). Legal-proceedings detail is therefore inferred from the 10-Q summary and public news rather than read directly.

- **2026 open-market sales:** EVP & Chief Accounting Officer **Neil Marchuk sold 45,150 sh @ $251.70 (~$11.4m, Feb 18)** and **41,932 sh @ $269.50 (~$11.3m, May 11)** — roughly **$22.7m**, cutting his disclosed holdings ~63% (176,938 → 65,115). VP & Controller Shultz sold 1,000 sh @ $260 (~$0.26m). Trailing period: EVP/Secretary Lola Lin sold 13,102 sh @ $180.51 (~$2.4m, Aug 2025).
- **Purchases: zero.** Every other filing is an RSU/option award, director deferred-fee award, or tax withholding — i.e., non-discretionary.
- Aggregators report ~919k shares sold by insiders over 24 months, with the largest historical sale being **John Plant's ~$126m at ~$157/share** (well below today's ~$213) — an earlier, non-prescient exit rather than a top-tick distribution.
- **Offsetting factor:** Plant still holds ~3.26m shares post-awards (roughly $690m notional) — genuine skin in the game. Director ownership is broad ($16k–$59k share counts plus RSUs).
- **Read:** selling is broad-based and continuous into strength, led by finance/accounting officers rather than by the CEO in 2026. Not a fraud tell, but it caps the "insider conviction" score.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Customer/competitor convergence and concentration.** Commercial aerospace is ~53% of revenue; **RTX and GE Aerospace are each ~11% of 2025 sales**. Now **SpaceX is casting gas turbine blades in-house** — i.e., a key demand pool vertically integrating. This "customers become competitors" narrative drove a ~12% drawdown into late October 2026 and is the highest-conviction operational threat to the margin story, because Howmet's 32.5% adjusted EBITDA margin rests on sole-source positions that are only as durable as customers' willingness to in-source.
2. **CAM integration + leverage.** The $1.8bn all-cash acquisition of Consolidated Aerospace Manufacturing (from Stanley Black & Decker, closed April 2026) adds ~$485–495m revenue at **>20% EBITDA margin — dilutive to a 32% company margin**, requires plant/customer requalification, and was funded into a period of aggressive buybacks ($1.1bn YTD; new $5bn authorization). Execution slippage or aerospace-grade quality escapes at a newly acquired fastener business would hit both margin and certification standing.
3. **Key-person and labor/site concentration.** John Plant is simultaneously Executive Chairman and CEO of a company whose strategy, capital allocation, and investor narrative are closely identified with him; there is no disclosed succession plan in retrieved materials. Separately, the Niles, Ohio plant (450+ USW-represented workers) is in open conflict (see §5), and single-site specialty casting/forging capacity cannot be quickly re-routed if a labor action or fire occurs. No disclosed material cybersecurity breach was found, but the 10-K risk text could not be retrieved to confirm incident history.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Moderate and rising (currently the weakest dimension)**

- **The Niles profit-sharing clawback is a live, ongoing reputational event.** Howmet overpaid profit-sharing for Q1 2025–Q2 2026 due to a calculation error and demanded repayment of $5,000–$10,000+ per worker **within 30 days**, including from retirees. The local union states the overpayment totalled **~$4 million** across 450+ workers and has filed/engaged on a **grievance** with USW international and its legal team. Local TV (WKBN) and regional press (The Vindicator, WFJM) coverage is sustained and overwhelmingly negative, with the obvious optics of a ~$10bn-revenue, record-margin company clawing back four figures from hourly workers. This is small in dollars and real in brand and labor-relations cost — and it lands against an NLRB charge at the same time.
- **Narrative risk from valuation and competition.** Sell-side is overwhelmingly bullish (roughly 19 Strong Buy/1 Moderate Buy/4 Hold), while bears argue the stock is priced for peak margins; multiple public pieces frame HWM as "priced for perfection." A single margin miss or a confirmed in-sourcing win by a major customer would convert an analytical debate into a momentum unwind.
- No product recalls, safety-of-flight events, ESG scandals, or data-privacy breaches were identified in retrieved sources.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Governance structure is genuinely good (8/9 independent, empowered Lead Director, clean SEC record, insider ownership), and regulatory/legal exposure is low and shrinking (UK litigation closed, no enforcement actions). Offsetting that: combined Chairman/CEO, persistent senior-officer selling with zero insider buying, a dilutive acquisition at a premium multiple, a credible customer-becomes-competitor threat to the core margin story, and an active union confrontation generating bad press. No fraud indicators, no accounting-integrity red flags, and no going-concern or control issues were found. This is a **high-quality franchise with a governance-decidedly-average power structure and a reputational issue that is cheap to fix and currently being mishandled.**

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Niles profit-sharing clawback (~$4m, 450+ USW workers, retirees, 30-day demand).** Escalate to management: a company earning record margins should absorb an error it admits was its own, not collect it from hourly workers. Unresolved, this feeds the NLRB case, invites a wage-and-hour follow-on, and is a gratuitous ESG/labor controversy.
2. **NLRB unfair-labor-practice charge 08-CA-381672 (8(a)(3))** running concurrently with the clawback — treat as a pattern of labor-relations deterioration, not an isolated filing.
3. **CAO sold ~63% of his disclosed position inside nine months (~$22.7m), with no insider purchases company-wide.** Not evidence of wrongdoing; escalate as a question for management/IR on any 10b5-1 plan changes, and cross-check against the CAM purchase-accounting and segment-margin disclosures.
4. **Combined Executive Chairman/CEO with no disclosed succession plan** at a company executing a transformational-priced acquisition and a $5bn buyback — request the board's succession and capital-allocation oversight detail from the proxy (not machine-retrievable in this session).
5. **SpaceX in-sourcing of gas turbine blade casting** — request management's customer-by-customer defense of sole-source positions and contract duration/qualification barriers; this is the thesis-level operational risk, not a footnote.
