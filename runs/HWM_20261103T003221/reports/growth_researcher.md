## Howmet Aerospace (HWM) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $212.99 | Market cap ~$86.5B | EV ~$88.8B | 52-wk range $185.91–$309.85 (intraday) | −31% from Aug peak**

---

### 1. Growth Story

Howmet is not a "disruptor" in the classic sense — it is the **irreplaceable choke point** in three demand cycles that are now colliding simultaneously: the narrowbody/widebody build-rate ramp, the LEAP engine shop-visit inflection (spares are the highest-margin revenue in aerospace), and gas turbines for AI data-center power, where Howmet holds **>50% global share of industrial gas turbine blades**. Q3 2026 revenue grew **29% (+25% organic)** — the strongest quarter in company history — while adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 310bps to 32.5%, proving that incremental volume converts to EBITDA at ~46%.

The stock has de-rated ~31% from its August peak on two *slow-burn* competitive headlines (SpaceX casting in-house; GE buying casting rival CPP), even as estimates went **up**. That divergence — accelerating fundamentals against a sentiment-driven multiple reset — is the entire investment case.

**Growth quality checks**
- **3-yr revenue CAGR (2022–2025): 13.4%**; 5-yr (2020–2025): 9.4% — but 2020 was COVID-depressed, so the 3-yr figure is the honest read. 2026 is a clear **step-function acceleration**.
- **Organic-dominated:** Q3 reported +29% vs **+25% organic**; only ~4pts from M&A. Fastening Systems +41% reported but **+10% organic** ex-CAM/Brunner — M&A is bolting on, not masking.
- **Margin trajectory is the standout:** gross margin +1,030bps and operating margin +650bps over three years. Engine Products segment EBITDA margin hit **39.1% (+580bps)** in Q3. This is pricing power + volume leverage, not cost-cutting.
- **Rule of 40:** trailing (FY2025) score **28.4** (11.1% growth + 17.3% FCF margin) — below the bar. **But forward 2026E is ~44** (24.8% growth + 19.4% FCF margin). The trailing metric materially understates the current run-rate.
- **Beat history: 100% beat rate over 7 quarters, average surprise +7.45%** — and surprise magnitude is *widening* (+6.6%, +7.3%, +9.9% in the last three). Guidance is structurally conservative.
- **Estimate revisions: UPGRADE.** 2030 EPS consensus moved from $8.89 → **$9.86 (+10.9%)** in three months. FY2026 revenue guide raised +$250M, EPS +$0.23, FCF +$100M at Q3.
- **EPS compounding:** 2025A GAAP $3.73 → 2030E $9.86 = **21.5% CAGR**.

### 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

| End market | Q3'26 growth | Position / opportunity |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial aerospace | **+32%** | Structural castings, airfoils, fasteners; sole-source on many engine programs. Multi-year aircraft order backlog supports build-rate increases "throughout 2027 and beyond." |
| Gas turbines (IGT) | **+45%** | **>50% global share in turbine blades.** Management: "the growth of that market is dependent upon our willingness to invest." Backlog expected to grow *exponentially*; demand expected to **double within 3–5 years** tied to data-center power. |
| Defense aerospace | +10% | Spares + legacy fighter build increases; Middle East conflict adds upside. |
| Commercial transportation | +28% | Forged aluminum wheels; North American recovery. Lowest-multiple, cyclical ballast. |

**The moat is time, not patents.** Single-crystal and directionally-solidified blade casting plus multi-chemistry coating capacity cannot be replicated quickly — a fresh capacity commitment today delivers no equipment before **2028** given multi-year casting-machine lead times. Management explicitly flagged that the ~12% FY2027 growth guide **"may not fully satisfy 2027 demand"** — i.e., Howmet is *supply-constrained, not demand-constrained*. That is the single most bullish fact in this analysis: it caps near-term growth optics but guarantees multi-year visibility and pricing power.

**Penetration math:** Revenue of ~$11.5B in 2027 against a commercial aerospace + IGT casting/fastener SAM comfortably north of $40B implies mid-20s penetration with share *gaining* in the fastest-growing sub-segment. The 2030 consensus revenue of $15.4B implies a 13.3% five-year CAGR — achievable on build rates alone, before any share gain in gas turbines.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Feb 11, 2027 — Q4/FY2026 results & formal FY2027 guidance.** Q4 guide: revenue $2.75B, adj. EBITDA $900M (32.7% margin), EPS $1.50. The 2027 framework (~$11.5B, +12%) gets formalized. *Highest-probability re-rating event.*
2. **H2 2027 — GE Aerospace / Consolidated Precision Products ($11.75B) deal close.** Removes the binary overhang one way or the other. Regulatory review is the gate.
3. **2027–2028 — Gas turbine capacity comes online.** Elevated capex beyond 2027 funds output additions; management sees demand supporting outlook *beyond 2028*. Watch for order backlog disclosures.
4. **2027 — LEAP engine shop-visit inflection.** Spares mix shift is the highest-margin revenue line; drives the next leg of margin expansion beyond the 32.5% Q3 level.
5. **Capital return:** **$5.4B buyback authorization** (~6% of market cap) plus a 17% dividend raise to $0.14/qtr. At 38x earnings, buybacks are accretive to EPS at a ~2.6% annualized shrink rate.
6. **Defense spares / legacy fighter build increases** from Middle East conflict — management sees no material spares disruption and net upside.

No euphoria multiple embedded; this is an institutionally-owned name that has lost favor. Low retail participation argues the selling is analytical (competitive thesis) rather than panic.

- **Elevated capex beyond 2027** — FCF conversion will be tested even as revenue compounds.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY**

The setup is a high-quality compounder de-rated on legitimate-but-slow-burn fears while fundamentals accelerate. At $212.99 the stock trades at:
- **38.7x 2026E EPS ($5.50)** with EPS growing ~48% → **PEG ≈ 0.8**
- **32.2x 2027E EPS ($6.61)** → PEG ≈ 1.6 on 2028 growth (19%)
- **26.7x 2026E EBITDA ($3.33B)** and **43x 2026E FCF ($2.0B)**
- vs. **GE Aerospace ~40x forward**, RTX ~27x, Safran ~28x

That is a **premium multiple on absolute earnings but a discount to its own growth**, and it sits **27% below where it traded in August** on identical-to-better estimates. Consensus price targets cluster **$298–$370** (median ~$337, ~58% upside); Deutsche Bank named it a top idea at $343, Guggenheim initiated Buy at $350. Recent cuts (Bernstein $328→$298, Jefferies $370→$350) trimmed targets — they did not change ratings.

**Why not Strong Buy:** 32x 2027 earnings leaves no room for a *visible* competitive leak, capex is rising, and the GE/CPP overhang persists until H2 2027. The thesis needs the February guide and the 2027 capex/FCF bridge to prove out. Upgrade to Strong Buy on (a) FY2027 guidance above $11.5B or (b) explicit contract-protection disclosure around GE.

### 7. Key Risks

1. **Customer vertical integration — the real one.** GE Aerospace, a top customer, is paying $11.75B for Consolidated Precision Products (≈18x 2027 EBITDA incl. synergies) to bring LEAP/GEnx/military castings in-house. No contracts transfer and nothing hits the near-term P&L, but over a decade GE could steer *next-generation* programs to a supplier it owns. This is a share-erosion risk measured in years, not quarters — and it is precisely what compressed the multiple.
2. **SpaceX casting turbine blades in-house.** Announced late August; already cost the stock before the GE news. SpaceX is not an aerospace casting incumbent, but it is a credible, well-funded, vertically-integrating buyer of gas turbine capacity — and its own demand for data-center-adjacent power is enormous.
3. **Valuation air pocket.** At 38.7x 2026E EPS the stock "cannot absorb even a slow leak in its position." Industrials rate-sensitivity and GE Aerospace's post-earnings read-through already drove HWM to $185.91 in late October — sector beta is high (beta 1.20).
4. **Capex intensity beyond 2027.** Management guided to elevated spend to fund aerospace, defense *and* gas turbine capacity. If FCF conversion stalls near 19%, the 43x P/FCF multiple compresses even with revenue on track.
5. **Build-rate dependence.** Narrowbody ramp depends on Boeing/airbus execution — outside Howmet's control. Any 737/A320 rate cut defers spares revenue, the highest-margin line.
6. **M&A masking in Fastening.** +41% reported vs +10% organic — CAM/Brunner integration must deliver, or the segment's 140bps margin *contraction* in Q3 widens.
7. **Concentration.** Four segments, four end markets, but commercial aerospace is the dominant profit pool and a small number of OEMs (GE, Pratt, Rolls, Safran) hold the pricing conversation.

---

**Bottom line:** A >50%-share monopolist in gas turbine blades, with 25% organic growth, 300bps+ margin expansion, a 100% beat rate, rising estimates, and a 6%-of-cap buyback — trading 27% below its August high because of two competitor headlines that don't touch a single dollar of 2026–2027 revenue. **Buy**, sized for volatility, with the February 11 guide as the catalyst.
