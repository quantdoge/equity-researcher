# Sector Brief: Howmet Aerospace (HWM) — Aerospace & Defense / Engine Components

## 1. Sector Overview

HWM sits in the **Aerospace & Defense** complex, specifically the highest-value niche of the supply chain: investment-cast turbine airfoils, seamless rolled rings, aerostructures and aerospace fasteners — plus a growing industrial gas turbine (IGT) blade book. The sector's demand driver is not discretionary: a global backlog of **17,000+ aircraft (≈10 years of production)** against deliveries that still trail the 2018 peak, with Airbus targeting ~870 deliveries in 2026 and Boeing constrained by the FAA's 38/month 737 MAX cap while studying a path toward ~70/month. Structure is oligopolistic at the sub-tier level — HWM is the **world's largest gas turbine blade manufacturer** and holds sole-source positions on several narrowbody engine programmes — with barriers built on NADCAP qualification, multi-year engine programme life cycles and captive casting/forging capacity (10–15% of revenue must be reinvested to hold share). A second, structurally new demand vector has emerged: **data-centre electricity demand pulling IGT orders**, which is why HWM's gas turbine revenue grew **+45% YoY in Q3 2026**. The 13 largest Industrials companies we aggregate posted FY2025 revenue of ~**$595bn at a 27.8% aggregate gross margin and ~9.0% net margin** — HWM is running roughly double the sector's net margin.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **OEM rate ramp with high fixed-cost absorption.** HWM's content per narrowbody engine is concentrated in the rotating parts (airfoils, discs, rings) whose economics improve fastest on volume. Organic growth accelerated from +21% (Q2 2026) to **+25% (Q3 2026), +29% reported**, with commercial aerospace +32% YoY.
- **Aftermarket/spares mix shift.** As the LEAP/GTF fleet matures and legacy engines stay in service longer, shop visits rise; spare parts price at a multiple of OEM pricing, mechanically lifting margin — adjusted EBITDA margin has expanded ~310–340bps YoY for three straight quarters to **32.5%**, versus 28.9% for FY2025.
- **IGT / data-centre electrification.** Gas turbine revenue +45% with management flagging a "longer runway" and mapping capacity additions — a non-aero growth leg that historically did not exist at this scale.
- **Defence budget super-cycle.** NATO rearmament toward higher GDP thresholds plus F-35 and munitions demand supports the Engineered Structures and Fastening Systems segments.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Structural supply-chain bottlenecks upstream of HWM.** Titanium sponge/ingot and nickel-superalloy availability, plus casting capacity, remain the binding constraint industry-wide — Airbus has publicly cited "less engines than needed." A supplier stumble upstream can cap HWM's own shipment schedule regardless of demand.
- **OEM pricing pressure and long-term agreement resets.** Primes are absorbing their own cost inflation and pushing back on supplier price; volume-driven margin gains can be partially conceded at contract renewal.
- **Cyclical and tariff-exposed non-aero segments.** Forged Wheels (heavy-duty truck) is late-cycle/soft and exposed to aluminium costs and Section 232 tariffs; commercial transportation was a drag on organic growth in prior periods.
- **Valuation and cycle-position risk.** At **$212.97 / $84.7bn market cap**, HWM trades ~57x FY2025 EPS ($3.71) and roughly **38–40x our ~$5.3–5.6 2026E EPS** — a premium to GE (37.6x trailing) and RTX (40.7x), pricing in several years of flawless execution. Beta of 1.20 means the multiple is exposed to any rate or risk-appetite shock.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **Growth:** HWM's FY2025 +11.1% lagged the peer median, but that is the wrong lens — its **2026 organic growth of ~25%** now leads every large-cap peer and is volume-, not price-led, on already-installed engine platforms.
- **Margins:** 24.8% operating margin beats the peer median of 19.1% and is achieved without aftermarket-only mix; the trajectory (17.4% → 24.8% operating over five years, 32.5% adj. EBITDA in Q3 2026) is the steepest expansion in the group.
- **Moat:** The largest global turbine-airfoil franchise, sole-source positions, and a five-year revenue CAGR of 9.4% with only a single down year (2021, COVID). Pricing power is evidenced by margin expansion *during* an input-cost inflation cycle — the clearest proof of structural advantage.
- **Where it is vulnerable:** Customer concentration in a handful of engine primes, and the fact that its best growth (IGT) is the least moated segment and most exposed to a power-capex cycle turn.

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

**Cycle position: mid-cycle expansion, not late.** Commercial deliveries remain below the prior peak with a decade of backlog visibility and aftermarket inflecting — the classic sweet spot where both OEM volume and spares mix improve simultaneously. Add a genuine second upcycle (IGT/electrification) and a defence rearmament cycle, and 2026–2028 sector earnings should compound in the mid-to-high teens. The risk is not demand, it is (a) execution through a fragile upstream supply chain and (b) valuation: the group already reflects much of this, so returns will be driven by delivery against raised guidance rather than multiple expansion. **Recommendation bias: favourable sector, with HWM the highest-quality operator in it — but at ~39x forward earnings, position sizing should respect the priced-for-perfection risk.**
