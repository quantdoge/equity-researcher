### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Tariff/trade escalation on aluminum and derivative products (Section 232): Probability: High (>65%); Impact: Low–Medium ($8–10B FY2027 revenue base, low-single-digit % EBIT risk)**
The April 2, 2026 proclamation raised Section 232 aluminum/steel/copper treatment, with a flat 50% tariff on full value effective April 6, 2026, then amended again June 1, 2026 (temporary 15% rate through 12/31/2027 for certain products). HWM is directly exposed through Forged Wheels (aluminum) and aluminum/titanium inputs. Mitigant: demonstrated pass-through — Q1 2026 revenue rose 17% with "higher aluminum cost and tariff pass-through" offset. HWM also previously reserved the right to halt tariff-affected shipments (Reuters, Apr 2025), which protects margin but risks customer relations and delivery penalties.

**Risk 2 — China critical-minerals/export-control retaliation disrupting superalloy and casting inputs: Probability: Medium (30–45%); Impact: Medium–High (revenue timing, not solvency)**
China's 2025 rare-earth and dual-use export controls (partially suspended for one year in Nov 2025) hit aerospace manufacturers relying on sole-sourced Chinese material. HWM's engineered-structures and investment-casting operations (nickel/cobalt/titanium/rare-earth-intensive) are structurally exposed. Direct China revenue is only 1.8% ($130M, +26% y/y) — the exposure is through the *supply chain*, not the P&L geography, which makes it harder to hedge and less visible in segment data.

**Risk 3 — Titanium/supply-chain concentration in geopolitically adversarial states + defense program concentration: Probability: Medium (25–35%); Impact: Medium**
Legacy exposure to Russia's VSMPO-AVISMA (historically 25–30% of Western aerospace-grade titanium; HWM's predecessor Alcoa formed a VSMPO JV in 2013) has been progressively replaced, and titanium has so far escaped direct Western sanctions — but EU sanctions packages continue to expand (21st Russia package), and Chinese titanium is filling the gap, importing a new geopolitical dependency. Compounding this, defense is 17% of revenue and F-35-linked; the 2023–24 Lockheed Martin titanium-parts dispute showed how quickly a single program relationship can become adversarial.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Lockheed Martin v. Howmet Aerospace (N.D. Tex., 4:23-cv-01204):** Filed late 2023 over F-35 titanium structural parts pricing after Howmet allegedly curtailed supply when Lockheed refused a price increase; **settled May 2024**. Resolved, but evidences contract/pricing leverage risk on sole-source defense programs.
- **Labor/employment litigation (active):** *Davis v. Howmet Aerospace* (N.D. Ohio 1:2024cv01548, decided Mar 19, 2025); separate unpaid-wage/overtime suits over pre-shift work and mandatory turnover meetings; **NLRB charge 08-CA-381672** (Region 8, Cleveland). Also a reported dispute in which ~450 employees were told to repay $5K–$10K overpayments within 30 days — a reputational and union-relations irritant in a tight labor market.
- **Environmental/remediation:** Legacy Arconic/Alcoa-era liabilities continue to generate penalties (e.g., Howmet Castings & Services air-pollution violation, NJ, 2017, $39,750 per Violation Tracker; Firth Rixson/Schlosser Forge workplace violation). Low dollar materiality, persistent disclosure burden.
- **Trade/export controls:** As a defense supplier, HWM is subject to ITAR/EAR; 10-K language explicitly flags "governmental constraints, including export restrictions, sanctions" and evolving tariff regimes. No current OFAC/ITAR enforcement action identified.
- **Regulatory posture:** No active DOJ/FTC/SEC investigation into HWM identified in this review — a genuine positive.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Legacy FCPA history (inherited, resolved):** Alcoa's Alba matter was resolved with the U.S. government in January 2014 (FCPA anti-bribery books-and-records/controls). No current FCPA matter surfaced, but the inherited compliance perimeter (global castings/forging footprint across Mexico, Poland, Hungary, China) remains a higher-risk anti-corruption profile than a purely domestic manufacturer.
2. **Dual-use/defense export-control sensitivity:** F-35 content flowing through non-U.S. facilities (UK, France, Italy, Japan, Poland) creates re-export and technology-transfer compliance load.
3. **Data privacy:** Low inherent exposure (B2B, no consumer data at scale); GDPR obligations for EU workforces — not a material flag.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

HWM's risk profile is fundamentally mitigated by a 60%+ U.S. revenue base and a European/Japanese footprint in rule-of-law jurisdictions with no material sanctioned-country revenue. The residual risk is **input-side and policy-side, not jurisdiction-side**: Section 232 derivative-product tariffs on aluminum, Chinese critical-minerals leverage over superalloys, and a concentrated defense program relationship. Demonstrated tariff pass-through and settlement of the Lockheed dispute, plus the absence of any live government investigation, keep this out of "High." The rating would migrate to High if (a) Section 232 coverage expands to aerospace derivative parts without pass-through, or (b) China extends critical-minerals controls to titanium sponge/nickel superalloys.
