## HOWMET AEROSPACE (HWM) — ESG ANALYSIS

**Company:** Howmet Aerospace Inc. | Industrials / Aerospace & Defense | Pittsburgh, PA | ~25,430 FTE | Market cap ~$84.7B | CEO & Executive Chairman: John C. Plant | 2026E revenue ~$10.6B (2027 guide ~$11.5B)

Howmet is a flight-critical component supplier (engine airfoils, seamless rolled rings, fasteners, titanium mill products, forged aluminium wheels). Its economic moat is **sole-source qualification on safety-critical parts** — which makes ESG risk structurally *asymmetric*: a single quality, safety or environmental failure at one plant can have outsized financial consequences because customers cannot quickly re-source.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives (verified, third-party assured):**
- **Delivered** its 2024 goal: **~21.5–21.7% absolute reduction in combined Scope 1+2 GHG vs 2019** (~217,000 tCO2e).
- Raised ambition: **33.6% / 343,000 tCO2e absolute Scope 1+2 reduction by 2027** vs 2019.
- 2025 progress: **15% improvement in GHG intensity**, **3.6% reduction in global energy use**; energy-efficiency projects avoided ~28,000 tCO2e in 2025 (~115,000 tCO2e/yr cumulatively).
- **Third-party assurance covers Scope 1, 2 AND 3, water withdrawal and hazardous waste** — this is above-median rigour for a mid-cap industrial and materially raises disclosure credibility versus self-reported peers.
- GRI + SASB + TCFD-aligned disclosure; publishes CDP and EcoVadis scorecards.

**Negatives:**
- Structural **energy intensity**: titanium ingot melting, vacuum casting and forging are among the most electricity/gas-intensive processes in industrials. Absolute emissions reduction is partly a function of grid decarbonisation, not just operational action.
- **No verified absolute Scope 3 reduction target or validated net-zero pathway** found in primary disclosure (a "net-zero commitment" is referenced in trade media but could not be confirmed in the assured data). Scope 3 is the dominant share of footprint given purchased titanium/aluminium/nickel.
- **CBAM exposure is genuine and two-sided**: aluminium and iron/steel are covered sectors. Howmet faces (a) cost pass-through on EU-bound aluminium/nickel inputs and (b) embedded-emissions documentation burden on EU-bound finished products. Its European plants (France, Germany, Hungary, Czech, Poland, Italy, Netherlands, UK) also sit inside EU ETS.
- **Water stress and heat** at casting/forging sites in Mexico, Italy and the US Southwest — a physical risk to throughput, not yet quantified publicly.
- Legacy remediation liabilities from the Arconic/Alcoa heritage (RCRA lineage) — currently immaterial in dollar terms.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives:**
- **Best-ever safety performance in 2025**: 15% improvement in DART incidents, **zero fatalities**, **no serious or life-altering injuries** — a genuine differentiator in heavy manufacturing.
- **Zero product safety-related recalls in 2025**; >300 unique third-party certifications. For a flight-critical supplier this is the single most important social metric and it is clean.
- Publishes its **EEO-1 report** (unusual transparency for a company this size), plus a Supplier Code of Conduct and supplier sustainability programme.

**Negatives — labour relations are the weak spot:**
- **Niles, Ohio overpayment dispute**: ~**$4M** in profit-sharing was overpaid to **450+ unionised workers**; the company demanded repayment of $5,000–$10,000+ per worker within 30 days. Triggered local/national press, a union grievance, and reputational damage disproportionate to the sum. This is a *social licence* failure, not a financial one.
- **Two pending NLRB unfair-labour-practice charges** (Region 8): Case 08-CA-376638 filed 12/09/2025 and 08-CA-381672 filed 02/23/2026. Allegations unproven; the pattern signals **deteriorating union relations during a capacity ramp** — precisely when labour leverage is highest.
- **Wage-and-hour collective litigation** (unpaid pre-shift work / mandatory turnover meetings).
- **Titanium supply-chain concentration** remains a social/geopolitical risk given historically elevated reliance on Russian titanium (VSMPO).
- Turnover and gender/racial pay-gap metrics not disclosed at a level comparable to EU peers.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Supporting the score:**
- **~9 of 10 directors independent**; independent **Lead Director James F. Albaugh** (ex-Boeing Commercial Airplanes CEO) — sector-relevant and genuinely independent.
- **Single class of common stock** — no dual-class structure, no poison pill identified. One share, one vote.
- **Proxy access** in place (shareholders may nominate directors into the proxy).
- Strong shareholder alignment: 2025 say-on-pay passed with **~98% support of votes cast**; $5.397B buyback authorisation and a 17% dividend raise in Q3 2026.
- Formal ethics infrastructure: Integrity Line whistleblower hotline, Supplier Code of Conduct, published Corporate Governance Guidelines and Director Independence Standards.
- No FCPA/bribery, securities class action, or accounting restatement issues identified.

**Deducting:**
- **Combined Executive Chairman AND CEO role** (John C. Plant). The independent Lead Director mitigates but does not eliminate the concentration of power. This is the single largest governance flaw and would be a routine withhold-vote trigger for major proxy advisors.
- **Outsized CEO compensation**: reported total compensation of roughly **$70.5M** in a single year (third-party data; ~97% performance/equity-weighted). Even granting pay-for-performance structure and excellent TSR, the absolute magnitude against ~$11.5B revenue invites say-on-pay backlash and is a headline risk.
- **No dedicated board ESG/sustainability committee** — ESG oversight sits with the full board, which is weaker than a chartered committee with defined reporting lines. For a company with CBAM/CSRD obligations and an energy-intensive footprint, this is a gap.
- Director tenure/succession and board refreshment pace not verified.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD**: Howmet's French, German, Italian, Czech, Hungarian, Polish and Dutch subsidiaries are very likely in scope; requires double materiality assessment, EU Taxonomy alignment and full Scope 3 reporting. Compliance cost is real but modest (~low single-digit $M/yr); the bigger risk is *inconsistency* between US voluntary disclosure and EU mandatory disclosure.
- **EU CBAM**: **aluminium and iron/steel are covered sectors** — direct relevance to Forged Wheels (aluminium truck wheels) and Engineered Structures (aluminium/nickel/titanium forgings) flowing into the EU. Financial phase-in begins 2026; embedded-emissions verification of upstream metal will become a procurement gate, favouring Howmet's assured data but raising input costs.
- **EU ETS**: applies to its European plants directly.
- **SEC climate disclosure rule**: adopted but stayed/contested; Howmet's TCFD report positions it ahead of a US requirement if reinstated. Low near-term exposure.
- **Section 232 aluminium/steel tariffs**: not an ESG rule, but it interacts with CBAM and carbon-cost pass-through; a key margin variable for Forged Wheels.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants**: none identified in the capital structure — no sustainability-linked bond step-up risk.
- **ITAR/EAR export controls**: material given defense and space revenue.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE (upper quartile operations, governance-capped)**

Howmet is a **strong operator** on the two factors that actually matter for this sector — **safety and product integrity** — with **assured, delivered emissions reductions** and an **independent, single-class board**. It is held back from "Leader" by **CEO/Chairman duality**, **extreme headline CEO pay**, the **absence of a board ESG committee**, **no verified absolute Scope 3 target**, and **visible union friction** during expansion.

**Not a Laggard. Not an ESG Risk name.** No controversy with a plausible >1% earnings impact.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

ESG is **not a driver of the HWM thesis** in either direction — it neither adds a valuation premium nor imposes a material discount. Three ESG factors are nonetheless worth monitoring because they can move the stock asymmetrically:

1. **Concentration × safety (the real risk).** Because Howmet holds sole-source positions on flight-critical parts, a single safety or quality incident at one forging or casting plant is a *company-level* event, not a site-level one. The clean 2025 record (zero fatalities, zero recalls) is therefore a genuine and underpriced asset — but the tail risk it prevents is fat.
2. **Labour relations during the ramp.** Growing 25% organically means hiring into unionised plants with fresh NLRB charges and a botched profit-sharing clawback. A strike at a key facility would be the most plausible near-term ESG-originated earnings hit.
3. **Carbon cost and the IGT revenue runway.** CBAM plus EU ETS create a slow, predictable cost creep on metal inputs — manageable, but a margin headwind for Forged Wheels specifically. Conversely, the ~45% growth in industrial gas turbine revenue ties part of the franchise to gas-fired generation: a long-term climate-transition question, currently offset by data-centre power demand extending the runway.

**Data gaps (honest):** Numeric MSCI/Sustainalytics/CDP/EcoVadis scores could not be retrieved from primary sources; the 10-K and DEF 14A were unavailable via the filing tool (PDF retrieval returned binary), so risk-factor language and full compensation tables are unverified; the $70.5M pay figure and 98% say-on-pay support derive from secondary sources. None of these gaps change the rating direction.
