---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: RISK-OFF / late-cycle reflation ("energy-shock plus hawkish pivot").**

The US economy is *not* slowing — it is re-accelerating into an inflation problem. Real GDP grew **3.4% annualised in Q3 2026** (from 2.2% in Q2, 2.5% in Q1 and just 0.2% in Q4 2025), with unemployment at a tight **4.2%**. But CPI inflation has reversed decisively: **+3.72% YoY in September 2026**, up from 2.65% in December 2025, with the last two prints at **+0.40% and +0.65% m/m (~6% annualised)**. The Fed has responded by *ending* its easing cycle and hiking: the effective funds rate bottomed at **3.63%** (June–September) and is back to **3.88%** in October.

The bond market has priced a full tightening cycle, not a pause: the **2Y is at 4.74%, 86bp above the effective funds rate**, and the curve has bear-steepened to **10Y 5.13% / 30Y 5.47%** (+39bp 2s10s). That is a **+1.4% real 10-year yield** — historically restrictive, and a term premium that reflects fiscal supply concerns.

Running underneath this is the macro variable that actually matters for VG: **a European gas crisis**. EU spot prices have roughly **tripled year-to-date**, storage sits at **decade lows**, and the incoming Russian LNG import ban removes ~8% of EU supply. **JKM is ~$32.84/MMBtu (30 Oct), TTF ~$33.78, up 27% in a month**, against **Henry Hub at $2.76** — a **~$30/MMBtu spread**, near a record. The US dollar has firmed alongside (broad index 121.7, +3.0% since 10 September), compounding affordability stress for price-sensitive Asian buyers.

**The paradox for VG:** this is simultaneously the best *operating* macro backdrop in the company's history and the worst *financing/valuation* backdrop.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

VG is a **tolling business with a very long duration**. In Q3 2026 it sold **465.8 TBtu across 124 cargoes at an implied weighted-average fixed liquefaction fee of $6.79/MMBtu** (~$3.2B revenue): Calcasieu 137.7 TBtu/37 cargoes, Plaquemines 328.1 TBtu/87 cargoes. That fee-based structure is the key transmission channel — and it cuts both ways.

**NEGATIVE — Interest rates (dominant factor).** VG has just closed **$15.1B of CP2 Phase 1 financing plus $8.6B for Phase 2, $20.7B total**, with a stated ambition of **85 MTPA by 2029** — a buildout requiring tens of billions more. At a 5.13% 10Y and 5.47% 30Y, (a) the discount rate applied to cash flows running into the 2050s rises sharply, and (b) incremental project debt costs roughly **$250M per year per 100bp on ~$25B of debt**. Because equity value is overwhelmingly the NPV of *unbuilt* 2028–2030 capacity, the discount rate — not the gas spread — is the marginal driver of the share price. This explains why VG trades **46% below its January 2025 IPO price ($25) and ~13% below its 30 October close** despite record global spreads. Beta is reported at 0.22, but that is meaningless for a 2025 vintage IPO whose actual range has been $25.19 → $5.89 → $17.56 → $12.87.

**NEGATIVE — Inflation / capex cost escalation.** CPI at 3.7% with 0.65% monthly prints is a direct threat to CP2's construction budget. Peer Cheniere's Q3 2026 miss was explicitly attributed to **rising costs** — this is an industry-wide LNG cost-inflation problem, not idiosyncratic. Fixed-price EPC assumptions made in 2024–25 are being eroded.

**NEGATIVE — Trade policy and regulation.** Section 232 steel/aluminium tariffs inflate CP2's equipment and module costs. Retaliatory Chinese tariffs on US LNG remove a large long-run demand pool. The EU's revived scrutiny of LNG **destination clauses** targets precisely VG's FOB/flexible model. And the **8 October 2026 arbitration ruling that Calcasieu breached its supply agreement with Galp** is a legal constraint on VG's ability to divert contract cargoes into spot markets — which is exactly how it would otherwise monetise a $30 spread.

**POSITIVE — Cheap feedstock.** Henry Hub at $2.76 (and falling into winter, versus $3.10 in June–July) against a $6.79 fixed fee implies a **tolling gross spread of roughly $3.5–3.6/MMBtu** after fuel gas — a healthy, widening margin. Low domestic gas prices are a structural competitive advantage for US Gulf Coast liquefaction.

**POSITIVE — Global demand desperation (but second-order).** JKM/TTF at ~$33 with decade-low EU storage and the Russian ban creates urgent, price-insensitive demand for US molecules. Critically, this is what allows VG to **contract CP2 and CP3 at premium fees** — the true equity story. QatarEnergy's reported interest in a long-term offtake is direct evidence. Guidance has been raised with **84% of 2026 volumes already locked in**, insulating near-term revenue from spot volatility. But note: VG largely does *not* capture the JKM-HH spread on contracted volumes — the buyer does. Only the ~16% uncontracted share and marketing optimisation are directly levered to the $33 print.

**MILDLY NEGATIVE — USD strength.** JKM is USD-denominated, so a 3% firmer dollar raises the effective cost for Asian utilities already balking at $33 gas, accelerating demand destruction in India, Pakistan and Bangladesh and softening the medium-term call on US cargoes.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating

### **NEUTRAL** — with an explicitly negative skew on equity valuation.

The split is stark and worth stating plainly:

- **Operating / cash-flow macro: FAVOURABLE (8/10).** Cheapest feedstock in years, record global spreads, a structurally short European market, 84% of volumes contracted, and rising demand for long-term US supply.
- **Financing / valuation macro: UNFAVOURABLE (2/10).** 5%+ long yields, a Fed back in hiking mode, 3.7% inflation eroding capex budgets, and a firming dollar.

These roughly cancel at the company level. The verdict is therefore **Neutral**, but the risk is asymmetric: **VG is a long-duration asset in a rising-discount-rate regime.** Record LNG spreads are already visible and priced; the next 100bp of long-end yield is not. The stock's 13% fall on 2 November — on the very day Plaquemines Phase 1 began commercial operations, an unambiguously good operational milestone — is the market telling you that rates, not cargoes, set the price. Q3 results are due **10 November 2026** and will be the near-term test.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**BEAR: A mild Northern Hemisphere winter detonates the JKM premium — at the same moment the Fed hikes again.**

The entire $30/MMBtu spread rests on a weather-driven European storage panic. EU inventories are at decade lows *because* the market is pre-buying for a cold winter and a full Russian LNG ban. If December–February runs mild, TTF and JKM can plausibly collapse from **$33 toward $14–16 by March 2027** — precisely the 2022→2023 pattern. Consequences compound:

1. Spot and marketing margins on VG's uncontracted volumes evaporate.
2. Buyers refuse to sign CP2/CP3 SPAs at premium fees into a collapsing curve, deferring the growth story that justifies the multiple.
3. Simultaneously, if CPI keeps printing 0.5%+ m/m, the Fed takes the funds rate above 4.5% and the **10Y clears 5.75–6%**, de-rating every long-duration energy infrastructure name.

That is a **double hit to both numerator and denominator** — plausibly a 25–35% drawdown from $12.87 toward the high single digits, retesting the December 2025 low of $5.89. The mirror-image bull case (a genuinely cold winter plus the Russian ban biting, JKM to $50+) would let VG contract CP2 at premium fees and re-rate hard — but note that even the bull case requires the *bond market* to cooperate, which is the binding constraint today.
