## VG — Venture Global, Inc.: Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*As of early November 2026. Company: US LNG producer/exporter (Calcasieu Pass, Plaquemines, CP2), Arlington VA, ~$32bn market cap, IPO Jan 2025.*

**Data caveat up front:** the SEC filing tool returned "No 10-K found"/"No 10-Q found" for VG and EDGAR browse was blocked (403), so a country-level revenue table directly from the filing was **not obtainable**. Geographic exposure below is built from the company's disclosed contract book (SPA announcements, Q2 2026 results, investor materials) and third-party reporting — treat percentages as directional estimates, not filed figures.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**#1 — EU Methane Regulation bites on US LNG from January 2027**
- *What:* EU Methane Regulation import requirements apply to imported gas from Jan 2027; importers must show MRV equivalence. US exporters have formally asked Brussels to delay enforcement to 2028 (Reuters, 19 May 2026).
- *Probability:* High that rules take effect on schedule or with modest delay. *Impact:* Medium — compliance cost, possible cargo-level friction with German/Polish/Italian buyers, reputational/attestation risk on flaring intensity. Europe is VG's core market, so this is the single most relevant external regulatory event.

**#2 — US–China trade war keeping Chinese buyers out of US LNG**
- *What:* China's 15% retaliatory tariff on US LNG (Feb 2025) cut US–China flows to ~0.2% of Chinese imports (Jan–Jul 2026). Washington and Beijing were discussing cutting/removing the tariff ahead of a Xi visit (Reuters, 18 Sep 2026); China Gas nonetheless signed a 20-yr, 2.5 MTPA Venture Global deal effective 2030, and VG was reported in talks with Chinese buyers (Bloomberg/Reuters, 6 Oct 2026).
- *Probability of tariff relief within 12 months:* ~50%, reversible either way. *Impact:* Medium — mainly a 2030+ growth-option risk rather than current cash flow; also removes a rhetorical/contractual hedge against European buyers.

**#3 — US policy reversal on export authorisations and trade-policy input costs**
- *What:* CP2's non-FTA export authorisation rests on DOE discretionary orders (non-FTA granted Mar 2025; DOE "final export authorization" signed Feb 2026) and FERC certificates. Both are politically reversible in a future administration and perennially litigated. Separately, steel/aluminium tariffs and Section 301 vessel fees inflate greenfield capex at a moment when VG is funding ~$28bn+ of CP2 plus two brownfield expansions.
- *Probability:* Low (near term, given a pro-LNG federal posture) — but *Impact:* Very High if it materialised, since 100% of capacity and all growth is US-permitted.

*(Honourable mention: the European counterparty arbitration wave — commercially the largest single threat, covered below because it is legal rather than geopolitical.)*

| **Unipec (Sinopec trading arm)** | **Settled** (announced 9 Oct 2025) | Undisclosed |

| **Orlen** | **Only remaining arbitration**; hearings late 2026, verdict not expected in 2026 | Undisclosed |

Aggregate initial claims across counterparties were disclosed at roughly **$5.5–6bn** pre-IPO (Jan 2025). Two panels have now ruled against VG on the same legal theory, which raises the probability of adverse outcomes in Orlen and of follow-on claims.

**Securities litigation:** putative federal class action filed Feb 2025 (FirstFire Global Opportunities Fund, et al.) alleging IPO disclosures hid commissioning/cost problems; reported **defeated/dismissed** (Law360, "Venture Global Beats Investor Action Over IPO Disclosures"). Appeal/residual risk remains, and the arbitration losses give plaintiffs a fresh fact pattern.

**Regulatory / permitting (net favourable):**
- CP2 FERC approval and CP Express pipeline **upheld** by the D.C. Circuit on 25 Aug 2026 (Dardar v. FERC; EarthJustice/Sierra Club/NRDC challenges rejected) — materially de-risks the ~$28bn project.
- DOE granted CP2 non-FTA export authorisation (Mar 2025) and a final export authorisation (Feb 2026).
- Pending: CP2 brownfield expansion (FERC + non-FTA application filed in 2026; FID target early 2027) and Plaquemines Expansion Phase 1 (applications filed late 2025; FID 1H 2027). Each new docket re-opens environmental-litigation and DOE-discretion risk.
- Plaquemines Phase 1 COD now targeted Q4 2026 — repeated slippage is itself a source of disclosure/contractual risk.

**Compliance posture:** no publicly disclosed FCPA, AML, sanctions or export-control enforcement actions. VG is a US person exporting US-origin LNG; DOE authorisations require destination compliance and anti-diversion commitments.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Sanctions/diversion screening on destination-flexible cargoes** — DES/FOB sales to trading houses (Vitol, TotalEnergies, ConocoPhillips) mean end-destination control is weaker; heightened scrutiny given EU sanctions escalation (21st Russia package, shadow-fleet measures) and any future re-export to restricted jurisdictions. *Watch item, not a current breach.*
2. **EU methane MRV attestation** — regulatory certification of emissions data from Jan 2027 creates a greenwashing/disclosure exposure on top of a compliance one, especially given Calcasieu flaring history central to the arbitrations.
3. **Vessel/trade compliance** — Section 301 US port fees on Chinese-built or Chinese-operated tonnage require contractual allocation (BIMCO USTR clause) across the chartered fleet; unallocated cost/legal risk on time-charter disputes.
4. **Litigation-driven disclosure risk** — a second wave of securities claims is plausible following the bp/Galp rulings.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM** *(with legal risk sub-rating: HIGH)*

**Rationale:** Country risk is genuinely low — all productive assets sit in the US, the federal posture toward LNG exports is supportive, CP2's main legal challenge was just defeated, and the customer base is largely OECD investment-grade. What drives the rating up is not geopolitics but **juridical and regulatory change**: roughly $3.7bn+ of bp damages at issue plus an adverse Orlen outcome, a European regulatory transition (methane MRV, Jan 2027) that touches the core market, and a China trade barrier that currently sterilises ~5–10% of the long-term book. Nothing here threatens solvency — 1H26 revenue of $9.2bn and FY26 EBITDA guidance of $8.7–9.1bn dwarf the arbitration exposure — but headline risk and headline-driven multiple compression are material.

**What would change the rating:** *Upgrade to Low* on a final, capped bp settlement and Orlen resolution within reserves, plus EU methane enforcement delay. *Downgrade to High* on an adverse bp quantum award approaching $3bn+, a successful appeal of the CP2 FERC/DOE authorisations, or a formal sanctions/diversion compliance event.
