## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Carbon intensity / stranded-asset logic.** VG is a pure-play methane-to-LNG infrastructure owner with four permitted facilities and a stated 85 MTPA ambition by 2029. Scope 1 (liquefaction compression) is large and rising fast; no accessible disclosed Scope 1/2 intensity per tonne. CP2 (20 MTPA) is characterised by opponents as one of the largest single US GHG sources — lifecycle estimates cited by NRDC/Earthjustice equate it to dozens of coal plants. This is advocacy arithmetic, not audited data, but the regulatory salience is real.
- **Ongoing compliance signal.** Third-party monitoring (Gas Outlook) reports chronic air-contaminant exceedances and operational flaring/venting at Calcasieu Pass; advocacy sources document an August fire, emergency shutdown and a 260-gallon spill. Company-reported FERC/EIA compliance is otherwise clean, and the D.C. Circuit **upheld FERC's CP2 approval in August 2026** (Case 24-1291), materially de-risking the permit.
- **EU Methane Regulation exposure.** VG has signed long-term European SPAs (e.g., ENI, 2.0 MTPA from CP2, 20 years) and the EU will impose importer methane-intensity reporting from 2027 and a maximum methane-intensity value from 2030. This is the single highest-probability, highest-dollar ESG constraint: it can impair deliverability into the highest-value market. VG's "net-zero methane / eliminate releases" positioning helps, but third-party certification is not yet evidenced.
- **Physical risk.** Gulf Coast hurricane exposure is acute and historically demonstrated (2020 storms during Calcasieu construction). Unpriced in most models but real for insurance, downtime and construction delay.
- **Offsetting positive:** gas-for-coal displacement — the credible "transition fuel" defence, and the basis for VG's social licence pitch.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Safety is genuinely strong:** 2025 TRIR of **0.17 vs. an industry average of 2.2** — roughly 13x better, a real operational-quality and cost-of-risk positive.
- **Community/social licence is the weak point.** Litigation from Louisiana commercial fishermen (dredging permit breaches, fishery damage), the *Dardar v. FERC* environmental-justice challenge, and CP2 Clean Air Act suits. These are low-dollar individually but repeatedly consume management time and carry injunction/schedule risk. Local opposition is durable and organised.
- **Labour opacity:** ~2,000 FTEs against a multi-billion-dollar annual construction programme means the workforce is overwhelmingly contractors. TRIR disclosure does not clearly reconcile contractor exposure — a gap in a company that would be at the top of the fatality-risk table if it did.
- **Product/consumer:** LNG supply to Europe is socially positive (energy security); no product-safety defect issue.
- **Controversy-adjacent:** the multi-year refusal to declare commercial operations at Calcasieu Pass while selling commissioning cargoes at spot is framed by buyers as a consumer/counterparty fairness problem, not merely contract law.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10**

| Item | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Ownership/control | **VG Partners (founders Pender & Sabel) holds ~97.5% of combined voting power** — dual/triple-class (A/B/C). Minority holders have effectively no voice. |
| Controlled company | VG self-identifies as a **"controlled company" under NYSE rules**, exempting it from majority-independent board and independent compensation/nominating committee requirements. |
| Board | Seven nominees; co-founders serve as co-chairmen alongside founder-adjacent leadership (Sabel is CEO). Independence is structurally constrained. |
| Compensation | **CEO Michael Sabel 2025 total comp $39.1M** (~1.7% of net income; ~29% of the founder-CEO pay of firms 10x larger). Built on large cash bonuses and **project-milestone** incentives — rewards capacity delivery over returns on capital, and is a plausible driver of the "build-at-any-cost" arbitration behaviour. Clawback policy and EY audit are positives. |
| Shareholder rights | No evidence of a poison pill, but control is absolute; ESG index screens (MSCI, FTSE) and many institutional mandates **exclude controlled/dual-class companies outright** — a structural exclusion from ESG passive flows. |
| Ethics/conduct | The defining issue: a multi-year pattern of selling commissioning cargoes on spot while withholding contracted volumes, producing **adverse rulings from BP (>$1.0bn claimed) and Galp (liability found; damages capped at ~$170M)**, a win vs. Shell, a win vs. Repsol, and Orlen still outstanding. Management publicly attacked a customer's motives. Aggressive-but-arguably-legal conduct that reliably converts into litigation cost and counterparty distrust. |
| Litigation record | IPO securities class action **dismissed with prejudice (S.D.N.Y., 15 June 2026)** — a genuine positive that removes a tail liability, though related registration-statement claims persist. |

**Rationale for 3/10:** strong controls, audit and disclosure hygiene cannot offset 97.5% control, NYSE exemptions, outsized founder pay, milestone-based incentives, and a demonstrated willingness to litigate with its own customers.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **United States:** FERC (CP2, Calcasieu, Plaquemines), DOE non-FTA export authorisations (politically reversible — the single biggest binary risk to the growth story), EPA Clean Air Act/NSR permitting and methane rules; SEC climate-disclosure rule currently stayed/uncertain, so limited near-term US reporting burden.
- **European Union:** EU Methane Regulation (importer reporting 2027, intensity cap 2030) — directly applies to European SPAs, including the 20-year ENI contract; CSRD scope reaches large EU customers and can cascade supplier data demands; CBAM does not cover LNG.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none disclosed in accessible sources, but the company is highly levered with ~$13bn/yr capex and repeated equity/debt needs — an ESG-linked or sustainability-linked margin ratchet is a live future consideration, and a weak ESG profile raises the spread on that marginal capital.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD** (borderline ESG Risk)

Best-in-class safety performance and a defensible transition-fuel narrative are outweighed by structural governance deficits (97.5% control, NYSE controlled-company exemptions, $39.1M founder-CEO pay tied to milestones) and a documented pattern of litigation with customers that has already destroyed ~half the IPO value. Environmental exposure is high and rising with the asset base rather than falling.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE** for the thesis

Three channels of financial transmission:

1. **Cost of capital.** Controlled-company status plus a sub-4 governance score narrows the institutional buyer base (many ESG and governance-screened mandates exclude dual-class controlled companies) and justifies a governance discount on a company that must repeatedly raise equity and debt.
2. **Cash-flow leakage.** ~$1.2bn+ of arbitration exposure (BP + Galp) plus Orlen is a direct claim on free cash flow, and the conduct that produced it depresses the value of the long-term contract book — the very thing underpinning the "68 MTPA, 68% contracted" story.
3. **Market access.** EU methane-intensity rules from 2027/2030 are the binding constraint on the highest-value destination market. Certification and measured, third-party-verified methane data are now a commercial necessity, not a reporting nicety.

**What would change the view:** completion of independent (MiQ or equivalent) methane certification across Calcasieu and Plaquemines; a clean, disclosed Scope 1 intensity metric; resolution/settlement of BP, Galp and Orlen within reserved amounts; meaningful declassification of the share structure or genuine independent-committee authority; and re-based compensation away from project milestones toward returns on invested capital. Absent those, ESG is a drag on the equity, not a neutral overlay.

**Key gaps:** no audited company Scope 1/2 emissions data, no MSCI/Sustainalytics score, and no direct 10-K access within this engagement — the emissions-intensity assessment above is therefore directional and should be re-based on the FY2025 10-K and any sustainability report before being relied on for a valuation adjustment.
