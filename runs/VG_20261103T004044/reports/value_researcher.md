# Venture Global, Inc. (NYSE: VG) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price (Nov 2, 2026 close): $12.87 | Market cap ≈ $32.2B (2.50B sh) | Net debt ≈ $39.6B | EV ≈ $71.8B**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW (and eroding)**

Venture Global builds and owns LNG liquefaction plants on the U.S. Gulf Coast (Calcasieu Pass, Plaquemines, CP2) and sells liquefaction capacity under 20-year take-or-pay SPAs, retaining some uncontracted volume for spot.

**Sources of advantage:**
- **Efficient scale / barriers to entry (real):** A $10–20B liquefaction train with an DOE export license, FERC permit, and EPC slot is not replicable by a new entrant. Global LNG demand growth is structurally supported by Asian and European gas displacement of coal.
- **Cost advantage in construction (real but narrowing):** VG's modular "design one, build many" approach has brought Calcasieu and Plaquemines online faster and cheaper per tonne than peers. It deployed ~$47B of gross PP&E to build ~30 mtpa of capacity — an impressive capital efficiency.
- **Intangible assets (contracted cash flow):** Fixed liquefaction fees of **$6.79/MMBtu** implied in Q3 2026 on 465.8 TBtu (~$3.2B of fee revenue per quarter) are contractual and largely insulated from Henry Hub.

**Why not Wide:**
- **No pricing power.** The 2026–2029 global supply wave (U.S. plus Qatar) means the marginal LNG molecule competes on price. VG is a price-taker on the fixed-fee portion and a spread-taker on the rest.
- **The moat was partly a regulatory/contractual arbitrage, and it has closed.** VG's outsized 2022–2025 margins came substantially from declaring commercial operation dates late and selling commissioning cargoes at spot prices. That behavior has now been litigated: **Shell lost its case, but BP won (Oct 2025, claiming ~$3.7B) and Galp won (Oct 2026); Edison settled.** The reputational and contractual cost is real, and other offtakers may follow.
- **The moat has not widened.** ROIC has been volatile and is not durably above WACC: **31.0% (2022) → 20.9% (2023) → 4.9% (2024) → 11.1% (2025).** Capital is deployed years ahead of earnings, so reported returns are a function of the construction cycle, not of franchise strength.

**Circle of competence:** Understandable in outline (contracted liquefaction economics), but the accounting for commissioning cargoes, project-finance structures across bankruptcy-remote SPVs, and commodity-linked margin makes the *reported* earnings low-quality and hard to normalize. This sits at the edge of, not comfortably inside, my circle.

**The critical fact: owner earnings have been negative for four consecutive years** — $(2.6)B, $(5.1)B, $(11.9)B, $(9.7)B. This is a company that consumes capital, not one that generates it. OCF conversion is actually good (Q2 2026 OCF $2.84B vs. net income $2.04B, >130%), so the assets *do* work; the deficit is entirely growth capex. But a Buffett-style buyer needs either self-funding economics or a bulletproof balance sheet, and VG has neither.

**Balance sheet — the decisive weakness:**
- Total debt **$42.8B** (6/30/26), up from $34.9B at YE2025 — **$7.9B of new leverage in six months**.
- Cash $3.2B → **net debt $39.6B**, vs. total equity of **$12.1B**. Net debt/EBITDA ≈ **5.4x**.
- Current ratio ~1.2x; the capital structure is entirely dependent on uninterrupted project-finance access. In a credit window closure, the equity is the shock absorber with only $12B of cushion under $61.5B of assets.
- 2026 H1 FCF was **–$6.5B**; full-year 2026 FCF likely **–$12B to –$14B**, with CP2 Phase 2 ($8.6B financing closed March 2026) and a Plaquemines expansion FID targeted H1 2027 still ahead.

**Positives:** Revenue nearly tripled in 2025 and is running at a ~$18B annualized pace; 2025 gross margin compression to 49% is largely a mix effect (Plaquemines commissioning volumes, higher gas pass-through), not price destruction.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples:** P/E (TTM diluted EPS ~$1.28) ≈ **10x**; P/B ≈ **2.7x** on Q2'26 book of $4.83/sh; EV/EBITDA ≈ **9.8x**; FCF yield **negative**. Sector peer median P/E is 23.1x, implying a ~35% discount — but I do not treat that as mispricing, because peers (Cheniere-type models) are further along the deleveraging curve with positive FCF.

**What the price implies:** At $71.8B EV, the market is paying ~9.8x *current* EBITDA for a business whose EBITDA should step up materially as Plaquemines completes ramp and CP2 comes online. If 2028–2030 EBITDA reaches $11–12.5B, today's EV is ~6x forward — genuinely cheap *if* execution holds.

**Two-scenario intrinsic value (FCFE, 9% discount rate, 5-year horizon, 2% terminal growth, 2.6B diluted shares):**

- **Base case** — projects on schedule, 2031 EBITDA ~$12.5B, debt peaks ~$50B, FCFE ~$6.6B: **≈ $24/share.**
- **Bear case** — LNG oversupply compresses fees, 2031 EBITDA ~$9B, debt $55B, FCFE ~$3.5B: **≈ $13/share.**
- **Severe case** — CP2 slippage plus a multi-billion arbitration payout requiring equity issuance: **$5–7/share.**

Probability-weighted (40/40/20): **≈ $17/share.** Versus $12.87, that is roughly a **25% discount** — right at the edge of, but not clearly inside, a 20–30% margin-of-safety band, and only if you accept that the bear and severe cases are fairly weighted. Note that the DCF tool's mechanical output is unusable here (trailing FCF is negative), and any model that capitalizes negative FCF is meaningless.

**A price that would make this a genuine buy: ~$10 or below** (≈35–40% discount to probability-weighted value, plus a cushion for the contingent arbitration liabilities).

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Below Average / Discount Warranted**

- **Founder-controlled, dual-class structure** (Sabel/Pender, with Stonepeak as the pre-IPO sponsor). Public shareholders have limited influence; the dual-class split concentrates voting control.
- **Capital allocation is a clear weak point.** Paying a dividend — raised 122% to $0.04/quarter (~$0.16/yr, ~1.2% yield, ~$400M/yr) — while burning $13B of capex and adding $8B of debt in six months is precisely the wrong sequencing. Growth capex should be funded before shareholder distributions. No buyback, appropriately.
- **Counterparty conduct is the deepest concern.** The commissioning-cargo strategy that produced 2022–2023's spectacular margins has now been found in breach of contract by multiple tribunals (BP, Galp). BP is pursuing **~$3.7B** — ~11% of market cap and 30% of book equity. Even where VG prevailed (Shell), the pattern is one an owner-operator with an integrity-first culture would not have pursued. Buffett's test — "would I want this management team running my family's business?" — fails.
- **Track record as a public company is poor:** IPO'd at $25 in January 2025; the stock has traded $6.02–$17.56 since and sits 49% below the offer price. Guidance credibility is untested.

To management's credit: the engineering and project-delivery record is genuinely exceptional, and they built a top-five global LNG franchise from scratch in twelve years. This is a high-competence, low-trustworthiness combination — which is the hardest kind to underwrite.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

*(Positioned as a Hold, not a Buy — and a candidate for Sale in a quality-focused, low-turnover portfolio.)*

**Why not Buy:** The business fails three of my four core tests. Owner earnings are negative and will remain so for years (#1). The balance sheet carries 5.4x net debt/EBITDA with only $12B of equity beneath $42.8B of debt (#2). Management has demonstrated a willingness to favor itself over contractual counterparties (#3). A ~25% discount to a wide, assumption-heavy valuation range is not the "fat pitch" I require — and nearly all of the upside depends on projects not yet built, financed at unknown cost.

**Why not Sell outright:** The contracted asset base is real and strategically scarce. Fixed liquefaction fees of $6.79/MMBtu, an ~$18B revenue run-rate, and demonstrated construction cost advantages give a genuine — if narrow — franchise. In the base case the equity is worth roughly double today's price, and the 10x P/E with a step-up in EBITDA through 2027–2028 is not a stretched starting point. Selling into a 75%-off IPO price risks capitulating near the low end of the option value.

**What would change my mind to Buy:** (a) two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow, or a credible path to it; (b) net debt/EBITDA under 3.5x; (c) final resolution of the remaining offtaker arbitrations at a quantifiable, contained cost; (d) suspension of the dividend until capex is self-funded; (e) a price below ~$10.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Leverage/refinancing risk (highest severity).** $42.8B of debt, growing, against $12.1B of equity. Rising rates or a closed project-finance market impairs equity value non-linearly; a covenant breach at one SPV can cascade.
2. **Arbitration contagion.** BP ($3.7B claimed) and Galp have prevailed. Remaining Calcasieu offtakers may file. A cumulative $4–6B of awards would consume a third to half of book equity and could force an equity raise at a depressed price — the single most dilutive scenario.
3. **Execution/schedule risk on CP2 and the Plaquemines expansion.** Every dollar of overrun and every quarter of delay is funded with debt, not retained cash.
4. **LNG price/oversupply cycle.** A 2027–2030 supply glut compresses both spot spreads and the fees available on uncontracted volumes. VG's uncontracted exposure is larger than peers'.
5. **Governance and disclosure quality.** Dual-class founder control, aggressive revenue-recognition timing, and complex SPV accounting limit the reliability of reported earnings — a permanent valuation discount is appropriate.
6. **Data gaps in this analysis:** Q3 2026 full financials had not been filed at the time of writing (only the operating update: 124 cargoes, 465.8 TBtu, $6.79/MMBtu), so TTM figures are through Q2 2026. Detailed peer multiple breakdown, insider transaction history, and debt maturity schedules were not obtained. The reported beta of 0.22 is statistically meaningless given fewer than two years of trading history; realized volatility has been roughly 3–4x that.

---

**Bottom line:** Venture Global is a genuinely impressive industrial builder trading at a superficially cheap 10x earnings and ~9.8x EV/EBITDA. But it is a leveraged, capital-consuming, founder-controlled commodity infrastructure developer with a demonstrated willingness to strain contracts and a balance sheet that leaves almost no room for error. That is a trade — a levered bet on flawless execution and a benign LNG market — not an investment with a margin of safety. **Hold; do not add until the free cash flow turns and the litigation is quantified.**
