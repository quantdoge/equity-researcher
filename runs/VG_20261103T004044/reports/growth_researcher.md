# Venture Global, Inc. (NYSE: VG) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $12.87 (Nov 2, 2026 close) | Market cap $33.9B (diluted) | Net debt ~$32.5B | EV ~$66.5B**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Venture Global is the cost-curve disrupter of the LNG industry: a modular, "design-one-build-many" liquefaction architecture that has delivered ~18 MTPA of Plaquemines capacity just 53 months from FID — roughly half the capex per tonne of the incumbent fleet. It has executed five FIDs in under seven years and raised $95B in capital markets without a dollar of outside equity for its latest phase, and now sits with ~49 MTPA contracted and >100 MTPA in production, construction or development — on a path to becoming the largest US LNG exporter.

The thesis just changed in a way the market has not fully digested: **on Oct 31, 2026 VG declared commercial operations for Plaquemines Phase 1**, converting >500 market-priced commissioning cargoes into 20-year SPAs at ~$5.05/MMBtu. The company's historic margin engine — self-funding construction by selling pre-COD volumes at spot (~$12.50–13.50/MMBtu equivalent) — is now largely litigated and legislated away. What remains is a pure **volume-compounding** story: EBITDA from $5.8B (2025) → $8.7–9.1B (2026E) → ~$14.7B (2028E) → ~$19B (2029E), with the margin questions largely answerable from locked contracts.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **Global LNG trade:** ~420 MTPA today, forecast to ~600–650 MTPA by 2030–35 — a $300–450B annual market. LNG is still only ~13% of internationally traded gas and ~7% of total gas consumption; electrification and AI datacenter load growth are pulling incremental gas demand.
- **VG's share:** ~28 MTPA operating = ~6.7% of global LNG. With CP2 (29 MTPA, fully financed, $20.7B) → **57 MTPA ≈ 9.5% by 2029.** CP3 (30 MTPA, in development) → ~87 MTPA ≈ **14%** — genuinely systemic scale.
- **EV per tonne of capacity:** $66.5B / 57 MTPA ≈ **$1,170/tonne**, versus $1,200–2,000/tonne replacement cost for greenfield liquefaction. VG trades *below* the cost of rebuilding its own asset base — the single most compelling number in this analysis.
- **Adjacent TAM (optionality, not base case):** 16 planned 720 MW power-island units (~11.5 GW) plus a grid-independent power JV targeting co-located multi-GW datacenter campuses. US datacenter capacity is forecast to roughly triple to ~80 GW by 2030. VG owns the land, gas supply, interconnection and turbines — a defensible right to win, but a capital-allocation risk if pursued aggressively.

**Differentiation:** lowest-cost producer, fastest FID-to-COD in the industry, modular redundancy (Calcasieu produced 37 cargos in Q2 *during* major turbine maintenance), and vertically integrated shipping/regas. **Why not Cheniere?** Cheniere is a contracted toll-road at 3% growth; VG still has 60+ MTPA of unbuilt capacity and 30%+ EBITDA CAGR. The premium is justified; the discount is not.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q3 2026 results — Nov 10, 2026** (consensus EPS $0.50, revenue $4.71B). Watch for: first 2027 guidance post-Plaquemines COD, capex guidance, and any damage-provision disclosure.
- **Arbitration quantum phase (next 6–18 months)** — liability already found against VG in the **BP** and **Galp** cases; VG *prevailed* against **Shell** and **Repsol**. Damages rulings are the single largest binary in the stock.
- **CP2 construction milestones** — 29 MTPA, Phase 1 & 2 financed ($20.7B total, largest standalone US bank project financing ever). First LNG targeted 2028–29; on-time/on-budget delivery re-rates the multiple from 4.5x toward peer 7–8x.
- **CP3 (30 MTPA) FID** — the next leg of the growth ladder; requires DOE non-FTA authorisation and long-term contracting in a soft market.
- **Dividend trajectory** — Q3 2026 dividend raised 122% to $0.04/qtr (~1.2% yield). First buyback would be the signal that the capex super-cycle is peaking.
- **Datacenter/power JV announcements** — new TAM optionality, but the Oct 15 and Nov 2 announcements each cost the stock 6–13%; the market wants execution on LNG, not a new narrative.

## 5. Alternative Data Signals

**Supportive:**
- Cargo volumes: 127 cargos exported in Q2 2026 (+38 y/y); 466.4 TBtu sold (+42%); 2026 guidance raised to 500–518 cargos. Plaquemines alone to contribute 351–364 cargos in 2026.
- Contracting momentum is the strongest in the sector: >2 MTPA of new/increased SPAs in Q2 alone (Atlantic-SEE to 1.0 MTPA, EnBW 0.82 MTPA, TotalEnergies 0.85 MTPA, Vitol to 1.7 MTPA). **91% of 2026 available cargos already contracted.**
- Hiring: active reqs across structural, rotating equipment, project engineering and procurement in Houston, Cameron and Plaquemines — consistent with CP2 construction ramp, not contraction.
- Estimate momentum turned positive: 3 consecutive EPS beats (+5.3%, +51.5%, +18.5%) after four straight 2025 misses. Wells Fargo upgraded to Overweight with a $21 target on Oct 20, 2026.

**Contradicting:**
- Gross margin structurally down from 78.7% (2023) → 49.3% (2025) as spot exposure disappears; post-COD the mix shifts decisively to $5.05/MMBtu fixed fees.
- Negative revision momentum on the out-year (−21% on 2030 EPS).
- EIA sees Henry Hub at $3.16/MMBtu in 2027 (−9% y/y) — feed-gas tailwind, but a soft global curve compresses the spot spreads that made 2026 exceptional.
- Heavy-volume distribution days on every negative headline (34M+ shares on Oct 15 and Nov 2) suggest institutional selling, not retail noise.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (high-conviction, high-variance)

Mean analyst target **$15.83** (range $13–22); Wells Fargo $21, Morgan Stanley $22, Goldman $18, Bernstein $14.

**Scenario math (2029 EBITDA × multiple, less ~$42B net debt, ÷ 2.64B shares):**
- **Bear** ($13B EBITDA, 5.0x) → **~$8/share** (−38%)
- **Base** ($17B EBITDA, 6.5x) → **~$20/share** (+55%)
- **Bull** ($21B EBITDA, 8.0x) → **~$47/share** (+265%)

The asymmetry is decisively favorable — roughly 2.5:1 upside/downside. You are buying 30% EBITDA CAGR, ~49 MTPA of 20-year contracted revenue, a fully-financed 29 MTPA project, and 30 MTPA of unbuilt optionality at **below replacement cost**, at 4.5x 2028 EBITDA. The stock is down 49% from its $25 IPO and up 121% from its December 2025 low of $5.82 — the reset has happened, and the multiple already discounts a litigation loss and an LNG glut.

**Why not Strong Buy:** growth *quality* is B-grade. This is capex-intensive growth with declining unit margins, $32.5B of net debt (3.7x 2026E EBITDA), no FCF until 2028, and a founder-controlled dual-class structure that gives minority holders no recourse on capital allocation. The Oct 31 COD removed the upside optionality that made the 2023–2026 numbers look like a software company's.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Arbitration damages (highest-probability, hardest to size).** BP and Galp have won liability; quantum rulings could plausibly total $1–3B, plus precedent that forces earlier COD declarations across the fleet — structurally capping future commissioning-cargo windfalls and raising effective capital costs on CP2/CP3.
2. **Leverage + execution.** $32.5B net debt and 2025 capex of $13.4B against negative FCF. A CP2 cost overrun or schedule slip does not just delay EBITDA — it compounds interest into a fixed-fee revenue base. VG's own history includes Plaquemines cost inflation.
3. **LNG oversupply 2027–2029.** ~200+ MTPA of new global capacity (US + Qatar) lands into a market where VG has only ~9% spot exposure left. Contracted volumes are insulated; CP3's ability to contract at attractive fees is not.
4. **Margin mix erosion / 2030 EPS rollover.** Consensus already models EPS peaking in 2029. If the $5.05/MMBtu fee base is the ceiling rather than the floor, the terminal multiple deserves to be 5x, not 8x.
5. **Capital allocation drift.** An 11.5 GW power-island and datacenter program is a new business with new counterparties, permitting and execution risk. Two of the last three announcements on this theme cost shareholders 6–13%. Watch for capex creep here.
6. **Governance.** Founder control via dual-class; limited minority influence over leverage, M&A or related-party transactions; a short reporting history (IPO Jan 2025) with no through-cycle disclosure.

**Bottom line:** an asymmetric growth-at-a-deep-discount position where the disruption is real, the contracts are locked, and the market is still pricing the old business model's collapse rather than the new one's volume ramp. Own it, size it modestly, and watch the arbitration quantum and the 2027 guidance on Nov 10.
