# Sector Brief — Venture Global (VG)
**Sector:** Energy / Oil & Gas Midstream (LNG liquefaction & export)
**Last price:** $12.87 (2 Nov 2026) · **Market cap:** $32.2bn · **Beta:** 0.22

## 1. Sector Overview

VG operates in the **global LNG liquefaction and export** industry — technically "midstream," but economically a **commodity-processing arbitrage business**: buy US natural gas at Henry Hub, liquefy, and sell at JKM/TTF-linked prices. The industry is entering the largest capacity buildout in its history, with a US wave (Plaquemines, Corpus Christi Stage 3, Golden Pass, Port Arthur, Rio Grande) plus Qatar's North Field expansion taking global liquefaction capacity from roughly 420 MTPA toward 600+ MTPA by 2029–2030. Supply is highly concentrated at the country level (Qatar, US and Australia ≈ two-thirds of global capacity) but the merchant/portfolio market is fragmented. Barriers to entry are very high — $10bn+ per train, multi-year FERC/DOE permitting, and a near-duopoly in credible EPC execution — yet that same capital intensity has produced synchronized FIDs and a coming supply glut. The sector is in a **late-expansion cycle**: demand (Asia power, European substitution, coal-to-gas) is growing 3–5%, but capacity additions are growing faster, so the 2027–2029 setup is one of **price convergence and margin compression**, front-run by a genuinely tight 2026 spot market.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Widening feedstock-to-outlet spread.** EIA sees Henry Hub averaging **$3.16/MMBtu in 2027, down 9% YoY**; Enverus cut its 2027 forecast to **$3.25** as Haynesville/Permian supply outpaces LNG demand. Falling feedstock cost against still-elevated JKM/TTF is direct gross-margin expansion for US liquefiers.
- **2026 supply-shock pricing.** Hormuz disruptions have constrained Qatari volumes; OIES baselines TTF at **$16.05/MMBtu in Q1-2027** before easing to ~$12.39. Spot-exposed volumes (VG's model) capture this disproportionately.
- **Structural demand growth and contracting.** Asian coal-to-gas switching plus European loss of Russian pipe gas underpins long-term SPA demand; VG is targeting **85 MTPA by 2029** from a ~30 MTPA base, with Plaquemines Phase 1 entering commercial operations (Nov 2026) converting spot sales into contracted, bankable cash flow.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **The 2027–2029 oversupply wall.** Global LNG supply could "reshape gas pricing regimes," with Asian and European prices *converging toward the short-run cost of US supply* from 2027. That is a structural cap on the arbitrage that generates VG's margin.
- **Contract-execution / litigation risk is sector-specific and severe here.** A tribunal found VG **breached its Galp supply agreement (Oct 2026)**; **BP won its arbitration and is seeking >$1bn**; Shell lost its case (Mar 2026). Two adverse panels erode the credibility needed to sign the long-term SPAs that fund new trains.
- **Rivalry and capital intensity.** Cheniere (~45 MTPA operating), Qatar, NextDecade and multiple Gulf Coast projects compete for the same offtakers and the same EPC/contractor capacity, while interest expense (VG: **$1.45bn in FY25**, annualising near $1.9bn) is a fixed claim on cash flow through the cycle.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position — **Strong (with a governance discount)**

**Rationale:** On pure operating economics VG is the best-in-class asset base in the peer set. It delivers **49.3% gross and 36.6% operating margins** — roughly double the Energy sector aggregate (22.2% / 8.0%) and 1.7x Cheniere's gross margin — because Calcasieu and Plaquemines are new, large-train, low-cost Gulf Coast assets selling into spot-linked prices rather than legacy fixed tolling. Growth (+177% FY25, +53% H1'26) is an order of magnitude above sector (−3.0%) and above Cheniere (+24%). It is the **share gainer**, not the incumbent defending position.

Two offsets keep this from "dominant." First, **scale and balance-sheet**: at $32bn market cap against Cheniere's larger contracted backlog and investment-grade profile, VG carries meaningfully more leverage risk at a cyclical turning point. Second, **the integrity of its contracting model** — adverse BP and Galp rulings, with >$1bn of potential damages, directly attack the credibility of the long-term SPA book VG needs to pre-sell CP2 and beyond. Valuation already embeds much of this: ~13.8x FY25 EPS (9.1x annualised H1'26) and ~5.6x FY25 EBITDA territory versus Cheniere's structurally higher multiple — the Zacks "VG vs LNG" comparison frames VG as the cheaper, higher-beta option. The stock's 2026 path ($7.00 → $17.56 March peak → $12.87, −27% from high) confirms the market prices VG as a **levered bet on the LNG spread plus a litigation discount**, not as a defensive compounder.

## 6. Sector Rating: **Neutral**

Attractive *today*, unattractive *into 2028*. The 2026–early 2027 window is unusually favourable — cheap Henry Hub feedstock, Hormuz-constrained Qatari supply, elevated TTF/JKM — and that window is exactly where VG's uncontracted volume sits. But the forward curve for the industry is a capacity tsunami that converges global prices toward the marginal cost of US supply, which is precisely where VG's margin advantage lives. I rate the sector Neutral rather than Attractive because the tailwind is **cyclical and dated**, and Neutral rather than Unattractive because demand growth is real and the low-cost Gulf Coast producers (VG, Cheniere) are the structural winners of a price-convergence world even as the spread narrows.

**What would change the rating:** an accelerated Asian demand surprise or sustained Hormuz disruption (→ Attractive); a wave of 2027–28 SPA cancellations or further adverse VG arbitration awards (→ Unattractive).
