## TSCO — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company:** Tractor Supply Company (NASDAQ: TSCO) — largest US rural lifestyle retailer. HQ Brentwood, TN. FY2025 10-K filed Feb 2026 (FYE 12/27/2025). 2,672 locations at Q2 2026 (TSCO + Petsense by Tractor Supply).

**Bottom line:** TSCO is a *single-country retailer*. Country risk is therefore not a "map" problem — it is a **US policy-risk** problem. The binding constraints are (1) a tariff regime that is legally unstable and being rebuilt statute-by-statute, and (2) state-level consumer-protection/environmental enforcement that has already produced two multi-million-dollar actions in twelve months.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**Risk 1 — US tariff-regime instability (highest)*
- *Sequence:* The **Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs (Feb 2026)**, opening refund claims. A replacement **Section 122 temporary import surcharge ran 24 Feb–24 Jul 2026 (150 days) and expired** without congressional extension, while **Section 232 and Section 301 activity expanded** (Ropes & Gray; Covington; Baker Donelson; StratTrade).
- *TSCO impact:* Q2 2026 gross margin rose 24bp on "disciplined product cost management and **tariff-related benefits**," and management guided to **total 2026 tariff refunds of $100M+** (Barclays Global Consumer Staples Conf., 9 Sep 2026). Q1 2026, by contrast, saw profit fall on "**tariff rate pressures**" (Reuters, 21 Apr 2026).
- *Assessment:* Probability of further regime churn **Very High**; impact **Medium**. The refund is a *one-off cash/ margin tailwind*, not a durable earnings improvement — investors should not capitalize it. The residual risk is that Section 232/301 expansions are re-aimed at hardgoods, tools, and pet consumables, where TSCO's private-brand penetration (JobSmart, Ridgecut, Retriever, 4health) is highest and price elasticity in a rural customer base is real.

**Risk 2 — State AG / regulatory enforcement expansion (California as template)**
- Two actions in 12 months, both with remediation/monitorship terms rather than fines alone. California's enforcement posture is explicitly expansionary ("We will continue to look broadly across industries," CPPA Enforcement Chief) and CPPA leads a **bipartisan multi-state Consortium of Privacy Regulators** — the mechanism by which a California precedent becomes a 10-state template.
- *Assessment:* Probability of recurrence **High**; impact **Low–Medium** (individual penalties $1–5M are <0.3% of net income, but aggregated multi-state actions and mandated compliance infrastructure raise the run-rate cost).

**Risk 3 — Brand politicization / stakeholder-opposition risk**
- TSCO became a national test case in June 2024 when it cut **DEI roles, DEI goals and carbon-emission targets** after a conservative social-media campaign (AP, NPR, WSJ). Its retreat is now cited by name in *other* companies' proxy fights — referenced in **Apple's 2025 DEF 14A** and **Home Depot's 2026 proxy** as precedent, and targeted in TSCO's own 2025 proxy by activist shareholders.
- *Assessment:* Probability of episodic flare-up **Medium**; impact **Low** on fundamentals, but non-trivial on multiple and on employee/labour relations. This is a two-sided risk: re-escalation from either flank generates headline and proxy-cost noise, not cash-flow damage.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Privacy consent architecture was proven defective** (CCPA) — and is now under a 4-year officer-certification regime. The **VIP Petcare acquisition** (Q2 2026, $9.5M deal costs) adds veterinary/health-adjacent data, a materially stricter category than retail loyalty data (Neighbor's Club). New compliance surface immediately post-remediation.
2. **Product-labeling/"green" claims are an active enforcement target** — "biodegradable" already cost $5.1M in California. TSCO's private-brand portfolio (including the *American Farmworks* label) sits squarely in the FTC's **Made in USA** and green-claims enforcement zone. This is the most likely next regulatory action.
3. **Pesticide/ag-chemical licensing is a per-store administrative risk** across 2,000+ stores in 49 states — the Sonoma case shows how unglamorous licensing lapses convert into multi-county UCL penalties.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **LOW–MEDIUM**

**Decomposition:**
- Classic geopolitical/country risk: **Low** (100% US revenue; no sanctions, FX, expropriation, or cross-border litigation exposure).
- Trade-policy/COGS risk: **Medium–High** (proven in both directions — tariff costs hit Q1 2026, refunds helped Q2 2026; regime still in flux post-SCOTUS).
- Domestic regulatory/litigation risk: **Medium** (two 8-figure state enforcement actions in 12 months, recurring settlement drag, active greenwashing/privacy frontiers).

**What would change the rating:** a Section 232/301 expansion covering hardgoods and pet consumables **without** offsetting refunds (→ High); a multi-state privacy/consumer-protection sweep joined by CPPA's regulator consortium (→ High); or any evidence of a federal investigation (→ Very High).
