## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional, tilting risk-off**

**As of 16–18 October 2026.** The US is in a *reflation-with-hawkish-policy-reversal* regime — the worst combination for a long-duration, discretionary consumer retailer. The Fed cut from 4.50% to 3.75% through late 2025/early 2026, but CPI has re-accelerated from **2.65% YoY (Dec 2025) to 3.72% YoY (Sep 2026)** — and the last three months annualise at **4.6%**. The FOMC responded by **hiking 25bp on 17 Sep 2026 to a 3.75–4.00% target range** (DFEDTARU), ending the easing cycle.

Critically, the long end is selling off on *real* yields, not inflation expectations: 10Y breakevens are **contained at 2.35%**, while the 10Y nominal has risen from 4.48% (1 Jul) to a peak of **5.31% (5 Oct)**. That implies the 10Y real yield has jumped ~**+65bp in three months to ~2.95%** — a term-premium/fiscal-supply shock. This is the single most important macro fact for TSCO: it is a pure valuation event hitting high-multiple consumer equities, and it is not offset by easier money.

Growth is decelerating but not contracting (Q2 2026 real GDP **+2.2%**, Q1 +2.5%, after a 0.2%/3.9% whipsaw in H2 2025), with unemployment stable at **4.2%**. The labour market is fine; the *consumer* is not: University of Michigan sentiment is **51.7 (Aug 2026)**, down 30% from 74.0 in Dec 2024 and near 1980/2022 troughs.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Tractor Supply is ~100% US revenue, rural/exurban, with high-ticket seasonal and farm/hardware exposure. Beta is low (0.51), so its 2026 collapse is idiosyncratic *and* rate-driven, not market beta.

- **Real-rate shock → multiple compression (strongly negative, already partly realised).** TSCO de-rated from ~28–30x to **~17.0x** on FY26E EPS of ~$1.95 (price $33.16, 16 Oct). A ~65bp rise in the real 10Y mechanically compresses a long-duration retail multiple by roughly 15–20%; roughly half the 45% drawdown from the Aug 2025 peak ($59.94 → $33.16) is macro discounting, half is estimate cuts (Q1 EPS missed by 9%; Q2 comps **-1.5%** with transactions **-1.7%**).
- **Housing freeze → big-ticket (strongly negative).** Mortgages at 7.40% and 10Y at ~5% stall existing-home turnover; TSCO's mowers, trailers, structures and fencing are financed, home-equity-linked purchases. Management explicitly cited **"softness in seasonal categories, including big-ticket items"** in May 2026.
- **CPI at 3.7% vs sentiment 51.7 → real-income squeeze (negative).** Comp *ticket* rose only **+0.2%** — pricing power is exhausted even as costs rise. This is the classic trap: input inflation without the ability to pass it through.
- **Tariffs (net neutral-to-positive).** Q2 gross margin *expanded* 24bp to 37.2% on "disciplined product cost management and **tariff-related benefits**" that offset higher freight and deliberate price investment. Import-cost risk is being absorbed better than feared — a genuine positive surprise vs 2025 consensus.
- **Consumer mix (partial offset).** ~50%+ of sales are consumable/usable/edible (feed, pet, hardware) — needs-based and low-ticket. This is why comps are -1.5% rather than -8%; the model is defensive-adjacent.
- **Currency (neutral).** Negligible FX translation; broad USD strength (+2.5% YoY) is immaterial to revenue but mildly helpful on imported COGS.
- **Restructuring noise (non-macro).** ~75 Petsense closures, $5.9M write-down, VIP Petcare acquisition drove SG&A +14.4% (26.8% of sales vs 23.9%). Q2 diluted EPS $0.69, adjusted $0.81; FY26 guidance cut to ~**$1.90–2.00**.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **Unfavourable**

Three of the four macro channels that matter — real rates, housing/financing costs, and the consumer real-income squeeze — are all pointing the wrong way simultaneously, and the Fed's September hike removed the "policy easing will bail out the multiple" thesis. The offset is that the stock has already absorbed an enormous amount of this (17x, ~2.8% dividend yield, -47% from peak), so the *direction* of macro is unfavourable while the *magnitude priced in* is now substantial. I would not characterise this as a macro tailwind at any horizon under 2–3 quarters.

## 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **Bull: "Hike-and-Hold Becomes Hike-and-Cut"**

The most violent surprise would be **upside**. Q4 2025 GDP printed **+0.2%** and sentiment is at 51.7 — the economy is far more fragile than the 2.2% Q2 headline. If shelter disinflation plus a demand shock pulls CPI from 3.7% back toward 2.5% by Q1 2027, the term-premium trade unwinds violently: the 10Y falls from ~5.0% to 4.2–4.3%, mortgages drop below 6.5%, and sentiment re-rates off 1980-era lows. A defensive, needs-based retailer with 37% gross margins and a 2.8% yield is precisely the asset that gets re-rated in that rotation — **20–21x on $2.10 EPS ≈ $43–44, roughly +30–33%**, with the added kicker that tariff benefits are already flowing through gross margin and the Petsense drag is self-inflicted and fixable.

The bear mirror image is a term-premium spiral (30Y >6%, mortgages >8%) that takes comps to -4% and EPS to ~$1.60 at a 13x multiple (~$21, -37%) — I weight that at lower probability given anchored breakevens, but it is the tail that a 5.37% 30Y makes live.
