### 1. Geographic Risk Map

**Critical structural point:** Fabrinet reports revenue by *customer shipping/billing location*, not where it manufactures. Almost all *production* sits in one country — Thailand — with a smaller China footprint and US/Israel design-NPI sites. That mismatch is the core of the risk profile.

| **Thailand** | ~small direct revenue share, but **~bulk of global manufacturing** (Chonburi, Pranburi campuses; new ~$132.5M Chonburi building) | Primary production base, ~20k+ employees | **High** (tariff, transshipment scrutiny, FX, flood precedent) |

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US policy action on Chinese optical transceivers (two-sided, highest salience)**
- *Fact base:* Reuters (Aug 4, 2026) reported the White House/FCC drafting a ban on Chinese-made optical transceivers; Fierce Network (Sep 8, 2026) details scope uncertainty — finished goods vs. components, and whether it captures China-linked subsidiaries or China-sourced lasers, where "most lasers come through China." Innolight was added to DoD's 1260H list in June 2026; FCC could add Innolight/Eoptolink to the Covered List. Microsoft has been lobbying the White House against the ban (247 Wallst, Nov 12, 2026).
- *Assessment:* **Probability of some restriction enacted: ~55–65%** (12–24mo). **Impact: net ambiguous, skewed positive on share, negative on supply chain.** Fabrinet's Thailand capacity is the natural beneficiary of Innolight/Eoptolink exclusion (a genuine structural tailwind for a Thai-based contract manufacturer). The downside is a component-level rule: Fabrinet's China optics/crystal operations (Casix) and China-sourced laser/components would face origin and qualification friction, and customer re-qualification cycles are 2–4 quarters.

**Risk 2 — Thailand single-country concentration + trade enforcement**
- *Fact base:* US maintains a **19% reciprocal tariff** on Thailand (USTR Fact Sheet, Oct 2025; White House joint statement). The White House "Great Transshipment Scam" report (Aug 13, 2026) placed **Thailand in Tier 2** alongside Vietnam/Malaysia/Indonesia, and Thai electronics/electrical machinery is one of eight flagged sectors ($52.2bn US exports) facing intensified CBP origin scrutiny (Nation Thailand, Aug 16, 2026). Fabrinet's own 10-K flags trade-policy/tariff risk.
- *Assessment:* **Probability of origin-documentation/CBP friction: High (>60%); probability of a company-specific material tariff hit: Medium (~25%).** Because Fabrinet performs genuine, high-value manufacturing in Thailand (not label-swapping), it should withstand origin tests — but the burden of proof is now on the exporter, and a high-China-content optics BOM is exactly the profile CBP screens. Compound risk: baht appreciation (FX headwind repeatedly cited by management) + 19% duty + single-campus flood exposure (2011 Thailand floods shut a Fabrinet plant — the precedent exists).

**Risk 3 — China supply-chain and export-control entanglement**
- *Fact base:* China's export-control net has kept widening through 2025–26 (Global Trade Alert); rare-earth restrictions eased only temporarily in Nov 2025 (CSIS). Fabrinet sources optics, crystals, lasers and rare-earth-dependent materials from China even while assembling in Thailand.
- *Assessment:* **Probability of a supply interruption or licence delay: ~30% over 24 months; Impact: moderate (revenue timing, expedite costs),** low existential risk given Fabrinet's vertical integration in custom optics/glass and multi-sourcing history.

*Watch item (not top-3):* customer concentration in AI/data-center optics means Fabrinet's geopolitical risk is partly **transmitted** from Nvidia/hyperscaler policy exposure — a US-China deal or a Chinese retaliation against US AI hardware would hit orders before it hits Fabrinet's own facilities.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Litigation:** No material pending litigation, government investigation, or securities class action against Fabrinet surfaced in searches or filing metadata. Filings are routine (10-Q, 8-K, DEF 14A, Form 4s) — a clean docket. *(Gap: the "Legal Proceedings" note in the FY2026 10-K could not be retrieved directly; SEC.gov and investor.fabrinet.com fetches returned 403. Confidence: moderate-high that litigation risk is immaterial.)*
- **Regulators in scope:** FCC (equipment authorisation/Covered List), BIS (export controls/ECCN classification of transceivers and lasers), CBP (origin/transshipment), OFAC (minimal), Thai BOI/Board of Investment incentives, and US listing/audit rules for a Cayman-domiciled, Thailand-operated issuer.
- **Regulatory tailwind:** The Covered List dynamic is arguably the single largest *value-creating* regulatory event available to Fabrinet — a policy-driven share shift toward non-China contract manufacturing capacity.
- **Tax/domicile:** Cayman incorporation with Thai and Chinese subsidiaries = transfer-pricing scrutiny and Thai BOI tax-holiday dependency; no debt, $934M cash (FY25), so no financing covenants at risk.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Low legal/contamination risk (no litigation, no debt, no sanctioned-jurisdiction revenue) is offset by **extreme single-country manufacturing concentration in Thailand**, a 19% US tariff regime, Tier 2 transshipment classification, and direct exposure to the FCC's China transceiver rulemaking — where Fabrinet is simultaneously a potential winner and a potential casualty depending on rule scope. Nothing here is unmanageable, but the policy sensitivity of the equity is unusually high for a company with a clean legal record.

**Confidence:** Geographic revenue splits high (filed data); litigation status moderate (filing text inaccessible); FCC rule scope low (unresolved as of Nov 2026).
