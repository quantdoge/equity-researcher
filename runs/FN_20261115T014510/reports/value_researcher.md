## FABRINET (NYSE: FN) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $327.98 (13 Nov 2026) | Market cap $11.9B | EV ≈ $11.0B | Shares 36.2M diluted*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW**

Fabrinet is a precision optical/electro-mechanical contract manufacturer (EMS), incorporated in the Cayman Islands, with essentially all manufacturing in Thailand. It builds optical transceivers, ROADMs, amplifiers, tunable lasers, industrial lasers and sensors for OEMs — and has become a key manufacturing partner in the 800G/1.6T AI datacenter optical ramp.

**What is genuinely good:**
- **Switching costs via qualification cycles.** Optical packaging is a process-knowledge business; customer design-in and qualification cycles run 12–18 months. Once a customer's line is qualified at Fabrinet, moving it is expensive and risky. That is a real, if modest, moat.
- **Cost/scale advantage.** Low-cost, high-skill Thai workforce plus vertical integration (crystals, lenses, prisms, PCB assembly, final test) has produced remarkably *stable* margins through cycles: gross margin 12.3% → 12.7% → 12.4% → 12.1% → 12.0% (FY22–FY26); operating margin 9.0% → 10.0%. No leverage collapse, no boom-bust margin.
- **Returns above cost of capital, consistently.** ROIC 16.0–18.8% and ROE 16.8–19.3% in each of the last four years. That is the hallmark of a modestly moated business.

**What caps the moat rating:**
- **No pricing power.** Gross margin has drifted *down* ~70bp over four years despite 36% revenue growth and record demand. A business with real pricing power does not lose 20bp of gross margin to FX while growing 51%.
- **Extreme customer concentration and rising.** In the latest 10-Q, customers representing ≥10% of revenue together accounted for **59.1%** of revenue, up from 48.5%. Several individual customers exceed 10%. This is the single largest structural flaw.
- **Capital intensity inflecting hard.** Capex was $45M/quarter a year ago; it was **$158.7M** in Q1 FY27, with a new Thailand facility ("Building 11") under construction. Depreciation will ramp materially against a 12% gross margin base.
- **Commoditization risk.** Transceivers are increasingly contested by Chinese vendors and larger ODMs (Foxconn, Jabil), and customers can dual-source.

**Moat trend over five years: scale widened, pricing power narrowed.** Net: Narrow, not Wide.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Where the stock is:** $748 peak (May 2026) → **$328 now, –56%**. The de-rating reflects concentration fears, margin dilution from FX/depreciation, and negative FCF — not a demand miss. Revenue has beaten guidance every quarter.

**Forward build (my estimates, GAAP):**
- FY27 revenue ≈ $6.5–6.7B (+41–45%), Q2 guide $1.60–1.65B
- FY27 GAAP EPS ≈ **$15.50** (Q1 $2.86 + Q2 guide $3.81–3.96 + H2 ramp); non-GAAP ≈ $17.90
- FY28 GAAP EPS ≈ $18.50–19.50

**Multiples:** 21.2x FY27E GAAP EPS (18.3x non-GAAP); ~13.6x FY27E EV/EBITDA (~$810M); 1.7x EV/sales; 4.8x book; **FCF yield ≈ 0% and negative in FY27.**

**Intrinsic value (owner-earnings DCF, $560M normalized FY27 base, maintenance capex ≈ D&A):**

**Intrinsic value ≈ $400 (range $370–430).** At $328 the stock sits at an ~18% discount — real, but short of the 25–30% margin of safety I require, particularly for a 10%-operating-margin contract manufacturer with 59% customer concentration and negative free cash flow. My buy threshold is **$280–300**, or a demonstrated turn in FCF conversion.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good**

- **Seamus Grady (CEO since 2017):** operator with deep manufacturing background; has compounded revenue at ~19% while holding margins in a 12–13% band. Delivered exactly what he said he would.
- **Capital allocation discipline:** no dividends, no empire-building M&A (goodwill $2.5M), steady buybacks ($29.1M in Q1 FY27; $637.3M total authorization, expanded by $103M). Repurchases at $500–750 would have been value-destructive; the current pace at $328 is more sensible.
- **Balance sheet stewardship:** historically net-cash with minimal debt. The new $74.8M borrowing is defensible given a once-in-a-decade capacity build, but it marks a change in character worth monitoring.
- **Gaps in my diligence:** I could not verify insider ownership or the FY26 10-K risk factors (both the SEC filing tool and the IR site returned no data/403). Treat the management assessment as incomplete on alignment.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

This is a good, honestly run, genuinely profitable business — 19% ROIC, net cash, no acquisition waste, growing 50% — trading at a price that is *fair* rather than *cheap*. But three things keep me from buying:

1. **The moat is Narrow and narrowing on price.** 12% gross margins and 59% customer concentration mean Fabrinet is a price-taker supplying a boom. A fair price for a price-taker is not 21x earnings when free cash flow is zero.
2. **The cash conversion has broken down at exactly the wrong time.** $159M of quarterly capex against $21M of operating cash flow, funded partly with new debt, into demand that is 59% concentrated in a handful of AI-adjacent customers. If the cycle turns, they own the depreciation without the revenue.
3. **No margin of safety.** $328 vs ~$400 intrinsic value is an 18% discount. I want 25–30% for this quality tier.

**I would become a buyer at $280–300**, or above $328 if two things appear in the FY27 filings: capex guidance stepping down toward maintenance levels, and customer concentration stabilizing below ~50%. A 10-K-level disclosure of a large NVIDIA-adjacent customer becoming a formal long-term agreement would also justify a higher multiple.

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Customer concentration (the dominant risk).** 59.1% of revenue from ≥10% customers, rising. Loss or in-sourcing by one program would be a step-function hit to both revenue and the multiple.
2. **AI capex digestion.** Revenue is levered to hyperscaler optical transceiver demand. A pause in 800G/1.6T orders would hit a business that just added $600M+ of annual capacity.
3. **Depreciation cliff / margin compression.** D&A was $68M in FY26; a $600M+ annual capex program implies D&A of $250–350M within three years. At a 12% gross margin, that is ~4 points of operating margin at risk unless revenue doubles again.
4. **Negative FCF and rising leverage.** FCF yield is ~0% and Q1 FY27 FCF was roughly –$137M. Continued debt issuance would erode the net-cash cushion that is a core part of the thesis.
5. **Inventory/obsolescence.** Inventory +76% YoY against revenue +36%, in a product market that iterates every 12–18 months.
6. **Geographic/FX concentration.** All manufacturing in Thailand; Thai baht strength already cost 20bp of gross margin. Flood, political, tariff or labor shocks are undiversified.
7. **Earnings-quality overhang.** FY26 GAAP EPS was inflated by a near-zero tax rate; reported growth will optically decelerate as the tax rate normalizes to the mid-teens, even if operations improve.

---

**Bottom line:** A well-run, Narrow-moat compounder in a cyclical upcycle, at a fair price with no cushion. **Hold — do not chase; set a limit near $290.**
