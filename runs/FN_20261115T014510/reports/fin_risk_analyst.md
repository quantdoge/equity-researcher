### 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: INVESTMENT GRADE (effectively AAA-profile, unrated)**

Fabrinet is, from a credit standpoint, one of the cleanest balance sheets in the technology hardware universe. Total debt is **$4.0M** against $2,454M of equity and $875M of cash + short-term investments — a **net cash position of $871M**. Interest expense for FY26 was **$84,000**. There is no refinancing cliff (long-term debt is literally $0), no covenant exposure, no pension or off-balance-sheet debt disclosed, and the company could retire all debt from 0.1% of one quarter's cash flow.

The Altman Z-Score of **4.12** sits in the safe zone (>2.99), driven by a 0.66 retained-earnings/assets ratio and 1.19x asset turnover. Note the tool's caveat: x4 uses *book* equity ($2,454M / $1,457M liabilities = 1.68) rather than market cap; on a market-cap basis x4 would be ~8.0x, pushing the Z-score materially higher. The 4.12 is therefore a floor, not a ceiling.

The Piotroski F-Score of **5/9** is the weakest credit-adjacent score, and its failure points are informative rather than solvency-related: accruals quality (F4), leverage trend (F5), gross margin (F8) and asset turnover (F9). Abstractly: Fabrinet's *balance sheet* scores 9/9; its *cash conversion and margin trajectory* score 2/5.

Interest coverage of **5,510x** (EBIT $555.2M / interest $0.084M) is not a meaningful ratio — it is a measure of the absence of leverage, not of coverage capacity. No public credit rating or CDS spread exists because there is no public debt to rate. **Flagged as a data gap, but one that is immaterial given the capital structure.**

**The decisive dynamic is working capital reversal.** A 20% revenue contraction releases capital rather than consuming it: receivables fall ~$204M and inventory falls ~$204M, a **+$408M cash inflow**, against a $259.9M drain at +36% growth. Stress-case FCF:

> OCF ≈ $301.6M NI + $68.4M D&A + $408M WC release ≈ **$778M**; less capex cut to $120M (management would halt expansion) → **FCF ≈ $658M**, or a **5.6% FCF yield** at today's market cap.

**Conclusion:** a 20% revenue decline *improves* Fabrinet's free cash flow and net cash position. The company does not merely survive the downturn — it becomes a stronger credit and a more generous cash generator. Operating breakeven (covering $161.9M of fixed SG&A + D&A at a 12% gross margin) requires revenue of only **~$1,349M, i.e. a 71% decline**. Solvency is not at risk in any realistic scenario.

**The real downside is equity repricing, not insolvency.** At a stressed EPS of $8.35, the current price embeds 39x stressed earnings. If the market simultaneously de-rates the multiple from 25x to 15x, fair value is ~$125 — an additional **−62%**. Combined with the observed −61% drawdown, a −65% to −75% peak-to-trough equity loss in a genuine AI-capex pause is entirely plausible, *while the balance sheet remains pristine*.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating

# MODERATE

*(Split rating required for accuracy: **Credit/Solvency Risk = LOW**; **FCF & Earnings-Quality Risk = MODERATE-HIGH**; **Equity Market Risk = HIGH**)*

Fabrinet cannot go bankrupt on any plausible set of assumptions — it has no debt, $875M of liquid assets, 25% ROIC, and a business model whose working capital self-liquidates in a contraction. But it has stopped converting profit into cash: **$473M of net income generated $3.0M of free cash flow**. That is the single most important financial fact in this analysis, and it is a *quality-of-earnings* signal, not a survival signal. The investment risk here is valuation and volatility, borne entirely by equity holders.

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Free cash flow has effectively gone to zero.** FCF fell from $365.6M (FY24) → $206.5M (FY25) → **$3.0M (FY26)**. Cash conversion of 0.6% against 10.2% net margin is a structural break, not noise.
2. **Capex more than doubled** to $253.7M (5.5% of revenue, from 3.6%). If the AI-datacom capacity build is mistimed, this becomes stranded capital and future D&A drag — and FY26's D&A is already up 28%.
3. **Working capital absorbed $259.9M**, with **inventory +75.8% vs revenue +35.8%** — inventory of $1,021M now equals 22% of annual revenue, and inventory days rose ~8 days. In a demand pause, this is write-down exposure.
4. **Gross margin has declined four consecutive years** (12.71% → 11.99%). Growth is being purchased with price. FY26 revenue grew 36% while gross profit grew only 35% — the incremental business is margin-dilutive.
5. **Equity/assets has fallen from 0.75 to 0.63 in two years**, and current liabilities now represent 96% of all liabilities. Debt-free, yes — but the balance sheet is being progressively consumed to fund the expansion.
6. **~17% of net income may be non-operating.** EBIT ($555.2M) exceeds GAAP operating income ($462.9M) by **$92.4M**, versus only $30.7M in FY25 — despite a *flat* cash balance. The dataset does not decompose this (interest income vs FX/other). If a large share is non-recurring, core earnings power and the true P/E (~30x) are materially worse than headline. **Flagged as unverified — this deserves primary-source confirmation.**
7. **Piotroski 5/9, failing exactly on accruals, leverage trend, gross margin, and asset turnover** — the four factors that matter most for a growth-at-a-peak-multiple manufacturer.
8. **Extreme equity risk:** 85% vol, beta 2.0–3.0, **−60.75% drawdown**, negative Sharpe, and repeated −10% to −22% single-day events. The equity fell 19% on a quarter in which it beat every estimate.
9. **Customer concentration — unquantified.** Fabrinet's largest customers (datacom/hyperscaler-linked) have historically exceeded 10% of revenue each. The tools returned no customer-level disclosure, and this is the single largest unmeasured risk. **Data gap.**

**Explicit data gaps:** no credit rating or CDS (no public debt); no debt maturity schedule beyond $1.2M current / $0 long-term; no revolver or credit-facility disclosure; no customer concentration data; Sortino ratio unavailable; peer multiples unusable (the screener returned only $800B+ mega-caps — AAPL, NVDA, MSFT, TSM, AVGO — which are not valid comparables for an $11.7B optical EMS; a proper comp set would be CIEN, CLS, CTS, GLW, MRCY); the FY26 earnings press release returned HTTP 403, so the −19% earnings-day move (18-Aug-2026) is attributed from secondary reporting (record Q4: revenue $1.32B, GAAP diluted EPS $3.83, 43% forward growth guided) rather than primary source.
