## SK hynix (NASDAQ: SKHY) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Korean memory semiconductor manufacturer; Nasdaq ADR/ordinary-share listing 10 July 2026 (~$29.4bn raised, 17.79m new shares). Market cap ≈$1.17tn. Report date: late Oct 2026.*

---

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

Revenue split derived from SK hynix's Korean regulatory filing (FY2025 revenue KRW 97.1tn), as reported by Yonhap/Korea Herald — the FY2026 regional mix has not yet been disclosed (Q3 2026 release gave product, not geographic, detail).

| Geography | Rev. exposure (% of FY25) | Nature of exposure | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | **68.8%** (KRW 66.8tn) | Billing location of NVIDIA + hyperscaler/CSP customers; tiny physical footprint ($4bn Indiana HBM packaging plant, mass production ~2029). Source of tariff, export-control and now securities-law jurisdiction. | **High** |
| **China** | **19.7%** (KRW 19.1tn) | Wuxi fab (~40% of company DRAM per Futurum), Dalian NAND (Solidigm, ex-Intel), Chongqing packaging; operating only under **annual US licences** | **High** |

| **Europe / other** | <5% (not separately disclosed) | Minimal direct revenue; subject to EU/US aligned export regimes | Low |

**Key structural point:** ~88% of revenue sits in the two principal protagonists of the techno-nationalist rivalry, while ~100% of leading-edge *manufacturing* sits in Korea plus China fabs operating on licences that must be renewed annually.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**#1 — US Section 232 semiconductor tariffs / "build in the US or pay" (Probability: High | Impact: Medium‑High)**
A 25% duty took effect in January 2026 on a narrow set of advanced computing chips, with a much broader "phase two" still unfinalised. Commerce Secretary Lutnick (2 Sep 2026): *"if you build here, you don't pay… if you don't build here, expect to pay."* The mooted mechanism is duty-free quotas linked to planned US output (the Taiwan model: 2.5x planned capacity during construction, 1.5x thereafter). **SK hynix's problem is definitional:** its >$4bn Indiana project is *packaging and R&D*, not wafer output, so a wafer-based exemption test may leave it fully exposed. Korean officials also fear Samsung/SK hynix's existing US projects will not be credited against Seoul's separate $350bn investment package. Because 69% of revenue is booked to US customers but almost nothing is *made* in the US, this is the single largest quantifiable policy exposure.
*(Korea Herald, 3 Sep 2026; White House proclamation, Jan 2026)*

**#2 — Loss of Validated End-User (VEU) status and the annual China licence treadmill (Probability: High — already partly realised | Impact: High)**
Washington revoked the special export exemptions for SK hynix/Samsung/Intel China fabs in Sept 2025, effective 31 Dec 2025; operations now run on **calendar-year licences**, and the Dalian 2026 licence was issued with **quantity limits on equipment**. SK hynix is, in effect, trading US factory commitments for China equipment approvals — Solidigm is seeking approval to install US tools at Dalian as a condition for continuing its planned US NAND fab (upstate New York candidate). Any renewal breakdown, or a widening of the entity-list logic to memory tools, would strand ~40% of DRAM capacity (Wuxi) and the Dalian NAND base with no near-term substitution.
*(Reuters 15 Oct 2026 via TechCrunch; Tom's Hardware; Futurum)*

**#3 — China retaliation and Taiwan chokepoint (Probability: Medium | Impact: High)**
Beijing's export-control architecture now covers gallium, rare earths and at least seven other materials, with **extra scrutiny on semiconductor end-users** (CFR; CSIS). Korean fabs in China are the most accessible hostage. Simultaneously, HBM revenue depends on a single physical routing — Korea → TSMC (Taiwan) → US customers — so any cross-strait disruption or Chinese pressure on Korea's "one-China"/THAAD-era coercion playbook hits the highest-margin product (HBM = 56.4% share by revenue in Q1 2026 per the F-1). The 34% peak-to-trough drawdown between 9 Sep ($198.63) and 22 Oct ($131.69) — coinciding exactly with the tariff-threat revival and the China licence bargaining — shows the market already prices this headline risk (though shares rebounded +13% to $164.70 on 28 Oct results).

*Secondary:* Korean domestic political/trade risk is contained — the Oct 2025 US–ROK pact cut most tariffs 25%→15%, and Seoul formalised new import/export restriction procedures in Oct 2026 — but the $350bn package remains a live source of friction.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active / live matters**
- **DRAM price-fixing class action (filed 25 June 2026, N.D. Cal.):** 14 consumers + 3 small businesses allege Samsung/SK hynix/Micron coordinated the HBM transition and DDR3/DDR4 discontinuation, driving DRAM prices up ~700% in four years. Seeking class certification; **treble damages** if successful. Small today, but the plaintiff firm (Bathaee Dunne) has class-action form. Aggravating factor: SK hynix's predecessor paid a **$185m criminal fine in 2005** for DRAM price fixing (Samsung $300m) and executives served prison time.
- **Monolithic 3D v. SK hynix & Kioxia (ITC, complaint 29 Jan 2026):** NPE alleges HBM2E, HBM3 and HBM3E infringe 3D-stacking patents — a direct attack on the highest-margin product line. Industry view: exclusion orders unlikely, settlements likely.
- **Netlist enforcement campaign:** 2021 settlement ($40m + royalty-bearing cross-licence). Netlist has now **dragged SK hynix into its ITC case against Samsung** (discovery granted Feb 2026), citing SK hynix's US investment to satisfy the "domestic industry" requirement. SK hynix is a third party, not a defendant, but its data may support an import ban on a competitor — and the licence terms (royalty-free from April 2026 per secondary reporting, unverified) are a latent renegotiation risk.
- **Favourable precedent:** the memory trio recently **won an antitrust appeal over DRAM pricing** in the earlier (2016-vintage) collusion litigation (Bloomberg Law) — a meaningful defence anchor for the 2026 case.

**Regulatory environment**
- **US:** BIS export controls (primary), Section 232 (tariffs), and — new since July 2026 — full US securities-law exposure: FCPA, SOX, Reg S-K disclosure and 10-Q/10-K obligations, plus US class-action securities risk on a stock that moves 13% on earnings. Note: SKHY's SEC filing metadata shows **no 10-K/20-F yet on file** and the F-1 text was not retrievable (SEC HTTP 403) — a disclosure-gap flag for the memo.
- **Korea:** KFTC antitrust/subre contracting oversight; Commercial Act/dividend-tax reform agenda; capital-return mechanics (≈KRW 100tn programme, special dividend from Kioxia gains, share cancellation) and the abandoned Solidigm IPO leave the affiliate-structure and governance discount as the main local issue.
- **China:** SAMR/regulatory discretion over the Wuxi/Dalian entities; PIPL/cross-border data constraints on fab data.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Deemed-export / entity-list adjacency** — China fabs serve Chinese customers under US licences; screening for Huawei-affiliated and military end-users is the sharpest FCPA-adjacent (BIS/OFAC) tail risk, and enforcement is now presidential-priority.
2. **Annual licence dependency (concentration, not misconduct)** — a single BIS decision each December can impair ~40% of DRAM capacity. This should be treated as a going-concern-adjacent disclosure item.
3. **Remediation of price-fixing history** — antitrust compliance programme credibility will be tested by the 2026 class action; prior criminal convictions are admissible context.
4. **NPE/patent exposure concentrated in HBM** — two separate NPE actions in 2026 (Netlist-adjacent, Monolithic 3D) suggest royalty-extraction campaigns are scaling with HBM profits.
5. **New-issuer disclosure controls** — first-year US listing, no 10-K/20-F publicly confirmed; SOX 404 and ICFR build-out risk is real for a 36k-employee chaebol affiliate.
6. **No evidence found** of active FCPA/DOJ/OFAC investigation into SK hynix itself, and no data-privacy enforcement action identified. Absence of evidence noted as such.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Two jurisdictions that are ~88% of revenue are in open economic conflict; essentially all leading-edge capacity is manufactured in a *third* country (Korea) that is itself the target of US tariff coercion and Chinese leverage; and China-based production runs on permits renewable annually at Washington's discretion. Legal exposure is **manageable but non-trivial** — a treble-damages antitrust class action with historical precedent against the company, plus two HBM-focused NPE actions — and the HBM franchise's Taiwan routing is an un-hedgeable single point of failure. Mitigants keeping this out of "Very High": strong net cash (KRW 98.7tn), 14 LTAs covering >50% of 2027–28 capacity with price-collared mechanisms, $4bn Indiana investment giving partial tariff/ political cover, and $41bn+ of combined Korean chip investment in the US that aligns Seoul and Washington incentives.

**Highest-conviction monitorables:** (i) the December 2026 BIS annual licence renewal for Wuxi/Dalian and any quantity conditions; (ii) finalisation of Section 232 "phase two" and whether exemptions count packaging/R&D; (iii) class-certification ruling in the N.D. Cal. DRAM case; (iv) whether SK hynix discloses a regional revenue split in its first 10-K/20-F — a failure to do so would itself be an asymmetry.
