# Sector Brief: Memory Semiconductors — SK hynix (SKHY)

*Note on data: SK hynix reports in KRW; Micron in USD (FY ends early Sept). Peer fiscal years are not aligned, so growth/margin comparisons are directional, not exact. The `sector_financials` aggregate endpoint returned only one contributing company (not usable) — peer medians below are hand-computed from individual company filings.*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

SK hynix sits in the DRAM/NAND memory segment of semiconductors — a textbook oligopoly (three firms control ~87% of DRAM: Samsung 38%, SK hynix 25%, Micron 24% in Q2'26; HHI ≈ 2,700) now in the most violent up-cycle in its history. AI datacenter build-out has collided with fixed wafer capacity: AI workloads reportedly absorb ~70% of DRAM supply, and TrendForce tracked conventional DRAM contract prices +58–63% QoQ in 2Q26 (with some segments +93–98% QoQ) and NAND +70–75% QoQ; Gartner models DRAM prices +47% for CY2026. The result is margins with no precedent in the industry — SK hynix posted an **80% operating margin** in Q3'26 (KRW 83.3T operating profit on KRW 104.6T revenue, +328% YoY), and Micron ran **74.6%** for FY26. This is unambiguously a **late-cycle / peak-earnings** position, not early growth, even though the demand driver (AI) is structurally durable.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **HBM/DRAM supply deficit with contracted visibility.** SK hynix has LTAs covering **>50% of 2027–28 production capacity**, with pricing structures that "reflect market conditions"; management guides to shortages persisting toward 2030. HBM4 becomes mainstream from Q4'26, and HBM carries structurally higher ASP and lower competitive intensity than commodity DRAM.
- **Pricing power converted directly to margin.** Q3'26 DRAM and NAND ASPs rose >20% QoQ, gross margin reached ~86%, and operating margin hit 80%. With D&A largely fixed, incremental price flows almost entirely to operating profit — Q2'26 revenue rose 51% QoQ while operating profit rose 61%.
- **Cash return + hyperscaler lock-in.** ~KRW 100T (~$75B) of shareholder returns planned for 2026 (funded partly by Kioxia investment gains), plus long-dated DRAM agreements being negotiated by Meta/Google/Microsoft/Amazon. Trailing valuation sits near **8.8x operating profit**; Micron trades under 6x forward earnings — a rare combination of peak earnings and trough multiples.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Share-price fragility to cycle rhetoric.** Memory names sold off hard in mid-Oct 2026 — SK hynix -26% and Samsung -22% in five days — purely on (a) CXMT's new server DRAM architecture announcement and (b) a Korean press column flagging **2029 oversupply**. Beta of 2.39 confirms this is the highest-torque cyclical in large-cap tech.
- **Chinese capacity and 2027–28 supply response.** CXMT already holds ~10% of DRAM revenue (Yole: ~11% of capacity, potentially 13.9% by 2027). Simultaneously all three incumbents are raising capex — Samsung targets +50% HBM capacity by end-2026 — and the industry has never added capacity at a supply deficit without eventually overshooting.
- **Demand destruction in non-AI end markets.** Memory cost inflation is compressing OEM margins and driving a PC shipment contraction; consumer/legacy DRAM buyers are being crowded out. If AI capex growth decelerates while new capacity lands, the correction is amplified by how far margins have stretched.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **Technology/mix leadership where it matters.** HBM is the tightest node in the supply chain, and SK hynix is the incumbent leader with HBM4 ramping as the mainstream product from Q4'26 — the highest-ASP, highest-barrier product in memory. Its 25% DRAM revenue share understates its profit share because HBM commands a premium.
- **Margin superiority through the cycle.** SK hynix exited FY23 at a -23.6% operating margin, i.e. it took the down-cycle hit and still out-earned peers on the way up; at 80% operating margin it is now the most profitable memory maker in the world on a rate basis.
- **Visibility that peers lack.** LTAs on >50% of 2027–28 output materially de-risk the revenue line versus prior cycles, when all volume was spot-indexed.
- **Caveats:** share is ceded to Samsung (38%) and CXMT (10% and rising); net margin (38% in Q3'26) lags operating margin by ~42pts on tax/other charges, so earnings quality is below the headline; and the Kioxia-linked shareholder return (~KRW 100T) is partly a one-off gain, not recurring free cash flow. Beta 2.39 = no defensive characteristics.

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE — late cycle, expiring**

The next 4–6 quarters are supported by contracted volume, HBM4 ramp, and prices still rising sequentially; the multiples (8.8x operating profit at SK hynix, <6x forward at Micron) are not discounting that. But an 80% operating margin in a commodity semiconductor is a **sell signal on a 2–3 year view**, not a buy signal: capacity additions by all three incumbents plus CXMT's ramp point to a 2028–29 oversupply, exactly as the press and the market began pricing in October. **Recommendation to the investment committee: rate the sector Attractive for a 12–18 month horizon only, and treat the first sequential decline in DRAM contract pricing or any LTA renegotiation downward as the trigger to move to Unattractive.** SK hynix is the correct vehicle to express the up-cycle (best mix, best margins, contracted visibility), but position size should reflect that this is the top of a cycle, not the middle.
