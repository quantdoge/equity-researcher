Charter is a **pure-play domestic U.S. operator** — there is no meaningful foreign revenue, no emerging-market exposure, and therefore no direct sanctions, currency-control, or expropriation exposure. Post-merger it operates in ~41–45 states serving ~37–38 million customers (Forbes, 20 Aug 2026; Fierce Network, 2 Mar 2026).

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**Risk 1 — U.S. rate regulation spreads beyond New York (Probability: High | Impact: Medium-High)**
New York's Affordable Broadband Act ($15/mo for 25 Mbps; $20/mo for 200 Mbps) took effect Jan 2025 after the **Supreme Court declined to disturb** the lower-court ruling (Broadband Breakfast; Digital Inclusion, 2026). Charter now markets a "$15/mo" NY tier (Spectrum Internet Assist). This is the single most important precedent in U.S. broadband: it establishes that a state PUC can set retail rates for a cable ISP. The Cox merger approvals **added further price-like conditions** — CPUC required Lifeline service tiers for five years, honoring "price-for-life" contracts, automatic outage bill credits, and free service to schools/libraries/community centers (CPUC Commissioner Baker, 12 Aug 2026, via CAGW). If California, Massachusetts or others adopt ABA-style tariffs, ARPU — Charter's core value driver — is directly at risk.

**Risk 2 — Federal merger conditions have converted the FCC into a de facto operating partner (Probability: Certain | Impact: Medium)**
The FCC approved Charter/Cox on 27 Feb 2026 only after Charter agreed to: (i) **eliminate DEI policies and workforce diversity targets**; (ii) **onshore all Cox offshore job functions within 18 months**; (iii) a minimum starting wage commitment; and (iv) rural buildout (FCC DOC-419093A1; Fierce Network, 2 Mar 2026). State approvals then layered on more: NY PSC (19 Mar 2026) and CPUC (12 Aug 2026, last approval). Consequences: Charter is now exposed to **condition-compliance enforcement** (the historical precedent — a ~$2M penalty for missing buildout goals, per press reports; and NY PSC scrutiny of legacy TWC commitments). It also demonstrates that under Chairman Carr, **non-economic political conditions are the price of FCC approval** — a recurring tax on any future M&A.

**Risk 3 — Subsidy/affordability policy whiplash and spectrum dependency (Probability: Medium-High | Impact: Medium)**
Two structural dependencies: (a) Charter captured roughly **$3B in ACP/EBB subsidies** before the program's funding lapsed in June 2024, and its unwind drove visible subscriber losses (WSJ; Light Reading/Benton, 26 Aug 2025); future subsidy regimes (BEAD "cleanup round", Lifeline) are politically volatile and are now the subject of state-level experimentation. (b) Charter remains **spectrum-light and dependent on the Verizon MVNO** for Spectrum Mobile, making the **Upper C-band auction (starting 27 Apr 2027)** a binary strategic event — a bid outcome that depends on FCC auction rules and on leverage capacity already strained by Cox (S&P has tightened its downgrade threshold to 4.25x debt/EBITDA post-Cox). Tariffs on imported network gear are a secondary but real capex risk (S&P Global Market Intelligence, Apr 2025; Fierce, 2025).

*Also monitored:* the "termination monopoly"/interconnection theory pressed by Public Knowledge, CWA, Benton and the Center for Accessible Technology in their (rejected) petition to deny — the FCC found no evidence of paid interconnection, but the theory is a live template for future Title II-style rules.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Federal subsidy program integrity (highest priority).** Charter was the largest single recipient of ACP/EBB dollars (~$3B) and the FCC's Enforcement Bureau has an open verification inquiry. Combined with post-Cox scale in Lifeline/BEAD-funded buildouts, this creates a structural **False Claims Act / reimbursement-clawback** exposure that is both financial and reputational, and is politically salient in an administration hostile to "waste" in broadband subsidies.
2. **Merger-condition compliance as a political flashpoint.** The DEI-elimination and onshoring commitments were extracted under political pressure (Commissioner Gomez called the DEI rollback "shameful"). Any slippage becomes an enforcement and press story; conversely, the DEI rollback itself invites shareholder and EEOC-adjacent retaliation litigation risk.
3. **FCPA / AML / sanctions: effectively nil.** Domestic-only revenue, U.S.-domiciled counterparties, no foreign agents. The only residual third-party risk is Cox's offshore service vendors until onshoring completes (~Feb 2028), and Asian equipment vendors (export-control/Covered-List risk, not FCPA).
4. **Leverage-driven litigation risk.** Post-Cox debt (S&P threshold 4.25x) plus a $6.3B impairment and a ~46% YTD 2026 share decline is the classic setup for a second securities class action and for covenant-related creditor disputes (the 10 Nov 2026 $1.5B eight-series tender offer shows active liability management).
5. **Data privacy: no red flag, but thin disclosure.** State AG enforcement against ISPs is rising; Charter's ad-tech/Spectrum Reach business (audience data) is the most likely target.

## 5. Overall Rating

**Overall geopolitical/legal risk: MEDIUM-HIGH** — explicitly decomposed:

- **Geopolitical risk (sanctions, expropriation, war, EM, FX): LOW.** Zero foreign revenue; no Russia/China/EM exposure; no currency controls or repatriation risk. This is close to the lowest geopolitical risk profile in the sector.
- **U.S. regulatory risk: HIGH.** Rate regulation precedent (NY + CPUC conditions), a transaction-conditioned FCC, spectrum-auction dependency, politically contingent DEI/labor posture, and volatile subsidy programs.
- **Litigation risk: MEDIUM.** One live securities class action with a pending MTD, an open FCC subsidy-verification probe, and elevated probability of a new class action off the Q3 2026 impairment.
- **Compliance/red-flag risk: MEDIUM.** Concentrated almost entirely in federal broadband subsidy programs.

**Investor takeaway:** the discount here is not geopolitical — it is *political-regulatory and balance-sheet*. Two dated catalysts to diarise: the **Sandoval motion-to-dismiss ruling** (briefed as of mid-2026) and the **Upper C-band auction on 27 Apr 2027**, which will price Charter's spectrum/MVNO dependency. Watch for a second securities case following the Q3 2026 impairment.
