## 1. Macro Environment Summary — Regime: **"Late-cycle re-tightening / bear steepener" (risk-off for long-duration equity)**

As of 15 Nov 2026 the US economy is running **hot enough to stop the easing cycle but not hot enough to be called a boom** — a stagflation-lite configuration that is close to the worst possible macro mix for a levered, secularly-challenged cable equity like Charter.

- **Growth is solid, not slowing:** Q3-26 real GDP $24,611bn (chained 2017$), **+3.3% SAAR QoQ** and **+2.1% YoY** — Q2 +2.2%, Q1 +2.5%. There is no recession signal in activity data.
- **Inflation has re-accelerated:** CPI **+3.82% YoY (Oct-26)**, up from ~3.7% in September, and the Nov-10/13 prints (CPI then PPI) triggered a bond rout on "expectations of higher Fed rates" (CNN, WSJ: yields "surge most in 19 months").
- **The Fed is hiking again, not cutting:** effective fed funds **3.88% (Oct-26)**, up from 3.63% held Feb–Aug 2026. Ex-post **real fed funds ≈ 0%** — policy is effectively *not* restrictive, which is precisely why the long end is repricing.
- **Curve:** 4.74% (2Y) / 4.93% (5Y) / **5.13% (10Y)** / 5.47% (30Y); **2s10s +39bp, bear-steepening**. The 10Y is up **~68bp since early May (4.45%)** and ~35bp in the last six weeks. This is a term-premium/fiscal-risk steepener, not a growth signal — the most damaging shape for long-duration equities.
- **Credit is still wide open, which is the one mercy:** HY OAS **320bp (12 Nov)** — near cycle tights despite the Treasury selloff. So Charter can refinance, just at a much higher coupon. No funding cliff, but no funding relief.
- **Labour market stable:** unemployment **4.1% (Oct-26)** vs 4.4–4.5% a year ago — supports broadband bill-pay, but also keeps the Fed hawkish.
- **Housing frozen:** 30Y mortgage **7.11% (12 Nov)**, up ~65bp since late February (6.00% on 5 Mar 2026). This is a *direct* hit to Charter's gross-add engine (move-related connects).

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Charter is a **domestically-generated, subscription-revenue, ~$100bn-debt business** (Q3-26: pro-forma revenue **−1.5% YoY**, opex-savings target **$2bn annualised**, Cox integration adding ~11m passings/5m customers, net leverage target **3.5x** vs roughly **4.3–4.5x** today). That profile makes it maximally exposed to exactly the variables that are moving the wrong way:

| Macro variable | Impact | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **10Y/30Y yields (5.13%/5.47%)** | **Strongly negative** | Triple hit: (a) discount rate on a long-dated FCF stream; (b) cost of new debt — Charter is tendering for 8 note series worth up to **$1.5bn (10 Nov-26)** and refinancing legacy 3–5% coupons at 6%+; (c) the equity is a thin sliver ($18.7bn market cap) sitting on ~$100bn of debt, so every turn of EV/EBITDA multiple is a 60–90% move in equity value. Cox + Liberty Broadband debt adds incremental interest expense. |
| **Inflation 3.8% / re-hiking Fed** | **Negative** | Programming, labour and network costs inflate faster than ARPU (broadband pricing is politically and competitively capped; video is structurally shrinking). Rate increases drive churn among price-sensitive subs — a live issue given the ACP subsidy ended June-2024 and is now the subject of an investor/legal investigation. |
| **Solid GDP / 4.1% unemployment** | **Neutral-to-slightly positive** | Bill-pay and low churn, but no volume growth: broadband net adds are being lost to fibre overbuild and fixed-wireless, not to the cycle. Good macro is *bad* here because it keeps the Fed hawkish. |
| **Mortgage rates 7.11% / frozen housing** | **Negative** | Move activity is the single biggest driver of cable gross connects. +111bp in eight months suppresses household formation churn — a quiet but real drag on subscriber trends through 2027. |
| **HY OAS 320bp** | **Neutral (mitigant)** | Refinancing is achievable; no liquidity cliff. But spreads are near tights, so all of the pain is showing up in the *base rate*, not the spread — no offset from spread compression. |
| **Tariffs / import costs** | **Mildly negative** | CPE, set-tops and network electronics are imported; capex ~$11–12bn/yr means modest but non-trivial cost inflation on the hardware line. No offsetting pricing power. |
| **USD / FX** | **Neutral** | Essentially no FX translation exposure; no unhedged foreign-currency debt. |
| **Fiscal policy (BEAD subsidies to fibre builders)** | **Negative** | Public capex is subsidising Charter's overbuild competitors — a structural competitive headwind, not a demand tailwind. |

**Market confirmation:** CHTR has fallen from **$249 (20 Apr 2026) to $138.39 (13 Nov 2026), −44%**, and is **−38% YoY** (from $222 in Nov-2025). The drawdown accelerated *after* Q1 results (a −36% two-day gap, 24–29 Apr) and again in Sep–Oct as the 10Y broke 5%. That is the signature of a bond-proxy equity de-rating on rates *plus* an operating miss — not a beta story (reported beta 0.73 understates true rate sensitivity).

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Rationale in one line: **sticky ~3.8% inflation + a re-hiking Fed + a bear-steepening curve to 5.13% on the 10Y + a frozen housing market** is the exact quadrant that compresses multiples on leveraged, long-duration, domestically-competitive-declining cash flows. Charter has no FX hedge need, no commodity leverage and no cyclical upside to offset it. The only macro variable working in its favour — tight HY spreads — merely prevents a crisis; it does not create value.

The inversion of the usual logic is important for the investment memo: **stronger US growth is now a *negative* for CHTR**, because it forces the Fed to stay hawkish and holds the 10Y near 5%+. The equity needs *disinflation and lower long yields*, not a stronger consumer.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (Bull tail)

**"Disinflation shock → long-end rally → violent re-rating."** Consensus (and this report's base case) assumes inflation stays sticky and term premium keeps grinding higher. But shelter disinflation, an AI-driven services-cost deflation impulse, or a modest growth scare could pull CPI from 3.8% back toward 2.5% by mid-2027 and take the **10Y from 5.13% to ~4.25%** without HY spreads widening (they are at 320bp with room to stay tight).

The arithmetic is asymmetric because equity is the residual claim on a levered balance sheet: on ~$115–120bn EV and ~$22–23bn EBITDA (~5.2x), a **0.7–1.0x multiple re-rating** — historically what a 90bp drop in the 10Y delivers for cable — is worth **$16–23bn of enterprise value against an $18.7bn market cap**, i.e. **+85% to +120% equity upside** from $138. Add any evidence that the $2bn opex programme and Cox synergies are landing and the same rate move compounds with estimate revisions. This is the scenario in which CHTR is a double, and it requires only that the *macro* cooperates while the operating story stops deteriorating.
