### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Carbon intensity:** Low relative to utilities/industrials but not trivial — network power draw, data centres, and one of the largest technician fleets in the US drive meaningful Scope 1 (fleet) and Scope 2 (grid) emissions. **No verified Scope 1/2 figures were obtainable**; Charter has been repeatedly criticised by investors (NYSCRF 2019 proposal; Ceres engagement) for *not publicly disclosing energy use or GHG data*.
- **Transition targets:** Effectively absent at scale. Third-party trackers reference a legacy "25% Scope 1&2 reduction by 2025" goal, but no net-zero commitment or SBTi validation is evident. **Every large peer discloses and targets:** AT&T (Scope 1&2 carbon neutrality), Verizon (net zero 2035), T-Mobile (net zero 2040), Comcast (carbon neutral Scope 1&2 by 2039). Charter is the clear disclosure laggard in its own peer set.
- **Stranded/transition assets:** Not a carbon story — but the **$6.33bn franchise impairment booked in Q3 2026** is the clearest signal that legacy video/franchise asset values are being written down. Read this as asset-quality risk, not climate risk.
- **Controversies:** Environment-related penalties are small — $3.4m across 4 records (incl. $57k EPA 2014, hazardous-waste/air violations at acquired Cox/Brightwater entities). No spills, no remediation overhang.
- **Physical risk:** Real and recurring — storm-driven outages across a 45-state footprint, with a **$15m FCC settlement (2024) for 911 and network-outage reporting failures** where Charter *admitted* violating rules.

**Verdict:** Financially immaterial today; becoming a disclosure-compliance cost via California SB 253 as scope expands post-Cox.

---

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

**Workforce (highest-conviction risk).** 35 employment-related penalty records totalling $32.2m, including an $18m federal wage-and-hour class settlement, $3.75m and $3.5m TWC-era settlements, EEOC disability/discrimination settlements, and OFCCP findings. 49 OSHA safety records (small dollar, high frequency) reflect an inherently hazardous field-installation workforce.

**Restructuring.** Charter is executing $2bn+ of incremental annualised opex savings, largely through headcount: ~1,200 corporate/management cuts (Oct 2025), call-centre closures (452 in Ontario, ~1,000+ across sites), and 6,105 employees across 51 WARN notices. Union/NLRB records exist (IBEW/CWA-represented pockets). Integration of Cox (11m passings, 5m customers) multiplies severance, morale, and service-quality risk — and service quality is precisely what regulators penalise.

**Customers / social licence.** This is where Charter's social risk becomes *financial*: $243.5m of consumer-protection penalties, including the **$174.2m NY AG settlement** (2018, over-stated internet speeds), $18.8m California multi-district, $13m NY PSC utility-service penalty, and $995k Washington AG. New class actions allege the ~$28/month "Broadcast TV Surcharge" is falsely presented as government-mandated. The end of the **Affordable Connectivity Program (June 2024)** removed a subsidy that had been supporting subscriber counts — and the resulting securities class action alleges management misled investors about its ability to offset ACP-driven losses. Affordability/price-gouging is now the central political vector against cable, and Charter is a prime target.

**Data privacy.** The May 2026 breach (entry via vishing into a Microsoft Entra account → Salesforce exfiltration) is the most financially consequential single S-factor event: class action filed, HIBP-confirmed 4.9m emails/names/phones/addresses, extortion leak after ransom deadline. Charter's assertion that no CPNI was exfiltrated limits but does not eliminate FCC exposure under the 2023 breach-notification rules. **Estimated direct cost $25–75m** (penalties + settlement + remediation), benchmarked to T-Mobile's $15.75m FCC penalty and ~$31.5m aggregate; plus 1–3 quarters of elevated churn and CISO/security capex.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Positives (genuine):**
- **Chair/CEO separated** — Eric Zinterhofer is Non-Executive Chairman (Lead Independent Director 2016–2023); Winfrey is CEO and only joined the board in Nov 2023. No duality.
- **CEO pay is unusually conservative for scale:** ~$6.5m total FY2025 for a ~$55bn-revenue company, heavily equity-weighted. This is materially below media/telecom peers and is a real alignment positive — the prior CFO retention package ($23m/yr option grants) is the exception, and he is now CEO.
- Majority-independent board; Rutledge transition to Chairman Emeritus was orderly.

**Negatives (score-limiting):**
- **Dual-class control:** Class B carries 50 votes/share, held via Advance/Newhouse (~14.35% economic, contractually capped at 19% stake / 15% voting), layered on the Liberty Broadband/Malone legacy stockholders' agreement. Minority shareholders have essentially no ability to change board composition or strategy. This is the single biggest governance discount.
- **Serial disclosure/accounting failures — a pattern, not an accident:** SEC **$25m penalty (2023) for accounting fraud/deficiencies**; $144m securities class settlement; $87.5m settlement; live ACP-related class action; new KSF investigation (Nov 2026). When regulators repeatedly find that subscriber/operational metrics were misstated, the market applies a disclosure discount to *all* reported KPIs — directly raising cost of capital.
- **Shareholder rights:** Political-spending proposal received only 18% support; separate-chair and sustainability-reporting proposals have been recurring but the company's response has been minimal. Proxy access absent.
- **Capital allocation opacity:** The $34.5bn Cox deal (plus $12.6bn assumed debt) coincided with a $6.33bn franchise impairment within the first quarter of ownership — a heavy governance question about diligence and price discipline.

**5/10:** structurally sound board mechanics undermined by control concentration and a documented pattern of reporting failures.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **FCC:** outage/911 reporting ($15m precedent), 2023 data-breach-notification rules (live exposure from May 2026 incident), net-neutrality/privacy posture, merger-condition compliance. FCC conditioned Charter/Cox on rural builds, jobs, and anti-DEI-discrimination terms — an unusual *ideological* condition set that creates compliance-monitoring risk.
- **State AGs & PUCs (the most active enforcer against CHTR):** NY AG ($174.2m), NY PSC ($13m), California (CPUC imposed digital-equity, affordable-broadband and consumer-protection conditions on Cox; $18.8m prior), Connecticut AG (billing transparency, fee limits), Washington AG, Maine PUC. Franchise renewals are the chokepoint — poor service records have direct renewal value.
- **SEC:** climate-disclosure rule is not operative; but securities-fraud and KPI-disclosure enforcement is very much operative and Charter is a repeat defendant.
- **California SB 253/261:** revenue >$1bn and substantial CA operations → **Scope 1, 2 and 3 disclosure obligation** (with third-party assurance under SB 261 climate-risk reporting). This is the single rule that will force Charter to publish the emissions data it has so far withheld. Enforcement uncertainty is high, but the direction is one-way.
- **EU CSRD / CBAM: not applicable** — no material EU revenue or EU-located operations; supply-chain CBAM pass-through negligible.
- **ESG-linked debt:** Charter's ~$90bn+ debt stack is largely conventional high-yield/investment-grade with leverage covenants; **no ESG-linked margin ratchets identified**, so no direct coupon step-up risk. Note leverage (~3.5x target post-Cox) is the binding constraint, not ESG covenants.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD**

Charter is a controversy-driven laggard, not a carbon laggard. On environment it is a disclosure non-participant while every large peer has a public net-zero pathway. On social it has the worst consumer-protection and privacy record in US telecom, freshly compounded by a 2026 breach. On governance, board mechanics are better than average (independent chair, low CEO pay) but control is concentrated in a 50-vote Class B held by Advance/Newhouse, and management has repeatedly been found to have misstated metrics to investors. Cumulatively $306.7m in regulatory penalties and >$275m in securities settlements.

**Would I call it "ESG Risk"?** No — because the environmental liability is small, there are no stranded carbon assets, no ESG-linked debt triggers, and the governance structure, while shareholder-unfriendly, is functioning and has delivered strong per-share FCF historically. But it sits at the bottom of its peer group on every ESG dimension that third-party raters and large asset owners screen on.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL (with negative tail skew)**

**Not a thesis-changer, but it sells the multiple.** The ESG factors that matter here are the ones that flow straight into unit economics:

1. **Consumer protection and privacy are operating costs, not reputational noise.** $243m of consumer-protection penalties plus a live breach plus fee-litigation over a ~$28/month surcharge line item means ongoing legal reserves, compliance spend, and — critically — state-level leverage over franchise renewals and merger conditions. This is a structural drag on the price/ARPU lever the bull case depends on.
2. **The $2bn cost-savings plan is a social risk in disguise.** Delivering it through 1,200+ corporate cuts and call-centre closures, while absorbing Cox, in an industry whose regulators penalise service quality, is a plausible source of both execution slippage and renewed AG attention.
3. **Governance and disclosure discount is the biggest quantifiable cost.** A company that paid $25m to the SEC for accounting deficiencies and $144m to shareholders, and is now defending an ACP-related claim, will carry a permanent disclosure discount. The $6.33bn franchise impairment one quarter after closing a $34.5bn acquisition reinforces doubts about capital-allocation discipline under a controlled shareholder structure.
4. **Watch item that could turn this negative:** California SB 253 compels Scope 1–3 publication; combined with the breach fallout, expect a step-up in compliance cost and a possible index/ESG-fund exclusion cycle. Conversely, if management appoints a credible sustainability disclosure function and settles the breach litigation cleanly, the controversy overhang is cheap to remove.

**Practical stance:** treat ESG as a **0.5–1.0x turn multiple de-rating factor and a litigation-reserve line item**, not a standalone short thesis. The investment case for CHTR will be decided by broadband subscriber trends and Cox integration; ESG primarily determines whether the terminal multiple is 6x or 7x FCF and how much franchise-renewal friction management faces in 2027–2030.

**Evidence gaps to close before finalising:** (i) verified Scope 1/2/3 emissions and any 2025 target outcome — Charter does not publish these in an accessible form; (ii) the 2026 DEF 14A's board-independence and pay-ratio detail (SEC.gov fetch was blocked); (iii) reserve/disclosure quantification for the May 2026 breach in the next 10-Q; (iv) Cox severance and retained-workforce data.
