## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT** — but not in the way it looks

This is **not a genuine disagreement about facts**. Every agent agrees on the same five data points: (1) broadband units are falling and accelerating; (2) pro-forma revenue −1.5%, EBITDA −4%; (3) leverage ~3.9–4.6x with negative tangible equity; (4) $6.33B impairment; (5) the equity is optically the cheapest large-cap telecom asset in the US.

**The split is entirely mandate-dependent:**

- **Fundamental / risk agents (Sector, Growth, FinRisk, NonFinRisk, Macro, ESG) → negative.** They are grading *business quality and trajectory*. On that axis CHTR is unambiguously bad and getting worse.
- **Price / positioning agents (Value, Quant-value, Alt Data, Short) → constructive.** They are grading *price versus a non-apocalyptic outcome*. On that axis CHTR is unambiguously cheap.

**Two conflicts I am explicitly resolving:**

**(a) Value's $230–240 IV is too high — I haircut it to ~$195–215.** Value's base case needs EBITDA of ~$25.5B in 2028 at a **6.5x exit multiple**. Pro-forma EBITDA is *currently declining 4% YoY* and the deleveraging plan *requires* EBITDA growth. A base case that assumes the inflection it is supposed to be testing is not a base case; it is a bull case. Probability-weighting my own: **bear 30% ($15–40), base 45% (~$200), bull 25% (~$420) → ≈$208**, still **+50%** vs $138. The discount survives the haircut. It does not survive a 30% bear weight *and* a wipeout — which is why size, not the thesis, is the binding constraint.

**(b) Governance/fraud override does NOT trigger.** My framework says fraud or governance findings outrank a value call. Here the fraud case is **explicitly absent** — Short Analyst ran Beneish (−2.65), accruals (−0.072, negative four years running), and cash conversion (3.22x) and found clean accounting. Nonfin's concerns are **execution and disclosure quality, not fabrication**. That converts a veto into a *sizing haircut*. But Nonfin's escalation items 1–3 (CFO vacancy, impairment-vs-underwriting reconciliation, cyber disclosure adequacy) are **unresolved and must be monitored as explicit kill-switches** (see §6).

**Agreement level: SPLIT (high agreement on facts, low agreement on action).** The only true two-sided risk in the file is **timing** — Value wants it now, Growth wants it after two clean quarters. I side with neither: scale in.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **1.0% now, 2.5% maximum**

**Kelly logic, then heavily haircut:**

| Input | Value |
|---|---|
| Expected value | ~$208 (+50%) |
| Win probability | ~70% |
| Avg win / avg loss | ~+150% / −75% → payoff ratio b ≈ 2.0 |
| Full Kelly f = p − q/b | 0.70 − (0.30/2.0) = **0.55 (55%)** |

55% is obviously unusable — Kelly on a fat-tailed, binary, levered-equity outcome with a $0 floor produces nonsense. Apply the standard discounts:

- **÷4 for parameter uncertainty** (share count unknown ±30%, net debt three different figures, four primary filings unretrievable, PT dispersion $19.30–$66.34 on FY27 EPS) → ~14%
- **÷3 for the 59% vol / 6.1x leverage multiplier** — position *risk contribution*, not weight, is the constraint. A 1.5% weight at 4x market volatility contributes ~6% of portfolio risk.

→ **Max position: 2.5% of portfolio. Starter today: 1.0%.**

**Rationale in one line:** the asymmetry is genuinely exceptional (5:1 up/down skew from here), but the bear case is a **permanent capital loss, not a drawdown**, the CFO seat is empty, and the entry has already improved 35% in two weeks. A 1.0% starter buys the optionality at a size where a total wipeout costs 100bp and a double adds 100bp — genuinely symmetric in *portfolio* terms even though it is 5:1 in *security* terms.

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector concentration — CAUTION.** Communication Services is rated **UNATTRACTIVE** by the Sector Researcher. But note the composition: the sector's +6.7% growth is entirely Alphabet/Meta/Netflix. Buying CHTR adds exposure to the **telecom/cable sub-segment growing 0–3%**, which is the *worst* part of an already-unattractive sector. **Hard rule: total telecom/cable sleeve (CHTR + CMCSA + VZ + T + TMUS) must not exceed 8% of portfolio.** If the portfolio is already at that cap, funding must come from VZ or T — and that is a downgrade in credit quality, which is the wrong trade.

**Correlation — the most important number in this report:**

| Pair | 252d correlation |
|---|---|
| **CHTR – CMCSA** | **0.742** ← critical |
| CHTR – VZ | 0.357 |
| CHTR – T | 0.341 |
| CHTR – TMUS | 0.263 |
| CHTR – SPY | **−0.029** |

The Quant Analyst called CHTR "almost pure idiosyncratic risk." **That is wrong for portfolio purposes.** CHTR is 0.74 correlated with Comcast — the highest pair in the entire peer matrix outside the VZ/T pair (0.82). **If this portfolio owns CMCSA, a CHTR position is largely a duplicate cable bet, not a diversifier.** Check CMCSA first. If CMCSA >2%, either fund CHTR out of CMCSA or halve the CHTR starter.

The **−0.03 correlation to SPY is a trap, not a gift.** R² = 0.0009; the beta is statistically meaningless (t ≈ 0.48). Do not count CHTR toward equity-market diversification or as a hedge. Its true factors are **credit spreads, long-duration rates, and broadband net adds**.

**Factor exposure.** This position adds: **deep value (95th pct)**, **severe negative momentum (5th pct)**, **high volatility (3rd pct)**, **average quality (60th)**. Net effect: increases the portfolio's value tilt and its realized volatility. It is *not* a low-vol or defensive add despite the "cable utility" label — realized vol 59% vs 26% for VZ.

**Hidden rate duration — the real portfolio risk.** The Macro Researcher's math is the clearest thing in the file: on ~$126B EV, a 0.7–1.0x multiple re-rating from a 90bp drop in the 10Y is worth **$16–23B against a ~$22B market cap**. **This position is functionally a levered long-duration bond.** If the portfolio is already long duration (long TLT, utilities, REITs, growth equity), buying CHTR doubles an existing bet. **Do not own this alongside a duration overweight.**

**Recommended pairing:** Short Analyst's structure is the professionally correct one for a risk-budget-conscious book — **long VZ/T, short CHTR** captures the relative-decline thesis with no squeeze risk. I am not recommending it as the primary expression because the equity's downside is now largely priced, but it is the right *hedge* if the 2.5% max is reached.
