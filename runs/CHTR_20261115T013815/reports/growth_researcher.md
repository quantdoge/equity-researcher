# Charter Communications (CHTR) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Price $138.39 | Market cap ~$19.1B | EV ~$115.7B | Net debt ~$97B**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Charter is the largest US cable operator after closing the Cox Communications and Liberty Broadband transactions on **August 19, 2026** — but the deal bought scale, not growth. The core franchise is now in **structural decline**: pro forma revenue fell 1.5% in Q3'26, pro forma internet customers fell 233,000 (worse than the 171,000 lost a year earlier), and the company booked a **$6.33B franchise impairment** in Q3'26, a formal admission that the cash flows it paid for are worth less than book. This is not a disruptive-innovator story in the Cathie Wood sense — Charter is the *incumbent being disrupted* by fixed wireless access (T-Mobile/Verizon) and fiber overbuilders. The only remaining bull case is a **capital-allocation/cash-return arbitrage** at 4.3x price-to-free-cash-flow, which sits outside a growth mandate.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

- **US residential broadband is a ~$90B mature market growing ~2–3%/yr**, and Charter's combined footprint passes roughly 70M homes with penetration already near 50%. There is no penetration runway left to exploit — growth must come from ARPU and share, both under attack.
- **The share is moving the wrong way.** Fixed wireless added millions of subscribers at T-Mobile and Verizon, and fiber overbuild (AT&T, Frontier, Brightspeed) is taking share in Charter's densest markets. Low-income customer losses are called out explicitly by the CFO — Charter's value positioning is being undercut by $25–$50 fixed wireless plans.
- **Real incremental TAM is small relative to the base:** mobile (Spectrum Mobile, MVNO economics → potential own-spectrum), enterprise/wholesale (Cox business services), and advertising. Even generous sizing puts converged mobile at $10–15B of addressable revenue against a $55B base — insufficient to offset broadband erosion.
- **Innovation moat: weak.** DOCSIS 4.0 and symmetrical multi-gig are *defensive* catch-up to fiber, not proprietary advantage. No data network effect, no platform dynamic. The $1B+ Cox synergy target is cost-cutting, which compresses the expense base rather than expanding the market.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4/FY2026 results — Jan 29, 2027 (next scheduled event).** First full quarter with Cox consolidated. The single most important print: does pro forma internet subscriber loss exceed 233k?
- **2027 capex guide ≤$11B vs ~$12–13B in 2026.** Management has guided to "significant free cash flow growth," with sell-side models pointing to **$8–9B FCF by 2027–2028**. If credible, this is the one genuine re-rating lever — at a ~$19–22B market cap, $8B FCF is ~2.5x.
- **New operating model execution** (launched with Q3'26) targeting faster connects, better retention, and $1B+ Cox synergies. Watch for headcount and opex proof points.
- **Debt reduction / buyback cadence** post-deal. Buybacks slowed sharply ($2.1B in Q3'25 → $21M in Q3'26) as cash went to the transaction — a resumption at 4x FCF would compound per-share value fast.
- **Wireless convergence ramp** and any own-spectrum network decision — the only credible *growth* vector.

## 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Google Trends, "Spectrum internet" (US):** interest rose sharply into mid-2026 (peak 91 in late May, 87 in mid-August) then faded to 58 by late October. Critically, this demand surge coincided with *accelerating* net subscriber losses (Q1 −120k, Q2 −172k, Q3 −233k). Elevated search interest alongside negative net adds is the signature of **churn and shopping behavior**, not healthy gross-add demand. Read this as **negative**, not positive.
- **Job postings:** ~1,907 Charter listings on Indeed, including ~700 sales-rep postings and new wireless retail consultant roles (Centerville OH, Las Vegas, Phoenix call center). Consistent with a push into converged/wireless retail sales — but retail headcount expansion is a cost, and market-level coverage looks thin (14 jobs in Dayton, 7 in Indianapolis). Neutral-to-slightly-negative.
- **Analyst revision breadth:** unanimously and severely negative across 7+ houses over six months. Downward revision cycles of this magnitude historically take 3–4 quarters to stabilize; there is no evidence of a trough yet.
- **Insider/sentiment:** stock is 43% below its 2025 highs (~$428) and 33% below where it traded in February 2026 (~$239). Falling price + falling estimates = classic value trap until FCF inflects.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **SELL**

Judged as a *growth* investment — which is the mandate here — Charter fails on every test that matters:

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Revenue CAGR 3–5 yr | 2.6%, now **negative** ❌ |
| Rule of 40 | **7.5** (vs 40 threshold) ❌ |
| Gross margin trajectory | Flat at ~46%, no scale leverage ❌ |
| Market penetration runway | None — mature, share-losing ❌ |
| Innovation moat | Defensive catch-up, no proprietary edge ❌ |
| Estimate revisions | Collapsing across the street ❌ |
| Earnings beats | 2 of 7 quarters ❌ |
| Valuation | Genuinely cheap: 5.5x EV/EBITDA, 4.3x P/FCF, 0.93x book ✅ |

The valuation is the only bull argument, and it is a *value* argument, not a growth one. **CHTR is the cheapest name in telecom** — 5.5x EV/EBITDA vs AT&T 5.8x, Verizon 7.5x, T-Mobile 9.9x; 4.3x P/FCF vs AT&T 8.2x, Verizon 10.0x, T-Mobile 12.5x. At a 2.0x EV/revenue multiple, the market is pricing **roughly zero growth in perpetuity** — 2030 consensus revenue of $62.3B implies just ~1.6% CAGR from 2026E, and the market is broadly in line with that. So there is no obvious mispricing on revenue.

The asymmetry rests entirely on capex falling to $11B and FCF doubling to $8–9B. That is a real possibility and a legitimate special-situation thesis — **but it requires an investor with a value/distressed mandate, 4+ year patience, and tolerance for ~4.2x leverage against a declining subscriber base.** Within a disruptive-growth framework, owning the disrupted incumbent with negative organic growth is a category error. I would revisit only on evidence of two consecutive quarters of improving pro forma internet net adds *and* confirmed capex discipline.

## 7. Key Risks

**To the downside (why SELL, not HOLD):**
- **Execution risk is acute.** The new operating model and $1B+ Cox integration land simultaneously, during peak competitive intensity, with a balance sheet at ~4.2x net debt/EBITDA and $5.0B+ annual interest expense. Integration missteps are amplified by leverage.
- **Subscriber loss is accelerating, not stabilizing:** −116k (Q2'25) → −171k (Q3'25) → −120k (Q1'26) → −172k (Q2'26) → −233k (Q3'26). The trend line is the single most important number in the thesis and it is getting worse.
- **A second impairment is plausible.** The $6.33B Q3'26 franchise write-down signals the carrying value of the cable plant is above its economic value; further write-downs would pressure the 0.93x book value support.
- **FCF definition risk.** Q3'26 FCF of $1.00B on my calculation vs $674M company-reported "actual" FCF — definitions differ materially around transition capital. The "$8–9B by 2027–2028" figure excludes transition capital and is management's framing, not GAAP.
- **Dividend: none.** Unlike AT&T (5.0%) and Verizon (6.1%), shareholders are paid only through buybacks — which were nearly halted ($21M) during the deal. No income floor.

**To the upside (what would make me wrong):**
- Capex genuinely drops to ≤$11B and FCF inflects to $8–9B → equity re-rates violently from ~2.5x FCF; at 4.3x P/FCF the bar is exceptionally low.
- Fixed wireless growth saturates as T-Mobile/Verizon capacity fills — a well-documented industry dynamic that could restore cable net adds in 2027–28.
- A converged mobile/wireless story (own spectrum, off-MVNO economics) creates genuine ARPU expansion, and the Cox footprint gives enterprise/wholesale scale that wasn't there before.

**Data gaps:** Post-deal diluted share count is uncertain (Q3'26 weighted 116.9M vs FY2025 137.7M vs a Cox issuance that should raise it) — market cap could be ~$19–22B, which would make P/FCF 4.3–5.0x rather than 4.31x. I could not access the full Q3'26 release or earnings-call transcript (the PR page returned only navigation boilerplate), so the $8–9B 2027–28 FCF target is sourced from secondary reporting rather than primary filings and should be verified against the 10-K.
