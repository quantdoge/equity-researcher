# Charter Communications (CHTR) — Financial Risk Assessment

**Price $138.39 · Market cap ~$18.7B · Debt principal $109B · Net leverage 3.93x · Ratings BB+ / Ba1 · Overall Risk: HIGH**
Data as of 2026-11-15. Latest GAAP balance sheet is FY2025 (pre-Cox); latest period is Q3'26 (post-Cox, impairment-affected). See data gaps.

---

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **Sub-Investment Grade (BB+/Ba1), high-quality HY, deteriorating**

Charter sits **one notch below investment grade** (S&P 'BB+' affirmed Aug-2026; Fitch 'BB+'; Moody's 'Ba1' senior secured). The IG upgrade thesis has stalled: management closed the **Cox Communications acquisition on 19-Aug-2026** (advancing 11M+ passings, 5M+ customer relationships), which levered the balance sheet back up to **3.93x net debt/EBITDA versus a 3.5x target** (~$104B net debt on ~$26.4B annualized pro forma Adjusted EBITDA).

The credit is **not** covenant-fragile — HY indentures of this type are incurrence-based, not maintenance-based, so a bad quarter does not trigger default. The real risk is **structural decline plus refinancing cost**, not a technical breach.

**Service capacity is adequate but thin relative to the balance sheet:**
- Annualized cash interest **$5.8B** against $26.4B PF Adj. EBITDA = **4.55x EBITDA/interest**.
- But EBIT coverage is only **~2.7x** on $15.8B EBIT ($26.4B EBITDA less ~$10.6B PF D&A). FY2025 as-reported coverage was **2.65x** (operating income $13.34B / interest $5.04B), or **2.48x** on EBIT.
- Free cash flow covers interest just **~1.2x** ($7.0B PF FCF / $5.8B interest). That is the tightest of the coverage measures and the one to watch.
- Weighted average cost of debt **5.3%** vs. a **5.13% 10Y Treasury** and a non-inverted curve (2Y 4.74%, 30Y 5.47%). Charter is refinancing at approximately its existing coupon — no rate tailwind, no acute shock.

**The equity cushion is the weakness.** Book equity $20.5B (13% of assets) against $97.1B YE2025 debt, of which **goodwill + intangibles = $97.6B (63% of total assets)** → **tangible book equity is approximately –$77B**. Retained earnings are a **–$5.4B deficit**. Equity is a thin, option-like claim: each 1.0x turn of EV/EBITDA on $26.4B of EBITDA moves equity value by ~$26B — roughly **140% of the entire market cap**.

**Altman Z-Score 0.60 (Distress zone)** — I treat this as directionally, not literally, informative. The model was built for manufacturers and is systematically biased against goodwill-heavy, negative-working-capital cable operators (X1 = –0.053 from working capital, X2 = –0.035 from the retained deficit, X4 = 0.15 on book equity / 0.14 on market cap). Only X3 (0.081) and X5 (0.355) contribute. That said, a 0.60 reading vs. a 2.99 safe-zone threshold correctly captures a **very thin equity buffer**.

**Piotroski F-Score 7/9 (Moderate-to-strong)** is the counterweight: positive ROA, positive operating cash flow, good accruals quality, lower leverage, no dilution, gross margin expansion. Misses on ROA improvement and asset turnover. Financial *direction* of the legacy business is not yet broken — the Cox consolidation clouds the forward read.

**Liquidity:** The 0.39 current ratio and $477M cash balance look alarming but are **business-model artifacts**, not imminent default risk. Cable collects in advance (deferred revenue), holds no inventory, and generates ~$16–20B of operating cash flow annually against a revolving facility. Liquidity is genuinely dependent on the revolver — a $477M cash buffer is roughly **9 days of revenue**. With **$109B principal, an 11.1-year WAL, and FY2026 maturities largely pre-funded** (management exchanged $5.5B of secured notes for cash plus new notes, realizing ~$1.5B of deleveraging, and repaid $1.0B of legacy Cox notes at maturity), there is **no near-term refinancing cliff**.

**FCF sustainability:** FY2025 FCF of $4.42B was the best in four years (2022: $6.10B → 2023: $3.49B → 2024: $3.16B → 2025: $4.42B), as capex peaked and began rolling off. Pro forma capex guidance is **~$13B for FY2026 and ≤$11B for 2027**, plus **$2B of new annualized opex savings** (18-month horizon) on top of **$1B+ of Cox synergies**. That combination — falling capex plus cost-out — is the entire deleveraging engine: roughly **$7B of PF FCF in 2026 rising toward $9–11B in 2027**, i.e., ~7–9% of net debt retired per year. Reaching 3.5x from 3.93x requires ~$11B of net debt reduction: **achievable in roughly 18 months if FCF holds, which is precisely the risk.**

**This is no longer a low-beta defensive cable stock; it trades like a distressed credit.** A 76% three-year drawdown and 48–59% volatility are consistent with a security whose value is dominated by terminal-value and refinancing debate.

The beta figures are **unstable and should not be used for hedging**: –0.12 over one year, 0.53 over three, 0.73 as reported. The negative one-year beta is not a diversifier signal — it reflects idiosyncratic collapse overwhelming market co-movement. **Treat CHTR's true risk factor as credit spreads and broadband subscriber trends, not the equity market.**

Sharpe ratios are **worse than shown**: they assume a 0% risk-free rate, while T-bills yield **4.74%**. On a 4.74% rf, the realized risk-adjusted return is deeply negative. Negative Sharpe in both windows confirms **no compensation for the risk taken** over 1–3 years.

## 4. Downside Scenario: –20% Revenue

Applied to ~$66B pro forma revenue → **$52.8B (–$13.2B)**. Cash costs are ~$39.6B; I assume ~40% variable (programming, mobile device, variable labor) → ~$5.3B of cost relief, plus the $2B opex program.

| Line item | Base (PF) | –20% Revenue | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $66.0B | $52.8B | –20% |
| Adj. EBITDA | $26.4B (40.0%) | **$18.5B (35.0%)** | –30% |
| EBITDA margin | 40.0% | 35.0% | –5.0 pts |
| Cash interest | $5.8B | $5.8B | fixed |
| **EBITDA / interest** | 4.55x | **3.2x** | –30% |
| EBIT / interest | ~2.7x | **~1.36x** | –50% |
| **Net debt / EBITDA** | **3.93x** | **5.6x** | +1.7 turns |
| Capex (discretionary cut) | $13.0B | $9.0B | –31% |
| **FCF** | ~$7.0B | **~$3.2B** (≈$5.2B with full $2B savings) | –54% |
| **FCF / interest** | 1.2x | **0.55x** | –54% |
| Implied equity value @4.5x EV/EBITDA | ~$18B | **negative (~–$21B)** | wiped out |

**Interpretation:** A 20% revenue decline does **not** produce a payment default — EBITDA still covers cash interest 3.2x and FCF stays positive. It **does** produce a severe equity event: leverage goes to 5.6x, EBIT coverage halves to 1.36x, and at any normal cable multiple **equity is functionally wiped out**. Operating leases, capex cuts, and the $2B cost program are the shock absorbers; the $5.8B fixed interest bill is the fulcrum. A 20% revenue decline sustained beyond two years, with capex already cut to maintenance (~$7–8B), would push FCF toward zero and make the 2028+ maturity wall a genuine refinancing problem.

**Covenant sensitivity:** no maintenance covenant to breach; the binding constraint is the **rating and the cost of capital**. At 5.6x net leverage the BB+ would likely cut to BB/B+, pushing new-issue yields to 7–8% vs. a 5.3% average, adding $1.5–2B of annual interest over time.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical, because: FCF is positive and inflecting upward, the maturity ladder is long (11.1-year WAL) with no 2026–27 cliff, EBITDA covers cash interest 4.55x, and the cost-savings program is credible and self-funded. Not Moderate, because: leverage is 3.93x and above target, tangible equity is deeply negative, EBIT coverage is 2.5–2.7x, the equity cushion is only ~17% of debt, the credit is sub-investment-grade, and the market has repriced the equity –76% with negative risk-adjusted returns. **The equity is a leveraged call option on broadband stabilization.**

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **$6.3B non-cash franchise impairment (Q3'26)** — a formal admission that acquired/owned franchise value is impaired; the largest single signal in the file. Pro forma net loss $2.1B vs. $1.0B net income in 3Q25.
2. **Broadband subscriber erosion** — pro forma Internet customers –233,000 in one quarter (legacy Charter –156,000 vs. –109,000 a year earlier); residential revenue –4.1% YoY; pro forma revenue –1.5% and EBITDA –4.0% YoY. Deleveraging requires EBITDA *growth*, which is currently going the wrong way.
3. **Leverage re-levered by the Cox transaction to 3.93x vs. a 3.5x target**, with the IG upgrade path deferred up to three years.
4. **Tangible equity of ~–$77B** and a retained-earnings deficit — no book-value floor under the equity.
5. **$477M cash / 0.39 current ratio / –$8.2B working capital** — no standalone liquidity buffer; revolver-dependent.
6. **FCF/interest of only ~1.2x** — the thinnest coverage metric, and the one most sensitive to a downturn.
7. **23% FCF yield at BB+** — the market is pricing terminal decline in the broadband business, not merely leverage. A high FCF yield on a sub-IG credit is a value trap signature until it is converted into actual debt paydown.
8. **Buyback capital misallocation** — $10.3B repurchased in 2022 at multiples far above today's price; buybacks are now appropriately redirected to debt reduction.
9. **Negative 1-year beta** — unstable risk exposure; unusable for portfolio hedging.
