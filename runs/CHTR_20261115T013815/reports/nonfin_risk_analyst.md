## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**Active / pending matters (no SEC or DOJ enforcement action surfaced in EDGAR full-text search — a meaningful negative finding, but thin comfort):**

- **Securities class action — ACP (active, consolidated).** *Sandoval v. Charter Communications* (and related Kessler Topaz / Shamis & Gentile / Edelsberg filings). Class period **26-Jul-2024 to 24-Jul-2025**. Core allegation: Charter falsely stated it had "managed the end of the ACP successfully" when subsidized low-income subscriber losses were material. Lead-plaintiff deadline passed Oct-2025; case litigating.
- **Fresh investigation — Kahn Swick & Foti (11-Nov-2026).** Newly opened investigation into officers/directors on the same ACP subsidy conduct. Suggests onward plaintiff-bar momentum, not resolution.
- **Liberty Broadband merger claims (multiple, Oct–Nov 2026).** Girard Sharp, Levin Law, and others investigating claims of former Liberty Broadband holders who received CHTR shares on 20-Aug-2026. Underlying theory (per prior derivative litigation naming **Greg Maffei and John Malone**): Liberty allegedly bought Charter shares at an unfairly low price and obtained disproportionate control via proxy. Post-merger CHTR decline is the damages hook.
- **Data-breach litigation (filed 1-Jun-2026).** Class action over the May-2026 breach; CCPA/CPRA statutory damages exposure plus **state AG notification** obligations (CA AG and others). Separate California call-recording class action also pending.
- **FCC posture — permissive but unstable.** The FCC approved **Charter/Cox without behavioral conditions** on interconnection or data caps (unlike the 2016 Time Warner Cable conditions, which expired May-2023). Charter is therefore free to impose data caps — a live policy option that would invite immediate political and state-regulatory attack. Advocacy groups pressed California for affordability conditions; state franchise authorities retain leverage.
- **ACP clawback / False Claims Act tail risk (unquantified).** Federal subsidy program terminated June-2024. If subscriber-eligibility certifications are challenged, exposure is open-ended and not reserved.
- **Forward M&A regulatory risk.** Press speculation about a **Comcast combination** would trigger a far harder antitrust review than Cox did.

**Assessment: Moderate-to-High.** Litigation is multi-front and overlapping (securities + merger + privacy), with no evidence of a near-term clearing event.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Simultaneous mega-integration while the core business shrinks.** Cox ($34.5B; 11M passings, 5M customers, 45 states) closed 19-Aug-2026, alongside Liberty Broadband. In the same quarter, pro forma Internet customers fell **233K** (156K legacy Charter, 77K legacy Cox) and pro forma revenue fell **1.5%**. Management is asking the market to underwrite **$3B+ of combined cost cuts** ($2B new opex + $1B+ Cox synergies) *and* a broadband turnaround, executed by an **interim CFO** and a **COO nine weeks into the job**. Cost-cutting and service-quality recovery are in direct tension — and the plan itself concedes the need to fix "service reputation."

2. **Secular fixed-broadband substitution — not a management problem, a structural one.** Losses are being driven by **5G fixed-wireless from T-Mobile/Verizon** and fiber overbuild. Charter's response (mobile bundling: +508K pro forma lines, 13.5M total) is convergent but lower-margin. This is the single largest destroyer of the equity story and is outside management's control. A **$6.33B franchise impairment** is the accounting acknowledgment that the legacy cable cash-flow arc is impaired.

3. **Cybersecurity — a realized, materialized incident with unresolved scope.** **May-2026 breach** by **ShinyHunters** via **voice-phishing (vishing) social engineering** of an employee, leading to compromise of internal systems and connected cloud platforms (**Salesforce**), with bulk customer-record exfiltration. Claimed volume ranges **4.9M to 42M records** — the discrepancy itself is a disclosure-quality problem. Insurer advisories flag elevated **follow-on attack risk**. For a 34.3M-Internet-customer, 91,900-employee critical-infrastructure operator, this is a control-failure finding, not bad luck: the entry vector (human/social engineering) is the most preventable category.

**Secondary:** labor/continuity — ~1,200 roles cut Oct-2025, ~1,200 more Jul-2026, call-center closures (Portland: 176 jobs); key-person concentration on Winfrey; supplier concentration in network gear/set-top boxes.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Worst-in-class service reputation is now a stated strategic problem.** Management explicitly ties the turnaround to "improving its service reputation" — an admission. Cable/ISP net-promoter and ACSI scores have been structurally poor for a decade.
- **Data breach** — brand-damaging, national, with the ShinyHunters name attached and leak-site exposure. Insurance and enterprise/B2B sales cycles will feel it.
- **Politically charged low-income-subsidy controversy.** Having taken ACP subsidies for low-income households and then been sued for allegedly misrepresenting the impact of the program's end, Charter is exposed to a **"profited from the poor, then abandoned them"** narrative — high ESG-social salience, and one that state regulators and legislators can weaponize.
- **Data-cap and affordability flashpoint.** With FCC behavioral conditions absent, any move toward caps or usage pricing would generate immediate national backlash (cf. 2016-era coverage of Charter's data-cap lobbying).
- **Precedent of large consumer-protection penalties.** NY AG's **$174M** consumer-fraud settlement over misleading internet speeds establishes that regulators will pursue Charter on marketing claims.
- **"Broken growth story" narrative risk.** Three separate >20% single-day drawdowns in 24 months, a $6.3B write-down, and a 66% peak-to-trough decline have made CHTR a value-trap case study — compounding recruiting, counterparty, and financing friction.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

The risk profile is dominated by **governance and execution**, not litigation quantum. Charter has simultaneously (a) closed two related-party-tinged transactions at the top of the cycle, (b) written down $6.3B of franchise value, (c) lost its CFO, (d) suffered a material cyber breach, and (e) been named in overlapping securities and merger suits — while its core broadband franchise is structurally eroding. Each is survivable; the **correlation** among them, executed by an interim finance chief and a newly-installed COO, is what justifies High rather than Moderate. Not Critical: the asset base is real and cash-generative, the FCC was permissive on Cox, no SEC/DOJ enforcement action is on record, and the reported insider selling is largely mechanical post-merger unwinding.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **CFO departed 11 days after closing the largest acquisition in company history; post is still interim (15-Oct-2026 → present).** Demand the 8-K/10-Q basis for departure and the interim-CFO mandate. Inability to name a permanent CFO within ~6 months should be treated as a control-environment failure. **Escalate to IC.**
2. **$6.33B franchise impairment in the first quarter post-close.** Reconcile the impairment trigger to the 3.5x leverage / $3B synergy underwriting presented at announcement. If deal-model assumptions differ materially from impairment-model assumptions, the synergy case is not investable as stated. **Escalate to IC.**
3. **Adequacy of cyber disclosure.** Claimed record counts span 4.9M–42M; confirm what was disclosed to regulators and when, and whether any 8-K Item 1.05 was triggered and skipped. Understatement here is both an SEC risk and a securities-litigation accelerant.
4. **Earnings quality.** Q3 "beat" driven by a debt gain alongside a $6.3B impairment — request a clean operating-bridge and reconciliation of non-GAAP adjustments.
5. **Litigation map is expanding, not consolidating** (ACP class action → KSF investigation Nov-2026; Liberty merger investigations Oct–Nov 2026; breach class action Jun-2026). Confirm whether D&O coverage and reserves are adequate, and whether any demand-letter/derivative action has been served on the new board.
6. **No insider open-market purchases in six months through a 40% additional drawdown**, and Rutledge's ~97% liquidation of his direct stake. Neither is dispositive; together they weaken the alignment thesis.
7. **Board independence after the Cox close** — affiliated parties (Cox chairmanship, Advance/Newhouse two seats + standstill, Cox as a >10% holder) should be mapped against majority-of-independent-director thresholds for related-party transaction approval.
