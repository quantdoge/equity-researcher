### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Hawkish Reflation / "Transitional" (risk-on in growth, risk-off in duration)**

The US is in a late-cycle re-acceleration, not a slowdown. Real GDP grew **+3.4% annualized in Q3 2026** (the strongest quarter since 2023), unemployment has fallen to **4.2%** from 4.5% in Nov 2025, and inflation has re-accelerated: **CPI +3.7% y/y in September 2026**, up from a ~2.4% trough in Jan–Feb 2026, and running at **~4.6% annualized over the last three months**.

The Fed has therefore reversed course. After cutting from 4.33% to 3.63% through 2025 and H1 2026, the FOMC **hiked 25bp on 16 September 2026 to 3.75–4.00%** (effective rate 3.88% in October). 16 of 18 participants signalled further tightening, and sell-side (J.P. Morgan) now expects a **December 2026 hike**; the Fed has "yet to lay out a roadmap for tackling inflation."

Crucially, the long end is doing the tightening, not just the Fed: the **10Y yield has risen ~65bp since 1 July (4.48% → 5.13%)**, peaking at 5.31% on 5 October, with the **30Y at 5.47%**. The 2s10s curve is **+39bp and bear-steepening** — no recession signal, but a rising term premium that is precisely the mechanism that de-rates long-duration equities.

Credit is the one unambiguously calm input: **HY OAS at 3.40%** (3 Nov), though it has widened 75bp from the 2.65% cycle low on 11 September. This is a modest risk-off in credit quality, not stress.

**Market implication:** the 10-year at 5.13% is a direct tax on the present value of 2029-and-beyond ARR. DT's -28% drawdown from its 12 Oct high of $65.25 to $46.78 has already partly paid that tax — but the shock on 3 Nov was company-specific (Arize acquisition diluting FY27 EPS, large expansion deals slipping to H2), layered on top of a hostile rate tape.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The growth and demand backdrop is genuinely supportive (3.4% GDP, 4.2% unemployment, tight credit, AI capex). DT is nonetheless rated unfavourable because the **two macro variables with the highest beta to this specific business model both point against it**: a 10Y at 5.13% with a rising term premium (discount rate) and a dollar appreciating 3% in eight weeks (translation). A company with no refinancing risk and 16% ARR growth does not get hurt by macro through its cash flows — it gets hurt through its multiple, and that channel is currently the most hostile it has been in this cycle.

The offset is valuation: after a 28% drawdown, DT trades at roughly 6x ARR with net cash, versus the 8–9x it commanded in 2025. Much of the rate pain is priced.

### 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **Bear: "The 6% 10-Year"**

Base case assumes the December hike is the last and the 10Y drifts to 4.75–5.00%. The live risk is a **term-premium shock**: sticky 4.5%+ core inflation plus $2T+ of Treasury supply pushes the 10Y through the 5.31% October peak to **5.75–6.00%** by Q1 2027. In that world:
- Long-duration software de-rates another **20–25%** irrespective of earnings; DT's multiple compresses toward 4.5x ARR (~$33–36).
- The expansion deals DT pushed into H2 FY27 do not close — slippage becomes discounting, and net-new ARR decelerates from 16% toward 11–12%.
- The USD rallies a further 4–5%, taking another 2pts off reported growth.
- The $246M/quarter buyback becomes a poor use of cash and a governance question.

**Mirror bull case (equally plausible, lower probability near term):** inflation rolls over hard in Q1 2027 (CPI back under 2.5%) on the lagged effect of the 2025 cuts, the Fed abandons the December hike, the 10Y falls to 4.25%, and DT re-rates 25–30% *while* the deferred deals land. The asymmetry is that DT's own guidance already embeds the deal slippage — so the surprise in the bull case is purely macro, and macro is what has been driving the shares.

**What to monitor:** the 10Y (5.31% level), the December FOMC, monthly CPI prints, HY OAS above 4.00%, and the DXY at 121.7.
