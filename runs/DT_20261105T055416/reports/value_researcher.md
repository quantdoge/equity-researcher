# Dynatrace (NYSE: DT) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $46.78 | Market cap ≈ $13.5B (288M basic shares) | Net debt ≈ $0.3B post-Arize → EV ≈ $13.8B | FY ends March 31*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW**

**What it is:** An AI-assisted observability platform (application performance monitoring, infrastructure, logs, digital experience, application security, cloud automation) sold almost entirely as subscription/consumption to large enterprises. FY26 revenue $2.02B; Q2 FY27 total ARR $2.21B (+16% y/y).

**Evidence for the moat:**
- **Gross margin 81.6%** (FY26), stable in a tight 80.8–81.6% band for five years — pricing power intact. Non-GAAP operating margin 32% in Q2 FY27.
- **High switching costs:** OneAgent auto-instrumentation and the Grail data lake become embedded in production toolchains; average ARR per customer now **>$500K** and 14 deals >$1M ACV in a single quarter (incl. one nine-figure total contract value). Ripping this out is an operational project, not a purchase decision.
- **Intangibles/efficient scale:** Named a Leader in the Forrester Wave AIOps Q4 2026; FedRAMP High "Initial Implementation"; CrowdStrike partnership. Serving the largest, most complex multi-cloud estates is a segment where a small number of vendors can credibly compete.
- **Consumption growing >20% y/y**; log management >100% growth; infrastructure consumption >$500M annualized, +30%.

**Evidence the moat is *not* widening:**
- Revenue growth has decelerated every year: **32% (FY22) → 25% → 23% → 18.8% → 18.8% → guide ~16% cc for FY27**. ARR growth is now 16%, and FY27's 17.5–18.5% cc ARR guide depends on ~200bps of *acquired* (Arize) accretion.
- The Nov 3, 2026 sell-off (−13% to −15%) was triggered by **flat net-new ARR** — expansion is carrying growth while new-logo/addition velocity stalls. That is the single most important moat datapoint in this file.
- Datadog is growing ~26% and is the share-gainer in cloud-native observability; hyperscalers (CloudWatch, Azure Monitor, Google Cloud Ops) bundle "good enough" telemetry at zero incremental cost.

**Verdict:** durable, high-margin, sticky franchise — but contested on the growth frontier, in a market where the largest competitor is outgrowing it 1.6x. **Narrow moat, stable to slightly narrowing.**

| GAAP op margin | 8.0% | 9.0% | 10.6% | 13.1% | 14.6% |

| FCF ($M) | 333 | 352 | 433 | 527 | **577** |

- **Cash generation is excellent and improving:** FCF margin ~27% of revenue, FCF has compounded ~23% over three years. FCF/net income is >250% — flattered by heavy stock comp (see below), but even SBC-adjusted owner earnings are ~$520M.
- **Balance sheet:** solid. At 9/30/26: cash + investments **$2.13B**, total debt **$1.56B** (a $1.4B long-term facility raised in Q2). Arize ($915M; ~$815M cash) closed October 1, so pro forma cash ≈ $1.3B against $1.56B debt → **roughly net-neutral, ~$0.25–0.5B net debt**. No maturities risk, no dividend, negative net debt for most of its listed history.
- **The markdowns:** (a) **SBC is ~13–14% of revenue** (~$300M/yr) and is the main gap between 32% non-GAAP and 15% GAAP margins; (b) FY25 net income ($484M) exceeded EBIT ($179M) because of a one-time tax valuation-allowance release — do not annualize that ROE; (c) revenue is consumption-linked, which adds cyclicality to the "subscription" label.
- **Returns on capital:** reported ROIC 9.5% (FY26) versus ~9–10% WACC — *not* a moat-level number. But that is an artifact of $1.37B of goodwill/intangibles against a small tangible capital base; **ROIC excluding goodwill/intangibles is roughly 20%+**. The honest statement: the *operating* business earns high returns; the *capital allocation* history (M&A) dilutes consolidated ROIC toward the cost of capital.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Multiples today:** 86.6x GAAP P/E (economically meaningless given SBC), **~24x EV/TTM FCF** (4.3% FCF yield), ~26x on SBC-adjusted owner earnings, ~5.9x EV/sales, ~13x EV/non-GAAP EBITDA. Peer median P/E is ~39x, a 120% GAAP premium — but that comparison mixes non-GAAP peers with DT's GAAP number, so treat it as directional only.

**Owner-earnings DCF** (base $520M SBC-adjusted, net debt $0.3B, WACC 9%, terminal 3%):

| Scenario | FCF growth yrs 1–5 / 6–10 / terminal | Value/share |
|---|---|---|
| Bear (flat net-new ARR persists, Datadog pressure) | 8% / 5% / 2.5% | **$38** |
| Base (mid-teens revenue, modest margin expansion, Arize neutral) | 13% / 8% / 3.0% | **$55** |
| Bull (Arize opens AI-observability TAM, Rule of 50 by FY29) | 16% / 10% / 3.5% | **$71** |

**Intrinsic value ≈ $55 (range $45–$64; probability-weighted 25/50/25).** Current price $46.78 = **~15% discount to base case**, at the bottom of the range.

**What the price implies:** ~4.3% FCF yield growing ~10–11% for a decade — i.e., the market is pricing DT as a low-double-digit compounder, roughly consistent with flat net-new ARR and mid-teens total growth. The shares are down ~28% from the ~$65 October 2026 high and are *below* where they traded in September 2024 ($49.71) even though revenue is up ~45% and FCF up ~65%. That is multiple compression, not deterioration — which is where future returns come from.

**But:** 24x FCF for a 16%-and-decelerating business with a leveraged, unproven acquisition is a *fair* price, not a *cheap* one. My discipline requires a 25%+ discount; that means **≤ $41**.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **7 / 10 (Good)**

- **Operator credibility:** CEO Rick McConnell since 2019 has taken the company from ~$600M to >$2.3B guided FY27 revenue with expanding non-GAAP margins; CFO Jim Benson. Guidance is deliberately conservative (DT has beaten consensus EPS in each of the last eight quarters). Forthcoming Investor Day (Feb 16, 2027) with a stated **"Rule of 50" target for FY2029** and a capital-allocation framework — a good sign of disclosure discipline.
- **Shareholder alignment in action:** no dividend, but aggressive and *well-priced* buybacks — $479M in FY26, ~$522M in H1 FY27, 5.2M shares repurchased at an average $47.38 in Q2 (below my $55 base intrinsic value: value-accretive), plus a new $1B authorization ($1.3B total capacity). Basic shares are down from ~300M to **288M**, so net dilution is *negative* ~4%/yr — SBC is being more than neutralized. That is genuinely owner-minded.
- **The blemish — capital allocation with leverage:** simultaneously borrowing $1.4B, spending $915M on Arize, and authorizing $1B of buybacks is the least Buffett-like thing in this file. Either the stock is undervalued (buy it back) or the acquisition is strategically essential (pay cash) — doing both on debt signals a less disciplined framework, and Arize's 175bps of FY27 margin dilution plus an undisclosed revenue base means the purchase multiple is likely rich. Prior M&A has been tuck-in sized; this is the first real test.
- **Gap:** I could not verify current insider ownership or recent insider buying in this budget; the company is PE-spawned (Thoma Bravo, 2014 take-private, 2019 IPO) rather than founder-led, which typically means modest insider stakes. Treat as an open item.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** *(accumulate ≤ $41; trim/reassess > $65)*

This is a high-quality, high-margin, cash-generative narrow-moat compounder with a strong balance sheet, a serious buyback, and a reasonable—not compelling—price. It fails my margin-of-safety test by roughly 10 points of price: I want 25% off $55 ($41), and I am offered 15%.

**Where I could be wrong (and would upgrade to Buy):** if Q3/Q4 FY27 show net-new ARR re-accelerating (Arize's ~200bps accretion plus AI-agent instrumentation converting into gross adds), base case moves toward $65–70 and today's price is a 30%+ discount. Watch the Feb 16, 2027 Investor Day and the next two ARR prints — that is the whole thesis.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Growth stall becomes structural.** Flat net-new ARR is the leading indicator; if total ARR growth slips to 12–13% organically, the bear case ($38) is in play and the multiple compresses further. Datadog outgrowing DT ~26% vs 16% is the competitive evidence.
2. **Consumption-model cyclicality.** ~all revenue is consumption-linked; enterprise optimization or an IT-budget slowdown hits revenue faster than a seat-based model, and the same dynamic cuts FCF (working capital swings are already violent: +$303M FCF in Q1 FY27, +$36M in Q2).
3. **Arize integration and price paid.** $915M for a pre-scale AI-evaluation vendor, 175bps of margin dilution, and an undisclosed revenue contribution. If AI observability commoditizes (model vendors ship native evals), DT has bought a depreciating asset at a high multiple.
4. **New leverage.** $1.56B gross debt introduced into a business that was net-cash-rich; combined with a $1.3B buyback authorization, financial flexibility is now lower than at any point since IPO.
5. **Stock comp of ~13.5% of revenue** — high absolute dilution pressure; buybacks currently mask it, but a lower share price raises the cash cost of neutralizing it.
6. **AI disruption of the observability layer itself.** Telemetry volumes are exploding, but agentic AI could shift value to the model layer or to hyperscaler-native tooling; DT's "AI-powered" positioning is credible but unproven as a pricing lever.
7. **Valuation risk at 24x FCF:** a 9% WACC and 3% terminal growth carry most of the value in the terminal period; a 100bp higher discount rate alone takes roughly $6–8/share off intrinsic value.

**Bottom line:** own it, don't chase it. A great business at a fair price is a hold; a great business at a 25% discount is a buy. DT is 10 points of price away from being the latter.
