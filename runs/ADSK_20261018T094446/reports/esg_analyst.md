### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- Business model is asset-light software with essentially no Scope 1 process emissions; Scope 1+2 are in the low tens of thousands of tCO2e. Revenue intensity is a fraction of industrial/utility levels (our estimate <15 tCO2e per $1M revenue; precise FY26 figures were not retrievable — **gap flagged**).
- **Mitigants in place:** SBTi-validated net-zero target for 2050 (validated 2024); 100% renewable electricity matched for cloud services and data centres; CDP Climate score **B**; ranked #38 on Forbes Net Zero Leaders 2025. This is genuinely above-median for the sector.
- **Watch items:** (a) Scope 3 is >90% of the footprint and *structurally rising* as the desktop-to-cloud and generative-AI migration increases compute demand — Scope 3 reduction is therefore partly outside management control (AWS-dependent); (b) net-zero by **2050** is a long-dated target versus peers targeting 2030–2040 for operational emissions, leaving a credibility gap if AI workloads scale; (c) the largest E exposure is **indirect**: Autodesk sells design tools into construction, oil & gas, mining and manufacturing, so its customers' embodied-carbon and CBAM costs eventually feed into seat counts and renewals. Autodesk manages this as an *opportunity* (embodied-carbon analysis in BIM, sustainable-design tooling), which is the correct strategic framing.
- No environmental fines, spills, remediation liabilities or Superfund exposure identified in filings. No environmental litigation.

**Verdict: Low.** Not a cash-flow or cost-of-capital driver.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Talent (elevated, most material S issue):**
- February 2025 restructuring eliminated **1,350 roles (~9% of workforce)** to redirect spend toward AI and cloud, following earlier rounds (2023 and prior). Restructuring charges are recurring, not one-off — a pattern that erodes the "one-time" framing and signals repeated strategy resets.
- Consequence: engineering-talent attrition in a market where AI-native design competitors are hiring aggressively. For a company whose assets are code and engineers, this is a genuine, quantifiable risk to R&D output and therefore to the durability of the 20%+ FCF growth narrative.

**Customer trust / product practices (elevated and under-appreciated by ESG raters):**
- Sustained user backlash over licence-compliance audit letters, subscription price increases and forced migration to named-user licensing. This is a *renewal-rate and net-retention* risk in a subscription model, i.e., directly revenue-material. It is a "social licence with customers" issue rather than a labour issue, and classical ESG frameworks miss it. We cannot quantify churn attributable to it from public data (**gap**), but it is the single most cash-flow-relevant S item.

**Data privacy, IP and AI ethics (medium, rising):**
- Autodesk hosts customers' proprietary design IP in the cloud and is embedding generative AI into its products. The material risk is **training-data consent and IP leakage** — a contract-liability and trust issue, now with EU AI Act transparency obligations layered on. No material breach disclosed, but this is the sector's largest latent S tail.

**Other:** DEI programme exists (published representation data historically) but US-listed peers are broadly de-emphasising 2025–26 targets — treat as neutral, not a thesis input. Community/philanthropy (Autodesk Foundation, in-kind software donation) has negligible financial materiality. No supply-chain labour exposure (no manufacturing), though the ~14,300 headcount sits behind a global reseller channel with associated third-party conduct risk.

**Verdict: Medium.** Not a headline risk, but the restructuring/talent equation and customer-trust erosion are real, recurring drags.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10** (Laggard, with a credible repair trajectory)

**Why not lower:** the board responded to activist pressure and completed a genuine refresh.
**Why not higher:** the underlying integrity event was serious and governance mechanics still lag best practice.

| Issue | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **2024 FCF/non-GAAP investigation** | Audit Committee investigation into free-cash-flow and non-GAAP operating-margin practices, prompted by an April 2024 disclosure that delayed the annual report. Result: **no restatement**, but management effectively conceded that charges were timed to hit FCF targets. For a company valued on FCF, this is a first-order governance failure — it goes to the reliability of the metric the entire equity story rests on. |
| **CFO turnover / interim arrangement** | CFO Debbie Clifford departed May 2024; **a sitting board member (Elizabeth Rafael) was installed as interim CFO** — a conflict-adjacent arrangement that weakens board oversight of management. A permanent CFO (Janesh Moorjani) has since been appointed, which normalises the situation. |
| **Litigation overhang** | Securities class action *24-cv-02431* (N.D. Cal., class period Feb 2023–Apr 2024) alleging inadequate internal controls over FCF/non-GAAP practices. **Motion to dismiss granted 26 Jan 2026**; lead plaintiff appealed to the Ninth Circuit 12 Mar 2026 — **still live**. Dismissal-with-prejudice reduces expected loss materially but the appeal keeps a residual tail. |
| **Board independence** | 11 nominees at the 17 Jun 2026 AGM; **10 of 11 independent**; CEO Anagnost is the only insider (9.3 yrs on board). Independence ratio is fine; **refreshment was the problem**. |
| **Tenure / entrenchment** | Stacy Smith **15.8 years**; Blasing 8.6; Irving 7.6; Howard 7.1 — a long-tenured core, which is precisely what Starboard attacked. Partial fix: Cahill (1.8), Krishnan (1.8), Abbosh (0.3), Epstein (1.3), Simons (new) now provide meaningful new blood. Third-party board grade: **C+**. |
| **Activism** | Starboard Value public campaign (Feb 2025) on underperformance/margin; **cooperation agreement 23 Apr 2025** adding **Jeff Epstein and Christie Simons** to the board, with other demands (cost discipline, margin targets) largely conceded. Shareholders won here — but only via external pressure, which is itself a governance signal. |
| **Shareholder rights** | **Special-meeting right set at 25%** of outstanding shares; management recommending **against** a 10% threshold proposal (Chevedden) at the 2026 AGM. 25% is a high bar and constrains the exact remedy shareholders just had to use. |
| **Officer exculpation** | 2026 charter amendment to add officer exculpation under DGCL 102(b)(7). Now standard practice, and framed as prospective — but it lands two years after an internal-controls scandal and **reduces personal accountability for the officers involved in disclosure**. Mild negative. |
| **Compensation alignment** | CEO Anagnost FY2026 pay **$30.1M, +19.4% YoY** — roughly the 47th percentile of disclosed peers — while **1-year TSR was –28.1%** (to mid-2026). Say-on-pay support sat in the mid-80s% at the 2025 meeting (aggregated fund votes), i.e., material dissent, and FCF/margin metrics now carry more weight in the plan post-activism. Pay-for-performance optics remain weak. |

**No dual-class supervoting structure identified** in the current capital structure (single class of common stock; one-share-one-vote). No poison pill disclosed. Audit firm: EY (long tenure — routine rotation question only).

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (Governance = **Laggard**; Environmental = **Leader**; Social = **Average**)

Autodesk presents an unusually divergent profile for a mega-cap software name: genuinely strong environmental credentials that are almost *financially irrelevant*, and a governance record that is financially *highly relevant* and only now being repaired. The company's third-party coverage (MSCI/Sustainalytics/LSEG all in coverage) will likely reward the E and the S narrative; the equity risk sits in the G.

The honest read: **ESG raters will over-rate this company.** Cluster-risk scores are flattered by an asset-light model, while the two items that actually move cash flows — accounting credibility on FCF and customer-trust erosion in the subscription base — sit outside or at the margins of standard ESG frameworks.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (with a negative tilt on governance, offset by a positive repair trajectory)

For the investment thesis:

- **Not a disqualifier.** There is no stranded-asset, litigation, or regulatory exposure that should move a DCF. Environmental and product-level ESG is a modest brand asset and a legitimate selling point into the AEC/manufacturing installed base.
- **Governance is a genuine discount-rate input, not a checkbox.** Two facts should be carried into the valuation work: (i) FCF is the metric that was subject to an internal investigation, and every FCF-based valuation therefore carries a small credibility haircut until a full cycle is delivered under the new CFO; (ii) the Ninth Circuit appeal keeps a low-probability securities tail alive into 2027.
- **Monitor, don't underwrite, the S issues.** The 25%+ special-meeting threshold, the over-tenured directors and the officer-exculpation amendment mean accountability still depends on activists rather than mechanics — a repeat campaign is plausible if execution slips. Conversely, the Starboard settlement, the board refresh and the new CFO are real, verifiable improvements; if margin and FCF targets are delivered cleanly through FY2027, the governance discount should compress and this rating moves to **Positive**.
- **The under-priced risk is customer trust.** Licence-audit aggression plus price increases is the mechanism by which a "social" issue becomes a revenue issue. Track net revenue retention and disclosed churn commentary each quarter as the primary ESG-linked KPI — ahead of any carbon metric.

**Bottom line:** ESG is not the reason to own or avoid ADSK. It is a **watch-list overlay on governance credibility and renewal quality**, and it argues for requiring a slightly higher margin of safety than the peer multiple alone would imply.
