# Autodesk, Inc. (ADSK) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price context:** Last observed trades ~$209–210 (late Sept 2026); the data profile carries a stale $239.90. Stock is −36% from its 52-week high of $325 and −19% YTD. All per-share work below is run at **~$210** with the $240 case shown as sensitivity. Shares out: 211M diluted.

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **WIDE**

Autodesk sells the design language of the physical world. AutoCAD (.dwg) and Revit (.rvt) are de facto file standards in architecture, engineering, construction and manufacturing; the trained labor pool, the reseller/partner ecosystem, and decades of project archives all sit on top of them.

**Evidence the moat is real:**
- **Pricing power without volume loss:** Revenue compounded **14.1%** over five years ($3.79B → $7.21B, FY21–FY26) almost entirely on price and seat expansion, with gross margin *rising* from 90.4% to **92.3%**.
- **Returns on capital far above cost of capital:** ROIC of **27.3%** (FY26), averaging ~26% across FY23–FY26 vs. a ~9% WACC. This is the single strongest moat tell.
- **Recurring, entrenched revenue:** subscription/term model with high renewal rates; every one of the last six years grew revenue, including through COVID.
- **Operating leverage:** GAAP operating margin from 14.1% (FY22) → 21.9% (FY26); non-GAAP ~39% guided for FY27.

**Where the moat is narrower than it looks:** switching costs are high at the *firm* level but weak at the *individual/startup* level, where Blender, Onshape and cheaper point tools compete. And the market's central fear — that generative AI collapses the value of a CAD seat — is a genuine, unproven threat to the terminal value, not noise. Competitors are real but non-threatening near-term: Dassault (SolidWorks/CATIA), Siemens (NX), PTC, Bentley, Nemetschek.

**Moat direction: widening** — margins and ROIC both up over five years, and the AECO/construction push (now growing >20%) extends the franchise beyond design into operations.

Asset-light, cash-generative, ~1x EBITDA leverage post-deal (interest coverage still >10x). Balance sheet is a non-issue.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**What the market is charging.** At $210: market cap $44.3B, EV ~$47.5B (post-deal net debt).
- EV / TTM FCF (reported) = **16.8x**
- EV / TTM FCF (ex-SBC) = **22.3x**
- Forward non-GAAP P/E (FY27E ~$12.60) = **~16.7x**
- Forward GAAP P/E (~$9) = ~23x
- Reported FCF yield = 6.4%; fully-loaded ~4.8%

**Intrinsic value (owner-earnings DCF).** Normalized FY27E: revenue $8.3B × 39% non-GAAP margin = $3.24B, less $0.75B SBC = $2.49B, less ~20% cash tax = $1.99B, plus ~$140M D&A net of capex, less ~$160M after-tax interest on the new term loan → **~$2.0B of true owner earnings**, growing high-single digits fading to 3–4% terminal, discounted at 9%.

- **Base case (intrinsic value ≈ $235/share):** ~22x owner earnings, growth path 12% → 4.5% over ten years.
- **Bull case ($265):** margin reaches the FY29 41% target on schedule; construction sustains >20% growth; MaintainX accretive by FY29.
- **Bear case ($175):** AI erodes seat pricing, growth decays to 5% by FY29, multiple compresses to 18x owner earnings.

**Conclusion: the stock is fairly valued, not cheap.** At $210 it trades at a **~11% discount to a $235 midpoint**, well short of the 20–30% margin of safety I require. At the stale $240 print it is at fair value, period.

**What the price implies:** that Autodesk grows high-single digits indefinitely while expanding margins to 41% — a reasonable, not pessimistic, assumption. You are being paid nothing for the AI-disruption risk. Note also that the new transaction model added ~2pp to Q2 revenue growth and ~1.5pp to FY27; the CFO confirms it does not recur in FY28. FY2028 headline growth will therefore *optically decelerate*, which is a live de-rating catalyst not yet in estimates.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Andrew Anagnost (CEO since 2017)** — track record is good, not flawless. Revenue roughly doubled and non-GAAP margins expanded several hundred basis points on his watch; the subscription transition was executed well. Public reporting in 2025 indicated activist involvement (Starboard) pressing for margin discipline and succession planning, which plausibly explains the commitment to a 41% non-GAAP margin by FY29; I could not verify the current CEO-succession status within my data window and flag it as an open item.

**Capital allocation — mixed:**
- *Good:* Buybacks of ~$1.6B TTM (~3.6% of market cap); diluted shares down from 222M (FY22) to 211M — real, if modest, shrinkage (~1.1%/yr). Prior tuck-ins (Delcam, PlanGrid, Innovyze) were sensible.
- *Concerning:* **MaintainX at $3.575B all-cash** — the largest acquisition in company history, for an unprofitable, high-growth asset, funded with cash and a **364-day term loan**. This is exactly the profile of a deal that dilutes margins (management concedes FY27 dilution) and risks a goodwill write-down. It is empire-building until proven otherwise.
- *Neutral:* No dividend. Negative retained earnings reflect buybacks, not losses.

Insider ownership data was not retrievable — an acknowledged gap.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

This is a genuinely wonderful business — wide moat, 27% ROIC, 92% gross margins, cash conversion above 100% of earnings — now run by a management team that just levered up for a $3.6B unprofitable acquisition at the same moment the market began questioning whether AI undermines software moats.

The 36% drawdown has repaired the *valuation excess*, not created a *bargain*. At ~$210 you get a fair price for a great business — and Buffett's rule is that a fair price is not a buy.

- **Accumulate below ~$185** (≈30% discount to the $235 midpoint, EV/FCF ex-SBC ~19x, ~14x forward non-GAAP).
- **Trim/avoid above ~$265** (at or above bull-case intrinsic value).
- Existing holders: **hold** — do not sell a widening-moat compounder at a fair price to chase a narrative.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI disruption of seat-based pricing (the big one).** If generative design materially reduces the number of professional seats required, the terminal multiple is wrong, not just the terminal growth rate. This is the reason I demand a discount rather than paying fair value.
2. **MaintainX integration and margin dilution.** $3.6B for an unprofitable business, plus a 364-day term loan refinancing risk, plus ~$3.5B of incremental goodwill. A write-down would be a direct hit to my estimate of management quality.
3. **FY2028 "air pocket."** The new transaction model's ~1.5pp lift vanishes next year; combined with a full year of MaintainX costs, reported growth and margins could both look worse than underlying economics, extending the de-rating.
4. **Billings/FCF lumpiness and SBC.** Peak-cycle FCF ($2.83B TTM) is not the run-rate; $706M of annual SBC is real dilution risk if buybacks slow.
5. **Cyclical exposure.** AECO and manufacturing are economically sensitive; Western European sales productivity is already showing friction.
6. **Execution/leadership continuity.** CEO succession questions under activist pressure, mid-integration, add key-person risk.

---

**One-line thesis:** Wide-moat, 27%-ROIC design-software standard-bearer, now fairly priced after a 36% drawdown — wait for a margin of safety around $185; the AI question is not yet answerable at $210.
