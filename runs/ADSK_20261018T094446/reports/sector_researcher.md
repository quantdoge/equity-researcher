## Sector Brief — Autodesk (ADSK) | Design & Engineering Software (AEC / CAD / EDA)

**Note on data:** FMP classifies ADSK in "Technology / Software – Application," whose peer list is dominated by semis and mega-cap platforms (NVDA, AAPL, MSFT) — not comparable. I therefore constructed a purpose-built **design, engineering and vertical-application software** peer set: PTC, Bentley Systems (BSY), Cadence (CDNS), Synopsys (SNPS) and Adobe (ADBE). The `get_sector_financials` endpoint returned malformed aggregates (company_count=1, bucketed by fiscal period-end date, not year) and was discarded. ANSS is excluded — acquired by Synopsys (closed 2025), a live consolidation datapoint. The price-history feed also appears ~7 months behind the news feed; I use news-dated pricing for the de-rating call.

---

### 1. Sector Overview

Design and engineering software is a **mature-growth, high-barrier vertical application market** of roughly **$12–14bn in AEC software alone in 2026**, forecast to compound **8–9.5%** through the early 2030s (Mordor, Grand View, Business Research). It sits at the intersection of two structures: the *durable* one — 85–92% gross margins, mission-critical workflow software, file-format lock-in (DWG, RVT, IFC) and 8–10%+ structural growth from BIM mandates and construction labour shortages — and the *cyclical/AI-repricing* one — 2026 has seen application-software multiples compress hard on fears that AI agents collapse per-seat pricing (the software complex fell ~13% in a single session on 3 Feb 2026), while nonresidential construction demand is softening. The incumbent set (Autodesk, Nemetschek, Bentley, Trimble, Dassault, Siemens, PTC, plus Procore adjacently in construction management) is consolidated but not concentrated enough to support oligopoly pricing discipline: top-3 share in AEC design authoring is roughly 55–65%, with the balance fragmented across regional and open-source/BIM tools.

**Porter's Five Forces:** Supplier power **Low** (COGS just 7.7% of ADSK revenue); Threat of new entrants **Low–Moderate** (40 years of file-format network effects, but VC-funded AI-native CAD is real); Buyer power **Moderate and rising** (AEC firms consolidating, enterprise procurement, seat-count sensitivity); Threat of substitutes **Moderate and rising** (openBIM/IFC interoperability, generative design, in-house tools); Rivalry **Moderate** (price competition muted, but feature competition intense). Net: a structurally favourable industry whose *pricing model*, not its *moat*, is under attack.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Secular digitization of AEC with a long runway.** BIM/ISO 19650 mandates, infrastructure/public-works programmes and chronic skilled-labour shortages (~8–9 months of backlog across contractors) push design work into software. AEC software TAM compounding 8–9.5%; Autodesk's underlying market is growing well below its own 17.5% FY26 growth, meaning **share gains and pricing are doing the work**.
- **Demonstrable pricing power — the standout datapoint.** FY26 (Jan) **billings +30% to $7.77bn** vs revenue +18% to $7.21bn; Q4 billings **+33%**. That spread is renewal price escalation plus a mix shift toward cloud/industry clouds, and it converts to margin: FY26 gross margin **92.3% (+170bps YoY)**, Q4 non-GAAP operating margin **38%**, Q4 FCF **~$1.0bn (+43%)**.
- **AI as attach/upsell plus TAM extension via M&A.** Agentic AI shipping across the three industry clouds (Forma/AEC, Fusion/PDM, Flow/M&E; previewed at AU Sept 2026) monetises data gravity rather than cannibalising seats. The **~$3.6bn MaintainX acquisition** (>\$135m CY26 ARR, growing >50%) pushes Autodesk downstream from *design* into *building operations* — a genuine TAM expansion. Data-centre and manufacturing-reshoring capex is the strongest end-market pocket.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Sector-wide multiple compression from the AI/pricing-model debate.** This is the dominant 2026 variable and it is *not* company-specific: per-seat pricing's share of SaaS models reportedly fell 21% → 15% in twelve months, and vertical application software has been de-rated wholesale. ADSK traded ~$326 in Sept 2025 and ~$240 in Oct 2026 — a **~26% de-rating** despite *raising* guidance. Even a 92%-gross-margin franchise can't outrun a discount-rate/pricing-regime reset.
- **Softening nonresidential end-market.** The AIA Consensus Construction Forecast now expects total construction spending to **decline slightly in 2026** before returning to ~2% growth in 2027, with the ABI (a 9–12 month leading indicator) persistently sub-50. High rates, tariffs and labour costs squeeze customer budgets, and in a seat-based model **AEC headcount is the revenue base** — architecture-firm staffing cuts hit licences directly.
- **Deceleration already visible, and GAAP margins lag peers.** Revenue growth has stepped down from +18% (Q1 FY27) to **+16% (Q2 FY27, +14% cc)** and billings from +18% to **+10%**. FY26 GAAP operating margin of **21.9% sits ~580bps below the peer median (27.7%)**, inflated-cost drivers being heavy SBC and acquired-intangible amortisation (SNPS shows the same dynamic). MaintainX is dilutive near term. Meanwhile Nemetschek, Bentley, Trimble, Procore, Dassault and Siemens all contest the same budget line, and openBIM/IFC erodes the file-format moat incrementally.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **Share leadership in the core franchise.** Autodesk is the clear #1 in AEC design authoring (AutoCAD/Revit), with the only platform spanning all three verticals — AEC (Forma), product design & manufacturing (Fusion/Inventor), and media & entertainment (Maya/3ds Max/Flow). Nemetschek (Archicad/Allplan), Bentley (infrastructure) and Trimble (Tekla) each hold a slice; none matches the breadth.
- **Quantitatively superior.** Highest gross margin in the peer set (92.3% vs 85.1% median), fastest current-year growth (+17.5% vs +14.6%), 5-yr revenue CAGR 13.7% vs 12.7% median, and FY26 billings growth of **+30%** — nearly 2x revenue growth, which is the clearest available evidence of pricing power and forward-revenue visibility.
- **The two genuine soft spots.** (i) GAAP operating margin trails peers by ~580bps, and though Q4 non-GAAP OM of 38% shows the underlying engine is competitive, ADSK's SBC intensity is structurally higher — a real dilution cost, not just accounting noise. (ii) **Fusion 360 has not dislodged SOLIDWORKS (Dassault) or NX/Solid Edge (Siemens) in mainstream mechanical CAD**, so manufacturing is a challenger position, not a leadership one; and in a flat seat-count world, ADSK is more exposed to AEC headcount than Cadence/Synopsys are to design-starts.
- **Moat durability:** high but no longer widening. DWG/RVT switching costs are formidable; openBIM and AI-native generative design are the vectors that could narrow them over a 3–5 year horizon.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Structurally attractive (85–92% gross margins, mission-critical workflow, 8–9.5% durable TAM growth, low supplier power, high switching costs) but **cyclically and valuation-regime unfavourable in 2026**. The earnings engine is intact — ADSK is beating and raising, with billings outrunning revenue by 12 points — yet the sector is in an active de-rating on AI/per-seat concerns and its primary end-market (nonresidential construction) is forecast to contract slightly in 2026 before a modest 2027 recovery. That combination argues for **owning the share gainers (ADSK, CDNS) on weakness rather than owning the sector**; ADSK's own ~26% de-rating to ~6.8x trailing EV/sales and low-20s x annualised non-GAAP EPS (Q1 FY27 non-GAAP EPS $2.99) is beginning to price the seat-erosion thesis, but the Q2 FY27 billings deceleration to +10% argues the thesis is not yet fully de-risked.
