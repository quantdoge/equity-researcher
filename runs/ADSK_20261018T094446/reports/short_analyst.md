X2 is negative from ~$5B of cumulative buybacks. X4 uses *book* equity ($3.04B) not market cap ($50.1B); substituting market cap lifts Z to roughly **4.0 — the safe zone**. ADSK is not a solvency story; interest coverage is effectively infinite.

**Rating: Medium-High.** Real cash generation, genuinely conservative accruals, no revenue-recognition fraud signal. The problems here are behavioural and structural, not fraudulent.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**Flag 1 — The forward-visibility metric has broken. This is the short thesis.**
- Q2 FY27 (Jul-26): Revenue **+16%**, but **total RPO only +2% to $7,433M** — down from +9% in Q1 and **+24% a year ago**.
- **Unbilled deferred revenue -8% YoY to $3.17B.**
- **Billings $1.85B, +10%** vs revenue +16% — a 6-point gap.
- Current RPO +12% vs revenue +16%.

When revenue growth persistently exceeds billings growth, you are either burning the deferred-revenue cushion or annualising a prior-year upfront-billing tailwind. Both are self-liquidating. **Revenue growth must converge toward billings growth (10–13%) within a few quarters.**

**Flag 2 — Management is shrinking contract duration while telling you it's a pricing win.** The explanation offered — deliberately ending multi-year discounted renewals to improve lifetime price realisation — is economically coherent but has a cost nobody is modelling: it **shortens customer lock-in and re-exposes the installed base to competitive displacement, during an active global sales-force reorganisation.** Management concedes EMEA GTM disruption and "gradual" normalisation. You do not voluntarily dismantle multi-year contracts at the same moment you are re-plumbing renewal incentives unless you are confident — or unless you need the near-term revenue.

**Flag 3 — A proven history of managing exactly the metric the bull case rests on.** The FY2024 Audit Committee investigation found that actions taken by management "generally served to reduce reported free cash flow and/or lower reported margin in the current period" — i.e., **spending was deferred to manufacture current-period FCF and margin.** FCF carried a 40% weight in executive PSUs. No restatement of earnings, but the 10-K was delayed to 10-Jun-2024 and the CFO transitioned. Autodesk had also under-capitalised cloud-computing implementation costs, **overstating historical FCF**. Remediated and filings are timely again — but the institutional reflex to protect the FCF number is on the record.

**Flag 4 — Receivables spike, largely reversed.** FY26 AR +42.8% vs revenue +17.5%; DSO 72.9 days vs 60.0. At constant DSO, AR would have been ~$1,185M, not $1,439M — **~$254M, or a quarter of the year's revenue growth, sat uncollected at year-end.** To be fair: Apr-26 DSO 27.2 vs 27.5 prior year, Jul-26 DSO 30.4 vs 27.5. **The anomaly was a January balance-sheet timing event, not ongoing channel stuffing.** It weakens this flag considerably, and I weight it accordingly.

**Flag 5 — GAAP/non-GAAP gap of 1,200bp, sustained by SBC and acquired-intangible amortisation.** Q2 FY27: non-GAAP operating margin 41% (+200bp) vs **GAAP 29.3%**; non-GAAP EPS $3.30 vs **GAAP $2.33** — a 42% gap. The "margin story" is substantially a definitional one.

**Flag 6 — Serial M&A escalation at peak prices, debt-funded.** MaintainX, announced 28-May-2026, **$3.6B all-cash — largest ever — for ~$135M ARR (~27x ARR, >50% growth)**, funded ~$1.6B cash plus debt. Short-term debt jumped to $1,545M; net debt swung from net cash. Goodwill + intangibles are **$4.75B = 38% of total assets and 156% of book equity**; post-deal that approaches ~$8B+, or >200% of equity. This is a company **buying growth** at the same moment organic bookings visibility deteriorates, and deploying the margin gains its activist (Starboard) fought for.

**Flag 7 — Buybacks are dilution maintenance, not capital return.** FY26 buybacks $1,402M against $788M SBC; diluted shares fell only 217M → 215M (**-0.9%**). Roughly 56% of the buyback simply offset compensation.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment: **Real but not yet visible in the P&L**

- **Direct competitors:** Bentley Systems, Nemetschek, Dassault Systèmes, Procore, Trimble, Hexagon — plus cloud-native and AI-native entrants in design and construction.
- **The AI question is the existential one.** The bull answer is credible: Autodesk's moat is *not* the drafting UI, it is 3D geometric data, embedded workflow context, and **deterministic parametric validation** — probabilistic AI generating ideas is worthless if the output cannot be checked against physics and manufacturability. Forma's Building Layout Explorer and Fusion's AutoConstrain are early evidence of this.
- **The bear answer is that the value migrates to the generation layer and the "validation + geometry" layer gets commoditised or absorbed by a hyperscaler/foundation-model provider.** No P&L evidence of this — gross margin expanded to 92.3%, which is the opposite of commoditisation.
- **The nearer-term threat is self-inflicted:** a global sales reorganisation (partner incentives shifted from renewals to new business, slowed by EMEA labour law) is being run simultaneously with the multi-year contract wind-down. Renewal-rate erosion would show up *after* the RPO line already looks weak.
- **No moat-erosion signal in the fundamentals.** Gross margin up, AECO +17% (data-centre/infrastructure tailwind offsetting commercial real estate), Make +26%. This is not a business losing pricing power today.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary: is there a credible bear case?

**Yes — a credible *expectations* bear case. No credible *fraud* bear case.**

**The bear case:**
ADSK is a ~6x EV/Sales, ~18x EV/FCF, ~19x non-GAAP earnings subscription business whose growth engine is decelerating faster than the income statement admits. Total RPO +2% against revenue +16%, unbilled backlog -8%, and billings +10% are three independent confirmations that recognised revenue is outrunning contracted demand. Management is converting multi-year contracts to annual — a one-time ARR/price tailwind (~1.5% of FY27 growth, by their own admission) that flatters FY27 and **annualises into a headwind in FY28**, precisely when the GTM reorg and EMEA disruption bite. The margin lever that activist pressure created has already been pulled (39% FY29 target hit two years early), and instead of letting it flow to EPS, management is spending it on $3.6B of ~27x-ARR M&A that pushes goodwill past 200% of book equity and converts net cash into leverage. Strip out $788M of SBC and the 5.5% FCF yield becomes ~3.8% — and this is a management team with a documented history of deferring spend to protect that very FCF number. Revenue growth should converge to high-single digits; consensus FY28 +11% is 1–2 points too high, and 18x EV/FCF for that is not a bargain.
