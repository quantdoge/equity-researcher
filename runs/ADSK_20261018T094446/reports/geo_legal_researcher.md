### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Export controls on design/engineering software spreading beyond semiconductors (Probability: Medium; Impact: Medium-High)**
Autodesk's 10-K risk factors explicitly warn about "violations of export controls and sanctions laws and regulations" and note that "if our channel partners fail to obtain appropriate import, export, or re-export licenses," results could suffer. The live precedent is instructive: BIS imposed curbs on **EDA (chip design) software exports to China in May 2025**, then **lifted them in July 2025** as part of the trade truce (Reuters, Kirkland & Ellis client alert). Design software has now been demonstrated to be a policy weapon that can be turned on and off by executive action. Autodesk is not an EDA vendor, but APAC is 17% of revenue and China is the largest APAC component. A broadening of "design software" controls would hit a mid-single-digit share of revenue — not existential, but a sharp sentiment shock and a re-rating risk for the whole applications-software complex.

**Risk 2 — European regulatory and AI-governance load (Probability: High; Impact: Medium)**
EMEA is the **largest international block at 38.8%** of revenue and grew as a share. Three vectors: (a) **EU AI Act** — Autodesk launched "agentic AI" across Forma, Fusion, and Flow in September 2026 (PR Newswire, 15-Sep-2026), which pulls the company into GPAI/transparency obligations and high-risk-use classification debates for AEC and manufacturing workflows; (b) **data privacy** (GDPR) with cloud-shifted revenue raising data-residency exposure; (c) **EU competition posture toward software licensing** — the Commission opened a probe into SAP's practices in September 2025 (Reuters), signalling appetite to scrutinise subscription/support lock-in models directly analogous to Autodesk's subscription transition.

**Risk 3 — FX and emerging-market macro in EMEA/APAC (Probability: Medium; Impact: Low-Medium)**
With 56% non-US revenue and a beta of 1.33, a strong-dollar episode or EM currency dislocation (e.g., Turkey, Argentina, Nigeria, Egypt) creates reported-revenue drag and receivables risk. The 14% constant-currency vs 16% reported growth gap in Q2 FY2027 quantifies this at roughly 2 points of growth.

*(De-escalated: Russia. Autodesk is not flagged as a remaining Russia operator; the Yale "over 1,000 companies curtailed" list was reviewed but I could not independently confirm Autodesk's current Russia status from primary filings within budget — treat as a gap, though the sanctions nexus appears immaterial given the FY24–FY26 revenue mix shows no anomalous EMEA growth that would indicate retained Russia business.)*

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Resolved — Securities class action dismissed with prejudice.** The 2024 federal securities fraud class action in N.D. Cal. (arising from the audit committee investigation into free-cash-flow and non-GAAP operating margin practices) was **dismissed with prejudice on 26-Jan-2026**; Law360 reports the suit was "axed for good." This removes the single largest disclosed litigation overhang. Source: Kessler Topaz case page; Law360.

**Governance scar tissue (not litigated, but a compliance red flag).** The 2024–25 audit committee investigation concluded **no restatement was required**, but the company effectively conceded that charges were timed to meet free-cash-flow targets (Compliance Week; Deep Quarry analysis; MarketWatch). Internal controls and disclosure culture have been a live issue; the dismissal of the class action does not eliminate the possibility of follow-on SEC inquiry or renewed shareholder activism on compensation/EPS metric design.

**Regulatory perimeter:** primary regulators are the SEC (reporting/controls), EU Commission (competition, AI Act, GDPR via member-state DPAs), UK CMA/ICO, and BIS/OFAC (export controls and sanctions on channel partners). No active DOJ, FTC, or EU antitrust action against Autodesk itself was identified in this review — **this is a negative finding based on available sources, not a certified clean bill**.

**AI-generated content and liability:** agentic AI features embedded in design and construction workflows raise novel professional-liability and product-defect questions (who is liable when an AI-generated structural or MEP design fails). Expect contractual indemnity expansion and insurers to price this in.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Channel-partner export/sanctions certification** — 10-K flags reliance on resellers obtaining import/export/re-export licenses. With TD Synnex down to 14% of revenue, direct-sales share has risen sharply; direct sales into APAC raise Autodesk's own end-use/end-user screening burden. *Watch item, not a violation.*
2. **Financial-controls history** — the FCF-timing episode at a company whose equity story rests on FCF conversion and margin expansion is a governance discount worth monitoring.
3. **No evidence of FCPA/AML/bribery matters** in the filings reviewed; Autodesk's bribery exposure is structurally low (software, no in-country physical supply chain or extractive concessions).
4. **Data privacy** — GDPR/UK GDPR exposure is proportional to the 38.8% EMEA revenue share and growing cloud/AI data flows; no disclosed enforcement action found.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM (low end)**

**Rationale:** Pure *geopolitical* risk is genuinely low — no Russia nexus, no active sanctions exposure, geographically stable revenue mix, and a distributor base whose largest single concentration fell from 39% to 14%. The single largest legal overhang (securities class action) was dismissed with prejudice in January 2026. The rating is held at Medium rather than Low by two factors: (i) a demonstrated policy precedent that design software can be caught in US–China export controls, over which Autodesk has zero control, and (ii) an elevated European regulatory/AI-governance load against a 38.8% EMEA revenue base that has been *growing* as a share, combined with a recent internal-controls governance episode.

**Watch items for the next 12 months:** any BIS action touching non-semiconductor design software; EU AI Act implementing guidance for AEC/manufacturing AI; expensive FX translation if the dollar strengthens; and whether the FY2027 10-K discloses any SEC correspondence arising from the 2024 FCF episode.

**Disclosed gaps (honest limitations):** (1) No country-level revenue disclosure — US, China, and Japan shares are estimates only. (2) SEC EDGAR and Autodesk IR direct fetches were blocked (HTTP 403), so the FY2026 10-K Item 3 "Legal Proceedings" text and Risk Factors could not be read in full; legal conclusions rest on secondary sources plus search-index excerpts of the 10-K/10-Q. (3) I could not verify a current list of active lawsuits beyond the dismissed class action, nor confirm the status of any DOJ/SEC inquiry. (4) No peer comparison on litigation or geographic exposure was completed within the budget.
