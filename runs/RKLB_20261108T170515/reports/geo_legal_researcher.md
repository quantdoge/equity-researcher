### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US–China critical-minerals and tariff shock to a thin supply chain — Probability: High / Impact: Medium**
The FY2025 10-K (filed 26 Feb 2026) **added a brand-new risk factor**: "Changes in trade policies, including tariffs, could cause adverse impacts to our business," explicitly citing *increased U.S. tariffs since April 2025* and exposure "through imported inputs and operations in Canada and New Zealand." It also carries a dedicated risk factor on "restrictions on our ability to obtain **rare earth minerals**." This is not theoretical: China's April 2025 heavy-rare-earth/magnet restrictions caused "rapid disruptions across allied defense" supply chains and bottlenecks are expected to persist through 2026 (CSIS; S&P Global, Jan 2026); China's 1 Jan 2026 export-licensing catalogue added further controls (Andersen Institute). Rocket Lab builds flight hardware (Electron/Neutron engines, solar, reaction wheels, avionics) with single- or limited-source vendors — a risk factor it flags separately. Margin, not solvency, risk: gross profit was $84.6M on $234M Q2-2026 revenue.

**② Iridium acquisition becomes a single-point regulatory failure — Probability: Medium / Impact: High**
Signed **28 June 2026** ($27.00 cash + RKLB stock per share); Iridium stockholders approved **24 Sept 2026**; the two filed for **FCC consent on 10 Aug 2026** (public notice **DA-26-985**), covering the 66-satellite constellation, global spectrum and gateway earth stations. Target close: **mid-2027**, "subject to remaining required regulatory approvals." Filings state no shareholder holds ≥10% and the deal meets FCC foreign-ownership limits — but this is exactly the profile that draws **Team Telecom/CFIUS-adjacent review and executive-branch conditions**, an area of "intense and escalating scrutiny" with unprecedented FCC enforcement of Team Telecom agreements in Jan 2026 (HLC). Complicating: **SpaceX has asked the FCC to scrutinise** the deal (not formally opposing). A divestiture/condition package (or a second request) is the realistic downside; outright block is low probability.

**③ Customer and political concentration in the US national-security budget — Probability: Medium / Impact: High**
Top-five customers = **49% of 2025 revenue but 77% of year-end backlog** (up from 69%) — i.e., concentration is *increasing* in forward revenue. The 10-K carries risk factors on US government funding disruption (55% rewritten in FY2025, adding a new example) and on government policy/priority changes. Watch items: (a) whether NSSL Lane 1 ($5.6B programme ceiling, on-ramped Mar-2025) converts to actual task orders, which SSC conditioned on a **first successful Neutron launch**; (b) continuing-resolution/appropriations timing; (c) the Nov-2026 US midterm outcome (Democratic wave per Reuters/Investopedia 6 Nov 2026) and any shift in space/defence priorities. Note the macro overlay: an **ongoing Iran war** and oil-price surge are driving market volatility (Reuters, Guardian 6 Nov 2026), and RKLB's beta is 2.6.

*Runner-up:* **Neutron execution at Wallops** — a failure is an operational/legal event (FAA mishap investigation, licence conditions, environmental litigation risk around launch-site expansion; PEER and others are already pressing FAA to reject a rule waiving environmental review for commercial launches).

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action — resolved at the trial level.** In re Rocket Lab securities litigation (Neutron test/debut-timeline allegations, class period around the Feb-2025 Bleecker report) — **motion to dismiss granted with prejudice on 16 April 2026**, with plaintiffs given 30 days to appeal to the **Ninth Circuit** (Q1-2026 10-Q; Bloomberg Law, 17 Apr 2026). Residual risk: a Ninth Circuit revival would reopen an expensive, reputationally noisy matter. No accrual disclosed.
- **No disclosed DOJ/SEC/FCPA/OFAC enforcement** in the filings reviewed. No active government investigation identified.
- **Export controls / FOCI (the structural issue).** US launch and spacecraft technology sits under **ITAR (DDTC)** and **EAR (BIS)**; the company must obtain export authorisations for any sharing with its NZ, Canadian, Australian and German subsidiaries and for foreign-national employees — including a **New Zealand-national founder-CEO** and an NZ launch range. This creates real **FOCI/DCSA facility-clearance** tension as classified NSSL/SDA work scales. Watch for the classic failure mode in this sector: deemed-export violations and voluntary self-disclosures rather than headline fines.
- **Launch licensing.** LC-1 Mahia operates under **New Zealand's** Outer Space and High-altitude Activities Act regime (NZ's approach is still evolving — JD Supra); Wallops operates under **FAA/AST** licence with environmental-review exposure.
- **FCC** exposure today is spectrum/earth-station related; post-Iridium it becomes a primary, permanent regulator (title III, landing rights, 27/28 GHz, EPFD conditions).
- **Acquisition-related FDI screening is live and proven:** the Mynaric deal required **German foreign-direct-investment clearance** (final approval ~31 Mar 2026). Expect replication across jurisdictions for Iridium.
- **IP/contract risk:** risk factors on third-party IP claims, indemnity provisions and fixed-price cost overruns; two **new** FY2025 risk factors (performance-based payment terms; tariffs).

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**
*(with a High-severity single-event tail: FCC/Iridium approval and Neutron first-launch mishap)*

**Rationale:** Country risk is genuinely low — every operating jurisdiction is a US treaty ally with functioning rule of law, no sanctions exposure, no expropriation regime, no currency-repatriation controls, and the only FX translation risk is NZD/CAD. What lifts RKLB above "Low" is **concentrated regulatory dependency** (FCC + FAA + DDTC/BIS + USSF, all simultaneously, on a deal and a rocket that must both work) and **increasing customer concentration (77% of backlog in five customers)**. There is no legal overhang of consequence today.

**What would change the rating:** downgrade to **High** on (i) an FCC second request, Team Telecom condition package or Iridium timeline slip past CY2027; (ii) a Neutron first-launch failure triggering FAA mishap/grounding; (iii) a Ninth Circuit revival of the securities case or any ITAR voluntary self-disclosure becoming public. Upgrade to **Low–Medium** on FCC approval of Iridium and a successful first Neutron flight.
