## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Rocket Lab Corporation (RKLB)

**Snapshot:** $53.07/share, ~$31.8B market cap, 2,600 employees, Aerospace & Defense, beta 2.61. Stock is ~70% below its May 2026 record close. Data as of Nov 7, 2026. *Data note: direct SEC EDGAR fetches returned HTTP 403 and the 10-K/10-Q/DEF 14A did not resolve through the filing tool; governance and insider facts below are sourced from Form 4 aggregators, the 13D/A, SEC filing excerpts surfaced via search, and press coverage. Items I could not independently verify are flagged.*

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (with a disclosure-credibility discount)

**Positives:**
- **Single class of stock, one vote per share** — the legacy dual-class structure was collapsed in the May 2025 holding-company reorganization. No super-voting founder bloc.
- **Board independence: 7 of 8 directors independent** under Nasdaq rules, with Peter Beck the sole non-independent director (2025 proxy).
- **Say-on-pay 2026: ~319.1M for vs. 33.5M against (~90% support)** — no compensation revolt; audit and equity-plan proposals passed.

**Concerns:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality:** Beck is founder, President, CEO *and* Chairman (Chair since Aug 2021). Unusual for a company of this size and removes the board's most important check on management.
- **Founder halo / key-person concentration:** Beck holds ~46.4M shares (~8% of the ~572.5M shares outstanding as of Mar 20, 2026 per 13D/A). Not a control block economically, but combined with founder-CEO-Chair status and the "one unproven rocket" narrative, board challenge to timelines appears weak in practice.
- **Reported CEO compensation ~$20.1M (2025)** — high absolute level for a pre-profit company with negative EBITDA; largely equity-based, so nominally aligned, but see insider selling.
- **Timeline credibility is a governance issue, not just an operating one:** Neutron's first flight has now slipped from mid-2025 → 2026 → **2027 (announced Oct 14, 2026)**.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **No SEC or DOJ enforcement actions** were found against RKLB in EDGAR full-text enforcement search — a genuine positive.
- **Securities class action (filed April 2025)** alleging false/misleading statements about Neutron's barge-landing tests and debut timeline (catalyst: Bleecker Street Research short report, Feb 25, 2025; lead-plaintiff deadline Apr 28, 2025). **Rocket Lab defeated/dismissed the suit** per Bloomberg Law. Legal win, but the fact pattern has now partly recurred.
- **Iridium acquisition ($7.6–8.0B, cash-and-stock) — the dominant regulatory item:**
  - DOJ/HSR antitrust: **cleared** (Aug 2026, no objections).
  - **FCC applications filed Aug 10, 2026** (Space Bureau); the FCC *may refer the transaction to the national-security review body* — satellite spectrum + a 66-satellite constellation is a high-scrutiny asset. **FCC consent is the key gating approval and is not yet granted.**
  - Iridium shareholders approved **99.6% (Sept 24, 2026)**; closing targeted **mid-2027**.
  - A **restructured ~$3.6B financing plan** accompanies the deal — regulatory delay directly increases financing and dilution risk.
- **Structural regulatory dependency:** FAA launch licensing, ITAR/export controls, and U.S. defense/national-security programs (NSSL, HASTE hypersonic tests, Golden Dome work). Policy shifts are a live exposure.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Neutron execution — the single largest risk.** Second consecutive schedule slip (to 2027) tied to **carbon-composite first-stage tank manufacturing defects** (forward dome still in production as of Oct 2026). Neutron is the entire medium-lift growth thesis; a third slip or a first-flight failure would be severely value-destructive. Competitors (Relativity, Stoke) are closing the window.
- **Launch reliability / mission failure.** Electron's brand is its cadence and reliability (19 launches in 2026), but the fleet has experienced anomalies (2020, 2021, and a 2025 in-flight data-loss timeout that destroyed payloads). Each failure triggers customer-loss, insurance, and reputational costs; HASTE/hypersonic and defense missions raise the stakes.
- **Key-person and integration concentration.** ~2,600 employees supporting Electron, Neutron, Photon, components, *and* now the $60M Motiv acquisition and eventually a 66-satellite Iridium constellation. Management bandwidth and Beck dependence are acute; an Iridium close would roughly double operational complexity while Neutron remains unflown.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **ELEVATED**

- **Credibility deficit:** a short-seller report and shareholder suit over Neutron timelines, followed by a further ~2-year slip, has hardened a "serial over-promiser" narrative in the market. Each slippage renews litigation surface.
- **Competitive de-rating:** SpaceX's (SPCX) summer IPO created a scale-and-profitability benchmark RKLB cannot match (Starlink profits vs. RKLB's $8.8M Q2 adjusted EBITDA loss). RKLB has underperformed its sector since.
- **Governance optics:** $286M of CEO share sales at ~$87, followed by a 70% drawdown and further insider sales at ~$53, is precisely the pattern retail and proxy advisors punish.
- **ESG/defense controversy:** growing weapons-adjacent revenue (Golden Dome, hypersonics) creates ongoing NGO/activist exposure, modest for now.
- **Stabilizers:** strong customer traction (record ~$2.36B backlog, 19 Electron launches, NASA/DoD awards); no product-safety, data-breach, or environmental controversy identified.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Driven by (i) a second failure to hit a marquee program milestone, (ii) one-sided insider distribution with no insider conviction buying, (iii) unresolved FCC/national-security approval plus a $3.6B financing need on an $8B acquisition, and (iv) CEO/Chairman power concentration. Set against a clean enforcement record and a genuinely independent board majority.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Disclosure-quality pattern (highest priority).** Neutron missed mid-2025, then 2026, now 2027 — the exact allegation in the (dismissed) 2025 securities class action. A further slip or a first-flight failure materially raises re-litigation and enforcement risk. Re-underwrite every management timeline.
2. **Insider selling: ~$400M+ / >13.7M shares over 12 months with zero purchases**, concentrated at $118–$149 and continuing below $53. Treat as a strong negative signal on management's own view of near-term value.
3. **Iridium/FCC binary event.** FCC consent (with possible national-security referral) plus a restructured $3.6B financing; delay to mid-2027 extends dilution and financing-market exposure. Deal break or heavily dilutive terms would be a step-change negative.
4. **Governance remediation gap.** Combine CEO and Chairman roles in one person at a company whose core risk is management forward guidance; no independent chair offsets the conflict.
5. **Key-person dependence on Peter Beck** with no disclosed succession plan at a $31.8B market cap.

**Recommended monitoring:** Form 4 flow (any first insider *buy* would be a meaningful tell), FCC docket status, Neutron stage-1 tank qualification milestones, and 8-K disclosure language versus delivery.
