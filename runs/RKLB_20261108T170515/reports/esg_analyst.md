## ESG ANALYSIS — ROCKET LAB CORPORATION (NASDAQ: RKLB)

**Snapshot:** Launch + space systems, Long Beach CA; ~2,600 FTEs; FY2025 revenue $601.8M, net loss $198.2M; market cap ~$32bn (beta 2.6). Reorganised under new holding company **Rocket Lab Corporation** on 23 May 2025. Peer set: GE Aerospace, RTX, LMT, BA, SpaceX (private).

Note the inversion vs. a typical industrial: for RKLB, **absolute Scope 1/2 emissions are immaterial** (small-launch cadence ~20 flights/yr, LOX/kerosene engines). The financially material ESG vectors are **safety execution and governance** — both of which have already moved the stock.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Carbon:** Small absolute footprint; no Group-level Scope 1/2/3 disclosure located, **no CDP disclosure found**, and no sustainability report since the **2021 Impact Report** (published Aug 2022). This is the weakest leg of the E profile — a disclosure gap, not (yet) a performance problem.
- **Launch-site exposure:** LC-1 Mahia (NZ, private site, EEZ Act marine consent via NZ EPA), LC-2 Wallops (NASA-owned, FAA-licensed, EIS-based), LC-3 Stennis (engine test). NZ regulators concluded environmental risk remains **low up to 1,000 launches** provided debris is not deposited on sensitive features — a permissive backdrop that caps near-term regulatory cost.
- **Physical/transition:** No carbon-tax-exposed heavy assets; negligible CBAM exposure. The dominant E risk is **stakeholder/regulatory permission to increase cadence** (debris wash-up, blast noise, marine effects), not carbon pricing.
- **Controversy:** 2018 "Humanity Star" (unauthorised light-pollution object, criticised by astronomers) set an unhelpful precedent for scientific-community trust. Pre-revenue impact, but shows weak external-consultation instincts.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

- **Process safety is the binding constraint.** FY2025 10-K flags that "manufacturing operations are hazardous at times and may expose us to safety risks, including environmental risks and health and safety." The **Neutron Stage 1 tank rupture during hydrostatic qualification testing** (2026 8-K) is the concrete proof point — reporting states **no injuries and no significant facility damage**, i.e. containment worked, but it evidences a high-energy test regime at scale.
- **Mission-assurance record:** Electron history includes the Sept-2023 in-flight anomaly; the FAA-required mishap investigation and return-to-flight were completed in **under three weeks** with FAA authorisation — a *positive* signal on regulatory responsiveness and safety-culture transparency.
- **Human capital:** Headcount ~2,600 growing fast via Mynaric/GEOST-type deals; integration and attrition are the live risks. Median pay $78k (FY2025); no evidence found of union disputes or strikes.
- **Social licence/"controversial weapons" screen:** Defense is now structural — SDA Tranche 2 satellites (~$515M), HASTE hypersonic test, and a **Golden Dome** role in partnership with Raytheon. This does not breach any "controversial weapons" (cluster munitions/APM/DU) screen, but it **does** create exclusion risk for Article 8/9 and Nordic/European mandates with defence-revenue thresholds, and it is politically polarised (Senate oversight letters on Golden Dome).

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **6/10**

Positives:
- **Single class of common stock — no dual-class structure.** Founder influence tracks economic ownership (rare and shareholder-friendly vs. SpaceX/Blue Origin-style control).
- All directors **other than Peter Beck are independent** per the proxy; audit by Deloitte; no restatements, no disclosed material weaknesses in the sources reviewed.
- Founder stake ~7.5% (down from ~10%) — still a meaningful alignment block.

Negatives:
- **CEO/Chairman duality:** Beck is President, CEO *and* Chairman — combined with founder-personality dependency (Neutron architecture, strategy, external voice). Key-person risk is real.
- **Weak pay-for-performance:** FY2025 CEO pay $6.83M, of which **88% is *time-based* RSUs** — no PSUs, and named executives received **no non-equity incentive payments**. Fixed $800k salary under the amended employment agreement (Mar 2026). Alignment is via equity ownership, not performance hurdles.
- **Say-on-pay support only ~80%** (2025 meeting) — a 20% dissent is meaningfully above the ~90%+ norm and signals investor discomfort with the compensation programme.
- 2025 holding-company reorganisation adds structural complexity; 62 risk factors, incl. new performance-based-payment and tariff risks, with **no new ESG-specific risk factor** — disclosure lags peers.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US:** FAA launch/re-entry licensing (14 CFR Part 450) and mishap oversight — the de facto ESG regulator; ITAR/EAR export control; FCPA/anti-kickback exposure via defence prime subcontracts; CMMC/DoD cyber. SEC climate rule remains stayed and would in any case be scaled for a smaller reporting company — **low near-term disclosure obligation**, which partly explains the disclosure lag. Notably, FAA has *proposed* removing 13 launch-related environmental requirements — near-term compliance cost down, tail reputational/litigation risk up.
- **New Zealand:** EEZ Act marine consent (EPA) for LC-1; RMA/regional consents; NZ climate-reporting standards apply only to large NZ entities — likely **not** binding on RKLB's NZ subsidiaries, but a 2028+ watch item. Rising launch-rate limits reduce regulatory friction.
- **EU:** No manufacturing footprint of consequence → **minimal CSRD/CBAM direct scope**. Indirect CSRD pressure arrives via European institutional investors and any EU defence-prime customers.
- **No ESG-linked debt covenants identified** (equipment financing covenants; one such covenant risk factor was *dropped* in FY2025).

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (governance-led, disclosure-lagging)

Not a Laggard on substance: single-class shares, independent-majority board, credible safety-culture response to anomalies, constructive regulator relationships, low absolute environmental footprint. But it is **not a Leader**: no ESG report since the 2021 Impact Report, no emissions data, no CDP, CEO/Chair duality, time-based-only equity, 20% say-on-pay dissent, and a founder-centric control culture. Third-party agency scores were not retrievable within budget — treat the rating as bottom-up, evidence-based, not score-anchored.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (slightly negative for ESG-mandated capital, positive-neutral for generalists)

- **For the thesis (Neutron-delay, defence-backed growth), ESG is not the swing factor — but two ESG-adjacent items are genuine risk vectors:** (i) **process safety / mission assurance** — the tank rupture is the second time hardware reliability has re-rated the stock, and further qualification failures would hit both the launch-services and defence-contracts narratives; (ii) **key-person and governance concentration** — Beck's combined CEO/Chair role and the stock-sale cadence (~$750m in 2026) create headline and succession risk that a 2.6-beta name prices harshly.
- **Defence ramp cuts both ways:** Golden Dome/HASTE/SDA support the revenue story but progressively narrow the ESG-constrained shareholder base and invite political oversight risk.
- **Screening verdict:** passes "controversial weapons" screens (no cluster munitions/APM/DU); fails or requires monitoring under defence-revenue-threshold and climate-disclosure screens. Expect **below-average Sustainalytics-style disclosure sub-scores** to persist until a refreshed sustainability report with Scope 1/2/3 data is published.
- **Engagement asks:** (1) publish GHG inventory + safety metrics (TRIR/LTIR) annually; (2) split Chair/CEO or appoint independent lead director; (3) introduce PSUs with mission-assurance/safety and ROIC hurdles; (4) formal cadence-linked environmental monitoring at Mahia/Wallops.

**Bottom line:** ESG does not change the investment conclusion on RKLB, but the *substance* beneath the ESG label — safety execution and governance discipline — is directly load-bearing for a stock whose valuation rests on flawless Neutron execution. Neutral on the thesis; monitor the safety/disclosure trajectory rather than the ratings.
