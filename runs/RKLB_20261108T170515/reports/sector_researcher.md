# Sector Brief — Rocket Lab USA (RKLB)
**Sector:** Industrials → Aerospace & Defense → Space launch / space systems
**Price $53.07 | Market cap $31.8B | Beta 2.61 | TTM revenue ~$769M (≈41x EV/S)**

---

## 1. Sector Overview

RKLB competes in orbital launch and space systems, a sub-sector of Aerospace & Defense that is growing far faster than its parent. Global orbital launch attempts reached roughly 236 in 2026 YTD (325 in full-year 2025), but SpaceX alone accounts for ~116 attempts (~49%), and its Falcon 9 reusable pricing (~$3,000/kg to LEO vs ~$54,000/kg on Shuttle) sets the marginal price for the entire industry. Demand is being pulled by two engines: commercial constellation replenishment and a step-change in US defence space spending — the FY27 budget request includes $17.9B for Golden Dome and $19.1B of Air Force space procurement, with Space Force Golden Dome-supporting program funding up to $1.4B from $675M. Against this, the listed space pure-plays have just de-rated violently: the SpaceX IPO (SPCX, $1.77T market cap) in mid-2026 catalysed a sector-wide rally to a 134% median peak gain, followed by a 58% drawdown in the basket; RKLB is ~65–70% below its May 2026 record. The sector is early-cycle on demand but late-stage on multiple.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Defence budget inflection.** FY27 Golden Dome request of $17.9B and space procurement of $19.1B; Space Force Golden Dome-support programs up 107% YoY ($675M → $1.4B). This is the fastest-growing line item in a US defence budget that is otherwise growing mid-single digits — highly relevant to RKLB's satellite components, Photon bus and Neutron (national-security medium launch).
- **Launch demand outrunning supply at the small/medium end.** RKLB flew 19 Electron missions in 2026 with a record $2.36B backlog (Q2'26) — backlog covers ~3.9x TTM revenue, an unusually strong visibility ratio for a hardware company.
- **Structural cost deflation expanding the addressable market.** Cost/kg to LEO has fallen ~95% vs Shuttle-era pricing, converting previously uneconomic use cases (constellation services, in-orbit manufacturing, responsive defence) into real demand — a secular volume tailwind even as unit prices fall.

---

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **SpaceX is a structural price-setter, not a peer.** At ~49% of global launch attempts and with Starship scaling, SpaceX caps industry pricing power and can compress RKLB's medium-launch economics before Neutron ever reaches orbit. Near-monopoly economics in the profit pool's largest segment.
- **Execution risk concentrated in one program.** Neutron's debut has slipped again to NET Q2 2027 (carbon-composite first-stage tank/dome defects) — its fourth schedule reset. Roughly a third of the RKLB thesis rests on a vehicle that has not flown.
- **Multiple compression and dilution.** After a ~58% drawdown in space equities post-IPO, the group still trades at venture-like multiples (RKLB ~41x TTM sales) while the $8B Iridium acquisition ($27.00 cash + stock per IRDM share) adds leverage, integration risk and equity issuance into a sector whose cost of capital just rose.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **#2 in Western launch, and the only credible non-SpaceX small-launch franchise.** Electron's 19 missions in 2026 and its reliability record are a genuine switching-cost moat with institutional and defence customers who demand launch redundancy.
- **Vertically integrated across launch + spacecraft + components** — a model only SpaceX and now (post-Iridium) RKLB possess. Iridium adds ~$450M+ of high-margin recurring service revenue and a proprietary spectrum/network asset, structurally lifting group gross margin and reducing dependence on lumpy launch contracts.
- **Best-in-class margin trajectory:** +2,540bps of gross margin expansion in four years is the strongest in the cohort; Q2'26 adjusted EBITDA loss narrowed to $8.8M, indicating the core is near cash breakeven ex-Neutron spend.
- **The caveat:** strong *relative* to peers, structurally *weak* relative to SpaceX on unit cost. RKLB's position is strong only so long as the market requires a second supplier — which, for defence redundancy reasons, it currently does.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE** (fundamentals) — with high intra-sector dispersion

Demand structure (defence space budgets growing ~2x the rate of the broader defence budget; cost-driven TAM expansion; launch supply constrained below medium-class) is among the best in Industrials. RKLB's growth/margin combination is the best risk-adjusted expression of that theme outside SpaceX.

**Two qualifications to carry into the memo:** (1) the *profit pool* is unattractive even though the *revenue pool* is attractive — SpaceX captures the majority of launch economics and sets price, so sector revenue growth does not automatically translate to sector returns; (2) multiple risk remains the dominant near-term driver, with a 2.61 beta and a group that has already shown it can halve. **Key catalyst:** Q3'26 results (scheduled Monday, 9 Nov 2026) and any Neutron schedule slippage beyond Q2 2027.
