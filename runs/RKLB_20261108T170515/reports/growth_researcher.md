## Rocket Lab USA (RKLB) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $53.07 | Market cap ~$31.8B | Sector: Industrials / Aerospace & Defense**

---

### 1. Growth Story

Rocket Lab is the only vertically integrated, publicly traded pure-play on the *commercialization of space* — the #2 Western launch provider by cadence (Electron), the largest merchant supplier of satellite components (reaction wheels, solar, avionics, composites), and a spacecraft prime (Photon), all under one roof. The thesis hinges on **Neutron**, an 8-ton partially reusable medium-lift vehicle that converts the company from a niche small-launch shop into a direct competitor to Falcon 9 in the constellation-deployment and national-security market — a TAM roughly 10–20× Electron's. The transformation is already visible in the numbers: record Q2'26 revenue of $234M (+62% YoY), record backlog of **$2.36B (+137% YoY)**, and gross margin expanding from −3% to 36% in four years. The stock has fallen **65% from its May 2026 high of $150** to $53 after Neutron's debut slipped again — to 2027 — creating an unusually wide gap between improving fundamentals and a collapsing multiple.

### 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **Launch**: global launch services ~$10–12B today, on track toward $25–30B by the mid-2030s as constellations, defense (Golden Dome, SDA), and commercial LEO scale. Neutron's medium-lift segment (Falcon 9's home turf) alone is ~$6–8B/yr and structurally supply-constrained outside SpaceX.
- **Space Systems**: satellite manufacturing and components ~$25–30B/yr. RKLB is already one of the largest independent component merchants — a picks-and-shovels position that wins whether or not Neutron succeeds.
- **Penetration**: TTM revenue of $769M against a $35–40B combined SAM is **~2%**. Even the 2030E consensus $2.7B implies only ~6% penetration. **TAM is not the constraint — execution is.** Notably, Space Systems is now ~60% of revenue and 43% government, which de-risks the model relative to a pure launch bet.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q3'26 earnings — November 9, 2026 (3 days away).** Consensus $257.2M revenue (+66% YoY), EPS −$0.07. Watch: backlog trajectory off $2.36B, Electron cadence, and any Neutron schedule language. Highest near-term variance event.
- **Neutron first flight, 2027.** The single largest re-rating catalyst. A clean orbital insertion would validate the $1.8B+ 2028 revenue line and likely compress the risk premium sharply. Delayed Oct 14, 2026 due to voids in a carbon-composite first-stage propellant tank.
- **Neutron cadence ramp 2027–2028** — prior plan was 3 flights in 2026 / 5 in 2027; the slipped schedule pushes the "5 flights" milestone into 2028. Confirmation of a Wallops pad flow rate is the key proof point.
- **National security awards** (NSSL Lane 1 task orders, SDA Tranche 3, Golden Dome intercept/tracking layers) — large, fixed-price, multi-year and the primary driver of the 137% backlog growth.
- **HASTE hypersonic suborbital** — a quiet, high-margin defense franchise with rising DoD demand and no SpaceX competition.

### 5. Alternative Data Signals

**Supporting the thesis:**
- **Hiring**: ~150 open roles in Long Beach alone, plus dedicated reqs at **Wallops Island, VA (Neutron launch site — "Test and Launch Operations Engineer")** and Middle River, MD (Space Systems composites). Footprint expansion into the Neutron pad is a real-time proxy for production ramp, not retrenchment.
- **Backlog QoQ**: +20.2% in Q1'26 and +6.1% in Q2'26 — bookings are compounding faster than revenue, the cleanest leading indicator available for this name.
- **Revenue beat consistency**: 7 of 7 quarters beat consensus revenue, typically by 1–7%. Management guides conservatively.

**Refuting / cautioning:**
- **Google Trends**: US search interest peaked at 100 in late May 2026 and has collapsed to **~29–30** (down ~70%) — retail attention has fully washed out. Contrarian-positive, but confirms the momentum crowd has left.
- **Price action**: A 13% single-day drop on Nov 5 ($52 → $46) followed by a +15% rebound to $53 in one session. Beta of 2.6 means position sizing matters more than the call itself.
- **Insider/dilution signal**: share count up 24.5% in five quarters — the equity is being used as currency to fund Neutron, which caps per-share value creation.

---

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (high-volatility, position-sized)

This is a genuine frontier-innovation franchise: 62% growth at scale, gross margins up 39 points in four years, a $2.36B backlog, near-breakeven EPS in 2027, and a 2030 EPS estimate that analysts have raised 71%. On a growth-adjusted basis, 33× 2026E sales against 59% growth is **0.56× — cheap**, and the median analyst target of $115 implies +117%.

But I stop short of Strong Buy for three reasons: (1) **Rule of 40 is +13.7, not 40** — this is a capex-and-cash-burn story, not yet a self-funding compounder; (2) **Neutron is now ~3 years behind its original 2024 target**, and the carbon-composite tank defect is a hard manufacturing problem, not a paperwork delay; (3) at 63× 2030E EPS, the market already discounts a decade of ~30% compounding, so the margin for error is thin. The drawdown has improved the risk/reward materially — I would build the position in tranches, adding aggressively only on evidence of a Neutron vehicle **on the pad at Wallops**.

---

### 7. Key Risks

- **Neutron execution (primary risk).** Third schedule slip. If first flight moves to 2028, the 2027E $1.36B and 2028E $1.83B revenue lines fall materially and the 33× sales multiple becomes indefensible.
- **Competitive window closing.** SpaceX rideshare compresses Electron pricing; Starship's success could reset medium-lift economics; Relativity Terran R, Stoke Nova, Blue Origin New Glenn, and Firefly all arrive in the same 2027–28 window. Neutron may reach market *last* among medium-lift entrants.
- **Cash burn and dilution.** −$371M TTM FCF, −$110M in Q2'26 alone, with $26M/qtr capex plus Neutron development. Further equity raises (and the 24.5% share growth already incurred) dilute the per-share growth story.
- **Multiple compression.** 33× forward sales with negative earnings leaves no valuation floor; a 50% de-rating is a normal event for this stock (it just did one).
- **Contract concentration / fixed-price risk.** 43% government revenue, much of it fixed-price development work — margin write-downs are a recurring industry hazard (see Boeing/RTX).
- **Data gaps in this analysis:** balance sheet cash/debt not retrieved, so EV is approximated at market cap; peer multiples not directly comparable (only SpaceX — itself in the sector list — is a true peer, and no standalone launch comparables trade publicly at scale).
