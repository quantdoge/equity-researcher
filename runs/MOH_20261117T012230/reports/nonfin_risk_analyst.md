## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE** (with specific blemishes)

**Supporting evidence:**
- **No CEO/Chairman duality.** Molina maintains a separate **Lead Independent Director** role formalized in its Corporate Governance Guidelines, and the board has explicitly stated support for an independent chairman. Zubretsky sits as "President, CEO & Director" — not chair.
- **Board expertise is genuinely relevant.** Current directors (Brasier, Grohowski, Lockhart MD, Orlando, Orlando, Romney, Schapiro, Soistman, Wolf, Zoretic) include deep payer/healthcare operators — Lockhart (physician), Soistman (ex-Centene), Zoretic. Director refreshes have occurred (Zoretic added recently).
- **Strong equity alignment at the top.** FY2025 CEO comp of **$18.3M** ($1.6M salary + $16.2M stock awards ≈ **89% equity-weighted**), down 16% from $21.9M. CEO holds **~366,965 shares (~$85M)** — real skin in the game. Contract extended through 2027 with comp unchanged, providing continuity through the current restructuring.
- **Litigation defense held.** A federal judge **dismissed the securities class action on 16-Sep-2026**, and the parallel **shareholder derivative suit against the board was voluntarily dropped**. Boards that survive a derivative oversight claim generally had defensible committee processes.

**Blemishes:**
- **Pay-for-performance mismatch on the short-term element.** Zubretsky received a performance cash bonus at **105% of target** for FY2025 — the same year the stock fell 27% in a single session and the company posted a ~$160M Q4 loss. The compensation committee did not meaningfully reprice for a forecast failure.
- **Board staleness.** Ronna Romney has served since **2003 (~23 years)** and is vice-chair. Long tenure plus a small (9-seat) board limits refreshment.
- **Legal-officer monetization during litigation.** The CLO sold $4.9M of stock across 2026 while securities litigation was live (see §3).

**Pending regulatory/legislative exposures (far larger than the fines above):**
- **Federal Medicaid restructuring — the single biggest threat.** OBBBA §71115 freezes/caps Medicaid provider taxes at 4-Jul-2025 levels, and CMS rules curb state-directed payments. CMS estimates **~$510B of federal Medicaid funding reduction over 10 years**; Commonwealth Fund estimates **~$226B and 2.4M coverage losses**. Molina is overwhelmingly a Medicaid premium business — this is a direct hit to the revenue base and to state rate-setting capacity.
- **Medicaid work requirements effective January 2027** — churn, redetermination and eligibility-system risk. Management is framing this as rate-positive (six states raising rates partly for this reason), but it is an execution and compliance event.
- **Medicare Advantage Part D exit by 31-Dec-2026** — announced February 2026, but **members were not notified until October 2026**. An eight-month disclosure-to-notification gap on a senior/vulnerable population is a plausible CMS marketing-guideline and notice-timing exposure, and it was covered critically in the press.
- **ACA Marketplace retreat** — ~$1B less Marketplace premium planned for 2027 and a ~63% membership decline, against expiration of enhanced subsidies. Pricing/exit decisions in a repricing market invite state DOI scrutiny.
- **CY2027 MA rate** finalized at a net **+2.48%** — constructive, but Molina is shrinking MA anyway.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Actuarial forecasting / IBNP reserve integrity — the proven failure point.** The 6-Feb-2026 session took the stock from $176.84 to $131.72 (**−25.5% in one day**, −27% on the print) after FY2026 guidance and a $160M Q4-2025 loss exposed a "dislocation between premium rates and medical cost trend." The class action alleged Molina's models "could not sufficiently forecast utilization trends." This is a systems/data/actuarial-capability defect, not a market accident — and it is the one risk that has already crystallized.
- **Monoline program concentration with no diversification buffer.** Effectively all revenue comes from CMS and state Medicaid agencies across ~19 states, on 3–5 year contracts the state may decline to renew. Molina is simultaneously **exiting MA-PD** and **shrinking Marketplace ~63%**, concentrating the company further into Medicaid at the precise moment Washington is restructuring Medicaid financing.
- **Simultaneous multi-front operational change.** Unwinding MA-PD by 31-Dec-2026, cutting $1B of ACA premium for 2027, absorbing Medicaid work requirements in January 2027, and onboarding the Florida Children's Medical Services plan (live 1-Oct-2026) all land in the same two quarters — claims-platform, eligibility-system and member-transition execution risk.
- **Data privacy / cyber.** Molina shut down its patient portal after a flaw allowed **unauthenticated access to patient medical claims data (PHI)**; plaintiffs' firms are investigating. HIPAA and state-AG exposure is live and, per available sources, not yet resolved.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **MODERATE-HIGH**

- **The MA-PD exit notification is the worst item.** Disclosing to investors in February but informing members in October produced the damaging "eight-month silence" framing in national personal-finance coverage. Damage to a low-income/senior brand is slow to heal and directly feeds state procurement scoring.
- **The cost-concealment narrative is now permanent public record** via the class action complaint, plaintiff-firm marketing, and the 2026 coverage — even though the case was dismissed.
- **Marketplace exits** add to a "insurer abandoning members" narrative at open enrollment.
- **Dollar-small but culture-relevant:** the Washington OIC fine, the Florida OIG CMP settlement, and repeated "enrollment and billing error" findings speak to compliance/back-office control quality in a business whose entire moat is state relationships.
- **Offsets:** MolinaCares community giving, high quality scores (Medicaid 92.9%, Medicare 94.0%), and the Florida CMS award are real reputational assets with state partners.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Governance is sound and litigation has largely cleared, which argues against Critical. But four factors keep this at High: (i) a **demonstrated failure of a core control** (actuarial forecasting) that has already destroyed 27% of market value in a day and could recur; (ii) **existential-scale regulatory restructuring** of the company's dominant revenue program; (iii) **unanimous insider selling with zero buying** at the highs; and (iv) a heavy concurrent operational work-load across four programs.

---

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Repeat actuarial/guidance failure.** Monitor IBNP reserve development and prior-period development each quarter. A second guidance reset would validate that the Feb-2026 event was a control failure, not a one-off.
2. **CLO sold $4.94M in 2026**, including $1.62M on 10-Nov — five days after the 6-Nov 8-K and days before a director selling cluster. Escalate for 10b5-1 plan verification and trading-window review.
3. **100% sell-side insider activity with zero purchases** concentrated in a 6-day window after a 72% rally.
4. **FY2025 bonus paid at 105% of target despite the guidance failure** — compensation committee did not reprice for a value-destroying outcome.
5. **Eight-month member-notification lag on the MA-PD exit** — CMS notice/marketing compliance and vulnerable-population conduct risk.
6. **Unauthenticated PHI exposure on the patient portal** — unresolved HIPAA/state-AG/class exposure.
7. **Litigation dismissed "for now"** with leave to amend; derivative suit dropped. Watch for an amended complaint or a new class period around the 2026 guidance events.
