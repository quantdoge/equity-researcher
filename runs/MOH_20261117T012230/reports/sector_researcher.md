## SECTOR BRIEF — US Government-Sponsored Managed Care
**Target: Molina Healthcare (MOH)** | Sector: Healthcare → Managed Health Care Plans | Data as of 16-Nov-2026 (close $233.09, mkt cap $12.2B)

---

### 1. Sector Overview

Molina competes in US government-sponsored managed care — Medicaid MCOs, Medicare Advantage, and ACA Marketplace — a ~$1.29T revenue pool across the six listed comparables (UNH, CI, ELV, CNC, HUM, MOH). The sector is **highly concentrated but structurally commoditised**: top-3 revenue share of 71.4% and an HHI of ~2,235, yet revenue is not truly consolidated because UNH/Optum, CI/Evernorth and ELV/Carelon blend non-insurance earnings. After a 2021–23 margin boom (MOH peak operating margin 4.62% in FY23), the sector is in its **deepest underwriting trough in a decade**: FY25 operating margins collapsed to 1.7% at MOH, 1.1% at HUM and −3.9% at CNC, driven by medical-cost normalisation post-redetermination, Medicare Advantage rate/risk-model reset, and ACA subsidy expiry. **Cycle position: late trough, early recovery.** 2026 is the bottom year (MOH 9M26 revenue −5.3% YoY to $32.3B; 9M26 EPS $2.20 vs $8.92 FY25), with 2027 state rate resets now moving ahead of trend for the first time since 2023.

**Porter's Five Forces — unfavourable:** Buyer power *very high* (state Medicaid agencies are monopsonists that set rates and can claw back margin via procurement); supplier power *high* (hospitals, specialty drugs, behavioural health); entry barriers *high* for scale players (capital, actuarial infrastructure, RFP track record) but *low* for price competition at each state rebid; rivalry *intense*; substitutes *low* (government programmes are statutory).

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **The rate/trend spread is inflecting positive.** MOH raised its 2026 Medicaid rate outlook to 4.5% (from 4.0%) and now guides **2027 rate increases of ~6% against medical trend of 5.5–6.0%** — the first non-negative spread in three years. Industry-wide, CMS's 2026–27 rate-development guide enforces actuarially sound rates, giving states political cover to fund increases.
- **Aggressive self-help is compressing the cost base.** MOH is exiting its Medicare Advantage prescription-drug (MA-PDP) business, is holding SG&A near $2.95B annualised (vs $3.01B FY25 on a larger revenue base), and has cut diluted shares 5.1% YoY (51.5M vs 53.6M). Management has laid out **preliminary 2027 EPS building blocks above $9.50** versus a ~$3 trough in 2026 — a ~3x earnings recovery.
- **De-rated sector + procurement churn creates share-shift opportunity.** MOH fell 57% peak-to-trough (Jul-25 $306 → Mar-26 $131) before rebounding 78% to $233. MOH's 5-year revenue CAGR of **18.5% is the highest in the comparable set**, and multi-state Medicaid re-procurements (several large states rebidding 2026–27) allow growth well above the ~2–4% underlying Medicaid enrolment trend.

---

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **OBBBA is a structural, dated overhang.** Federal Medicaid funding reductions to states begin **October 2027**, alongside work requirements. MOH explicitly expects **~6% Medicaid membership attrition** from work requirements; one elimination provision alone is projected to remove ~700k people from Medicaid by 2034. States facing budget gaps are already raising managed-care "savings" targets and benefit changes — a direct threat to the 2027 rate upside.
- **Medical cost trend remains elevated and volatile.** MOH raised its 2026 trend assumption to 5.5% from 5.0% mid-year; PwC projects group-market **trend of 9.0% for 2027, the highest in 17 years**. MOH's implied medical cost ratio has deteriorated ~370bp structurally — 91.0% FY25 vs 87.3% FY23, with Medicaid MCR at 93.0% in Q3-26. Q4-25 alone produced a −$162M operating loss and −$2.77 EPS on reserve strengthening.
- **ACA Marketplace shock is unresolved.** Enhanced premium tax credits lapsed entering 2026, driving a **≥2.9M national enrollment decline** and a sicker remaining risk pool. MOH's Marketplace segment (a 2021–24 growth engine) now faces adverse selection and pricing risk, and the 2027 marketplace rule changes add further uncertainty.

### 5. Molina's Competitive Position — **AVERAGE** (strong franchise, sub-par economics)

**Strengths:** Pure-play government-programme specialist with a 40-year Medicaid operating record; top-3 national Medicaid MCO by membership and one of the "Big Five"; highest 5-year revenue CAGR in the group (18.5%); lowest absolute cost structure and highest contract win-rate in low-income/expansion-state markets.

**Weaknesses:** No Optum/Evernorth/Carelon-style diversified earnings buffer, so **100% of earnings are exposed to state rate-setting and CMS policy** — the reason MOH's margin swing (−248bp) exceeded ELV (−96bp) and CI (−49bp). Sub-scale absolute revenue ($45.4B, 3.5% revenue share) limits fixed-cost leverage vs UNH/CI. No demonstrated pricing power — the 370bp MCR deterioration since FY23 was absorbed rather than passed through.

**Verdict:** Structurally sound, cyclically leveraged. MOH is the highest-quality *pure* Medicaid operator but is a price-taker with below-median margins; its earnings are a levered bet on the 2027 rate reset landing at ~6% against sub-6% trend.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL** (improving bias)

The sector is at a cyclical trough with the **direction of change now positive** — rate resets above trend, self-help levers (MA-PDP exit, SG&A, buybacks), and a 2027 EPS recovery already guided. That argues against "Unattractive." But the sector cannot be rated "Attractive" on today's numbers: 2026 revenue is *shrinking*, the peer median operating margin is only 2.5%, and OBBBA's October 2027 funding cuts plus ~6% membership attrition from work requirements cap the recovery's durability and timing. Concentration is high but competition is on price against a monopsony buyer, and returns on capital are structurally thin (~1–4% operating margins leave almost no buffer for a 100bp trend surprise). **Sector: Neutral — own it for the 2027 rate-driven earnings recovery, not for structural quality.** Note the market has already begun discounting that recovery: at $233.09 MOH trades at ~24x the >$9.50 preliminary 2027 EPS, versus ~15–18x its historical norm — leaving limited margin of safety if 2027 rates disappoint.
