# Molina Healthcare (MOH) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $233.09 (16-Nov-2026) | Market cap ~$12.0B | 51.5M diluted shares | No dividend**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Molina is a pure-play government-payer MCO (Medicaid ~85% of premium, plus Medicare D-SNP and a shrinking Marketplace book) that is executing a **margin-recovery, not a growth, thesis**. After a catastrophic 2025–early-2026 (consolidated MCR blew out to ~91%, Q4-25 EPS of –$2.75 vs. $0.43 expected, and a –25% one-day gap on 6-Feb-26 to $131.72), management has stabilised the rate/trend spread and is now bridging toward **"at least $9.50" of 2027 EPS** off a ~$5.25 base — driven by four self-help levers: Florida CMS Kids reaching break-even (+$1.50), exiting general-issue MAPD (+$1.00), Marketplace reaching break-even (+$0.75), and $4.00 of embedded earnings. This is textbook **mean reversion on a policy-set spread — not disruptive innovation**, and the stock has already re-rated **+92% off the $121 low**.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

- **TAM:** US Medicaid spend ~$900B annually, with managed care covering ~70% of the ~75M enrollees → an **MCO premium pool of ~$450–550B**. Molina's ~$43B of premium is roughly **8% share**.
- **The pool is shrinking, not growing.** Medicaid enrollment peaked near 91M (2023) and has fallen post-redetermination; Molina's own Medicaid membership is **–2% since YE25** with ~6% attrition assumed for 2027 (work requirements). **TAM growth is now rate-driven (+4–6%/yr), not volume-driven** — which mathematically caps revenue CAGR in the mid-single digits.
- **Best remaining runway — dual eligibles:** ~12.5M duals nationally, ~6.5M in D-SNP. Molina's MA book is only ~221K (Sept-26), so **penetration is ~3%.** Management is concentrating Medicare here (1.4% pre-tax margin, $1.25 EPS in 2026) — genuinely under-penetrated, but this is a small absolute number and Molina is shrinking its MA footprint to 43 plans from 83.
- **Marketplace is being abandoned, not scaled:** 2027 footprint down to 7 states, still guided to a –$0.75 loss.
- **Verdict:** Large TAM, **near-zero incremental penetration velocity**, and no proprietary technology or data network effect. The moat is state RFP relationships and scale in a 2–3% margin business — real but not compounding.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **4-Feb-2027 — Q4-26 results + first formal 2027 guidance.** Consensus Q4 EPS $0.52 (after a ~$1 Florida drag). Converting the "$9.50 framework" into formal guidance is the single biggest re-rating event. *(~11 weeks)*
2. **Florida CMS Kids ramp to break-even through 2027** — 99% MCR / 5% pre-tax loss in Q4-26, needs 500bp (200bp reserve roll-off, **300bp operational**). Worth +$1.50 EPS. *(quarterly, 2027)*
3. **Georgia and Texas contract litigation** — **$1.3B of premium** delayed by court proceedings and explicitly *excluded* from the 2027 outlook. A favourable ruling is pure upside. *(unscheduled)*
4. **2027 Medicaid rate notifications.** Jan-1 renewal cohort = 55% of premium; draft rates known for 80% at ~7%, overall ~6% vs. 5.5–6% trend. Watch for final rates clearing trend. *(state budget cycles, Q1–Q2 2027)*
5. **ACA subsidy policy / 2027 Marketplace open enrolment** in the 7 retained states — a clean break-even removes the –$0.75 drag. *(Q4-26/Q1-27)*
6. **D-SNP build-out and M&A optionality** — management prioritises organic, but is historically acquisitive (Magellan, AgeWell, MyChoice).

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

**Molina is not a growth investment — it is a levered bet on state Medicaid rate cycles, and the easy money has been made.**

*The bull case:* 2026–2029 EPS CAGR of ~59% ($5.26 → $21.04) off a depressed base, three consecutive beats, positive revisions, a self-help bridge that doesn't require Medicaid margin improvement, and a Florida contract that goes from –5% pre-tax margin to break-even. At 11x 2029E, that's not demanding.

*Why I won't pay up here:* (1) Revenue is **declining** in 2026 and compounds at only 4.4% to 2030 — the growth engine is arithmetic, not market capture; (2) Rule of 40 of 10 means this business structurally cannot fund its own growth; (3) the entire EPS recovery hinges on a **50–100bp rate/trend spread** that is set by state legislatures, not by any moat — 50bp of Medicaid MLR is ~$200M pre-tax, or ~$3/share; (4) at 24x 2027E and ~14–15x 2027E EBITDA there is **no valuation cushion** versus ELV (12.7x) or a 2.9x P/B versus CNC's 1.83x; (5) the stock trades **above its own street median target** after a +92% move. 

**Action: Hold.** I would become a buyer on a pullback toward **$180–190** (≈19x 2027E, ~1.1x the bear-case book value), or on a formal 2027 guide above $10.50 that includes Florida at break-even *plus* Medicaid rate > trend. I would sell on a Florida ramp slippage or a 2027 rate print below 5.5%.

## 7. Key Risks

- **Rate/trend mismatch (the whole thesis in one number).** 2026 rates are 4.5% vs. 5.5% trend — Medicaid is *currently underfunded*. Recovery depends on state budgets improving, not on Molina's execution.
- **Medicaid eligibility risk.** ~6% membership attrition assumed for 2027 on work requirements; 80% of states lean retention-friendly, but a harsher federal interpretation cuts premium volume directly.
- **Florida execution risk.** $6B run-rate, 115K members, needs 500bp of margin — and **200bp of that is reserve roll-off (accounting), leaving 300bp of genuinely operational improvement** Molina must earn. It's now ~13% of revenue and rising.
- **Balance sheet and capital allocation.** Net debt/capital **45%**, FCF of **–$636M in 2025**, no dividend, and **$1B of buybacks in 2025 at a ~$290 average** — buying back stock at nearly 2x today's price and immediately before a 60% drawdown. Management credibility on capital allocation is impaired.
- **Competitive/political.** Molina competes against UNH, ELV, CNC and Centene-scale players for state RFPs with no differentiated technology; CMS rate policy, risk-adjustment changes and star ratings are exogenous.
- **Valuation/positioning risk.** Above street median target, 44x trough EPS, 2.9x book against a depressed 11% ROE. Any guidance disappointment from here has a long way to fall.
