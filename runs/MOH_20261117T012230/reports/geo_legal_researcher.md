### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — OBBBA Medicaid reset: work requirements + eligibility verification (HIGH probability, MEDIUM-HIGH impact)**
The 2025 reconciliation law (OBBBA) imposes community-engagement (80 hrs/month) requirements on expansion adults **effective January 1, 2027**, plus new eligibility/verification conditions. Urban Institute projects **3.0–7.0 million expansion enrollees** lost from work requirements alone; Commonwealth Fund/KFF project ~7.3M losing ACA-related coverage in 2026 with 4.8M becoming uninsured. For a plan whose membership is overwhelmingly Medicaid, this is direct volume risk in exactly the expansion states where Molina is concentrated (CA, MI, OH, NM, KY, NV, VA, etc.).
*Mitigant:* rate adequacy is improving — states are proposing an **average ~7% Medicaid rate increase** for 2027, and management has publicly argued work requirements actually improve its rate position (Modern Healthcare). Molina's Q3 print showed Medicaid MCR stabilising at 93.0%.

**Risk 2 — Collapse of enhanced ACA premium tax credits (OCCURRED, MEDIUM impact)**
The enhanced PTCs expired **January 1, 2026**. KFF estimates average benchmark premiums would more than double absent the credits; CBO projects a decline in subsidised exchange enrollment. Molina's Marketplace segment (CA, FL, MI, NM, OH, TX, WA, UT, WI historically) faces membership attrition plus adverse morbidity mix and risk-adjustment swings. Marketplace is Molina's smallest but most volatile segment.

**Risk 3 — Medicare Advantage / Part D rate and program integrity (MEDIUM probability, MEDIUM impact)**
CY2027 MA final rate announcement delivered a **net +2.48%** (≈$13B) increase — a favourable swing from the 0.09% proposed — which supports 2027 revenue. But Molina is **exiting its Medicare Advantage prescription drug (MA-PD/Part D) business effective December 31, 2026**, announced February 2026 and communicated to members only in October — a decision that removes a loss-making line but also creates member-transition, CMS marketing/notice-compliance, and reputational risk. Ongoing exposure: CMS risk-adjustment data validation (RADV) audits, Star Ratings, and the CY2027 Part C/D final rule (published 2026-04-06) tightening risk adjustment.

**Secondary risk:** state procurement/rebid concentration. Molina's growth model depends on winning RFPs; a single lost state contract (or a protested award) can move consolidated revenue by several percent given ~10%+ concentration in CA/TX/WA.

**Regulatory posture:** primary regulators are (i) **CMS** (Medicare Advantage/Part D, Medicaid managed-care rules), (ii) **state Medicaid agencies** (contract, rate, and capitation approval — notably CA DHCS, Texas HHSC, Florida AHCA, Michigan MDHHS), and (iii) **state insurance departments** (market conduct, MLR rebates, licensure). Note: unlike fully-licensed commercial carriers, several of Molina's plans operate under Medicaid-only authorities — a structural difference that historically limited some state insurance oversight but is itself a licensure/scope risk (cf. the Massachusetts licensure allegations).

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Repeated False Claims Act / state fraud settlements** (TX $40M, MA $4.625M, FL $257K) across three separate jurisdictions → elevated **qui tam** probability. Molina's care-management timeliness and provider-capitation practices are the demonstrated weak points.
2. **Securities-disclosure credibility** — class action centers on medical cost trend assumptions; the 8-K of 2026-02-05 disclosed "medical cost pressure due to continued high levels of utilization." Expect heightened SEC/plaintiff scrutiny of reserve development (IBNR) commentary.
3. **Part D exit execution** — CMS notice, marketing, and formulary-transition rules are unforgiving; the eight-month gap between the February decision and October member letters is the kind of fact pattern that draws regulator letters.
4. **No FCPA, OFAC sanctions, AML, or foreign data-residency exposure** — a genuine positive; HIPAA/privacy exposure is domestic and no material breach was identified.
5. **Puerto Rico Promotora/fiscal exposure** — unverified; obtain disclosure before relying on any PR revenue figure.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Component ratings:**
- Jurisdictional/geopolitical (sanctions, expropriation, currency, trade): **LOW** — 100% U.S. revenue; strongest rule-of-law jurisdiction in the portfolio universe; no FX repatriation risk.
- Policy/regulatory dependence: **HIGH** — 100% of revenue is government-priced, government-renewed, and now being legislatively reshaped (OBBBA work requirements from 1/1/2027; PTC expiry already in force). No commercial diversification cushion.
- Litigation/enforcement: **MEDIUM** — a recurring FCA/AG settlement pattern and an active securities class action, but individual matter sizes ($0.26M–$40M) are small against $11B quarterly revenue.

**Bottom line:** Molina's risk is not geopolitical in the traditional sense — it is **legislative**. The two dominant swing factors for 2027 are (a) whether states implement work requirements more gently than the Urban Institute's 3–7M loss scenario and (b) whether the ~7% average Medicaid rate increases hold. Legal risk is a recurring cost of doing business rather than a solvency threat, but the Texas $40M care-timeliness precedent is the item most worth monitoring for replication in California and Florida, Molina's other large states.
