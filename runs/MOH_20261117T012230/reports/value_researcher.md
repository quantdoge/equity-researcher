## MOLINA HEALTHCARE (MOH) — VALUE INVESTMENT ANALYSIS
**Price: $233.09 | Market cap: $12.17B | EV: ~$11.9B | 52-wk range: $125–$236**

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW (borderline None)**

Molina is a pure-play government payer: Medicaid (largest segment, ~$30B), Medicare (dual-eligibles plus a MAPD book being exited), and ACA Marketplace. It serves ~5M low-income members across ~18–20 states, with 19,000 employees.

**What genuinely resembles a moat:**
- **Switching costs at the state level.** Medicaid managed-care contracts run multi-year, are competitively re-bid, and states face real member-disruption costs in changing plans. Incumbency is worth something.
- **Cost advantage.** Adjusted G&A is ~6.3–6.4% of premium — among the leanest in the industry. In a 1–2% net-margin business, that matters.
- **Efficient local scale** and a 40-year specialization in dual-eligibles and low-income populations.

**Why I cannot call it wide — the decisive test:** Molina has **no pricing power**. Premium is ~97% of revenue, medical costs are ~92–93% of premium, and the capitation rate is set administratively by state actuaries. The state captures the economics; Molina keeps the residual. That is a cost-plus contract, not a franchise.

The five-year record proves the point brutally:

| | 2020 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $19.4B | $45.4B (**+134%**) |
| Net income | $673M | $472M (**−30%**) |
| Operating margin | 5.55% | 1.72% |
| ROIC | — | 9.7% |

An 18% revenue CAGR delivered **lower absolute profit**. There is no operating leverage — gross margin fell from 13.8% to 9.0% and operating margin from 5.6% to 1.7%. Growth has been purchased with capital (goodwill + intangibles now $2.2B, **54% of equity**) rather than earned.

ROIC history confirms fragility: 25.8% (2022) → 23.8% (2023) → 22.4% (2024) → **9.7% (2025)**. A few hundred basis points of medical cost ratio wipes out sixteen points of return on capital. A durable moat does not behave that way. **The moat has clearly narrowed over five years.**

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **4 / 10**

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth | +15.1% | +6.6% | +19.3% | +11.8% |
| Gross margin | 12.3% | 12.7% | 11.7% | **9.0%** |
| Operating margin | 4.3% | 4.6% | 4.2% | **1.7%** |
| ROE | 26.7% | 25.9% | 26.2% | **11.6%** |
| ROIC | 25.8% | 23.8% | 22.4% | **9.7%** |
| FCF | $682M | $1,578M | $544M | **−$636M** |
| FCF / net income | 86% | 145% | 46% | **negative** |
| Owner earnings | $877M | $1,178M | $1,265M | $566M |

**Revenue quality: high. Earnings quality: poor.**

- **FCF is essentially unforecastable.** It swung from +$2.04B (2021) to +$682M to +$1.58B to −$636M. The driver is claims-reserve and working-capital timing (−$1.29B in 2025), not capital intensity (capex is only ~$100M/yr, ~0.2% of revenue). For an insurer this is normal — but it means FCF is a bad valuation anchor and Buffett's "owner earnings" ($566M in 2025) should be normalized, not extrapolated.
- **2026 is worse, not better, on a GAAP basis.** Nine-month 2026 revenue of $32.3B produced just $112M of net income — a **0.35% net margin**. Management's *adjusted* nine-month EPS is $4.75, implying ~$2.00/share of adjustments in Q1 2026 alone. Investors should treat the gap between GAAP and "adjusted" with suspicion here.
- **Balance sheet looks strong but is not as strong as it appears.** Cash and investments $8.26B against $3.95B debt → reported net cash of ~$298M. But most of that "cash" is claims reserves and statutory capital at insurance subsidiaries. **S&P downgraded Molina to BB− (junk)** in 2026, citing capital adequacy deteriorating to a *deficiency* at the 99.5% confidence level at YE2025. Interest coverage fell from 14.5x (2024) to 4.1x (2025) and is running near **2x annualized in 2026**.
- **Tangible book is thin:** $4.07B equity less $2.20B goodwill/intangibles = $1.87B, or **$36.9/share**. At $233 you pay **6.3x tangible book**.

**Normalized earnings power.** At mid-cycle — a 2.1% net margin on ~$46B of revenue (versus 2.9% in 2024 and 0.7% in 2026) — Molina earns **~$965M, or ~$18.70/share**. Owner earnings add back D&A (~$200M) less maintenance capex (~$110M): **~$1.05B, ~$20.30/share**.

**Scenario-weighted intrinsic value:**

| Scenario | Prob. | Normalized EPS | Multiple | Value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear — rate/trend gap persists, policy pressure | 30% | $11.00 | 11x | $121 |
| Base — partial recovery to ~1.9% margin | 45% | $16.00 | 13x | $208 |

**Weighted IV ≈ $208.** The current price sits ~12% *above* that, and only ~5% below the bull case. Even in the bull case — revenue of $50B, margins restored to peak, and a 15x multiple — you earn roughly **10–13% annually over three years**. In the base case you earn ~4–5%. There is **no margin of safety**; my required entry would be ~$165 (20–25% discount to IV).

Corroboration: the **median sell-side target is ~$220**, and the current price is *above* it. Targets range $163 (Deutsche Bank) to $255 (Mizuho). After a **+78% rally from the $131 low in March 2026**, the recovery is already in the price.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **C+ / B−**

**Strengths:** CEO Joe Zubretsky (since 2017) is a genuine operator. Revenue nearly tripled from ~$20B to $45B, GAAP EPS went from ~$4 to $20.42 at the 2024 peak, and G&A is held at ~6.4% of premium. He has also taken decisive portfolio action — exiting MAPD for 2027, shrinking the Marketplace footprint, and pivoting Medicare to dual-eligibles.

**Serious flaws:**
1. **Guidance credibility is damaged.** In 2025 management cut full-year guidance four times: from ~$22 → "no less than $19" → ~$14 → actual adjusted ~$10.65. Then in Q3 2026 it trimmed the 2027 premium outlook by $1.5B. This is not a management team with a firm grip on its own cost trend.
