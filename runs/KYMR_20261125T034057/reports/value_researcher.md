*Data note: the feed's market cap ($14.85B) uses the pre-offering share count (85.4M). Kymera priced an upsized **$1.75B offering at $150.37 on Nov 24, 2026** (11.64M new shares), so I use ~97M shares and ~$2.2B pro-forma net cash.*

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **None (pre-commercial)**
Kymera is a 273-person, clinical-stage targeted protein degradation company. FY2025 revenue of **$39.2M is collaboration income, not product revenue**; 2026 revenue (9M: $100.8M) is dominated by a lumpy Sanofi milestone (Q2 $65M, Q3 only $1.4M). There is no pricing power, no installed base, no switching cost, and no recurring cash flow — the five moat sources are all absent today.

The *only* genuine moat candidate is the TPD platform and the **wholly-owned KT-621 (oral STAT6 degrader)** franchise. BROADEN2 Phase 2b (Nov 2026) hit its primary endpoint with EASI reductions of **-72% mid-dose vs -27% placebo (+45% placebo-adjusted)**, low-dose -64%, high-dose -61% — Dupixent-like efficacy from a pill. That is a real, potentially best-in-class asset. It is **not yet a moat**: Phase 3 has not started, and the franchise is unprotected until approval and patent enforcement.

*A discriminating detail the tape is ignoring:* the **dose-response is non-monotonic** — the 200 mg arm (61%) underperformed the mid-dose (72%). A flat-to-inverse curve invites FDA scrutiny on dose selection and raises the probability that the Phase 2b "win" includes noise. I weight this as a genuine scientific yellow flag, not a footnote.

### 3. Valuation — **No margin of safety; price already exceeds my bull case's base**
Discounted cash flow is inapplicable (trailing FCF is -$234M). The correct method is probability-weighted rNPV:

- **KT-621, AD only:** ~$4.0B peak sales (sell-side consensus per STAT, Nov 2026), launch ~2030, 60% approval probability, ~30% after-tax cash margin, 12x terminal → **~$4.5–5B** risk-adjusted.
- **KT-621, expanded Type 2 (asthma/COPD/other):** adds $3–4B peak in a bull case → **~$6–7B** incremental risk-adjusted.
- **KT-474/IRAK4 (Sanofi-partnered, HS/AD):** $1.5–2B peak, tiered royalties to Kymera → **~$1.5–2.5B**.
- **Net cash:** ~$2.2B. Less PV of Phase 3 + commercial build (~$3–4B gross spend).

**Fair value range ≈ $8–14B equity, or ~$85–145/share.** At $173.92 the stock trades **20–100% above** the midpoint of that range, and above the *upper* end of my base-case rNPV. To justify $16.9B of equity you must underwrite ~$8B of peak KT-621 sales, ~65%+ approval probability, *and* near-wholly-owned economics — i.e., the bull case priced as the base case. Analyst targets ($132 RBC / $165 Truist-Jefferies / $191–201 Wells-Morgan Stanley-BTIG-Stifel; median ~$180) cluster around, not above, the current price — the re-rating has closed the gap the analysts were pointing at.

### 4. Management Quality — **Good operator, unproven allocator**
Founder-led (Nello Mainolfi, co-founder/CEO) — genuine scientific conviction and alignment, and the Sanofi IRAK4 deal was value-accretive validation of the platform (opt-in plus milestones rather than an empire-building M&A spree). Against that: capital allocation is entirely "raise and burn" — $1.75B raised at $150.37 to fund Phase 3 and a commercial build, with no buybacks, no dividend, and relentless dilution. Insider Form 144 filings for ~$13.7M of stock on **Nov 24, 2026 — the day of the +16% pop and the offering pricing** — warrant scrutiny even if 10b5-1 pre-scheduled.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**
This is a scientifically credible, founder-led platform with a potentially best-in-class oral immunology asset and a fortress balance sheet. It is *not* an investment I can underwrite at this price. My rules are explicit: a great business at a fair price is not a buy, and this is a **pre-revenue, cash-burning, binary-risk** business at a price that discounts the successful outcome of every remaining trial. The stock is up ~170% in twelve months and +46% in two sessions on a single 16-week, ~100-patient dataset with a non-monotonic dose response. Circle of competence fails, margin of safety is negative, and the moat is a hope rather than an evidence-based fact. **I would not own it here.** A price below ~$85–100 (roughly $8–10B market cap) would begin to offer a defensible discount to risk-adjusted value — that is where I would re-engage.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis
1. **Binary Phase 3 failure.** Ph2b EASI deltas in a small trial routinely fail to replicate; a Ph3 miss takes the equity to cash value (~$22/share).
2. **Dose-response inversion** (200 mg < mid-dose) — FDA may require an additional dose-ranging study, adding years and capital.
3. **Competitive crowding.** Oral JAKs, oral IL-23, and next-gen biologics are all racing into AD; Dupixent's incumbency plus payer pressure limits realized pricing.
4. **Continued dilution.** At ~$350M annual burn, further raises are near-certain if partnering economics disappoint; each 10% dilution removes ~$1.7B of per-share value.
5. **Partner dependency.** KT-474 economics run through Sanofi's priorities; a deprioritization removes ~$2B of value without Kymera's control.
6. **High-beta tape risk** (beta 2.03): the shares are a sentiment instrument on one molecule, and the offering priced 14% below the current price — the buyers of size demanded a discount.
7. **Insider selling into strength** immediately post-catalyst.

None of these gaps is large enough to change the verdict.*
