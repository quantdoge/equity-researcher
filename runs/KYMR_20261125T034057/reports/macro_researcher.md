# Macroeconomic Assessment — Kymera Therapeutics (KYMR)

**As of 25 November 2026.** KYMR: $173.92 close (24 Nov), market cap $14.85B, beta 2.03, 273 employees, Watertown MA. Clinical-stage, **zero product revenue, zero debt**.

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **TRANSITIONAL, tilting risk-off at the macro level while biotech is risk-on**

The dominant fact is that **the 2026 disinflation thesis has failed and the Fed has reversed course.** The Fed cut to an effective 3.63% by June 2026, then **hiked 25bp in September and held at 3.75–4.00% on 28 October** with a hawkish statement; October CPI printed **+0.4% m/m** (after +0.6% in September), leaving **CPI at 3.82% y/y — nearly double target and accelerating.** Reuters (18 Nov) reports several FOMC participants saw the case for a *bigger* move. The March 2026 dot plot had twelve officials pencilling a cut and none a hike; that guidance is now stale by ~100bp of hawkish repricing.

The long end is the binding constraint. **The 10Y Treasury is 5.13%**, up **+75bp from the 4.38% June low**, with the **2s10s at +39bp** — a *bear steepener*, not a growth signal. Critically, 10Y breakevens have *fallen* from 2.38% (late Oct) to **2.12%**, so the entire yield back-up is **real rates and term premium**: the **real 10Y is ~3.01%, the highest in a generation.** That is the single most important number in this report, because KYMR is among the longest-duration assets in the market.

Yet credit and equity risk appetite are *not* behaving as a hawkish-Fed regime would suggest: **HY OAS is 3.63%** (only ~103bp off the 2.60% August tights and far inside the ~4.5% long-run median), and **XBI is +44% y/y** (109 → 157). Growth is non-recessionary (**real GDP +2.1% y/y, Q3 saar +3.3%**; **unemployment 4.1%**, down from 4.5% a year ago). The **broad dollar is 123.6, +4.7% off its August low**, a function of Fed hikes and safe-haven demand.

**Read-through: this is a "higher-for-longer real rates + open funding window" regime.** For a pre-revenue biotech, that combination is unusual and the funding window is the channel that actually matters.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Transmission channels, ranked by materiality for KYMR:**

**① Discount rate — NEGATIVE (large, quantified).** With Phase 3 starting mid-2027, KT-621 launches ~2031 and peak sales land ~2035–36. At a 10% cost of equity, a 2036 cash flow discounts at 0.386x; at 11%, 0.317x — an **~18% NPV hit per 100bp**. KYMR is roughly **8–10% of NPV per 50bp on the long end**. The +75bp back-up since June is a **~12–15% valuation drag** that has been utterly swamped by the clinical catalyst. This is the framework's biggest theoretical negative, and it should be modelled as a persistent multiple ceiling, not a thesis-breaker.

**② Equity financing window — STRONGLY POSITIVE (the dominant channel).** KYMR raised an **upsized $1.75B equity offering priced 23 Nov at $150.37** (11.638M shares; 30-day greenshoe for 1.746M more), closing 25 Nov. That is ~12% of market cap and likely funds Phase 3 through launch. It was **oversubscribed and upsized from $1.5B**, and the stock then **rose 15.7% to $173.92** on 24 Nov — the market treating dilution as validation. This is the single most important macro read-through: **for a pre-revenue biotech, the relevant "rate" is not the 10Y, it is the price of growth equity, and that price is currently attractive.** Tight HY spreads (3.63%) and XBI +44% corroborate. **But this window is pro-cyclical and shuts abruptly** — which is precisely why the raise matters so much.

**③ Refinancing risk — NEUTRAL / IMMUNE.** KYMR has **no debt**. The standard "higher-for-longer" playbook (floating-rate coupons, 2026–27 convertible maturities, covenant risk) does **not apply**. Zero exposure to the HY spread channel. Post-raise cash of roughly $2B+ also removes going-concern and dilution-at-the-lows risk for ~3–4 years.

**④ Drug-pricing / tariff policy — NEGATIVE (structural, under-appreciated).** **Section 232 tariffs of up to 100% on patented pharmaceutical imports took effect 31 July 2026**, with 0% carve-outs for certain specialty drugs, alongside **MFN pricing agreements now embedded in law** (April 2026). KYMR is US-domiciled with no product and negligible near-term exposure, but two second-order effects matter: (a) **MFN reference pricing caps the immunology pricing pool** that underwrites KT-621's peak-sales estimate against Dupixent — a haircut to *terminal* value; (b) an **oral small molecule carries structurally lower COGS and no cold-chain logistics** vs injectable biologics, a genuine relative advantage under a tariff/onshoring regime, provided API sourcing is diversified. Note also ~$400B of announced domestic pharma onshoring commitments — supportive of US-based manufacturing but a reminder that capex costs are rising sector-wide.

**⑤ Inflation on cost structure — MILD NEGATIVE.** Phase 3 enrolment costs, CRO pass-through pricing and 4%+ wage inflation for a 273-person Boston-based R&D workforce mean per-patient trial costs are rising faster than the 3.8% CPI. The $1.75B raise is being deployed into an inflating cost base; real duration of the cash runway is shorter than the nominal figure implies.

**⑥ FX — NEUTRAL near term, mild NEGATIVE long term.** No revenue today. A 4.7% stronger dollar trims the USD value of eventual EU/Japan KT-621 revenue and modestly reduces foreign partners' ability to pay. Immaterial to the current thesis; becomes relevant only at launch.

**⑦ Sector beta / risk appetite — POSITIVE.** Beta 2.03. With unemployment at 4.1%, GDP ~2%, and credit spreads tight, there is no recessionary de-rating pressure on high-beta healthcare. Note the *falsification*: 3% real rates have not stopped XBI's +44% — biotech is trading idiosyncratic catalysts over macro. Do not over-weight the rate-sensitivity narrative in the near term.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL**

The textbook read for a long-duration, pre-revenue asset into a hiking Fed with 3% real rates is *Unfavourable*. The evidence does not support that today. **Three offsets carry the rating to Neutral:**

1. **The financing channel is wide open and KYMR just used it at a premium** ($1.75B upsized, stock +15.7% post-pricing) — the macro variable that actually determines survival for a clinical-stage company has resolved favourably through 2029–30.
2. **No debt** — full insulation from the refinancing and credit-spread regimes that punish rate-sensitive issuers.
3. **Policy and FX exposure are negligible in the near term** because there is no revenue to tariff, translate, or price-control.

**Balancing negatives:** ~3.0% real rates structurally cap the multiple; MFN pricing truncates the immunology terminal value; a bear-steepening 10Y above 5% is a persistent headwind to *any* 2035-weighted DCF; and inflation is eroding the real value of the freshly raised cash. Net, macro is a **second-order variable for KYMR over the next 12 months** — clinical execution on KT-621 dominates by roughly an order of magnitude. The rating would shift to **Unfavourable** if the follow-on window closes or the 10Y clears ~5.75%.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BEAR: a Fed-hike/term-premium accident colliding with MFN escalation**

**The setup:** the market is not positioned for further tightening. Breakevens at 2.12% show no inflation fear, HY OAS at 3.63% shows no credit fear, and XBI has rallied 44% into a hiking cycle. That is a crowded, complacent configuration.

**The shock:** sticky 0.4%+ monthly CPI forces a **50bp hike in December** (or an explicit hike bias for Q1 2027), term premium takes the **10Y to 5.75–6.0%**, and credit finally reprices (HY OAS 3.63% → 5.0%). Simultaneously, MFN reference pricing is extended to oral immunology, cutting consensus KT-621 peak sales 25–30%.

**The KYMR impact:** the double hit is on *terminal* value, not on solvency. A 100bp move on the discount rate is ~18% of NPV; a 25–30% peak-sales haircut on a company trading at $14.85B against ~$2B of cash is another 25–35%. A **40–50% drawdown to the $85–105 area** (roughly the pre-data November 2026 level) is entirely plausible — and note that KYMR would be *falling from a post-catalyst high*, with 11.6M fresh shares and a live greenshoe adding supply. **Because KYMR is now fully funded, this is a multiple event, not a liquidity event** — the company survives comfortably and the science is untouched. That asymmetry is the point: for a cash-rich, de-risked biotech, a macro accident is a *valuation* opportunity, not an existential one.

**The mirror (bull) surprise:** breakevens are already rolling over (2.38% → 2.12%). If inflation breaks decisively in H1 2027 and the Fed pivots back to cuts, driving the 10Y to 4.25%, the ~8–10% NPV-per-50bp sensitivity works *in reverse* — a mechanical ~15–20% re-rating into the Phase 3 start and late-2027 eosinophilic asthma readout. I weight the bear scenario as more probable over the next two quarters given the Fed's demonstrated hawkishness, but the bull tail is fatter in magnitude.
