### 1. Growth Story

Kymera is the first mover in **oral targeted protein degradation for immunology**, and its lead asset KT-621 (STAT6 degrader) just delivered the single most important readout in the company's history: in the BROADEN2 Phase 2b, the mid dose produced a **72% mean EASI reduction vs 27% placebo — a 45-point placebo-adjusted gap that beats the ~34–36 point deltas Dupixent posted in its own trials**, with 62% EASI-75, 43% EASI-90 and 58% itch reduction, all p<0.0001, and placebo-like tolerability.

The thesis is not "another eczema drug." It is **an oral pill with biologic-level efficacy against a $25B Dupixent franchise**, addressing the ~50–60% of moderate-to-severe AD patients who never get on a biologic because of injections, access, or cost — plus asthma, COPD, EoE and CRSwNP behind FDA Fast Track. That is a TAM-expansion story, not a share-shift story.

The stock is up ~8x from its April 2025 low (~$22) and +124% since June 2026; it rose ~50% in two sessions on the data and **still absorbed a $1.5B equity offering on the second day** — that is institutional conviction, not retail froth.

### 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

- **Atopic dermatitis:** global AD drug market ~$16–23B today → **$33–46B by 2034–35** (7–9% CAGR). Dupixent alone is a ~$14–16B franchise. Kymera's penetration today: **0%**.
- **Adjacent type-2 indications (KT-621):** eosinophilic asthma (BREADTH Ph2b, data late 2027), COPD, EoE, CRSwNP. Biologic type-2 respiratory spend is $30B+. Fast Track in asthma de-risks timeline.
- **Lupus / IRF5 (KT-579):** wholly owned, ≥90% median IRF5 degradation in skin, LUMORA Ph1b (~30 patients) starting by early 2027 — an entirely separate multi-billion option the market is barely pricing.
- **Penetration math:** a best-in-class oral capturing just **10% of a $25B AD market = $2.5B**, plus $1.0–1.5B across asthma/COPD/EoE ⇒ **$3.5–4.0B peak unadjusted**. Bull case with oral-driven market expansion: $6–7B. At ~$13.5B pro forma EV, the market is capitalizing roughly the base case at 3.4x peak sales — i.e., **Phase 3 success and base-case peak are already substantially in the price; the incremental upside is asthma/COPD and the IRF5 option.**
- **Differentiation:** oral dosing + clean safety (TEAE 51.9% vs 50.8% placebo; 1.1% discontinuations) vs injectable biologics and vs JAK inhibitors with boxed warnings. Durability through the 52-week OLE is the key remaining unknown.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **52-week OLE data for KT-621** (patients rolling from BROADEN2) — durability of EASI response and conjunctivitis rates; the single most important near-term derisker for a chronic-oral label. 1H–2H 2027.
- **Phase 3 AD trial initiation by mid-2027** (subject to FDA alignment) — trial design/size read-through on probability of success, plus confirmation of a superiority-vs-dupilumab design if pursued.
- **KT-579 LUMORA Phase 1b lupus data** — first-in-human clinical proof for IRF5 degradation, ~30 patients/16 weeks, starting early 2027; a completely separate call option.
- **BREADTH Phase 2b eosinophilic asthma topline, late 2027** — the second leg of the TAM; a win here re-rates peak sales toward the bull case.
- **Sanofi KT-485 (TYK2) and Gilead KT-200 IND (2027)** — validation/milestone cash from two large partners; low cost, free optionality.
- **Q4'26 results (~Feb 25, 2027)** and 2027 guidance — first look at post-offering cash and Phase 3 spend cadence.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY**

*(Conviction: high on the science, moderate on the entry price. A pullback toward $130–140 would make this a Strong Buy.)*

This is exactly the profile I want: **first-in-class platform, wholly owned lead asset, best-in-class-leaning data against a $25B incumbent, two independent shots on goal (STAT6 + IRF5), $3B+ pro forma cash through 2029, and a validated strategic acquirer set.** The $1.5B raise removes financing risk through Phase 3 AD and asthma, which is the correct thing to do with a currency trading at all-time highs.

What keeps me at Buy rather than Strong Buy: at a ~$13.5B pro forma EV, the base-case AD success is already capitalized at ~3.4x a $4B peak-sales scenario. The next leg requires **asthma/COPD TAM expansion or Phase 3 execution** — both 12–24 months out. And 11% short interest plus a 2029 Phase 3 readout means the equity will be volatile in the interim.

**Primary model:** if KT-621 reaches $4B peak with 70% probability of Phase 3 success, risk-adjusted peak value ≈ $2.8B; at 4–5x peak sales plus $3B net cash, fair value is **$150–190/share** — i.e., fairly valued to modestly undervalued. Bull case (asthma + COPD + lupus option, $6–7B peak or M&A at Apogee-like premium): **$250+**.

### 7. Key Risks

1. **Binary Phase 3 execution risk.** Efficacy from a 2b (n≈185 treated) to Phase 3 (likely 700–1,000 patients) is a genuine step; a miss takes the stock to $40–60. Cross-trial comparisons vs Dupixent are explicitly not head-to-head — management flagged this in the 8-K.
2. **Durability and conjunctivitis.** Pooled conjunctivitis ran 11.1–19.7% (class effect, mild-moderate, no discontinuations). Rising rates with longer exposure in the 52-week OLE would dent the "cleaner than Dupixent" narrative.
3. **Oral-degrader CMC/COGS.** Scaling a heterobifunctional degrader to commercial volumes with biologic-like efficacy is unproven at scale; a COGS problem caps the pricing/margin thesis (100% booked gross margin today is an accounting artifact).
4. **Dilution.** $1.5B now (+$225M greenshoe) ≈ 10–13% dilution; a further raise is likely before 2029 given a $500–700M/yr burn at Phase 3 scale.
5. **Competitive entry.** JAK orals (Rinvoq/Cibinqo), oral IL-23/TL1A, AbbVie's Apogee IL-13, Novartis remibrutinib, and fast-following STAT6/STAT3 degraders. Amlitelimab's failure removed one rival but also signals how hard AD Phase 3 is.
6. **Commercial risk beyond the pill:** payer access, "oral biologic" pricing expectations, and a launch (~2029–30) that sits entirely in consensus' long-dated, low-confidence $349M 2030 revenue line.

**Data gaps noted:** no balance sheet tool available (cash figures from filings/press); the sector-peer tool returned only mega-cap pharma (LLY, JNJ, ABBV) with no comparable clinical-stage STAT6/immunology multiples; long-range consensus estimates (2027–2029) show unusable dispersion (revenue low of -$25M) — treat sub-2030 numbers as unreliable.
