### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**1) US pharmaceutical policy: Section 232 tariffs + MFN pricing (Probability: High; Impact: Medium, 2027+)**
100% Section 232 tariffs on imported patented pharmaceuticals are in force, with 0% relief for firms pairing an onshoring plan with an MFN pricing agreement (through 20 Jan 2029); 15% treatment for EU/Japan-origin product (FiercePharma; VisionLifeSciences; STAT, Oct 2026). Kymera is pre-commercial so near-term exposure is indirect — via CMO supply chain and via partner economics — but this becomes a direct launch-economics issue if KT-621 is approved and manufactured abroad. Compliance guidance remains unsettled, which is itself a cost/risk for small biotechs (bio.news).

**2) IRA small-molecule Medicare negotiation asymmetry (Probability: High; Impact: Medium)**
As an **oral small molecule**, KT-621 (and the IRF5/STAT6 oral portfolio) becomes Medicare-negotiation-eligible **9 years** post-approval vs 13 years for biologics (KFF; PMC "Small Molecule Drug Pricing and the Future of Dermatology"). For an AD/asthma franchise targeted at large Medicare-eligible populations, this compresses peak-year pricing and LOE value relative to injectable biologics competitors. Pending legislation to extend the window to 13 years is a swing factor, not a base case.

**3) Transatlantic / alliance-partner and supply-chain friction (Probability: Medium; Impact: Medium)**
Sanofi (France) is the economic source of ~86% of FY2025 revenue; US MFN pressure on European pharma and EU–US tariff dynamics could affect Sanofi's prioritisation and milestone-timing decisions for the IRAK4 program. Separately, undisclosed third-party drug-substance manufacturers create latent exposure to China-plus-one/BIOSECURE-type sourcing rules and export-control friction (Kymera 10-K: "We have engaged third-party manufacturers to supply the drug substances…").

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights
- **Litigation: none material.** The FY2025 10-K and Q1/Q3 2026 10-Qs state the company "is not currently a party to any litigation or legal proceedings that…" would be material. No active IP suits, no ITC/PTAB actions, no securities class action identified in retrieved sources.
- **Investor-rights solicitation (watch item, not a filed claim):** Kuehn Law has solicited Kymera shareholders since at least 17 Jul 2026 and Halper Sadeh launched a similar alert on 23 Nov 2026, both investigating "potential self-dealing" / fiduciary breaches by officers and directors. No complaint was identified; these are typically low-cost solicitation campaigns, but they create headline and derivative-suit tail risk. Kymera's 10-K itself flags "increased risk of securities class action litigation."
- **FDA/regulatory posture is favourable and active:** Fast Track for KT-621 in moderate-to-severe AD (Dec 2025) and in asthma (2026); IND clearance for KT-579 (IRF5) with Phase 1 dosing from Feb 2026; first Phase 3 AD trial planned by mid-2027 "subject to regulatory clearance"; BREADTH asthma topline by late 2027. No clinical hold or safety signal identified in retrieved sources.
- **Capital-markets event:** upsized **$1.75B** public offering priced 23 Nov 2026 at $150.37 (11.638M shares, +1.746M greenshoe), closing 25 Nov 2026 — removes financing risk but is materially dilutive and followed a +30–34% data-driven move in the stock (GlobeNewswire; Reuters).
- **Data-privacy/ABAC:** GDPR + EU Clinical Trials Regulation obligations for EU/EEA sites; minimal FCPA/AML/sanctions surface given US-only operations, but EU transparency (EFPIA Sunshine) and future ex-US commercial conduct will require build-out.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags
None identified. No sanctions nexus, no FCPA/AML exposure surfaced, no disclosed government investigation (DOJ/SEC/FTC/EMA). The only governance-adjacent flag is the pair of "self-dealing" investor-rights solicitations, which are unsubstantiated in the public record.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **LOW**
Rationale: US-only operations, zero ex-US product revenue, no material litigation, no sanctions or export-control nexus, and a supportive FDA posture (two Fast Track designations). The residual risk is **policy/regulatory rather than geopolitical** — Section 232 tariffs, MFN pricing, and the IRA's 9-year small-molecule negotiation window — which is why I would carry the *policy* sub-score at **Medium** even though the headline country/legal risk is Low. Secondary considerations: concentration of collaboration revenue in Sanofi (~86% of FY2025 revenue) and undisclosed CMO geography.

**Gaps:** exact geographic revenue split beyond the Sanofi/Gilead collaboration lines was not obtained (no product revenue exists, so the split is essentially binary); CMO/API manufacturing locations were not disclosed in retrieved sources; the trigger for the "self-dealing" investigations could not be confirmed (BusinessWire and GlobeNewswire pages blocked/403).
