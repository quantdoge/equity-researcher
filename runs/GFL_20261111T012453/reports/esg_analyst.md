## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Carbon intensity.** Scope 1+2 intensity is **711 tCO₂e per US$M of sales (2025)**, improved from ~930 in 2020 (~24% reduction) — but gross Scope 1+2 is roughly **~4 Mt CO₂e**, overwhelmingly landfill methane. That is inherently carbon-intensive per dollar, though it is *service*-intensive rather than commodity-production.

**Targets & credibility (genuine positives).** GFL has raised its goal to a **30% absolute cut in Scope 1+2 by 2030 vs. 2021**, framed against the Global Methane Assessment, SBTi and IEA NZE pathways, with independent third-party review concluding the approach is consistent with a 1.5°C pathway. Supporting goals: **double beneficial use of biogas by 2030**, **+40% recyclables recovered**, ≥50% of diesel vehicle replacements to CNG/alternative fuel, 85% of CNG fleet on RNG by 2030. Reporting stack is institutional-grade for the sector: TCFD-aligned climate report, SASB, GRI, CDP, limited assurance.

**The credibility gap.** Two issues. (a) **Bolt-on M&A structurally fights an absolute target** — roughly **$2B of annualised revenue acquired in 2026** (including SECURE at ~C$6.4B / 11x EBITDA) mechanically adds emissions; GFL has already **restated 2021–2024 Scope 1/2** for divestitures, which invites scepticism about like-for-like progress. (b) The **US$4.6B SECURE acquisition** pushes GFL into **produced-water and oilfield waste handling in Western Canada** — higher-emission, oil-price-linked, cyclical revenue that raises spill/contamination tail risk and dilutes the "pure-play circular economy" narrative. Conversely, the **March 2025 divestiture of the Environmental Services (soil remediation) business** exited the segment most exposed to legacy contaminated-site and PFAS clean-up liabilities — a material de-risking of the environmental tail.

**Controversy-driven environmental risk is concentrated and rising** (see §5): the Upper Stoney Creek landfill in Hamilton, Ontario generated ~4,400–4,500 odour complaints by end-2025, a **guilty plea to environmental offences in July 2026 with fines exceeding C$775,000**, and a closure petition with ~4,400 signatures. This is the single clearest manifestation of licence-to-operate risk in the portfolio.

**Physical climate risk:** moderate — landfill gas generation is temperature/precipitation sensitive, and extreme-weather events disrupt collection routes; GFL even maintains an employee disaster-relief fund, implicitly acknowledging physical-risk frequency.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Safety is the core social issue and the trend is favourable but the target is unmet.** Total Recordable Incident Rate fell **4.3 (2021) → 3.7 (2022) → 3.67 (2023)** against a stated **"Safe For Life" goal of ≤2.0**. Progress is real but GFL is running well above its own ambition and above best-in-class peers in the sector. This is a high-hazard industry (rear-load collection, heavy equipment, landfill slopes); US OSHA records show recurring GFL citations including 2025 and 2026 actions ($5,600–$25,033 range), consistent with chronic rather than catastrophic safety failure.
- **Labour:** ~17,000 employees, largely unionised in Canada, with acute driver-labour scarcity in North America. Turnover and wage inflation are the real social-financial transmission, not diversity metrics.
- **Community relations / social licence: this is the weak spot.** Stoney Creek has escalated from nuisance to **~130 civil lawsuits at C$250,000 each (~C$32.5M claimed)**, alleging GFL knew of harm and failed to remediate. Approximately 25 lawsuits' worth of that exposure plus prosecutorial findings create a **template for organised opposition at other sites**, and municipal customers are politically sensitive counterparties — franchise/collection contract retention is the asset at risk.
- **Product/consumer:** not material. **Diversity:** disclosed but not decision-relevant at this scale.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10**

This is the weakest pillar by a wide margin, and it is the only pillar I would call genuinely poor.

| Issue | Evidence | Read |
|---|---|---|
| **Dual-class control** | Founder/CEO/Chair Patrick Dovigi controls **~25.5% of votes on <6% economic ownership** via multiple-voting shares (10 votes each) | Votes decoupled from capital at ~4x |
| **Sunset 2040** | Only triggered in 2040, or if Dovigi departs / falls below 2% | ~20-year entrenchment; far outside the 7-year best-practice norm |
| **CEO/Chair combined** | Dovigi is Founder, President, CEO **and** Chairman; Dino Chiesa is Lead Independent Director | Concentration mitigated only partially |
| **Pay-for-performance failure** | **36% opposition to say-on-pay (2025)**; **>20% opposition to four directors** on the Nomination, Governance & Compensation Committee; say-on-pay **narrowly approved in 2026** | Sustained, multi-year shareholder revolt |
| **Compensation design** | Twice-yearly option grants **without performance criteria or holding requirements** | Rewards tenure/expansion, not per-share value |
| **Related-party disclosure** | Glass Lewis flags **incomplete disclosure of related-party transactions** | Transparency gap in a serial acquirer |
| **Share pledging** | **Extensive pledging under margin loans** by the founder | Forced-selling / overhang risk on drawdown |
| **Sponsor rights** | Nomination and pre-emption rights held by BC Partners, Ontario Teachers', Magny Cours | Minority holders structurally excluded |
| **Proxy access** | Effectively none; low board refresh (8 directors, several long-tenured) | Limited accountability channel |

**The offsetting positives — and they are real.** When unsolicited take-private proposals arrived in 2026, the board formed a **Special Committee composed entirely of independent directors**, retained independent financial and legal advisers, and concluded the review on **10 November 2026** by unanimously reaffirming the public strategy. Process integrity was defensible. But the market's verdict was blunt: **shares fell ~9–14% on the announcement**, implying the market believed a sale at a premium was the value-maximising outcome and that **founder control blocked it**. GFL is now also facilitating the sale of financial-sponsor stakes — which, absent a broader process, mostly converts a control block into free float without capturing a control premium for anyone.

**Net:** GFL is a company where the founder's operational track record (value creation from ~2007 start-up to $14.8B market cap) is genuinely exceptional, but where minority shareholders have **no practical recourse** and pay a persistent multiple discount. Score 3/10.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

**Highest-impact: landfill methane rules.**
- **Canada: MSW Landfill Methane Regulations (SOR/2025-279), in force** (registered 31 Dec 2025) — targeting **~50% reduction in landfill methane vs. 2019 by 2035**. GFL is a large Canadian landfill owner: this is **mandatory gas-collection capex**, with timing/scope risk and a real possibility of accelerated capital outlays landing ahead of the associated RNG revenue.
- **California:** landfill methane regulation effective **1 January 2027**; **Colorado** and other states adding rules — GFL's US landfill footprint is exposed state-by-state.
- **US federal:** EPA is **reconsidering/loosening NSPS Subpart OOOOc landfill methane requirements** — a genuine near-term offset that lowers compliance capex in the US relative to 2024 expectations.

**PFAS / CERCLA.** EPA's designation of PFOA/PFOS as hazardous substances (2024) plus state leachate limits create **downstream liability at landfills where PFAS is present in >95% of cases** — treatment capex and third-party cost-recovery litigation risk. Mitigant: the 2025 exit from soil remediation removed the most direct exposure.

**Disclosure regimes.** **US SEC climate rule: not in force / low risk. EU CSRD: not directly applicable** (Canadian issuer, minimal EU operations). Practical exposure is **investor- and customer-driven demand** for CDP/SASB-consistent data — GFL's voluntary, assured reporting means low compliance-cost shock, but also that its ESG disclosure is **not legally enforced**.

**CBAM: not material** (no exports of CBAM-covered goods). **ESG-linked debt covenants:** no sustainability-linked margin ratchet was identified in the material reviewed — **flagging this as an unverified gap**; given GFL's leveraged, acquisition-heavy balance sheet, a KPI-linked facility would be a live risk if introduced.

**Tailwind worth pricing:** EPR/packaging rules, recycled-content mandates, RNG/RIN and clean-fuel credit monetisation, and landfill-gas-to-energy incentives all convert GFL's methane problem into revenue. This is genuine, not greenwashing.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (material governance caveat)

- **E: Leader-adjacent** — best-in-class target-setting rigour for the sector, assured disclosure, genuine avoided-emissions story; offset by high absolute Scope 1, M&A-driven target drift and a live odour/permit controversy.
- **S: Average** — safety improving but failing its own ≤2.0 TRIR goal; community relations actively impaired at Stoney Creek.
- **G: Laggard** — dual-class control to 2040, combined CEO/Chair, unaligned pay with no holding requirements, opaque related-party disclosure, pledging, and repeated >20–36% shareholder opposition with no recourse.

I would describe GFL as an **"ESG-Average company with Laggard governance"** — the kind of profile where the E and S narrative is used, consciously or not, to draw attention from the G problem.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (governance discount is a valuation drag, not a thesis-breaker)

**Why neutral rather than negative:**
1. **The material ESG risks are quantifiable and small relative to scale.** C$775K in fines and ~C$32.5M in claimed damages against a US$14.8B market cap and ~$678.7M quarterly Adjusted EBITDA is a rounding error. Even a worst-case Stoney Creek outcome (closure) is a **site-level**, not enterprise-level, impairment.
2. **Environmental regulation is largely a capex problem GFL can fund and monetise** — gas capture and RNG are accretive, and the US federal methane retreat offsets Canadian compliance cost.
3. **The operating thesis is intact and improving:** 7.4% organic growth, 115bp underlying margin expansion, third guidance raise in 2026, >30% Adjusted FCF growth, beta 0.49. ESG factors are not consuming the cash flow story.
4. **The 2025 exit from soil remediation** removed the largest tail liability from the portfolio at exactly the right time.

**Why not positive:**
1. **The governance discount is permanent, not temporary.** A 2040 sunset means no catalyst to narrow it. The 9–14% drawdown on the take-private rejection is direct, dated evidence of value that minority holders **cannot access**. Expect GFL to trade at a persistent discount to Waste Connections/Republic-type peers on governance alone — and note the sponsor-stake sale mechanic captures no control premium for public holders.
2. **Founder concentration is a key-man risk** on a serial acquirer whose strategy and capital allocation are inseparable from Dovigi, amplified by margin-loan pledging.
3. **Pay with no performance criteria or holding requirements** in a roll-up story is precisely the structure that historically precedes value-destructive M&A. SECURE — 11x EBITDA, 80% stock-funded, into oilfield waste — is the test case and adds **oil-price cyclicality plus carbon-transition exposure** to what was a defensive compounder.
4. **Absolute emissions targets and an acquisitive model are structurally incompatible**; expect either a missed 2030 goal or another restatement, either of which triggers ESG-mandated selling.

**Positioning conclusion.** For a generalist investor, ESG is **not a reason to own or avoid GFL**; it is a **reason to demand a discount and to size the position against a governance floor** rather than the E&S narrative. For an ESG-constrained or governance-screened mandate, GFL likely **fails** dual-class/board-independence screens on mechanical grounds — which itself caps the ESG-dedicated ownership base and explains part of the persistent valuation gap. **Monitoring triggers:** (i) any 2027–2028 GHG target re-basing or slippage, (ii) resolution or escalation of Stoney Creek (site closure or a certified class action), (iii) renewal of take-private interest or a sponsor-block sale at a premium public holders cannot join, (iv) any introduction of an ESG-linked debt covenant on the back of SECURE leverage, (v) TRIR failing to converge toward 2.0.
