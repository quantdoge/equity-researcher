## GFL Environmental Inc. (NYSE/TSX: GFL) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Prepared November 2026. Note on sourcing: the SEC filing-text tool returned no 10-K/40-F for GFL (Canadian issuer filing 6-K/40-F exhibits) and direct sec.gov fetches were blocked (HTTP 403), so segment geography is sourced from GFL's investor financials page and results releases rather than the AIF itself. **Gap flagged:** province/state-level revenue is not disclosed.*

### Company posture (context for risk)
GFL has been transformed into a **pure-play North American solid waste** business: it sold its Environmental Services (soil remediation/infrastructure) unit effective March 1, 2025 at a C$8B enterprise value, repaid debt and repurchased $3.0B of shares (>10% of subordinate voting shares). FY2025 revenue **$6,615.9M**, Adj. EBITDA **$1,985.0M** (30.0% margin), net leverage **3.4x**; FY2026 guidance ~**$7,000M** revenue (Feb 11, 2026 release). On **Sept 1, 2026** it closed the **C$6.4B acquisition of SECURE Waste Infrastructure** (55 liquid waste facilities, 10 metals recycling sites, 12+ landfills, 2,000+ employees, concentrated in **Western Canada plus North Dakota**).

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US–Canada trade war turning specifically against cross-border waste flows (HIGH probability, MEDIUM impact).**
This is no longer hypothetical. A White House proclamation on **Oct 23, 2026** amended the Section 338 tariff list: **22 Canadian goods were exempted while 100 were added, including municipal and clinical waste, subject to 50% tariffs from Oct 30, 2026** — a measure explicitly championed by Michigan Senate candidate Mike Rogers as a strike at Canada's "trash trucks" (Yahoo News Canada/CP, Oct 27, 2026). Separately, the U.S. imposed a **50% tariff on $27.6B of Canadian goods effective Aug 22, 2026**, with Canadian counter-tariffs in response. GFL is the most exposed North American hauler to this corridor: Ontario exported **27% (3.44Mt) of its landfilled waste to the U.S. in 2024**, primarily Michigan, New York and Ohio (W2RO), and Canada is the largest single source of waste into Michigan landfills. Mechanism of impact: (i) compression of cross-border disposal margins or volume loss; (ii) repricing onto Ontario municipal customers; (iii) upside optionality if exports are curtailed — Ontario is projected to exhaust landfill capacity by **2034**, which raises the value of GFL's Ontario disposal and would support domestic tipping-fee inflation. **Quantification:** cross-border volumes are a modest slice of the ~$7.4B revenue base (likely low-single-digit % of EBITDA), so this is a margin/strategic irritant rather than an existential threat — but it is politically durable and could escalate into volume bans.

**Risk 2 — Canadian competition/regulatory activism around the SECURE deal (MEDIUM-HIGH probability of remedies, LOW-MEDIUM impact).**
The **Canada Competition Bureau's review of the SECURE transaction was still listed as "ongoing" at closing** (Waste Dive, Sept 1, 2026, updated Sept 8; FTC cleared it May 5, 2026). Canada's Competition Act permits the Commissioner to challenge **completed** mergers, and GFL has direct form: it closed **Terrapure in Aug 2021**, was challenged in **Nov/Dec 2021**, and settled in **April 2022 by divesting seven facilities — four liquid waste facilities and three tank farms in Western Canada**. The SECURE deal stacks GFL's Western Canadian liquid/oilfield-waste overlap on top of that same footprint, making a consent agreement with divestitures the most likely outcome. Watch also for the Commissioner's expanded use of the Act's **greenwashing/ESG marketing provisions** — a live enforcement theme for waste and recycling companies.

**Risk 3 — PFAS/leachate regulation and environmental litigation (HIGH probability, MEDIUM impact).**
The EPA found PFAS in leachate at **>95% of 200+ landfills surveyed** and is revising **Effluent Limitation Guidelines** for landfill leachate; it issued **2026 interim guidance on PFAS destruction and disposal** (April 2026) and draft biosolids guidance (July 2026). GFL's own AIF flags that "compliance with new PFAS regulations may require our landfills to monitor groundwater, leachate or other environmental media." Cost transmission: leachate pre-treatment capex (GFL is already seeking an Ontario ECA for an on-site leachate treatment system at Stoney Creek), higher disposal costs, and CERCLA/state cost-recovery claims. Offsetting: PFAS cost pass-through to municipal customers has historically been achievable, and GFL is a claimant (not only a defendant) against chemical manufacturers.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights
- **Ontario environmental prosecution (adverse, resolved at low end):** GFL was charged with **10 violations under Ontario's Environmental Protection Act** over Stoney Creek odours and **pleaded guilty to three charges in July 2026, fined over C$775,000**. Odour complaints continue (9 logged Oct 29–Nov 4, 2026).
- **Civil nuisance claims:** ~**70 Stoney Creek residents** have filed individual claims alleging persistent noxious odours since 2023. GFL's track record on odour litigation includes a **$950,000 South Wake Landfill (NC) settlement** and reported aggregate payments of **more than $15M** across similar matters — precedents are modest in size but repeatable.
- **Employment discrimination:** **Oct 21, 2024 — Waste Industries U.S.A., TransWaste, Waste Industries Atlanta and GFL Environmental agreed to pay $3.1M** to settle an EEOC suit alleging qualified female applicants were denied truck-driver roles in Atlanta. A pattern/PR risk given ~15,500 employees and heavy driver hiring.
- **Take-private process (governance, not litigation):** On **Nov 10, 2026** GFL concluded its special-committee review and **rejected unsolicited proposals valuing the company at roughly US$22B** (Globe and Mail), reaffirming a public strategy. Residual risk: a renewed approach, and shareholder litigation typical of contested M&A.
- **Regulatory patchwork:** ~dozens of state/provincial regulators plus municipal franchisors; landfill permitting, closure/post-closure obligations, EPR and organics-diversion mandates are the main earnings levers.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags
- **Environmental enforcement with a guilty plea** — the single clearest compliance flag; repeat-offence and permit-condition risk at Ontario/Quebec sites warrants monitoring.
- **No sanctions, export-control, AML or China/Russia exposure** — minimal OFAC/BIS surface given a Canada+US footprint.
- **FCPA/anti-bribery: Low** — U.S. municipal and provincial contract bidding is the theoretical vector; no disclosed investigations.
- **Data privacy: Low** — residential customer data at scale, but no material disclosed breach or enforcement.
- **Governance:** dual-class structure (subordinate voting shares) with founder-CEO Patrick Dovigi's influence; combined with the rejected US$22B bids, expect continued takeover speculation and possible activist pressure.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**
GFL operates exclusively in two mature, high-rule-of-law jurisdictions with no sanctions or emerging-market exposure — structurally the lowest geopolitical risk profile in the industries sector. The rating is held at **Medium** rather than Low by three factors: (1) an **active, waste-specific US–Canada tariff regime** that hits GFL's core cross-border corridor; (2) a **demonstrated willingness of Canadian authorities to litigate and prosecute** (Terrapure consent divestitures; Ontario EPA guilty plea); and (3) rising **PFAS/leachate compliance costs**. None of these appears individually capable of impairing the investment case; the aggregate is a low-single-digit-percentage EBITDA drag with tail risk concentrated in a politically driven shutdown of Ontario→Michigan waste flows.
