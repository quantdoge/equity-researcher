## GFL Environmental Inc. (NYSE: GFL) — Value Analysis

**Price $42.41 | Mkt cap ~$16.4B (C$22.9B) | Net debt C$12.1B | EV ~$25.0B | FY26E: Rev C$8.06B, Adj. EBITDA C$2.49B, Adj. FCF C$1.02B**

*Note on currency: GFL reports in CAD; the NYSE quote is USD. I use CAD for operating metrics and USD for per-share values (USD/CAD ≈ 1.40).* *Data caveat: the vendor's quarterly "EBITDA" field is inconsistent with company-reported Adjusted EBITDA, so I rely on GFL's own press releases and guidance.*

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow** (narrowing-to-widening, but returns still sub-par)

GFL is now a pure-play North American solid & liquid waste business (~C$8.1B revenue run-rate, 17,000+ employees) after selling its Environmental Services/soil remediation division in 2025 and redeploying the proceeds.

**What is genuinely good:**
- **Permitted landfill and transfer-station assets.** New landfill capacity is effectively unbuildable in most US/Canadian jurisdictions. This is the classic waste-industry moat: local oligopoly, vertically integrated collection → transfer → disposal (internalization), and route density that raises competitor cost.
- **Pricing power, demonstrated.** Q3 2026 core pricing +5.7%, Q3 organic revenue +7.4% — industry-leading, delivered in a "challenging macro backdrop." Contractual, CPI-plus-like escalators with municipal/commercial customers, ~90%+ retention.
- **Margin transformation.** Adjusted EBITDA margin has gone from ~19% (2021) to **31.5%** (Q3 2026), essentially matching Waste Management. Underlying margin expansion of 115bps ex-M&A and diesel.
- **Revenue predictability:** 95%+ recurring/annuity-like; economically insensitive (trash is non-discretionary).

**What is not good — and it matters:**
- **ROIC has never cleared WACC.** ROIC: 0.25% (2022), 1.57% (2023), 1.25% (2024), **2.24% (2025)**. Even excluding C$14.9B of goodwill/intangibles, ROIC on tangible capital is ~7% — below a ~9% cost of capital. The moat exists at the *asset* level but has been consumed by **the price paid for acquisitions**. Buffett's test — "consistently ROIC > WACC" — fails.
- Goodwill + intangibles are **51% of total assets**. Organic, tangible capital intensity is fine; the balance sheet is the problem.

**Verdict:** a real but narrow moat, improving operationally, historically harvested by sellers of assets rather than by GFL shareholders.

**Strengths:** FCF inflection is real — adjusted FCF conversion is heading from ~20% toward the mid-40s % of EBITDA; interest coverage ~5.5x on cash interest of C$455M; no near-term maturity wall; dividend is nominal (C$0.0676/yr, 0.16%) so it doesn't compete with deleveraging.

**Weaknesses:** (a) GAAP net income is noise — C$3.83B "earnings" in 2025 was a divestiture gain, and 9M-2026 shows a C$410M *loss* from continuing ops on intangible amortization and interest. Guidance of "mid-3s" net leverage by YE26 implies ~4.85x on the Q3 balance sheet — I'd treat **~4x** as the realistic figure, not 3.5x. (c) FCF conversion is still ~41% of EBITDA versus WM's ~50%+.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Intrinsic value (FCFE DCF):** 2026E adjusted FCF of C$1,020M grown 8–11%/yr for ten years, discounted at 9% (beta 0.49 but 2x+ leverage-adjusted), terminal growth 2.5%:

- Bear (8% growth): **C$63/share ≈ $45**
- Base (11% growth): **C$79/share ≈ $57**
- **Midpoint: ~$51/share (~C$71)**

**Cross-checks:**
- **Private-market test:** a PE consortium's **$52/sh (~$22B)** bid was rejected as inadequate in Nov 2026; another proposal reached **$58**. At $58, EV = ~C$43B = **17.4x** 2026E EBITDA. The board's rejection of $52 is a credible, informed floor on value.
- **Multiples:** 14.0x 2026E EV/EBITDA, ~12.7x 2027E; ~22x 2026E adjusted FCF (4.5% equity FCF yield). Waste peers (WM, RSG historically) trade 13–15x EV/EBITDA — so GFL is **in line with the best operator in the industry despite 4x+ leverage and inferior returns on capital**. No statistical discount remains.
- The 88.6% "discount to peer median P/E" flagged by screeners is an artifact of GAAP distortions; ignore it.

**Current $42.41 vs. ~$51 midpoint = ~17% discount.** Sufficient for a Hold, **insufficient for the 25–30% margin of safety this discipline requires.** Buy zone: **≤$36** (C$50).

### 4. Management Quality: **Mixed (6/10)**

- **Founder-led, aligned-ish.** Patrick Dovigi founded GFL in 2007 and still runs it. He controls **25.5% of votes with <6% economic ownership** via 10-vote multiple voting shares — control disproportionate to capital at risk. Insider economic ownership is only ~3% of the class; he has been a net *seller/exerciser*, not a buyer.
- **Superb operator.** The margin expansion and 7.4% organic growth are not luck — they reflect pricing discipline, procurement and route-density work. Execution credibility is high.
- **Capital allocation is the weak link.** 2025: repurchased **C$2.97B** of stock (share count 381M → 359M — good). 2026: issued ~26M shares plus ~C$4B of debt for SECURE and other deals, reversing the buyback. Serial M&A at prices that have kept ROIC at 2–3% for four years. The company is now **re-accelerating M&A** right after deleveraging — the same playbook that produced the poor returns record.
- **Governance:** the take-private process was run properly (independent Special Committee, unanimous board reaffirmation to stay public on 10 Nov 2026). But note the sequence: unsolicited bids arrived, stock re-rated to ~$50, bids were rejected, and the stock fell 14% to today's $42. That leaves holders with the standalone business and no deal.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

GFL is a materially better business in 2026 than it was in 2021 — better margins, better pricing, better FCF, and a cleaner (pure-play waste) story. But three things keep me from buying:

1. **Price is fair, not cheap.** ~14x EV/EBITDA and ~22x adjusted FCF for a company with sub-WACC returns on capital and ~4x leverage.
2. **The margin of safety isn't there.** ~17% discount to my ~$51 midpoint; I want 25–30% (~$36) because the leverage and M&A risk are asymmetric.
3. **Re-leveraging plus re-accelerated M&A** reintroduces exactly the behavior that has destroyed per-share returns for a decade. Adjusted FCF per share, not EBITDA, is the number to watch — and it has yet to compound.

If you own it: hold. The FCF trajectory, pricing power, and demonstrated third-party interest at $52–58 support the position. **Add below $36.** Sell if net leverage exceeds ~4.5x on a full-year basis, or if M&A consideration shifts back toward equity issuance at these multiples.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

- **Leverage/refinancing:** C$12.4B debt, ~4.8x on the latest balance sheet, floating-rate exposure and C$455M annual cash interest. A recession that dents volumes hits FCF and the covenant math simultaneously.
- **Capital allocation relapse:** more large deals at high multiples (SECURE-style) would entrench sub-WACC ROIC and dilute per-share value. Dilution already offset 2025's C$2.97B buyback.
- **Take-private optionality is now priced out** — the Nov 2026 rejection removed the bid floor; upside requires standalone execution.
- **Volume risk:** organic growth is currently price-led (+5.7% pricing); underlying volume is roughly flat. If pricing power normalizes, the growth algorithm thins fast.
- **Governance/control:** Dovigi's 25.5% voting bloc means minority holders cannot force change, and a future take-private could be struck at a price that suits the control holder.
- **Data quality:** no reliable peer set in the vendor screen (returned aerospace/rail names), and reported GAAP figures are heavily distorted by the 2025 divestiture. My valuation leans on company-reported Adjusted EBITDA/FCF and the rejected bid range — both judgment inputs, not audited comparables.
