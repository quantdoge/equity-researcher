## 2. Key Red Flags (data-supported)

**Flag 1 — Disclosed "Adjusted Free Cash Flow" does not trace to cash.**
9M 2026: OCF C$933.5M less capex C$913.9M = **C$19.6M** of actual free cash flow. GFL reports **Adjusted FCF of C$484.9M** for the same nine months. Adjusted EBITDA of C$1,748.4M less ~C$340M cash interest and ~C$50M cash taxes implies ~C$1,358M of pre-working-capital cash generation, versus C$933.5M delivered — a **~C$425M annualised gap (~25% of Adjusted EBITDA)** that can only be explained by cash costs (integration, transaction, severance, restructuring) being added back to Adjusted EBITDA. The company's "mid-40s% FCF conversion" narrative rests on a measure that reconciles to cash only after removing a quarter of its own EBITDA.

**Flag 2 — The leverage target is unreconcilable to the reported balance sheet.**
At 9/30/26: total debt C$12,376M, cash C$296M, **net debt C$12,080M**. Against FY26E Adjusted EBITDA of C$2,490–2,495M that is **4.85x**. Management guides to "net leverage in the mid-3s by year-end 2026." Mid-3s implies ≈C$8.5–8.8B of net debt — a **C$3.3–3.6B reduction in one quarter**, against guided Q4 Adjusted FCF of ~C$535M. The only reconciling explanation is that leverage is struck on a **pro-forma run-rate EBITDA ~C$800M above the FY2026 reported figure** (SECURE stub + Frontier + synergies). That may be contractually legitimate; it is not what a credit analyst or a covenant-based buyer sees.

**Flag 3 — Capex guidance implies disposal proceeds.**
9M gross capex of C$913.9M already equals the **full-year "net capex" guidance of C$910M**. Prudent assumption: FY gross capex ≈C$1.2–1.4B, with the shortfall funded by asset sales. Guided C$1,020M Adjusted FCF therefore depends on both the add-back in Flag 1 *and* disposal proceeds.

**Flag 4 — Balance sheet is 51% acquisition accounting; tangible book is negative.**
Goodwill + intangibles: **C$14,868M on C$29,029M of assets (51%)**, versus total equity of C$11,900M → **tangible book equity ≈ −C$3.0B**. Intangibles jumped C$5.0B in Q3 alone (SECURE). Current ratio 0.93, with C$12.4B of debt against C$296M of cash.

**Flag 5 — DSO re-expansion (reversing a flattering ratio).**
Receivable days: 44 in FY2025 → **~76 days** at 9/30/26 (AR C$1,669.5M / annualised revenue C$8,055M). Part is SECURE's oilfield/joint-interest billing mix — but note this cuts both ways: GFL's Beneish DSRI of **0.59** (a very low receivables-days ratio) is precisely what held the M-Score in the "moderate" rather than "high" band. That protection has now reversed.

**Flag 6 — Capital allocation whipsaw.**
FY2025: **C$2,967M of buybacks**, including C$2,135M in Q1 2025 alone, funded by the C$8B Environmental Services sale to Apollo/BC Partners — executed at ~C$62–68/share. Twelve months later, April 13, 2026: **C$6.4B acquisition of SECURE Waste Infrastructure**, largely debt-funded (closed Sept 1, 2026), which management called dilutive to the share price. Diluted shares then rose from 358.5M (Q1 2026) to **385.7M** (Q3 2026). Buy high, issue paper, re-lever — three capital-allocation decisions in eighteen months.

**Flag 7 — Organic volume is thin behind the headline.**
Q3 2026 revenue +27.2%, of which **5.7% core pricing** and ~20% M&A. That leaves ~1.7% volume. Pricing at 5%+ in solid waste historically extracts a volume toll 2–4 quarters later — a 2027 issue, into a 4.9x levered balance sheet.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment: **moat largely intact**

This is where I part company with the reflexive bear. Integrated landfill-anchored solid waste is one of the better moats in industrials: landfill permitting is a 7–10 year barrier, route density is self-reinforcing, and switching costs are high for commercial customers. 5.7% core pricing with only 1.7% volume loss is *evidence of pricing power*, not erosion. Underlying Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded 115bps excluding M&A and diesel — on a shrinking revenue base (post-ES divestiture). Collection and disposal in North America is a rational, consolidated oligopoly (WM, RSG, WCN, GFL ~60%+ of the market).

The genuine competitive threat is **self-inflicted, not external**: SECURE pushes GFL into oilfield waste (Alberta/Williston), a cyclical, E&P-spend-levered business with materially lower moat quality than a landfill. That is the analogue to be watching — not Waste Management.

## 5. Management & Governance Red Flags

1. **Dual-class control.** Subordinate voting shares plus multiple voting shares held by the Dovigi Group. Glass Lewis has criticised the structure as disenfranchising ordinary holders. Founder-CEO control with ~11.1M shares of insider holdings total means the economic stake is small relative to the voting stake — the classic alignment gap.
2. **CEO negotiating his own buyout.** Dovigi publicly signalled a willingness to roll his stake and stay as leader at ~$50–58/share while his board ran a process. On Nov 9, 2026 the special committee (chair Dino Chiesa) **unanimously rejected a ~$52/share bid from a Blackstone/KKR/Energy Capital Partners consortium**, reportedly seeking ~$58. Chairman's rationale: standalone value exceeds the bid.
3. **Exit overhang.** A decade of sponsor ownership (HPS, Macquarie, Hawthorn, then BC Partners and Ontario Teachers') has been unwound through secondary offerings; GFL itself repurchased 7.06M shares inside one of those secondaries. Backers have been selling into strength.
4. **Data gap, disclosed.** SEC Form 4 retrieval returned **zero filings** for GFL despite a 10-K filer profile. I could not verify net insider buying/selling over the trailing twelve months. Treat the "no insider buying" question as **unresolved, not exonerating**.
5. **Promotional language.** "Industry-leading organic growth," "laser focused," "ultimate financial flexibility," "meaningfully inflected" — consistent with GFL's historical pattern of guiding Adjusted EBITDA loudly and GAAP results quietly. To be fair: guidance has now been **raised three times in 2026** with beats on revenue, Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted FCF. Delivery has, so far, matched the rhetoric.
