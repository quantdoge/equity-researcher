## SECTOR BRIEF — Waste Management / Environmental Services (Target: GFL Environmental)

**Data note:** GFL reports in **CAD**; all peers report in **USD**. Multiples and margins below are as reported by each company, not FX-converted.

### 1. Sector Overview
North American solid waste is a **~US$100bn** industry (Waste Business Journal) growing at a **~7% CAGR since 2020 vs ~3% in 2012–19** (RBC), with the four majors — WM, RSG, WCN, GFL — now collecting **~half of all industry revenue**. Growth is overwhelmingly *price-led*: core pricing has run **5–6%** across the majors (Q2'26: CWST solid waste pricing +5.5%; GFL core price +6.1%), while volumes are flat-to-negative. It is a mature, late-cycle, **defensive/oligopolistic** sector with the highest structural barriers of any "waste/industrial services" niche: landfill permitting, NIMBY-driven entitlements, route-density economics, and decades of sunk disposal capacity. National landfill tipping fees are **~US$72.14/ton** and rising as capacity tightens — the single best proxy for sector pricing power.

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Pricing power with cooling cost inflation.** Price is decelerating modestly (from 5–6% toward ~4–5%) but input deflation (diesel, CPI-linked cost recovery rolling off) means margin *expansion* per point of price. GFL's Q3'26 adj. EBITDA margin was **31.5%**, with the Canadian segment at a record **34%**; Q2'26 consolidated underlying margins rose 125bp.
- **Consolidation and disposal-capacity scarcity.** Top-4 ~50% share of a $100bn market, with disciplined tuck-in M&A (CWST: 5 deals / ~$165m annualised revenue in H1'26 alone; GFL: ~$2bn annualised acquired revenue in 2026 incl. SECURE). Private capital (PE, infrastructure funds) is competing for assets — GFL itself drew a ~**US$52/sh, ~US$22bn** take-private consortium bid (Blackstone/KKR/ECP), evidence of private-market value above public marks.
- **Regulatory complexity is monetisable.** PFAS, state-level EPR, landfill-emissions rules and organics diversion raise technical content and disposal value per ton; RNG/landfill-gas and recycling-modernisation capex create new high-return profit pools (WM, RSG, GFL all investing).

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Volume softness is broad-based.** Q2'26: collection & disposal volumes **−0.4%** (ex-wildfire comps), landfill volumes only **+1.7%**; C&D, residential and manufacturing/special waste remain weak. Sector revenue growth is now ~entirely price — a bad setup if pricing folds.
- **Price-led growth has a shelf life.** With inflation normalising and EPR shifting recycling cost economics, organic growth converges toward ~4%. The sector's counter-cyclical appeal also means less upside in a manufacturing recovery.
- **Leverage and cost of capital.** GFL is the most levered major (~4.5x net leverage territory post-SECURE; C$456m FY25 interest expense), and debt-funded M&A is happening into higher rates. Also GFL-specific: **US operating margins fell 2.3pp** in Q3'26, flagging integration/mix drag.

### 5. GFL's Competitive Position — **Average** (improving, but structurally sub-scale)
- **Scale:** clear #4 in North America; revenue is ~26% of WCN and ~18% of WM. Sub-scale means less route density and thinner disposal self-haul, the core driver of the margin gap.
- **Growth:** best-in-class organic growth among the big four in 2026 (**+7.4%** same-business Q3'26 vs WM/RSG low-single-digit), but growth is heavily acquired (+18.7pp from M&A in Q3), not organic.
- **Margins:** adj. EBITDA margin **31.5%** is genuinely competitive (above WM, just below RSG/WCN) — but **operating margin of 5.2% vs peer median 18.1%** reflects heavy D&A on an acquired asset base and a C$456m interest burden. FCF, not EBITDA, is the unresolved question (the bear case is explicitly "cash question").
- **Optionality:** post-Apollo divestiture it is a simpler pure-play solid waste story; SECURE adds western Canadian disposal (12–15% accretive to adj. FCF); and a **rejected US$52/sh bid (9 Nov 2026) vs management's ~US$58 aspiration** — with the stock now at **$42.41** — implies the market discounts standalone execution, and the private-market bid provides a partial valuation floor.

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**
Structural pricing power, ~7% industry growth, near-highest barriers to entry and a consolidating oligopoly (Porter: low new-entry, low buyer power, low substitution, moderate rivalry). The caveat is that growth is price- and M&A-funded rather than volume-funded, so returns depend on cost discipline and on the majors defending 4–5% pricing as inflation cools. Sector is **mature/late-cycle** — buyers should expect mid-single-digit organic growth, margin expansion, and M&A as the primary value levers, not cyclical volume recovery.

**Implication for GFL:** the right to own GFL is a *self-help/scale* story in an attractive sector — margin convergence toward RSG/WCN and FCF conversion post-SECURE — not a sector-tailwind story.
