# GFL Environmental Inc. (NYSE/TSX: GFL) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**As of November 2026 | Price $42.41 | Market cap ~$14.8B | 17,000+ employees | Foreign private issuer (40-F filer, CIK 0001780232)**
*Disclosure note: GFL files Form 40-F (not 10-K), so SEC-native risk-factor/Form 4/EDGAR enforcement feeds returned empty. Findings below are rebuilt from company releases, Canadian regulatory records, AGM voting results and press. Data gaps flagged explicitly.*

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK** (strong evidence)

This is the dominant non-financial risk at GFL, and it is structural rather than episodic.

| Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Controlled company** | Dual class: multiple voting shares carry **10 votes each**. Dovigi Group owns **11,812,964 MVS = 100% of the class**. At the May 13, 2026 AGM Dovigi received **386.96M votes FOR (94.37%)** — i.e., the founder personally controls ~94% of votes cast. Minority holders have effectively no ability to remove directors or block related-party deals. |
| **CEO/Chair duality** | Patrick Dovigi is **Founder, Chairman, President & CEO**. Power concentration is extreme: economic owner, board chair, and management in one person, protected by supervoting stock. |
| **Board accountability breakdown** | 5 of 8 director nominees received **28.7%–33.0% withheld** (Notarnicola 32.96%; Chiesa, Levy, McDonald, Nayar ~28.7%–29.2%). Only Konkle and Poole cleared 97%. Withheld votes of ~30% across a majority of independents is a severe rebuke; the board is insulated from it by the MVS. |
| **Compensation misalignment** | Glass Lewis Controversy Alert (May 2025): incentive approach that "eschews both performance criteria and holding requirements"; **36% opposition to say-on-pay** in 2024 and >20% opposition to four directors on the governance/compensation committee; ~39% fund opposition reported for 2025. Persistent, not one-off. |
| **Share pledging** | "Extensive pledging of shares under margin loans" (Glass Lewis) — creates forced-selling / margin-call overhang and aligns the controlling shareholder's incentives with share-price defence rather than long-term value. |
| **Related-party disclosure** | Glass Lewis cites **incomplete disclosure of related party transactions**. Combined with founder-affiliated entities (Josaud II Holdings) participating in equity offerings, this is a recurring transparency gap. |
| **Entrenchment rights** | Nomination and pre-emption rights granted to a core group (BC Partners, Ontario Teachers', Magny Cours Investment). Board now expanded to **nine** with Joseph Lenz, a **TPG partner** added as SECURE nominee — a sponsor-affiliated director arriving via acquisition, another related-party vector. |
| **Special committee integrity** | Positive: the take-private review was run by an all-independent special committee with independent financial and legal advisors. Negative: it was chaired by **Dino Chiesa — who drew 29.2% withheld votes** and who has served on the committee shareholders have most protested. |

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **MODERATE** (localised, rising)

- **No SEC or DOJ enforcement actions found** against GFL in EDGAR full-text search. No restatement, no securities class action identified.
- **Ontario environmental prosecution (the key live item):** The Ministry of Environment laid **10 charges** under the *Environmental Protection Act* (April 11, 2025) over the Stoney Creek Regional Facility — discharge of odour/total reduced sulphur compounds causing adverse effect, leachate exceeding limits, failure to comply with the Environmental Compliance Approval. Alleged offences span **Aug 2021–Nov 2023**. GFL **pleaded guilty to three charges and was fined over $775,000** (press-reported, Nov 2026 — confirm against the 40-F/MD&A). Press reports indicate **further odour charges** were laid after that conviction.
- **Aggregate penalty history:** Violation Tracker records **23 penalty events, ~US$1.24M since 2010** — Canada $865k / US $378k; environment-related $957k (10 records), safety-related $280k (12). Recent: C$261,910 air-pollution (2025), C$96,654 waste (2024), C$93,223 workplace safety (2026), C$315,001 waste (2023).
- **Assessment:** Dollar amounts are immaterial to a $14.8B enterprise, but the **pattern and direction are adverse** — repeat environmental *and* workplace-safety findings across two countries, an active prosecution file, and a guilty plea on the company's largest odour controversy. Rising permit-renewal and expansion-approval friction is the real cost, not the fines.
- **Employment/regulatory:** Prior **$3.1M EEOC settlement** (Waste Industries/GFL) for systemic denial of truck-driver roles to women — a hiring-compliance and reputational data point.
- **No pending FDA/FCC-type approvals.** Exposure is environmental permitting, antitrust (M&A-heavy roll-up) and data-privacy (immaterial).

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis: **NO EVIDENCE OF INSIDER BUYING; CONTROLLING HOLDER RETAINS 100% OF SUPERVOTING STOCK**

- **SEC Form 4 feed returns zero filings** — expected for an FPI; insider disclosure runs through SEDAR+/early-warning releases. Third-party aggregators (Yahoo, MarketBeat, SecForm4) returned no usable transaction data. **This is a genuine data gap, not a clean bill of health.**
- **What is verifiable:** The Dovigi Group's **11,812,964 MVS (100% of the class) is unchanged**; an October 31, 2024 internal reorganisation moved holdings from indirect to direct ownership **with no change in share count or economics** — a structural/estate step, not a sale. No MVS disposition has been reported.
- **Directional signals (negative):** (i) Historical pattern of **selling-shareholder secondary offerings** by Dovigi-affiliated Josaud II Holdings and by the sponsor group (BC Partners / OTPP; e.g., secondary closed March 31, 2025) — the controlling bloc has been a persistent net supplier of subordinate voting shares. (ii) **No open-market insider purchases** identified despite the stock trading ~18% below the rejected $52 take-private bid. (iii) The company **repurchased zero shares in Q3 2026** despite an authorisation — buybacks were not used to signal confidence into the take-private rejection.
- **Interpretation:** Insider conviction is expressed through *refusing to sell control* and *wanting to roll into a buyout*, not through buying equity at market. That is a valuation-defence posture, not a shareholder-alignment posture.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Key-person concentration at the extreme.** A single founder is Chairman, CEO, 94% vote controller, and the stated permanent leader of any future transaction. No succession plan is evidenced. His personal margin-loan pledging links the control block to share-price volatility — a margin call could force a control event at the worst possible moment.
2. **SECURE integration plus roll-up indigestion.** SECURE closed Sept 1, 2026; YTD acquisitions represent **~$2B of annualised revenue** on a ~$8.1B revenue base. **Goodwill more than doubled to $12,583M from $6,895M — roughly 85% of market capitalisation.** A single large, dilutive, cyclical (Alberta oilfield waste) deal absorbed into a levered balance sheet creates real impairment and integration-execution risk, with the CFO of record and a new COO (Allen Gransch, arriving *via* SECURE) still settling in.
3. **Landfill/environmental operating liability and capex dependence.** Leachate, odour and TRS control at legacy sites are chronic failure points (Stoney Creek being the live example); remediation, cell construction and closure obligations are long-tailed, and permit/community opposition is the binding constraint on volume growth. Layered on: "mid-3s" net leverage target, ~$455M cash interest, diesel and commodity sensitivity.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **ELEVATED (localised, not systemic)**

- **Active brand damage in Hamilton, Ontario.** Stoney Creek odour complaints since 2023 ("rotten fish," "sulphuric"), residents reporting headaches and nausea, a **guilty plea**, and **~70 individual lawsuits** filed (nuisance, negligence, statutory breach; $250k claimed each) via Napolilaw and McKenzie Lake. Municipal-approval risk and potential class-action escalation are live.
- **Take-private failure is a shareholder-relations wound.** The board rejected a **$52/share Blackstone/KKR/Energy Capital Partners** bid (stock $45.88 pre-announcement) while seeking ~$58; shares fell **~7.6% to $42.41** on the news (intraday declines reported as steep as 8–14%). Management had spent five months signalling interest, and the CEO said he would stay regardless of outcome. Expect **activist pressure, criticism of the process, and possible litigation** over whether the special committee adequately pursued value. The "management wanted too much / founder wouldn't sell control" narrative is already forming.
- **Countervailing positives:** NWRA 2026 Recycling Facility of the Year (Montréal-East MRF), sustainability recognitions (Squamish, Victoria). These are real but small against odour litigation.
- **ESG controversy score: high on community/environmental controversy, moderate on governance, low on social/diversity disclosure.** Note that a 2020 **Spruce Point Capital short report** (opaque model, understated cash burn, aggressive accounting) was rebutted and never substantiated by a restatement or SEC action — but a very wide **GAAP-to-"Adjusted" gap persists** (9M 2026: net loss from continuing operations **$409.8M** vs. positive Adjusted Net Income), which keeps the accounting-scepticism constituency alive.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

The operating business and financial trajectory are performing (Q3 2026: revenue +27.2%, Adjusted EBITDA +26.8%, guidance raised for the third time, 7.4% organic growth, AFFCF tracking ~$1.02B). But **non-financial risk is governance-dominated and the governance is genuinely poor**: a 94%-vote controlling founder who is simultaneously Chair and CEO, ~30% of independent directors rejected by the minority, pledging, undisclosed related-party dealings, and a compensation model without performance or holding conditions. Regulatory and reputational risk are moderate-to-elevated and concentrated in landfill operations and the failed sale process. This combination — a levered roll-up run with minority-shareholder disenfranchisement — warrants a discount, not a premium, to waste-sector peers. **Rating: HIGH (one notch below Critical; would escalate if pledging or related-party issues crystallise into a control event or enforcement action).**

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Take-private process conflict.** The CEO/94% vote controller publicly committed to rolling his entire stake and remaining CEO in any transaction — meaning he was negotiating the price of his own continuation with bidders while the board fenced only the announcement side of the process. Escalate for legal review of the special committee's mandate and disclosure adequacy.
2. **Share pledging under margin loans (controlling block).** Unquantified, undisclosed magnitude. A margin call at GFL would be a forced control event. **Demand disclosure of pledge ratio and loan-to-value; this is the single least-priced risk here.**
3. **Incomplete related-party transaction disclosure** (Glass Lewis) — persistent, unresolved, and now compounded by a TPG-affiliated director arriving through the SECURE deal.
4. **~30% withheld votes against five of eight directors, and 29% against the special committee chair** — board legitimacy is impaired, yet unaddressable due to the MVS structure. Treat any future related-party or M&A transaction as presumptively conflicted.
5. **Goodwill $12.58B ≈ 85% of market cap** after SECURE. Any softness in Alberta oilfield volumes or a re-rating of acquired assets risks a large non-cash impairment and a credibility hit to the "mid-3s leverage" story.
6. **Stoney Creek litigation cluster (~70 suits) plus a guilty plea and fresh charges** — precedent setting for other legacy landfill communities; watch for class certification.
7. **Compensation without performance or holding requirements** while the founder is pledged — incentives favour short-term share-price defence (including rejecting a 13%-premium bid) over durable per-share value.
