## DLTR — Dollar Tree, Inc. | Value Investment Analysis
*Price $154.65 (Nov 24, 2026 close) · ~196M sh · Mkt cap ~$30.3B · Net debt (ex-lease) ~$1.5B · EV ~$31.8B*

**Important context:** DLTR is no longer the company in most databases. Family Dollar was sold to Brigade/Macellum, closing July 2025 for ~$1.01B headline (~$800M net proceeds) — against an original purchase price of ~$8.5B plus ~$2B of subsequent investment. All figures below are the post-divestiture, pure-play Dollar Tree U.S. + Canada business (9,511 stores).

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: NARROW (trend: narrowed 2019–2024, now stabilizing)

Dollar Tree runs a genuine value proposition: a "treasure hunt" assortment at entry-level fixed price points, in 9,511 small-format stores that Walmart and Amazon cannot economically replicate on a corner-by-corner basis. The multi-price conversion (~7,400 of 9,511 stores now carry $1.25/$3/$5 tiers) has restored the pricing flexibility that a rigid $1.00 price point destroyed during the 2021–23 inflation shock. Q3 FY2026 evidence is encouraging: comps +4.6%, and critically **traffic +3.8%** rather than ticket-driven growth — that is a healthy demand signal, not a price-hike artifact.

But the honest moat assessment is narrow:
- **No switching costs, no network effects.** The customer is the most price-elastic in America and will walk across the street.
- **No supplier pricing power.** Gross margin of 35.7% is set by the market, not by DLTR.
- **Scale advantage is real but not decisive** — ~$20.7B revenue buys purchasing scale, yet Walmart (~50x the size) and Dollar General (20,000+ stores) sit on the same trade areas.
- **Returns clear the bar, but not by much:** ROIC has improved 11.1% → 12.1% → 12.4% → **14.8%** (FY22–FY25) versus a ~8.5% WACC. On a pre-lease, tangible-capital basis (invested capital ~$5.5B post-writedowns, since only $423M of goodwill survives) returns are closer to ~22%. Value-creating, yes; a fortress, no.
- **The moat's history is one of self-inflicted damage:** the 2015 Family Dollar acquisition, the 2019 store-safety crisis, and the 2024 guidance collapse. This is a business that has repeatedly lost more value through management decisions than through competition.

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **6 / 10**

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B, cont. ops) | 15.41 | 16.78 | 17.58 | **19.41** |
| Gross margin | 37.5% | 35.9% | 35.8% | **36.4%** |
| Operating margin | 13.6% | 10.6% | 8.3% | **8.2%** |
| ROIC | 11.1% | 12.1% | 12.4% | **14.8%** |
| Capex ($B) | 1.25 | 2.11 | 1.30 | **1.13** |

**What's good:** revenue compounding at 5–10%; gross margin stable and slightly improving; revenue is non-discretionary (consumables), so it is recession-resilient; balance sheet is sound — net debt ~$1.5B ex-leases (~0.6x EBITDA), no dividend obligation, $966M cash at Q3.

**What's not:** operating margin has fallen ~540bp in four years and has not recovered — this is a structurally thinner business than the one the market valued at 25x in 2021. Cash conversion is the bigger issue: **owner earnings (NI + D&A − *all* capex) were just $798M in FY2025, only 62% of net income.** Reported FCF of $1.40B looks better largely because the cash-flow statement still carries Family Dollar stub contributions. Normalized, sustainable FCF is closer to **$1.2–1.3B**, against a 6% of sales capex treadmill for store conversions and new builds. That is mediocre FCF conversion for a "defensive" retailer.

Q3 FY2026 showed the fragility: revenue +6.9% yet operating margin contracted 110bp (6.1%), with 195bp from the net cost of tariff reinvestments. Management is choosing to absorb tariffs into price/donations rather than pass them through — admirable for the franchise, painful for reported earnings.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Multiples at $154.65:**
| Metric | DLTR | Dollar General (DG) |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (GAAP trailing) | 26.0x | 20.2x |
| P/E (normalized) | **~20.6x** | ~20x |
| EV/EBITDA (ex-lease) | ~12.8x | 13.9x |
| P/FCF | **~24x** | 12.8x |
| FCF yield | **~4.1%** | 7.8% |
| ROIC | 14.8% | 9.1% |
| Dividend | None | 1.7% |

Normalization is essential here. FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance is **$8.05–8.25**, but that includes ~$0.65/share of one-time IEEPA **tariff refunds**. Strip it and true earning power is **~$7.50**, i.e. **20.6x**. Note the divergence from DG: DLTR earns similar P/E credit but delivers roughly *half* the free cash flow yield and pays no dividend. On the metric that matters most to an owner, DLTR is materially more expensive than its closest comparable.

**Intrinsic value (owner-earnings DCF, normalized FCF $1.25B):**
- Base case: 6% FCF growth yrs 1–5, 4.5% yrs 6–10, 8.5% WACC, 2.5% terminal → **EV ~$26.6B, equity ~$25.1B, ~$128/share**
- Bull case (op margin recovers to 10% on multi-price mix, FCF $1.4B, 8% growth, 8% WACC): **~$175/share**
- Bear case (comps fade to 2%, tariffs stick, capex stays elevated): **~$95/share**

**Fair value range: $125–160; base ~$140.** At $154.65 the stock trades at a ~10% *premium* to my base case and roughly at the midpoint of the fair value range. The implied growth at today's price (~6% perpetual FCF growth) is entirely reasonable — which is the problem: nothing is priced in your favor.

The stock is up **~78% from its May 2026 low of $86.80**, including +12% on the Q3 beat. That move was driven by a guidance raise that was itself partly a one-time tariff refund. Momentum, not value.

---

### 4. Management & Capital Allocation: **Adequate, unproven**

- **Mike Creedon (CEO since Dec 2024)**, a long-tenured internal operator (ex-COO). Operational execution since taking over has been credible: traffic inflection, 800 multi-price conversions/quarter, comps at the high end.
- **The Family Dollar exit was the right decision, executed at a terrible price.** Writing a ~$9B+ cheque and selling for ~$800M net is the single largest capital-allocation failure in the company's history — and it defines the culture Creedon inherits. Credit for stopping the bleeding; no credit for the entry.
- **Post-divestiture discipline looks better:** $1.548B of buybacks in FY2025, another $205M in Q3 FY2026, **$2.3B authorization remaining (~7.6% of market cap)** — meaningful support and deployed at $114–150, broadly in line with intrinsic value. No dividend, which is appropriate given the reinvestment needs.
- **Gap in my analysis:** I could not verify current insider ownership or recent insider transactions from available data; historically DLTR insider ownership has been low (<1%), typical of a professionally-managed retailer. Treat that as an unquantified negative, not a positive.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (Do not initiate at $154.65)

This is a decent, improving, cash-generative retailer with a narrow moat, a scarred capital-allocation history, and a visible multi-year self-help story. It is **not** a bargain.

- Fair value ≈ **$140** (range $125–160) vs. price **$154.65** → **negative margin of safety**.
- Requiring a Buffett-standard 20–30% discount to base intrinsic value implies an accumulation zone of **~$100–112**.
- The business must still prove that 8% operating margins are a floor and not a waypoint. Operating margin is down 540bp in four years; owner earnings cover only 62% of net income. Those are not the marks of a wide-moat compounder deserving a premium multiple.

**What would make me a buyer:** price below ~$115 (which the market offered as recently as September/October 2026), *or* evidence of two to three consecutive quarters of operating-margin expansion to ~9% organically (not via tariff refunds) with FCF conversion above 90% of net income. Absent either, I'd rather own Dollar General at 12.8x free cash flow with a dividend.

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Tariff/import exposure (highest impact).** DLTR's assortment is import-heavy; Q3 alone absorbed 195bp of margin from tariff reinvestments. A shift in tariff policy or refund treatment swings EPS by 8–10%.
2. **Low-income consumer health.** The core shopper is stretched. Rising unemployment or reduced SNAP benefits would hit traffic first and margins second — and traffic is the one thing management has just fixed.
3. **Multi-price execution risk.** Converting 800 stores a quarter is cheap to announce and expensive to get right. If $3/$5 tiers dilute the value perception that drives the treasure hunt, the 3.8% traffic gain reverses.
4. **Competitive price war.** Walmart, Dollar General, Amazon and Temu all target the same basket. DLTR has no tool to defend price other than its own margin.
5. **Capex treadmill / poor cash conversion.** ~6% of sales in capex against ~8% operating margins leaves thin owner earnings. Any cost overrun lands directly on FCF.
6. **Valuation risk is the proximate one.** At ~20.6x normalized EPS and ~24x free cash flow, with no dividend, the stock has no cushion against a single disappointing quarter. The 78% rally off the May low has borrowed returns from the future.
