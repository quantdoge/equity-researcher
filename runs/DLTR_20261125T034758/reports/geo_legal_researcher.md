## Dollar Tree, Inc. (DLTR) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company snapshot:** Discount retailer, ~$19.4B FY2025 net sales (continuing operations, 52 weeks ended 1/31/26), ~8,000+ Dollar Tree stores in the 48 contiguous states plus ~275 Canadian stores in seven provinces. Family Dollar was divested to Brigade Capital / Macellum for $1.01B, closing July 7, 2025, and is now reported as discontinued operations — meaning the residual business is a **near-pure US retailer with a long import supply chain**. Q3 FY2026: net sales $5,073.7M (+6.9%), comps +4.6%, operating income $308.6M (−10.1%).

---

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

| Geography | Revenue Exposure | Basis | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | ~100% (essentially all) | 48 contiguous states; FY2025 10-K states revenue and assets in Canada "are not material" | **Low–Medium** |
| **Canada** | Not material (<~2%, not separately disclosed) | ~275 stores, 7 provinces; CAD $1.75 price point; "revenue and assets in Canada are not material" (2025 Annual Report) | **Low** |
| **China (sourcing, not selling)** | Cost-side, not revenue-side | "China is the source of the majority of our direct imports" (FY2025 10-K) | **Medium–High** |
| **Other Asian sourcing (Vietnam, etc.)** | Cost-side | Diversification away from China; Section 301 exposure broadened to 60 economies in July 2026 | **Medium** |

**Key insight:** DLTR's geographic risk is *inverted* versus a typical multinational. It sells almost entirely into one jurisdiction, so there is no currency-translation, repatriation, expropriation, or EM-sovereign exposure. The real geographic risk sits in the **cost of goods** — i.e., the import origin of merchandise — and in the US policy regime that taxes those origins.

---

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Tariff-regime volatility (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
The IEEPA tariffs imposed in early 2025 were **struck down by the Supreme Court in February 2026**, triggering a CBP refund process launched April 20, 2026. DLTR submitted claims of **$379.0M** and, through October 31, 2026, had received **$372.4M in refunds plus $14.4M of interest** (ProPublica tariff-refund database; Q3 FY2026 10-Q). But the structural exposure persists: Section 301 tariffs remain in force, a July 24, 2026 action extended Section 301 duties to imports from 60 economies at 10–12.5%, and the 178 China product exclusions expire **January 9, 2027**, with the US–China trade truce expiring **January 10, 2027**. DLTR is already spending the refunds — $27.0M cost-of-sales and $75.6M SG&A of "tariff-reinvestment" costs in Q3, plus ~$40M for a philanthropic fund — which is a reinvestment commitment against a non-recurring cash inflow.

**Risk 2 — Consumer "tariff double-recovery" litigation wave (Probability: High / Impact: Low–Medium)**
*Fennessy v. Dollar Tree, Inc.*, No. 2:26-cv-00932 (E.D. Va., filed Sept 3, 2026), ten named plaintiffs plus nine state-specific subclasses, alleges DLTR raised shelf prices on tariff-affected items (red-stickered, increases up to $1.75) and then collected ~$369M in refunds without rebating shoppers. No class certified, no settlement, allegations unproven. The same template has been filed against Five Below, Target, Amazon and Nintendo. The **reputational/political** dimension is more material than the legal one: ProPublica's November 2026 series explicitly criticizes retailers for using refunds for bonuses rather than repaying consumers, which invites congressional and state AG attention.

**Risk 3 — US–China escalation and sourcing concentration (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium–High)**
China supplies the majority of direct imports. Combined with the January 2027 expiry of both the truce and the Section 301 exclusions, DLTR faces a plausible re-escalation window in its FY2027 sourcing cost base. The company has been re-routing supply chains (Vietnam and other origins), but "majority from China" is a concentrated single-point-of-failure for a chain whose entire brand promise is a fixed/low price point.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Tariff refund class action** — *Fennessy v. DLTR* (E.D. Va.), Judge Elizabeth W. Hanes; named entities include Dollar Tree Sourcing Company LLC and Greenbrier International (the importer of record), which is what creates the "double recovery" theory. **No accrual disclosed; not yet material.**
- **EEOC disability discrimination** — Dollar Tree Distribution settled for **$50,000** plus non-monetary relief (hearing-impaired applicant denied hire).
- **NLRB** — Case 22-CA-371641 reached a bilateral conformed settlement agreement in July 2026; labor-relations activity is live but contained.
- **Wage & hour class action** — overtime claims pending in the Central District of California.
- **State AG action** — a **$560,000 NY AG food-safety violation** settlement against Dollar Tree Stores in 2026 (Violation Tracker/Good Jobs First).
- **Trade-law contingencies** — the 10-Q term set includes "antidumping and countervailing duties," implying AD/CVD exposure and potential duty-evasion scrutiny on import programs.
- **Family Dollar divestiture** — completed July 2025; TSA income of $23.1M in Q4 FY2025 continues to run. *Gap:* I could not verify the specific indemnification/retained-liability terms in the purchase agreement from available sources; this should be checked in the 10-K's commitments and contingencies note.
- **Governance/control** — no going-concern, restatement, or material-weakness language; Q3 CEO/CFO certifications were clean. Debt: $2,934.4M long-term, including a $500M term loan due March 2029; $1.5B undrawn revolver.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** The single-country revenue base is a genuine mitigant — no expropriation, currency-control, or sanctions exposure, and the Canada footprint is immaterial. The rating is held at Medium rather than Low by three factors: (1) a China-concentrated import book sitting directly in the path of a tariff regime that has already been rewritten twice in eighteen months and whose key exclusions lapse in January 2027; (2) an active consumer class action that, while small today, is part of a coordinated multi-defendant wave with political momentum; and (3) routine but persistent employment-law exposure. The Supreme Court's IEEPA reversal and the $372M refund recovery have **reduced** near-term tariff cash risk considerably — but the company has already committed much of that cash to reinvestment and philanthropy, so it is not a durable buffer.

**Monitoring priorities:** the January 9/10, 2027 Section 301 exclusion and truce expiries; any motion to dismiss ruling in *Fennessy*; the 10-K's AD/CVD and divestiture-indemnification disclosures; and any state AG action following the ProPublica tariff-refund reporting.
