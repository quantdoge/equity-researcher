## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: Investment Grade — lower tier (S&P BBB / Moody's Baa3), stable.**
Source: AMCREIS tenant credit profile (S&P BBB, Moody's Baa3). *CDS spreads unavailable — data gap.*

The credit story is a tale of two leverage measures, and the distinction matters enormously here:

| Basis | Gross debt | Net debt | Net debt/EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Including operating leases** (Q3 FY26) | $7.74B | $6.78B | ~2.6x |
| **Excluding operating leases** (Q3 FY26) | $2.93B | $1.97B | ~0.75x |

Lease liabilities ($1.02B current + $3.79B long-term) dominate the debt stack. This is normal for a 9,000-store retailer — but it means the lease book is a **fixed contractual obligation that cannot be cut quickly** in a downturn. On the ex-lease basis DLTR is barely levered; on a lease-adjusted basis it carries mid-2x leverage, appropriate for a BBB retailer.

**Interest coverage is exceptional: 18.7x** (EBIT $1.715B / interest $85.5M, FY2025). Interest expense has fallen from $127M (FY2022) to $85.5M as the 4.00% 2025 notes retired. This is not a company with a debt-service problem.

**No refinancing cliff.** From the debt footnote ladder, the outstanding senior notes are:
- **4.20% Senior Notes due 2028 — $1.25B**
- **2.65% Senior Notes due 2031 — $800M**
- **3.375% Senior Notes due 2051 — $400M**

That is ~$2.45B principal, all fixed-rate, long-dated, unsecured. The next meaningful maturity is **2028**. Liquidity backstop: a **$1.5B revolver** (five-year facility maturing March 21, 2030, plus a 364-day facility) with a maximum leverage and minimum fixed-charge-coverage covenant. Critically, the $1.0B 4.00% notes that appeared as short-term debt on the Jan. 2025 balance sheet were repaid — short-term borrowings are now **$0**.

**Historical earnings quality is the weak link.** FY2023 and FY2024 produced net losses of −$998M and −$3.03B on Family Dollar goodwill impairments. Goodwill is now down to **$422M**, so the impairment reservoir has largely been drained — a genuine de-risking versus 2023–24.

**Altman Z-Score: 2.51 (grey zone) — but misleadingly conservative.** The tool uses book equity in x4 (0.387). Substituting the ~$29B market cap for book equity raises x4 to ~2.6–3.0 and pushes **Z to ~3.8–4.1, comfortably in the safe zone**. The grey-zone reading is an artifact of a thin book equity base created by buybacks, not a solvency signal.

**Piotroski F-Score: 8/9 (strong).** Profitable ROA, positive operating cash flow, improving ROA, clean accruals, lower leverage, no dilution, gross margin improvement, asset turnover improvement. The single miss (F6, positive equity change) is due to buybacks shrinking equity — a deliberate capital allocation choice, not distress.

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue −20%

Base: FY2025 revenue $19.41B, gross margin 36.4%, SG&A $5.469B, EBITDA $2.363B. Assumptions: gross margin −100bp to 35.4% (de-leverage on fixed occupancy costs, promotional response), SG&A down only 8% (store labor and rent are largely fixed), capex cut to ~$700M.

| Line item | Base FY25 | Revenue −20% | Comment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $19.41B | **$15.53B** | Comps −20%, no store closures |
| Gross profit | $7.07B (36.4%) | $5.50B (35.4%) | −100bp |
| SG&A | $5.47B | $5.03B | −8% only |
| EBIT | $1.60B | **~$465M** | Margin 3.0% (from 8.2%) |
| EBITDA | $2.36B | **~$1.11B** | −53% |
| Interest expense | $85.5M | $85.5M | Fixed-rate, no floating reset risk |
| **Interest coverage** | 18.7x | **~5.4x** | Still comfortably above covenant |
| Net debt/EBITDA (lease-adj.) | 2.7x | **~6.1x** | Above likely revolver covenant threshold |
| Net debt/EBITDA (ex-lease) | 0.75x | **~1.8x** | Manageable |
| FCF | $1.40B | **~$0 to +$200M** | Positive but thin |

**Verdict: survivable, but not comfortably.** Even at −20% revenue DLTR services interest ~5x over, generates non-negative FCF, pays no dividend (nothing to cut), and has no maturity before 2028. The binding constraint is the **maximum leverage ratio covenant on the revolver** — at ~6x lease-adjusted the company would likely breach, forcing a covenant reset or equity issuance rather than default. The mitigants are real: $1.5B undrawn revolver, $966M cash, capex flexibility of ~$400M, and a $1.5B+ annual buyback that can be switched off instantly (a ~$1.5B annual liquidity lever). **The failure mode here is a ratings downgrade into BB and a dilutive equity raise — not a liquidity crisis or default.**

A more realistic stress is −10% revenue: EBIT ~$1.0B, EBITDA ~$1.75B, lease-adjusted net leverage ~3.9x, FCF ~$700M. Manageable with buybacks paused.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Leverage creeping up while buybacks run hot.** Long-term debt rose from $2.43B (Jan 2026) to $2.93B (Oct 2026) — roughly **+$500M** — while the company repurchased $1.548B of stock in FY2025 against $1.398B of FCF. Shareholder returns exceeded free cash flow, and part of the funding came from the balance sheet and new borrowings. Equity fell from $3.75B to $3.47B in nine months. This is the clearest negative trajectory in the file.

2. **Thin unadjusted liquidity buffer.** Cash is 27% of current liabilities. The company runs on trade credit (AP $1.865B) and its revolver. A vendor-terms shock or a sudden inventory build would bite quickly. The reported 1.07x current ratio is depressed by $1.02B of current lease liabilities — ex-lease it is 1.58x — but the lease payments are still cash out the door.

3. **High idiosyncratic volatility (43%) on a 0.57 beta.** Two drawdowns of −57.5% (2024) and −38.5% (2026) in a "defensive" stock demonstrate event risk that market exposure does not explain. Position sizing, not beta, is the risk control.

4. **Lowest-rung investment grade (Baa3/BBB).** A downgrade to BB would raise refinancing costs on the 2028 notes and could trigger revolver pricing tiers. There is little cushion between the current rating and high yield.

5. **Tariff and import cost exposure** *(qualitative — external, not tool-verified)*: DLTR's model is built on direct imports at fixed $1.25–$1.50 price points, leaving limited ability to pass through cost inflation without traffic loss. This is the most likely trigger for a gross-margin shock in the downside scenario.

6. **Impairment history.** Two consecutive loss years (FY2023, FY2024) driven by Family Dollar goodwill write-downs. Goodwill is now only $422M, which materially reduces recurrence risk — this flag is fading.
