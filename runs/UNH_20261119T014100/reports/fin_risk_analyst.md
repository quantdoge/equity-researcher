# UnitedHealth Group (UNH) — Financial Risk Assessment

**Price $349.91 | Market cap $313.5B | Sector: Healthcare / Managed Care | FY-end 2025-12-31, latest data Q3 2026 (filed 2026-11-03)**

---

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: INVESTMENT GRADE (high, but deteriorating)** — Moody's A2, S&P A+ (senior unsecured), Fitch IDR A / IFS AA-. **All three agencies carried a NEGATIVE outlook as of 2025–26** on medical cost trend, earnings erosion, and legal overhang. At A-category, there is a 3–4 notch cushion to the IG floor; fallen-angel risk is not a 12-month concern.

The balance sheet is levered but servicable. Gross debt has risen from $46.0B (2021) to $78.4B (2025), while EBITDA *fell* from $27.1B to $23.1B — net debt/EBITDA expanded from **0.91x to 2.18x** (1.8x on TTM Q3'26 EBITDA of $28.0B). Debt-to-capital of 43.5% aligns with S&P's stated 43–45% expectation, which corroborates the data.

Two genuine credit-relevant items:
- **Negative tangible equity:** equity $101.7B less goodwill & intangibles $131.0B = **–$29.3B**. Goodwill is 42% of assets. Asset quality, not solvency, is the weak link.
- **Interest coverage is thin and earnings-volatile:** FY2025 EBIT/interest of 4.74x, vs 7.8x in 2023. TTM recovered to **6.2x** ($23.7B / $3.85B).

This is the single most important market-risk observation: **UNH is not behaving like a defensive stock.** Beta of 0.33–0.50 — extremely low — yet 42% realized volatility and a **61% peak-to-trough drawdown** over three years. Nearly all of that risk is **idiosyncratic**: medical cost shock, the Change Healthcare cyberattack, a DOJ Medicare-billing investigation, and policy/CMS rate risk. For a portfolio manager this is the worst combination — low market sensitivity creates the *illusion* of diversification while the name carries fat company-specific tail risk that hedges won't offset. The 3-year Sharpe of –0.28 confirms a value-destroying stretch; the 1-year Sharpe of 1.26 reflects a sharp recovery off the 2025 low.

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue –20%

Base FY2025: revenue $447.6B, medical cost ratio 81.5%, non-medical opex $63.9B, interest $4.00B, pretax margin just **3.3%**.

Assumes MLR +300bp (adverse mix — membership losses typically shed lower-acuity commercial first) and 45% of opex fixed:

| Line | Base FY2025 | Scenario (–20% rev) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 447.6 | **358.1** |
| Medical costs (84.5%) | 364.7 | 302.6 |
| Gross profit | 82.9 | 55.5 |
| Operating costs | 63.9 | 56.9 |
| **Operating income** | **19.0** | **–1.4** |
| EBITDA | 23.1 | **3.0** |
| Interest expense | 4.0 | 4.0 |
| **EBIT / interest** | 4.74x | **–0.35x (breach)** |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 2.18x | **16.8x** |

**The core vulnerability: a 4% operating margin means operating breakeven arrives at roughly a 16% revenue decline**, and EBIT-based interest coverage breaks near an 12–13% decline. A 20% shock produces an operating loss before interest. Mitigants: $28.1B cash plus positive operating cash throw-off (~$16–20B) covers ~$4B of interest for years, so this is a *ratings and equity* event, not a default event — expect a downgrade to BBB and a **dividend suspension** (but the $7.9B dividend is already only 49% of FCF at trough, so it survives a *mild* stress).

**Reality check on the scenario:** a 20% revenue decline is extreme for a diversified payer. The more probable path is the one already visible — low-single-digit revenue contraction with MLR stuck in the high 80s, which caps EPS and keeps coverage in the 5–6x band.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Split verdict, deliberately: **solvency risk LOW** (A-rated, 1.8x net leverage, $28B cash, no refinancing wall); **earnings and equity-impairment risk HIGH** (3.3–4.4% pretax margin, negative tangible book, revenue now shrinking, 42% vol / 61% drawdown, all three ratings outlooks negative).

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Negative tangible book value (–$29.3B)** — $131B goodwill (42% of assets) from a premium-priced Optum/Change deal stack. Impairment risk is the primary equity risk, and it also reduces the cushion under the A rating.
2. **Operating margin halved in one year** (8.7% → 4.2%); Q4'25 operating income collapsed to $380M on $113.2B revenue (0.3% margin) — a reserve/charge-heavy quarter suggesting weaker reserve adequacy than previously signaled.
3. **Revenue turned negative** (–0.4% YoY in Q3'26) — growth engine has stalled as the company exits unprofitable Medicare Advantage business.
4. **Q3'26 MCR of 88.4% was flattered by ~$610M of favorable reserve development** — underlying margin is ~90–100bp worse than reported. Quality-of-earnings flag.
5. **Capital returns outran deleveraging:** $13.4B of dividends + buybacks in FY2025 vs $16.1B FCF (1.2x) while debt rose — buybacks at $5.5B should arguably have been curtailed.
6. **Legal/regulatory tail:** DOJ Medicare-billing investigation and CMS rate policy remain unbounded wildcards absent from any ratio above.

**Data gaps flagged:** (a) no debt maturity ladder by year; (b) no covenant package or revolver availability disclosed; (c) sector peer set returned pharma names (LLY, JNJ, ABBV) — **not valid comparables** for a managed-care payer; true comps (ELV, CI, CVS, HUM, CNC) were unavailable, so no relative leverage benchmarking is offered; (d) quarterly cost-of-revenue line items are internally inconsistent (Q2'26 implies a 32.7% gross margin vs 19.5% in Q3'26) — all margin analysis above relies on operating income, which reconciles cleanly to the annual statements; (e) TTM free cash flow could not be computed (no quarterly cash flow data).
