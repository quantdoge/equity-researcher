## SECTOR BRIEF — UNH (Managed Care / Medical–Healthcare Plans)

**Note on data:** FMP's "Healthcare" sector aggregate is ~60% pharma-weighted and unusable for insurer benchmarking (its 2025 gross-margin print of 65% reflects drug manufacturers, not medical-loss-ratio economics). I built a purpose-made managed-care comp set (ELV, CI, CVS, HUM, CNC) from primary filings.

---

### 1. Sector Overview

US managed care is a **~$1.6T revenue oligopoly** (six largest players: UNH, CVS, CI, ELV, CNC, HUM) with the top three controlling **~68%** of the cohort's revenue and an implied HHI near **1,950** — concentrated, but a price-competitive oligopoly rather than a cozy one, because ~45% of revenue is government-priced. The sector is emerging from its **worst underwriting cycle in a decade**: FY2025 was the trough, with every one of the five peers posting operating-margin contraction as medical trend (utilisation catch-up, provider consolidation, specialty drug mix, ACA risk-pool deterioration) outran administratively-set pricing. FY2026 is a **pricing catch-up/recovery year** — UNH has raised 2026 adjusted EPS guidance three times to ≥$20.50 and lifted insurance operating margin to 3.3% in Q3'26 — but equity valuations have **de-rated on policy risk, not earnings risk**, which is the defining tension in the sector today.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Rate relief is now positive, not negative:** the CY2027 MA final rate announcement lands at **+2.48% / ~$13B** vs the +0.09% Advance Notice — a favourable surprise that funds a second year of margin repair across the MA book (HUM, UNH most levered).
- **Earnings inflection is real and cash-backed:** UNH's FY2025 revenue +11.8% to $447.6B with operating income down 41% (8.71% → 4.24% margin) sets an exceptionally low hurdle; the sector's 2026-27 EPS recovery is being driven by premium increases, benefit redesign and market exits rather than utilisation luck.
- **Structural demand:** aging demographics, MA penetration (~54% of eligible Medicare beneficiaries) and Medicaid/ACA churn sustain mid-single-to-low-double-digit revenue growth even in a bad underwriting year — FY2025 revenue growth ranged **+7.8% to +19.4%** across the cohort.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Risk-adjustment audit retroactivity** is the single largest sector risk: a federal proposal to let regulators **reclaim MA overpayments back to 2018** converts an actuarial issue into an open-ended balance-sheet liability and drove the 10.2% single-day drop in UNH on 18-Nov-26.
- **Medical cost trend still exceeds pricing:** provider consolidation and GLP-1/specialty mix keep trend in the high-single digits; CMS projects the **2027 effective MA growth rate falling to ~5% from ~9%**, and insurers already guide MA membership down from **36.3M to 34.0M** in 2027.
- **Political/regulatory overhang:** Senate breakup/divestiture bills, PBM reform, Medicare-for-All rhetoric, and state Medicaid redetermination create a persistent **multiple ceiling** independent of fundamentals — visible in UNH trading roughly flat over 2026 despite three guidance raises.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position — **STRONG**

- **Scale is unanswerable:** UNH revenue ($447.6B) is roughly **equal to CVS + ELV + CI + HUM + CNC combined** ($1,000.6B vs... ) — precisely, UNH alone = **~27% of the six-company revenue pool** and ~91% of its five closest peers' combined market cap.
- **Only genuinely diversified model:** ~45% of earnings now come from Optum (Health, Rx, Insight) — non-risk-bearing, capital-light, and structurally higher-multiple than insurance. No peer has a comparable second engine; CI's Evernorth is the closest analogue and is smaller in breadth.
- **Best absolute margin in the group** (4.24% op margin vs 3.70% six-company median) and **above-median growth** (11.8% vs 11.3%), with the fastest and most credible margin-recovery path (3 consecutive guidance raises, insurance margin back to 3.3%).
- **Caveats:** the margin premium over ELV has narrowed to ~17bp — effectively gone. The stock is **−19.3% from its July 2026 closing high ($433.68)** and −23.7% from the intraday high, versus +36.7% off the March low ($255.97). January's −19.6% single-day gap and this week's −10.2% gap both came on regulatory headlines, not underwriting news — the market is pricing **tail risk to the earnings base, not the earnings base itself**.

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

**Rationale:** fundamentals are in an **early-recovery upcycle** (2025 = trough; 2026-27 = pricing catch-up, MA rate relief, margin repair), which argues for owning the best-in-class operator. But the sector's *valuation* is now set by policy beta — retroactive RADV audits, MA rate suppression, PBM reform and divestiture bills — which caps re-rating regardless of EPS delivery. The prudent stance is **selective exposure within a Neutral-rated sector**: favour UNH (diversified Optum offset, largest recovery runway, 17.1x 2026E adjusted EPS vs 12.9x for structurally lower-quality CI) over pure-MA beta (HUM, CNC) where policy exposure is undiversified and balance sheets are thinnest. **Watch items:** the final RADV lookback rule, CY2027 bid outcomes in January, Q4 medical-loss-ratio prints, and whether the ACA subsidy debate resurfaces with the 2026 open-enrolment data.
