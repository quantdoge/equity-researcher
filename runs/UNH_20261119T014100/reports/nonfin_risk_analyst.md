# UnitedHealth Group (UNH) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment (as of Nov 18, 2026)

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (with material blemishes)**

**Evidence supporting adequacy:**
- Board is genuinely independent and unusually deep in healthcare/regulatory expertise: Scott Gottlieb (former FDA Commissioner), John Noseworthy (former Mayo Clinic CEO), Valerie Montgomery Rice, Charles Baker (former MA Governor), F. William McNabb (former Vanguard CEO). Madeline Bell (ex-CHOP CEO) added Nov 9, 2026 — a standard, sensible refresh.
- Director ownership is real, not nominal (Flynn ~17.2k shares, McNabb ~15.8k, Noseworthy ~7.9k).
- CEO alignment is exceptional: Hemsley holds **1,055,339 shares** directly — a very large personal stake.
- All Sept/Oct/Nov 2026 Form 4s are routine option awards (Oct 1 grants), dividend equivalents, and tax withholding — **no irregular related-party or preferential treatment signals**.

**Evidence against (the blemishes):**
- **Chairman/CEO duality confirmed.** Hemsley signs the Q3 2026 10-Q certification as *"Chair and Chief Executive Officer"* — concentrated power, and a reversal of the post-2006 governance reform that had separated the roles.
- **Hemsley is a returned, septuagenarian CEO** reinstalled mid-crisis — i.e., the board concluded no internal successor was credible. That is itself a succession-planning failure signal.
- **Very recent CFO turnover**: Wayne DeVeydt now signs as CFO. A new CFO inside a DOJ-investigation window raises disclosure-controls transition risk.
- Several directors hold token positions (Gottlieb 921 shares, Baker 2,668, Gil 6,667) — alignment for outside directors is thin.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **THE DOMINANT RISK**

**Active/unresolved:**
- **DOJ criminal AND civil investigation.** UNH publicly confirmed it has begun complying with **formal criminal and civil requests** (company statement, July 24, 2025). Reporting indicates the criminal probe extends **beyond Medicare** into other matters. No resolution, no charge quantified. This is an open-ended tail risk on the equity.
- **Qui tam / FCA litigation (Poehling, C.D. Cal.):** A court-appointed Special Master ruled **for UNH** (Mar 2025) on ~$2.1B of alleged MA overpayments, finding the government's case "depends entirely on speculation." Favorable — but the DOJ was widely expected to appeal, and one adverse ruling does not dispose of the broader investigation.
- **CMS "CRUSH" proposed rule (Nov 16, 2026):** fraud/waste/abuse rule with tighter Medicare Advantage oversight, and — critically — a mechanism to **recoup MA overpayments dating back to 2018**. Retroactive recoupment across the industry's largest MA book is the single largest quantifiable policy liability now on the table.
- **Break Up Big Medicine Act** (Warren/Hawley): would bar insurers, PBMs, and drug distributors from owning providers. Aimed precisely at the UnitedHealthcare + Optum vertical model. Passage unlikely as drafted; reintroduction plus rhetorical escalation is a persistent headline/multiple drag.
- **Antitrust:** DOJ non-public antitrust investigation disclosed to the company Oct 10, 2023; current status not confirmed in available sources.
- **Medicare for All** renewed push (Sanders) — low probability, existential impact; contributes to a structurally capped valuation multiple.

*Note: EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned no matching SEC enforcement actions against UNH — litigation exposure runs through DOJ/CMS, not SEC administrative proceedings.*

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis: **NEUTRAL-NEGATIVE (low current, severe legacy issue)**

**Trailing ~12 months:** Predominantly **non-discretionary** activity.
- **No open-market insider purchases** found in the recent window (notable: after the Nov 18 −10% drawdown, absent insider buying is a weak signal).
- Discretionary sales are **small and administrative**: Patrick Conway (CEO, Optum) sold 1,169 sh ≈ $455,910 (Aug 21, 2026 @ ~$390) and 500 sh ≈ $205,000 (Aug 5, 2026 @ ~$410). Aggregate insider sales ~$0.9M over 90 days — de minimis against compensation.
- Sales occurred in the **$390–$410** zone, i.e., insiders did not sell into the July highs or buy the March lows. No strong information signal either way.

**⚠️ The legacy red flag (must be weighed):** In April 2024, Senators Warren and Markey and Rep. Auchincloss plus 13 House members formally **urged the SEC to open an insider-trading investigation** into **$101.5M of executive stock sales** — including **~$85M by Chair Stephen Hemsley** — executed *after* UNH received DOJ's confidential antitrust notice (Oct 10, 2023) but *before* public disclosure (Feb 26, 2024). Whether the SEC opened a formal inquiry is **not confirmed in available sources — this is a gap requiring verification.** If a live SEC matter exists, governance rating drops to Weak.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Cybersecurity / single-point-of-failure infrastructure.** The Change Healthcare attack remains the defining event: **$799M in final recovery costs** within a **$2.88B pre-tax Q4 2025 charge**. Optum Insight's clearinghouse processes a huge share of U.S. healthcare claims — a systemic concentration risk that invites heightened regulatory oversight and class litigation.
2. **Margin/turnaround execution under policy shock.** FY2025 adjusted MCR of **88.9%**; Medicare Advantage membership **down 935,000 since YE2025**. The recovery is being delivered by raising premiums, cutting benefits, and exiting markets — levers that are finite, politically inflammatory, and only lifted insurance margin to **3.3%**. Thin buffer against any adverse rate or recoupment outcome.
3. **Key-person and executive churn.** Chair/CEO duality in the hands of a returning CEO; new CFO; and repeated leadership disruption at segment level (UnitedHealthcare CEO role following the Dec 2024 killing of Brian Thompson; Optum CEO turnover). Institutional knowledge and regulatory-relationship continuity are impaired during an active DOJ engagement.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH / STRUCTURAL**

- UNH is the **pricing symbol of public anger at the U.S. health insurance system**, crystallized by the December 2024 killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson and the ensuing nationwide commentary on claim denials and prior authorization. This is not a passing news cycle — it is a durable social and political liability that keeps the company at the center of legislative targeting.
- Active negative-press vectors: Warren's breakup campaign; the "UnitedHealth Group Abuse Tracker" maintained by Economic Liberties; DOJ probe coverage; MA audit/overpayment narratives.
- **Shareholder litigation solicitation (Nov 12, 2026)** from a plaintiffs' firm soliciting investors over alleged **fiduciary-duty breaches by insiders** — the leading edge of derivative litigation, which typically follows DOJ disclosure events.
- Market signal of reputational/policy risk intensity: **Jan 27, 2026 −20% single-day drop** (66M shares, ~10x normal) on the Q4 print; **Nov 18, 2026 −10.2%** ($389.68 → **$349.91**, 17.1M shares vs ~4.4M average) on the CRUSH rule. The stock is ~24% below its July 2026 intraday high ($458.80) despite three guidance raises — the market is discounting policy tail risk, not earnings.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Earnings momentum is real (2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised three times to ≥$20.50; Q3 adjusted EPS $4.69 vs $4.15 consensus), and the Poehling ruling plus strong director expertise argue against a Critical rating. But the combination of an **unresolved DOJ criminal investigation**, a **retroactive MA recoupment mechanism**, a **legislative assault on the Optum vertical model**, and a **structurally hostile public reputation** means a material portion of enterprise value sits outside management's control. The non-financial risk profile is not investable-ignorable at this position size.
