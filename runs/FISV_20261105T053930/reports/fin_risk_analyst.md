# FISERV, INC. (FISV) — Complete Financial Risk Assessment

**Price $62.05 | Market cap $33.0B | Net debt $28.3B | EV ~$61.3B | FY2025 (period end 2025-12-31)**

**Context that frames everything below:** Fiserv is a leveraged payments/processing franchise that just suffered a disclosure and credibility shock. The stock fell from a $237.79 close (2025-03-03) to $57.79 (2026-02-03), a **−75.7% close-to-close decline**, including a **−44% single day on 2025-10-29** ($126.17 → $70.60) on a guidance cut and leadership shakeup (Takis Georgakopoulos now CEO). This is an *idiosyncratic* event, not a market drawdown — which matters enormously for how beta should be read.

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: INVESTMENT GRADE — lower tier (Moody's Baa2, stable outlook; S&P BBB equivalent).** S&P-adjusted leverage is 3.4x on $29.4B adjusted debt. Investment grade is intact, but the cushion has narrowed materially and the trajectory is wrong.

Coverage is the weakest link. EBIT/interest has fallen from **4.88x (FY2023) → 4.64x (FY2024) → 3.72x (FY2025)**. Interest expense has risen **+105% since FY2022** ($746M → $1,531M) and **+24% in FY2025 alone**, driven by both absolute debt growth and a rising effective rate (~4.5% in FY2023 → **~5.66% in FY2025** on average debt). EBITDA/interest is 5.85x — adequate, but D&A of $3.16B is ~35% of EBITDA, so the gap between the two coverage measures is unusually wide.

Critically, the debt increase was **not** used to fund growth. FY2025 share repurchases of $5.899B equaled **137% of free cash flow** ($4.299B); total debt rose $4.17B. Over FY2024–FY2025, $11.74B of buybacks were funded against $9.36B of FCF, with debt up $6.76B. Shareholder returns were debt-financed into a deteriorating operating trend — a classic credit deterioration signal.

**Refinancing environment is a mild headwind, not a cliff.** The Treasury curve (as of 2026-11-05) is upward sloping and un-inverted: 2Y 4.74%, 5Y 4.93%, 10Y 5.13%, 30Y 5.47%, 2Y-10Y +39bp. A Baa2 5Y new issue prices near 6.0% (5Y UST + ~110bp), roughly 35bp *above* the current 5.66% blended coupon — so each refinanced tranche adds modestly to interest expense. Fiserv is still accessing unsecured markets (recent Euro senior unsecured issuance), which is the key positive.

**Altman Z-Score: 1.27 — Distress zone** (safe >2.99). Adjusted for market capitalization rather than book equity (x4 = $33.0B/$54.3B = 0.607 vs. 0.475 book), the Z-Score is only **~1.35** — still deep distress. The score is structurally depressed by the balance sheet composition (x1 = 0.0095, negligible working capital), not by imminent default. Treat the tool's explicit caveat seriously: this is indicative, and for an IG-rated, FCF-generative issuer, the Z-Score overstates bankruptcy probability substantially.

**Piotroski F-Score: 5/9 — Moderate.** Points lost on leverage (F5=0), equity change (F6=0), gross margin improvement (F8=0: 59.4% vs 60.8%, −141bp), and asset turnover improvement (F9=0). Points earned on profitability, positive operating cash flow, ROA improvement (mainly from a shrinking asset base, not better returns), accruals quality, and no dilution.

**The beta of 0.628 is a trap and I want to be explicit about it.** A 0.63 beta implies the stock falls 63% as fast as the market. In reality this stock lost ~76% of its value while the S&P rose. Beta is low precisely *because* the decline was idiosyncratic — it is uncorrelated with market direction, not insulated from it. Any risk model that sizes this position off a 0.63 beta is understating the true risk by a wide margin. Downside correlation to the market would rise in a genuine recession scenario (payments volumes are GDP-linked), so the realized beta in a stress is likely materially higher than the historical beta.

**Tail risk is extreme and event-driven.** Three separate single-day gap-downs exceeding 14% occurred within twelve months:
- 2025-05-15: −14.3% ($184.07 → $159.13, 23.3M shares)
- 2025-07-23: −20.3% ($165.98 → $143.00, 29.0M shares)
- 2025-10-29: **−44.0%** ($126.17 → $70.60, **103.5M shares**)

That third day is the signature of a disclosure/credibility reset, not a cyclical selloff. It also means the 49% three-year volatility figure *understates* the current regime — the trailing twelve months alone runs far hotter. A −0.68 Sharpe is a rigorous verdict: investors have been paid nothing for this volatility.

**The scenario is survivable, and the single reason is the buyback.** FY2025 repurchases of $5.899B were entirely discretionary — more than double the $2.3B EBITDA shortfall in the 55% case. Suspending buybacks (which new management has effectively already flagged by prioritizing deleveraging) converts a 20% revenue decline into a *deleveraging* event rather than a liquidity event: FCF of $2.72B still covers $1.53B of interest 1.8x, covers capex, and there is no dividend to defend.

**What actually breaks in the stress:**
1. **Net leverage of 4.3–4.6x breaches the ~3.5x tolerance consistent with Baa2.** A downgrade to Baa3/BBB− is the base case in a 20% revenue decline, and to sub-investment grade at the severe end.
2. **Coverage of ~2.0–2.3x** is thin for a company with a 60%-goodwill balance sheet. It remains well above 1.0x, so solvency is not in question over a 12-month horizon.
3. **Goodwill impairment becomes probable.** With market cap ($33.0B) plus net debt ($28.3B) = $61.3B EV against $51.0B of goodwill+intangibles+PP&E, the market is already valuing the business near 1.2x its goodwill. A 30% write-down ($14.4B) would erase over half of $25.8B book equity, push debt/assets toward 44%, and mechanically deepen the Z-Score distress reading — with no cash impact but real covenant and rating optics.
4. **Liquidity, not solvency, is the binding constraint.** With $798M of cash and a 1.03 current ratio, a downgrade that impaired CP/revolver access would be the genuine transmission channel to distress. This is why the rating matters more than the leverage math.

**Second-order risk I'd flag:** the new CEO "opening the books" is a recurring pattern that precedes *further* negative revisions. A −20% revenue scenario is not a tail case here — revenue growth has already decelerated from +7.6% (2023) to +7.1% (2024) to **+3.6% (2025)**, from a base that management has conceded was flattered.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Split by risk type, because the answer differs materially:

- **Credit risk: MODERATE.** Baa2/BBB investment grade, stable outlook, supported by $4.3B of FCF, 2.8x FCF/interest, no dividend obligation, only $762M of short-term debt, and continued unsecured market access. There is no refinancing cliff and no covenant breach visible. This is a *deteriorating* IG credit, not a distressed one.
- **Equity / market risk: CRITICAL.** −49% annualized volatility, −81% max drawdown, −0.68 Sharpe, three >14% single-day gaps in twelve months, and a governance/disclosure reset with a new CEO still resetting expectations.
- **Liquidity risk: HIGH (but structurally manageable).** A 0.03 cash ratio and 13.7 days of revenue in cash is thin in absolute terms; tolerable only because FCF is large and continuous.

The composite is **High** because the equity is the instrument at risk, and the equity's risk is not mainly about whether Fiserv can pay its debts — it can — but about whether the market's confidence in reported earnings can be restored. That is unresolved.

**The bull case for the balance sheet:** EV/EBITDA of 6.85x, a 12.6% FCF yield, and $5.9B of annual discretionary cash flow against $28.3B of net debt is a genuinely powerful deleveraging engine if management simply stops buying back stock. Fiserv could retire ~20% of net debt in three years from FCF alone with zero growth. The balance sheet is fixable. The credibility problem is the harder one.
