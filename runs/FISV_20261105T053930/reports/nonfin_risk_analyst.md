# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Fiserv, Inc. (NASDAQ: FISV / FI)

**Snapshot:** $62.05/share · ~$33.0B market cap · 38,000 employees · Brookfield, WI · CEO Takis Georgakopoulos (since June 14, 2026) · Beta 0.80
**Data note:** SEC filing-text tool returned no 10-K/DEF 14A for the FISV ticker (Fiserv migrated to "FI" in 2023); board page returned HTTP 403. Governance findings below are reconstructed from 8-K summaries, press releases, and litigation reporting rather than the proxy itself. Flagged as a gap.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK** (trending toward Adequate)

**Evidence for weakness:**
- **Serial CEO turnover — three CEOs in ~13 months.** Frank Bisignano departed May 2025 (confirmed to lead SSA); Mike Lyons took over, executed an "organizational reset" in Oct 2025, then resigned June 12, 2026 to lead Truist; Takis Georgakopoulos appointed June 14, 2026. Lyons received only accrued salary — **no severance, no accelerated equity** — an unusual structure that suggests a board-initiated or contested exit rather than a planned succession.
- **Compensation design is the core allegation.** Plaintiffs in the securities suit assert ~90% of named executives' income was tied to hitting specific targets, creating "strong incentive to commit fraud." Whether or not proven, the plan design is a genuine misalignment flag.
- **Board was publicly blamed by its own activist.** JANA Partners' July 30, 2026 letter faulted the board for "failing to attract and retain talent" and demanded further board change — an unusual, on-the-record indictment of board oversight.

**Evidence of remediation (real, but recent):**
- **CEO/Chair separation restored.** Gordon Nixon (ex-RBC CEO) joined as **independent Chairman in January 2026**, ending the Bisignano-era CEO/Chair duality.
- **Audit Committee strengthened:** Gary Shedlin (former BlackRock CFO) joined as Audit Committee Chair — a credible, technically qualified appointment.
- **Board refreshment underway:** Céline Dufetel added; **Raj Seshadri joins Nov 30, 2026**; Henrique De Castro not seeking reelection in 2027. This is a board responding to pressure, not one ahead of it.

**Verdict:** The *current* board configuration is defensible. The *record* it is cleaning up — duality, churn, an incentive plan alleged to reward the wrong behavior, and a $6.45B write-off of prior capital allocation — is weak. Governance risk is elevated but directionally improving.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Cybersecurity and platform integrity.** The recurring allegation across 10+ lawsuits is missing/inadequate multi-factor authentication on the **Premier core banking platform**, with actual client losses (FiCare FCU reports stolen member funds). For a processor sitting inside thousands of financial institutions, this is an existential-trust risk, not a legal-cost risk. The VikingCloud partnership (Oct 2026, live early 2027) is a merchant-side response and does not address core-platform exposure.
- **Execution risk on a turnaround being run by a five-month-tenured CEO.** 3,000 employee and contractor positions cut (Nov 2026); Project Elevate savings target **doubled to $1B from $500M**; organic revenue flat with a return to growth promised in Q4. Doubling a cost target mid-year usually means either the original target was sandbagged or the revenue hole is deeper than disclosed. Both readings are bad. Institutional knowledge loss in payments tech is not quickly recoverable.
- **Key-person and leadership continuity.** Three CEOs, a CFO transition (Bob Hau out), and a new CLO/CAO in under two years. The bench has been rebuilt twice. Any further senior departure — particularly CFO Todd, who just bought stock — would be read as a crisis signal.

*Secondary:* client attrition in core banking (credit unions actively exiting and litigating), and concentration of profit deterioration in Merchant Solutions/Clover.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **The $6.45B goodwill impairment (Q3 2026)** is a public admission that prior M&A capital was overpaid. It converted a GAAP profit into a **$5.94B net loss / $(11.17) per share**. Whatever the non-cash mechanics, the headline is "Fiserv wrote off six and a half billion dollars."
- **A vendor-accountability campaign is forming against Fiserv.** Credit unions are litigating *and* publicizing — "shockingly easy" cyberattacks, punitive exit fees. This is the worst kind of reputational damage for a B2B infrastructure provider: it is being narrated by its own customers, in public, in a segment where switching costs are supposed to protect it.
- **Political/press entanglement.** Bisignano now runs SSA (and has held an IRS role) while being a named defendant and having sold hundreds of millions in stock ahead of a collapse. Congressional and press attention (NYT, House committee material) attaches reputational risk that Fiserv cannot control.
- **Activist pressure is public and unresolved.** JANA's demand for a full portfolio review and a **sale of the debit network** signals the board has not yet convinced its own shareholders it has a plan.
- **Offsetting:** Q3 2026 beat estimates, guidance reaffirmed, $500M buyback announced, and sell-side tone has turned ("positive inflection"). Reputation with the buy-side is stabilizing faster than reputation with customers.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Three independent, simultaneously-live exposures — an active securities fraud class action with specific and internally-corroborated allegations, a *widening* cybersecurity docket that has survived every dismissal attempt, and a governance record of serial CEO turnover and a $6.45B write-off — would each individually justify a Moderate-to-High rating. They are not diversifying; they share a common root (a growth narrative that outran the underlying business) and a common amplifier (the same 2023–2025 period).

**What keeps this from Critical:** the 2026 insider buying cluster is genuine and broad-based (chairman, CFO, CLO, four directors); the CEO/Chair split has been repaired with a credible independent chairman; the Audit Committee now has a former large-cap public-company CFO; and the False Claims Act matter is closed. The board is behaving like a board that has been frightened, which is historically the second-best time to own a company.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🚩 Bisignano's ~$423M–$700M share sale (2025) ahead of the reset.** Whether or not the ethics-agreement defense holds, the optics are severe and the plaintiffs are building their case directly on it. Escalate to compliance/legal for independent verification of the trading-window and 10b5-1 status of those sales.
2. **🚩 Securities class action has survived to the opposition-to-MTD stage with detailed, non-generic allegations** (Argentina hyperinflation as the true growth driver; "temporary cash grabs"). This is materially more specific than a typical strike suit. Escalate for a litigation-probability reserve review.
3. **🚩 Zero outright dismissals across 10+ cybersecurity suits, with one judge denying the motion "in its entirety."** Adverse precedent is compounding. Escalate for a systemic exposure estimate across the core-banking client base.
4. **🚩 Project Elevate savings target doubled from $500M to $1B in a single quarter, alongside 3,000 job cuts.** Escalate to question whether the incremental $500M reflects newly-discovered cost bloat or a revenue shortfall being masked by expense cuts.
5. **🚩 Three CEOs in 13 months, with the most recent predecessor receiving no severance or accelerated equity.** Escalate to assess whether the board's succession process is functional or whether further turnover is likely.
6. **⚠️ Board refreshment is reactive, not proactive** — driven by JANA's public campaign rather than internal review. Monitor the 2027 annual meeting for a proxy fight.

---

**Bottom line for the investment memo:** Fiserv's non-financial risk profile is **High but improving at the margin**. The financial-engineering era is over and the cleanup is being done in public — impairment taken, costs cut, board rebuilt, insiders buying. The unresolved question is whether the cybersecurity litigation is a legacy cost or an ongoing product defect. Until the core-platform MFA allegations are answered on the merits rather than procedurally, this remains a governance-and-liability story as much as a turnaround story.
