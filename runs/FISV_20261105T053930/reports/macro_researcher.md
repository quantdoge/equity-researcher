# Macroeconomic Assessment — Fiserv, Inc. (FISV)
**Data as of 5–6 November 2026 | Source: FRED, FOMC releases, company filings**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-off / Late-cycle Transitional**

The US is in a **re-acceleration regime, not a landing regime**. Headline CPI has gone from **2.39% YoY (Jan 2026) to 3.72% YoY (Sep 2026)** — and the last three months annualise at **4.5%**, so the trend is accelerating, not merely sticky. The Fed responded by ending its easing cycle and **hiking 25bp on 16 September 2026 to 3.75–4.00%**, then holding on 28 October ("wait for more data on inflation").

The bond market has repriced violently at the long end: the 10Y went from **4.44% (30 Jun) to a 5.31% peak (5 Oct)** — a bear steepener — while the 30Y sits at **5.47%** and the 2s10s is **+39bp**. This is the worst shape of curve for leveraged, long-duration equities: term premium rising, no recession signal, no policy relief.

Offsetting that, the **real economy is genuinely fine**: Q3 2026 real GDP grew **3.35% SAAR (2.07% YoY)**, unemployment is **4.2%**, and **nominal retail sales are +6.19% YoY** (Sep) — i.e. **+2.4% real**. Credit markets are late-cycle complacent: HY OAS at **3.40%**, though that is **+77bp from 2.63% on 31 August**, the only genuine warning light in the set.

**Read-through for Fiserv:** the macro is *not* the reason its organic revenue was flat in Q3 2026. Nominal payment volumes in the US are running mid-single-digit. Fiserv's own Small Business Index showed SMB sales **+2.3% YoY** — roughly **1.4 points *below* CPI**, a real decline. That gap is share loss/attrition/pricing, not a consumer recession.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE** (mildly)

Fiserv is exposed on four fronts simultaneously: (i) a re-accelerating inflation cycle that has restarted Fed hikes and pushed the long end to cycle highs; (ii) **~$28bn of debt** refinancing into a 5%+ world; (iii) a dollar near the top of its range hitting ~20% international revenue; and (iv) an SMB-centric growth engine in exactly the segment most rate-sensitive. What keeps this from "strongly unfavourable" is that **there is no recession in the data** — GDP 3.3%, unemployment 4.2%, nominal retail +6.2%, HY spreads still tight — and the equity has already de-rated to ~9x, so the macro headwinds are substantially in the price rather than incremental to it. The 2026 problem at Fiserv is **execution (flat organic revenue, Clover slowdown, $6.45bn charge), not macro**; macro is the constraint on how fast it can be fixed.

---

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Would Materially Surprise — **Bear: inflation breaks 4%, the Fed hikes again**

CPI is already printing **4.5% annualised** over three months. The consensus assumes the September hike was a one-off. If the next two CPI prints come in at 0.3%+ m/m, the Fed hikes in December or January and 2027 dots shift to a tightening bias.

Chain of consequences: **10Y through 5.75% and 30Y toward 6.0%**; HY OAS from 3.40% back toward 5% (it has already widened 77bp since August — the market is starting to price this); BBB spreads widen with it. Fiserv then refinances 2027–29 maturities at 5.5–6% rather than the assumed 4.5–5%, adding **$150–250m** of interest expense and pushing net leverage (already ~3x EV/EBITDA) toward the ceiling of its comfort zone — right as the $500m Q4 buyback consumes the free cash flow that would otherwise delever. The multiple compresses from ~9x to 7x on FY27 numbers: **a return to $40–45, i.e. –30%.**

**The mirror (bull) case is equally live and just as under-priced:** if the inflation impulse proves to be tariff pass-through that rolls off by Q1 2027, the Fed's September hike is the last one, the 10Y falls back to ~4.5% and the dollar retraces. Fiserv's guided Q4 return to organic growth plus $1bn of Elevate savings and a $500m buyback then re-rate a 9x stock to 11–12x — **$78–85, +25–35%**. Watch the **HY OAS and the 10Y term premium** as the leading signals for which tail you are in.
