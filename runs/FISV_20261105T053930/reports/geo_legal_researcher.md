## 1. Geographic Risk Map

It discloses only that revenue from its three international regions — EMEA, LATAM and APAC — comprised **approximately 16% of total revenue for FY2025**, up from ~14–15% in prior years (2025 Annual Report; FY2024 10-K). FY2025 total revenue was **$21.19B**, +4% YoY (GlobeNewswire, 10-Feb-2026).

**Key structural point:** Fiserv is fundamentally a **US-domestic payments business** (~84% of revenue). Its geopolitical risk is therefore *low on revenue weighting* but *concentrated* — the international book is small enough to be strategically optional, which is precisely why management is now exploring divesting pieces of it.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — Argentina macro/currency shock (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)
The most concrete, named geopolitical exposure. At the KBW Fintech Payments Conference (Nov-2025), then-CEO Mike Lyons attributed Fiserv's guidance misses to "anemic growth in Argentina," noting organic revenue there **more than tripled in 2023, more than quadrupled in 2024, then decelerated to 56% growth in 2025** (Payments Dive, 17-Nov-2025). Argentina remains a live drag: the **Q3 2026 earnings release explicitly cites "Argentine Peso devaluation"** as a factor in Merchant Solutions revenue (Fiserv Q3'26 release, 3-Nov-2026), and the FY2025 annual report flags "exposure to risks related to currency devaluation in certain countries." Under a Milei government that has periodically toyed with FX-band and capital-control policy, a renewed peso step-devaluation or a policy reversal would hit LATAM growth optics disproportionately relative to its revenue share. **This risk has already materialised once — treat it as recurring, not tail.**

### Risk 2 — US payments regulation: interchange legislation and stablecoin re-architecture (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium–High)
Two-sided. (a) The **Credit Card Competition Act** (Durbin–Marshall) keeps threatening issuer/network economics; it was stripped from the Senate housing bill in Mar-2026 but sponsors are actively hunting a new legislative vehicle (Payments Dive). Any routing-competition mandate would compress the economics of Fiserv's issuing-processing and debit-network assets. (b) The **GENIUS Act** (enacted 18-Jul-2025; OCC final rule published as Bulletin 2026-50) creates a federal stablecoin framework that re-routes payment flows away from legacy card rails over time — a structural disintermediation risk. Compounding this: the **CFPB has been largely shuttered since Feb-2025** and has dismissed 23 pending enforcement actions, but **state AGs are explicitly filling the enforcement void** (Gibson Dunn Jan-2026; Jenner & Block). Net: *less* federal consumer-protection risk, *more* unpredictable state-by-state and multi-jurisdictional risk.

### Risk 3 — International/compliance architecture during a forced portfolio restructuring (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium)
Fiserv is simultaneously cutting **3,000 employees and contractors** (Reuters, 3-Nov-2026), doubling Project Elevate savings to **$1B**, and **exploring asset sales including one of its debit networks** (Payments Dive; Milwaukee Business Journal, 8-Sep-2026). It also has new leadership (three CEOs/CFO transitions in ~18 months) and activist shareholders. This is the classic setup for compliance-control degradation: sanctions screening, merchant underwriting (KYC/KYB) and AML monitoring are exactly the functions that get thinned during cost programmes at a merchant acquirer. Add new-market launches (Clover into Japan, Brazil, Mexico) and you have net-new regulatory regimes being entered with a shrinking compliance bench.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Securities class action (the dominant legal overhang).** Consolidated putative class action in the **Southern District of New York**, filed July 2025, class period **24-Jul-2024 to 22-Jul-2025**. Core allegation: Fiserv "forcibly migrated" merchants to Clover in late 2023 through H1 2024, artificially inflating Clover GPV and revenue growth, then disclosed a step-down (Clover GPV growth of only 8% in Q1-2025). Named defendants include **current CEO Michael Lyons and former CEO Frank Bisignano** (now in the US government). Lead-plaintiff contest between the Hollywood, FL police pension fund (Labaton, $1.67M loss) and Luxembourg's Ethenea (Motley Rice, $5.95M loss). **Amended complaint filed 12-Jun-2026; defendants' motion to dismiss filed 11-Aug-2026** — a ruling is the key near-term catalyst. A **second shareholder suit was filed in Nov-2025** ("Fiserv shareholders sue again," Payments Dive, 13-Nov-2025). Fiserv states it "disagrees with the claims and will vigorously defend itself."

**$6.45B Q3-2026 charge / GAAP loss.** Q3'26 GAAP EPS of **$(11.17)** driven by a **$6.45B charge** (impairment-related), with GAAP revenue of $5.20B (−1%) and flat organic revenue; 9M'26 revenue $15.52B (−2%) (GlobeNewswire, 3-Nov-2026). A charge of that magnitude is a *litigation-risk multiplier*: plaintiffs will cite it as corroboration that prior growth claims were not durable, and it complicates the "no scienter" defence.

**Regulatory posture by regime.**
- **US:** OCC/Fed bank-partner oversight of Clover's embedded-finance/lending adjacencies; BSA/AML as a merchant acquirer; state money-transmitter licensing.
- **EU/UK:** GDPR (fines to 4% of global turnover); **EU Instant Payments Regulation** verification-of-payee obligations; **PSD3/PSR**; UK **APP-fraud reimbursement** liability shifting to PSPs.
- **Data privacy named in 10-K:** EU, **Argentina, Uruguay, Brazil, India** — a deliberately EM-heavy list that mirrors the LATAM/APAC footprint.
- **PCI-DSS:** noteworthy that Fiserv signed a multi-year partnership with VikingCloud (Oct-2026) to deliver PCI compliance and cyber-risk tooling to merchants across North America and EMEA — partly a product play, partly a signal of rising merchant-side cyber/compliance expectations.

**No material public GDPR, AML or OFAC enforcement action against Fiserv surfaced in research.** I found none; absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, and this is the single largest gap in my work (see below).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Merchant-acquiring sanctions/AML exposure (structural, unquantified).** A merchant acquirer is a chokepoint: onboarding merchants in high-risk jurisdictions (LATAM, Middle East via EMEA) creates OFAC/UK OFSI screening and BSA/AML liability that scales with merchant count. Given 3,000 job cuts, control-density risk is elevated. No specific incident identified, but this belongs on the diligence list.
2. **Data-privacy jurisdictional sprawl.** Operating under EU GDPR, Brazil LGPD, Argentina PDPA, Uruguay and India DPDP simultaneously — with a large India-based technology/operations footprint — creates data-localisation and cross-border-transfer exposure.
3. **Disclosure-controls stress.** Two guidance cuts, a CEO/CFO churn, a restatement-adjacent impairment, and a securities fraud suit alleging misleading growth statements is a pattern regulators notice. No SEC investigation is publicly confirmed, but the SOX 302 certifications signed 4-Nov-2026 should be read in this context.
4. **Card manufacturing / physical supply chain.** Fiserv's Payments segment includes card manufacturing and print services — physical production is tariff- and trade-route exposed in a way its software peers are not.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale.** The **geopolitical** component is genuinely **low-to-medium**: ~84% of revenue is generated in the US, international is only ~16%, and there is no Russia, China, or broad emerging-market concentration of consequence. The one true hotspot — **Argentina** — is already a realised, recurring earnings drag rather than a latent shock, and management has signalled willingness to shrink non-core geography via divestiture.

The **legal/regulatory** component is **elevated relative to payments peers**: a live SDNY securities fraud class action naming two CEOs, a second suit filed within months, a $6.45B impairment, active interchange and stablecoin legislative risk, and a compliance function being restructured at speed. These are enterprise-level risks, not country-level ones.

**Net: MEDIUM** — driven by legal/regulatory overhang, *not* by geopolitical exposure. The risk is idiosyncratic and US-centric; an investor should monitor the **MTD ruling in the SDNY class action** and **Argentina FX policy** as the two highest-signal catalysts.
