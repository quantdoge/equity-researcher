# Bending Spoons S.p.A. (NASDAQ: BSP) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price $46.29 (Nov 25, 2026) | Mkt cap ~$33B diluted | Pro forma EV ~$41B | Sector: Software / serial acquirer**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW — and it resides in the factory, not in the products.**

Bending Spoons is not a software company in the conventional sense. It is a **capital-allocation and operating-efficiency machine** that buys established-but-stagnant consumer software (Evernote, WeTransfer, Meetup, Remini, StreamYard, Brightcove, Vimeo, AOL, Eventbrite, Airtable, and now Mailchimp) and rebuilds it on a standardized, AI-assisted codebase with a ruthlessly lean cost structure.

What the moat is *not*:
- **No network effects, no meaningful switching costs, no pricing power at the product level.** These are consumer/prosumer subscriptions facing free alternatives and AI-native competitors. Evernote raised prices ~20% in 2025 and *revenue still declined* — that is the signature of a harvested asset, not a franchise.
- **No portfolio coherence.** Evernote, Eventbrite and AOL share nothing but an owner.
- **Organic growth was just 3% in Q3 2026.** Q3 revenue rose 125%, but almost entirely from acquisitions (Airtable alone contributed $38M). Inside the mix, WeTransfer and Komoot grew >30%, while **Remini and Brightcove declined**.

What the moat *is*: a genuine, hard-to-replicate **cost/process advantage** — 2,187 employees, full-stack engineers, centralized infrastructure, and a repeatable post-acquisition playbook (consolidate vendors, rewrite code, cut headcount, reprice). That is real and has produced spectacular results on small deals. But it is an *operational* edge that must be re-earned on every acquisition, not a structural advantage that compounds on its own.

**Direction of the moat: narrowing.** ROIC has fallen 11.6% (2023) → 8.5% (2024) → **7.6% (2025)** — below a ~10% cost of capital. That is the tell: as the company has scaled from $340M Evernote deals to a **$3.68B Mailchimp deal**, it is paying progressively higher multiples for progressively larger, lower-margin, declining assets. Gross margin confirms the mix deterioration: 61.1% (2023) → 65.6% (2025) → **52.9% in Q3 2026** as ticketing (Eventbrite) and hosting-heavy (Brightcove, AOL) revenue displaces high-margin app subscriptions.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **5 / 10**

| Gross margin | 61.1% | 63.9% | 65.6% | **52.9%** |
| Operating margin | 21.8% | 19.0% | 21.3% | 22.0% (adj. OI margin 54%) |
| Net income | $161M | $89.5M | **~$0** | $90M |
| Free cash flow | $55M | $204M | $290M | $458M (9M) |
| ROIC | 11.6% | 8.5% | **7.6%** | n/a |
| Net debt | $100M | $629M | $2,041M | **$3,982M** |

**Strengths:** Asset-light (capex <$6M/yr), cash-generative at the operating line, 54% adjusted operating margin, and a demonstrated ability to convert acquired cost bases into cash.

**Serious blemishes:**
- **GAAP earnings are not meaningful.** FY2025 net income was ~$0 despite $278M of operating income, consumed by $143M interest and ~$111M of non-operating items (FX/fair-value). 2023 net income ($161M) *exceeded* operating income ($84M) — pre-IPO financial-instrument noise. Do not anchor on EPS.
- **FCF is flattered by what it excludes.** The $290M (FY25) / ~$700M (FY26E) FCF line excludes ~$250M/yr of stock compensation (a real cost) and, more importantly, **excludes the ~$2B+/yr of acquisition capital required to produce the growth**. For a roll-up, reported FCF is not owner earnings.
- **Balance sheet is levered and intangible-heavy.** Goodwill + intangibles are $7.47B against $2.61B of equity → **tangible book value of roughly –$4.9B**. Net debt of $3.98B becomes **~$7.3B pro forma** for Mailchimp's $3.31B cash consideration — about **3.5x pro forma adjusted EBITDA**, borrowed at roughly 8%.
- **Dilution is rapid:** diluted shares 549M → 713M in nine months (+30%), with another ~8M to Intuit.

**Verdict:** the income statement is engineered to look excellent on "adjusted" metrics and mediocre on every GAAP and capital-efficiency measure. That is not fraud — it is a business model whose economics only work if the deal machine keeps running.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Pro forma base (post-Mailchimp, closing early 2027):** FY2026 revenue guidance $3.07–3.09B; + Mailchimp ~$1.0B ≈ **$4.1B**. Adjusted operating income ~$1.45B + Mailchimp ~$0.45B ≈ **~$1.9–2.0B**. Cash FCF ~$900M; less ~$250M SBC → **owner earnings ~$650–700M**.

| Multiple | BSP (pro forma) | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| EV / Revenue | ~10x | software peer median P/E 27x implies far lower growth expectations |
| EV / Adj. Operating Income | ~20x | excludes SBC *and* ~$800M amortization of decaying assets |
| EV / Owner Earnings | **~60x** | the honest number |
| FCF yield (pre-SBC) | ~2.7% | vs. 8% cost of debt |
| P / Tangible book | negative | — |

**Intrinsic value (owner-earnings DCF, 10% discount, 2.5% terminal):**

| Scenario | Owner-earnings path | Equity value/share |
|---|---|---|
| Bear — machine stalls, legacy assets decay | $600M, –2% | **~$0** |
| Base — 3% organic, margins plateau | $680M, +5% | **~$5** |
| Bull — M&A stays value-accretive | $680M, +12% | **~$16** |
| Blue-sky — flawless decade of compounding | $680M, **+18% for 10 years** | **~$31** |

Two things make these numbers *generous*, not harsh: (1) they charge nothing for the acquisition capital required to generate that growth, and (2) they already net out SBC. The current price of $46.29 requires roughly **22%+ annual owner-earnings growth for a decade** — from a company growing organically at 3%, levered 3.5x, buying declining assets at rising multiples.

**Fair value estimate: $12–20/share (central ~$15).** A 20–30% margin of safety implies buying below roughly **$11–12**. The stock is ~2.5–3x my estimate of intrinsic value. Sell-side targets span $36–$72 (Wells $52, BofA $38, Bernstein $47, Mizuho $72) — a 2x spread that itself signals how little consensus exists on normalized earnings.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Grade: A on operating skill and owner-orientation; C on capital-allocation discipline at scale; D on shareholder governance.**

- **Owner-operator mindset: yes.** Four co-founders (Ferrari, Patarnello, Danieli, Querella) founded the company in 2013, still run it, and hold **~82.7% of voting power** via 5-vote Class A shares. They took no dividends, pay no dividend, and think in decades. This is exactly the temperament Buffett admires.
- **Operating execution: exceptional.** Turning a $100M-revenue Evernote bought for ~$340M into a cash machine is real skill. The AI-assisted codebase rewrite is a genuine, repeatable cost advantage.
- **Capital allocation at scale: unproven and increasingly aggressive.** Deal size has escalated 10x (Evernote ~$340M → Vimeo ~$1.4B → Mailchimp $3.68B, ~3.7x revenue for a *declining* franchise), funded largely with 8% debt. Management is now underwriting at multiples far above their historical entry points. Empire-building risk is real, and there is no buyback discipline to act as a valuation governor.
- **Disclosure quality: mixed.** Heavy reliance on "Adjusted Operating Income" (excludes SBC and amortization), organic growth disclosed only recently, and a sharp Q3 gross-margin decline that arrived without prominent explanation. Founders' 82.7% voting control means minority holders have essentially no influence over capital deployment.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

Bending Spoons is a genuinely impressive **operator** and, so far, a competent **capital allocator**. It is not, on the evidence available, a great *business* — and it is priced as though it were the best business in software.

The core problem is arithmetic, not opinion:
- **3% organic growth** is what the existing asset base produces on its own.
- **ROIC of 7.6%** is below the cost of capital, meaning recent growth has *not* yet created value per dollar invested.
- **~60x owner earnings and ~10x sales**, with net debt heading to ~$7.3B and tangible equity of –$4.9B, leaves no room for error.
- The value of the enterprise depends almost entirely on **deal flow and continued multiple arbitrage** — an input the shareholder cannot underwrite and that gets harder as AUM grows.

Buffett's test applies cleanly here: *"When a management with a reputation for brilliance tackles a business with a reputation for bad economics, it is the reputation of the business that remains intact."* Bending Spoons may prove the exception because its edge is operational rather than structural. But an exception is not a margin of safety — and at $46, you are paying for the exception plus 22% annual compounding.

**What would change my mind:** sustained organic growth of 8%+; ROIC recovering above 12% as Mailchimp is integrated; gross margin stabilizing above 60%; net leverage below 2.5x; and a demonstrated willingness to *stop* buying when prices are high. A price below ~$15–18 would make this interesting; below $12 it would become a serious candidate regardless of the moat debate.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Roll-up fragility / melting ice cube.** Acquired products are largely in secular decline. Price increases mask user churn until they don't (Evernote 2025). A portfolio of melting ice cubes needs constant refilling — and each refill is bigger and more expensive.
2. **Leverage.** ~$7.3B pro forma net debt at ~8% against ~$2B adjusted EBITDA. A 200bp rate reset or an EBITDA stumble pressures both equity value and refinancing.
3. **Integration risk at unprecedented scale.** Mailchimp ($3.7B) is ~5x larger than any prior deal, in a category (SMB email marketing) with aggressive, well-funded competition and AI disruption.
4. **Goodwill impairment.** $7.5B of intangibles against $2.6B of equity; a single writedown could erase book equity.
5. **Governance.** 82.7% founder voting control; no minority recourse on capital allocation or related-party decisions.
6. **Key-person and cultural risk.** The edge is a small team's operating system; scaling headcount post-IPO without diluting it is unproven.
7. **Lock-up expiry.** IPO was July 2026; the 180-day lock-up rolls off around January 2027, coinciding with the Mailchimp close — a supply overhang against a founder-controlled float.
8. **Valuation compression.** At 60x owner earnings, a de-rating to even 25x would cost ~60% of the share price with no change in operating performance.
