## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional, tilting risk-off.** As of 27 November 2026 the US economy is in a *re-acceleration* regime, not a landing. Q3 2026 real GDP grew **4.3% annualized** (24,665.2 vs 24,408.0 in Q2, GDPC1), unemployment fell to **4.1%** (Oct, UNRATE), and CPI is running **3.82% y/y** (Oct, 337.476). The Fed cut 175bp from 5.33% (Aug 2024) to 3.63% (Jun 2026), inflation re-accelerated, and it has **resumed hiking** — effective fed funds 3.88% in October, with a **25bp hike to 4.00–4.25% expected at the 8–9 December FOMC**. Fed minutes (18 Nov) showed "almost all" officials expect more hikes and **two floated more than 25bp**.

The critical detail is *why* yields are rising. The 10Y went from 4.91% (24 Nov, DGS10) to **5.13%** (27 Nov) — roughly **+22bp in three sessions** — while 10Y breakevens *fell* to 2.12% from 2.38% on 28 Oct (T10YIE). Nominal yields up, inflation expectations down ⇒ **the 10Y real yield has risen to ~2.8–3.0%**. This is a real-rate / term-premium shock, not an inflation scare. That is the most hostile possible configuration for long-duration, leveraged growth assets. The curve is bear-steepening (2Y 4.74%, 10Y 5.13%, 30Y 5.47%, +39bp 2s10s — no inversion).

Credit is repricing but not stressed: HY OAS **3.72%** (24 Nov) vs a 2.66% low on 21 Sep — **+106bp in two months**, yet still tight versus its ~4.5–5% long-run average. Equity markets are attempting a fragile counter-trend bounce (Stoxx 600 +1.7%, Nasdaq 100 pulled back from the brink of a bear market on 26 Nov) even as bonds sell off and a new round of US tariffs on the EU, China and 14 other partners was unveiled 25–26 Nov.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Bending Spoons (BSP) is an Italian-founded, USD-reporting software conglomerate: market cap **$29.4B**, beta **1.48**, Q3 revenue **$780M (+125% y/y, but only +3% organic)**, adjusted operating income **$424M (+130%)**, FY26 guide **$3.07–3.09B revenue / $1.58–1.60B adjusted operating income**. Its model is acquiring mature, proven apps (Evernote, Remini, Meetup, StreamYard, WeTransfer, AOL) and extracting margin via AI-driven code rewrites and price increases. **Growth is acquired, not organic — so the cost and availability of capital is the single most important macro variable for this company.** The CEO stated on the 20 Nov call that BSP could deploy **more than $4–5B on acquisitions in 2027 "depending on financing,"** and it is buying **Mailchimp for $3.68B all-cash, closing early 2027**.

**Valuation context:** at $29.4B market cap, BSP trades at roughly **9.5x FY26 revenue** and **~18.5x FY26 adjusted operating income** — cheap for software, which materially cushions the duration/discount-rate hit relative to peers trading at far higher multiples. Sector peer multiples from the FMP list (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, TSM, AVGO) are not economically comparable to an Italian app roll-up, so I have not used them as a benchmark. The stock is 22% below its 15 Oct high of $59.50 and 14% above its $40.50 first-day close, with extreme daily volatility ($39.95–$46.42 on 25 Nov).

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **Unfavourable**

The negatives are structural and the positives are defensive. Rising **real** yields, a Fed still hiking into re-accelerating inflation, credit spreads widening 106bp in two months, a 5.9% dollar rally and fresh EU-US tariff escalation all press on the same nerve: a high-beta, debt-funded acquirer of long-duration software cash flows that has explicitly tied its 2027 M&A programme to financing conditions. Strong nominal GDP and 3.8% inflation genuinely help its pricing playbook and keep churn low — but they are the reason the Fed is hiking, so they are not a durable offset. I rate this **Unfavourable**, with the caveat that a modest ~18.5x adjusted operating income multiple limits how much further the discount-rate channel can bite.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**Bear: a hawkish acceleration — December +50bp, or a January follow-up hike.** Fed minutes show two officials already floated "more than 25 basis points" in December. If the December CPI print (mid-January) comes in near the **5.7% three-month annualized** pace, the market must price a terminal rate near 4.75% rather than 4.25%. In that world the 10Y breaks 5.50%, HY OAS pushes through 5%, and the Nov 26 equity bounce reverses. BSP would be hit three ways at once: multiple compression on a 1.48 beta, a more expensive Mailchimp close (possibly equity-funded, i.e. dilutive), and a ~28 December lockup expiry into a falling tape. A retest of the **$30** July low (−35%) is the plausible outcome. The symmetry is that this is the *opposite* of what the market is currently doing — equities are rallying on 26 Nov, so the hawkish tail is genuinely under-priced.

**Bull (for balance):** if tariff pass-through laps and inflation rolls over in Q1 2027, the Fed stops after December, the 10Y retreats to ~4.3%, and spreads retighten — BSP re-rates as a cheap, cash-generative roll-up with a large accretive deal funded cheaply, back toward the **$59.50** October high (+29%).
