## 4. Downside Scenario — Revenue –20%

Applied to ~$2,781M revenue and ~$1,293M EBITDA, 61.5% gross margin, SGA ~$681M annualized:

| Scenario | EBITDA | Net debt/EBITDA vs 4.00x cap | EBIT/interest | Post-int. FCF |

| Base | $1,293M | 3.08x ✅ | 1.86x | ~$199M |
| –20% rev, GM held, 10% SGA cut | $1,019M | **3.91x** ⚠️ (2% headroom) | 1.20x | ~$90M |
| –20% rev, GM held, no cost action | $951M | **4.19x — BREACH** | 1.06x | ~$22M |
| –20% rev + GM to 55% (trend continues) | $874M | **4.56x — BREACH** | 0.95x | ~–$55M |

**Covenant math:** at a reported 4.00x leverage cap, EBITDA must stay above **$996M** — only a **23% cushion** from here. Under the more conservative EBITDA definition lenders typically use (excluding add-backs, deducting ~$55M+ SBC), the cushion is materially smaller than headline.

**Liquidity in the downside:** $1,023M cash against a ~$55M cash burn in the severe case gives ~18 months of runway, and $849M of short-term debt sits inside the next 12 months. Manageable, but they would be refinancing into a stressed market at 9%+ while breaching a covenant.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical — the business generates cash, EBITDA covers interest 3.1x, ratings are being upgraded, and the covenant is currently met. But this is a **leveraged, acquisition-addicted, tangibly insolvent balance sheet** with EBIT coverage under 2x, negative FCF headroom of only ~$200M after interest, and a covenant cushion that a 20% revenue decline erases. The credit is entirely dependent on management's ability to keep expanding EBITDA faster than debt — debt more than doubled in nine months.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Covenant proximity.** ~3.1x actual vs 4.0x cap; a 20% revenue drop breaches it absent aggressive cost cuts. Reported leverage cap (4.00x) and 9.18% TLB cost are from a third-party research note — **unverified against filings**.
2. **D&A/EBITDA quality.** Q3'26 D&A jumped to $190M/quarter (from $84M) on acquisition intangibles, bridging EBIT of $216M to "EBITDA" of $406M. EBITDA here substantially reflects non-cash purchase-accounting amortization — the $1.29B figure overstates true cash earnings power.
3. **Negative tangible equity of –$4.9B** and goodwill/intangibles at 78% of assets. Recovery value for unsecured lenders is weak; only the secured notes have meaningful protection (Fitch RR2).
4. **Interest is consuming FCF.** ~$413M annualized interest vs ~$611M FCF — a 1.5x coverage ratio on the cash metric. Interest expense grew 205% YoY in Q2'26.
5. **Heavy dilution.** Shares 559M → 713M (+27.5%) YoY; Piotroski F7 = 0. Growth is partly bought with equity, undercutting per-share value while leverage rises.
6. **Gross margin collapse in Q3'26** (67.9% → 52.9% in two quarters). If structural, it invalidates the resilience math in §4.
7. **Current ratio 0.69 and –$761M working capital.** Mitigant: a large share of current liabilities is likely deferred revenue, which is settled in service not cash — so this overstates true liquidity pressure. **Unverified** without the deferred-revenue line.
8. **$849M short-term debt** due within twelve months against $1,023M cash — covered 1.21x, but thin for a company with 77.8% intangible assets and no undrawn-facility disclosure available.
9. **FY2025 net income ≈ $0** on $411M EBITDA — the gap (interest, PPA amortization, tax) shows reported earnings are far below operating cash generation, and any EBITDA miss transmits directly to equity.
