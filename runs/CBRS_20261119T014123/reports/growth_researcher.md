## CBRS — Cerebras Systems | Growth Investment Analysis

*Price $94.15 | Mkt cap ~$21.3B (~$22.5B diluted) | EV ~$19.4B | Setup: post-IPO de-rating, −70% from May-2026 debut close of $311*

---

### 1. Growth Story

Cerebras has built the only commercially deployed **wafer-scale engine (WSE)** — an entire silicon wafer as one chip — and is attacking the fastest-growing slice of AI compute: **low-latency inference**, where its 750+ tokens/sec throughput is architecturally advantaged versus GPU clusters that must span the network to serve a single model. The company has converted that technical lead into contracted demand at a scale almost no semiconductor company has ever shown pre-scale: **$26.1B of remaining performance obligations (RPO) at 9/30/26**, roughly **27x FY2026 revenue**, anchored by a ~750MW multi-year OpenAI deployment, an AWS agreement, and a newly announced Microsoft partnership. The stock has collapsed ~70% from its IPO close not because growth broke, but because the market re-priced the *quality* of that growth — GAAP gross margin, capex intensity, and the fact that most of the backlog sits with one customer.

### 3. TAM and Penetration

- **TAM:** AI accelerator/compute spend is on a path toward $400–600B annually by 2030, with **inference overtaking training** as the majority of workloads. Inference is where Cerebras' architecture is genuinely differentiated (memory-on-wafer eliminates the bandwidth wall).
- **Penetration today:** FY2026 core revenue of ~$950M against a ~$300B+ 2026 accelerator market ≈ **0.3% share**. Subscale — but that is the point.
- **The math that matters:** Consensus FY2028 of $7.47B implies ~1.5–2% share of a 2028 market in three years. **That is the reasonable-case, not the bull case.** A 3–4% share (still a distant #2/#3 position behind NVIDIA) supports $15B+ revenue by 2030.
- **Differentiation, honestly assessed:** Cerebras wins on tokens/sec and time-to-first-token for latency-critical inference; it loses on ecosystem (no CUDA), memory capacity per dollar for large-model training, and supply (single-source wafer from TSMC). It is a **niche leader in a niche that is becoming the main market** — the highest-quality kind of disruption, but not a general-purpose NVIDIA replacement.

---

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4'26 earnings (~Feb 2027):** must deliver $310–330M core revenue *and* 47–49% core gross margin — the margin guide is the most watched number in the story, and the Q1'26→Q2'26 margin collapse is exactly what triggered the de-rating.
- **OpenAI 750MW deployment milestones (rolling, 2026–2028):** proof-of-execution on the ~$20B contract. Q3 was the first clean delivery quarter ("passed with flying colors" per Barron's).
- **Microsoft partnership ramp (just announced):** the first credible second hyperscaler relationship and the best available answer to concentration risk.
- **AWS expansion** — converts a channel relationship into durable cloud distribution.
- **Post-lockup supply clearing (Nov 2026) + potential index inclusion:** the ~180-day lockup has washed through the float; index eligibility (Nasdaq-100/S&P) would force mechanical demand.
- **G42 stake / sovereign AI (MEA) approvals** — regulatory clearance unlocks committed capital and a non-US demand pillar.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (High conviction on growth; size for volatility)

This is the archetype of a frontier-innovation name in a drawdown for reasons *adjacent* to the thesis rather than fatal to it: growth is **re-accelerating** (Q4 guide ~+87% core), the RPO book is **27x revenue** and still growing, and share-price-driven multiple compression has taken EV/S from an absurd ~40x+ at IPO to **~20x FY26 and 2.6x FY2028 consensus**. Under a TAM-first framework, the question is not "is 20x sales expensive" but "is 1.5% share of a half-trillion-dollar inference market in 2028 achievable" — and with $26B contracted, the answer is yes with room to spare.

I stop short of Strong Buy because the business model is **capital-hungry (negative $393M FCF) with structurally lower gross margins (~47–49% core vs NVIDIA ~70%+) and revenue concentrated in a single counterparty whose own funding model is under scrutiny.** The 5-year payoff is real; the path is not smooth.

**What would make it a Strong Buy:** two consecutive quarters of ≥47% core gross margin *with* GAAP gross margin above 30%; and a second hyperscaler contributing >15% of revenue (i.e., Microsoft ramping).

**What would make it a Sell:** Q4 core gross margin below 44%, or evidence the OpenAI deployment schedule is slipping, or RPO declining sequentially.

---

### 7. Key Risks

1. **Customer concentration (the #1 risk).** Most of the $26.1B RPO is OpenAI. Trading G42 risk for OpenAI risk is not diversification — it is a single point of failure on both revenue *and* counterparty solvency. A schedule slip or renegotiation removes the entire forward curve.
2. **Gross-margin structure.** GaAs/wafer-scale systems are expensive to build and the "core" adjustment excludes real costs. Core GM in the 47% range means ~53 cents of every dollar funds hardware and rented-back capacity — a systems/cloud economics profile, not a chip-designer profile. If 47–49% is a ceiling rather than a waypoint, the terminal multiple is far lower than a semi.
3. **Capital intensity and financing.** $383M capex against $510M revenue, rising interest expense ($18.6M in Q3), and a need to pre-build 750MW of capacity *before* collecting — dilution or debt are the likely funding paths.
4. **Quality of earnings.** The gap between GAAP (20% GM, −$161M net loss) and core (47–49% GM, breakeven) is unusually wide. Investors are being asked to underwrite a definition.
5. **NVIDIA / ecosystem competition.** NVIDIA is not standing still on inference, and CUDA/software lock-in plus a broad partner channel remain Cerebras' greatest structural handicap. Also watch hyperscaler in-house silicon (TPU/Trainium/MTIA) competing for the exact inference dollars Cerebras targets.
6. **Supply-chain single point of failure.** Wafer-scale engines depend on leading-edge wafer supply and advanced packaging; a single node or packaging constraint is existential, not incremental.
7. **Post-IPO overhang.** ~70% drawdown, price discovery still ongoing, and a sell-side target set ($170–$240) anchored to a $262 stock — expect continued negative revisions even as fundamentals improve.

---

**Bottom line:** A genuine frontier-innovation franchise with accelerating growth, a contracted backlog that dwarfs its revenue base, and a valuation that has already de-rated into "achievable if they execute" territory. The growth verdict is **Buy**, with position sizing dictated by gross-margin trajectory and OpenAI concentration — the two variables that will determine whether this becomes the next great AI infrastructure company or a cautionary tale about scaling a capital-intensive challenger against NVIDIA.
