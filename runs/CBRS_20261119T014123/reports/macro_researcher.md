## Macroeconomic Assessment — Cerebras Systems (CBRS)

**As of 18–19 November 2026 | Price $94.15 | Market cap $21.3B | Beta 1.85**

---

### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **RISK-OFF / Transitional (Hawkish Re-Acceleration)**

The US economy is in an unusual and specifically hostile regime for this company: **growth is solid, but the Fed is hiking again into a re-acceleration of inflation, and the entire rise in long yields is *real*.**

The Fed **raised the target range 25bp on 16 September 2026 to 3.75–4.00%** and held in October. Effective Fed Funds has risen from a 3.63% trough (Jun–Aug 2026) to 3.88% in October. The September FOMC median projects **4.00–4.25% through end-2026 and 2027**; sell-side houses (BofA) expect two further hikes. Critically, the **2Y at 4.74% sits ~87bp above the effective funds rate**, meaning the market is pricing *more* tightening than the Fed's own dots.

Growth is not the problem: Q3 2026 real GDP grew ~3.4% annualized (QoQ) and +2.1% YoY, with unemployment at **4.1%** — down from 4.5% a year ago. This is a "high nominal rates, decent real growth" regime: **earnings-friendly, multiple-hostile.**

The decisive macro fact for CBRS: **the 10Y has risen ~69bp since June (4.44% → 5.13%), but 10Y breakevens have *fallen* 15bp (2.34% → 2.19%). The 10Y real yield (TIPS) rose from 2.11% (3 Jun) to 2.89% (17 Nov) — roughly +80bp.** This is a **term-premium / fiscal-supply / real-rate shock**, not an inflation-expectations shock. The curve is a **bear steepener** (2s10s +39bp, 30Y 5.47% above 10Y 5.13%) — the single most punitive configuration for long-duration equities, because discount rates rise without growth expectations improving. Core CPI 3-month momentum has re-accelerated to ~3.7% annualized, which is what forced the Fed's hand.

Meanwhile the **broad USD has strengthened +2.9% since 1 June (119.2 → 122.7)** and +3.2% since mid-September.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Cerebras is the **longest-duration asset in the AI complex**: ~$210.8M Q3 revenue (+55% YoY; core +70%), **negative free cash flow**, a **$26.1B backlog heavily concentrated in OpenAI** (750MW agreement), and a valuation that rests almost entirely on management's target of **2027 revenue tripling to ~$3B+**. That profile makes it maximally sensitive to discount rates.

| Macro Variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **10Y real yield +80bp** | **STRONGLY NEGATIVE** | ~5–6x 2027E sales with value concentrated in 2028–2032 terminal cash flows. An 80bp real-rate rise mechanically removes 25–35% of such a valuation. This is the dominant driver. |
| **Fed hiking / 2Y +87bp over EFFR** | **NEGATIVE** | Removes the "rate-cut tailwind" that IPO pricing in May 2026 implicitly assumed. Risk appetite for pre-profit growth is being withdrawn. |
| **Headline CPI 3.8%** | **NEGATIVE (indirect)** | Not a direct cost input, but it forces the Fed's hawkishness and raises the hurdle rate for the debt-financed data-centre buildout that is CBRS's demand pool. |
| **Solid GDP / 4.1% unemployment** | **POSITIVE** | Enterprise IT and hyperscaler capex budgets are funded from strong operating cash flow. No demand destruction yet. |
| **Stronger USD (+2.9%)** | **MILDLY NEGATIVE** | Meaningful EMEA and Middle East / Sovereign AI exposure. USD-denominated systems become more expensive for foreign buyers; translated revenue compresses. |
| **Bear steepener / fiscal term premium** | **NEGATIVE** | Raises the cost of the debt that funds both Cerebras and its customers. |
| **Electricity flat ($0.19/kWh)** | **NEUTRAL / slight positive** | Power is the binding constraint on 750MW-class deployments; flat power prices ease the COGS path for cloud/services (gross margin expanded 510bp to 46.9%). |
| **Tariff / export-control risk** | **NEGATIVE (tail)** | Leading-edge wafer fabrication is outsourced to Asian foundries; CBRS is a price-taker on tariffs and export controls with no scale to absorb them. |

**Empirical confirmation of the macro channel:** since the 14 May 2026 IPO, **SMH −20.5%**, **NVDA −35.8%**, **CBRS −69.7%** ($311.07 → $94.15; **−75.6%** from the $386.34 intraday high). With a 1.85 beta, roughly **−38pp of CBRS's decline is explained by AI-complex factor de-rating alone** — i.e., *more than half the damage is macro*. The remainder is idiosyncratic (OpenAI concentration, capacity/construction delays, negative FCF, IPO-related securities litigation). Note the acute phase: NVDA fell from $200 to $151 between 4 and 18 November, precisely as the 10Y pushed to 5.13–5.15% and real yields to ~2.9%.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

This is close to the worst possible macro configuration for CBRS's business model: **rising real yields, a Fed in hiking mode, sticky headline inflation, a firm dollar, and a bear-steepening curve** — applied to a pre-profit, negative-FCF, high-beta, terminal-value-dependent equity. The offsetting positives (solid GDP, flat power costs, contained breakevens) support *fundamentals* but not the *multiple*, and the multiple is what has been repricing.

**Important nuance for the investment committee:** because breakevens are *falling* while nominal yields rise, this is **not** a 2022-style inflation shock replay — it is a **fiscal/term-premium shock**. That distinction matters: if term premium is the driver, a fiscal consolidation or a shift in Treasury issuance/duration could compress the long end quickly and violently, independent of Fed policy. CBRS is a levered play on that outcome.

---

### 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (Bull)

**The "term-premium unwind."** Consensus (and the FOMC dots) assumes the 10Y stays at 5%+ and the Fed hikes again. But with **breakevens already down to 2.19%** and core CPI momentum merely a 3-month artefact, the inflation case for further hikes is thin. If Q4 CPI prints soft and the Fed signals a pause in December, the **30Y at 5.47% and 10Y at 5.13% contain an unusually large term-premium cushion**. A 70–90bp rally in the 10Y to ~4.25% would:

- compress the discount rate on a 5–6x 2027E sales multiple, and
- re-open the high-yield/convert market that funds OpenAI's 750MW obligation — the single largest risk to CBRS's $26.1B backlog.

On a 1.85 beta and a −70% drawdown, that is a plausible **+60–90% re-rating** with no change in operating fundamentals whatsoever. Symmetrically, the bear case is a 30Y break above 5.75% on fiscal supply, taking the 10Y to ~5.75–6.00% and CBRS to the $60–70 range. **The macro, not the product roadmap, is the near-term swing factor — which is precisely why the risk rating is Unfavourable today despite a fundamentally improving revenue trajectory.**
