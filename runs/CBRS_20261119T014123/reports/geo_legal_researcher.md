### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**R1 — UAE export-control licence cliff (April 6, 2027). Probability: Medium-High · Impact: Severe**
BIS's 10-Jul-2026 Final Rule moved the UAE from Country Groups D:3/D:4 to A:5 and created Supplement No. 8 "license-free" approved end users: UAE government agencies, **G42 and Core42**, and eight US-headquartered AI companies — but **"the authorization provided for G42 and Core42 shall automatically expire 270 days after the effective date (April 6, 2027)"** unless they become US-headquartered or re-apply (MoFo client alert, 20-Jul-2026). Cerebras' anchor customer sits on a dated, entity-specific authorisation that is not country-wide and must be re-earned. Any non-renewal removes the licence basis for the majority of historical revenue.

**R2 — Customer concentration has rotated, not diversified. Probability: High · Impact: High**
The $26.1B contracted backlog and the Dec-2025 **OpenAI 750MW (to 2028, option to 2GW by 2030)** agreement plus Microsoft/Oracle/AWS wins shift the concentration from Abu Dhabi to a handful of US counterparties (Seeking Alpha 18-Nov-26; Barron's: "significant amount" of backlog is OpenAI). Analysts explicitly frame this as "trading G42 risk for OpenAI risk… a bigger single point of failure" (TradingView/Stocktwits). Delivery delays and capacity constraints are already flagged (Zacks Q3 call takeaways).

**R3 — Gulf regional conflict & Hormuz/energy shock. Probability: Medium · Impact: Medium-High**
2026 coverage shows an active **US–Iran conflict** (erupted 28-Feb-2026), Strait of Hormuz risk (crude $82, 10-yr UST 5.07%), an expanding Treasury campaign against Iran ("Operation Economic Outcast"), and supply-chain inflation persisting "even if there is a lasting agreement to end the war" (WSJ, Bloomberg, Treasury press release, Nov-2026). Gulf-hosted compute for sovereign customers is physical-asset exposure to this theatre — while the UAE is simultaneously deepening US alignment (designated Major Defense Partner; BIS cites countering Iran as the rationale for liberalisation).

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **CFIUS / national security agreement.** The 2024-25 IPO was delayed by CFIUS review of G42's investment; clearance (31-Mar-2025) followed G42 cutting Huawei ties and signing a **national security agreement** with the US government (Reuters, CNBC, Yahoo). This is a live, ongoing mitigation regime with reporting/visitor obligations, not a one-time approval.
- **Securities litigation overhang (active).** Following a ~20% drawdown on Q1-2026 results and a further ~5.7% fall on 28-Oct-2026, **Pomerantz, Levi & Korsinsky, Block & Leviton, Bragar Eagel & Squire and Frank R. Cruz** have each opened securities-fraud investigations of CBRS and its officers/directors. The Culper Research short report (28-Oct-2026) alleges **wafer-scale engine failures within ~1.5–2.5 years** and cites January-2026 engineering research. No confirmed filed class action yet — natlawreview explicitly describes these as investigations — but the runway to a §10(b) complaint is short.
- **Export compliance as a disclosure item.** The most recent 10-Q (12-Nov-2026) files as **Exhibit 10.1 an "Amendment No. 1 to Purchase Order" with Mohamed bin Zayed University of Artificial Intelligence (MBZUAI)**, dated 18-Sep-2026 (PO originally 30-Dec-2025). The amendment converts inference systems into a training+inference cluster whose **"Delivery Location shall be [redacted] storage facility,"** with title passing on delivery and Cerebras "serving as exporter of record," and with recitals that the equipment is **"not intended to be deployed, operated or otherwise used by MBZUAI in the United States"** and **"not intended to be supplied… shipped through, imported into, or made available for use… in the United Arab Emirates."** MBZUAI is *not* on BIS Supplement No. 8 (only G42/Core42, UAE government agencies, and listed US companies are), and the government-agency approval expressly **"does not extend to… government contractors or grantees."** A contractual architecture that moves UAE-government-linked hardware to a redacted third-country storage location is exactly the pattern EAR anti-diversion enforcement targets (BIS's $252M Applied Materials penalty, Feb-2026, shows the current enforcement temperature).

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Anti-diversion structuring (highest severity):** the MBZUAI amendment's fictions — UAE university buyer, non-US/non-UAE delivery to an undisclosed storage facility, Cerebras as exporter of record — raise deemed-export, re-export and end-use/end-user questions requiring rigorous ECCN classification and possibly a BIS licence.
2. **Licence-cliff dependency:** G42/Core42 approval expires 6-Apr-2027; no roadmap disclosed to investors.
3. **CFIUS NSA covenant compliance** and the residual G42 equity/board influence (a foreign person with historical Huawei ties holding ~24%).
4. **Sovereign/state-linked counterparties** (G42, MBZUAI, Mubadala ecosystem) → FCPA/AML third-party and state-owned-enterprise diligence; Gulf sovereign-AI programmes are politically directed.
5. **Data privacy/sovereignty**: EU/Gulf data-localisation and GDPR for cloud workloads; EU AI Act.
6. **Saudi Arabia**: no BIS approved-user pathway → expansion plans carry per-shipment licence risk; also potential outbound-investment/technology-transfer controls.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: ~63% EMEA revenue with an ~86% historical UAE-customer-HQ concentration, an anchor customer whose US export authorisation **expires 6-Apr-2027**, a live CFIUS national-security agreement, an active conflict in the operating neighbourhood, a redacted third-country delivery structure for a UAE government university, and a coordinated securities-plaintiff bar with two discrete negative catalysts (Q1-2026 miss, Culper report) already in the record. Mitigants: revenue mix is genuinely diversifying toward US hyperscalers, BIS has *eased* rather than tightened UAE controls, and the UAE is a US Major Defense Partner — so the base case is licence renewal, not revocation.

**Key gaps:** no 10-K is on file (first fiscal year as a public company), so risk-factor language could not be pulled directly; the 10-Q full text returned only Exhibit 10.1; no confirmed filed complaint or DOJ/BIS enforcement action against Cerebras was found — the exposure is prospective, not yet realised.
