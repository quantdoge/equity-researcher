# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Cerebras Systems Inc. (NASDAQ: CBRS)

**Snapshot:** $94.15 (close 18-Nov-2026) · Market cap ~$21.3B · Semiconductors / AI infrastructure · IPO 14-May-2026 at $185.00 · ~708 employees · CEO & Chairman: Andrew Feldman

**Price context:** Shares are **-49% vs. the $185 IPO price**, **-70% from the $311 first-day close**, and **-76% from the $386 first-day high**; the post-IPO low was $77.84 (5-Nov-2026). A ~$21B company that was valued near $42B at offer. Every non-financial risk below has already been *priced* by the market — the question is what remains un-priced.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

**Evidence for Weak:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality.** Andrew Feldman is co-founder, Chairman, CEO *and* President — unfettered concentration of power at exactly the moment the company is making a contested accounting judgment (see §7, flag #1).
- **Controlled-company, multi-class structure.** The S-1 confirms a multi-class capital structure under which the company "may be excluded from" certain Nasdaq requirements — i.e., self-designated controlled company status, exempting it from majority-independent board and independent compensation committee mandates.
- **No proxy on file.** A `DEF 14A` search returned nothing — there is no shareholder say-on-pay, no disclosed executive compensation structure, and no related-party transaction disclosure to audit. **Compensation alignment is unverifiable, which is itself a governance gap** for a company whose insiders sold ~$150M+ within five months of the IPO.
- **Sponsor-weighted board.** Board includes Eric Vishria (Benchmark) and Steven Vassallo (Foundation Capital) alongside 10% holders Benchmark (~5.65M sh) and Foundation Capital (~9.52M sh). Credible independents were added pre-IPO (Glenda Dorchak — ex-IBM/Intel/Spansion; Paul Auvil — ex-Proofpoint CFO; Elena Donio; Susan Lior), which is a genuine mitigant, but they sit on a board chaired by the founder-CEO.

**Mitigants:** insider sales were executed under **Rule 10b5-1 plans** (not discretionary), and independent directors with audit credentials were seated before the offering.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **ELEVATED**

- **Securities class action filed.** Lead-plaintiff deadline **8-Jan-2027**. Class period centres on the IPO (14-May-2026) through the **24-Jun-2026** disclosure. Multiple firms active: Pomerantz, Glancy Prongay, Block & Leviton, Kaplan Fox, Howard G. Smith, Frank R. Cruz, B&G, SBS Law.
- **No SEC enforcement action found** in EDGAR full-text enforcement search (empty result). This is the single most important *negative* finding — there is no formal SEC order or DOJ matter disclosed. Treat as "pre-enforcement" stage.
- **Export controls / national security — the structural risk.** The G42 relationship forced a **CFIUS review that delayed the prior IPO attempt**. Cerebras holds a **UAE sovereign contract with MBZUAI**, and Congressional scrutiny is live: Rep. Gallagher has asked the USG to investigate **G42's PRC military-intelligence ties** and **MBZUAI's relationships with PRC universities under US export controls (incl. Tsinghua)**. Sen. Warren has publicly attacked license-free AI-chip access for G42. BIS's July-2026 upgrade of UAE status (G42/Core42 no longer needing licences for AI chips/servers) eases near-term friction but **raises political salience**, not lowers it.
- **The September 18, 2026 MBZUAI Amendment No. 1 is a compliance-structure red flag.** It converts inference systems into a training+inference cluster delivered to an **undisclosed third-country storage facility**, with express recitals that the hardware is **"not intended to be deployed, operated or otherwise used by MBZUAI in the United States"** and **"not intended to be supplied, shipped through, imported into, or made available for use or enjoyment in, the United Arab Emirates,"** with **Cerebras acting as exporter of record**. Contractual architecture engineered to sit outside both US and UAE jurisdiction attracts deemed-export, end-use/end-user, and diversion risk — and is politically indefensible if it surfaces in a Congressional hearing.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Existential customer concentration — the risk was rotated, not diversified.** ~$26.1B contracted backlog (~$25.4B RPO) is overwhelmingly tied to **OpenAI**, via a 750MW / up-to-$20B agreement with **cancellation rights**. Cerebras traded G42 concentration for OpenAI concentration — arguably a *larger single point of failure*. In Aug–Sep 2026 **Nvidia took over a key OpenAI inference workload**, cutting the stock ~20% in a session. One customer's sourcing decision can halve this equity.
- **Build-out execution failure, admitted in public.** The CEO stated on CNBC (13-Nov-2026): **"We are behind schedule"** on data centre builds. Q1-26 forced the company to **rent its own systems back from an existing customer** to service backlog — a circular capacity arrangement that depressed cloud gross margin. Physical constraints (power, 600–750MW of capacity) are the binding limit on the entire 2027 thesis.
- **Hardware durability / useful-life question.** Culper Research alleges wafer-scale chips last **1.5–2.5 years in the field** due to thermal-expansion stress from continuous operation. Combined with a thin 708-person organisation scaling toward $3B revenue and single-foundry dependence on leading-edge wafer manufacturing, asset-productivity assumptions carry unhedged technology risk.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Retail damage is already crystallising.** A ~70% drawdown from the first-day close, amplified by the **2X leveraged CBRG ETF**, which lost over half its value in a month. Losses concentrated in retail holders is the historical precursor to both litigation and political attention.
- **An active short-report narrative exists and has stuck** — Culper's depreciation allegations are now embedded in eight competing securities-fraud investigations. That is a reputational flywheel: report → investigation → headline → new investor losses.
- **Sovereign/UAE association** is a live political liability given Congressional and Senate attention to G42/MBZUAI and PRC ties. This is the kind of controversy that invites hostile hearings rather than fades.
- **Offsetting positive:** product narrative remains credible — Q3 revenue +55–70%, cloud/services +272%, Microsoft and Oracle added as customers, three guidance beats and two raises. Reputation with *technologists* is intact; reputation with *shareholders* is not.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not **Critical**, because there is no restatement, no going-concern language, no disclosed SEC formal order or DOJ matter, revenue is accelerating, gross margin is expanding, and insider sales were plan-driven rather than panic-driven. But the combination of an active securities class action, a credible accounting-integrity short report, one-way insider selling at prices ~2x today's, CEO/Chairman duality under a controlled-company structure, existential OpenAI concentration, admitted build delays, and export-control structuring is materially worse than the average semiconductor peer.

---

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Depreciation useful-life extension — escalate to forensic accounting, highest priority.** Draft IPO filings through Dec-2025 reportedly used **1.5–3 year** equipment lives; four months later Cerebras nearly **doubled them to 3–5 years**, inflating gross margin. A pre-IPO change in a critical accounting estimate that flatters the offering metric is the single most serious indicator in this file. *Action: obtain the original policy documentation, audit-committee minutes, and the critical accounting estimates disclosure; quantify the margin impact of the original lives.*
2. **Circular capacity arrangement.** "Renting our own systems back from an existing customer" — confirm the counterparty, pricing, and revenue/COGS recognition. If the counterparty is the same OpenAI entity driving the backlog, related-party-adjacent optics and revenue-quality questions follow.
3. **MBZUAI Amendment No. 1 export structuring.** Hardware routed to an undisclosed third-country storage facility, expressly excluded from US and UAE use, with Cerebras as exporter of record. *Action: confirm BIS licence classification, end-user certificates, and whether counsel signed off; assess diversion and deemed-export exposure.*
4. **Insider selling ~$150M+ with zero purchases**, concentrated at $195–$209, with lockup now expired. *Action: quantify remaining unregistered supply; monitor Form 4s weekly.*
5. **Litigation-Attorney-General risk.** Track the 8-Jan-2027 lead-plaintiff deadline and watch for (a) a consolidated complaint naming officers/directors, (b) any SEC subpoena or informal inquiry, (c) any 8-K disclosing an audit-committee inquiry. Any of these would move the rating to **Critical**.
