# ESG ANALYSIS: ServiceNow, Inc. (NYSE: NOW)

**Sector:** Technology — Software (Application) | **Market cap:** ~$96.4B | **Price:** $92.90 | **Employees:** 29,187 | **FY2025 revenue:** $13.278B

> **Context flag:** NOW is down ~38% (~$59B of market value) since its October 2026 earnings report and is trading near $93 versus a much higher base earlier in the year. Critically, **this drawdown is an AI-disruption/valuation event, not an ESG event.** Distinguishing the two is the central task of this memo.

**Key insight:** ~93% of ServiceNow's footprint sits in Scope 3, and >75% of that is suppliers. This is a *procurement-standard* question, not an *operations* question — and it is what enterprise customers will audit.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

Independently verified FY2025 GHG data (Apex, limited assurance):

| Metric | FY2025 | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Scope 1 | 2,485 tCO₂e | |
| Scope 2 (market-based) | 901 tCO₂e | 100% renewable via RECs/green tariffs |
| **Scope 1+2 total** | **3,386 tCO₂e** | vs. ~16,597 t in 2019 → **~80% cut** |
| Scope 3 | 317,094 tCO₂e | 6 of 15 categories only |
| **Total disclosed** | **~320,480 tCO₂e** | |
| Intensity (S1+2) | **0.26 tCO₂e / $M revenue** | Trivial |
| Intensity (total) | **~24 tCO₂e / $M revenue** | Very low for a $13B company |

**Strengths:** SBTi-validated 1.5°C targets (2021); the 70%-by-2026 Scope 1+2 absolute target is already *beaten*; net-zero-by-2030 commitment is ~20 years ahead of sector norms; TCFD-structured Climate Transition Plan (Aug 2024); GRI/SASB/TCFD-aligned annual Global Impact Report.

**Weaknesses (the real E story):**
- **Scope 3 is rising in absolute terms:** 217,084t (FY22) → 284,828t (FY23) → 285,790t (FY24) → **317,094t (FY25)**, +46% since FY22. The Scope 3 target is *intensity-based per unit of value added*, not absolute — so growth can continue indefinitely while the target is "met."
- **Only 6 of 15 Scope 3 categories disclosed**; some estimates are modelled, not measured. True footprint is understated.
- **Offset reliance:** the "carbon-neutral cloud" claim depends on offsets covering **54% of in-scope FY2025 emissions.** This is now a disclosure-risk item under California AB 1305 and a live greenwashing vector.
- **CDP Climate: not disclosed.** A conspicuous gap for a company this size that sells ESG/sustainability software to others — a credibility asymmetry.
- Market-based Scope 2 (901t) vs. location-based (84,164t) reveals heavy REC dependence; additionality is not verifiable from public disclosure.

**Verdict:** LOW environmental risk. Carbon is not a valuation input here. The exposure is *reputational and disclosure-based*, not physical or transition-cost-based.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

| Sub-factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Workforce safety | Low risk — office/remote software model |
| Human capital / restructuring | **Elevated.** ~29,187 employees at YE2025; 2026 saw "several hundred" roles cut (June 2026 action), with reports of up to ~1,000 and management explicitly targeting **flat headcount into 2026** via attrition plus AI. Multiple reported rounds. |
| Employment litigation | **Elevated.** Live disability/race discrimination + retaliation claims (California CFRA/FEHA), an ERISA 401(k) class action (*Rubke v. ServiceNow*, N.D. Cal. 3:24-cv-01050), and a $27M sales-commission dispute. |
| Diversity & inclusion | Under simultaneous pressure: named in discrimination suits *and* exposed to the post-*SFFA* anti-DEI litigation wave (DOJ False Claims Act EEO enforcement against federal contractors — cf. Deloitte $21.5M, Lockheed $29.5M). ServiceNow is a large federal/state government vendor. **Two-sided risk.** |
| Product safety / consumer protection | Low direct risk; but AI-output liability embedded in customer workflows (HR screening, credit, healthcare) is an indirect product risk |
| Data privacy & security | **Highest S-weight.** Platform holds IT, HR, security, and customer data for governments and financial institutions; multi-jurisdiction GDPR exposure |

**Why not High:** no fatalities, no child/forced labour in a first-tier supply chain, no systemic safety product recalls. The litigation is individually small and normal-course for a 29k-person US tech employer.

**Why not Low:** the AI-driven restructuring is happening *while* the company is simultaneously marketing itself as an AI growth story. That combination — cutting headcount to fund an AI narrative — is exactly the fact pattern that draws discrimination claims, employee activism, and press scrutiny, and it feeds the "AI washing" securities theory described below.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**Positives:**
- **Single class of common stock** — no dual-class super-voting structure. Genuine one-share-one-vote alignment, which is *better than* many SaaS peers (e.g., dual-class structures at Palantir, Snowflake historically).
- **All board committees composed entirely of independent directors.**
- **Lead Independent Director** role exists, explicitly appointed by the independent directors to provide "an effective independent voice in Board leadership structure."
- Independent-director determination process documented in the 2026 proxy; board affirmatively determines independence annually.
- $5B incremental buyback authorized (Jan 2026) — capital return discipline; no ESG-linked debt covenants identified.

**Deductions:**
- **Combined Chairman & CEO.** William R. McDermott has been CEO and Chairman since 2022. The Board asserts this is "the most appropriate and effective" structure; that is precisely the claim proxy advisors discount. It concentrates agenda-setting and succession authority in the person whose AI narrative the market is currently re-pricing. **This is the single largest G deduction.**
- **Disclosure credibility under stress.** The November 2026 Pomerantz investigation and the broader 2026 "AI washing" securities-suit surge raise a live question about the accuracy of forward AI/ACV commentary — a governance-of-disclosure issue, not merely a stock-price issue.
- **Compensation alignment (unverified).** I could not parse the full 2026 proxy (PDF returned binary content). Executive comp metrics, CEO pay ratio, and equity-design detail are **not confirmed** in this memo. Given the 38% drawdown, whether the comp plan rewards ACV growth over durable FCF is the key unresolved question. *Flag as an evidence gap.*
- **Shareholder proposal on the 2026 ballot (Proposal 6)** — content unverified; presence of a contested proposal signals some shareholder dissatisfaction.

**Score rationale:** 6/10 = structurally sound, above-median for US tech on shareholder rights (single class, independent committees, LID), but held back by executive/board concentration and an active disclosure controversy. Not a 7+ (which requires an independent chair or a clean disclosure record); not a 5 or below (which would require dual-class, a staggered board, or a failed say-on-pay).

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**The honest conclusion: ESG is not the thesis driver for NOW, in either direction.**

- **Environmental:** financially immaterial at 0.26 tCO₂e per $M of Scope 1+2 revenue. There is no carbon-tax, stranded-asset, or physical-climate thesis here. Ignore it for valuation purposes — but note the offset/greenwashing claim as a low-probability PR tail.
- **Social:** the AI-restructuring human-capital story is worth monitoring as an *execution* risk, not an ESG risk per se. If attrition-driven flat headcount degrades delivery quality or triggers a systemic bias claim, that shows up in net retention — which is the metric that actually matters.
- **Governance is where the money is.** The combined CEO/Chair structure and the AI-disclosure controversy are the only ESG items with genuine cost-of-capital consequences — and they are already priced, brutally. A 38% drawdown *is* the market's governance discount being applied in real time.
- **Critical framing for the investment memo:** the bear case on NOW is **AI disintermediation of workflow software** (Anthropic/Claude Sheets, agentic tooling). That is a *competitive/technological* risk, not an ESG risk. Do not let an ESG "Average" rating be mistaken for an explanation of the drawdown, nor let a favourable carbon profile be mistaken for a defence of the equity.

**Actionable ESG conditions to attach to the thesis:**
1. **Watch item (G):** resolution or escalation of the Pomerantz/securities matter, and whether the board separates the Chair and CEO roles at the 2027 annual meeting — a cheap, high-signal governance fix.
2. **Watch item (S):** Q4 2026 / Q1 2027 attrition and net expansion; a rising attrition + falling NRR combination would convert the human-capital story into a financial one.
3. **No action (E):** carbon is a non-issue for valuation; recommend against weighting it.
