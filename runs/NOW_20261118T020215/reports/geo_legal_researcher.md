## ServiceNow (NOW) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Snapshot:** US-domiciled enterprise software (Santa Clara, CA); ~35,000 employees (Sept 2026); FY2025 revenue $13.28B; Q3 2026 revenue $4.11B (+20.5% y/y). Market cap ~$96B. Note: SEC EDGAR direct fetches were blocked (403) and no 10-K was retrievable through the filing tool, so geographic splits below are from secondary aggregators of the FY2025 10-K/Annual Report and are flagged accordingly.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US federal/public-sector concentration and budget politics (Medium-High likelihood, Medium-High impact)**
US public sector is one of ServiceNow's marquee verticals (nearly all 50 states; DOJ Antitrust Division itself runs ServiceNow in a GCC environment). Q2 2026 explicitly benefited from federal demand *accelerating on-prem subscription revenue out of Q3*, i.e. **government procurement timing injects real quarterly volatility**. Exposure to continuing resolutions, efficiency mandates, and GSA price-reduction pressure on large software vendors is ongoing and non-diversifiable. This is the single largest political risk in the model — and it is also plausibly part of the Q3 2026 "slowing contract growth" narrative that triggered the sell-off.

**Risk 2 — EU tech sovereignty regime (High likelihood of enactment, Medium impact)**
The European Commission adopted the **Tech Sovereignty Package on 3 June 2026**, including the proposed **Cloud and AI Development Act**, which would codify the aim of reducing strategic dependency on non-EU cloud providers and establish a **four-tier "cloud sovereignty framework"** that providers must meet to host public-sector data. EMEA is 25.6% of revenue and is the company's growth engine for public-sector and regulated-industry deals. A US-owned provider could face preferential-treatment disadvantage in EU public tenders. **Mitigants already in place:** ServiceNow Protected Platform for the EU (SPP EU) and a sovereign-cloud alliance with Deloitte/STACKIT — evidence management is treating this as a first-order strategic issue rather than a compliance afterthought.

**Risk 3 — EU AI Act / DORA / NIS2 compliance and liability stack (High likelihood, Low-Medium impact)**
**2 August 2026** brought full application of the EU AI Act, including Article 50 transparency obligations for AI interactions — now directly relevant to **Now Assist** deployments across the EU install base, plus AI-literacy duties. Layered on top: **DORA** operational-resilience obligations for financial-services customers (a core vertical) and NIS2. ServiceNow is both a *deployer-facing compliance vendor* (it sells GRC/AI-governance tooling for exactly these rules) and an *in-scope provider*, creating dual-sided risk: compliance cost and potential contractual liability on one side, but also a genuine regulatory tailwind for its GRC product line. Net: margin/contract-term friction, not existential.

**Watch item (not top 3):** US-China export controls and tariff escalation have limited direct read-through — software is not tariff-exposed and China revenue is negligible — but AI-chip restrictions can slow customer AI-infrastructure readiness and thus Now Assist attach rates.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**A. Securities litigation — the most probable near-term cash cost (High likelihood of suit, Medium impact)**
On **29 October 2026** shares fell **~19.5–27%** (worst single day on record) after Q3 2026 earnings — revenue beat but EPS missed and the report evidenced **slowing contract growth**, reviving "AI disruption" fears. Multiple plaintiff firms have opened investigations: **Pomerantz, Bronstein Gewirtz & Grossman, SBS Law, SueWallSt, BG&G** — all targeting statements about **growth outlook and business prospects**. As of the BG&G case page, **no complaint had yet been filed**. Given H1 2026 market conditions (median settled 10(b) damages ~$660M vs ~$290M in 2025), a filed class action and a nine-figure settlement range is the base case, likely largely insured. **Primary investor risk here is disclosure/governance quality, not solvency.**

**B. DOJ antitrust — Moveworks ($2.85B) (Resolved, with residual tail)**
DOJ's Antitrust Division opened an **in-depth review in June–July 2025 and issued a second request** to both companies. ServiceNow subsequently announced **completion of the Moveworks acquisition**, implying the review terminated without a blocking action. Residual risk: any continuing conduct scrutiny of AI-agent bundling, and signalling that ServiceNow's AI M&A program will face extended Hart-Scott-Rodino timelines.

**C. Patent / IP litigation — recurring, individually immaterial**
An active NPE/housekeeping docket: **DataCloud Technologies v. ServiceNow** (D. Del., patent infringement complaint; docket activity into mid-2026), **InQuisient v. ServiceNow** (1:22-cv-00900, settled), **WebSock Global Strategies** (closed 2024), plus historical **BMC** and **HP** disputes (HP patents invalidated under *Alice*). Pattern is normal for large enterprise software; no single case appears material.

**D. ERISA / employment**
An **ERISA 401(k) settlement** is in the court-approval process in 2026. Immaterial.

**E. M&A integration & Broader regulatory posture**
Continued bolt-on cadence (e.g. certain assets of Bravium Consulting, K&S advised) keeps integration and disclosure-control risk elevated — relevant given the securities investigation centers on forward-looking commentary.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

**None identified that are material.** Specifically:
- **No FCPA/UK Bribery Act enforcement action or disclosed DOJ/SEC investigation** found. Residual structural risk: a meaningful **reseller/partner channel** (especially in emerging markets — APAC/EMEA/LatAm) creates third-party intermediary exposure, which is the classic FCPA vector. This is a *generic* exposure, not an observed problem.
- **Sanctions:** Russia operations suspended/exited since 2022; no disclosed OFAC/BIS penalty. Partner-led distribution requires ongoing screening; note ServiceNow's own Third-Party Risk Management product markets sanctions-screening — reputational asymmetry if it ever failed its own control.
- **Data privacy:** No identified GDPR fine or major breach. As a processor for large enterprise and government customers, a breach or an unlawful EU–US transfer event is the principal tail risk; the SPP EU platform is the mitigation.
- **AI governance:** Now Assist EU deployments post-2 Aug 2026 create **Article 50 disclosure obligations that sit with the deployer but land contractually on ServiceNow** — the most likely source of new regulatory friction and customer indemnity demands.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Revenue is 63% US — a low-geopolitical-risk jurisdiction with high *political* (budget) volatility; there is **no Russia revenue, no meaningful China exposure, no nationalisation-prone jurisdictions, and no currency-repatriation constraints**. That caps the geopolitical ceiling well below "High." What pushes the rating off "Low" is (i) the **EU sovereignty/AI regulation stack** landing squarely on the 25.6%-of-revenue EMEA segment, (ii) **US federal budget/procurement dependence** in the largest vertical, and (iii) a **near-certain securities class action** following the October 2026 collapse. None are solvency-threatening; all are margin-, multiple-, and disclosure-quality-relevant.

**Monitoring triggers that would move this to High:**
1. An actual **DOJ/SEC subpoena or formal investigation** tied to the Q3 2026 growth commentary (would upgrade to High).
2. Cloud and AI Development Act provisions that **exclude or penalise non-EU-owned providers in public tenders** (direct EMEA revenue hit).
3. A **US federal shutdown or across-the-board software contract review** materially delaying public-sector bookings.
4. Any GDPR enforcement action or AI Act penalty against Now Assist EU deployments.
