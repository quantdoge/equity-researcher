# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — ServiceNow, Inc. (NYSE: NOW)

**Snapshot (as of Nov 17, 2026 close):** $92.90 | Market cap ~$96B | −38.1% in 14 trading days since the Oct 28 Q3 report (−26.9% on Oct 29, worst single day since the 2012 IPO) | −60% from the Jan 2025 record close ($234.08, split-adjusted) | −39.4% YTD. Sector: Technology / Application Software. ~29,200 employees.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE**

**Positives (verified):**
- All board committees are comprised **entirely of independent directors**; a **Lead Independent Director** role exists and is selected by the independent directors (2026 proxy).
- Annual **say-on-pay** advisory vote; no controlled-company or dual-class structure evident.
- Board includes credible independent voices (Anita Sands, Paul Chamberlain, Lawrence Jackson, Teresa Briggs) alongside executive director Paul Fipps (President, Global Customer Ops).
- A genuine alignment signal: **CEO McDermott bought $3.0M of stock on the open market** (28,682 sh at $104.60 avg, Feb 27, 2026) — not a token grant.

**Concerns:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality:** William R. McDermott is both Chairman and CEO. Mitigated by the Lead Independent Director, but power remains concentrated in a single, highly visible executive.
- **Key-person and bandwidth risk:** McDermott is the company's principal narrative-setter and has joined **external public boards (Figma; Intuit, effective Aug 1, 2026)** — an overboarding question at precisely the moment execution credibility is under attack.
- Compensation/discussion quality could not be verified at the primary-source level (see Data Gaps), so incentive alignment is assessed only indirectly: the FY26 story is heavily tied to AI ACV ($1.7B+ target by year-end) and cRPO — both metrics the company itself controls in definition and disclosure.

## 2. Regulatory & Legal Risk Summary — **MODERATE, DETERIORATING**

- **SEC/DOJ enforcement: NONE FOUND.** EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned no actions against NOW. No restatement, no auditor change, no insider-trading case.
- **Securities litigation is the live exposure.** Multiple plaintiff firms (Pomerantz, Bronstein Gewirtz & Grossman, SueWallSt/Federman) launched investor investigations between **Nov 10–17, 2026**, alleging the company made **materially false or misleading statements about its growth outlook**. No formal complaint appears filed yet, but a 10b-5 class action is the historical base case within 60–90 days of a one-day 27% drop. The implied theory: pre-Oct 28 optimism on cRPO/AI monetization colliding with an 18.5% Q4 cRPO guide. **Directors' and officers' insurance and indemnification are the mitigation, not avoidance.**
- **Data protection:** The June 2026 incident (below) creates plausible **GDPR/UK GDPR, state AG, and contractual notification** exposure. I found **no confirmed regulatory inquiry** — treat as an unverified gap, not an all-clear.
- **Federal procurement / political risk (underappreciated):** DOGE-driven federal contract spending cuts have hit ServiceNow as a **prime contractor**, and management explicitly attributed part of the softer Q4 cRPO guide to a **higher mix of on-premise federal deals and "specific backlog exclusions (T4C)."** Public-sector exposure is now a guidance variable.
- **EU AI Act:** Now Assist deployers in the EU face Article 50 disclosure obligations from **Aug 2, 2026** — a product-compliance cost and potential friction for EU sales.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Cybersecurity incident with confirmed exploitation (highest severity).** On **June 5, 2026** ServiceNow patched an unauthenticated-access endpoint flaw; on **June 9** it disclosed that threat actors exploited it and there was **evidence of successful queries of instance tables against a subset of customers** (customers on the Australia release, or earlier releases with certain custom configs). A separate **critical RCE (CVE-2026-0542)** in the AI Platform followed. For a vendor whose own product suite sells security operations, GRC and resilient workflow, a multi-tenant data-access event is a **trust impairment**, carrying churn risk in regulated verticals, indemnity/notification costs, and a template for customer litigation.
- **AI disintermediation and pricing-model risk.** The Oct 29 collapse was not about the quarter (revenue $4.11B, +20.5%, beat; subscription $3.99B, +21%) but about **cRPO growth guiding to 18.5% from 20.5%** — the first sub-20% forward guide — plus adj. EPS of $1.00 missing by $0.02. Anthropic's launch of Claude Sheets/Whiteboards/Data (Nov 10) knocked the whole group lower; NOW is now treated by the market as **"the canary for AI-driven worries about software."** Seat-based monetization is structurally exposed to agentic substitution, and smaller vendors are attacking back-office AI (EmployeeWorks already at $100M).
- **Execution churn in go-to-market and headcount.** **Two senior sales leaders are departing** as the sales org is reshaped (Bloomberg), and the company cut **several hundred roles** to hold headcount flat for 2026 — both landing in the same quarter as a demand-confidence shock. Simultaneously, delivery complexity is rising: the Q4 federal on-prem mix shift and T4C backlog exclusions show revenue quality becoming more heterogeneous and harder to forecast. Key-person concentration in McDermott compounds all three.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **ELEVATED**

- **Market narrative damage is severe and self-reinforcing:** −38% in 14 sessions, −60% from the peak, coverage framed around "historic sell-off," "should you hold or fold," and AI obsolescence. A $59B value loss equal to ~3.8× full-year 2026 subscription revenue has been erased in three weeks.
- **Litigation- and disclosure-driven headline risk:** serial investor alerts (Nov 10, 12, 17) keep the securities-fraud framing in front of institutions.
- **Trust/security narrative:** the June data-access incident plus CVE-2026-0542 directly undercut the "platform of record for enterprise control" positioning.
- **Employee-relations pattern (low financial materiality, real reputational drag):** *Boyd v. ServiceNow* (S.D. Cal., 3:25-cv-01568) alleging discrimination/retaliation after medical leave, multiple EEOC claims reported, and a **$925K 401(k) target-date-fund class settlement** (preliminary approval). Add layoffs to the mix and employer-brand risk rises.
- **Counterweight:** genuine commercial momentum — AI ACV tracking to **$1.7B+**, 20%+ top-line growth, and a Google Cloud/Gemini Enterprise partnership expansion. The franchise is not impaired; the credibility premium is.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not **Critical** — there is no evidence of fraud, no SEC/DOJ action, no restatement, no auditor issue, all-independent board committees, a Lead Independent Director, and a real (if small) CEO open-market purchase. But three independent risk vectors are simultaneously elevated and correlated: **a confirmed exploited security incident**, **imminent securities class-action exposure over growth-outlook statements**, and **a credibility reset on the core forward metric (cRPO)** complicated by federal procurement cuts. These are not offset by governance strength, and the absence of any insider buying or buyback during a 38% drawdown removes the market's strongest reassurance signal.

---

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **June 2026 exploited data-access incident** — confirmed actor access to customer instance tables; scope = Australia release / custom-configured instances. No confirmed regulator inquiry yet. Escalate for: notification completeness, contractual indemnity exposure, and whether any regulated-vertical customer has signaled churn. *Verify against the 10-Q risk factors and the trust.servicenow.com advisories.*
2. **Securities-fraud investigations (Pomerantz, BG&G, SueWallSt/Federman)** alleging misleading growth-outlook statements. No complaint filed as of latest check — **most likely formal filing within 60–90 days.** Escalate: D&O coverage adequacy, and any 10b5-1 plan adoptions/modifications by executives in the 90 days before Oct 28 (not yet verified — this is the single most important untested question).
3. **cRPO disclosure discretion** — the Q4 guide leans on "specific backlog exclusions (T4C)" and a federal on-prem mix shift, while the CFO publicly conceded cRPO is "a very nuanced accounting metric… certainly not perfect." Definitional discretion in the metric that just destroyed $59B of value warrants monitoring for **changes in metric definition or composition** — a disclosure-quality flag, not an allegation.
4. **Federal/DOGE contract exposure** as a prime contractor, now explicitly embedded in guidance — political risk that is not diversifiable via product execution.
5. **Go-to-market leadership departures + several hundred role cuts** into a demand-confidence shock — execution risk on the FY27 plan.
6. **No insider purchases and zero Q3 buyback** during the drawdown — weak internal conviction signal at the margin.
