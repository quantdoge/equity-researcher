## 1. Macro Environment Summary — Stance: **Risk-Off in duration assets; "tightening into a supply shock"**

The US is in a **late-cycle re-tightening regime with supply-shock inflation** — the opposite of the 2023-25 disinflation trade that made long-duration software a winning asset class.

The Fed cut from a 5.33% peak to an effective 3.63% by Jan 2026, held through H1, then **hiked 25bp on 16 Sept 2026 to 3.75–4.00% — the first hike since 2023**. It held again on 28 Oct (10-2 vote, upgrading its economic assessment), with CPI at a **three-year high of 3.82%** and FOMC minutes flagging another hike as "likely"; markets price a **55-60% chance of a December hike**. The June 2026 statement attributed elevated inflation "in part to supply shocks" — i.e., tariff/input-cost driven, not demand driven. Wall Street's read is that the Fed is behind the curve.

Crucially, this is **not a recession scare**. Real GDP grew ~3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026 (+2.1% y/y), unemployment is 4.1%, and IG credit spreads are **90bp** — within 15bp of cycle tights. That combination is the worst of both worlds for a 20%-growth software multiple: **hot inflation and rising long rates with no growth collapse to force the Fed's hand.** The entire adjustment is landing on the discount rate — 10Y at **5.12%**, 30Y at **5.47%**, a bear steepener (+39bp 2s10s) that is a term-premium/fiscal signal, not a growth signal. The 10Y has risen ~65bp since late June 2026 alone.

Layered on top is a **sector-specific repricing**: the Feb 2026 "Anthropic wake-up call" wiped ~$1trn from software on AI-disruption fears, and NOW is the epicentre of the second leg.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

ServiceNow: ~$96bn market cap at $92.90 (17 Nov), 1.035bn shares, ~29,200 employees, ~40%+ revenue outside the US. Q3'26 revenue $4.11bn (+20.5%), subscription +21%, cRPO +20.5% — but Q4 cRPO guided to **18.5%**, triggering a **-26.9% single-day drop** (worst since its 2012 IPO) and **-38% in 14 sessions**; the stock is **-54% from June 2025**.

- **Discount rate / equity duration — STRONGLY NEGATIVE (the dominant channel).** With ~80% of DCF value beyond year 5, each +50bp on the discount rate is worth roughly 10-13% of fair value. The 10Y is up ~65bp since June, which mathematically explains ~13-17% of the de-rating; the remaining ~35-40% is AI-disruption risk-premium expansion. Note beta (0.98) badly understates realised rate sensitivity.
- **Balance sheet — NEUTRAL/IRRELEVANT.** NOW is a net-cash company with minimal, long-dated, low-coupon debt. There is **no refinancing risk** and higher rates raise interest income. This is purely a valuation event, not a solvency one.
- **Enterprise IT budgets — NEGATIVE.** Supply-shock inflation is squeezing CFO margins at the same moment hyperscaler AI capex is making a competing claim on the same budget pool. Legacy SaaS seat rationalisation is the funding source. The cRPO deceleration from 20.5% to 18.5% is partly this, partly AI fear.
- **USD strength — NEGATIVE.** The broad dollar is +3.9% in eight weeks. On ~40% international revenue that is a ~1.5-1.7pp headwind to reported growth — material when the entire debate is over 200bp of cRPO deceleration.
- **Labour market / automation demand — POSITIVE.** 4.1% unemployment supports the core ROI case (headcount substitution, workflow automation). But it cuts both ways: AI is the substitute investors now fear.
- **Inflation & pricing power — MILD POSITIVE.** Subscription renewals carry 3-5% escalators; ARPU rises with CPI while the cost base is largely fixed-salaried. Margin-accretive at the product level — invisible against the multiple compression.
- **Government vertical (~mid-teens of revenue) — MIXED.** Federal cost-cutting pressure, offset by efficiency/automation mandates.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Three independent headwinds stack simultaneously: a Fed with a live December hike, a 5%+ 10Y with an expanding term premium, and a 4% dollar rally — all hitting an asset whose value is 80% terminal-value. Solid GDP and 90bp credit spreads are real offsets (they say "no recession, no funding crisis"), and inflation actually helps renewal pricing, but the discount-rate-times-duration channel dominates. **The macro is not the reason NOW fell 54%, but it is the reason the multiple could not hold and the reason any fundamental wobble got amplified 3x.** Post-derating, much rate damage is now in the price — but the regime remains a headwind, not a tailwind.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BULL: a disinflation shock**

The market is priced for two simultaneous bad outcomes: an AI-broken SaaS model *and* a persistent rate shock. **Credit is saying neither is happening** — 90bp IG spreads and 4.1% unemployment are incompatible with a broken enterprise economy. If the supply-shock inflation the Fed itself cites reverses (tariff relief, energy, shelter rollover) and CPI falls from 3.8% to ~2.5% by mid-2027, the Fed's September hike becomes a policy error it must undo: 100bp of cuts, 10Y back to ~4.2%, 30Y term premium collapsing.

In that world a 20%+ grower with $1.7bn AI ACV and an intact $4.1bn quarterly revenue base — trading at a valuation that now embeds secular decline — re-rates **40-60% off $93 without a single change in fundamentals**. The trigger is one CPI print, not one earnings call. *Bear mirror-image: if term premium runs to a 6% 10Y on fiscal/Fed-independence concerns, the same duration math takes NOW to $65-70 on an 18.5% cRPO guide.*
