### 2. Key Red Flags Found

1. **Gross margin inflected negative for the first time.** 79.2% (FY24) → **77.5%** (FY25), −170bps. Cost of revenue grew **+30.4%** vs revenue **+20.9%** — a sharp break from 2022–24 where CoR grew *slower* than revenue every year. Two explanations: (a) benign — $3.2B of M&A added acquired-intangible amortisation into CoR; (b) structural — AI inference/hosting cost per transaction. The CFO's disclosure that capex is being *cut* while FCF margin guidance *rises* to 36% is consistent with inference costs migrating into third-party cloud opex — i.e. **structurally lower steady-state gross margin than the seat-based model ever had.** A 78%-GM business is a different asset than an 80%+ one.

2. **Leading indicator is decelerating while headline beats continue.** cRPO growth: **21.5% → 21% → 20.5% reported, guiding 18.5% cc for Q4.** That is ~300bps of decay in three quarters against guided subscription revenue growth of 21%. cRPO leads reported revenue by 1–2 quarters. Consensus FY27 revenue of $19.24B (+18.6%) is therefore ~2pts optimistic in my model.

3. **Simultaneous rewrite of pricing, packaging AND the backlog metric.** April 2026: five legacy seat tiers (Standard/Pro/Pro Plus/Enterprise/Enterprise Plus) replaced with three AI-native tiers (Foundation/Advanced/Prime). Q3 2026 call: explicit shift "toward consumption-based monetisation." Q4 cRPO guidance explicitly reflects "**specific backlog exclusions (T4C)**" and a "higher mix of on-premise federal deals." When a company changes *how it sells*, *how it bills*, and *what it counts in its headline backlog* in the same year that metric decelerates, the comparability of the series is compromised. This is where aggressive recognition would hide. I have **no evidence** it is hiding there — but I cannot underwrite the series either.

4. **AI ACV is substituting, not adding.** AI ACV target raised $1.0B → $1.7B (+70%) — but $1.7B is ~10% of $16B subscription revenue, and "AI net new ACV" includes uplift on renewals that would have closed anyway. Total cRPO decelerates anyway. **The AI number is growing fast from a small base while the aggregate slows — that is the definition of cannibalisation, not accretion.**

5. **Shareholder capital is being destroyed by the SBC treadmill.** SBC $1.955B = **14.7% of revenue and 43% of operating cash flow**. Share count: 1,006.9M (2021) → 1,029.2M (2024) → **1,037.0M (2025)**. The company spent **$1.84B on buybacks in 2025 and the share count still rose 8.0M.** Gross dilution was ~1.8%. The "36% FCF margin" guide is a construct that treats a 15%-of-revenue cash compensation expense as free. **SBC-adjusted FCF: $4.576B − $1.955B = $2.62B** (FY25), ~$3.6B on FY26 guidance — i.e. ~27x, not ~16x.

6. **Growth increasingly bought.** Goodwill + intangibles $1.48B → **$4.70B** (+$3.2B in one year), now 35% of equity. Paying $3.2B to sustain a 21% top line is lower-quality growth, and it seeds future impairment and amortisation drag on GAAP EPS.

7. **GAAP vs. narrative gap.** GAAP operating margin 13.7%, GAAP EPS $1.67 vs. street non-GAAP ~$3.48. A 2.1x gap.

8. **Promotional management language has intensified as fundamentals decelerated.** Q3-26 press release, verbatim: *"Super Intelligence Control Tower," "the elixir that simplifies tech debt," "one-of-one, rule of 57 growth company firing on all cylinders," "What other company … has no capital or compute constraints?"* Category renaming + "elixir" + a self-invented "rule of 57" while cRPO decelerates is behaviour I mark down. Delivery has repeatedly undershot the tone: single-day drops of −11% (Jan-25), −11% (Dec-25), −10% (Jan-26), −18% (Apr-26), **−27% (29-Oct-26)**.

9. **Form 4 cluster.** Seven separate insiders filed Form 4s on 17-Nov-2026 and six on 10-Nov-2026 — same-day clusters are typically vest-and-sell. **Direction unverified** (tool returned filing metadata only); I will not claim net insider selling without transaction codes.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment — **the core of the bear case, and it is real**

- **Per-seat → per-consumption is defensive, not offensive.** The whole reason to overhaul packaging in April 2026 is that agentic AI removes human seats. A workflow platform priced per human agent has a shrinking unit of account when the work is done by software. Every vendor making this transition (Salesforce, Adobe, Workday) does so because the old metric was about to break, and every one of them trades at a lower multiple afterwards.
- **Gross-margin physics.** Seat-based software has near-zero marginal cost. Consumption-based AI has real marginal cost. NOW's 170bps GM decline and the migration of inference to third-party cloud is the visible tip.
- **Hyperscaler encroachment.** OpenAI/ChatGPT plugin, Google Gemini Enterprise, NVIDIA — NOW is announcing these as partnerships; strategically they are also distribution channels owned by parties who could disintermediate the governance layer. Microsoft remains the structural threat in the ITSM/workflow stack.
- **But the counter-evidence is strong:** 680 customers >$5M ACV (+23%), 19 deals >$5M net-new ACV (+135% YoY), RPO $29.7B (+22%). Large-enterprise demand is not cracking. This is not a melting ice cube.

### 6. Short Thesis Summary — **Is there a credible bear case?**

**Yes, but it is a 2025 thesis that has already paid.** The stock has fallen from a peak close of $234.08 (28-Jan-2025) to **$92.90 — a 60% drawdown** — including −38% in the 14 sessions since the Q3 print. The market has already repriced NOW from "premium compounder" to "AI-disrupted software."

What the bear still owns: cRPO heading to 18.5% and likely low-to-mid teens through 2027; gross margin that never recovers to 80%; SBC at 15% of revenue making "36% FCF margin" a marketing number; a consumption-model transition with no disclosure yet sufficient to prove revenue-per-customer is holding.

What the bear no longer owns: **valuation.** At $92.90 the stock is ~5.9x FY26E EV/Sales, ~23x FY26E non-GAAP EPS, ~19x FY27E, ~16x FY28E — with net cash, a guided 36% FCF margin (~$5.8B FY26 FCF, ~6% yield), 311% cash conversion, clean accruals, and a 20% grower. **The bear case now requires the growth rate to break below ~12% to work from here. The evidence says ~17–20%.** Betting on a further 40% decline from an already-de-rated multiple, against a clean balance sheet and real cash generation, is a low-probability trade.

**Bull case is currently stronger on risk/reward, and I say so plainly.** The single most bullish datapoint a sceptic must respect: receivables are growing *slower* than revenue and accruals are −14% of assets. Companies stuffing channels don't look like that.
