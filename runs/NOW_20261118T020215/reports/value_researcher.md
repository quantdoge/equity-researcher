# ServiceNow, Inc. (NYSE: NOW) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price: $92.90 (as of 2026-11-17) · Market cap ≈ $97B · EV ≈ $103B (incl. $6.0B net debt)*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **WIDE** (narrowing at the margin)

ServiceNow is the system of record for enterprise IT and employee workflow. The moat is built on genuine **switching costs**, not fashion:

- **The CMDB is the moat.** ServiceNow's configuration management database maps an enterprise's infrastructure and process dependencies. Ripping it out means re-architecting thousands of workflows, integrations and audit trails. Renewal rates have historically run ~98%, and 85%+ of revenue is subscription with multi-year contract terms.
- **Intangibles + efficient scale.** World-class reference base in regulated verticals (federal, financial services, healthcare), FedRAMP-level certifications, and a partner/developer ecosystem on the Now Platform. Few competitors can match the compliance surface.
- **Gross margin 77–79%** through FY2025 and **FCF margin ~30–35%** are the financial fingerprints of real pricing power.

**However, the moat is showing stress for the first time in a decade:**
- Gross margin fell to **70.5% in Q3 2026** (vs 77.3% a year earlier), and GAAP operating margin collapsed to **9.4%** from 16.8%. Part of this is purchase-accounting amortization from the Q2 2026 acquisition (D&A tripled to $390M/qtr), but part is real: a new "forward-deployed engineering" cost layer and rising AI infrastructure spend.
- Revenue growth has decelerated only modestly (30% → 21%), but **TTM FCF grew just ~4%** ($4.58B FY25 → $4.75B TTM) while revenue grew 16%. That is the classic signature of a business defending a franchise by spending into it.
- The market's fear — that agentic AI disintermediates seat-based workflow software — cannot be falsified yet. Management's answer (AI ACV +65% YoY, raised 2026 AI ACV target to $1.7B) is evidence the platform is monetizing AI, but AI ACV is still only ~10% of subscription revenue.

**Verdict:** still a wide moat, but for the first time I would call it *stable-to-narrowing* rather than *widening*.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **8 / 10**

| Revenue growth | +23.8% | +22.4% | +20.9% | +20.2% |
| Gross margin | 78.6% | 79.2% | 77.5% | ~73% |
| GAAP op margin | 8.5% | 12.4% | 13.7% | ~9.7% |
| SBC ($B) | 1.60 | 1.75 | 1.96 | 2.32 |
| SBC % of revenue | 17.9% | 15.9% | 14.7% | **15.1%** |
| FCF ($B) | 2.70 | 3.42 | 4.58 | 4.75 |
| FCF margin | 30.1% | 31.1% | 34.5% | ~30.8% |
| FCF / net income | 156% | 240% | 262% | 329% |

**Strengths:** 24% five-year revenue CAGR; FCF conversion is exceptional (>250% of GAAP net income); no customer concentration; deferred revenue funds working capital.

**Deductions:**
1. **SBC at 15% of revenue** (~$2.3B/year) is a genuine economic cost. FY2025 buybacks ($1.84B) and Q1'26 buybacks ($2.23B) only offset dilution — diluted shares are flat at ~1.04B. True owner earnings are therefore closer to **FCF − SBC ≈ $2.4B**, not $4.75B.
2. **FCF growth stalled** to ~4% TTM on ~16% revenue growth — margin, not demand, is the issue.
3. **Balance sheet flipped from net cash (+$1.3B at YE25) to net debt of $6.0B** after a ~$7.6B debt-funded acquisition in Q2 2026. Still modest at ~0.4x revenue and ~1.6x EBITDA, but the direction is wrong.
4. GAAP ROIC 11.9% is only modestly above a ~9% WACC; on a cash/non-GAAP basis ROIC is a healthy ~18–20%, but the trend is down.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

Current multiples: **55.6x GAAP P/E · 23.5x forward adjusted EPS (~$3.95 FY26E) · 20.5x TTM FCF · 4.6% TTM FCF/EV** — the cheapest ServiceNow has ever traded, versus a historical band of 40–65x FCF.

My own DCF (WACC 9%, terminal growth 3%, 10-year explicit):

| Scenario | Base | FCF growth yrs 1–5 / 6–10 | Implied value/share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** (SBC fully deducted as a cost; owner earnings ≈ $2.4B) | $2.4B | 20% / 10% | **~$97** |
| **Base** (reported FCF $4.75B; 12% then 8%) | $4.75B | 12% / 8% | **~$130** |
| **Bull** (reported FCF; 15% then 8%) | $4.75B | 15% / 8% | **~$144** |

**Intrinsic value range: ~$97–$144; midpoint ~$113–$120.** At $92.90 the stock trades at roughly an **18–22% discount to midpoint** — attractive, but *short of* the 25–30% margin of safety I require. Note that the vendor DCF tool's $498/share output is unusable: it extrapolates 28% FCF growth for a decade, which the last four quarters of data already contradict.

What is the price implying? At ~20x FCF with $6B of net debt, the market is pricing roughly **8–10% long-run FCF growth with terminal margins below today's**. That is a defensible bear case, not an absurd one. Sell-side consensus remains $140–$200, which I regard as too high given the margin reset.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment: **6.5 / 10**

- **Bill McDermott (CEO since 2019)** has executed superbly operationally — revenue nearly 3x, consistent 20%+ growth at scale, disciplined enterprise sales culture.
- **But capital allocation has deteriorated.** In 2026 the company (a) spent $2.2B on buybacks at prices 2x today's, (b) issued ~$6B of debt to fund a ~$7.6B acquisition (goodwill + intangibles jumped $6.0B → $13.6B), and (c) let SBC rise 33% YoY. Paying peak-cycle prices with debt while the core margin compresses is precisely the empire-building pattern Buffett warns about. The Moveworks-type AI acquisition logic is strategically coherent, but the price and financing are unproven.
- Insider ownership is low (~1%), there is no dividend, and buybacks have not reduced share count in five years. Management is competent but not yet owner-operator aligned.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (accumulate below ~$85)

This is a genuinely great business at a *fair-to-good* price — not a great price. My midpoint intrinsic value of ~$115 sits ~24% above the current quote; a 30% margin of safety requires entry around **$80–85**, or evidence that FCF margins have bottomed (watch Q4'26 and Q1'27 free cash flow, which are seasonally the strongest quarters, and the FY27 subscription guide).

- **If you own it:** hold. Selling a wide-moat, net-cash-generative, 20%-growth franchise at 20x FCF to avoid volatility would be a mistake.
- **If you don't:** do not chase. Start a half-position only if the price breaks $85, or if two consecutive quarters show FCF growth re-accelerating toward revenue growth.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI disintermediation (the dominant risk).** If agentic AI collapses the seat-based workflow model, revenue growth and pricing both compress, and today's 20x FCF is not cheap. This is genuinely outside my circle of certainty.
2. **Structural margin reset.** If the 70% gross margin and ~10% GAAP operating margin are a new normal rather than a purchase-accounting trough, my Base case FCF ($4.75B growing 12%) is too generous by a third.
3. **Serial M&A.** A $7.6B debt-funded deal at peak valuations, with more likely, risks value destruction and goodwill impairment in a downturn.
4. **SBC dilution.** If buybacks are curtailed to deleverage, dilution resumes at ~1.5%/year, silently eroding per-share value.
5. **Competitive encroachment** from Microsoft (bundled ITSM), Salesforce, and AI-native service desk startups attacking the low end.
6. **Data caveat:** the peer set returned by the screen is mega-cap technology (AAPL/MSFT/NVDA), not workflow-software comparables; the 75% P/E premium versus that median is not a meaningful benchmark.

**Bottom line:** A wide-moat compounder undergoing its first real stress test, available at the cheapest multiple in its public history. The quality is Buffett-grade; the price is merely reasonable, and the margin trajectory and capital allocation demand a discount I am not yet being offered. Buy the business — at $85, not $93.
