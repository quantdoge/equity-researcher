## SECTOR BRIEF — ORACLE CORP (ORCL)
**Sector:** Technology | **Industry:** Software – Infrastructure (Cloud / AI Compute)
**Price $103.88 · Mkt cap $314.1B · Beta 1.78 · FY26 revenue $67.36B · Data as of 25-Nov-2026**

---

### 1. Sector Overview

Oracle sits at the intersection of two subsectors that are diverging violently in 2026: **enterprise application software** (a mature, ~8–12% growth, high-gross-margin oligopoly) and **AI/cloud infrastructure** (a capital cycle growing 20–30%+ but consuming unprecedented capital). Global cloud infrastructure spend is running at roughly **$800B annualised in 2026**, with the top three — AWS (~32%), Azure (~23%) and GCP (11–13%) — holding **~65–68% share**, an HHI of roughly 1,900–2,100 (moderately-to-highly concentrated). Total hyperscaler capex is tracking **~$828–900B in 2026** and is guided toward **>$1 trillion in 2027** (JPMorgan, Morgan Stanley). Oracle is the clear #4 in cloud infrastructure but has become a top-tier *contractor* of AI capacity: **RPO hit $664B at Q1 FY27, up $209B YoY**, against FY27 guided revenue of ~$90B — a backlog-to-revenue ratio of ~7x versus ~1.2–1.5x at Microsoft and Google. The sector's defining 2026 dynamic is that **economic power is migrating from the software layer (which sets prices) to the physical layer (power, HBM, accelerators — which sets costs)**, and the credit market has begun repricing that shift. Oracle CDS spreads hit records in November 2026 alongside Alphabet, SpaceX and CoreWeave, with CoreWeave spreads implying a 68% five-year default probability. **Cycle position: late-stage in the AI infrastructure capital cycle, mid-stage in AI monetisation.**

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds (3)

- **AI infrastructure demand is still in excess of supply, and Oracle is winning disproportionate share of it.** OCI IaaS revenue grew **121% YoY in Q1 FY27**, and Oracle booked **>$30B of incremental AI cloud contracts** in the quarter, driving RPO from $455B to **$664B**. Oracle's contracted backlog is now **~8.6x annualised revenue** — the highest coverage ratio in the sector and a multi-year revenue annuity if it converts.
- **Hyperscaler capex is still being revised upward, not down.** Forecasts for 2026 have already moved from $736B to ~$900B; Morgan Stanley models **$1.6T of AI infrastructure spend in 2027** across four companies. For the supply chain (and for anyone with spare contracted capacity), the demand signal remains intact even as equity multiples compress.
- **Opex leverage is real and still flowing through.** Oracle's SG&A fell from **20.8% of revenue in FY23 to 14.8% in FY26**, and operating margin expanded **+485bps to 30.6%** (34.8% in Q1 FY27) despite a collapsing gross margin. That is best-in-class opex discipline — second only to Microsoft's 46.8% sector-high operating margin — and it is the one operating metric where Oracle is genuinely differentiated versus enterprise-software peers.

---

### 3. Key Headwinds (3)

- **Gross margin is in free-fall — the sector's single most important negative data point.** ORCL gross margin fell from **80.6% (FY21) → 72.9% (FY23) → 65.8% (FY26) → 60.0% (Q1 FY27)**. No large-cap peer has anything remotely comparable: Microsoft −98bps, ServiceNow −76bps, while Alphabet (+427bps), Amazon (+648bps) and Salesforce (+434bps) all *expanded*. Root cause is supplier power at the physical layer (**Nvidia ~90% of AI accelerators; HBM supplied by 3 vendors now in a memory supercycle**) plus a mix shift from licence/support to capital-intensive IaaS. **Each point of further GM compression costs ~$900M of gross profit at FY27 scale.**
- **The debt-funded build-out has created a genuine credit story, and the credit market is leading the equity.** Interest expense rose from **$3.51B (FY23) to $4.60B (FY26)** and is running at **$5.7B annualised in Q1 FY27 (+35% YoY)**; senior bonds now yield **~9%** and CDS spreads are at record wides. FY26 capex plus the Q1 FY27 D&A run-rate ($3.4B/qtr, annualising near $13B) means capital intensity is roughly **3x the FY23 level**. Q1 FY27 operating cash flow was flattered by **$11.4B of customer prepayments** — a real cash inflow, but one that (correctly) raises the question of what happens when prepayments stop.
- **Counterparty concentration into speculative-grade AI tenants.** A $664B backlog is only worth its counterparties. Oracle's largest AI customers are pre-profit AI labs whose ability to fund 10-year take-or-pay commitments is unproven — the market's central 2026 concern. Compounding this, the **software layer is de-rating sector-wide** as investors question whether AI app revenue will arrive fast enough to justify the compute being contracted for it; SaaS growth has decelerated to the 8–12% range (CRM +9.6%), and the "AI buildout echoes the 1990s" comparison is now mainstream commentary.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **AVERAGE (deteriorating from Strong)**

**For:** #4 in cloud infrastructure with the fastest growth in the peer set at the IaaS layer (+121%), a $664B RPO that is 7–8x revenue coverage versus ~1.2–1.5x at Microsoft/Alphabet, entrenched high-switching-cost database franchise, a 30.6% operating margin that is second only to Microsoft in the group, and a valuation (14x FY27E) that already discounts substantial execution failure.

**Against:** Oracle is the **only** company in this peer set converting revenue growth into *lower* gross margin at scale (−2,060bps from FY21 to Q1 FY27), which means the incremental dollar of AI revenue is materially less profitable than the incumbent dollar — the opposite of the operating leverage the sector is priced for. It is funding a ~$70B+ capex programme with 9%-coupon debt against counterparties of uncertain credit quality, while Amаzon, Microsoft and Alphabet fund equivalent build-outs from operating cash flow with investment-grade-plus balance sheets. **Porter's Five Forces for Oracle's position are net negative and worsening:** supplier power (Nvidia, HBM oligopoly, power/grid interconnect) is *high and rising* — the binding constraint has moved from capital to electrons and HBM allocation, and Oracle has less of both than the top three; buyer power is *moderate* but concentrated in a handful of AI labs; threat of new entry is high in merchant AI compute (CoreWeave, Nebius) though low in enterprise database; rivalry is *intense but capacity-rationed* for now. Oracle's structural advantage — customer lock-in in database and Fusion ERP — protects the 65% gross margin software business, not the 20%-margin IaaS business that is now 30%+ of growth.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL — with a negative tilt on AI infrastructure**

Underlying end-market demand (+15–20% growth, $1T of 2027 capex) is genuinely attractive, and Oracle's ~53% YTD decline has de-rated it to 14x forward earnings — the cheapest large-cap in the group. But the sector is in the **late phase of a capex boom in which returns accrue to suppliers (Nvidia, HBM, power) and to owners of diversified cash flows, not to leveraged lessees of compute.** Three things would change the rating to Attractive: (1) ORCL gross margin stabilising above 60%, (2) capex guidance capped and self-funding achieved (management's FY30 FCF target implies an 8.5% FCF yield at $104), or (3) credit spreads on AI infrastructure stabilising. Until then, the equity is a levered call on other companies' ability to pay — a risk the bond market is pricing at 9% and the equity market at 14x. **Sector: Neutral. Oracle within it: Average competitive position, but the highest-beta expression of the sector's key risk.**
