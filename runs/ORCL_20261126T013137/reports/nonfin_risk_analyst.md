## ORACLE CORPORATION (ORCL) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**As of 25 November 2026 | Price ~$103.88 | Market cap ~$314bn | 52-wk decline ~53% from Sept-2025 high**

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

| Factor | Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Board composition | 13 directors, but **3 are executives** (Ellison – Exec Chairman *and* CTO; Catz – Exec Vice Chair; Henley – Exec Vice Chair) plus **two sitting co-CEOs** (Sicilia and Magouyrk). Only **8 of 13 (~62%) independent** | Below the 75% norm; executive-heavy |
| Power concentration | Ellison is simultaneously Executive Chairman, CTO, and largest shareholder (~1.15bn shares, roughly 40% of the company). This is de facto founder control dressed as a public board | **High** |
| CEO structure | **Unusual co-CEO arrangement** (Sicilia + Magouyrk, both filed as "Chief Executive Officer" on Form 4s), layered under an Executive Chairman who is also CTO, alongside a former CEO (Catz) still on the board as Exec Vice Chair | Diffuse accountability — no single CEO to hold responsible for the AI capex bet |
| Director quality | Genuine strengths: Boskin (Stanford), Fairhead (ex-BBC Trust), Mihaljevic (Cleveland Clinic CEO), Rusckowski (ex-Quest), Moorman (ex-Amtrak/Norfolk Southern), Chizen (ex-Adobe). Board is *refreshing* (Ablo, Mihaljevic added recently) | Adequate-to-good bench |
| Shareholder rights | Annual elections; no disclosed dual-class supervoting structure. But Boardroom Alpha groups ORCL with controlled/staggered peers where "founders, families or staggered boards leave outside holders little say" | Constrained in practice |
| Executive pay | **Ellison moved from a $1 salary to $950,000 plus options reported at ~$117.8m** — in a year the stock fell ~41%. Also received 223,099 option awards in the Oct-2026 grant cycle | **Pay-for-performance disconnect red flag** |

**Verdict:** The board is professionally credentialed but structurally compromised: an owner-founder who is Chairman, CTO and ~40% holder; a two-headed CEO office; a 62% independence ratio; and a nine-figure option package awarded into a 40%+ share-price decline. It is a board that is unlikely to tell the architect of the AI buildout "no."

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**Pending litigation (material):**
- **Securities class action, D. Del. (filed 5 Feb 2026)** — *In re Oracle* §10(b)/§20(a), class period 12 Jun–16 Dec 2025. Alleges Oracle misrepresented that AI infrastructure spend would convert to near-term revenue growth and understated CapEx/debt/credit-rating/free-cash-flow risk. Trigger facts: 10 Dec 2025 FQ2 print (revenue miss, CapEx blowout, >$10bn negative FCF), FY26 CapEx guided to ~$50bn (+$15bn vs Sept-2025), and disclosure that a **debt financing source backed out** of a commitment. Lead-plaintiff deadline was 6 Apr 2026 (Kessler Topaz et al.). Directors/officers named individually.
- **Bondholder suit (filed ~14 Jan 2026)** — creditors allege Ellison and the company failed to disclose that significant *additional* debt issuance would be required to fund the AI buildout. Unusual and more dangerous than the equity suit: it puts Oracle's credit story itself on trial.
- **Oracle Health / Cerner data-breach litigation** — breach occurred Jan–Apr 2026 on **legacy Cerner servers not yet migrated to OCI**; ~**20 million individuals** affected across ~80 hospitals (disclosed via the Texas AG on 2 Oct 2026). Claims sound in negligence, inadequate security controls, **delayed breach notification**, HIPAA and state-law violations. This is multi-district, multi-state AG exposure.
- **$115m privacy class settlement** (Oracle Advertising/data-collection matter) — payouts commenced Sept 2026. Confirms a pattern of privacy/data-monetisation enforcement exposure.

**Antitrust / information-blocking:** Oracle Health (as Cerner's successor) is in the crossfire of the FTC's Epic Systems investigation into health-IT data-sharing; Oracle is both complainant and target in related "information blocking" petitions. Regulatory pressure in healthcare IT is intensifying, not easing.

**Enforcement history:** EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned **no active SEC enforcement action** naming ORCL — a genuine positive — but note the archive is a lagging indicator for recent matters.

**Credit-rating/regulatory interface:** **S&P downgraded Oracle to BBB– on 9 July 2026** (one notch above junk); **Moody's outlook is negative**. Total debt stood at **~$125.3bn as of 31 Aug 2026**, with bonds yielding ~9% and CDS at record levels. A junk downgrade by mid-2027 is a live market probability, not a tail scenario.

**Read-through:**
- **Net sales ≈ $69m vs. $3.5m of open-market buying — roughly 20:1 selling.** The only buy is a single non-executive director, though it is a large, conviction-sized one at $139.
- **Mitigant 1 — mechanical sales:** most officer sales track RSU vesting and tax withholding (e.g. Sicilia exercised 109,213 units and had 52,806 withheld on 19–21 Sept). These are sell-to-cover, not discretionary bearishness.
- **Mitigant 2 — the Ellison cancellation is the single most important governance datapoint in the file.** A $7.5bn plan adopted in June at the highs and cancelled in October with nothing sold is a genuine alignment signal.
- **The overhang that remains is not sales — it is pledges.** Ellison has **~413 million shares pledged as collateral for margin loans (~36% of his holding, valued around $54bn)** — the largest single exposure in Boardroom Alpha's $71bn AI-insider pledge study. SEC rules require disclosure of *share counts*, not loan size or margin terms. **In a 53% drawdown, a margin call is decided by the lender, not by Ellison.** The Governance Committee reviews the pledge quarterly, but no hard cap or loan-terms disclosure exists. This is a reflexive, forced-seller risk on ~40% of the shareholder base's controlling holder.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Customer concentration in a single counterparty's balance sheet.** RPO reached **$638bn at FY26 year-end (+363% y/y)** and ~$664bn in FQ1-27, driven by a **five-year, ~$300bn OpenAI contract**. Oracle is building data centres to a counterparty whose own funding is unproven; management's own disclosure that certain top customers "might not be able to follow through on their commitments," and that Q1 operating cash flow was flattered by **$11.4bn of customer prepayments**, are the tell. Oracle only confirmed on 20 Nov 2026 that the OpenAI build is "fully funded" — after a $20bn raise. **This is a build-to-spec counterparty credit risk, not a demand risk.**
- **Funding, leverage and the refinancing treadmill.** ~$125bn of debt against negative free cash flow (>$10bn negative in FQ2-26), CapEx guided to ~$50bn, BBB–/negative-outlook ratings, 9% new-issue yields, record CDS, and a lender already walking away from a commitment. Dilution and higher borrowing costs are now embedded in the funding plan. Balance-sheet/financing execution is the dominant operational risk in the name.
- **Cybersecurity and mission-critical reliability.** Three distinct 2025–26 incidents: (a) the **Oracle Health/Cerner breach (~20m patients)** on unmigrated legacy infrastructure with delayed notification; (b) **ShinyHunters exploitation of a PeopleSoft zero-day (CVE-2026-35273, PeopleTools 8.61/8.62)**, with an alleged FBI intrusion; and (c) a FINRA member-firm alert over an **alleged large-scale Oracle Cloud (OCI) credential/data breach**. Oracle runs the ERP, HR and clinical systems of the world's largest enterprises and governments — the tail risk is systemic and uninsurable at the reputational level.
- *Secondary:* key-person dependency on Ellison (age 82) who personally anchors the OpenAI/Stargate relationships and the CTO function; and data-centre construction/power/permitting execution (Abilene campus only "nearly complete").

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Market narrative has turned adversarial.** Stock at a **three-year low**, ~53% off its Sept-2025 high, down 41% YTD versus +2.5% for the Zacks tech sector. Oracle is now the shorthand for "AI debt," quoted alongside CoreWeave in CDS stories; Chamath Palihapitiya's "CDS don't lie" line circulated with Oracle as Exhibit A (25 Nov 2026). Recovery now requires Oracle to *prove* funding, FCF and counterparty quality publicly.
- **Healthcare is the reputational fault line.** A ~20m-patient breach on un-migrated legacy Cerner servers, with no public advisory from Oracle and a state AG having to surface the numbers, is precisely the wrong posture for a vendor selling clinical trust. Add the Cerner integration narrative (Oracle Health ~999 US hospitals vs. Epic's ~1,951) and Oracle risks being branded the *consolidator that degraded* a public-health asset.
- **Trust deficit with creditors specifically.** The bondholder suit alleging non-disclosure of required debt issuance damages Oracle's standing with the exact audience it now depends on for $50bn+ of annual funding. Reputational damage to the credit story *is* financing cost.
- **Mitigants:** genuine product momentum (triple-digit OCI growth, Life Sciences AI agents, multicloud wins, healthcare software as a second engine), a real customer base of governments and F500s, and a cancelled Ellison sale that showed restraint.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH** *(upper end)*

Not **Critical** — there is no going-concern question, no SEC enforcement action, no accounting restatement or whistleblower allegation, revenues are still compounding at 30%+, and the controlling holder just cancelled a $7.5bn sale. But the combination of **founder-controlled, executive-heavy governance**, **~$125bn of debt at the last investment-grade notch**, **counterparty concentration in OpenAI**, **three cybersecurity incidents in eighteen months including a 20m-patient breach**, and **parallel securities *and* bondholder fraud litigation** elevates this well above the sector norm. The risk is not that Oracle is a fraud; it is that governance is too captured to stop a leveraged bet whose downside falls on public shareholders and creditors.

---

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Ellison's 413m pledged shares (~$54bn, ~36% of his stake) with undisclosed loan terms** — the largest single insider pledge in the AI complex. In a 53% drawdown, lender-forced selling is a mechanical, non-fundamental risk to ~40% of the vote. *Escalate to: portfolio risk — request loan-to-value and margin-call thresholds via engagement.*
2. **Bondholder suit alleging non-disclosure of required additional debt issuance** (Jan 2026) running in parallel with the §10(b) securities class action — this is a disclosure-credibility problem with the creditor base Oracle now depends on.
3. **Two co-CEOs + Executive Chairman/CTO + Executive Vice Chair = four executives on a 13-person board (8/13 independent).** No independent chairman; unclear single-point accountability for the CapEx program.
4. **Ellison compensation reset to $950k salary plus ~$117.8m in options in a year of −41% shareholder return** — a clear pay-for-performance failure and a likely target for a low say-on-pay vote.
5. **Oracle Health breach (20m individuals) with delayed notification and no voluntary public advisory** — HIPAA/state-AG and class-action exposure, plus patient-safety reputational tail.
6. **RPO concentration: ~$664bn backlog anchored on a ~$300bn OpenAI contract, funded with $125bn of BBB– debt against negative FCF**, and $11.4bn of customer prepayments flattering reported operating cash flow.
