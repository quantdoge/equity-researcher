## 1. Business Quality Assessment — **Moat: None (today). Potential Narrow if KT-621 reaches market with a differentiated label.**

Kymera is a **pre-revenue, clinical-stage** targeted protein degradation (TPD) company with 273 employees. There is no product revenue, no approval, no pricing power, and no switching costs. FY2025 revenue of $39.2M was collaboration/milestone income, not a commercial business — and it *fell* from $78.6M (2023) → $47.1M (2024) → $39.2M (2025) as legacy collaborations wound down. This is definitionally outside a Buffett circle of competence built on predictable cash flows.

What is genuinely valuable is the asset, not yet the business:

**KT-621 (oral STAT6 degrader)** — BROADEN2 Phase 2b, n=244, week 16 (23-Nov-2026):
- Mean EASI −72% (mid dose) vs −27% placebo (p<0.0001); low −64%, high −61%
- EASI-75 **62% vs 22%**; EASI-90 **43% vs 10%**; vIGA-AD 0/1 38% vs 11%
- Pruritus −58% vs −21%, significant by **week 1**
- Mostly mild/moderate TEAEs, no dose-dependence, 4 SAEs unrelated; conjunctivitis 11–20%; 98% of eligible patients entered the 52-week open-label extension

That is a **dupilumab-comparable efficacy profile in a once-daily pill with no JAK boxed-warning mechanism** — the single most commercially attractive profile in immunology. It is also *not* a clean win: efficacy was flat-to-inverted across doses (64/72/61%), a reminder that the exposure–response story is not fully settled, and conjunctivitis rates are at or above biologic benchmarks, which matters for a drug whose entire commercial pitch is "biologic efficacy without biologic safety baggage."

**Moat verdict:** the TPD platform and chemistry know-how are real intangible assets, but an unapproved molecule facing dupilumab, lebrikizumab, nemolizumab, oral JAKs (upadacitinib, abrocitinib) and a crowded field of oral IL-23/STAT6 programs has **no demonstrated moat**. Pricing power in 2031 will be set in a market where Dupixent biosimilars exist. Moat could widen to **Narrow** only after approval, label differentiation and formulary access are proven.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — probability-weighted rNPV ≈ **$60–90/share**; price embeds the bull case

Because there is no earnings base, P/E, EV/EBITDA and FCF yield are meaningless (all negative/NA). Valuation must be a risk-adjusted NPV of KT-621 plus cash, with the early pipeline as free option value.

**Base case (50% weight) — $60/share**
- Launch 2031 (Phase 3 AD starts mid-2027; 2-yr enrollment + review); asthma Ph2b reads late 2027
- Risk-adjusted peak sales ~$7B (AD ~$4B, asthma ~$2B, other type-2 ~$1B), 32% peak FCF margin → ~$2.2B FCF at peak (2038)
- 11% discount rate, 2.5% terminal growth, 60% probability of approval
- Unrisked commercial PV ≈ $10.7B × 0.60 = $6.4B; less PV of 2027–2030 Phase 3 burn ≈ −$1.9B; **plus** $1.5B net cash → **≈$6.0B equity ≈ $60/share**

**Bull case (25%) — $170/share:** dupilumab-scale franchise (peak $11–12B), 65% PoS, 9% discount rate, plus partial credit for KT-579 (IRF5/lupus) and platform deals.

**Bear case (25%) — $20/share:** Phase 3 safety/label disappointment or a partner extracting most of the economics; ~$3.5B peak, 45% PoS.

**Probability-weighted value ≈ $78/share.** The stock at $150 trades at ~2× my base case and ~1.9× the probability-weighted value — i.e., **the market has jumped straight to the bull column.**

**What the price implies:** an EV of $13.5B requires roughly **$11–13B of peak sales at ~65% probability**, or ~$9B of peak sales discounted at 8%. That is a top-decile immunology outcome, awarded before a single Phase 3 patient has been dosed, in a market where the incumbent biologic goes biosimilar this decade. The sell side has repriced accordingly (Stifel $200, Wolfe $180, Guggenheim $170, Jefferies/Truist $165, BTIG $139, Oppenheimer/Raymond James/Piper $140; median ≈ $165) — note that even the *bullish* consensus median sits only ~10% above the current price, and the post-data move (+31%) largely closed the gap to fair value in one session.

**Margin of safety: none.** A 20–30% discount to my *probability-weighted* value implies a buy zone of roughly **$55–62**; even a discount to the bull case ($170) implies ≤$135.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good (B+), with one structural flaw**

- **Founder-led:** CEO Nello Mainolfi, co-founder, scientist-operator; 273 employees executing on a fast, credible clinical cadence (Ph1 → Ph1b → Ph2b in ~18 months with clean data).
- **Trial design discipline:** BROADEN2 deliberately controlled placebo response (site selection, prior-med restrictions, screening) — a sophisticated sponsor move that protects the readout's credibility.
- **Capital allocation:** opportunistic, large equity raises ($1.6B cash, runway into 2029) rather than debt or desperate financing; zero M&A empire-building; no buybacks/dividends (appropriate).
- **The flaw:** the model is structurally dilutive (~20% share growth per year). Management's shareholder-aligned move now is to **partner KT-621 for ex-US/large-cap economics or sell the company**, funding Phase 3 and commercialization without another 15–20% dilution. History with Sanofi (IRAK4 collaboration) suggests the partnership path is well understood internally.
- **Unverified:** exact insider ownership and recent insider transaction pattern — I could not obtain DEF 14A detail within budget.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID** (for a value mandate)

This is not a criticism of the science — BROADEN2 is arguably the best oral immunology dataset of 2026, and Kymera is a legitimate best-in-class TPD company. It is a criticism of the **price relative to a defensible intrinsic value**:

1. **No margin of safety:** $150 vs probability-weighted value ~$78 and base-case value ~$60. The price already assumes a dupilumab-successor outcome.
2. **No moat yet, no FCF, no revenue durability** — three of the four pillars of a Buffett-quality business are absent.
3. **Binary risk sits with the buyer:** Phase 3 AD (from mid-2027) and asthma Ph2b (late 2027) are financing-and-outcome events that can halve the equity, and shareholders will likely absorb another large raise before approval.
4. **Asymmetry has inverted:** the pre-data setup (sub-$100 with optionality) was interesting; post-data, the stock has captured two-thirds of the upside while retaining all of the clinical and financing risk.

**Honest caveat:** my rNPV leans on conservative peak-sales (~$7B) and an 11% hurdle. If one believes with high conviction that KT-621 is a $12B+ franchise, $150 is defensible and a strategic acquirer could pay $200+. That is a venture-style bet on a single molecule, not a value investment — and it should be sized accordingly (i.e., not at all in a value portfolio).

**What would change my mind:** (a) a price of **$75–90** (25–30% discount to probability-weighted value); (b) a **large pharma partnership** that funds Phase 3 and commercial build-out while Kymera retains meaningful US economics — that would lift PoS and cut financing risk simultaneously; or (c) confirmatory Phase 3 data with a clean, differentiated label.
