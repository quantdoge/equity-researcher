## 1. Macro Environment Summary — *"Late-cycle re-tightening" (risk-on by price, deteriorating by policy)*

The 2025 soft landing has given way to a 2026 **inflation re-acceleration and a resumed Fed hiking cycle**. The Fed cut from 5.33% to a 3.63% trough in mid-2026, then reversed: effective fed funds 3.75% (Sep-26) → 3.88% (Oct-26), with a Reuters poll (10-Nov-26) and UBS both expecting **another 25bp in December to 4.00–4.25%**, and UBS adding March-2027.

Crucially, **there is no recession signal** — this is not a growth scare:
- Real GDP Q3-26: **+2.1% YoY**, **+3.35% QoQ SAAR** (the strongest quarter since 2025)
- Unemployment **4.1%** (Oct-26), *down* from 4.5% a year earlier
- Nominal PCE **+6.3% YoY** (Sep-26) — nominal demand running hot

The curve has re-steepened to **+39bp (2s10s)** but as a **bear steepener**: the 10Y is up ~75bp since late June to 5.13% while the front end is anchored by Fed hikes. That is a **term-premium/fiscal-inflation signal, not a growth signal**. Credit is the one amber light: HY OAS has widened **+113bp since late August (2.63% → 3.76%)** — still tight in absolute terms, but the direction of travel is caution.

Yet risk assets are at highs — **XBI +25% YTD 2026**, outperforming the S&P 500. The sector has **decoupled from rates** on clinical-catalyst momentum. That decoupling is the single most important fact for KYMR.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact — KYMR

KYMR is a **pre-revenue, long-duration, high-beta (2.03) clinical-stage biotech**: $12.8B market cap on ~$99M of 2026E collaboration revenue, 273 employees, and **$1.646B of cash at 30-Sep-26 with runway into 2029** — now being reinforced by a **$1.5B equity offering (+$225M greenshoe) launched 23-Nov-26 at a fixed $150.37, i.e. Monday's close, with no discount.**

| 10Y 5.13% / real 2.77% | Negative | **Strongly negative** | Dominant headwind. A terminal-value-heavy 2032+ DCF loses roughly 15–25% of NPV per 100bp of WACC; real yields are +61bp in 5 months |

**The key insight:** KYMR's macro exposure is almost purely a **discount-rate and risk-appetite** exposure, not an operating one. It has no debt maturity wall to refinance at 5%+ and no near-term revenue tied to GDP. Its vulnerability is that a 5.13% 10-year caps the multiple the market will pay for 2031+ cash flows — precisely why the same-day $1.5B raise is the right macro move: it converts rate risk into locked-in capital at a 3.7x-off-the-low share price, taking total pro-forma liquidity toward ~$3.1B and removing financing risk through Phase 3.

Note the volatility proof: KYMR fell ~46% ($40.89 → $22.07) between 2-Jan-25 and 4-Apr-25 during the tariff shock, before rallying **+268%** to today's $150.37. A beta-2.03 long-duration asset is a macro-shock amplifier.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE** (severity: moderate, not acute)

The macro regime is the worst possible *backdrop* for a pre-revenue biotech — Fed hiking, CPI at 3.82%, 10Y at 5.13%, real yields +61bp, spreads widening, USD firm, and a tariff/MFN regime that caps long-run US drug pricing. **But it is not binding on KYMR right now**, because (a) the sector is in a catalyst-driven bull market (XBI +25% YTD) that has decoupled from rates, (b) KYMR carries no refinancing exposure, and (c) it just accessed $1.5B of equity at zero discount — the clearest possible signal that capital markets remain open for de-risked assets. Treat macro as a **multiple ceiling and volatility source**, not a solvency or operating threat.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario (which way the tail risk sits)

**Base case the market is pricing:** one more hike in December, then a long hold, with 10Y 4.90–5.20% and XBI holding its gains.

**Bear surprise (asymmetric and under-hedged):** December hike *plus* a March-2027 hike as CPI holds above 3.5%, 10Y breaks 5.50% (it already touched 5.31% on 5-Oct-26), HY OAS goes through 450bp, and XBI gives back 20%. For a 2.03-beta name, that is a **30–40% drawdown** regardless of BROADEN2 — with the added mechanical risk that a $1.5B follow-on launched into a tape reversal prices at a widening discount or shrinks. Compounding it, an aggressive MFN/IRA price-cap outcome would directly haircut the peak-sales number that justifies today's $12.8B cap.

**Bull surprise (the more likely positive tail):** inflation rolls over on energy/goods in Q1-27, the Fed's December hike is the last, the 10Y retraces to 4.50–4.70% and real yields back toward 2.30%. XBI re-rates 15–20% and Kymera — now with ~$3.1B of cash, a clean Phase 2b, a Phase 3 starting by mid-2027, and a class-defining oral STAT6 asset — becomes the scarce US-domiciled oral-immunology takeout/partnering target for an MFN-signing pharma that needs US manufacturing commitments. That is a setup where **falling rates and strategic scarcity compound**, and the move is 40–60%, not 15%.

**Positioning conclusion:** the macro is a headwind to *own*, not a reason to *avoid* — but it argues for sizing KYMR as a high-beta, catalyst-dated position and for treating 5.30% on the 10Y and 400bp HY OAS as explicit de-risking triggers.
