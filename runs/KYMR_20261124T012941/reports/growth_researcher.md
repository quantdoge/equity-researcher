## 1. Growth Story

Kymera is the first company to make protein degradation work as an *immunology* platform, and KT-621 — an oral, once-daily STAT6 degrader — is the asset that converts the platform into a potential Dupixent-class franchise. On **November 23, 2026**, the BROADEN2 Phase 2b trial in moderate-to-severe atopic dermatitis (n=244) hit **all primary and secondary endpoints**, with mid-dose patients achieving **72% EASI reduction vs 27% placebo** and **62% EASI-75 vs 22%** at Week 16 — efficacy in the range of injectable biologics, from a pill. The stock rose **29% on 7.8M shares (4x average volume)**. This is no longer a platform story with an option on a drug; it is a pre-Phase 3 immunology asset with a validated mechanism, a clean safety profile, and a validated delivery advantage over the entire injectable class.

**Why this matters for growth**: the addressable prize is not eczema — it is the ~$25B+ Type 2 inflammation market currently monopolized by injectables. An oral agent with biologic-comparable efficacy resets the treatment hierarchy from "step-up to injections" to "oral first-line," which historically has driven 2–3x penetration versus biologics in analogous switches (oral JAKs, oral GLP-1s).

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

**TAM**: Type 2 inflammation spans atopic dermatitis, asthma, chronic spontaneous urticaria (CSU), COPD, eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE), and CRSwNP. Dupixent — the incumbent — is immunology's top-selling drug with **1.4M patients currently on therapy** and tens of billions in annual revenue. Total Type 2 I&I branded market is **>$25B and growing at high single digits**, with biologics penetrating <15% of the eligible pool because injections limit adoption.

**SAM for an oral**: 8–12M moderate-to-severe AD patients in the US/EU5 alone, of whom <20% are on advanced therapy. Add asthma (~10% severe), CSU, and COPD, and the oral-eligible SAM exceeds **$35–40B**.

**Penetration today: 0%.** First Phase 3 AD trial starts **by mid-2027**; commercial launch is realistically **2030–2031**. The consensus $4B-by-2035 estimate implies only ~**10–12% share** of the Type 2 market — a conservative bar for a drug with biologic-comparable efficacy, once-daily oral dosing, and no cold-chain or injection burden. A bull case of $8–10B peak (oral-first-line positioning across 4–5 indications) is credible if asthma replicates AD.

**Innovation moat — rated Strong**: KT-621 is the **most advanced STAT6 degrader in the clinic globally**, with 94–98% median STAT6 degradation in blood and skin, and it **closed Phase 2b enrollment six months early** (a powerful demand/physician-enthusiasm signal). STAT6 was long considered undruggable; Kymera's CRBN-based degrader chemistry is the proprietary asset. Oral small molecules also carry durable cost-of-goods and manufacturing advantages over bispecifics/biologics. The moat is a **~18–24 month clinical lead**, not a permanent one — large pharma STAT6 programs are close behind.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (not Strong Buy)

**The thesis is now de-risked but no longer cheap.** I am upgrading the *quality* of the story while tempering the *expected return*: the probability-adjusted value of eczema has largely been capitalized (EV = 2.8x consensus 2035 peak sales, ~65% embedded PoS), and the stock sits at $150.37 versus a post-data analyst target cluster of $165–200 (**10–33% upside**).

What earns a Buy rather than a Hold: (1) **$1.646B of cash funding runway into 2029** eliminates the financing overhang that kills most clinical-stage theses; (2) **free optionality on asthma, CSU, COPD and EoE**, where the market assigns little value and where late-2027 BREADTH data could double peak-sales estimates; (3) a **first-in-class clinical lead** in a validated, high-value mechanism; (4) a **second pillar (KT-579/IRF5)** that validates the platform, not just the drug.

**Position sizing implication**: this is a binary-event asset with beta 2.03 trading at the top of its range. Accumulate on post-data consolidation into the $120–135 zone rather than chasing $150; add aggressively ahead of BREADTH asthma data in late 2027 only if the balance sheet remains un-diluted.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Phase 3 failure risk (highest severity)** — Phase 2b is 244 patients at Week 16. Dupixent-scale programs succeed or fail on 1,000+ patient, 52-week durability and long-term safety. Historical Phase 2→Phase 3 success rates in immunology are ~55–65%. A Phase 3 miss implies **60–75% downside** to platform-value-only.
2. **Safety — conjunctivitis signal** — 13.1% conjunctivitis at the mid-dose (all mild/moderate, no discontinuations) is manageable but is a known class issue that has limited other I&I agents. Phase 3 exposure will sharpen this.
3. **Competitive intensity is arriving fast** — STAT6 is now "the hottest target in immunology," with large pharma and multiple biotechs racing behind Kymera. Oral TYK2 (Alumis, Sotyktu), oral IL-4R approaches, and next-gen biologics (Ebglyss, Nemluvio) all compete for the same script. Kymera's 18–24 month lead is real but **not durable past 2030**, precisely when it needs to launch.
4. **Full valuation with a 12-month dead zone** — at 2.8x 2035 peak sales, there is limited room for error and no near-term catalyst. Multiple compression on any sector-wide biotech risk-off is amplified by beta 2.03.
5. **Commercial execution from scratch** — Kymera has **273 employees and has never commercialized a drug**. Either it builds a specialty sales force (~$300–500M/yr) or it partners, capping economics. The BD hiring suggests partnering is live — which means the terminal revenue share may be materially below the headline $4B.
6. **Dilution history** — shares outstanding have grown from 48M (2021) to 85M+ (2026), and Phase 3 immunology programs cost $400M–$1B+. Runway into 2029 is comfortable, not infinite; a raise before 2029 is plausible and would be dilutive at whatever price prevails.
7. **Revenue optics will worsen before they improve** — consensus models revenue *falling* to ~$40M in 2027 and 2028. Expect headline "growth" screens to flag KYMR negatively; this is an artifact, but it suppresses systematic/quant demand.
