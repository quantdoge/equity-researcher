# T-Mobile US (TMUS) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price:** $146.29 (Oct 26, 2026 close) | **Market cap:** ~$165.5B | **EV:** ~$282B | **Shares (diluted):** 1.131B
**52-week range:** ~$143.92 – $261.56 | **Current position:** ~44% below the 52-week high

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW (trending from widening to stable)**

T-Mobile is the #2/#3 US wireless carrier, serving ~108.7M+ subscribers post-UScellular, with an industry-leading growth record and the deepest low-band (600 MHz) spectrum position in the market. The moat rests on four pillars:

| Moat source | Evidence | Trend |
|---|---|---|
| **Efficient scale** | US wireless is a 3-player oligopoly; ~$88B revenue with only two scaled national rivals. New terrestrial entry is economically irrational — which is exactly why the SpaceX/EchoStar spectrum deal matters. | Stable, but challenged at the margin |
| **Intangible assets (spectrum/licenses)** | Scarce, licensed low-band spectrum; ~$66.8B net PP&E plus multi-billion annual capex creates a decade-long build barrier. | Stable |
| **Cost advantage** | Sprint merger synergies (~$7.5B run-rate) delivered; industry-best incremental margins and revenue growth (+8.5% FY25 vs ~1% at VZ/T). | Stable |
| **Switching costs** | Postpaid phone churn in the ~0.9% range, but eSIM/SIM portability has structurally lowered friction. | **Narrowing** |

**Has the moat widened?** Operationally yes — ROIC has climbed from 3.6% (2022) → 8.0% (2023) → 10.2% (2024) → **10.3% (2025)**, now modestly above my ~7.5% WACC estimate. That positive spread is the single most important datapoint in this report. But it is a *modest* spread for a "wide-moat" claim, and it has plateaued.

**The countervailing evidence:** October 8–9, 2026 — SpaceX agreed to acquire 50 MHz of nationwide low-band plus MSS spectrum from EchoStar for ~$8B (and ~$8B of 800 MHz from Grain Management), intending a Starlink Gen2 direct-to-cell network. Telecom stocks lost ~$50B in market value; TMUS fell 13.3% in a single session on 19M shares (4x normal volume) to a 52-week low. A credible fourth national entrant with its own spectrum is precisely the event that erodes an efficient-scale moat. It is years from being a real terrestrial competitor, but the *terminal-value* assumptions must now carry a discount that they did not before.

**Verdict:** a genuinely good business, but not an impregnable one. Narrow moat is the honest call.

**What's working:**
- **Revenue predictability is excellent.** Subscription-based, ~1% monthly churn, contracted ARPA. The 5-year revenue CAGR is 5.2% and 2026 run-rate quarters are tracking ~$23B each (~$92B annualized).
- **FCF inflection is the core story.** From -$0.5B in 2022 to $15.4B in 2025 — a swing driven by capex discipline ($17.3B → $12.5B) and synergy realization. Management guided 2026 adjusted FCF to **$18.4–18.8B**, a further ~20% step-up.
- **FCF conversion is superb:** $15.43B FCF vs $11.0B net income = 140% (D&A of $13.5B exceeds capex of $12.5B — the classic capital-intensive-carrier cash machine).
- **Share count discipline:** 1.255B (2022) → 1.131B (2025), a 10% reduction, plus $9.97B of 2025 repurchases and $4.12B of dividends — $14.1B returned against $15.4B of FCF.
- **Dividend growth:** $0.65 → $0.88 → $1.02 → $1.17 quarterly — ~20%+ CAGR since initiation.

**What holds the score back:**
- **Leverage.** Net debt $116.7B (total debt $122.3B, cash $5.6B) = **3.7x EBITDA** on reported figures — elevated versus Verizon (~2.5x). Interest expense of $3.52B consumes ~32% of operating income. Refinancing risk is manageable given the maturity ladder, but this is not a fortress balance sheet.
- **Working capital drag** of -$1.95B in 2025 (better than -$7.06B in 2023).
- **Note on the gross margin "collapse":** reported gross margin fell from 63.6% to 47.6% in 2025 — this is a cost-of-revenue reclassification, not deterioration. Operating income *rose* from $18.01B to $18.74B. Do not be misled by headline gross margin here.
- Goodwill and intangibles dropped from $116B to $17.5B — a large write-down/impairment, worth monitoring, though it is non-cash and does not affect owner earnings.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Grade: Good — with a governance asterisk**

- **Capital allocation is genuinely shareholder-oriented:** $14.1B returned in 2025 against $15.4B of FCF; 10% share count reduction in three years; dividend up ~80% in two years. Buybacks at $215 in early 2026 were value-destructive in hindsight; buying at $146 today would not be.
- **Execution credibility is high:** Sprint integration delivered on schedule, synergy targets met, and the 2026 FCF guidance raise ($18.4–18.8B) came *despite* the subscriber-growth stumble — evidence management is managing the P&L for cash, not vanity metrics.
- **Candor:** Management explicitly warned that Q3 2026 postpaid account additions would decline sequentially as it migrates customers to pricier plans. That is voluntary near-term pain for long-term ARPA — a rational trade, and they flagged it rather than hiding it.
- **Asterisk — controlling shareholder:** Deutsche Telekom holds ~51–53%. DT has stated no plans to sell in 2026, but the structural overhang is permanent and DT's dividend appetite can compete with buybacks for the same FCF. Minority holders do not control capital allocation here.
- **Asterisk — leverage-funded returns:** returning ~90% of FCF while carrying 3.7x net debt/EBITDA leaves limited flexibility if the competitive environment deteriorates.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **BUY** (accumulate on weakness; not a Strong Buy)

**Thesis in three lines:**
1. A narrow-moat oligopoly with improving ROIC, 21% operating margins and a 9–11% FCF yield trading at 9x EBITDA.
2. The 32% drawdown since February 2026 — capped by the Oct 9 SpaceX spectrum shock — prices in permanent impairment from an entrant that cannot meaningfully contest dense-market capacity for years.
3. At $146, you collect ~8.5% total shareholder yield and pay ~8.9x guided 2026 FCF, with ~45% upside to base-case intrinsic value and only ~12% downside in a genuinely bearish terminal-value scenario. The asymmetry is favorable.

**Why not Strong Buy:** the ROIC-WACC spread is thin (~2.8 points), leverage is high at 3.7x, the competitive narrative has genuinely changed, and my bear case sits only 12% below today's price — the floor is close. A Strong Buy requires a wider gap between price and worst case.

**Action:** buy in tranches. First tranche now; add below $135 (approaching bear-case value); add on evidence that Q3/Q4 2026 subscriber trends stabilize post-plan-migration. Do not chase above $175 (~17% discount to base case).

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **SpaceX / Starlink direct-to-cell + owned spectrum (highest severity, long duration).** If satellite-plus-terrestrial service reaches parity in populated areas within 5–7 years, T-Mobile's pricing power and terminal growth compress. My bear case ($130) is the quantification of this risk. Watch for SpaceX capex commitments to terrestrial buildout and any MVNO/roaming partnership announcements — a roaming deal with T-Mobile would actually be *bullish* (wholesale revenue) rather than competitive.
2. **Cable competition (Comcast/Charter).** Fixed-wireless access and MVNO wholesale deals keep marginal pricing pressure on. Converged "home internet + mobile" bundles are Verizon's and AT&T's advantage, not T-Mobile's — T-Mobile's fiber position is smaller and JV-dependent.
3. **Leverage and interest cost.** $116.7B net debt at 3.7x EBITDA; $3.52B annual interest. A refinancing at higher rates or an EBITDA decline would pressure both FCF and the buyback.
4. **Subscriber growth deceleration.** The premium-plan migration is causing sequential postpaid account declines. If churn spikes rather than normalizes, the "quality upgrade" thesis fails and revenue growth stalls below 3%.
5. **Deutsche Telekom overhang.** A ~51% holder can block, direct, or eventually distribute shares — an unquantifiable but permanent discount factor.
6. **Capex creep.** FCF growth from $15.4B to $18.6B depends on capex staying near $12.5B. Network densification, mid-band buildout, or fiber commitments could consume the delta.
7. **Near-term event risk:** Q3 2026 results (Oct 28, 2026) arrive two days after this analysis — consensus EPS $2.89 on ~$23.1B revenue. A second consecutive subscriber disappointment could retest the $144 low.

---

**Bottom line:** T-Mobile is a high-quality, cash-generative narrow-moat business whose shares have been repriced by a plausible-but-premature structural fear. The fundamentals — 8.5% revenue growth, 21% operating margins, 10.3% ROIC, $15.4B FCF rising to ~$18.6B, and 10% annual share shrinkage — remain intact. At 8.9x forward FCF with a ~31% discount to base-case intrinsic value, the price now offers an adequate margin of safety. **Buy.**
