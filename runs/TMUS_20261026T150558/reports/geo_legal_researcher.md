# TMUS — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
**T-Mobile US, Inc. | Communication Services / Telecom Services | HQ: Bellevue, WA | Market cap ~$157B**

---

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

T-Mobile is effectively a **single-country business**: the FY2025 Form 10-K states that *"substantially all of our revenues for the years ended December 31, 2025, 2024 and 2023, were earned in the United States, including Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands."* FY2025 total revenue was $88.3B (service revenue $71.3B; net income $11.0B; core adj. EBITDA $33.9B; adj. FCF $18.0B), with ~108.7M subscribers.

| Geography | Revenue Exposure | Risk Level | Key Drivers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States (contiguous)** | ~98–99% | **Medium** | Heavy regulatory exposure (FCC, FTC, state AGs); cyber/national-security targeting; spectrum policy; litigation |
| **Puerto Rico & U.S. Virgin Islands** | <1–2% | **Medium-High** | Hurricane/natural-disaster network risk; US legal regime applies; limited scale |
| *Germany (parent, not revenue)* | 0% revenue | **Medium-High** | Deutsche Telekom holds ~52.8% of capital and ~57% of votes via proxy agreement — foreign control of US critical infrastructure |
| *Supply-chain exposure (not revenue)* | n/a | **Medium** | Handset OEM concentration in China/India/Vietnam; network vendors Ericsson (SE), Nokia (FI), Samsung (KR); tariff and export-control sensitivity |

**Key structural point:** Because there is no foreign revenue, T-Mobile avoids the classic EM/currency/sanctions exposures. The geographic risk is instead **concentrated, not diversified** — all revenue sits in one jurisdiction whose regulator and national-security apparatus are highly activist.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — Deutsche Telekom full-combination and foreign control of U.S. critical infrastructure
**Probability: Moderate (30–40% of a formal proposal within 12–18 months) | Impact: High**

Bloomberg (Apr 21, 2026) and Reuters (Apr 22, 2026) reported DT exploring a full combination with TMUS that would create the world's largest phone group (surpassing the $203B Vodafone–Mannesmann deal), likely via a new holding structure with U.S. listings. DT already owns ~53%.

Critically, Semafor/Investing.com reported (July 31, 2026) that **T-Mobile's U.S. executives withdrew support for the ~$300B plan**, citing shareholder concerns and regulatory risk, and that *"government officials indicated that U.S. regulators would likely require a guarantee that T-Mobile's US revenue would be reinvested in the country or otherwise remain in the United States."* Several large institutional minority holders also signaled opposition.

Why this is geopolitical, not just M&A: a combination would put America's largest wireless network under a foreign-headquartered parent (partly German state-linked via KfW), triggering **CFIUS/Team Telecom review, FCC public-interest scrutiny, and likely Congressional and national-security conditions** (data localization, network security commitments, U.S. board/management control, reinvestment guarantees). The 2020 CFIUS-era commitments DT gave when it took majority control would need to be renegotiated or extended. Even a failed attempt creates governance overhang and a minority-shareholder squeeze-out debate.

### Risk 2 — Chinese state-sponsored cyber espionage (Salt Typhoon) and network-security mandates
**Probability: High (already materialized) | Impact: High**

The WSJ reported T-Mobile was among the carriers compromised in the massive Chinese breach of U.S. telecom networks; CRS (IF12798) and CISA advisory AA25-239A attribute the activity to PRC state-sponsored actors overlapping with "Salt Typhoon." Senate Commerce (2025) and a House probe (Aug 2026) found U.S. networks remain vulnerable and Chinese telecom firms retained footholds despite FCC crackdowns. The 2021 breach (separate, criminal) already cost $350M in settlement plus $150M in security investment.

Exposure: remediation capex, potential government enforcement/consent-decree obligations, reputational damage, and possible liability if state actors exfiltrated call-detail records or lawful-intercept systems. This is the highest-probability *state-actor* risk and ties directly to U.S.–China decoupling. Note also T-Mobile has already removed Huawei gear (Secure and Trusted Communications Networks Act "rip and replace"), so vendor exposure is now Ericsson/Nokia/Samsung — lower risk but concentrated.

### Risk 3 — Trade policy, device supply chain and satellite/direct-to-device policy disruption
**Probability: High | Impact: Moderate**

Tariff policy has been volatile: April 2025 reciprocal tariffs exempted smartphones/computers (Reuters), but exemptions have shifted repeatedly, with Section 232 semiconductor tariffs and threatened new electronics duties; India faced 50% tariffs in 2025 (PIIE). Because T-Mobile funds device promotions and installment plans, handset cost inflation feeds subsidy costs and consumer pricing — a margin and political-sensitivity issue.

Separately, FCC spectrum policy is a live competitive/regulatory wildcard: the FCC has proposed auctioning 25 MHz of mid-band (1675–1695 MHz) with officials citing $100B+ potential auction proceeds (Reuters, Sept 2026); and a proposal to open up to 482 MHz for satellite-to-phone service could benefit SpaceX's Starlink Mobile (FCC approved 15,000 satellites Oct 6, 2026; AWS-3 license transfers to Starlink Oct 21, 2026). AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile are jointly lobbying to remove 5G bands from that plan. Direct-to-device could partially disintermediate terrestrial carriers — a policy-driven competitive threat rather than a classic geopolitical one, but with material strategic consequences.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**FCC enforcement history (repeat-offender pattern):**
- **$80M fine** for failing to reasonably protect customers' location information (FCC).
- **$15.75M civil penalty + $15.75M compliance investment** under a 2024 CPNI consent decree covering multiple data breaches (FCC DA-24-860; Hunton).
- **~$48M settlement** over undisclosed limits on "unlimited" data: $7.5M civil penalty plus up to $35M consumer benefits (Kelley Drye).
- **Sept 11, 2025 consent decree** resolving an FCC investigation into marketing/sale of unauthorized phones.

**Consumer protection / pricing litigation:**
- **New York AG settlement (Oct 20, 2026):** T-Mobile will provide up to **$38M+ in consumer value** and pay $98,000 to New York after investigating 2017–2019 "Price Guarantee Plans" (promising "never change the price you pay") that were raised ~$5/line in 2024, affecting ~252,957 New York lines; price freeze through at least 2029. This is a template for other state AGs (California previously settled Sprint-merger challenge).
- **Mass arbitration and class actions** over price-lock/rate-guarantee breaches are active; T-Mobile's terms impose individual binding arbitration, which caps aggregate exposure but invites mass-arbitration cost attacks. An FCC investigation into forced plan migrations is also reported.

**Data breach MDL:** *In re: T-Mobile Customer Data Security Breach Litigation*, Case No. 21-md-3019-BCW — $350M settlement plus $150M security investment; court proceedings complete and initial distribution concluded. Residual risk: repeat-breach recidivism and regulator skepticism.

**M&A regulatory track record:** FCC approved the **$4.4B UScellular acquisition on July 11, 2025** (closed Aug 1, 2025) **without conditions**, rejecting roaming/spectrum-aggregation conditions; Mint Mobile approved 2024 with a 60-day device-unlocking commitment; 50% Metronet stake approved. Current FCC posture is generally permissive on consolidation but highly activist on public-interest/DEI and national-security grounds.

**Political/regulatory entanglement:** T-Mobile ended its DEI programs in July 2025 while awaiting FCC approval of two deals (Reuters, NYT), following FCC pressure under Chairman Carr; Congressional Democrats (May 2026) accuse the FCC of abusing merger reviews to extract policy concessions. This cuts both ways: near-term approval tailwinds, but elevated headline and governance risk. State-level regimes (California, New York) remain adversarial on privacy and consumer pricing.

**Litigation gaps:** The tool could not retrieve the FY2025 10-K text (10-K fetch failed; only a 10-Q exhibit returned), so I could not verify the full "Legal Proceedings" footnote. Figures above are corroborated by regulator documents and press reports rather than the filing itself.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **CPNI / data privacy — repeated enforcement.** Multiple FCC consent decrees ($15.75M, $80M location-data fine) indicate recurring control weaknesses rather than one-off events. High priority.
2. **Consumer protection / deceptive marketing.** NY AG finding of misleading "price guarantee" claims with hidden fine print; similar "unlimited data" and unauthorized-phone decrees. State AG and FTC exposure is ongoing.
3. **National-security / foreign ownership.** DT's majority stake and any combination require CFIUS/Team Telecom assurances; Chinese state-actor intrusions invite mandatory security directives.
4. **Sanctions/AML/FCPA: low.** No material foreign operations or high-risk-jurisdiction revenue; exposure is indirect via device supply chains and vendor diligence (forced-labor/Uyghur concerns in component sourcing).
5. **Arbitration-clause dependence.** Legal strategy relies on individual arbitration; adverse rulings or mass-arbitration campaigns could raise effective settlement costs.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** T-Mobile's *revenue* geography is benign — ~100% U.S., no sanctions, currency, or expropriation exposure, and a strong rule-of-law jurisdiction. But three factors push the rating to High:
- **Single-jurisdiction concentration** in the most activist U.S. regulatory environment in decades (FCC enforcement, state AG consumer actions, spectrum and D2D policy).
- **Foreign-parent event risk**: DT's ~53% control and a contested ~$300B combination that would require CFIUS/Team Telecom/FCC approval with possible reinvestment and security conditions — plus reported opposition from T-Mobile's own executives and minority holders.
- **Persistent state-sponsored cyber targeting** (Salt Typhoon/PRC), with demonstrated breach history and rising remediation/compliance costs.

It is **not "Very High"** because there is no meaningful emerging-market, sanctions, or conflict-zone revenue, no commodity or currency-control exposure, and the U.S. legal system provides predictable, if costly, remedies. The principal ways this rating worsens: (a) a formal DT combination announcement that politicizes the stock and triggers national-security conditions; (b) a major new breach or confirmed exfiltration of lawful-intercept data; (c) a multi-state AG wave following New York's price-guarantee precedent; or (d) FCC satellite-spectrum rules that materially advantage Starlink Mobile.

**Monitoring triggers:** DT/TMUS deal announcements or withdrawal; CFIUS/Team Telecom filings; FCC actions on 1675–1695 MHz auction and D2D spectrum; additional state AG price-guarantee suits; CISA/FBI advisories on Salt Typhoon remediation; tariff changes on handsets/semiconductors; UScellular/Metronet integration conditions.
