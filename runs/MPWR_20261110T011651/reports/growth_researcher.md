### 1. Growth Story
MPS is no longer a diversified analog power house that dabbles in AI — it has become a direct **AI-factory power compounder**. Enterprise Data revenue hit **$506.7M in Q3'26, +165% YoY (42% of total revenue)**, driving the first-ever $1B+ quarter and 63% total company growth, and management *raised* its Enterprise Data growth floor from 130% to 150% YoY. With NVIDIA's 800 VDC architecture moving 1 MW racks to volume in 2027, **power-conversion silicon content per rack is inflecting upward** — MPS's proprietary BCD/DCDC process technology and module-level integration let it capture more dollars per AI rack each generation, while automotive, industrial and comms provide a second, less crowded growth engine (comms +135% YoY on 800G/1.6T optics).

### 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration
- **Base TAM:** global power management/analog ICs ≈ $50–60B, growing high-single digits.
- **The delta that matters:** AI data-center power. Rack power is scaling from ~10–15 kW to **1 MW**, and the 800 VDC transition (2027 ramp) collapses conversion stages at the perimeter — the value of power silicon per rack rises several-fold. Add DDR5/next-gen memory power ICs, optical module power, and IBC/VRM content: the AI power silicon SAM is plausibly **$15–25B by 2030**.
- **Penetration:** FY26E revenue of $4.33B against a ~$50B+ power-semi pool ≈ **~9% share**, and Enterprise Data annualizes at only ~$2.0B today. Management is explicitly building capacity for **$10B of annual revenue** — i.e., the company's own roadmap targets ~2.3x today's revenue, which is roughly what consensus already assumes for 2030. **Upside case: consensus 2030 of $10.5B is the capacity target, not a stretch.**
- **Moat:** proprietary process (BCD-plus), integrated module-level solutions sold at the board level (higher content, stickier design-ins), 4,500 employees, design centers in Asia adjacent to the ODMs/hyperscaler supply chain, and a 20+ year record of share gains in every market it enters. This is a design-win annuity, not a spot business.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts
- **Q4'26 results — Feb 4, 2027.** Guide $1.315–1.385B (midpoint +8% QoQ); watch whether Enterprise Data clears the 150% YoY floor and whether Q1'27 guide extends the sequential ramp.
- **800 VDC / 1 MW rack ramp — 2027.** NVIDIA's 800 V HVDC ecosystem transitions from architecture to volume in 2027; MPS content-per-rack is the single largest swing factor in out-year estimates.

- **Communications/optical (800G→1.6T)** — $187.4M in Q3, +135% YoY; sustainability here is the second leg of the thesis.
- **Capital returns & capacity:** newly upsized **$1B buyback** ($453M repurchased in Q3 alone), capex stepping to $172M+ (2025) to fund the $10B revenue capacity build; plus a **new CFO appointment / leadership transition** to monitor for execution continuity.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (not Strong Buy)
The operating business is executing at a level few semis ever reach — 55%+ growth at 55% gross margin, 100% beat rate, guidance raised on the growth *floor*, and a Rule-of-40 in the 70s. At **26x 2027E EPS for 51% growth**, the near-term multiple is genuinely cheap for this quality of compounding, and the 800 VDC cycle hasn't even started shipping.

But the honest math on the long tail caps the rating: at 25–30x 2030E EPS of $75, fair value is **$1,880–$2,250** — an **~9–13% IRR** from $1,200 over four years. That's good, not exceptional, and it hinges entirely on consensus being right that 2029–30 growth decays to 10–17%. **I'd be a Strong Buyer on any panic-driven break below ~$1,000–1,050 (≈20x 2027E), and a trimmer above ~$1,600.**

### 7. Key Risks
- **Execution/multiple risk is the dominant risk.** 41x FY26E EPS and 13x EV/S leave no room for a single soft guide; the stock already fell 26% from its April high *while fundamentals beat*. Beta 1.73 — expect ±30% drawdowns.
- **Customer & concentration risk in Enterprise Data.** 42% of revenue and nearly all incremental growth now derives from AI server/networking power. Share shifts on a hyperscaler or NVIDIA platform transition can gap a year of estimates in one quarter (this has happened to MPS before).
- **Competitive threat.** Infineon, TI, Vicor, Renesas, onsemi and Asian module vendors are all targeting AI power content; the 800 VDC standard also invites new entrants at the PSU/BBU level, potentially dis-intermediating board-level IC vendors.
- **Gross-margin mix erosion.** GM has drifted from 58.4% (2022) to 55.2–55.7% as data center/comms mix rises. If pricing pressure arrives alongside the ramp, operating leverage stalls.
- **Stock-based compensation is real:** $227M in 2025 (~8% of revenue), the entire gap between $12.75 GAAP and ~$17.07 non-GAAP EPS. GAAP earnings power is materially lower than the consensus numbers we're capitalizing.
- **Consensus deceleration and thin long-tail coverage:** only 2–6 analysts publish 2029–30 estimates; the "30% 5-yr CAGR" is thinly supported beyond 2028 and highly sensitive to AI capex digestion.
- **Capacity/capex execution** on the path to $10B revenue — supply-chain concentration in Taiwan/China fabs is a geopolitical and allocation risk.
