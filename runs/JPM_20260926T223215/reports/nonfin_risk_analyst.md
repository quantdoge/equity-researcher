# NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — JPMorgan Chase & Co. (JPM)
**Prepared by: Non-Financial Risk Analytics | Date: September 2026**
**Sector: Financial Services (Banks – Diversified) | Market Cap ~$919B | HQ: New York | CEO: Jamie Dimon**

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate** (leaning Weak on succession/concentration)

**Evidence:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality with extreme tenure concentration:** Jamie Dimon serves as **both Chairman and CEO**, a role he has held for ~20 years (CEO since 2005/2006). This is the single largest governance concentration in U.S. banking.
- **Succession bench has thinned materially.** Dimon had named **co-presidents (Jennifer Piepszak and Marianne Lake)** as successors. Piepszak departed the firm in **early 2025**, and **Marianne Lake announced her departure in 2025/2026**. The two principal internal successors are gone, leaving the board's succession plan effectively reset — a governance red flag for a G-SIB of this complexity.
- **Compensation:** Dimon's FY2025 total compensation was **~$40.6M** ($1.5M salary + ~$32.5M incentive). High absolute pay, but mitigated by substantial personal stock ownership (~$150M+ holdings), which aligns him with shareholders (majority of wealth tied to equity).
- **Board composition:** Board is large and majority-independent with strong banking/regulatory expertise; say-on-pay has consistently passed. No independent-lead-director structure that overrides the duality, though the board did engineer the co-president structure — indicating awareness.
- **No accounting/governance-misstatement scandals** in the review window; financial controls are generally regarded as the industry benchmark.

**Net view:** Board quality and controls are strong, but **Chairman/CEO duality combined with a decimated succession pipeline** is the defining governance weakness.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **Elevated / Material**

Active matters and recent enforcement:

- **BaFin record AML fine (Nov 2025):** JPMorgan SE (Frankfurt) fined **€45M (~$52.5M)** — the **largest fine in BaFin's history** — for **"systemic" and "serious" failures in the timely submission of suspicious activity reports** (AML). This is a headline regulatory event and signals control weaknesses at the European entity, with likely remediation/monitoring obligations.
- **Jeffrey Epstein legacy (resolved but reputational anchor):** Paid **$290M** to Epstein victims and **$75M** to the U.S. Virgin Islands (2023). Ongoing oversight scrutiny of the bank's relationship with Epstein-era clients persists (Senate Wyden report flagged AML deficiencies).
- **Zelle consumer-fraud litigation:** CFPB sued JPM (with BofA/Wells) over fraud on the Zelle network; the federal suit was dismissed in **March 2025**, but the **New York AG sued Zelle (Aug 2025)** alleging ~$1B in fraud — JPM is an owner bank and defendant set. **JPMorgan spent ~$2.14B on fines and legal costs** across recent matters.
- **Repeat recordkeeping/off-channel communications penalties:** JPMorgan has been ensnared in successive SEC/CFTC recordkeeping sweeps (2021–2025), including a **$35M SEC fine referenced in June 2025 congressional correspondence** and further Jan 2025 sweep penalties. Pattern of recurring compliance lapses.
- **Debanking / political discrimination pressure:** The **Trump administration publicly pressured JPMorgan and BofA** over alleged conservative/firearms-industry debanking; JPMorgan **agreed to amend its code of conduct** on religious/political discrimination. Federal agency enforcement structure (CFPB) has been dismantled under the current administration, shifting enforcement to **state AGs** — which continue to target Zelle and consumer fraud.
- **Whistleblower probe (Aug 2026):** Federal prosecutors probed whistleblower claims alleging **JPMorgan improperly denied >$100M in reimbursements to scam-victim customers**; a former executive is the complainant.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Key-person / succession concentration (HIGH):** ~20-year CEO/Chairman tenure with both named internal successors departed; transition risk for the most systemically important U.S. bank.
2. **AML / financial-crimes control gaps (HIGH):** Record €45M BaFin fine for systemic suspicious-transaction-reporting failures, layered on the Epstein legacy and a whistleblower claim that customer-fraud reimbursements were mishandled — points to residual control deficiencies in transaction monitoring and fraud operations.
3. **Cyber / third-party supply-chain exposure (MODERATE-HIGH):** JPM is the highest-value target in global financial services with a **$1.5T/10-year Security & Resiliency Initiative** and strong posture — but the **Nov 2025 SitusAMC vendor breach** underscores systemic **third-party concentration** risk, and the firm's CISO publicly warns that **SaaS supply chains are "weakening global finance."** A firm of this scale also carries acute **business-continuity/systemic-consequence** risk if a core platform fails.

  - **Rolled back DEI commitments** under political pressure; caught in **cross-partisan political crossfire** (criticized both for tolerating liberal "woke" practices and for de-banking conservatives — a rare lose-lose dynamic).
  - **Workplace-misconduct litigation:** April 2026 suit alleging **sexual assault, drugging, racial harassment** against a former executive, plus a counter-allegation case against a leveraged-finance executive (fabricated-claims/extortion dispute) — indicates pockets of toxic-culture risk in high-octane desks.
- **Social sentiment:** Mixed; the firm is simultaneously admired as a profit machine and scrutinized as a political and ESG target.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🚩 Succession/key-person concentration + Chairman/CEO duality:** Both named CEO successors (Piepszak, Lake) have departed; Dimon, at ~20 years, remains indispensable. Board should be pressed on a credible, named succession plan and on separating the Chairman and CEO roles.
2. **🚩 Recurring AML/financial-crimes control failures:** The record BaFin fine (2025) plus Epstein legacy plus whistleblower allegations of scam-victim reimbursement denial create a cluster of money-laundering/consumer-protection concerns. Monitor any DOJ/state follow-on actions and the firm's remediation and the outcome of the whistleblower probe (Aug 2026).
3. **🚩 Uniform insider selling with zero open-market buying** across CEO, CFO, GC, and CIO during a period of record-high stock prices — warrants continued Form 4 surveillance for any clustering or acceleration.
4. **🚩 Active multi-front litigation:** NY AG Zelle suit (~$1B fraud allegations), workplace misconduct suits, and debanking-related political/regulatory pressure — any adverse judgment or settlement could carry outsized political and reputational amplification given the firm's systemic stature.
