## Sector Brief — Large-Cap US Diversified Banking (JPMorgan Chase)

### 1. Sector Overview
JPM operates in **Banks – Diversified** (broad sector: Financial Services; ~1,000 global listings, but the competitive set that matters is the ~6 US/global money-centre banks). The sector is a **mature, consolidating oligopoly**: the six largest diversified banks (JPM, BAC, WFC, C, GS, MS) generate a combined **~$1.00 tn revenue and ~$157 bn net income (FY2025)**. Growth has normalised sharply — after the 2022–23 revenue-recognition shifts (which inflated statutory growth), FY2025 organic revenue was roughly **flat (+3.3% at best, negative for most)**. The cycle has turned from post-COVID boom to rate-normalisation, credit normalisation and margin compression, with scale and franchise quality — not industry growth — driving shareholder value.

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Regulatory softening:** The Basel III Endgame / GSIB capital surcharge proposals have been scaled back under the current administration, directly benefitting the largest banks (JPM most of all). JPM also won the open-banking rulemaking, forcing fintechs to pay for bank data access — an incremental competitive moat.
- **Stable-to-improving net interest income:** A measured rate-cut path kept 2025 NIMs stable, term-deposit repricing largely completed, and JPM raised its 2025 NII guidance (≈$92 bn ex-markets). Loan growth plus improving deposit costs supported NII sector-wide.
- **Capital-markets strength:** Global equities/trading volumes are robust — JPM led big-bank equities revenue growth — and M&A/ECM activity is recovering, lifting fee-based revenue with minimal credit risk.

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Credit normalisation:** Consumer card/auto charge-offs are returning to trend from pandemic lows, and commercial real estate stress is slow-burning — a drag on the loan-loss provision outlook across the group.
- **NIM/rate-cycle pressure:** Further fed cuts compress earning-asset yields and re-open deposit-beta competition; funding costs remain the single largest cost line (JPM's interest expense ≈ $98 bn in FY2025, ~35% of revenue).
- **Relentless expense growth:** Technology/AI and regulatory-compliance investment keeps expense growth sticky — JPM's operating income fell in FY2025 even as revenue rose, evidence of the cost challenge. Multiples are also stagnant (JPM's stock has traded sideways ~1 year despite record earnings).

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Strong** ✅
JPM is the sector's unambiguously strongest franchise:
- **Margin leadership:** Operating margin ~26% vs. ~19% peer median; net margin ~20% vs. ~15% — a 600bps structural advantage, driven by funding scale, the #1 market share in IB/trading/broker-dealer flows, and the largest AWM platform.
- **Growth outperformance:** Only money-centre bank with positive FY2025 organic revenue growth (+3.3%) alongside strong equities-revenue momentum.
- **Revenue-accounting comparability caveat:** absolute levels are amplified vs. GS/MS/C by convention, but the margin *gap* over BAC/WFC (same reporting treatment) is real and durable.
- **Resilience:** record ~$57 bn net income and ~$4.6 tn balance sheet give it the scale to absorb credit-cycle costs and fund the AI/tech moat that is widening the gap with regional banks.

### 6. Sector Rating: **Neutral**
Large-cap diversified banking is a **mature, pro-cyclical, oligopolistic sector** with very high barriers to entry (capital surcharges, GSIB regulation, deposit/branch networks, switching costs). Structural growth is low single-digit, revenue is now essentially flat, and returns are hostage to the rate/credit cycle. The deregulation tailwind and flight-to-scale consolidation are genuinely supportive — but they disproportionately accrue to the leader (JPM) rather than to the sector as a whole. As a *sector*, the setup is Neutral: stable, cash-generative, defensible — but with no volume growth engine and cyclical downside risk. Within that sector, JPM is the quality asset you want long: **Neutral sector, Strong name.**
