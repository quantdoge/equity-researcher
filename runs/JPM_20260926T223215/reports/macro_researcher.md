# Macroeconomic Assessment — JPMorgan Chase & Co. (JPM)
*Prepared September 26, 2026 · Money-center banking (diversified), $919B market cap, ~40–45% of revenue ex-US (Europe, Asia-Pacific, LatAm)*

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **TRANSITIONAL (risk-off tilt)**

The dominant macro event of September 2026 was the **Federal Reserve's first rate hike in ~3 years**: on Sept 16 the FOMC raised the federal funds target range 25bp to **3.75%–4.00%**, reversing what had been a steady easing glide path (effective funds rate fell from 5.13% in Sep-2024 to 3.63% by Aug-2026). The whoosh came through the long end: the 10Y Treasury surged from ~4.65% in late August to **5.18% on Sept 24** — a ~50bp repricing in four weeks — and the 2Y sits at 4.87%, implying the market prices more tightening ahead. The yield curve is **positively sloped and not inverted** (2s10s ≈ +31bp), so the classic recession signal is *absent* — this is a re-tightening into solid growth, not a brick wall.

Underlying fundamentals remain OK: real GDP +2.1% y/y (Q2-26), unemployment 4.1%, and headline CPI +3.05% with core +2.45% — **sticky above the 2% target**, which is exactly what forced the Fed's hand. The July-2026 SLOOS still showed large banks *easing* CRE standards with stable credit demand, i.e. credit conditions were improving into the meeting. The broad dollar has softened ~1% from its July peak (119.5 vs ~121), a modest tailwind for non-USD revenues.

**Net read:** growth positive, labor full, curve steep and un-inverted, policy rate at a level historically accretive for bank net interest income — but the policy whiplash and 10Y breakout introduce real two-sided risk. Markets are in a **risk-off pause**, not a risk-on chase.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates & monetary policy — POSITIVE (NIM) / NEGATIVE (duration & consumer).** JPM is *the* most rate-sensitive business model in the market. A 3.75–4.00% fed funds rate with a steep, un-inverted curve is historically the sweet spot for a money-center bank: JPM's enormous, low-cost, sticky consumer deposit franchise (deposit beta lags loan yields) means every incremental 25bp flows into net interest income at scale — material billions per annum across the net interest margin. The offset: the 10Y at 5.18% raises duration pressure on the HTM/AFS bond portfolios and corporate-credit marks, compresses mortgage origination/refi volumes (Chase Home Lending), and stresses leveraged CRE borrowers in the CIB loan book. The newly embedded hiking bias (2Y at 4.87%) is a headwind to consumer repayment capacity.

**Growth & inflation — POSITIVE.** ~2% trend growth with 4.1% unemployment keeps default rates low — the critical support for the #1 US credit-card issuer's charge-off trajectory (card NCOs are rising from a low base but nowhere near crisis). 3% headline inflation gives pricing power across card APRs and merchant fees while wages (~4%) still outrun it modestly; a delinquency spike is the single biggest domestic tail risk and it is *not* currently visible in the data.

**Credit conditions — POSITIVE.** July-2026 SLOOS: large banks eased commercial real estate standards, demand stable; household standards mixed and *residential demand weaker*. Loan growth is open, even as the September hike threatens to re-tighten. JPM's trading/liquidity franchise further benefits from the Sept volatility spike — markets and securities services revenue is directly prop-weighted by curve repricing and FX moves.

**Currency & trade — POSITIVE (mild).** A DXY/broad-dollar index easing ~1% off its July high, at ~119.5, is a modest tailwind for the 40–45% of revenue generated ex-US and inflates FX-volatility-driven trading income. No meaningful commodity or tariff input exposure — this is a services P&L, so the FX channel is nearly pure revenue translation.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating — **FAVOURABLE (with elevated duration sensitivity)**

Solid trend growth, full employment, sticky-but-not-runaway inflation, a steep positive curve, an easing-then-recovering credit cycle, and a policy rate sitting at NIM-accretive levels all line up in JPM's favor. The three flags to watch: (1) the whipsaw from cutting into hiking within 12 months, (2) the 10Y breakout toward 5.2%+, and (3) whether the market's embedded-hike pricing translates into a consumer credit rollover in 2027. On today's data, the macro tape is a tailwind — call it **Favourable, conditionally**.

## 5. Material Surprise Scenario — **The "whipsaw recession" (BEAR)**

**Setup:** The Sept-2026 hike proves to be the first of *several*: core inflation refuses to disinflate below ~2.5% as tariff- and wage-driven goods costs cascade into services, and the Fed hikes another 50–75bp through H1-2027. **Trigger:** with the front end pricing 4.9% and the 10Y at 5.2%+, the 2s10s spread inverts and the long end breaks above 5.5% — the yield curve flips from JPM's friend to its enemy. **Transmission:** mortgage/refi volume collapses, M&A and DCM issuance freeze just as JPM's league-table-leading IB books are geared up for a 2027 reopening; the consumer — carrying record post-pandemic card and auto balances at 21%+ APRs — rolls over; card and HELOC net charge-offs double; loss provisions jump $2–3B/quarter; and AUM marks compress. **Market outcome:** JPM de-rates toward single-digit P/E despite being a relative safe haven — a classic "good bank, bad tape" repricing. **The tell to monitor:** the 2s10s curve, card delinquency data, and whether the Fed's hawkishness outlives CPI prints. *(Bull mirror: the Sept hike is a one-and-done insurance move, disinflation resumes in 2027, the curve steepens on a falling front end, and JPM compounds record NIM + IB + markets revenue — but the bear has the higher beta per unit of surprise.)*
