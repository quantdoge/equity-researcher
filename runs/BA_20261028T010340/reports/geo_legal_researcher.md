### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — China order/delivery normalisation stalls or reverses (Probability: High; Impact: High)**
- China's Commerce Ministry confirmed (20 May 2026) that Chinese airlines would purchase **200 Boeing aircraft** plus engines and spares; USTR Greer said in Sept 2026 that China was "making progress" finalizing it ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/china-making-progress-finalizing-boeing-purchases-announced-may-2026-09-21/); [NYT](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/05/20/business/china-boeing-planes-summit.html)).
- But the commitment is **political, not a signed order** — CEO Ortberg has publicly tempered expectations ("We didn't get..." the order), and reports indicate the package is at risk over an **engine/spares standoff** ([Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/boeing-china-200-jet-order-engine-parts-standoff/); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/boeing-ceo-tempers-china-order-163009343.html)).
- Precedent for hard interruption: Beijing ordered airlines to **halt Boeing deliveries in April 2025** in retaliation for US tariffs ([Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2025/apr/15/china-orders-airlines-to-halt-boeing-jet-deliveries-us-tariffs)).
- Transmission: revenue timing, 737/787 rate planning, and working-capital/advance-payment flows. A 200-aircraft order at ~$50–60B list is a multi-year cash catalyst now hostage to a summit-level relationship.

**Risk 2 — Tariff and export-control whipsaw on a cross-border supply chain (Probability: High; Impact: Medium–High)**
- Section 232 steel/aluminium/copper duties have been repeatedly redefined (full customs value from 6 Apr 2026; further amendments June 2026; de minimis metal-content carve-outs), creating recurring cost and compliance reclassification work ([Amundsen Davis](https://www.amundsendavislaw.com/alert-section-232-tariff-cuts-what-lower-steel-aluminum-and-copper-duties-mean-for-u-s-businesses); [EY](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2026-0788-us-presidential-proclamation-modifies-section-232-tariffs-on-steel-aluminum-copper-and-their-derivative-products)).
- **China's rare-earth/magnet export controls** are biting US aerospace specifically: Reuters reported (Feb 2026) that shortages worsened *despite* the trade truce, with yttrium flows to the US collapsing (17 tons vs 333 tons prior) ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/rare-earth-shortages-worsen-us-aerospace-chips-despite-trade-truce-sources-say-2026-02-26/)). Boeing also sits inside China's Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law / blocking-statute perimeter, which can penalise compliance with US measures.
- The **US–EU large civil aircraft dispute** ($11.5B of suspended retaliatory tariffs) reached a reimposition deadline in June 2026 with no permanent zero-for-zero settlement confirmed — a live re-tariffing risk on Boeing exports to its #2 region ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/us-eu-near-deadline-115-billion-tariffs-long-running-aircraft-dispute-2026-06-11/)).

**Risk 3 — FAA/regulatory execution as the binding constraint on the turnaround (Probability: Medium-High; Impact: High)**
- The FAA lifted the 737 MAX production cap from 38 to **42/month** (Oct 2025) and certified the **737-7** (3 Aug 2026) and **737-10** (20 Oct 2026) ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/federal-aviation-administration-certifies-boeing-737-max-7-2026-08-03/); [Boeing IR](https://investors.boeing.com/investors/news/press-release-details/2026/U-S--FAA-certifies-Boeing-737-10-largest-member-of-737-MAX-family/default.aspx)).
- Yet Q3 2026 showed the ramp slipping: shares fell ~9–11% on **lower production rates and missed delivery targets**, with management explicitly "deliberate" about the ramp ([Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/boeing-shares-plunge-on-slow-737-max-production-ramp-plan-12196780); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/10/27/stock-market-today-oct-27-boeing-plummets-11-on-lower-production-rates-and-missed-delivery-targets/)). Backlog is a record $745B — value realisation, not demand, is the constraint.
- Program-level regulatory drag: **777X** first delivery pushed to early 2027 after a **$4.9B reach-forward charge** and a seven-year delay ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/boeing-takes-near-5-billion-hit-777x-program-2025-10-29/)); total setback cost estimated near $4.9–5.4B incl. 767 tanker issues. Any new safety event restores FAA/Foreign-authority rate caps and re-grounding risk with near-zero lead time.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Criminal / enforcement**
- **DOJ non-prosecution agreement (29 May 2025), two-year term**, resolving the 737 MAX fraud-conspiracy charge; includes an **independent compliance consultant** and ~$1.14B in additional penalties (fine $243.6M; victim/crash-victim fund $455M) ([DOJ case page](https://www.justice.gov/criminal-fraud/case/united-states-v-boeing-company); [DOT OIG](https://www.oig.dot.gov/library-item/47011); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/insider/2025/11/19/boeing-dpa-drama-comes-to-a-close-at-least-for-now/)). **This is the single most important legal date on the calendar: the NPA lapses roughly mid-2027** — a breach or new finding of non-compliance would expose Boeing to criminal prosecution as a recidivist.
- No active FCPA/bribery matter identified in this review; note that Boeing's FMS/broker-driven defense sales channel remains the structural FCPA/sanctions-adjacent exposure.

**Competition / antitrust**
- **FTC consent order (Spirit AeroSystems acquisition)** requires Boeing to **divest significant Spirit assets** to preserve competition ([FTC docket 241-0098](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/241-0098-boeing-co-spirit-aerosystems-holdings)). Integration is both a supply-chain fix and a live divestiture-compliance obligation.

**Civil litigation**
- **737 MAX 9 door-plug personal-injury litigation**: ~47 plaintiffs in King County, WA; an Oct 2026 ruling allows a jury to decide *where key design decisions were made*, opening the door to **punitive damages** ([Axios Seattle](https://www.axios.com/local/seattle/2026/10/14/boeing-737-max-door-plug-lawsuit-king-county-judge-alaska-airlines)). This is the largest open civil liability vector.
- **Shareholder/derivative suits largely dismissed**: Delaware judge tossed the investor suit over the 2024 door-plug blowout alleging directors ignored safety ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/boeing-directors-avoid-investor-suit-saying-they-ignored-safety)). Net favourable, though appeals and ERISA/401(k) participant class actions persist.
- **Spirit AeroSystems securities class action** settled for ~$29.2M (pre-acquisition period; now inherited/legacy exposure) ([settlement site](https://www.spiritaerosecuritiessettlement.com/)).

**Regulatory regime**
- Regulators: FAA (certification/production certificates), DoD/DSCA (FMS, ITAR/EAR), NASA (SLS/Starliner), SEC, FTC, EU EASA, CAAC (China), plus Treasury/OFAC sanctions screening of airline customers and lessors.
- 737-7/-10 certification removes the largest overhang; the constraint has shifted from *certification* to *rate and quality*, which is now the principal regulatory variable.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale.** Boeing is not a high-geopolitical-risk business by revenue mix — 53.7% US, and its non-US exposure is concentrated in allied, rule-of-law jurisdictions (Europe, Japan, Canada, Middle East partners). That diversification argues against "Very High."

It earns a **High** rating on four specific, idiosyncratic grounds:
1. **A live criminal NPA with an embedded compliance monitor** — a legal tail that no peer carries, with a discrete 2027 cliff.
2. **China optionality is binary and politically priced**: the 200-jet commitment is the largest single swing factor in the equity story, entirely unfinalized, and has already been weaponised once (April 2025 delivery halt).
3. **Regulatory capacity, not demand, caps the model** — the FAA relationship converts any safety event into immediate rate and cash-flow impairment, and the Q3 2026 ramp miss shows execution is still fragile.
4. **Tariff/export-control volatility on both inputs (metals, rare earths) and outputs (EU aircraft dispute)**, in a company with no pricing power to pass through near-term costs on a $745B fixed-price backlog.

**What would move the rating down:** a signed, financed China order; clean NPA expiry without extension; a sustained 42+ per month 737 rate with no quality escapes; permanent US–EU zero-for-zero aircraft tariff settlement.
**What would move it to Very High:** any new FAA rate cap or grounding, a DOJ NPA breach, or a formal Chinese halt to deliveries/rare-earth embargo on aerospace.
