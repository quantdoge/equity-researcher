## Boeing (BA) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $168.18 | Market cap $132.9B | EV ~$153B | Sector: Industrials / Aerospace & Defense*

---

### 1. Growth Story

Boeing is not a disruptive innovator — it is a **duopoly incumbent in violent rehabilitation**, and the growth case rests on capacity restoration rather than new-market creation. After cumulative net losses of ~$35B across 2020–2024, the company has crossed the inflection: FY2025 revenue rebounded +34.5% to $89.5B, FY2026 revenue is tracking to ~$97B, and free cash flow turned positive in Q3 2026 (+$464M) for the first time in years. The forward thesis is a **$2B → $10B FCF ramp through 2029** funded by 737 rate restoration (47/month → 52/month in H2 2027), the 737-7/737-10 certifications now in hand, and a record $745B backlog. The stock has de-rated 34% from its January 2026 high of $254 to $168 — the market is pricing execution failure, not the recovery.

### 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration Opportunity

Boeing's own 2026 Commercial Market Outlook calls for **43,625 new aircraft through 2045 (~$3.2T)**, matched by Airbus's ~42,060. This is a genuine two-player TAM with 20-year visibility, and Boeing's **$745B backlog covers 7.7x current revenue** — the demand is already contracted, not hypothetical. Defense adds a record **$101B backlog** after the F/A-XX sixth-generation fighter win, with defense revenue +15% to $7.9B in Q3.

**The penetration gap is the whole investment case.** Boeing delivered 161 aircraft through Q3 2026 — highest since 2018 — versus Airbus running at ~418 through July against an 870 target. Boeing is capturing roughly **20–24% of duopoly deliveries versus a historical ~45%**. Every point of share recaptured is pure incremental margin on a fixed cost base. That is the TAM-capture vector: not new markets, but reclaiming half of a $3.2T duopoly that it currently under-serves due to self-inflicted production constraints.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4/FY2026 results — Jan 26, 2027.** Consensus is +$0.06 EPS: the first positive quarterly print in years and the first annual FCF guide for 2027 (~$3B) to be tested. Highest-impact near-term event.
- **737 MAX 10 first deliveries — early 2027** (FAA certification achieved Oct 2026; MAX 7 certified July 2026). Triggers the 737-10 revenue block and clears the largest certification overhang.
- **777X first delivery — 2027** (7 years late; $4.9B pre-tax charge already taken in Q3 2026, moving the program timeline). A clean delivery de-risks the last big development program on the books.
- **737 rate step-up to 52/month — H2 2027**, from stabilization at 47/month. This is the mechanical driver of the 2028–29 FCF hockey stick.
- **Credit/deleveraging**: gross debt cut $8.3B to ~$45.9B in 1H26; Fitch moved outlook to Positive at BBB- (June 2026). An upgrade would compress the ~$2.4B annual interest burden — a direct EPS lever.
- **Capital return reinstatement** (dividend/buyback) once FCF is durable — sentiment catalyst, likely 2028+.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (speculative, execution-contingent)

This clears the bar on forward growth, not on innovation quality. Over four years the model goes from **negative FCF to $10B**, EPS from ~$0 to $14.56 (+60% CAGR off a zero base), with 11% revenue CAGR and a contracted $745B backlog. At 14.6x FY29E EPS and 15.3x FY29E FCF, you are paying roughly **nothing for the recovery** beyond the base case, while consensus targets sit 58% above spot.

What keeps this from a Strong Buy under a Cathie Wood lens: there is **no proprietary technology moat creating new markets** — the moat is a regulatory duopoly and a 20-year backlog, which is durable but low-ceiling. Management has explicitly guided to **low margins until 2030**. And the FCF target has already slipped a year (2028 → 2029), which is precisely the pattern that has burned Boeing investors for six years running. Position accordingly: a **Buy with reduced sizing and a hard catalyst calendar** (Jan 26, 2027 is the referendum), not a core compounder.

### 7. Key Risks

- **Balance-sheet fragility is the dominant risk.** $7.16/share of book value against $168 of stock means a single large charge (see: $4.9B for 777X) can render equity structurally insolvent again and force dilution. Shares are already up 37% since 2020.
- **Execution/guidance credibility.** The 40% beat rate and repeated target cuts reflect a company that chronically misses its own schedule. The 777X is seven years late; the 52/month rate has been pushed into H2 2027.
- **Fixed-price defense and development contracts** (KC-46, VC-25B, Starliner) have generated multi-billion cumulative charges and remain a recurring earnings hole.
- **Supplier and labor fragility.** Post-Spirit AeroSystems integration, a four-year engineering/technical labor deal just signed — but a single supplier or strike disruption halts the entire rate ramp.
- **Competitive share loss.** Airbus delivered roughly 4x Boeing's pace this year; every year of under-delivery lets Airbus lock in slots and customers on the A320/A350 families permanently.
- **$2.4B annual interest expense** consumes ~10% of revenue-driven cash generation and caps EPS recovery until deleveraging completes.
