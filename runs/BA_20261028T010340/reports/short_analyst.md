## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **LOW** (not fraud — but deeply misleading optics)

The headline number is an illusion. FY2025 reported net income of **+$2.235B** looks like Boeing's return to profitability. It is not.

**Decomposition of FY2025:**
| Item | FY2025 |
|---|---|
| Revenue | $89.46B |
| Gross profit | $4.29B (4.8%) |
| **Sum of quarterly operating income** | **−$5.42B** |
| Q4'25 net income | **+$8.22B** |
| Q4'25 operating income | **−$815M** |

Q4 2025 earnings "reflect a **$9.6 billion gain on sale**" (Boeing Q4'25 release). That single non-operating, non-recurring disposal gain is the entire FY2025 profit and then some. Strip it out and Boeing lost ~$7.4B on a pre-tax basis in 2025. Note also the **data inconsistency**: the annual income statement shows operating income of +$4,281M while the four quarters sum to −$5,416M. The annual line is contaminated (it almost equals gross profit, implying opex was dropped). **I trust the quarterly build; the annual operating-income line is not usable.**

Also: income statement net income $2,235M vs. cash-flow-statement net income $1,890M — a $345M mismatch.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**A. Interest expense exceeds operating profit — the fatal arithmetic.**
- 9M 2026: operating income **$665M** vs. interest expense **$1,789M** → **interest coverage 0.37x**

Total debt $54.4B. This is a company funding its P&L with borrowed money.

**B. Balance sheet in technical distress.**
- Total equity **$5.46B** against **$168.2B** of assets — leverage of ~31:1 on book
- Liabilities = **96.8% of assets**
- **Altman Z = 0.947** (distress zone). Adjusted for market-cap equity (x4 = 0.817 vs. book 0.0335), Z ≈ **1.28** — still firmly in distress (<1.81)
- Price/book **24.4x** — for a company with $5.5B of equity
- Retained earnings fell from $38.6B (2020) to $17.3B — **$21B of accumulated value destruction**

**C. Serial dilution is the business model.**
Shares: 568.6M (2020) → 647M (2024) → **762M weighted (2025)** → 790M outstanding. **+39% in six years.** Equity swung from −$3.9B (2024) to +$5.5B (2025) not from earnings (retained earnings rose only $1.9B) but from a **~$9–10B equity issuance** (+18% share count in one year). Piotroski flags this: F7 (no dilution) = **0**. Every "recovery" is funded by shareholders.

**D. Revenue growth has collapsed — the ramp has stalled.**
YoY revenue growth, quarterly: **+57.1% → +30.4% → +34.9% → +17.7% → +14.0% → +8.0% → +3.9%**
Sequential revenue: $22.2B → $24.6B → **$24.2B**. Flat for two quarters despite 737 at 38→42/month and 787 ramping. Tool signal: **DECELERATING**.

**E. Mix and pricing deterioration (hard evidence).**
Revenue per delivery: Q1'26 **$155M** (143 jets) → Q3'26 **$150M** (161 jets). Boeing is delivering *more* aircraft for *less* revenue — narrowbody mix shift. This is pricing power loss disguised as volume growth.

**F. Gross margin has peaked and is rolling over.**
Quarterly GM: Q1'26 **11.5%** → Q2'26 **9.8%** → Q3'26 **9.0%** — ~120bp compression per quarter, two quarters running. FY history: −9.8% (2020), 4.9%, 5.3%, 9.9% (2023), −3.0% (2024), 4.8% (2025). **Boeing has not sustained a double-digit gross margin since 2018.** Airbus runs materially higher.

**G. Serial "one-time" charges — the most underrated red flag.**
Q3 2025: operating loss **−$4.79B**; gross profit **−$2.38B**. FY2020 −$12.8B, FY2021 −$2.9B, FY2022 −$3.5B, FY2024 −$10.8B. Boeing books a multi-billion "one-off" almost every year. One-offs that recur annually are a **cost of doing business**, not noise, and consensus models them as if they will vanish.

**H. Inventory is a $84.7B black box.**
Inventory = **94.7% of annual revenue**, **363 days of COGS** (improved from 466 in 2024). This is pre-built aircraft, long-term contract costs, and deferred production costs. It is the single largest asset and the single largest write-down risk. Improve-*ment* here is real — but the absolute exposure is enormous relative to $5.5B of equity: a **6.5% inventory write-down wipes out book equity entirely.**

**I. Goodwill + intangibles jumped $8.8B in 2025** ($10.0B → $18.8B) — acquisition-related. Goodwill now **3.5x book equity**. Any impairment is an equity event.

**J. Channel-stuffing check: NEGATIVE (a genuine positive).** Receivables grew +7.8% vs. revenue +34.5% in 2025; AR days fell from 61.5 to **49.3**. No revenue-recognition abuse detected here. FY2024's flag (revenue −14.5%, AR +1.2%) reflected uncollected billings during the strike, now resolved.

- **TATA of 0.007 is an artifact of scale.** In dollar terms, (NI − OCF) = 2,235 − 1,065 = **$1.17B**. **Accruals ratio: 0.007 (annual series) — meaningless noise.** Both numerator and denominator are near zero; a company with $1B of net income and $1B of operating cash flow on $168B of assets cannot produce a diagnostic accruals ratio either way. **Do not read the low accruals ratio as a clean bill of health.**

Similarly, the **Piotroski F-Score of 7 flatters**: F1 (positive ROA) and F3 (ROA improvement) are both driven by the $9.6B disposal gain, while F4 (accruals) = 0 and F7 (no dilution) = 0. **The F-Score is contaminated by the one-time item.**

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

- **Duopoly intact, but Boeing is the junior partner.** Airbus leads on narrowbody scale by a wide margin (A320neo family build rate well above Boeing's 38→42/month). Boeing ceded the scale war permanently with the MAX crisis.
- **The 777X is the hidden liability.** Repeated certification delays push billions of deferred production cost forward, sitting inside that $84.7B inventory. Each slip adds to the eventual catch-up charge.
- **Defense/space is value-destructive.** Fixed-price development contracts (KC-46, VC-25B, T-7A, MQ-25) have generated cumulative multi-billion losses. Boeing's defense segment is a serial loss-maker, not a ballast.
- **Certification/regulatory is now a structural cost.** FAA production caps (38, now 42/month) mean Boeing's output — and therefore its revenue — is set by its regulator, not its order book. This is a permanent margin of safety *removal*.
- **COMAC C919** is a 5–10 year threat to the narrowbody franchise, particularly in China.
- **The genuine moat:** a record backlog and a two-supplier global market with 10-year lead times. This is why Boeing does not go to zero. It is also why the stock is not a zero.
