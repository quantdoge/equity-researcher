## 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (improving, but scarred)

**Positives**
- **CEO/Chair separation maintained:** Steven Mollenkopf is Independent Board Chair (since 2024); Kelly Ortberg is President/CEO and a director. Power is not concentrated in one person — a material improvement over the Calhoun era.
- **Board refresh:** Substantial turnover post-door-plug; directors show genuine skin-in-the-game via open-market buys — **Mortimer Buckley $500K at $224 (Mar-26)** and **Bradley Tilden $299K at $218.50 (May-26)**. Directors buying into a drawdown is a credible alignment signal, not option noise.
- **Insider ownership rising, not falling:** virtually all 2026 Form 4 "sales" are tax withholding on vesting (Ortberg 6,233 sh @ $234.09; CFO Malave 4,205 sh @ $228.65; BDS chief Parker 630 sh), plus routine director equity awards and one gift (Raymond).

**Concerns**
- The same board (largely pre-2024) presided over two fatal 737 MAX crashes, the door-plug event, and a DOJ finding that Boeing breached its 2021 deferred prosecution agreement. Oversight credibility remains under external supervision rather than self-corrected.
- Broad senior-management churn (CEO, CFO, BCA and BDS leadership all replaced since 2024) raises institutional-memory and execution-continuity questions, even if each individual change was defensible.
- Compensation/governance reforms were largely **forced by regulators and activist holders**, not volunteered — a persistent pattern of reactive rather than proactive governance.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: HIGH (structural, slowly de-risking)

**Criminal / DOJ**
- 737 MAX fraud conspiracy charge: after DOJ found Boeing **breached its 2021 DPA**, the parties entered a **non-prosecution agreement on 29-May-2025 (two-year term, i.e. to ~May-2027)** with a ~$243.6M fine and victim-payment obligations. The criminal case was dismissed.
- **No independent compliance monitor was imposed** — a decision publicly criticised as lenient ("cash for immunity"). Consequence: compliance durability is essentially self-certified. Any new violation before May-2027 reopens prosecution exposure.

**Aviation safety (FAA)**
- FAA production cap of 38/month has been lifted; Boeing is at **42/month with a target of 47** — but management confirmed on **16-Sep-26** the ramp is taking "longer than expected," and reiterated a deliberately slow ramp on the **27-Oct-26** Q3 call (triggering the −11% day).
- **$3.1M FAA civil penalty paid (Sept-2026)** following findings of widespread safety/quality system violations — including an allegation that a **Boeing employee pressured a co-worker acting on behalf of the FAA to sign off on a 737 MAX**. This is the single most serious culture/integrity datapoint in the file.
- Positive de-risking: **737-7 ATC in Q3-26 and 737-10 ATC in Oct-26** remove a multi-year certification overhang and unlock deferred production cost recovery.
- **777X certification is slipping into 2027** (engine issue plus Renton wing-shop hiccups) — a seventh year of delay on a program with large deferred costs.

**Litigation**
- A federal court **granted in part class certification in a shareholder securities action (16-Mar-26)** alleging concealment of 737 MAX safety deficiencies; ongoing. Separate wrongful-death and product-liability actions from the crashes remain live, and victims' families actively opposed the NPA.
- No SEC enforcement action surfaced in the indexed search; no EU/UK antitrust or data-privacy action of note identified despite the new "Dataliner" AI/data platform (a forward monitoring item, not a current exposure).

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Production-rate execution is the whole thesis, and it keeps slipping.** 737 ramp to 47/month has now been pushed twice (Sept and Oct 2026), starved by wing-shop bottlenecks and supplier constraints; Q3 deliveries missed targets with commercial-airplane losses widening despite record backlog. Each delay defers cash conversion on ~$745B of backlog and stretched deferred production costs.
- **Spirit AeroSystems integration and supplier quality concentration.** Boeing has reabsorbed its most troubled fuselage supplier while simultaneously being asked to raise rates — the worst possible time for integration friction. Fuselage quality is the historical root cause of both the door-plug failure and multiple whistleblower allegations; the 182,000-employee, multi-site manufacturing footprint remains susceptible to parts-handling and process-control noncompliance (the exact findings of the 2024 and 2026 FAA audits).
- **Fixed-price defense development programs plus a new one.** The KC-46 took a fresh **$565M charge in Q4-25**, and the USAF is **withholding a ~75-aircraft (~$7.5B) award** pending fixes to mission capability (24%), fuselage cracks and boom issues. T-7, VC-25B and Starliner carry similar fixed-price/development risk, and the new **F/A-XX** win adds another fixed-price-like development obligation. Key-person risk is material: the turnaround narrative rests almost entirely on Ortberg (CEO since Aug-2024) and a recently installed CFO.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: HIGH

- **Culture narrative is entrenched.** Whistleblower testimony (production shortcuts, retaliation, pressure on FAA designees to sign off), congressional scrutiny, and the "regulatory capture" framing have made Boeing a shorthand for broken safety culture. The Sept-2026 FAA penalty — involving alleged coercion of an FAA-designated signatory — directly reinforces that narrative and is far more damaging reputationally than the $3.1M size would suggest.
- **The DOJ resolution is a reputational liability, not an asset.** Avoidance of an independent monitor, following a finding that Boeing breached its prior agreement, has been widely characterised as accountability-lite, keeping the 346-victim story in the news cycle and fuelling family/lawmaker activism.
- **Customer/consumer backlash risk is real but currently masked by supply scarcity.** Airlines cannot punish Boeing by walking away — Airbus is sold out and the backlog is 6,300+ aircraft — so reputational damage is presently expressed as delivery-slot skepticism and discounted future orders rather than cancellations. That cushion disappears the moment Airbus capacity loosens.
- **Offsetting positives:** 737-10 and 737-7 certification, the Navy F/A-XX selection, a record $745B backlog, and positive free cash flow ($0.5B) in Q3-26 all support the recovery story and blunt the "uninvestable" thesis.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

This is a company whose financial risk is well understood and whose *non-financial* risk is the dominant variable. Governance is adequate and improving, insider alignment is mildly positive, and certification/DOJ overhangs have partially cleared — but the operating system that produced two crashes, a door-plug blowout and a breached DPA has not yet been demonstrably rebuilt. Repeated production-ramp slippage against the company's own targets, a compliance function still without independent verification, and an unproven single-executive turnaround make this High rather than Moderate. It is not Critical only because the NPA is in force, the FAA has permitted rate increases, and the backlog provides years of demand cover.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Alleged employee coercion of an FAA designee to sign off on a 737 MAX (FAA penalty, Sept-2026).** This is a potential fraud/document-integrity indicator, not a routine quality lapse. If pattern evidence exists, criminal exposure reopens under the NPA. **Escalate to compliance/fraud team and demand disclosure detail in the next 10-Q.**
2. **Production guidance credibility breakdown.** Two consecutive downward revisions to the 47/month target, with delivery misses now driving a −11% single-day move on 10x volume. Management's forecasting reliability is itself a governance issue.
3. **DOJ NPA expires ~May-2027 with no independent monitor.** Any new safety or documentation violation before expiry — or adverse findings in the securities class action — could trigger prosecution and a materially different outcome.
4. **777X slipping into 2027** — additional multi-billion-dollar forward-loss/deferred-cost risk with no certification date to anchor valuation.
5. **Defense fixed-price tail risk broadening** — KC-46 charge recurrence, USAF order withholding, and the new F/A-XX award all add to the loss-making development backlog.
6. **Absence of any insider open-market purchase after the 27-Oct-26 collapse.** If directors and officers do not buy into a 33% drawdown within the next window, the March–May buying should be re-read as routine, not conviction.
