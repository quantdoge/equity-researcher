# Financial Risk Assessment — The Boeing Company (BA)

**Data as of:** FY2025 10-K (filed 2026-01-30) and price data through 2026-02-20 (last close $232.03). Market cap ≈ $183bn (762.3m diluted shares × $232.03). *Note: the profile feed's "price" of $168.18 and $132.9bn cap are stale — I use the current price.*

---

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating band: Investment Grade, bottom tier (BBB− / Baa3) — "crossover" credit with high-yield characteristics.**

Boeing retains the lowest investment-grade rung, but the underlying metrics are firmly high-yield. Equity is a rounding error against the balance sheet: $5.46bn of book equity against $168.2bn of assets (**equity/assets 3.0%**) and $54.4bn of debt (**D/E 9.97×**). The company survived 2024 only through equity issuance and a large cash buffer, not through cash generation.

The FY2025 inflection is real and material: revenue +34.5% to $89.5bn, first net profit since 2018 ($2.24bn), operating cash flow positive at $1.07bn after −$12.08bn in FY2024, and gross debt reduced (short-term debt of $8.35bn vs $1.19bn prior year reflects 2026 maturities being pulled forward and retired with cash, not refinanced). Per Fitch (June 2026 action) Boeing cut gross debt $8.3bn in 1H26 to ~$45.9bn using cash on hand, and the outlook was revised to **Positive** while affirming BBB−. That is genuine de-leveraging — but it is being funded from a cash pile built by selling stock, and net debt/EBITDA at **3.40×** is not yet consistent with a mid-BBB profile.

The rating therefore rests on franchise value and liquidity, **not** on coverage. That is the definition of a crossover credit: one more MAX grounding, a labor action, or a program charge and the rating goes high-yield — and at ~$45bn of gross debt, a fallen-angel event is a multi-billion-dollar refinancing cost problem.

Each 100bp on $45bn ≈ $450m of pre-tax income, or ~0.6× of FY2025 operating income.

## 4. Downside Scenario: −20% Revenue

**I use FY2024 as the empirical elasticity test, because Boeing has already run this experiment.** FY2024 revenue fell **14.5%** ($77.8bn → $66.5bn); operating income went from −$0.82bn to **−$12.08bn** — a **~$11.3bn swing on a $11.3bn revenue decline, i.e. ~1.0× flow-through.** Boeing's operating leverage is essentially 1:1 on the decrement. That is unusually brutal and is the single most important fact about this company's risk profile.

**Viability arithmetic:** opening cash of $29.4bn against $14–15bn of annual burn gives roughly **two years of runway** — *if* the entire cash balance were available and nothing were funded. In practice: (a) ~$10bn is the operating minimum Boeing has publicly targeted as its floor; (b) $8.35bn of debt matures inside twelve months and must be repaid or refinanced; (c) the commercial airplane order book, while enormous, converts slowly and capex cannot go to zero. Realistically the burn is absorbable for **4–6 quarters**, after which Boeing is back to the 2024 playbook: a dilutive equity raise, a draw on the revolver, and a downgrade to high yield.

**Rating outcome in the downside:** two-notch downgrade to BB / Ba2 is the base case, with the BBB− floor breached. Refinancing $45bn at high-yield spreads (~300–400bp wider) adds roughly $1.4–1.8bn of annual interest — which makes this a **reflexive** downside: the rating cut causes the interest burden that causes the next rating cut. That is the defining credit risk here.

**Mitigants that keep this from being a distress call:** a $500bn+ backlog that is contractual, no dividend of consequence ($331m), a market cap of $183bn that provides ample equity-raise capacity (they raised ~$20bn in 2024 without difficulty), and the US government's implicit strategic interest in Boeing's survival — a factor no credit model captures and the strongest single support to the rating.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

The direction of travel is unambiguously good — first profit since 2018, positive operating cash flow, debt down $8bn, F-score 7, gross margin positive again, Fitch outlook positive. But the *level* of financial risk remains high on every absolute measure: leverage at 9.97× book equity, coverage at 1.54×, negative tangible equity of −$13.4bn, negative FCF, and a sovereign-scale inventory position funding the whole thing.

**Why not "Moderate":** the balance sheet has no cushion for a second consecutive shock. Equity of $5.5bn is 3% of assets; the entire common equity cushion could be erased by roughly one quarter of FY2024-style losses. **Why not "Critical":** $29.4bn of cash, a facility-backed liquidity position, an investment-grade rating, an accessible equity market, and a backlog that provides multi-year revenue visibility. Boeing is not a solvency story today; it is a *thin-equity, high-operating-leverage cyclical* that is one shock away from needing shareholders again.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Negative tangible book equity: −$13.4bn** (goodwill/intangibles $18.8bn vs equity $5.5bn). Goodwill also jumped from $10.0bn to $18.8bn in FY2025 (+$8.8bn) — likely an acquisition amid a repair program. **Impairment risk on a business already carrying negative tangible equity** is a live, underappreciated risk.
2. **Coverage of 1.54× on operating income / 1.95× on EBIT.** Any normalization of margins or a 100bp move in refinancing costs threatens it. Interest expense ($2.77bn) now exceeds net income ($2.24bn) — **the bondholders earn more than the shareholders.**
3. **Operating leverage ~1:1 on revenue decrements.** Empirically demonstrated in FY2024 (−$11.3bn op income on −$11.3bn revenue). This is the mechanism that converts a moderate revenue miss into a liquidity event.
4. **FCF negative in 3 of 4 years; cumulative −$9.55bn FY2022–FY2025.** The company has not demonstrated it can fund itself; the FY2025 improvement to −$1.88bn is progress toward a zero that has not yet been reached.
5. **Dilution: shares +28% since FY2022** (595m → 762m), and the F-score correctly penalizes this (no-dilution test = 0). Shareholders have funded the balance sheet repair. Another shock means another raise.
6. **Quick ratio 0.40×, inventory 50% of total assets ($84.7bn).** This is inventory of unfinished aircraft in a regulated, certificate-gated production system. It is not liquid, and its value depends on FAA approval velocity — a political variable, not a financial one.
7. **Accruals quality flag (F-score component = 0):** net income $1.89bn vs operating cash flow $1.07bn. Reported profit is running ahead of cash.
8. **Z-score 2.03 (market-cap adj.) — grey zone with negative tangible equity.** Grey zone plus negative tangible book plus 1.5× coverage is not a comfortable conjunction.
9. **Concentration in a single program and a single regulator.** 737 MAX production caps, labor agreements, and China delivery timing are all binary risk factors outside management control.

**Bottom line for the investment memo:** Boeing is a *recovery with a fragile balance sheet*, not a fortress. The equity is a leveraged call option on 737/787 production normalization — 1.1 beta, 36% vol, an already-realized 48% drawdown, negative tangible equity, and 1.5× interest coverage. It works if the recovery continues; a 20% revenue decline produces a ~$14bn cash burn, negative EBITDA, a two-notch downgrade, and almost certainly another dilutive equity raise within 18 months. Position size accordingly.
