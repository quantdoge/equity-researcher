# Macroeconomic Assessment — The Boeing Company (BA)

**As of:** 28 October 2026 | **Price:** $168.18 | **Mkt cap:** $132.9bn | **Sector:** Industrials / Aerospace & Defense

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-Off / Late-Cycle Stagflation-Lite**

The regime has changed materially from the one that framed Boeing's 2025 turnaround thesis. This is **no longer a disinflation-with-easing cycle — it is a re-acceleration cycle with a hiking Fed.**

Three facts define the current environment:

1. **Inflation has re-accelerated, driven by energy.** CPI rose from **2.65% YoY (Dec 2025) to 3.72% YoY (Sep 2026)** — with August at 3.35%, so the trend is up, not noisy. Michigan 1-year inflation expectations are **4.6% (Sep 2026)**, up from 3.4% in February and far from anchored. This is not a base-effect artefact; it is an oil shock.
2. **The Fed has pivoted to tightening.** After holding 3.64% through H1 2026 and easing to ~3.63%, the Fed **hiked 25bp to a 3.75%–4.00% target range** (effective rate 3.75% in Sep). The October meeting is expected to be a skip, with **near-unanimous economist consensus for another hike in December** (Reuters, 20 Oct 2026).
3. **The long end has dislocated from the policy rate.** The 10Y sits at **5.00%** (peaked at 5.31% on 5 Oct) and the 30Y at **5.37%** — the 10Y is up **+56bp since 30 June (4.44%)** *while the Fed was tightening only at the short end*. The 2s10s is **+34bp, positively sloped**, but the dis-inversion is **bear steepening** — rising term premium and inflation-credibility risk, not growth optimism. FOMC minutes (16 Sep) explicitly cite higher term premiums.

**The proximate shock is geopolitical.** WTI crude at **$107.89 (20 Oct)** is up **+55% from $69.74 on 1 July**, with October seeing the most attacks on commercial shipping near Hormuz since 2017. IATA reports jet fuel has roughly **doubled** since late February, crack spreads at a record **$80/bbl**, and the industry fuel bill jumping from **$252bn (2025) to ~$350bn (2026)** — halving global airline profits to ~$23bn and slowing air travel demand growth to **2.1%**.

Growth itself is fine: **real GDP +2.19% YoY in Q2 2026** (+2.64% in Q1), unemployment **4.2%**. This is a supply-shock-inflation economy with a solid real base — the worst combination for a capital-goods manufacturer with thin margins.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**a) Discount rate on a $745bn long-duration backlog — NEGATIVE (valuation)**
Q3 2026 backlog hit a record **$745.4bn** (+$30bn QoQ). This is one of the longest-duration cash-flow streams in the S&P 500 — aircraft orders convert to revenue over 5–15 years. A 10Y at 5.00% and 30Y at 5.37% mechanically compresses the present value of that backlog. Boeing is a **long-duration asset in a rising-term-premium world** and is being de-rated accordingly: the stock is **-30% from its 5 Aug peak of $240.19** and fell **11% on 27 Oct** on the 737 MAX ramp disappointment. This is where macro and company-specific risk compound.

**b) Refinancing / interest cost — NEUTRAL-to-modest NEGATIVE (improving sharply)**
This is the one place Boeing's macro sensitivity has *fallen*. Fitch revised Boeing's outlook to **Positive (26 June 2026)**, affirming **BBB-**, after the company **retired $8.3bn of gross debt in 1H26, cutting it to ~$45.9bn using cash on hand rather than refinancing**. Ratings remain **Baa3/BBB-, one notch above junk**. At a ~6.45% all-in Baa cost of capital, every 50bp of term-premium adds ~$230m of annualized interest on the remaining stack — material but no longer existential. Deleveraging into a rising-rate environment is the correct management decision and materially reduces this risk vector versus 2024–25.

**c) Energy shock / airline customer health — MIXED, near-term NEGATIVE**
This is the second-order channel that matters most. Airlines' fuel bill rising $100bn and profits halving to $23bn directly degrades Boeing's customer base's ability and willingness to take deliveries on schedule, to pay list pricing, and to fund pre-delivery payments. Expect **deferrals, pricing concessions, and more Boeing Capital financing support**. Offsetting this: at $108 oil, the fuel-burn advantage of the 737 MAX and 787 versus ageing fleets becomes a compelling replacement economics argument — high fuel *accelerates* the case for fleet renewal. The net: **near-term demand destruction and delivery risk, medium-term demand pull-forward.** It does not show up in the backlog, but it governs the conversion rate — precisely the metric the market punished on 27 Oct.

**d) Input cost inflation vs. fixed-price contracts — NEGATIVE (the biggest single headwind)**
With a **1.5% operating margin** (Q3 2026) and core EPS of **-$0.34**, Boeing has essentially zero cushion against cost inflation. Its book carries large fixed-price exposure — 777X, legacy 787 blocks, and fixed-price defense programs (KC-46, T-7A, VC-25B) — while inputs (labor across 182,000 employees post-machinists contract, aluminum, titanium, fasteners, energy for fabrication) inflate at 3.7%+. Inflation is therefore not a pass-through story for Boeing; it is a **direct gross-margin tax on a company that currently has no gross margin**.

**e) USD strength — NEGATIVE**
The broad dollar is **+2.8% since 11 May (118.06 → 121.32)**, supported by Fed hikes and safe-haven flows. Roughly half of Boeing's revenue is international, so translation is a headwind; more importantly, a stronger dollar **erodes price competitiveness versus Airbus** and raises the effective dollar cost of Boeing jets for foreign flag carriers — aggravating the pricing tension in (c).

**f) Trade policy / China — POSITIVE but fragile**
The May 2026 Trump–Xi summit produced a **200-aircraft Chinese commitment — Boeing's first major China deal in roughly nine years** — with progress on finalization reported as of 21 September. That is a genuine macro tailwind and a large part of why the backlog reached $745bn. But the 2025 episode (China refusing deliveries on tariff cost) shows this is a **policy variable, not a commercial one**. It can be reversed by a single executive action, and tariffs on aluminum/steel remain a structural input-cost tax.

**g) Geopolitics / defense — POSITIVE for BDS**
Renewed Middle East conflict, the Iran standoff, and the Hormuz shipping threat support elevated global defense budgets, benefiting Defense, Space & Security. This partially explains Q3's mix (defense and services offsetting commercial weakness). It is the cleanest macro tailwind in the model.

**h) Growth and demand — NEUTRAL**
2.2% GDP and 4.2% unemployment support domestic services volume, but IATA's slowing **2.1% air traffic growth** caps the order-rate acceleration the equity needs.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Boeing is simultaneously exposed to the **three worst features** of this macro configuration:

- **Rising real discount rates** (10Y 5.00%, bear steepening) against the longest-duration backlog in its sector;
- **A cost structure with no margin buffer** (1.5% op margin) facing 3.7% input inflation and fixed-price contracts;
- **A customer base whose profitability is being halved** by an energy shock, at the exact moment Boeing must accelerate deliveries to validate the turnaround.

The supporting positives — record backlog, China re-opening, positive FCF, $8.3bn debt reduction, Fitch Positive outlook, tight 1.45% credit spreads — are **company-specific and largely self-help**. They are real and they are why this is "unfavourable" rather than "severe." But the macro vector is working against the equity: the stock is -30% in twelve weeks while credit spreads tightened 24bp. That divergence is the market telling you this is a **rates-and-margin problem, not a solvency problem**.

**Critical asymmetry:** Boeing's rating sits **one notch above junk**. Spreads at 1.45% are historically tight and priced for benign conditions. The company can absorb high rates; it cannot absorb high rates *plus* a credit-spread widening cycle.

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise — **BEAR (near-term, high-probability trigger)**

**Trigger: the US resumes strikes on Iran after the 3 November midterms** — which the President has explicitly flagged as a live option — and/or Houthi attacks escalate further against Saudi and Hormuz shipping.

**Transmission:**
- WTI breaks **$130+** (from $108); jet fuel, already doubled, squeezes again
- Airline industry profit of $23bn turns to aggregate losses; 1–2 high-profile carrier failures or Chapter 11 filings
- Boeing customers defer deliveries → the 737 MAX ramp slips a *second* time → the 2027 FCF inflection the equity is priced for disappears
- **Credit spreads widen from 1.45% toward 2.00%+**, re-testing BBB- and putting a downgrade-to-junk narrative back on the tape for the first time since 2024
- The Fed is forced to hike further; 10Y clears 5.50%; the 30Y tests 6%
- Boeing de-rates on both earnings *and* multiple simultaneously

**Downside: a retest of the $140s is plausible** if the delivery ramp slips again into a spread-widening tape.

**The mirror-image BULL surprise is equally large and cheaper to own:** a US–Iran deal takes WTI from $108 back to $70, airline profits recover, the December hike is pulled, the 10Y falls to 4.50%, and Boeing re-rates on both legs with a $745bn backlog and positive FCF already in hand. Note that oil has already eased on diplomacy headlines in late October — **the single most important variable for BA in the next 90 days is not an aircraft, it is a barrel.**
