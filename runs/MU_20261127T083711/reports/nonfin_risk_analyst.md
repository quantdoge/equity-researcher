## NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — MICRON TECHNOLOGY, INC. (NASDAQ: MU)

**Assessment date:** November 26, 2026 | **Price:** $601.25 | **Market cap:** ~$680B | **Beta:** 2.37
**Context:** FY2026 (ended Sept 3, 2026) was the most profitable year in company history — revenue $133.2B (+256% YoY), GAAP gross margin 80.7% (vs. 39.8%), GAAP net income $85.0B (vs. $8.5B), diluted EPS $74.33. The stock has nonetheless fallen ~45% from its Oct 1 closing peak of $1,097.22 (intraday ATH $1,108.55; Nov 20 low $585.25, −47%). This divergence between record fundamentals and violent de-rating is the frame for everything below: the market is pricing cycle-top and conduct risk, not operating weakness.

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate (trending toward Weak)**

**Supporting evidence — strengths:**
- **Board independence is genuinely strong.** The board affirmatively determined that Beyer, Dugle, Gomo, Haynesworth, Liu, McCarthy, Simons, Swan, and Wright qualify as independent — roughly 9 independents against a single executive director. Lynn A. Dugle serves as Lead Independent Director.
- **Say-on-pay support was 95% of votes cast** at the FY2025 meeting — an unusually high endorsement, suggesting shareholders view the pay *structure* (PRSU-weighted) as aligned despite its size.
- **Clean audit relationship:** PwC ratification for FY2027 is on the ballot with no disclosed dispute, auditor change, or restatement. My EDGAR full-text search for SEC enforcement actions naming MU returned **zero results** — no enforcement history, no restatement, no delinquent filings.

**Supporting evidence — weaknesses:**
- **Combined Chairman/CEO role.** Sanjay Mehrotra is *Chairman and Chief Executive Officer* — a concentration of power the company itself acknowledges by carrying a Lead Independent Director. This is the single clearest governance deduction. At a $680B market cap with 80% gross margins and a politically sensitive pricing regime, unchecked executive authority is a live risk.
- **Unprecedented board turnover in one cycle.** Haynesworth has notified the Governance and Sustainability Committee she will not seek re-election, and Simons, Swan, and Wright are retiring. That is **4 of ~10 directors departing at the January 14, 2027 meeting**, leaving 8 nominees. Institutional memory on the audit and compensation committees is resetting precisely as antitrust and pricing scrutiny peaks. Swan's exit in particular removes a seasoned ex-CFO chair-level voice.
- **Board is actively resisting shareholder rights.** Management recommends voting **against** the proposal to let holders of 10% of shares call a special meeting, and **against** the just-transition/critical-minerals report. Declining to expand the special-meeting right at a moment of extreme price volatility and heavy insider sales is an entrenchment signal worth flagging to proxy advisors.
- **Director pay escalation:** non-employee director equity target raised to $300,000 (FY2027) from $250,000, plus new committee chair cash ($45K Audit, $35K Comp). Modest, but coupled with the turnover it reads as retention spending.

**Verdict:** Independence and process are above average; concentration of power and board discontinuity are below average. Net: **Adequate**, with a negative trajectory.

**Interpretation and caveats:**
- **Mitigating:** Nearly all sales are tagged to **Rule 10b5-1 plans**, and the CEO retains 607,075 shares. Scheduled diversification during a 10x run is rational and legally insulated. This is *not* prima facie evidence of information asymmetry.
- **Aggravating:** The **breadth** is the problem. When the CEO, CTO, CBO, CLO, CAO, and Chief People Officer are all selling within the same window — including the Chief Legal Officer and the Chief Accounting Officer — it constitutes a **cluster-sale pattern** that historically precedes negative surprises more often than isolated CEO diversification. The Chief People Officer offloading $43M (more than the CEO's single largest tranche) is an unusual tell for an HR executive.
- **Timing:** Sales clustered August–October 2026, immediately before a 45% collapse. Even under 10b5-1, plan *initiation dates* matter and should be checked for proximity to the August peak.
- **Notable absence:** No insider bought the November dip at $585–$650. Given management publicly guided to a *record* FY2027 with HBM sold out through 2027, the total absence of insider buying during a 45% drawdown is a meaningful negative signal.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Extreme customer/contract concentration in HBM.** 2027 HBM output is sold out under **26 take-or-pay contracts** with ~75% of CY27 capacity committed. This is a demand-side blessing but a concentration and counterparty risk — a single hyperscaler deferring or renegotiating (or an AI capex air-pocket) converts "sold out" into inventory write-downs fast. Micron's own history shows memory write-downs are violent.
- **Key-person and succession risk around Mehrotra.** Combined Chairman/CEO, the public face of the HBM strategy, and simultaneously the largest individual seller. With 4 directors departing and no announced succession architecture, the company is more dependent on one executive than its governance structure implies. The **2,000% increase in his personal security budget to $764,748** signals management itself perceives elevated physical/retaliation risk — a proxy for the CEO's outsized centrality.
- **Trade-secret and IP exfiltration vulnerability.** Three former employees of Micron's Taiwan subsidiary were charged with stealing IP **valued at over $400M** to benefit a Chinese state-controlled company. This is a demonstrated, successful penetration of Micron's process-technology perimeter — not a hypothetical. Combined with sector-wide ransomware activity at PCB suppliers (e.g., Unimicron) and a ~$400M+ IP loss event, Micron's crown jewels (DRAM/HBM process recipes) remain a standing target with known adversary success.

*(Secondary: memory supply additions land 2H2028+, with BNP Paribas flagging price declines from mid-2027 — a classic capacity-timing cliff.)*

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Elevated and Rising**

- **The "RAMpocalypse" narrative is now mainstream.** With DRAM prices up ~500%+ and standard DRAM contract prices allegedly up ~697% in 18 months, Micron is being named in consumer class actions and covered as a driver of laptop and phone inflation. Lawmakers are publicly pressing chipmakers on affordability. The reputational frame has shifted from "American memory champion" to "oligopolist profiteer" — a dangerous shift for a company that relies on political goodwill for export-control protection against Chinese rivals.
- **Political exposure is self-inflicted and two-sided.** Micron aggressively lobbied Congress for the MATCH Act to squeeze Chinese rivals' access to tooling, while itself being banned from Chinese critical infrastructure. Being simultaneously an aggressor in Washington and a target in Beijing maximizes retaliation surface.
- **Executive-pay optics are poor.** $39.7M CEO compensation plus a security budget up 2,000% to $764,748, disclosed on the eve of a 45% drawdown, against 80% gross margins and rising consumer prices, is exactly the combination that invites negative press, proxy-advisor "against" recommendations, and political grandstanding.
- **Shareholder-proposal friction:** opposition to a just-transition/critical-minerals report and to the 10% special-meeting right will draw ESG and governance-vote dissent.
- **Offsets:** Genuine strategic value to US supply-chain security, HBM leadership, record execution, and high employee scale (61,000). No product recall, no accounting scandal, no SEC action.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Individually, most factors are manageable. Collectively they are compounding in the same direction, at the same time, at the top of a historic cycle:
1. An **antitrust class action that just cleared dismissal**, on facts (500–700% price increases) that are unusually easy for a jury to find sympathetic;
2. **Congressional and consumer-affordability scrutiny** converting a pricing windfall into political liability;
3. **Simultaneous governance reset** — 4 directors leaving, combined Chair/CEO, board blocking shareholder rights;
4. **Broad-based insider liquidation** with no insider buying on a 45% drawdown;
5. **Demonstrated IP theft** of $400M+ of process technology by insiders acting for a state-linked actor;
6. An **80% gross margin base** that makes any normalizing price environment catastrophic to earnings power — and therefore magnifies every non-financial shock.

The financials are pristine; the risk architecture around them is not. This is a **High** non-financial risk profile, not Critical, only because there is no fraud allegation, no restatement, no SEC enforcement action, and no going-concern or liquidity question.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. 🚩 **DRAM antitrust class action survived motion to dismiss (Nov 2026).** Treble-damages exposure plus a political route to pricing/allocations intervention. Recommend legal counsel quantify worst-case and monitor discovery milestones quarterly.
2. 🚩 **Cluster insider selling by six senior executives including the CLO and CAO**, ~$160M+ concentrated at $900–$1,140, with **zero insider purchases** during the 45% decline. Verify 10b5-1 plan *adoption* dates for proximity to the August peak — if plans were adopted or materially modified within 90 days of the highs, escalate to Compliance.
3. 🚩 **Combined Chairman/CEO with 4 directors departing simultaneously** and the board recommending against expanding shareholder special-meeting rights. Watch the Jan 14, 2027 say-on-pay and director-election vote percentages for a dissent spike; a sub-80% say-on-pay result would confirm shareholder alarm.
4. 🚩 **$400M+ trade-secret exfiltration by former Taiwan-subsidiary employees** for a Chinese state-controlled beneficiary. Confirm whether related process technology underpins current HBM nodes — if yes, this is a competitive-destruction risk, not just a legal one.
5. 🚩 **Netlist license expires 2031** with $30M/quarter already locked in. Model renewal at a materially higher rate; treat the $600M as the opening price of a recurring royalty, not a one-time cleanup.
6. 🚩 **Key-person concentration on Mehrotra** with heightened personal-security spend and no visible succession plan — request the board's succession framework at the next engagement.
