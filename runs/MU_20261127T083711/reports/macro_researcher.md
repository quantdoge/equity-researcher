## Macro Assessment — Micron Technology (MU)
**As of 27 November 2026 | Price $601.25 | Mkt cap $680B | Beta 2.37**

---

### 1. Macro Environment Summary: **Risk-Off / Reflation-Tightening**

The regime has flipped from "disinflation + easing" to **reflation with a hawkish Fed**, and equities are repricing it violently. Three things define the tape:

- **The Fed is hiking again.** Effective fed funds at 3.88% (Oct 2026), up from a 3.63% trough held June–August. Markets price a 25bp hike at the 8–9 December FOMC to 4.00–4.25%; Governor Cook has said policy "is not high enough to lower inflation," and Ackman is publicly warning the Fed is "about to make another mistake." This is a hiking cycle restarting, not a pause.
- **Inflation is re-accelerating.** Headline CPI 3.82% y/y (Oct), with core PCE rising 3.6% → 3.8% in October. Sequential CPI momentum is running ~5%+ annualised over the last three months. Growth is *not* the problem — Q3 2026 real GDP printed **+4.3%** with unemployment at 4.1%. This is a demand-driven inflation problem, the hardest kind for a central bank to ignore.
- **Long yields are rising for the wrong reason.** 10Y at 5.13%, 30Y at 5.47%, 2s10s **+39bp** — a bear steepening. Critically, 10Y breakevens have *fallen* to 2.12% (from 2.38% on 28 Oct). So the ~95bp rise in the 10Y since June is almost entirely **real yield / term premium**, not inflation expectations. 10Y real yield ≈ **3.0%** — the highest sustained real cost of capital in two decades.

Meanwhile the AI trade is unwinding: the S&P 500 and Dow closed in a 10% correction, the Nasdaq erased its 2026 gains and had its worst day in 18 months (−5.52%). HY spreads have widened from 2.66% (21 Sep) to **3.72%** — credit is loosening its grip but is not yet stressed. The dollar is the other shock: the broad USD index has rallied from 118.0 (early Sep) to **123.6**, +4.7% in ten weeks.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**NEGATIVE — Real rates / discount rate.** Micron is the highest-beta name in the semis complex (β 2.37) and a quintessential long-duration asset: a fab-intensive business whose value sits in 2027–2030 earnings. A 3.0% real 10Y mechanically compresses the multiple on those cash flows. This — not memory fundamentals — is the single largest driver of the **−45% drawdown from the $1,097 close on 1 October** (peak intraday $1,108). The Nov 5 session alone was −17.5% on 78M shares. The stock is still **+111% YTD** and **+157% y/y**, so the correction is a de-rating of an extremely crowded position, not a demand collapse.

**NEGATIVE — Risk appetite and positioning.** MU is the poster child of the AI memory trade. With the Nasdaq in correction, HY spreads +106bp and the AI complex unwinding, the marginal buyer of high-beta semis has disappeared. The 20–25 November range of **$585–$720** (a 23% intra-week swing) is a liquidity/positioning signal, not a fundamental one. Micron's fiscal Q1 report in early December is the next catalyst and lands into a market with no tolerance for a miss.

**NEGATIVE — Currency.** Micron is US-domiciled but internationally earning: the large majority of revenue is booked to Asia (China, Korea, Taiwan, Japan, SE Asia) and Europe. A 4.7% broad dollar rally in ten weeks is a direct translation headwind on revenue and a competitiveness headwind versus Samsung and SK hynix, who book in won. Partly self-hedged, because Micron also manufactures in Singapore, Japan and Taiwan — but the net translation effect on a $680B cap with thin reported margins is material.

**NEGATIVE — Tariffs (the underappreciated one).** A 25% Section 232 tariff on specified semiconductor imports took effect 14 January 2026, and on 26 November the administration proposed Section 301 duties of **up to 20% on China plus 13–14 partners including Japan and the EU**. Micron's Hiroshima fab is a core DRAM source. Tariffs on Japanese and EU semiconductor trade are a direct cost-of-goods hit and a supply-chain re-routing cost — and they are *inflationary into 2027*, which keeps the Fed hawkish and closes the loop back to the discount-rate problem. Export controls on HBM/DRAM to China remain a structural cap on a mid-teens percentage of revenue, with Huawei now teasing domestic HBM.

**POSITIVE — Demand and pricing power (the offset).** Here the macro is genuinely a tailwind. 4.3% GDP growth plus the AI capex cycle has memory supply tight: TrendForce sees DRAM contract prices rising through 2027, HPE expects scarcity into H2 2028, and Micron has reportedly pre-sold ~75% of 2027 output. In an upcycle, inflation is *good* for Micron — it is a price-setter, and rising input costs (energy, construction, materials) are passed through with a lag and a spread. This is why the stock trades near **~3x earnings** on what press reports call its most profitable year ever: the market is applying a trough multiple to peak earnings, the classic late-cycle memory discount. The valuation debate is entirely a question of how many years of peak pricing to underwrite.

**NEUTRAL — Refinancing / balance sheet.** Not a rate-sensitive levered story in the REIT/utility sense. The relevant rate channel is cost of capital on the US fab buildout (Idaho, New York), where a 3% real 10Y raises the hurdle rate on multi-year, multi-tens-of-billions commitments — a slow drag, not a solvency risk.

---

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE (near term) — with a strongly favourable micro offset**

On a 3–6 month horizon the dominant transmission channel is the **discount rate and risk appetite**, and both are hostile: Fed hiking, real 10Y at 3.0%, curve bear-steepening on term premium, dollar +4.7%, tariffs escalating, semis in a crowded unwind. That channel is what has taken 45% off the stock and it is not yet exhausted.

The **earnings channel is the opposite**: record demand, tight supply into 2028, pricing power, and a ~3x multiple that already discounts a downturn. The macro is squeezing the multiple while inflating the earnings — a genuinely unusual combination. Net: macro is a **headwind to the stock price and a tailwind to the P&L**.

---

### 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**Bull surprise (non-consensus): a December Fed pause and a tariff truce.**

The consensus is a December hike plus implementation of the Section 301 duties. But 10Y breakevens at 2.12% and *falling* tell you the bond market believes this inflation is tariff- and energy-driven, not demand-driven — and Q3 GDP of 4.3% is flattered by AI capex. If the Fed hikes 25bp on 8–9 December but signals a stop, or if the Trump–Xi meeting produces a tariff de-escalation (the proposed 20% China rate was explicitly framed as leverage), then the two things compressing Micron's multiple simultaneously reverse. Add a solid fiscal Q1 print and 75% of 2027 output already sold, and you have the setup for a violent squeeze in the most crowded-short high-beta semi in the market. A 40–60% retracement of the drawdown is plausible within weeks — the 20 November low of $585 is the line in the sand.

**Bear surprise (the one to hedge):** a 50bp hike plus implemented 20% tariffs on Japan/EU. That would hit Hiroshima-sourced DRAM costs directly, break the "supply stays tight into 2028" thesis earlier than BNP's mid-2027 price-decline call, and — with GF Securities already flagging NAND surplus in 2027 — turn peak earnings into falling earnings while the multiple is still compressing. That is the path to a genuine 2022-style memory bear, and the -45% we have seen would be the first leg, not the last.

---

**Bottom line:** Micron is caught between an excellent micro cycle and a deteriorating macro regime. The macro is not telling you demand is breaking — it is telling you the price of money has risen sharply and the market will no longer pay for 2027 earnings at a premium. Watch the 9 December FOMC and the tariff decision, not the DRAM spot price.
