## MICRON TECHNOLOGY (MU) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*FY2026 (ended ~Sept 3, 2026) basis. Revenue $133.19B; GAAP net income $84.97B; market cap ~$680B.*

---

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

Micron's reported geographic split is by **customer billing location**, not end-market consumption — a critical caveat. Only ~13% of revenue is billed to the US, yet the US is the primary end-demand and IP/jurisdiction anchor; conversely, Taiwan's 12.7% is largely *re-export/ODM* billing for US hyperscalers and does not capture the real exposure, which is **manufacturing concentration**.

**Manufacturing footprint** (the real geopolitical exposure): fabs in **Taiwan, Singapore, Japan, USA, Malaysia, China, India** (per FY2026 10-K). This is a chain of allied-but-exposed Asian nodes with essentially no substitute capacity at short notice.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US–China export-control escalation cutting both ways (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
- *Outbound:* On **16-Nov-2026** Commerce/BIS imposed new licensing requirements on advanced AI chip exports to **Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil** — targeting overseas data centres Washington believes Chinese firms use to access restricted US AI compute. Micron's HBM feeds directly into that accelerator supply chain, so this is a demand-channel risk even though it is not a direct Micron licence. Shares fell ~5% on the news, extending a 20% monthly slide.
- *Inbound/retaliation:* The **CAC ban (May-2023)** barring critical-information-infrastructure operators from buying Micron products remains in force; Micron has previously flagged that roughly half of its China/HK-headquartered revenue was at risk from that action.
- *China's counter-levers:* Chinese export restrictions on critical minerals (indium, rare earths) have already forced Micron into US indium-processing investment.
- *Impact:* 11% of revenue is directly China/HK-billed; a full loss plus HBM demand destruction in the five newly-licensed countries could plausibly remove **15–20% of revenue**. Partly self-mitigating — management has already guided China down to single digits.

**Risk 2 — Taiwan Strait / Taiwan concentration (Probability: Low-Medium / Impact: Very High)**
- Taiwan is both a top-2 billing region (12.7%) and a core DRAM/HBM manufacturing site. Micron **paused a $4.5B Taiwan memory plant in Nov-2026** amid an extended labour dispute, and Taiwanese press reports (Commercial Times) suggest the board could reassess a combined **NT$1.6 trillion (~$50B)** Taiwan programme. This is a labour/political risk today, but it signals that a large share of forward capex sits in the single most geopolitically fragile location in the semiconductor supply chain.
- *Impact:* a blockade or quarantine scenario is a tail event with existential (not merely earnings) consequences and is unhedgeable. Rate **Very High** on impact, but assign it lower probability than Risk 1.

**Risk 3 — Tariff and trade-regime cost pass-through (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
- **Section 232**: Proclamation 11,002 (14-Jan-2026) imposed a **25% tariff on advanced semiconductors** effective 15-Jan-2026, with a US-testing/processing condition. Because Micron imports finished product from Singapore, Taiwan, Japan and Malaysia into the US, it is exposed on both cost and administrative-compliance dimensions — though as a US-headquartered manufacturer with a domestic expansion programme and CHIPS Act support, it is structurally advantaged versus pure importers.
- Secondary: the "chips-for-plant" US–Taiwan framework signals continued policy volatility; tariff scope can widen to derivative products containing memory.

**Competitive-geopolitical overlay (not in top 3 but material):** China's **CXMT** claimed a denser DRAM design with server-module shipments by year-end (Oct-2026), triggering a **12% single-day drop** in MU and a broader memory selloff. This is state-backed capacity addition aimed squarely at Micron's most profitable product (server DRAM). *No specifications, yields, or named customers were disclosed* — treat as a credible medium-term pricing threat rather than a near-term one, but it is the main vector by which Chinese industrial policy erodes Micron's economics.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Antitrust — the largest live contingency.**
- **Garciaguirre et al v. Samsung Electronics et al**, N.D. Cal. (filed 25-Jun-2026). 14 consumers + 3 small businesses allege Micron, Samsung and SK hynix **restricted conventional DDR3/DDR4 output while diverting capacity to HBM**, engineering an artificial shortage. Claims conventional DRAM prices rose ~**697% over four years**; the three defendants hold ~**90% of global DRAM**. Relief sought: class certification, **injunction and treble damages**.
- **On 14-Nov-2026 Judge Noël Wise denied the joint motion to dismiss**; the stock fell ~7% intraday. Defendants must answer by December 2026.
- *Aggravating history:* in the mid-2000s DOJ DRAM price-fixing probe, Samsung paid $300M and Hynix $185M criminal fines; **Micron cooperated and avoided a corporate fine**, though one Micron employee pleaded guilty to obstruction of justice. That history gives plaintiffs and regulators a ready narrative.
- *Assessment:* surviving a motion to dismiss is a real negative signal, but proving coordination (versus parallel conduct in a genuinely tight AI-driven market) remains hard. Treble damages on a market of this size are theoretically enormous but the probability-weighted expected value is modest today. Key milestones: class certification and any summary-judgment ruling.

**Intellectual property — largely de-risked in FY2026.**
- **Netlist**: Micron agreed to pay **$600M to settle** the patent dispute (announced 6-Oct-2026), following a **$445M jury verdict** (May-2024). Netlist had also filed a May-2025 E.D. Tex. complaint asserting an HBM patent; the settlement appears to resolve the cluster. The Federal Circuit issued mixed rulings in 2026 (Feb: affirmed PTAB upholding a Netlist patent; 2-Sep: affirmed invalidation of another), but the cash settlement materially caps this exposure. **Treat as quantified and largely behind the company.**
- Also noted: Micron and Intel lost an appeals-court bid to invalidate certain memory patent claims (Bloomberg Law) — immaterial to the financials.

**Securities litigation — routine, low severity.**
- A federal securities fraud class action with a **10-Mar-2025** lead-plaintiff deadline, plus a **new wave of investigations/filings in 2026** (Faruqi deadline 27-Oct-2026; Robbins LLP investigation opened 16-Sep-2026; Scott+Scott derivative/fiduciary-duty inquiry). These track the stock's volatility and disclosure around HBM ramp/qualification. **Gap: the underlying 2026 complaint allegations were not retrievable from the available sources — magnitude unassessed.**

**Regulatory posture.** No Micron-specific DOJ, SEC, FCPA or EU enforcement action surfaced in the 2026 coverage reviewed. Broader context: SEC FY2026 enforcement volumes declined sharply, and DOJ has closed roughly half of legacy FCPA files — a *less* aggressive white-collar environment, partially offsetting the export-control/antitrust channels where enforcement is intensifying.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Export-control diversion risk (amber).** Post-Nov-2026 BIS licensing for Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil creates exactly the third-country re-export/diversion scenario that DOJ now pursues criminally (DOJ has confirmed its corporate enforcement policy applies to export-control and sanctions matters; recent resolutions include criminal penalties for sanctions/export offences). Micron's own distribution through Hong Kong ($6.29B billed) and Southeast Asian ODMs is the highest-scrutiny channel. No violation alleged — but the *programme* needs to be best-in-class.
2. **Antitrust conduct risk (amber→red).** An adverse class-certification ruling in the DRAM case would create a multi-year, headline-generating overhang, plus follow-on private and potentially governmental actions in the EU, Korea or China.
3. **China regulatory asymmetry (amber).** The CAC action demonstrates that Micron's Chinese revenue is revocable by administrative decision, outside normal legal process, with no meaningful recourse. This is a political risk masquerading as a regulatory one.
4. **Data privacy (green).** Crucial consumer-brand exposure is small; no material GDPR/CCPA enforcement identified.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale.** Not "Very High," because the largest single legal contingency (Netlist) has been cash-settled at a quantifiable $600M, no government investigation into Micron is currently live, and Micron is a *beneficiary* of — not a victim of — US industrial policy (CHIPS Act, onshoring preference). But it is unambiguously High: an **11% China/HK revenue base that a Chinese regulator has already demonstrated it will cut off**, a **12.7%-plus manufacturing concentration in Taiwan** with $50B of forward capex now under board review, live **import tariffs** on its core product, and an **antitrust class action that just survived dismissal** in a market where it holds ~a quarter of a 90%-concentrated oligopoly.

**Key monitoring triggers:** (i) class-certification ruling in *Garciaguirre*; (ii) any widening of the Nov-2026 BIS country list or Section 232 derivative-product scope; (iii) resolution of the Taiwan labour dispute and the NT$1.6T capex decision; (iv) CXMT server-DRAM shipment confirmation with named customers; (v) any Chinese re-escalation of the CAC ban or rare-earth/indium restrictions.
