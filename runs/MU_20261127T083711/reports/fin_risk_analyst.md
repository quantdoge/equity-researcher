## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE (Low credit risk)**

**Ratings:** S&P **BBB+** (outlook positive), Fitch **BBB+** (stable), Moody's **Baa2** (stable).* Solidly investment grade, though only 2–3 notches above high yield — the rating ceiling is set by memory cyclicality, not by leverage.

The balance sheet is a fortress. Total debt of $5.18B against $43.4B of cash and short-term investments implies **net cash of $33–38B**, with debt/equity of **0.04** and debt/assets of **0.03**. Interest coverage of **937x** (FY26 EBIT $99.3B / interest $106M) is effectively meaningless as a risk measure but confirms the debt is a rounding error: Q4 FY26 interest expense was literally **$0**. Altman Z-Score of **5.13** (safe zone) is conservative here because it uses *book* equity of $138B; substituting the $680B market cap pushes Z far higher. Piotroski **F-Score = 8/9** (strong), with the only miss being share dilution (F7 = 0).

**Critical caveat:** the company repaid roughly $10B of debt in FY26 out of peak-cycle cash flow. Credit quality is therefore *cyclically earned, not structurally permanent* — but even so, absolute debt of ~$5B can be retired from two quarters of trough-cycle cash.

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis

Base: FY26 revenue $133.2B, COGS $25.7B (19.3% of sales), OpEx $8.2B, EBIT $99.3B.

| Scenario | Revenue | Gross margin | EBIT | Net income | FCF | Credit impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Mild (-20%, ASP-driven)** | $106.6B | 75.9% | ~$72.7B | ~$62B | ~$41B | None. Coverage >600x |
| **B. Mid-cycle (-40%, GM→55%)** | $79.9B | 55% | ~$35.8B | ~$31B | ~$20B | None. Net cash intact |
| **C. Severe trough (-50%, GM→-5%)** | $66.6B | **-5%** | ~$(11.5)B | ~$(9.9)B | ~$(10.5)B | No refinancing need |

**Scenario mechanics that matter:** memory downturns are *price*, not volume, events — COGS barely flexes because fabs run full. In Scenario B, COGS would actually *rise* to ~$36B on 40% less revenue (fixed-cost absorption plus underutilization charges). That is precisely what happened in **FY23: COGS of $17.0B exceeded revenue of $15.5B**, producing a 9.1% negative gross margin and a $5.8B net loss.

**Liquidity survives all three.** In the severe trough, ~$10.5B annual burn against $43.4B of cash gives >4 years of runway, before any capex cut (management cut capex from ~$8B+ to $7.7B in FY23 and could cut far harder from today's $30.7B). Debt of $5.2B is long-dated and 8x covered by cash alone. **There is no plausible scenario in which MU faces a liquidity or covenant crisis in this cycle.** The damage in Scenario C is entirely to the *equity*: at $601, the stock trades at 8.1x peak EPS of $74.33 — a multiple that already concedes the earnings are unsustainable. On a normalised ~$18–20 through-cycle EPS, the implied multiple is **30–33x.**

**Why not "Low" overall:** every risk metric above is computed on the single most profitable year in the company's history, in which quarterly gross margin hit **86.8%** and revenue grew 4x in three quarters ($13.6B → $54.2B). Margins of that level have no precedent in commodity memory and are not durable. The risk profile is therefore best described as **an investment-grade balance sheet attached to a deeply cyclical, high-beta earnings stream.** Solvency risk is de minimis; the real downside is a 40–60% earnings and price reset, not a balance-sheet event.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Peak-cycle margins at 80.7% (Q4: 86.8%)** — unprecedented for a DRAM/NAND producer; mean reversion is the dominant financial risk. FY23 is the proof-of-concept (9.1% negative GM, -$5.8B net loss).
2. **Extreme two-way operating leverage** — EBIT moved from -$5.7B to +$99.3B on an 8.6x revenue change. Interest coverage collapsed to *negative* as recently as FY23.
3. **Inventory (135→147 days) and DSO (90→99 days) rising into the peak**, with a $6.1B working-capital outflow in the boom year — a classic late-cycle demand signal.
4. **Beta re-rated to 3.5 and volatility to 92%** while Sharpe fell from 1.50 to 0.96 — froth signature.
5. **FCF yield of 8.58% is a peak artifact.** Through-cycle average FCF (FY23–FY26) is **$13.7B, a 2.0% yield**; FY23 FCF was **-$6.1B**. Anyone capitalising today's FCF is mispricing the cycle by ~4x.
6. **Dilution:** shares +1%/yr plus $1.33B SBC (Piotroski F7 = 0).
7. **Customer/end-market concentration:** not quantifiable from available data — but the 4x revenue surge in three quarters implies hyperscaler AI/HBM concentration that could reverse violently.
