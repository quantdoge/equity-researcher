# Micron Technology (MU) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price: $601.25 | Market cap: $687.2B | EV: $649.0B | Diluted shares: 1.143B**
*Data as of late November 2026. Stock peaked ~$1,108 in late September 2026 and is down ~46%.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat Rating: NARROW (improving from "None")**

Micron is one of three players in a genuinely concentrated DRAM oligopoly — Samsung (~37–38%), SK Hynix (~25–30%), Micron (~23%) — collectively holding **90–95% of global DRAM bit shipments** through 2027. That structure is the single most important improvement in this franchise versus its historical record. Three suppliers, brutal capital intensity, and a shared interest in not destroying pricing is a real, if imperfect, competitive moat.

Supporting evidence for a *narrowing-to-widening* moat:
- **Customer switching costs are rising in HBM.** High-bandwidth memory is co-designed and qualified into specific AI accelerators; requalifying a supplier takes 2–3 quarters. Micron has **26 strategic customer agreements** and reports **$150B in long-term commitments** — larger than its entire FY2026 revenue. This is a genuine structural change from quarterly spot repricing.
- **Supply discipline is visible.** Management states ~**75% of 2027 supply is already sold**, and expects demand to exceed supply in both 2027 and 2028.
- **Intangibles/scale:** ~$63B net PP&E against ~$5.2B debt; a leading-edge DRAM/HBM process roadmap that only three firms on earth can fund.

**But the moat is not wide, and the evidence is honest about that:**
- The same business posted a **-9.1% gross margin in FY2023** and a **-49.5% revenue decline**. A business that can lose money at the gross line in a downturn does not have durable pricing power in the Buffett sense. That is a commodity signature, not a moat signature.
- **ROIC has been wildly inconsistent:** -9.9% (FY23), 2.2% (FY24), 14.1% (FY25), 69.2% (FY26). The Buffett test — ROIC above WACC *consistently* — fails on a through-cycle basis. The FY26 figure is a cycle artifact, not a steady-state return.
- Conventional DRAM is still ~60%+ of revenue and still reprices with the cycle. TrendForce has already cut its 2027 outlook and now models **conventional DRAM prices falling 19–20% in 2028** (vs. -5% previously), with NAND down ~21%. BNP Paribas expects DRAM/NAND supply to reach **parity with demand from mid-2027**.

**Moat verdict:** The oligopoly plus HBM contracts have moved this from "no moat" to "narrow moat" — a real improvement worth paying *something* for. But the cycle has been dampened, not repealed. Widening: yes. Wide: no.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **7 / 10**

| Metric | FY2026 | FY2025 | FY2024 | FY2023 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $133.2B | $37.4B | $25.1B | $15.5B |
| YoY growth | +256.3% | +48.9% | +61.6% | -49.5% |
| Gross margin | 80.7% | 39.8% | 22.4% | **-9.1%** |
| Operating margin | 74.6% | 26.1% | 5.2% | -37.0% |
| Net income | $85.0B | $8.5B | $0.8B | -$5.8B |
| Diluted EPS | $74.33 | $7.59 | $0.70 | -$5.34 |
| FCF | $59.0B | $1.7B | $0.1B | -$6.1B |
| ROIC | 69.2% | 14.1% | 2.2% | -9.9% |

**The good:**
- **FCF conversion is excellent at the peak:** $59.0B FCF on $85.0B net income = **69%**; operating cash flow $89.7B vs. capex $30.7B. FCF yield **8.6%** on today's market cap.
- **Fortress balance sheet:** $43.4B cash and short-term investments against $5.2B total debt → **net cash of $33.2B** (~5% of market cap). Interest expense is now immaterial ($106M vs. $477M in FY25). Equity nearly tripled to $138.4B. This is the strongest balance sheet in the company's history, and it removes solvency risk from the thesis.
- **Margin expansion is real and recent:** 22.4% → 39.8% → 80.7% gross margin over three years. Q4 FY26 alone was 87% gross margin on $54.2B revenue.

**The deductions (why not 9 or 10):**
- **Revenue and margin predictability is poor.** A 6-year revenue range of $15.5B to $133.2B is an 8.6x spread. Gross margin has ranged from -9.1% to 80.7%. That is not a business you can underwrite on a 10-year DCF with confidence.
- **Peak margins are not sustainable.** 80.7% gross / 74.6% operating is the widest in the industry's history, driven by an acute shortage. The company itself is guiding to a tighter but still extraordinary $61.5B revenue / $38.15 EPS in Q1 FY27 — but the *direction* of conventional DRAM pricing into 2028 is down 19–20%.
- **Capex intensity is structural.** $30.7B capex in FY26 (23% of revenue), $15.9B in FY25, $12.1B in FY22. FCF is permanently hostage to the capex cycle. Note that FY25 FCF was just $1.7B on $37.4B revenue — a 4.5% FCF margin.
- **Working capital is a drain:** -$6.1B change in WC in FY26; receivables ballooned to $36.2B. Earnings quality is fine, but cash conversion lags reported profit at the margin.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples:** P/E 8.1x | P/B 5.0x | EV/EBITDA 6.0x | P/FCF 11.7x | FCF yield 8.6% | P/S 5.2x | Dividend yield 0.09%

**DCF (base = trailing FCF $59.0B, 5% growth, 11% WACC, 3% terminal):**
- Enterprise value ≈ **$825.3B** → **~$722/share** (plus $33.2B net cash, ~$29/share, gives ~$751 on an equity basis; the tool's figure is EV-based)
- At $601, that is a **~17% discount** — below the 20–30% margin of safety I require. **And this DCF is generous**: it grows peak-cycle FCF at 5% forever. Any honest model must normalize the base.

**Peer comparison:** MU at 8.1x vs. sector peer median **30.5x** — a 73% discount. But the peer set (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, TSM, AVGO, AMD, ASML) is software/fabless/logic, not commodity memory. The only true comparable is SK Hynix, whose reported multiples in this dataset are corrupted (P/E 0.02, negative EV) — **I cannot validate that comparison and will not rely on it.** The peer discount is therefore largely a sector-mix artifact, not proof of cheapness.

**Normalized (through-cycle) valuation — the more important test:**

| Approach | Normalized FCF | Multiple | Equity value | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5-yr average FCF ($11.6B) | $11.6B | 20x | $232B | ~$203 |
| Mid-cycle $110B rev × 25% FCF margin | $27.5B | 20x | $550B | ~$481 |
| Mid-cycle $120B rev × 32% FCF margin | $38.4B | 20x | $768B | ~$672 |

The first row is unfairly punitive (it averages in a pre-AI trough). The second and third rows bracket the honest range: **roughly $480–670 per share.** At $601, the stock sits at the **top of that normalized range** — i.e., roughly *fairly valued*, not cheap.

**What the price implies:** At $687B market cap, a 20x multiple implies **$34.4B of sustainable FCF** — about 58% of the FY26 peak. Given conventional DRAM prices are forecast to fall 19–20% in 2028 and supply reaches parity mid-2027, that ~58% normalization haircut is **reasonable, not pessimistic.** The market is not irrationally pessimistic; it is pricing a plausible normalization.

**The bull case in numbers:** consensus FY27 EPS of ~$173 (+133%) and FY28 ~$208 (+20%) put the stock at **3.5x FY27 and 2.9x FY28 earnings**. If the 26 take-or-pay agreements and 75% sold-out 2027 supply genuinely hold pricing, those numbers are achievable and the stock is absurdly cheap. Analyst targets span $775 (BNP Paribas) to $3,000 (D.A. Davidson), with a cluster at $1,250–$1,520. **The dispersion itself is the point:** a 4x spread in price targets is what you see when nobody knows where the cycle bottoms.

**Valuation conclusion:** Peak-earnings multiples say "deep value." Normalized multiples say "fairly valued." The truth for a value investor is that **8x peak earnings on a commodity is not the same as 8x normalized earnings** — it is frequently 20x trough earnings in disguise. The margin of safety here is ~17% on an optimistic DCF and roughly **zero** on a normalized basis. That is not enough.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Verdict: Competent operator, adequate but not exemplary allocator.**

**Positives:**
- **Sanjay Mehrotra** (CEO since 2017, co-founder of SanDisk) has executed the HBM ramp and the technology roadmap credibly — Micron reached 23% DRAM share and qualified HBM3E into major AI platforms. The transformation from spot commodity seller to contracted supplier is a genuine strategic achievement.
- **Balance sheet discipline:** debt cut from $15.3B to $5.2B while cash rose to $43.4B. Net cash position achieved at the cycle peak rather than leveraging into it — the opposite of the 2021–22 mistake pattern.
- **Dividend raised** from $0.115 to $0.15/quarter (+30%), 19 consecutive payments maintained even through the FY23 loss year. Commitment to the dividend through the trough is a good signal.

**Yellow flags:**
- **Capital return is token relative to cash generation.** FY26 buybacks of just **$1.78B** against **$59.0B of FCF** and a **$35.16B authorization**; dividends of $610M. That is ~4% of free cash flow returned. The company is retaining and reinvesting nearly everything into capex — rational if the AI buildout is durable, value-destructive if it is a peak. This is the single biggest allocation question and it is unresolved.
- **Insider selling.** The CEO sold shares at $942–$1,181 under 10b5-1 plans in June–July 2026, near the highs. These are pre-scheduled plans and not disqualifying, but there is **no evidence of insider buying** at $601 after a 46% decline. For a "be greedy when others are fearful" setup, insiders are not putting money where their mouth is.
- **No track record of counter-cyclical buybacks.** The company has not historically bought aggressively into troughs — which is precisely when a cyclical allocator earns their pay.

Management is honest and operationally strong. On capital allocation, they are still behaving like a growth-capex company, not an owner-operator compounding intrinsic value per share.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

**Not a Buy — and specifically not a Strong Buy — despite the 46% drawdown.**

The bull case is real and better than it has ever been: an oligopoly with 90%+ share, a fortress net-cash balance sheet, 26 take-or-pay agreements, $150B of backlog, 75% of 2027 sold, and an 8.6% FCF yield. The structural improvement in earnings visibility is genuine and I credit it — it is why this is a **Narrow** moat and not a **None**.

But the price still asks me to underwrite peak-cycle economics:
- At **$687B market cap on $133B peak revenue (5.2x sales)** and 80.7% gross margins, I am paying a premium multiple on a record revenue base for a business that has posted negative gross margins within the last four years.
- The **DCF gives ~$722/share — only ~17% upside**, short of my 20–30% margin-of-safety bar, and that DCF assumes peak FCF grows 5% forever.
- **Normalized valuation of $480–670/share brackets the current price**, meaning the margin of safety on a through-cycle basis is approximately zero.
- The market is already pricing a ~58% FCF normalization — which is not a mispricing; it is an accurate reflection of TrendForce's -19/-20% 2028 DRAM price forecast and supply/demand parity in mid-2027.

**"A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy."** Micron at $601 is a fair price for a narrow-moat, capital-intensive, deeply cyclical business with an improving structure. I want a discount, not a fair price.

**What would change my mind:**
- **Buy trigger: $400–450** (~2.5–3x FY27 consensus EPS; ~40–45% discount to DCF). At that level the normalized valuation range would be breached to the downside and the margin of safety would be real. That is a **Strong Buy**.
- **Confirmation trigger:** two consecutive quarters of *holding* DRAM contract pricing through 2027 supply parity, plus evidence of meaningful buybacks at trough multiples (utilizing the $35B authorization). That would prove the cycle is genuinely dampened and would justify raising the moat to Wide and the verdict to Buy at higher prices.
- **Downgrade trigger:** a conventional DRAM contract price decline exceeding 25% in 2028, or evidence that take-or-pay agreements are being renegotiated.

**Position sizing if held:** If already owned, hold — do not sell into a 46% drawdown with $33B of net cash and 8.6% FCF yield supporting the floor. But do not add here. Keep dry powder for $400–450.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Cycle reversion is the dominant risk.** Conventional DRAM prices are forecast to fall 19–20% in 2028 and NAND ~21%; supply reaches demand parity mid-2027. At 80.7% gross margins, a reversion to the FY2022 45% level would cut gross profit roughly in half. At the FY2023 -9% level, the business loses money. **The downside is not theoretical — it happened three years ago.**
2. **Capex overbuild.** $30.7B of FY26 capex plus industry-wide expansion (Samsung, SK Hynix) into a 2027–28 supply increase. Memory history is a graveyard of capacity added at the top. If all three oligopolists expand simultaneously, the "supply discipline" that underpins the moat thesis collapses — this is the structural flaw in relying on oligopoly rationality.
3. **HBM concentration and customer power.** A handful of hyperscalers (Nvidia, plus a few others) drive HBM demand. They have both the incentive and the leverage to dual-source and to squeeze pricing. The 26 SCAs improve visibility but do not guarantee margin — as one analyst noted, $150B of backlog "does not guarantee profits."
4. **Tariffs and geopolitics.** New tariffs are an active headwind cited in the November selloff. Micron manufactures in the US, Japan, Singapore, and Taiwan; export controls and China exposure (both demand and competition from CXMT) remain unresolved.
5. **Extreme volatility.** Beta 2.37. A 46% drawdown from peak is not an anomaly — it is the normal behavior of this security. Position sizing must reflect that a further 40% decline is entirely plausible.
6. **Data-quality caveat:** The only direct peer (SK Hynix) returned corrupted valuation fields (P/E 0.02, negative EV), and the sector peer set is dominated by non-memory businesses. **I could not validate MU's relative cheapness against its true comparable.** My valuation rests on absolute DCF and normalized-earnings analysis, not peer multiples. A 10-K filing was also unavailable for risk-factor verification; risk factors above are drawn from reporting and trend data.

---

### Bottom Line

Micron is a **materially better business than it was five years ago** — net cash instead of net debt, contracted HBM revenue instead of pure spot exposure, and a genuine three-player oligopoly. That deserves recognition and it is why this is a Hold rather than a Sell. But at $601 — 5.2x peak sales, 8.1x peak earnings, 80.7% gross margins — the market is paying for the structural improvement *and* the peak cycle simultaneously. The normalized value is roughly where the stock trades. **Patience, not action. Revisit at $400–450.**
