# Investment Memo: MU — 27 November 2026

**Verdict**: HOLD — *new money may initiate a starter position only (≤1%); aggressive adds reserved for ≤$480 or confirmed 2027 contract strength* | Conviction: **Medium**

**Investment Thesis**
Micron is the scarcest toll-road in the AI buildout at the exact moment the market has decided the cycle is over: FY26 revenue +256% to $133B at 80.7% gross margins, 2027 capacity reportedly sold out, trading at 3.4x FY27E EPS after a 50% drawdown. The bull and bear cases are both correct — about different years. 2027 is contracted and cheap; 2028 carries a supply wall, a $18–25B depreciation step-up, and consensus-modelled ASP declines of ~20%. With a fortress balance sheet removing the solvency tail but zero through-cycle margin of safety at $601, this is a position to size and stage, not a conviction buy.

**Bull Case**
1. **Sold-out, contracted 2027 at 3.4x earnings**: $150B in long-term commitments, 75% of 2027 supply pre-sold, Q1 FY27 guided as the margin "floor." The market already capitalises only ~52% of current EBITDA — a hard haircut is priced in.
2. **Fortress balance sheet + clean accounting**: $33B net cash vs $5.2B debt; no solvency scenario this cycle. Unlike prior peaks, Micron can survive a multi-year downturn — the equity risk is drawdown, not impairment.
3. **Structural improvement is real**: three-player oligopoly, HBM qualification lock-in, US-supply-chain premium. If 2027 contracts settle at ≥+30–40% and margins hold, the most crowded-short high-beta semi re-rates violently (Dec-23 print is the trigger).

**Bear Case**
1. **Peak margins on a commodity**: 86.8% gross margin with flat COGS is a shortage rent, not a moat. DRAM capacity +50% by 2028, ASPs forecast −19/−20% in 2028, and the sell-side's own FY2031 revenue model embeds a −60% decline. A $18–25B depreciation wave lands FY28 on top.
2. **Hostile macro owns the multiple**: Fed hiking into 3.0% real yields, USD rally, tariff escalation hitting Japan-sourced DRAM. At beta 3.5, a 10% S&P drawdown ≈ −35% here. The single largest price driver is unforecastable (Dec-9 FOMC, tariff decision).
3. **Conduct signals point the wrong way**: unidirectional insider selling at 30–90% above today's price, antitrust class action past dismissal in a 90%-concentrated oligopoly, and CXMT/Huawei as state-backed structural threats to the tail of the market.
