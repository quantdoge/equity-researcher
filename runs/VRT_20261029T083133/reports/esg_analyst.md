## 2. Environmental Risk: **MEDIUM**

- **Carbon intensity (ops):** low. Vertiv reports Scope 1/2 across operated facilities with a 2030 Scope 1+2 reduction target and a Scope 3 reduction goal (~–24% by 2030 per company materials) plus 100% renewable electricity ambitions. Absolute Scope 1+2 is plausibly ~150–250 ktCO₂e against FY26E revenue of $14.65B — on the order of **10–17 tCO₂e per $M revenue**, well below Industrials medians. *(Precise FY25 figures not retrievable — VRT's report is PDF-only and unaudited to a limited-assurance standard; treat as a disclosure gap.)*
- **The real exposure is Scope 3 and use-phase.** Vertiv sells the largest single lever its customers have on their own emissions — PUE/WUE, free cooling, 800VDC architecture, liquid cooling, low-GWP refrigerant chillers. That is a **commercial advantage** but also a **regulatory magnet**: EU F-gas phase-down pressures HFC chiller lines; the EU universal PFAS restriction and ChemSec's Sep-2026 report (PFAS demand rising specifically because of AI cooling) put fluorinated-fluid-dependent designs in the crosshairs. VRT is a *user*, not a producer, of PFAS chemistry — second-order but real, with redesign cost and possible stranded SKUs.
- **Physical climate / water:** indirect but live. Data-centre water and grid backlash (permitting delays, moratoria) is now a demand-timing variable, not a tail risk — VRT's own Q2-2026 "timing shifts" language is partly a symptom of customer-side site interdependency.
- **Controversies:** no spills, no Superfund-scale remediation liability, no material environmental fines identified.

## 3. Social Risk: **MEDIUM-LOW**

- **Safety deteriorating from a good base:** TRIR **0.37 (FY24) vs 0.27 (FY23)** — a ~37% worsening, still roughly a third of typical heavy-manufacturing rates. Direction of travel is the wrong way but not a red flag.
- **Product safety:** UPS lithium-ion battery cabinets and liquid-cooling loops are high-consequence failure points. Vertiv is exposed to the **EU Batteries Regulation** due-diligence and industrial-battery carbon-footprint declaration requirements — compliance cost and supplier-data burden, manageable.
- **Labour/supply chain:** ~34,000 employees across US, EMEA, India, China, Philippines; significant China manufacturing footprint creates tariff, UFLPA and customer-audit exposure. Vertiv publishes a Supplier Code of Conduct and a modern-slavery statement; no forced-labour enforcement actions found. Skilled electrical/field-service labour scarcity is a growth constraint, not a liability.
- **Social licence (derivative):** community opposition to data-centre siting is a customer problem that can transmit to Vertiv order timing. Worth monitoring as a demand risk, not a VRT conduct issue.
- **D&I:** disclosure is generic; no quantified pay-gap or representation targets verified. Minor gap.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.5 / 10**

**Positives:** majority-independent board with a designated Lead Independent Director; CEO and Chair roles separated; single class of common stock (no dual-class) after the 2021 collapse of the SPAC structure; Platinum Equity's economic stake down to ~4.7% with the sponsor overhang effectively retired; no poison pill; active board refresh.

**Negatives (why not higher):**
1. **Executive Chairman is not independent** — David Cote, 73, has held the role since Feb-2020 (~8.6 years) and is treated as management, not an independent check.
2. **Disclosure-credibility pattern.** The 2022 S.D.N.Y. class action (*Vinings* / *City of Riviera Beach*, class period Feb-2021–Feb-2022, tied to the Nov-2021 SPO) alleged false statements on **pricing visibility**, internal control failures on sales practices leading to **excessive discounting** ("cultural" issue) and mislabelling recurring costs as one-time. The 2026 Hagens Berman investigation alleges a near-identical narrative shape: confident FY26 guidance (Q1 call, 22-Apr-2026) followed by a July-2026 revenue miss blamed on "timing shifts." **Two consecutive cycles of "guidance was fine until it wasn't" is a governance signal, not bad luck.**
3. **Incentive alignment:** heavy equity comp in a stock up ~10x since 2023; pay design should be scrutinised for backlog/orders-based metrics that reward bookings the company cannot convert.
4. Limited ESG governance maturity: no net-zero target and **no SBTi commitment** (KnowESG, Oct-2026) — an outlier among large-cap Industrials and a barrier to ESG-mandated fund inclusion.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD:** as a non-EU parent with EU turnover well above €150M (EMEA ≈ $2.0B+/yr), Vertiv falls into the wave-in tranche — first sustainability statement for **FY2028** under current scope (subject to the 2025–26 "omnibus" simplifications). Requires full Scope 3 and EU-taxonomy-aligned reporting: a multi-year systems build.
- **EU CBAM:** definitive regime from 2026 on iron/steel/aluminium imports; Vertiv imports enclosures, busway, switchgear and racks into the EU from US/India/China — **modest but real embedded-emissions cost pass-through**, and a customer-requested EPD burden.
- **EU F-gas Regulation (2024/573)** and **EU PFAS restriction proposal (ECHA)** — the most consequential product rules for the thermal portfolio; favours VRT's low-GWP chiller line but penalises legacy HFC SKUs.
- **EU Batteries Regulation** — due diligence live, industrial-battery carbon footprint declarations phasing in; hits the Li-ion UPS/battery cabinet business.
- **EU Energy Efficiency Directive data-centre reporting** — customer-side, but drives demand for metered PUE/WUE data Vertiv's software must supply.
- **US:** SEC climate rule stayed; California SB 253/261 if revenue thresholds met; **UFLPA** on China-sourced content; tariffs on Chinese/Southeast Asian inputs.
- **No ESG-linked debt covenants** identified.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE (disclosure laggard)**

Operationally lower-risk than the Industrials average, commercially *enabling* the energy transition in one sense (efficiency) while *enabling* the AI build-out's resource intensity in another. Held back by the absence of SBTi/net-zero targets, thin third-party rating coverage, and a governance record scarred by repeated guidance-vs-execution disconnects.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL (governance-tilted negative watch)**

ESG is **not** the thesis driver and is not a reason to own or avoid VRT on its own. Two actionable conclusions for the memo:

1. **The financially material ESG risk here is G, not E.** The binding constraint on the multiple is management credibility and disclosure quality around backlog conversion and site-level execution — not carbon. A second consecutive guidance reset would invite formal securities litigation, compress the multiple, and raise cost of equity. **Underwrite the backlog-to-revenue conversion assumption with more scepticism than the sell-side, and treat the Jul-2026 –23% drawdown as a preview rather than an anomaly.**
2. **Product regulation (PFAS/F-gas) is the medium-term E risk to size**, not plant emissions. Watch the EU universal PFAS restriction timeline and Vertiv's mix shift to low-GWP/refrigerant-free (liquid/direct-to-chip) designs — a share-shift opportunity for whoever gets qualification first.
