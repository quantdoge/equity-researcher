# SECTOR BRIEF: Vertiv Holdings (VRT)
### Sub-sector: Critical Digital Infrastructure — Data-Centre Power & Thermal Management
*(FMP classifies VRT in Industrials / Electrical Equipment & Parts. The meaningful comparable set is the electrical/thermal equipment complex, not the broad Industrials sector.)*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

VRT competes in data-centre power train and thermal management plus attached lifecycle services — a high-beta carve-out of Electrical Equipment & Parts that has structurally decoupled from industrial GDP. The sector's demand driver is no longer general electrification but AI-dense compute: global data-centre capex was ~$434B in 2024 and is forecast to exceed $1T by 2029, with JLL projecting ~100 GW of new capacity added 2026–2030. Critically, the physics have shifted value toward Vertiv's core competencies — rack densities that air cooling cannot serve, forcing liquid cooling adoption (Dell'Oro: liquid cooling roughly doubled in 2025 to ~$3B, tracking toward ~$7B by 2029) and pushing power distribution from ~10 kW to 100 kW+ per rack. The sector is a **mid-cycle growth phase, not mature**: revenue growth of 20–28% at Vertiv exceeds the 7–11% of diversified peers, pricing power is intact, and order books (not installed base) set near-term revenue. Concurrently, sub-sector equities de-rated sharply from the July 2026 peak as investors questioned capex durability — VRT −32% from its May 2026 high of $376 into late August, versus ETN −15%, GEV −24%, TT −13%.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Hyperscaler capex acceleration, not deceleration.** VRT's Q3 2026 (reported 28 Oct 2026) organic orders *more than doubled*; revenue of $4.3B and adjusted EPS of $2.21 beat guidance, and management raised full-year sales, profit and FCF outlook while guiding ~38% growth into 2027. GE Vernova's orders simultaneously jumped 96% — corroborating demand at the grid-interconnection layer rather than a single-vendor anomaly.
- **Liquid cooling mix shift is margin- and share-accretive.** Air-to-liquid transition is a technology discontinuity that punishes legacy CRAC incumbents and rewards vendors with integrated CDU + rack-manifold + controls portfolios. VRT's gross margin expanded from 28.4% (2022) to 36.3% (2025) — 790bp in three years — evidence the mix shift is being captured, not competed away.
- **Time-to-power is the binding constraint, favouring integrated vendors.** With grid queues multi-year in Northern Virginia, Dublin and Frankfurt, buyers optimise for speed of deployment. This favours pre-integrated, factory-tested modular solutions and the service annuity attached (VRT's lifecycle services create switching costs post-installation).

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Customer concentration and capex cyclicality risk.** Demand is concentrated in a handful of hyperscalers and neoclouds whose capex is discretionary, equity-financed and increasingly scrutinised for ROI. A single large customer digestion pause would create a violent order-rate air-pocket given the sector's short backlog relative to aerospace peers.
- **Multiple compression / rate sensitivity.** VRT's beta is 2.05 and it trades at ~70x FY25 earnings ($1.33B net income on $93.5B market cap). The sector's 2026 drawdown was valuation-driven, not fundamental — meaning even flawless execution may not protect the share price if long rates rise or AI sentiment rotates.
- **Input costs, tariffs and capacity race.** Copper, transformer/switchgear lead times, and electrical labour are tight; competitors (Schneider, Eaton, ABB, nVent) are all adding thermal and switchgear capacity simultaneously. Capacity added into a slowdown would compress pricing — the 2027–28 risk, not the 2026 one.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

**Rationale:**
- **Purest exposure with scale.** At $10.2B revenue VRT is large enough to serve global hyperscalers yet has ~100% of revenue levered to digital infrastructure — versus ~25–30% for ETN and TT. This is why VRT grew 27.7% while peers grew 8%.
- **Demonstrated pricing power.** Gross margin up 790bp in three years with SG&A held at ~15.8% of sales is the signature of a supplier raising price and mixing into higher-value product, not one buying share.
- **Balance-sheet and earnings quality improving.** Interest expense fell from $180M (2023) to $86M (2025) while EBITDA reached 20.9% margin — deleveraging funds capacity expansion without dilution.
- **Caveats that cap the rating at "strong" rather than "dominant":** margins are only *at* peer median, so the growth advantage has not yet converted into superior unit economics; customer concentration is high; and the 32% drawdown plus 2.05 beta show the market is pricing execution risk aggressively.

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

Demand visibility (order growth >100% at VRT, +96% at GEV), a genuine technology discontinuity favouring incumbents with integrated thermal portfolios, and a structural multi-year capex runway outweigh the cyclicality and valuation concerns. The 2026 de-rating has improved the entry point on a risk-adjusted basis — VRT now at ~$243 versus $376 in May — even though absolute multiples remain demanding. **Key monitorables:** hyperscaler capex guidance for 2027, order book conversion vs. backlog duration, and whether liquid-cooling competition triggers the first gross-margin degradation — the single cleanest signal of the cycle turning.
