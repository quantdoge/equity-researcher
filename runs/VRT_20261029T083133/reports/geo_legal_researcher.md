# VRT — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment (Vertiv Holdings Co)

**Snapshot:** $93.5bn market cap · Industrials/Electrical Equipment · HQ Columbus, Ohio · ~34,000 employees (FY2025 10-K) · FY2025 net sales $10,229.9m → FY2026E ~$14,650m (38% organic growth guide) · backlog ~$15.0bn at 12/31/25 with 12–18 month delivery windows. Demand is concentrated in AI data centre power/thermal infrastructure, which is precisely where geopolitical and regulatory friction is concentrating.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Tariff and trade-policy whiplash on a Mexico/China-weighted manufacturing base (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
Vertiv's own disclosures name "Section 301 tariffs; IEEPA tariffs (20% China; 25% Mexico/Canada; 35%…)" as live cost variables, and management has repeatedly pointed to shifting production to lower-/no-tariff origins. The FY2025 10-K retains a dedicated risk factor: *"Changes in U.S. or foreign trade policies, including additional tariffs or global trade conflicts, could increase the cost of our products."* Mexico's January-2026 MFN increase of 5–50% across 1,463 tariff lines squarely hits a nearshoring strategy built on USMCA qualification. Mitigant: pricing power — management states it expects "positive price cost in 2026" and pricing to exceed inflation, and Q3-26 gross margin actually expanded to 38.1% (from 37.8%). Margin has so far absorbed tariffs; the risk is a step-change, not a drift.

**Risk 2 — US–China technology decoupling cutting both ways (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium–High)**
APAC is ~20% of revenue and +50% y/y. China is both a demand market (management cites "encouraging green shoots" and pipeline movement) and a component source (single-digit share of demand sourced there). Two-way escalation is live: China's new proposed AI export curbs (Reuters, 28-Oct-2026) and draft trade-secret/blocking regulations that could bar offending foreign entities from trade or investment in China, plus activation of its 2021 blocking rules against US sanctions. The FY2025 10-K keeps its risk factor on "governmental export and import controls and sanction programs." A US export-control expansion to data-centre thermal/power equipment (plausible as AI infrastructure is securitised) would be the higher-impact tail; China-side retaliation hits the $779m/quarter APAC line.

**Risk 3 — Customer-side permitting and data centre moratoria (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
This is the most under-appreciated item and it is *newly elevated in the FY2025 10-K*: the company added a new risk factor on "future legislation and regulation could disrupt our customers' markets" (AI, cybersecurity, energy consumption, data centre construction), and reworded its demand risk factor to explicitly add "local zoning decisions, community opposition, and temporary or permanent local moratoria restricting data center construction." Reality check: 27 states advanced data-centre bills in 2026 and a federal "AI Data Center Moratorium Act" has been introduced (Forbes, Oct-2026; MultiState tracker, Apr-2026). Vertiv's $15bn backlog with 12–18 month delivery is directly exposed to customer project deferrals — a slowdown would show up as book-to-bill compression rather than a legal charge.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action still live.** *In re Vertiv Holdings Co Securities Litigation*, No. 1:22-cv-03572 (S.D.N.Y.), filed 3-May-2022 over 2021–22 inflation/supply-chain disclosures. Defendants won partial dismissal (Westlaw), a Report & Recommendation issued before Judge Gregory H. Woods (Magistrate Ona T. Wang), and the docket remained active into 2026. Not material to a $93bn-cap balance sheet, but it is a recurring disclosure and headline risk; a related earlier case was voluntarily dismissed in May-2022.
- **Otherwise clean Item 3.** Q3-2026 10-Q boilerplate: "With the exception of the below, we are not a party to any material, pending legal proceedings or claims." No disclosed DOJ/FCPA, antitrust, or SEC enforcement action was found in any filing or search.
- **Regulatory/approval exposure from M&A.** Three deals in ~12 months: Great Lakes (Jul-2025), PurgeRite (Dec-2025), and Utility Innovation Holdings ($1.45bn cash, up to $1.15bn more in EBITDA earn-outs, closed 26-Oct-2026). Each carries merger-control/foreign-investment review and integration risk; the earn-out structure adds a litigation/valuation dispute surface.
- **Regulatory tailwinds, not just headwinds.** AI/energy-efficiency efficiency mandates (EU codes, US federal efficiency standards) favour high-efficiency thermal and 800V DC architectures — Vertiv's core roadmap.
- **Peer/regulatory context.** No sector-wide enforcement wave against data-centre equipment makers was identified; the regulatory pressure is on *customers'* construction and energy consumption, not on Vertiv's products.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **FCPA/anti-corruption in emerging markets — structural, not incident-driven.** The 10-K explicitly flags "anti-corruption laws and regulations of the U.S. government and various other international jurisdictions" alongside a risk factor noting "additional or exacerbated risks may exist in emerging markets" (34,000 employees, service centres and plants worldwide, heavy Middle East/APAC/India project business). No enforcement action disclosed; this is a residual control risk on turnkey projects funded by state-linked buyers.
2. **Export controls / sanctions classification of products.** Vertiv products (secure KVM, Avocent management platforms, DC power) have dual-use and cybersecurity-relevant characteristics; classification errors are a plausible unquantified liability.
3. **Data privacy.** Dedicated risk factor on evolving privacy laws (EU/UK/US state) given software/monitoring offerings — low severity, routine.
4. **⚠️ Watch item: a geopolitical risk factor was *dropped*.** The FY2025 10-K removed the explicit FY2024 risk factor on "wars, conflicts and other types of geopolitical tensions, and any resulting sanctions by the U.S., European Union and other countries." Eight factors were dropped and five added. Removing it in a year of record geopolitical volatility is a disclosure-quality observation worth flagging to the committee even though the underlying exposures (sanctions, export controls, tariffs) remain covered elsewhere.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

Legal risk is genuinely low: one legacy securities class action, no material pending proceedings, no enforcement exposure disclosed. Geopolitical and regulatory risk is the binding constraint: a ~38% non-US revenue base, a Mexico/China-anchored supply chain inside an active tariff regime, ~20% APAC exposure inside a two-way US–China tech escalation, and — most importantly — a $15bn backlog whose conversion depends on customers obtaining permits in a US environment where 27 states are pushing moratoria. Balance sheet and pricing power (38.1% gross margin, +290bps adjusted operating margin) provide real absorptive capacity, which is why this is Medium rather than High. **Escalation triggers to monitor:** any US export-control listing of data-centre thermal/power equipment; Mexico tariff renegotiation breaking USMCA qualification; a state-level construction moratorium in Texas/Virginia; adverse ruling or settlement in 22-cv-03572; and disclosure of any new China blocking-rule constraint on Vertiv's China exports.

**Gaps:** no country-level revenue disclosure below the three segments (China revenue share unquantified); SEC filings were inaccessible directly, so Item 3 detail in the FY2025 10-K and Q3-26 10-Q was verified only via secondary extracts; EMEA country mix including Middle East/Gulf concentration is undisclosed.
