# Macroeconomic Assessment — JPMorgan Chase (JPM)

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: TRANSITIONAL, tilting RISK-OFF.** The US macro regime flipped in mid-September 2026. After a ~150bp easing cycle (Fed funds effective rate fell from 5.13% in Sep 2024 to 3.63% by Aug 2026), the FOMC **hiked 25bp to 3.75%–4.00% on Sep 16, 2026 — its first hike since 2023** — on sticky, tariff-influenced inflation. The 10-year Treasury has repriced to ~5.0% (spiking to 5.18% on Sep 24), the yield curve has steepened with a large term premium, and the Fed's own SEP is said to show inflation not returning to 2% until ~2029. Growth is decelerating (real GDP up only ~1.5% annualized in Q2 2026), unemployment has drifted up to 4.1%, and Jamie Dimon is publicly flagging recession risk, sticky inflation, and credit-spread widening. This is an environment where bank NII tailwinds persist but credit-cycle risk and discount-rate pressure are rising simultaneously.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Franchise context (public disclosures):** FY2025 revenue $185.6B, net income $57.0B, ROTCE ~20%. Q2 2026: net income $16.9B, EPS $6.14, credit costs $2.5B (net charge-offs $2.4B). Full-year 2026 NII guided to a **record ~$105.5B** (raised from $103B).

| Variable | Impact | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **High/sticky rates → NII** | ✅ **Positive** | JPM is asset-sensitive; record NII guidance (~$105B) is directly levered to the 3.75–4.00% funds rate. Higher beta income from $1.4T+ of earning assets. |
| **Fed pivot to hikes** | ⚠️ **Mixed / turning negative** | Near-term NII tailwind, but signals higher-for-longer: raises borrower stress, compresses multiples on long-duration assets (private equity, real estate collateral), and pressures deposit costs. |
| **10Y at ~5% (discount rate)** | ⚠️ **Negative (valuation)** | Higher long-end yields de-rate JPM's own equity (long-duration cash flows) and slow capital-markets activity/AUM flows; partly offset by higher money-market & wealth spreads. |
| **CPI 3.05%, sticky** | ⚠️ **Negative (credit)** | Tariff-inflated pricing erodes real consumer purchasing power → higher card/auto charge-off risk. Q2 net charge-offs already $2.4B and rising; Dimon warns of credit-spread widening. |
| **Slowing real GDP (~1.5% ann.)** | ⚠️ **Negative (growth)** | Softens loan origination and consumer spend; investment-banking wallet growth fewer M&A/IPO volumes. |
| **Unemployment 4.1%** | ✅ **Positive (near-term)** | Still historically full employment supports consumer credit quality and deposit stability — though the trend (higher) is adverse. |
| **Firm USD (113.6 broad index)** | ⚠️ **Slightly negative** | JPM's primary FX exposure is GBP (UK CIB franchise) per its 10-K; a still-strong dollar trims translated international revenue. Modest, as ~two-thirds of revenue is US-centric. |
| **Tariff regime volatility** | ⚠️ **Negative (sentiment/activity)** | JPM's own survey shows midsize US clients destabilized by tariff swings — dampens business-borrower confidence and commercial loan demand. |

**Net read:** The rate regime is the dominant driver, and it is now a **two-sided coin** — record NII today, building credit risk and valuation drag tomorrow.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL** (with an unfavorable tilt)

- **Favourable within the window:** record NII, fortress capital (ROTCE ~20%), cheap core-deposit funding, near-full employment supporting credit, elevated trading volatility aiding markets revenue.
- **Unfavourable build:** first Fed hike since 2023, 10Y at ~5%, sticky ~3% CPI, rising charge-offs, decelerating real growth, and Dimon's own recession warnings. If credit costs and the discount rate keep climbing together, the NII tailwind will be swamped by provisioning and multiple compression.
- **Verdict:** Neutral-to-risk-off. JPM is better positioned than peers (funding, capital, diversification), but the macro beta is no longer one-way positive.

## 5. Material Surprise Scenario (Bear)

**"Sticky-Tariff Stagflation → 2027 Recession":** CPI, instead of drifting back toward 3%, re-accelerates above 4% as tariff pass-through compounds with energy stickiness. The Fed, having already hiked once, is forced into a repeat-2022-style catch-up (funds rate → 4.5–5.0%), the 10Y breaks decisively above 5.5–6.0%, and credit spreads blow out. Unemployment rises past 5%, card/commercial charge-off rates normalize sharply higher, and JPM's record NII is offset by a provisioning cycle while the banking sector re-rates below tangible book value. JPM's diversified franchise and capital would cushion the blow, but a combined earnings miss + multiple compression could take the stock down 25–35% — the "Dimon skunk at the party" scenario playing out in full.

*Bull mirror:* disinflation resumes despite tariffs, the Fed pauses and re-cuts, the 10Y falls to ~4%, credit costs stay benign — JPM keeps record NII *and* re-rates higher. That path requires the September 2026 hike to prove pre-emptive rather than the start of a new cycle.
