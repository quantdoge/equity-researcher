# NVDA — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Analysis
**NVIDIA Corporation (NASDAQ: NVDA) | Sector: Technology / Semiconductors | HQ: Santa Clara, CA | Mkt cap ≈ $5.67T | CEO: Jensen Huang**

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

NVDA discloses revenue by customer billing location. Latest reported quarter (Q2 FY2027, ended Jul 2026): total revenue **$96.2B** — US **$60.1B (~62%)**, Taiwan **$27.0B (~28%)**, remaining ~$9B spread across Singapore, China (incl. HK), and other international — per QuiverQuant aggregation of the Jul 26, 2026 earnings release. For FY2025 (ended Jan 2025) China (incl. Note: a material share of "Singapore"/"Taiwan" billing is indirect China demand (ODMs and Singapore-based data center operators), so true China end-exposure exceeds the direct billing split.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**R1 — US–China tech export controls (H20 and successors).** *Probability: High (already realized) | Impact: High.* The H20 was placed under a license requirement in April 2025, triggering a **~$4.5B charge in Q1 FY2026** and ~$5.5B total; CEO Huang estimated up to **$15B in revenue loss**. Licenses were assured in July 2025 and sales resumed, but only subject to a **15% US government revenue share on China-bound H20/AMD MI308 sales** (confirmed Aug 11, 2025) and licenses remain "for the indefinite future." The January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule was rescinded in May 2025, but the underlying export-control regime is unchanged and can tighten again at any time — the single most material swing factor in the model.

**R2 — Cross-strait conflict / TSMC concentration.** *Probability: Low-to-Medium | Impact: Very High (existential).* Nearly all of NVDA's leading-edge GPUs and advanced packaging (CoWoS) flow through Taiwan. China's new mobilization law (effective Oct 2026), expanded export controls, and Beijing's stated ambiguity over Taiwan keep a low-probability/high-severity supply-chain shock live. Even short of conflict, China's October 2026 fuel-export suspension and counter-sanctions add volatility to shipping/logistics for AI supply chains.

**R3 — China retaliation & antitrust: SAMR probe + counter-sanctions regime.** *Probability: Medium-High | Impact: Medium.* SAMR opened an investigation in Dec 2024 into commitments tied to the Mellanox acquisition (2019/2020); by Dec 2025 a preliminary finding of an anti-monopoly law violation was reported, exposing NVDA to fines of **1–10% of China revenue** and behavioral remedies. Compounding this, China's April 2026 regulations targeting Western "long-arm jurisdiction" and its new anti-sanctions law create direct legal conflict-of-laws exposure for US entities selling into China — a credible lever for retaliatory fines well beyond the antitrust matter.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Export controls (US BIS):** H20 and related AI accelerators require licenses for China "for the indefinite future" (April 2025–present); 15% govt revenue-share condition on resumed China sales (Aug 2025). GAIN AI Act (pending US legislation) would further restrict exports of H100/B300-class parts and impose safety duties; NVDA publicly opposes it. Passage probability low, but it shows the policy direction.
- **China antitrust (SAMR):** Active investigation re: Mellanox merger commitments; preliminary finding of violation reported (Dec 2025); fine exposure 1–10% of China revenue.
- **US DOJ:** Escalated antitrust probe in Sept 2024 (subpoenas re: alleged switching-cost/CUDA lock-in and supply conditions); continues, no charges filed to date; NVDA has publicly denied receiving subpoenas at points. Watch outcome given renewed focus on AI accelerator dominance.
- **France (ADLC):** Probe since Sept 2023 office raids; charges recommended and investigation reported "nearing its end" (2025–26), focused on CUDA software lock-in and GPU sales conditions — first enforcer to move formally.
- **EU Commission:** Informal third-party consultations over potential foreclosure via sales practices; no formal case opened.
- **Trade/tariff backdrop:** Thin Trump–Xi trade truce (Sept 2026 summit); US semiconductor tariff proposals and EU–China brawls keep import/export costs and rule-changes in flux.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **Export-control complexity:** Licensing conditions, 15% revenue share, and "indefinite" uncertainty make compliance costly and forecastability poor; risk of inadvertent diversion via Singapore/Taiwan/third-country channels is structurally elevated.
- **Conflict-of-laws exposure:** China's new counter-sanctions and long-arm-jurisdiction regulations (Apr 2026) criminalize compliance with certain foreign measures — any future US control expansion puts NVDA's China operations in legal tension between two sovereign regimes.
- **Third-party channel risk (FCPA/AML):** Heavy reliance on ODMs/distributors and state-aligned Chinese customers raises third-party due-diligence requirements; no FCPA/AML enforcement or material litigation against NVDA has been disclosed — the current bill of health is clean but the exposure is structural.
- **Supply-chain concentration (Taiwan):** Not a legal violation, but a compliance/governance exposure as a sanctioned-jurisdiction (China) seeks to restrict Taiwanese high-tech exports.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

NVDA enjoys a defensive domestic demand base (~62% US billing) but sits at the epicenter of US–China tech decoupling: export controls have already cost it billions, China has an active antitrust case pending, and three jurisdictions (US, EU/France, China) are scrutinizing its AI-market dominance. The combination of realized revenue losses, pending enforcement actions, and existential Taiwan supply-chain concentration supports a **High** rating — not Very High largely because Taiwan conflict remains low-probability and enforcement actions to date are fines/remedies rather than existential.
