## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **Medium**

- **Direct footprint (Scope 1&2): Low.** As a fabless firm, NVDA's direct emissions are small. It **achieved 100% renewable electricity for offices and data centers in FY2025** (up from ~90% prior year) and set an SBTi-aligned target to maintain 100% and cut Scope 1&2 emissions to net zero by FY2030.
- **Scope 3 (upstream): Medium.** TSMC manufacturing is energy/water-intensive in water-stressed Taiwan; single-supplier concentration (4nm/5nm-class nodes) means NVDA's carbon intensity is largely determined by TSMC. Taiwan's Jan 2025 earthquake demonstrated physical-disruption exposure.
- **Scope 3 (downstream use phase): High and rising.** The true climate materiality is in *customers'* data-center electricity. NVDA partially mitigates through efficiency leadership (GB200 Grace Blackwell: ~25x energy-efficiency gain per unit of work vs prior gen) — this is the key climate lever and it is currently a *positive* differentiator.
- **Physical risk:** Low direct; indirect via Taiwan concentration and data-center water use in arid regions (e.g., Phoenix-area water projections).
- **Transition risk:** Low near-term (no direct carbon-tax/CBAM exposure on silicon), but EU data-center energy-efficiency regulation and AI sustainability reporting pressure will grow.

**Verdict:** Strong disclosure trajectory for a company of this scale and size-class; the gap is audited Scope 3 completeness (category 1 upstream + category 11/13 downstream), which caps the score.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **Medium-High**

- **AI ethics (core S risk).** Product-level harms are NVDA's defining social exposure. A December 2023 Bloomberg report revealed Nvidia staffers had warned the CEO that AI posed disproportionate threats to minority groups (internal report, "One Voice in the Machine"), which leadership reportedly deprioritized. Huang's public stance that AI "doesn't need regulation" adds reputational/regulatory tail risk. No material litigation has landed yet, but this is an emerging-liability exposure in the AI-safety era.
- **Dual-use / product misuse.** NVIDIA GPUs have been found in Russian and Iranian drone/weapons programs (Reuters/NYT reporting), tying product distribution into export-control enforcement — a social *and* governance issue.
- **Labour.** ~42,000 staff; industry-leading pay and historically very low attrition. However, a U.S. federal court allowed a class action to proceed alleging NVIDIA favored H-1B visa holders over qualified U.S. workers (discrimination suit) — a live legal overhang.
- **Supply chain labour.** Limited direct exposure as a fabless company; concentration risk where TSMC's contract-manufacturing labour practices (incl. Arizona construction disputes) are scrutinized.
- **Community/product safety.** Data-center water/power draw at customer sites increasingly becomes a community-relations issue aggregated to NVDA's brand.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **7/10**

**Strengths:** Single class of common stock (no dual-class entrenchment); board is independent except the CEO (all other directors independent per FY2025 annual review); investor-aligned, performance-based equity compensation (CEO ~$49.9M FY2025, heavily equity-linked); large shareholder returns via buybacks; founder leadership has delivered exceptional long-term value.

**Weaknesses:** Jensen Huang serves as **co-founder, CEO, and Chairman** (duality) with long tenure — succession planning is a genuine governance question at a $5.7T company; he occasionally makes unilateral strategic calls (e.g., public positions on chip exports to China that cut against U.S. policy, drawing lawmaker scrutiny). Antitrust and export-control controversies (below) are the governance overhangs most likely to be financially consequential. Anti-corruption/ethics programmes are robust for a US-listed large cap.

## 5. ESG Controversies (with financial impact estimates)

1. **H20 China export ban (April 2025) — $5.5B charge.** U.S. government restricted H20 sales to China; NVDA recorded up to ~$5.5B in Q1 FY2026 charges (inventory + purchase commitments). Sales partially resumed July 2025, but the episode reset China revenue expectations (~10–13% of revenue). **Material.**
2. **DOJ antitrust investigation (subpoenas, 2024–2025).** Probe into AI-GPU dominance: alleged bundling (NVLink/NVSwitch), supply-allocation coercion, ~110% pricing to customers buying rival chips, and retaliation against customers working with competitors. Remedies (conduct restrictions/divestitures) are a low-probability but high-impact tail. **Material.**
3. **U.S. lawmaker allegations over "close China ties" (WSJ, 2025).** Political risk to license-to-operate in Washington; could feed stricter export rules. **Moderate.**
4. **AI-bias staffer warnings (2023, surfaced Dec 2023).** Reputational; no financial charge. **Low-Moderate.**
5. **H-1B discrimination class action (allowed to proceed, 2025).** Potential damages exposure; small relative to balance sheet. **Low.**
6. **China antidumping probe (Jan 2025)** into NVIDIA-interconnect products following the Mellanox acquisition. **Low-Moderate.**

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **BIS export controls / AI Diffusion Rule (US):** the single most financially material regulatory vector — directly gated NVDA revenue in China and could tighten further.
- **EU AI Act:** NVDA's frontier models (Nemotron family) may fall under GPAI obligations; non-compliance fines up to 7% of global turnover. Also relevant to customers deploying NVDA compute (EcoDesign data-center rules).
- **EU CSRD:** NVDA's EU revenue appears to exceed thresholds for non-EU parent reporting; expect CSRD-mandated sustainability reporting for EU activities (FY2028/29 timeframe) — likely a disclosure-quality upgrade.
- **SEC climate disclosure:** stayed/being dismantled under the current administration — near-term US disclosure pressure receded (note: SEC has also run a climate/ESG enforcement task force; greenwashing risk remains).
- **CBAM:** negligible direct exposure (semiconductors not listed CBAM goods).
- **Antitrust:** U.S. DOJ, EU (post-Mellanox commitments), and Chinese regulators all have live hooks.
- **ESG-linked debt:** not material — NVDA carries no significant debt/ESG covenants.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating — **Average** (leaning positive on E; capped by G/S)

NVDA is an **environmental leader** (100% renewable electricity, SBTi targets, energy-efficiency as the core climate product) with **clean structural governance** — but scope 3 disclosure remains partial, AI-ethics governance is immature relative to product-level risk, a DOJ antitrust probe is live, and export-control-driven revenue volatility is the dominant ESG-linked financial risk. This combination is **Average** overall — not a Laggard, not yet a full Leader on an integrated basis.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication — **Neutral**

ESG factors are **second-order to the AI-demand thesis** and do not impair the investment case today. However, the material ESG exposures are real: (a) export-control regime shifts (already cost $5.5B in one quarter), (b) antitrust remedies, and (c) AI-regulation escalation. Environmental factors are *not* a binding constraint on NVDA's cash flows in the forecast window — the company's efficiency franchise is, if anything, an environmental tailwind. Watch items that could downgrade the view: DOJ action beyond subpoenas, fat-tailed AI-safety litigation reaching hardware enablers, and any scope-3/CSRD disclosure shortfalls that attract greenwashing claims.

**Key monitoring flags:** China export-license outcomes; DOJ/EU antitrust developments; FY2026 Sustainability Report Scope 3 audit scope; AI-safety legislation targeting model-training compute.
