# Sector Brief — JPMorgan Chase & Co. (JPM)

## 1. Sector Overview

JPM operates in **Financial Services** (industry: **Banks – Diversified**), the most heavily regulated, capital-intensive corner of the US economy. The money-center banking oligopoly — JPM, BAC, C, WFC, GS, MS — generated roughly **$1.0T of revenue (FMP basis)** in FY2025, with JPM alone contributing ~28% of that and posting the **most profitable year in US banking history ($57.0B net income)**. The industry is in the **mature phase**: revenue growth is low single digit and highly geared to the Fed funds rate, deposit pricing, and the credit cycle. The 2023 step-change in revenue across every peer is a data-presentation artefact (post-First Republic consolidation plus a change in how bank revenue is captured), so the clean comparison window is **2023–2025**.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Falling funding costs are supporting net interest income**: With Fed funds normalising toward a 3.50–3.75% range, the FDIC's 2026 Risk Review notes NIMs improved modestly as deposit funding costs declined — a margin-positive repricing that lags rate cuts.
- **Credit normalisation, not deterioration**: Fitch (3Q25) reports US bank credit costs stable-to-improving with **commercial real estate stabilising** (lower outstandings), reducing the provisioning drag that weighed on 2024–25 results.
- **Fee-income resilience and scale moat**: Record investment-banking/trading revenue, wealth-management inflows, and AI-driven efficiency (JPM targets ~$1.5B+ annual AI value) are compounding at the top of the oligopoly, while G-SIB charter and capital requirements keep new entrants at bay.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Net interest margin compression**: As the Fed cuts, loan-deposit spreads narrow (S&P Global's 2026 outlook expects higher NIMs to be "tempered by increased credit costs"), and sticky deposit betas mean funding costs don't fall as fast as asset yields.
- **Consumer credit stress is building**: Commercial-bank credit-card delinquency reached ~**2.92%** in Q1 2026; higher card net-charge-offs directly trimmed JPM's FY2025 net income (−2.4% YoY) even as revenue grew.
- **Regulatory and capital constraints**: Basel III endgame, G-SIB surcharges and pending bank-tax proposals cap ROE and force capital retention — a structural drag on returns that rewards only the most efficient operators.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Strong**

JPM is the clear quality operator of the sector. In FY2025 it grew revenue (+3.3%) while most peers contracted, and its **26.0% operating margin leads the peer set by 5–6 percentage points** (C, the laggard, manages just 11.8%). Its 2-yr revenue CAGR (~8.8%) beats all consumer-heavy peers (BAC, WFC, C) and trails only MS, whose wealth/markets mix is growing off a much smaller base. Market share within the Big-6 is **rising** (+0.9pts since 2023), the stock has **~doubled since Jan 2024** ($162→~$343) at a market beta of 0.98, and it is the US's largest bank by market cap (~$919B vs BAC at ~$402B). The one blemish: peer operating margins converged upward 2023→2025 (most peers +3 to +7pts, JPM roughly flat at 26%) as JPM's own net income slipped on card charge-offs — its lead is real but no longer widening.

## 6. Sector Rating: **Neutral**

The diversified-banking sector is a mature, slow-growth oligopoly facing a live NIM-compression and consumer-credit headwind in the current rate-cutting phase, with structural regulatory constraints on returns. It is not a structurally attractive growth sector on its own. **However**, JPM sits in the most attractive pocket of it — the G-SIB money-center oligopoly with pricing discipline, record profitability, and a widening share of a ~$1T revenue pool — and is executing at the top of that class. Rating reflects the sector's cycle position; company selection within it is the differentiator.
