---

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW** (with one medium sub-risk)

**Positives (verified):** SBTi-validated targets as of Oct 2026 — **net-zero across the value chain by 2045**, near-term **absolute Scope 1&2 reduction of 60% by 2034 from a 2024 base year**, classified **1.5°C-aligned**. This is a genuinely rigorous commitment (validated, absolute rather than intensity-based) and better than most large-cap pharma peers on target quality.

**Why low financial materiality:** no carbon-intensive heavy industry, no CBAM-covered goods, no material water-stress manufacturing concentration, limited EU ETS exposure.

**The one real E-financial linkage — stranded capacity:** Moderna built pandemic-scale manufacturing (MA, NC, Canada, Australia, UK, Switzerland, Spain) against ~$18bn of 2021–22 revenue. With revenue now a fraction of peak, fixed footprint creates **emissions and depreciation without matching revenue** — i.e., carbon intensity per dollar of revenue has risen sharply. Evidence: a **$238m charge on cancellation of a contract manufacturing agreement** and active R&D/manufacturing retrenchment. This is where E and financial risk actually converge: *idle capital is also idle carbon.*

**Controversies:** none material (no spills, fines, or remediation liabilities identified).

---

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **CRITICAL**

This is the dominant ESG factor for MRNA and the one most likely to determine the investment outcome.

**(a) Government/policy licence to operate — severely impaired**
- **May 2025:** HHS terminated Moderna's **$766m** award to develop a pandemic/avian (H5N1) influenza vaccine (~$590m remaining value).
- **Aug 2025:** HHS announced a coordinated **wind-down of mRNA vaccine development under BARDA**, cancelling a further ~$500m in mRNA project funding — explicitly framed around claims that "mRNA technology poses more risks." The objection is to the *platform*, not the program.
- **FDA declined to review Moderna's mRNA flu vaccine** despite late-stage trial data — a regulatory-access barrier, not a data barrier.
- Management response: CEO has publicly confirmed **cutting late-stage vaccine R&D** because US policy has turned against the platform.

**(b) Product safety & trust:** ongoing political and legal contestation of COVID-19 vaccine safety (ACIP reconstitution, a Covid-vaccine safety review panel). Partial relief: a federal court **temporarily blocked** several RFK Jr. HHS moves in 2026, and 15 states sued HHS over childhood-schedule changes — the political pendulum is litigable. But policy volatility itself is the risk: it raises the discount rate on any US vaccine revenue.

**(c) Labour:** workforce cut from ~5,800 to **below 5,000**, across multiple restructuring rounds. For an R&D-dependent company, attrition of specialised mRNA talent is a real asset impairment; severance and facility charges hit cash flow.

**(d) Access to medicines:** historically strong (lowest-tier pricing to Gavi countries, third-party audited). This is a genuine positive and a partial mitigant to reputational risk.

**Mitigant:** diversification away from US government vaccine revenue toward Merck-partnered oncology (intismeran autogene/V940) and ex-US markets reduces — but does not eliminate — concentrated policy exposure.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Positives**
- **Chair/CEO separation:** Noubar Afeyan (Chair) vs. Stéphane Bancel (CEO) — better than the combined-role norm.
- Board refresh: recent additions (e.g., ex-Biogen CFO McDonnell) improve financial and audit depth.
- **Litigation resolution is a governance win:** the March 2026 settlement of *all worldwide* LNP patent litigation with Genevant/Arbutus **removed a large uncertainty overhang** — shares rose ~9–10% on announcement. De-risking via settlement, rather than prolonged litigation, is shareholder-aligned behaviour.
- 2026 annual meeting: all four management proposals passed.

**Negatives**
- **Compensation alignment is the weakest link:** 2025 **say-on-pay support was only 77%** — well below the ~90%+ norm and inside the sub-80% "dissent" zone that proxy advisers flag. History is worse: ~$400m in stock/options to the CEO in 2022 and a golden-parachute package reportedly valued >$900m, awarded *after* the stock had begun its post-pandemic decline. Pay has not tracked delivered shareholder value.
- **Founder-controlled chair:** Afeyan is a co-founder and reported ~10% holder through Flagship Pioneering. Combined with the CEO's long tenure (since 2011), this concentrates influence and weakens genuine board challenge.
- Capital allocation discipline: simultaneous multibillion litigation payouts, R&D cuts, and capacity cancellations suggest reactive rather than pre-emptive capital planning.

**Unverified gaps:** exact board size/independence ratio, share-class structure (potential super-voting rights), and full comp metrics — proxy retrieval failed.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US (highest severity):** HHS/BARDA discretionary funding risk; FDA review discretion; ACIP recommendation volatility; state AG consumer-protection actions; PREP Act/contract-based liability shields. Notably, **SEC climate disclosure rule is immaterial** here (stayed, and MRNA's direct emissions are small).
- **EU (medium):** **CSRD/ESRS** disclosure obligation given established EU commercial and manufacturing presence — compliance cost is modest but the double-materiality assessment will force disclosure of exactly the vaccine-access and policy issues above. EU pharma reform (regulatory data exclusivity cuts) is a separate, larger revenue risk. HERA pandemic-preparedness contracts are a partial offset to lost US funding.
- **UK/Canada/Australia (low-medium):** MHRA and allied approvals for flu/RSV filings — relevant as ex-US diversification.
- **CBAM:** **negligible** direct exposure (no covered commodity imports); indirect only via glass vials, cold-chain equipment, and logistics costs.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified in available materials — no sustainability-linked margin ratchets. Positive: no debt-covenant ESG tripwires.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **ESG RISK**

Not for classic reasons (no environmental scandal, no labour abuse, no fraud). MRNA earns the lowest tier because it carries a **critical, concentrated, politically-driven licence-to-operate impairment** — a government that was its largest customer has deliberately withdrawn from its core technology platform, and its regulator has declined to review its lead near-term product. Environmental performance is genuinely strong and governance is mediocre-to-average, but neither is decisive. In materiality terms: **E is a non-issue, G is a 5/10, and S is thesis-relevant.**

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE**

**For the thesis, the ESG overlay is a headwind, and it is not a soft one — it maps directly onto revenue, cost of capital, and terminal value.**

1. **US vaccine revenue should be underwritten at a structurally lower multiple.** The mRNA wind-down, the $766m termination, and FDA's refusal to review the flu shot are not sentiment — they are removed cash flows and removed optionality. Any DCF assuming a normalised US respiratory franchise needs a policy-risk haircut and a higher discount rate.
2. **The $2.25bn settlement is a governance positive but a cash negative.** It clears the overhang (correctly cheered by the market) yet consumes roughly all near-term financial flexibility at a company still burning cash — constraining the R&D investment needed to rebuild the pipeline. Watch the **$1.3bn contingent tranche** as a live call on flu-combo success.
3. **The bull case has effectively migrated from E/S to pure clinical execution.** Value now rests on the Merck-partnered oncology programme and ex-US markets — areas with *lower* ESG/policy entanglement. That is strategically helpful: it reduces the materiality of the very factor that rates MRNA poorly.
4. **Governance is the swing factor to monitor.** A 77% say-on-pay and founder-concentrated chairmanship are tolerable *if* capital is deployed well; they become value-destructive if the board allows further reactive, large-scale cash commitments without evidence of R&D productivity.

**Bottom line:** ESG does not drive the MRNA call — clinical data and cash runway do. But the ESG analysis materially shapes it: it argues for **discounting US vaccine cash flows, avoiding valuation credit for platform optionality that policy has foreclosed, and requiring a governance credibility discount until pay and capital allocation track delivered value.** Rated **Negative** for the thesis on ESG grounds, driven almost entirely by Social/licence-to-operate rather than environmental or governance factors.

*Caveats: board independence, share-class structure, and precise carbon intensity unavailable due to SEC retrieval failure; carbon intensity inference is directional, not measured.*
