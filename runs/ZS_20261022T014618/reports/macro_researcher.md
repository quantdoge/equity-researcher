### 1. Macro Environment Summary (Current Stance)

**Transitional / Cautiously Risk-On.**

The current environment is characterized by easing inflation and a pivot toward monetary accommodation, leading to a cyclical shift toward "Cautiously Risk-On." While the Federal Reserve is maintaining a restrictive, but predictable, stance (hovering near 3.75%), the forward expectation of rate cuts is beginning to lift valuations for high-growth, long-duration assets like Zscaler. Growth is stable, though moderating.

The primary macroeconomic narrative is the transition from a high-inflation, high-rate tightening cycle to a moderate-growth, lower-rate normalization cycle. For ZS, this suggests that the highest hurdle—interest rate risk—is beginning to ease, which is crucial for a market-cap-heavy, forward-earnings-dependent SaaS business.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: Favourable

The current macroeconomic transition favors the technology/SaaS growth profile of Zscaler. The shift from persistent high inflation and restrictive rates to predictable, easing monetary conditions is a powerful macro tailwind for high-multiple software companies.

**Key Tailwinds:**
1.  **Increased Cybersecurity Mandates:** The market has demonstrated a significant structural shift, driven by AI-supercharged threats (as noted in recent news), forcing corporate spending toward platforms like ZS.
2.  **Rate Normalization:** The expected future easing of the Federal Funds Rate directly improves ZS's present valuation metrics.

**Key Headwinds:**
1.  **Regulatory and Legal Risk:** Ongoing legal scrutiny (investor alerts mentioned in news) introduces idiosyncratic risk that can be exacerbated by broader corporate caution resulting from economic uncertainty.
2.  **Cyclical Spending Slowdown:** If the moderate GDP growth trend decelerates unexpectedly, corporate IT budgeting—while defensive—could face cuts, hitting SaaS revenue growth.

### 5. Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**Bear Scenario: Stagflationary Persistence (The "Higher for Longer" Bet Fails)**

If inflation remains sticky at a higher level (e.g., 3-4%) than currently expected, and the Federal Reserve is forced to keep rates at the upper end of their current range (3.75%+) for an extended period due to persistent service sector inflation, this would be a severe negative surprise.

**Impact on ZS:**
*   **Valuation Collapse:** Sustained high rates severely penalize long-duration growth assets, forcing a drastic downward revision of ZS's expected future cash flows and leading to a significant multiple contraction.
*   **Capex Freeze:** Corporations facing high borrowing costs and uncertainty would aggressively defer non-essential capital expenditures, directly impacting new ZS contract signings.
