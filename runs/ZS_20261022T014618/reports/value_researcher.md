## ZS — Zscaler, Inc. | Value Investment Analysis
**Price $231.33 (close 21 Oct 2026) | Market cap $37.7B | EV ~$35.4B | Net debt $927M**

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: NARROW (widening in scope, contested in economics)

Zscaler is the pure-play leader in Security Service Edge (SSE) / Zero Trust. Traffic is routed through its own global enforcement-node footprint, and its inline proxy architecture was purpose-built in the cloud rather than retrofitted from firewall appliances.

**Evidence of a real moat:**
- **Switching costs (primary):** ZIA/ZPA sit inline in the traffic path. Ripping out a live security control plane across 8,700-employee-scale enterprises is operationally and politically painful. Net revenue retention has historically run ~115%, which is the single best quantitative proxy for stickiness.
- **Intangible assets:** FedRAMP/DoD certifications and 100+ patents are meaningful barriers in federal/public sector, one of Zscaler's largest verticals.
- **Efficient scale in latency:** A global peering-heavy data center footprint creates a performance advantage competitors must replicate in capex.
- **Platform expansion:** ZIA → ZPA → ZDX → data protection → workload segmentation → AI/MDR (Red Canary) is a genuine land-and-expand engine, evidenced by revenue compounding 32.4% from FY22 ($1.09B) to FY26 ($3.35B).

**Evidence against a wide moat:**
- **Gross margin has drifted *down*, not up:** 77.7% (FY22) → 76.8% (FY26). A widening moat should show pricing power, not ~90bps of cumulative compression. This is a quiet but important tell — cloud delivery costs and competitive pricing are eating at the edges.
- **Growth is decelerating hard:** 62% → 48% → 34% → 23% → 25% → **guided ~19% revenue growth for FY27** ($935–939M Q1 guide). ARR guidance of $4.40–4.43B still implies ~25%, so backlog is healthier than recognized revenue — but the trend line is unambiguous.
- **The bundlers are here:** Microsoft (Entra/Defender SSE), Palo Alto (Prisma Access), CrowdStrike and Fortinet are packaging comparable functionality into larger platform purchases. Zscaler's architectural lead is real but no longer unique, and "good enough + already in the stack" is the classic moat-eroder in enterprise software.

**Bottom line:** A genuine franchise with high switching costs and a large TAM, but not a Buffett-style impenetrable moat — and the evidence over five years is that it is being contested rather than fortified. Also worth stating plainly: **this sits at the edge of my circle of competence.** Cloud security architecture is understandable, but the durability of a 25%-growth software moat against Microsoft's bundling is not something I can underwrite with the certainty I require.

**What is genuinely good:** top-line consistency, a 77% gross margin that has held for five years, GAAP operating margin improving ~2,600bps since FY22, and FCF that has grown 2.5x in three years to $852M. The balance sheet carries $3.47B of cash and short-term investments against $1.86B of mostly convertible debt.

**What disqualifies it from a high score:**
1. **SBC-adjusted FCF is essentially zero.** $852M reported FCF includes an add-back of $822M of stock compensation. Strip SBC out — as an owner must, because it is real compensation paid in ownership — and FY26 "free" cash flow is **~$30M** (FY25: ~$66M). The tool's owner-earnings figure (NI + D&A − capex) is **−$148M**, negative in all four years.
2. **Negative ROIC for four consecutive years** (−11.1% → −3.0%). The hallmark of a moated business — ROIC consistently above WACC — is absent. This is the single most damning statistic in the file.
3. **Dilution:** diluted shares 140.9M → 160.2M over four years, +3.2%/yr. Plus $1.86B of convertible debt that converts into more shares if the stock works. Per-share value creation is materially weaker than enterprise value creation.
4. **Capex is rising faster than revenue** ($129M → $277M), which is precisely what triggered the May 2026 guidance reset.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples:** EV/Revenue **10.6x**; EV/EBITDA **190x**; P/FCF **43.5x**; P/B **14.3x**; FCF yield **2.3%**. GAAP P/E is not meaningful (loss-making). Peer median P/E is 45.3x — but Zscaler's loss-making status makes that comparison meaningless, and the "1,409% discount to peers" the screen reports is an artifact, not an opportunity.

**Intrinsic value — my own owner-earnings DCF (the tool's automated run is unusable):** the automated DCF returned ~$566/share, but it mechanically extrapolates **30% FCF growth for ten years** — a rate inconsistent with FY27 revenue guided at ~19%. Discounting a realistic path (FCF growing ~20% for five years, decelerating to 3% terminal, from an $852M base, net debt $927M, 160.2M shares):

| WACC | Implied value/share | vs. $231.33 |
|---|---|---|
| 8.0% | ~$262 | +13% upside |
| 9.0% | ~$228 | ~fair |
| 10.0% | ~$199 | −14% downside |

**Base case ≈ $200/share** (9.5–10% WACC, terminal growth 3%). That implies the stock trades at roughly a **15% premium to fair value** — the opposite of the margin of safety I require. My buy threshold, at a 25% discount to base-case IV, is **~$150**.

**What the price implies:** at 10.6x EV/sales, the market is capitalizing ~20%+ revenue growth for several years *and* meaningful FCF-margin expansion. To earn a 10% return from $231, Zscaler needs ARR to compound in the low-20s through 2031 with FCF margins climbing toward 30% — and SBC, the item that currently consumes all of FCF, to shrink sharply as a share of revenue. Possible; not underwritable at this price.

**Context matters:** the stock peaked near $336 in Nov 2025, collapsed to $114.63 on 10 Apr 2026 (macro/tech selloff) and again **−31.5% on 27 May 2026** when management cut FY26 FCF-margin guidance to 22.8–23.3% and flagged billings delays. It has since **doubled off the low** to $231. The mispricing created by that panic has already been arbitraged away. Today's buyer is paying up for a recovery that has largely happened. Consensus targets sit at $215–$285 (median ~$250, but the six-month median is ~$200), and D.A. Davidson initiated **Neutral at $215** — below the current price — on 20 Oct 2026.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Positives:** Founder-led (Jagtar Singh Chaudhry, co-founder/CTO-CEO) with deep domain expertise and a long-term platform vision; insiders reportedly hold ~49% of shares (worth ~$5B), which is exceptional alignment. No dividends and no buybacks — appropriate for a company still reinvesting for growth. The Oct 2026 investor day was the first in five years, and management reaffirmed FY27 guidance rather than resetting it.

**Concerns:**
- **Capital allocation has turned acquisitive at a high price.** Goodwill and intangibles jumped from $465M to $1.43B in FY26 — nearly $1B of M&A (Red Canary and others) — while net cash of $592M flipped to net debt of $927M. Paying up for MDR/AI assets during a growth scare is a classic empire-building risk signal that needs watching. Impairment risk is real.
- **Compensation discipline is poor from an owner's perspective.** SBC of $822M is 24.5% of revenue — the shareholder pays for growth twice: once in cash costs, once in dilution. Absolute SBC rose 85% in two years even as a % of revenue barely moved.
- **Insider activity is one-directional.** 21 insider transactions in the past 90 days, all sales (much of it RSU tax withholding, and CEO direct holdings are modest at ~350,700 shares). Routine 10b5-1 selling, but zero open-market buying at a stock that halved is not a ringing endorsement.
- **Guidance credibility took a hit** in May 2026 when FCF-margin guidance was cut materially mid-year. That is a yellow flag on forecasting discipline.

Verdict: **Competent operators, founder-aligned, but not yet proven disciplined capital allocators.** Grade: B−.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD / AVOID at $231** (watchlist; buy below ~$150–160)

This is a good business at a full price — arguably a demanding one. The franchise quality is real: 77% gross margins, ~25% growth, $3.35B revenue, entrenched inline architecture. But three facts prevent me from buying:

1. **No margin of safety.** Base-case intrinsic value ~$200 vs. a $231 price. I need a 20–30% discount; I am being offered a premium.
2. **No owner earnings.** After treating SBC as the real expense it is, Zscaler earns roughly nothing. Paying 43x reported FCF — or ~1,200x economic FCF — for a business with negative ROIC in four straight years is not value investing; it is momentum investing with extra steps.
3. **Growth is decelerating into a more competitive market.** FY27 guided at ~19% revenue growth, with Microsoft and Palo Alto bundling the category.

**What would change my mind:** (a) a price near $150 or below, or (b) SBC falling below ~15% of revenue with FCF margin (post-SBC) above 10% and ROIC turning positive — i.e., evidence the moat converts into owner returns, not just revenue. Until then this is a fine company to admire and a poor one to own at this quote. **Strong Sell is not warranted** — the business is not impaired and the balance sheet is sound; the problem is purely price and capital efficiency.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **SBC/dilution risk (highest):** $822M annually, ~3.2%/yr share growth plus $1.86B convertible overhang. If SBC stays at ~25% of revenue, per-share economics never materialize and reported FCF is an illusion.
2. **Competitive bundling:** Microsoft, Palo Alto, CrowdStrike, Netskope packaging SSE into broader platforms; "good enough and already paid for" is the classic displacement mechanism. Gross-margin drift already hints at it.
3. **Growth deceleration:** revenue guided ~19% for FY27. At mid-teens growth, a 10.6x EV/sales multiple compresses violently — the May 2026 −31.5% day is the template.
4. **Capex/AI infrastructure:** FY26 capex $277M and rising; the FCF-margin guidance cut to ~23% reflects this. Gross margin could compress further.
5. **M&A integration and impairment:** ~$1B of FY26 acquisitions, goodwill now $1.43B against $2.60B of equity — a large, unproven bet.
6. **Public-sector/federal budget exposure:** meaningful government revenue concentration, subject to procurement and budget cycles.
7. **Key-man risk:** founder-CEO concentration in a technically complex platform.
8. **Multiple risk / de-rating:** even after a 65% peak-to-trough drawdown and a 100% rebound, valuation still embeds near-flawless execution. Any billings delay is punished asymmetrically.
9. **Circle-of-competence risk:** I cannot underwrite the durability of a 25%-growth software moat against Microsoft's bundling with Buffett-grade certainty. When I am unsure, I pass — and there is no penalty for passing on a fairly-priced wonderful company.

---

**Summary:** Wide-ish ambition, narrow-ish moat, excellent gross-level economics, but **negative ROIC, ~zero SBC-adjusted free cash flow, decelerating growth, and a price ~15% above base-case intrinsic value.** A quality business — not a value investment at $231.
