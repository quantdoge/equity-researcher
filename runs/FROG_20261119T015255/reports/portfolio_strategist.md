## 2. Consensus Assessment: **HIGH on diagnosis, LOW on direction**

This is the rare case where 11 agents with opposite conclusions agree on every underlying fact. That agreement is the signal.

**Consensus (10 of 11 agents):**
1. **Business momentum is real and accelerating** — growth, alt data, sector, fin risk, short, ESG, quant (cash flow) all concur. No one disputes the 30.9% quarter, the 124% NDR, or the operating-leverage inflection.
2. **Accounting and solvency are clean.** The short analyst — whose job is to find fraud — states plainly there is none. Fin risk confirms zero funded debt. **Fraud/solvency risk is off the table.**
3. **There is no margin of safety at $114.** Even the two bullish agents (growth, alt data) say so unprompted. Value, quant, short, and macro quantify it at 30–60%.
4. **Valuation risk is duration risk.** Macro, quant, and fin risk independently identify the same transmission channel: a ~20x-sales, 0.33%-SBC-adjusted-FCF-yield asset is the highest-torque expression of a rising discount rate.

**Genuinely contested:**
- **Durability of the AI consumption tailwind past FY27.** Sector sees structural (regulation + AI artifact volume); macro sees a possible consumption pull-forward that reverses when customers optimise AI spend. Watch Q4'26.
- **Is the $45 intrinsic value realistic?** Value's DCF capitalises SBC at full cost and gets $45; short's multiple-based anchor gets $80–95; sell-side gets $114–125. **A 2.7x spread across fair-value estimates is itself a sizing input — wide dispersion demands a smaller position.**

**Conflict resolution:** The Growth-BUY vs Value/Short-SELL disagreement is not a factual conflict — it is a *horizon* conflict. Same facts, different holding period. My job is not to adjudicate who is right but to size a position that survives being wrong on the path while keeping exposure to the business. **The fraud-risk precedence rule in my framework does not trigger here** — the closest analogue, the product-security risk (CVE-2026-82329), is real but is a *monitorable operational* risk, not a thesis-killer, and the market has already tested it (stock drifted 13% then recovered to new highs).

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **0.75% initial (target 1.25%, hard cap 2.0%)**

**Why not 3–5% (the "high conviction" band)?** Because conviction here is *asymmetric within the stack*: my read on the business is high, my read on the price is strongly negative. Those do not net to "high conviction long." A 4% position in an 88%-vol name contributes ~3.5% of portfolio volatility from a single stock — indistinguishable from a 17% position in a core holding. That is not portfolio construction, it is a factor bet.

**Why not 0% (PASS)?** Two reasons. First, opportunity cost is real: this is the only name in the peer set with *both* accelerating growth and ~1,600bp of operating-margin expansion, 7/7 beats, and clean books. Second, a small live position creates the operational discipline (monitoring, pre-committed adds, tripwires) that a watchlist item never gets.

**Risk budget check:** at 0.75%, a −50% drawdown costs **37bp** of portfolio. At the 2.0% cap, it costs 100bp — a real but survivable hit. Unacceptable would be 4% (200bp from one name).
