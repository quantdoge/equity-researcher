**Google Trends — "JFrog" (US, 12-mo):** Strong mid-year 2026 surge (peaked at 100 the week of Jun 14), then normalized. Recent weeks: 44 (Nov 1) → 38 (Nov 8). Off peak but stabilizing, with a modest uptick from September lows (~36–38). **Direction: neutral-to-slightly-positive vs. Sept, but clearly off the June hype peak.**

**Google Trends — "Artifactory" (US, 12-mo):** Corroborating product-level term. Peaked with the same June spike (100), then decayed to 22 by Nov 1–8 — down from ~30–35 mid-year and ~44 in March. **Direction: soft / decelerating.** Product-specific search intent is cooling even as the corporate brand stabilizes.

The June spike looks like an AI-coding news-cycle pop rather than a durable step-up in organic demand. Note the mismatch: consumption-based revenue (per Barron's, AI coding was the Q3 growth driver) is running ahead of public search interest, suggesting revenue is coming from existing/expanding accounts rather than new top-of-funnel interest.

### Data Limitations & Lag
- **Google Trends** is a weak leading indicator for enterprise/dev software — reflects hype, not seat consumption.
- **Job-posting and web-traffic** figures are search-proxy estimates, not Thinknum/SimilarWeb API counts; treat as directional only.
- **Patent feed failed** (2 attempts) — no signal.
- **Social sentiment unquantified** — no usable feed.
- **13F data is lagged up to one quarter** and predates the November re-rating.
- Alt data is a *leading* indicator, not definitive; the revenue beat is a lagging confirmation of what AI-coding usage already implied.

**Bottom line:** Alt data supports **above-consensus Q4 execution and continued fundamental momentum**, but the demand-side leading signals have cooled from peak and the valuation debate (not the demand debate) is the live risk into the Feb 11 print.
