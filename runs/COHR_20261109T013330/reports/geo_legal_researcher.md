## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — China critical-materials retaliation (gallium, germanium, rare earths, InP/GaAs feedstock)**
Beijing has imposed export controls on gallium, rare earths and at least seven other materials between 2023 and 2026 (CFR; S&P Global), and supplies >90% of global primary gallium (Geopolitechs). Coherent's 6-inch indium phosphide ramp is the core of its AI transceiver story (InP laser output +80% y/y in the June 2026 quarter) and depends on constrained III-V inputs and China-sourced rare-earth/noble-gas materials — a risk the company itself flags in its 10-K summary.
- **Probability:** 40–50% of a material licensing delay/disruption over the next 12–18 months (a partial mitigant: China "paused" enhanced dual-use licensing on gallium/germanium per Clark Hill, 2026).
- **Impact:** Medium–High. A two-quarter interruption to InP/optics output would hit the highest-growth, highest-margin line (Datacenter & Communications = **79% of Q4 FY2026 revenue**, $5.27B FY2026, +40%).

**Risk 2 — US–China export controls and tariff architecture**
Coherent is squarely in the crosshairs of dual-use photonics controls and the Administration's Section 232 investigations into semiconductors/optical imports (referenced expressly in the FY2026 10-K). Management has said tariffs and export restrictions have not been material to date (Q3 FY2026 10-Q), but the Oct/Nov-2026 environment is unstable: Washington extended (to Jan 10) the suspension of an expanded blacklist rule on Chinese firms' overseas units ahead of Shenzhen talks, while Beijing protests new US sanctions (Nikkei; SCMP; Global Times). Post-midterm, Democrats plan legislation to reassert Congressional authority over tariffs (CNBC, 2026-11-08) — direction of travel is toward *more* trade-policy churn, not less.
- **Probability:** High (80%+) that tariff/export-control rules affecting COHR change again within 12 months; Medium (~25%) of a specific license requirement disrupting a China or third-country customer relationship.
- **Impact:** Low–Medium. ~8–10% direct China revenue plus rerouting costs; the bigger vector is *customer* qualification delays for transceivers built outside the US.

**Risk 3 — Policy-driven AI capex shock (demand-side geopolitical risk)**
COHR's fate is now tied to hyperscaler AI buildout: FY2026 guidance implies >60% FY2027 growth, a **~$20B five-year PhotonLink anchor agreement** with a leading AI datacenter customer, and a target of a $3B quarter by end-FY2027. The Nov-2026 US election produced a Democratic Congress and state-level resistance to data centers; the AI trade sold off sharply (COHR −14% on Nov 5, −43% in a month at one point). Renewed export controls that remove China demand from Nvidia et al. indirectly remove optical attach demand.
- **Probability:** Medium (30–40%) of a ≥15% cut to consensus FY2027-28 revenue from a policy/political AI-capex pause.
- **Impact:** High (this is the dominant driver of the equity, far exceeding any direct tariff cost).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities litigation (active, moderate):** Coherent's stock fell 19.6% to $91.65 on **August 14, 2025**, triggering securities-fraud investigations and a class action by Rosen Law, with parallel inquiries from Pomerantz and Levi & Korsinsky. Raised again by the Oct–Nov 2026 drawdown, further plaintiff-firm activity is likely. Expected cost: modest relative to a $38B market cap; reputational/disclosure-control signal is the real issue. No pleaded fraud with a quantified claim is disclosed in the FY2026 filings.
- **Ordinary-course commercial litigation:** Justia dockets show routine product/commercial suits naming Coherent entities (e.g., *Coherent Global, Inc.*, S.D. Iowa, filed May 2026). The FY2026 10-K states legal proceedings are not expected to materially affect the Company.
- **No disclosed DOJ/SEC enforcement, FCPA matter, or BIS penalty** in the filings reviewed. That is a clean record for a company with this Asian manufacturing and sales footprint — but it also means compliance-program quality is not externally validated.
- **Regulatory subsidies:** a **$50M CHIPS Act Letter of Intent** (plus $20M local support) for US datacenter-photonics capacity expansion creates conditions-compliance, audit, and clawback exposure, and pulls COHR into national-security-linked facility obligations.
- **Export licensing:** dual-use laser/photonics exports are license-sensitive; the 10-K flags export restrictions and tariffs among principal regulatory risks.
- **Data privacy:** GDPR/UK-GDPR exposure via German, Swedish, UK and Finnish operations; low materiality.
- **Antitrust/competition:** the pending Marvell–Lumentum combination changes the competitive field rather than creating COHR antitrust exposure.

*(Data gaps, stated honestly: no direct read of the FY2026 10-K Item 1A or Item 3 text — my filing tool returned only an Exhibit 31.01 certification, and sec.gov returned HTTP 403; no FCPA/AML docket search available.)*

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **China nexus + dual-use photonics** — highest-severity flag. InP/GaAs lasers and transceivers have military/dual-use adjacency; China-based manufacturing (Fuzhou) plus China sales create diversion, re-export and end-use (BIS Entity List / military end-user) risk that requires best-in-class screening. Watch for any voluntary self-disclosure language in future 10-Qs.
2. **Section 232 / tariff classification** — optical transceivers and laser components cross multiple tariff lines; retroactive duty assessments and first-sale valuation are live audit areas.
3. **Conflict minerals / UFLPA** — tantalum, tungsten, tin, gold and rare-earth inputs from DRC/China require diligence; a forced-detention action at a supplier would be a supply shock.
4. **CHIPS Act award conditions** — national-security agreements typically carry "expansion of foreign production" restrictions that can constrain China capacity decisions.
5. **No FCPA/AML red flag identified** — direct sales model with in-house teams limits third-party intermediary risk, a genuine positive.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Revenue is majority-US and majority-AI-datacenter, direct China revenue is under 10%, and the litigation docket is limited to securities-investigation noise and routine commercial suits — all supportive of a Medium (not High) rating. The offsetting drivers are (a) disproportionate *operational* exposure to Asia, with most employees and key III-V fabs outside the US, (b) dependence on Chinese gallium/germanium/rare-earth inputs at exactly the moment those become coercive instruments, and (c) a demand base now hostage to a highly politicised AI capex cycle. **Escalation triggers to High:** any BIS license denial or entity-list action touching a COHR customer, a Chinese export-licence interruption to InP feedstock, or a formal securities class action with a credible revenue-recognition theory around the PhotonLink/guidance disclosure.
