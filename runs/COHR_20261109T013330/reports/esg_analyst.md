## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW–MEDIUM**

- **Disclosed performance (FY2024):** 78% of global electricity from renewables (>550 million kWh/yr), avoiding ~234,000 tCO₂e. Implies total electricity load ~705 GWh.
- **Target:** net-zero **Scope 1+2 by FY2040**, stated as 1.5°C-aligned (first goal announced 2023).
- **Estimated intensity:** Scope 1+2 on the order of **250–350 kt CO₂e** (derived, not disclosed to me), i.e. roughly **35–50 tCO₂e per $1M revenue** — low in absolute terms for a manufacturer and de minimis against ~$7B revenue. *Analyst caveat: derived figure, not company-confirmed.*
- **The real gap: Scope 3.** No quantified Scope 3 reduction target was located. For a company whose customers (NVIDIA, hyperscalers) are themselves under Scope 3 pressure, this is the binding constraint — and it is precisely the area where COHR's own value-chain emissions (upstream materials, Asia contract manufacturing) sit.
- **Positives:** Siemens-assisted GHG accounting methodology document (third-party methodology support), RBA membership since 2024, no CDP/SBTi-validation evidence found. **No SBTi-validated target = a credibility discount** vs peers claiming validated 1.5°C pathways.
- **Contamination/legacy:** No PFAS-style liability. Violation Tracker shows environment-related penalties of only **$60,500 across 5 records since 2000** (NJ-ENV hazardous waste $7,500; water pollution $13,500; CT-ENV $27,000). These are rounding errors.
- **Physical climate:** Fab concentration in Pennsylvania/Texas plus Asian coastal sites; Texas water stress and Asian typhoon/flood exposure affect capacity, not P&L directly.

**Verdict: low environmental risk, but a disclosure/Scope-3 maturity gap that will show up in customer scorecards before it shows up in fines.**

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Safety is the recurring theme.** Violation Tracker: **5 OSHA workplace-safety records totalling ~$94,019 since 2000**, including a **2025 OSHA citation ($18,916)**, 2023 ($11,249) and 2022 ($36,358). Small dollar amounts, but the *frequency* (three citations in four years) is a governance signal for a company handling toxic hydrides and Class-4 lasers. The company nonetheless claims Sapphire-level "Certification Champion" recognition from the Board of Safety Professionals (self-reported, third-party certification not independently verified).
- **Workforce scale and geography:** 51,478 employees with substantial manufacturing in China, Vietnam, Malaysia, Thailand and the Philippines — high forced-labour and audit intensity jurisdictions. RBA membership (2024) is a genuine positive step but is a commitment, not an outcome; no audit-scorecard disclosure found.
- **Human capital execution risk (most underrated item):** the AI-optics ramp requires adding manufacturing headcount at extraordinary speed while integrating prior acquisitions. In this sector, labour disputes, attrition of process engineers, or a site shutdown are direct revenue risks against a fully-booked FY2027 book and a $20B five-year customer agreement.
- **Product safety/quality:** optical modules have no consumer-safety dimension; quality escapes are a commercial, not ESG, issue.
- **No recent social controversy** found in the 12-month news scan (dominant coverage is AI-demand and valuation, not ESG).

**Verdict: medium — no crisis, but safety-citation cadence plus Asian manufacturing footprint means S is the pillar most capable of producing a nasty surprise.**

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6 / 10**

Supporting the score:
- **Independent non-executive Chair.** The 2026 proxy excerpt notes a director who "served as Lead Independent Director from 2022 until his election as Board Chair" — i.e. **no CEO/Chairman duality** with Jim Anderson. This is above-market for a US mid/large cap.
- **Single class of common stock**; no dual-class supervoting structure or poison pill identified. One-share-one-vote holds.
- **CEO transition done properly:** external CEO hire (Anderson, June 2024) with board seat, succeeding Vincent Mattera who retired as Chair & CEO — a clean generational handover rather than a founder-entrenchment pattern.
- **Board refresh post-II-VI/Coherent merger**, with directors holding other public-company audit experience (proxy cross-references show COHR directors serving on other boards).

Dragging the score down:
- **Simultaneous CEO and CFO turnover (2023–24)** removes two of the three most important control voices at once — a classic window for accounting-estimate and disclosure slippage.
- **Capital allocation:** the ~$7B 2022 Coherent acquisition loaded the balance sheet with goodwill and integration complexity; a **~$2B FY2026 equity issuance including strategic (NVIDIA) participation** materially dilutes holders (one sell-side account cites ~26% dilution — *unverified, treat as directional*). Strategic-customer equity stakes create a related-party dynamic on pricing and capacity allocation that deserves scrutiny.
- **Compensation alignment unconfirmed in detail:** TSR-weighted PSU structure could not be verified from primary text (DEF 14A fetch blocked). Given the stock's ~50% drawdown from highs and a >60% growth guide, I would want to confirm whether PSU metrics are revenue/EPS (which reward the capex ramp) or ROIC/FCF (which reward the *quality* of it). Unverified — a genuine gap in this analysis.
- **No controlling shareholder** and no evidence of a shareholder-rights flashpoint.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**ESG is not a thesis driver for COHR in either direction — and investors should not pay for a "green premium" here.**

What matters for the investment case:
1. **The ESG risk that can actually move the stock is social/operational, not environmental.** A site-level safety event, a forced-labour finding in the Asian supply chain, or a hyperscaler audit failure would hit *shipments* into a fully-booked FY2027 book and a $20B five-year agreement — that is a revenue-timing risk of hundreds of millions, versus a lifetime environmental penalty record of ~$203k.
2. **Governance is the pillar to watch, not E.** Simultaneous CEO/CFO turnover, a ~$2B dilutive raise with a strategic customer on the register, and a stock down ~50% from highs make compensation design (ROIC/FCF vs. revenue-growth PSUs) the key question: does management get paid for *building* capacity or for *earning a return on* it? **This is unresolved in the evidence I could access and should be confirmed from the proxy's CD&A before sizing the position.**
3. **The Scope 3 gap is a slow-burn commercial risk.** COHR's largest customers are the most Scope-3-constrained companies on earth. A missing quantified Scope 3 target is a procurement-scorecard deduction waiting to happen in 2027–2029, and it is cheap for management to fix.
4. **No exclusion risk.** COHR will not be screened out of mainstream ESG funds; single-class shares and an independent chair keep it out of the worst governance buckets.

**Bottom line:** ESG neither supports nor undermines the AI-optics thesis. Treat it as a **tail-risk monitor**, not a valuation input. Flag for the investment committee: (a) obtain the 2026 proxy CD&A to verify PSU metrics and dilution authority; (b) monitor OSHA/RBA audit disclosures as a leading indicator on the capacity ramp; (c) pressure-test customer concentration against supply-chain compliance covenants. **Neutral.**
