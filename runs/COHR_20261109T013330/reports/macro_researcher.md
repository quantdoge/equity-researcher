## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-Off / Transitional (fragile risk-on surface)**

The defining feature of this cycle is a **policy divergence that has turned hostile to long-duration growth equities**: the Fed has stopped easing and may be reversing, yet the long end of the curve is backing up hard. The Fed cut from 4.33% (Aug 2025) to 3.63% (Jun–Aug 2026), but the effective rate has since **risen to 3.88% (Oct 2026)** — a +25bp tightening impulse. Meanwhile the 10Y has climbed from ~4.45% in late June to **5.13%**, and the 30Y sits at **5.47%**. That is a textbook **bear steepening driven by term premium, fiscal supply and sticky inflation — not by growth optimism.**

Inflation is the culprit. **Headline CPI is running 3.72% YoY (Sep 2026) with a ~4.5% three-month annualised run-rate**, re-accelerating from 2.70% in Nov 2025. Core PCE is 3.12% YoY and has stalled at ~2.9% on a 3- and 6-month annualised basis — stuck roughly 100bp above target.

Growth itself is fine: **real GDP +3.4% annualised in Q3 2026 (+2.1% YoY)**, unemployment 4.1%. This is not a recession watch. It is an **inflation-and-rates problem layered on a solid economy** — the worst possible mix for a 40x P/E, high-beta AI hardware name.

Credit is the tell on complacency: **HY OAS at 3.48%** is historically very tight, but it has **widened 83bp since 1 September (2.65%)**. Liquidity is still open but deteriorating at the margin.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

**Rationale:** The two macro variables that dominate COHR's valuation — the long-end yield and the trajectory of hyperscaler capex — are both moving against it. Yields are at cycle highs with core inflation stuck ~110bp above target and the 2Y pricing further hikes; simultaneously, the marginal hyperscaler is trimming capex guidance. A 40x multiple, 2.23-beta stock cannot absorb a bear steepener and a capex deceleration at once, which is precisely what the tape shows (−55% peak-to-trough).

**Important qualification:** The rating is **not** "sell." COHR's *earnings* are accelerating (+49.9% YoY) and its *balance sheet* is de-risked (0.7x leverage, BB). The bear case is entirely a **valuation/multiple** case, and the FCC China ban is a genuine, large, non-consensus positive that could override macro. **The macro is a headwind on the multiple, not on the business.**

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

### Bear (base-case-underweighted): **"Inflation forces the Fed to hike into a capex slowdown"**

**Trigger chain:** Core PCE holds at ~3.0%+ through Q4'26 → the 2Y (already 4.74%, +86bp over EFFR) is proven right → the Fed delivers +50bp of hikes in H1'27 → 10Y breaks 5.50% and tests **6.0%** as term premium and fiscal supply compound.

**Why it surprises:** Consensus is anchored on "Fed cutting, AI supercycle intact." HY spreads at 3.48% show zero fear. The market is priced for a benign disinflation that the three-month CPI run-rate (4.5%) does not support.

**Transmission to COHR:** (i) Required return moves 8%→10%, compressing the growth multiple from ~22x to ~15x = **−30% to −35% on valuation alone**; (ii) hyperscalers, facing higher debt costs, trim 2027 capex a further 5–10%, cutting COHR revenue estimates mid-single-digits. Combined, a re-test of **$130–150** (roughly the Nov 2025 base) is arithmetically coherent — another 20–30% downside from $192.81. Note the stock has *already* demonstrated this elasticity, falling ~50% in five weeks on a $5B Microsoft capex trim.

**The symmetric bull surprise worth hedging against:** formalisation of the FCC ban on Innolight/Eoptolink (51% combined share) plus a resolution of the capex-guidance scare could drive 15–20% revenue upside and a violent re-rating toward the $394–440 Street targets. This is a **policy event, not a macro event** — it will not be signalled by FRED data, and it is the single largest risk to a bearish macro stance on COHR.
