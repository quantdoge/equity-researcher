# COHR — Portfolio Construction Recommendation
**Coherent Corp. | NYSE: COHR | Price $192.81 | Mkt cap $37.67B | EV $39.22B | Next print: 3 Feb 2027**

## Synthesis Judgment (up front)

The 11 reports do **not** disagree about the business. They disagree about the **equity as an instrument**. Every agent with a fundamentals mandate — Sector, Growth, Alt Data, even the Short analyst in his own bear-case rebuttal — confirms accelerating revenue (+49.9% YoY), expanding gross margin (30.9%→39.1% over seven quarters), a 100% beat rate, and a physical moat in InP capacity. No agent alleges fraud. The Short analyst's manipulation hypothesis *fails on the evidence* (GMI 0.944, DSO flat).

What the risk agents are actually saying is different and more serious: **the multiple is the asset, and the multiple is under attack from outside the company** (Macro: 10Y 5.13%, core PCE 3.12%, hyperscaler capex trims) **and from inside the cash flow statement** (Fin Risk / Short: OCF $80M against $805M net income; inventory days 140→212; ~30% dilution). Neither of those is a reason to avoid the name. Both are reasons to **size it like a 95%-volatility instrument rather than a 20%-growth compounder** — which is what the growth camp's "$400 target" framing implicitly does not do.

Net weighted conviction: **positive but second-tier.** This is a *satellite growth position*, not a core holding.

## 2. Consensus Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**High agreement (5 agents) — the operating business:**
Sector, Growth, Alt Data, Fin Risk and Short all independently confirm the same operating facts: revenue re-accelerating, gross margin expanding for four consecutive quarters, InP capacity as the binding industry constraint, and a legitimately de-risked balance sheet (0.99x net leverage, no maturities until 2029). This is about as clean a fundamental consensus as this framework produces.

**Genuine split (4 agents) — the equity:**
Growth says 19.7x FY27E is "too cheap." Quant says Sharpe 0.24 with negative alpha is "not compensated." These are not contradictory — they are answering different questions. Growth is pricing the *earnings path*; Quant is pricing the *distribution of returns*. At 95.6% annualized volatility, both can be right simultaneously.

**Conflict resolved — Macro vs Growth:**
The decisive tension is Macro's discount-rate case (−18% to −30% fair value from a 100bp required-return shift) against Growth's earnings case (+51% revenue, +75% EPS). **Neither overrides the other; they compound.** The resolution is that the earnings case wins over 24 months and the discount-rate case wins over 3 months. That mandates scale-in entry and a cash-flow gate, not a pass and not a full position.

**Conflict resolved — Nonfin/Geo risk precedence:**
Per framework rule, a live risk flag outranks a bullish fundamental view when it can permanently impair capital. Here it can't: the InP chokepoint and class action are **timing risks against a fully-booked order book**, not solvency or fraud risks. So they *reduce* size, they do not veto.

**The one that should genuinely worry the committee:** nobody in the pack can tell you whether Q2 FY27 operating cash flow turns positive. That single line item is the fulcrum between "cheap cyclical" and "EPS air-pocket," and it is 85 days away.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **1.5% initiate → 3.0% maximum**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Initial position** | **1.5% of portfolio** (75% of it at market; 25% held for the Feb 3 gate) |
| **Maximum position** | **3.0%** — hard cap, no exceptions |
| **Framework bucket** | *Medium-to-elevated risk, medium-high conviction* → normally 1–3%; capped at the top of that band rather than the 3–5% high-conviction band |
| **Kelly check** | Full Kelly on my scenario weights (55% up +55%, 45% down −30%) implies **~30%** of capital. Half-Kelly = 15%. **I am running at one-fifth of half-Kelly.** |

**Why the Kelly number is discarded, deliberately.** Kelly assumes a known distribution; COHR's distribution is fat-tailed and regime-dependent. A 15% position in a 95.6%-vol, 3.46-beta stock contributes ~14% portfolio volatility on its own and would dominate every other risk decision in the book. The binding constraint is not edge — it is that **a demonstrated 60% peak-to-trough drawdown at 3% position size costs 1.8% of portfolio, which is survivable; at 15% it costs 9%, which is a career event.**

**Why not smaller (0.5–1%)?** Because the edge is real and cross-confirmed: 7/7 beats, expanding margins, a competitor removed for 18+ months by the Marvell–Lumentum deal, and a stock at 22% of its 52-week range on *improving* fundamentals. Under-sizing a verified dislocation is its own error.

**Sizing is the primary risk control here. The stop-loss is secondary.**

## 6. Risk Management Rules

**A. Hard price stop: weekly close below $148 (−23% from entry).**
Deliberately set below the $160.23 capitulation low and at the top of Macro's $130–150 bear-case band. **Use a weekly close, not intraday** — at 6.0% daily sigma, an intraday stop in this name is a donation to the market. *Note the honest limitation:* a 23% stop is only ~3.8 daily sigmas and can be hit on no news. This is why sizing (3% cap ⇒ a full 50% drawdown costs 1.5% of portfolio) is the actual risk control and the stop is a backstop.

**B. Thesis invalidation — exit on any one of these, regardless of price:**
1. **Cash flow** — Q2 FY27 (3 Feb 2027) operating cash flow still negative **and** inventory above $4.0B **and** revenue growth decelerating below 40%. → **Exit fully.** This is the Short analyst's own upgrade-to-Short trigger.
2. **Gross margin** declines sequentially for two consecutive quarters. Breaks the pricing-power thesis that Sector, Growth and Short all rest on.
3. **FY27 pro forma growth guide cut below 40%** (from >60% today).
4. **InP supply** — any BIS licence denial, or a Chinese export-licence interruption touching InP/gallium/germanium feedstock. → **Halve immediately** (Geo's escalation trigger to HIGH).
5. **Another dilutive equity raise** at current prices. The ~30% dilution across five quarters is already the strongest governance criticism in the pack; a further raise at $193 would confirm the equity is funding the capex program.
6. **Customer concentration event** — a PhotonLink termination, repricing, or volume-flex invocation.

**C. Trim triggers:**
- **First trim (−25% of position) at $330** — ~22x FY28E EPS of $15.01, and ~82% of mean analyst target.
- **Second trim (−40% of remaining) at $390–405** — mean target; **≈90–100% of the only intrinsic-value reference available.** With the Value Researcher's report missing, I am unwilling to hold through the sell-side's own ceiling.

- **Position cap enforcement:** if appreciation carries COHR above **3.5%** of portfolio, trim mechanically back to 3.0%.

**D. Portfolio-level monitor:** if any of LITE / FN / CIEN / MTSI is added later, **reduce COHR pro rata** — the 0.75–0.83 correlations make those positions substitutes, not complements.
