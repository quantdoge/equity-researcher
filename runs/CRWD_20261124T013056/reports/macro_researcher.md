## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Risk-Off, driven by a rates/inflation shock rather than a growth shock.**

The US economy is in a re-acceleration regime, not a slowdown. Real GDP grew 2.07% YoY in Q3 2026 (3.4% annualized QoQ), and unemployment has *fallen* to 4.1% from 4.5% a year ago. That strength is precisely the problem: CPI inflation has re-accelerated to **3.82% YoY**, up from 2.70% in November 2025, and the last three months annualize to **~5.7%**. A hot November 10 inflation report broke the back of the easing narrative.

The Fed cut from 5.33% (2024) to 3.50–3.75% by January 2026, then **reversed and hiked 25bp in September 2026** to the current **3.75–4.00%** target, holding in October with officials openly discussing further hikes. The bond market has repriced violently: the 2Y is at **4.74%**, roughly 87bp *above* the policy midpoint, implying the market prices ~3 more hikes. The 10Y peaked at 5.31% on October 5 and sits at ~4.92%, with the 30Y at **5.47%**. The curve has bear-steepened to +39bp — a term-premium/fiscal-supply story, not a growth signal.

The equity market has cracked. The S&P 500 is **-11.6% from its October 6 peak** (7,818.93 → 6,910.39), including **-8.6% in the single week to November 20**, with VIX spiking from 19.3 to **35.9**. Critically, credit is *not* confirming: HY OAS at **3.76%** has widened 65bp since October 9 but remains historically tight. This is an equity-duration and risk-appetite event, not a solvency event. The dollar has also rallied hard — the broad index is up **4.2% since September 1**.

**The one-line macro:** strong growth + re-accelerating inflation + a hawkish Fed + a 5%+ long bond + a 36 VIX = the single worst configuration for a 35x-ARR long-duration software asset.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates / duration — the dominant risk (NEGATIVE).**
CRWD trades at **~34.6x ARR** ($214B market cap / ~$6.2B ARR). For an asset whose value is overwhelmingly in 2028+ terminal cash flows, the discount rate *is* the valuation. Moving the risk-free rate from 4.4% to 5.0% on a 35x-multiple asset compresses fair value by roughly **20–25%** mechanically. Two mitigants: (a) CRWD historically carries a net-cash balance sheet with no meaningful refinancing wall, so this is purely a valuation-channel exposure, not a solvency one; (b) the recent drawdown is *not* a rates story — the 10Y **fell** 39bp from October 5 to November 20 while CRWD fell 20%. That leg was equity risk appetite plus the Zscaler deal. Still, the 30Y at 5.47% now exceeds the S&P's earnings yield — a negative equity risk premium that structurally caps multiple expansion for exactly this kind of stock.

**Inflation and cost structure (MILDLY NEGATIVE, offset by pricing power).**
Software has negligible commodity or goods COGS, so input inflation is a **wage** story: 11,706 employees with heavy R&D and go-to-market intensity. Sticky 5%+ services inflation pressures opex per head. This is offset by genuine pricing power — 28% revenue growth, multi-year subscription contracts, and a non-discretionary security budget. Net margin effect is modest; the valuation effect of the inflation print (via rates) is far larger than the P&L effect.

**Growth and enterprise IT budgets (NEUTRAL TO POSITIVE).**
At 2% real GDP with 4.1% unemployment, there is no demand shock. Cybersecurity is among the last lines cut in an enterprise budget, and management is explicitly citing "agentic attacks" as a demand driver — a security-spend tailwind that is largely macro-insulated. CRWD's revenue is far less GDP-elastic than cyclical software or ad-driven models. The risk is second-order: if tariffs and higher rates eventually slow hiring and capex, seat-based expansion slows with a 2–4 quarter lag.

**Currency (NEGATIVE).**
**33% of revenue is international** (US 68%, EMEA 16%, APAC ~10%) per the FY2026 10-K. The broad dollar has appreciated **4.2% since September 1** and ~2% since July. Unhedged, that is roughly a **1.3pp headwind to reported growth** and a translation drag on ARR. A strengthening dollar also tightens global liquidity, which historically coincides with de-rating of high-beta US growth equities — a double hit.

**Trade / tariffs (LOW DIRECT, MODERATE INDIRECT).**
Software is largely tariff-exempt, so direct exposure is minimal. Indirect exposure runs through (a) tariff-driven inflation forcing a more hawkish Fed, and (b) enterprise customers facing input-cost pressure trimming discretionary software expansion.

**The Zscaler deal — a macro-signal event (NEGATIVE near-term).**
On November 23, CRWD announced a **$43.9B all-stock acquisition of Zscaler** (1.135 CRWD shares per ZS share, 34% premium), closing 2H FY2028. Shares fell **8.9% on 42M shares** (4x normal volume) even as the company reported Q3 FY27 revenue of **$1.58B (+28%)**, subscription revenue of $1.50B (+29%), and *raised* FY27 net new ARR growth guidance to 45% at the midpoint (+1,060bp). The market is penalizing dilution (~19% share count increase) and paying a premium with a 5% long bond as the currency. The macro read-through: management is betting its inflated equity currency on a macro-insulated consolidation story — defensible, but it raises the pro forma entity's duration and beta precisely when risk appetite is deteriorating.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The macro-financial channel — a 5%+ long bond, a Fed that has resumed hiking, a 4.2% dollar rally, VIX at 36, and an 11.6% index correction — is a headwind on three of CRWD's four macro sensitivities simultaneously.

The rating is **not** a business-quality judgment. CRWD's operating fundamentals are decoupling *positively* from the macro: growth accelerated to 28%, guidance was raised materially, and the security budget is non-discretionary. The company is macro-**resilient** on revenue but macro-**fragile** on multiple. That distinction is the whole investment case: earnings can compound through this regime; the multiple cannot expand in it. Note also that CRWD is **-27% from its October 6 high but +65% YoY and +78% YTD** — much of the rate pain has already been paid, and it has fallen *while* the 10Y fell, which suggests the current price embeds deal and risk-appetite concerns rather than discount-rate math.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario: A Disinflation Shock and Fast Fed Pivot (BULL)

**The setup:** Consensus has flipped decisively hawkish in eight weeks. The 2Y at 4.74% prices ~3 hikes; the Fed is publicly debating tightening; VIX is at 36. Positioning is now crowded *against* duration.

**The trigger:** The mid-December CPI print shows the 3.82% re-acceleration was concentrated in energy and tariff pass-through rather than services. Core services cool, the 3-month annualized run-rate breaks from 5.7% back toward 3%.

**The transmission:** The 2Y falls from 4.74% to ~4.0% as hike pricing unwinds, the 10Y drops to ~4.4%, and the dollar rolls over from 123.6 — removing the FX translation drag simultaneously.

**The payoff:** A 35x-ARR asset with 28% growth and freshly raised guidance, sitting 27% below its high, re-rates violently. A 60bp fall in the 10Y plus a 4% weaker dollar is worth roughly **25–35%** of upside on the multiple alone, before any earnings revision. This is the highest-asymmetry path because it requires only the *absence* of further acceleration, and because CRWD's operating momentum is already inflecting up.

**The mirror image (bear):** If CPI instead prints 4.5%+ and the Fed hikes in December, the 10Y clears 5.5%, the 30Y approaches 5.75%, and CRWD's 35x ARR de-rates toward 22–25x — roughly **$130–150**, another 30% downside, compounded by 19% dilution from the Zscaler share issuance landing into a hostile tape. The tightness of HY OAS at 3.76% is the cushion: as long as credit does not crack, the bear case is a multiple event, not a fundamental one.
