## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Zscaler merger review stalls (highest-conviction risk)**
Announced 23-Nov-2026: all-stock, ~$43.9B EV, 1.135 CRWD shares per ZS share, CRWD holders 84.1% of combined co, close targeted **H2 FY2028** "subject to Zscaler shareholder approval and regulatory clearance" (Help Net Security, 23-Nov-26). Both are top-tier US federal cyber vendors with overlapping SASE/SSE, zero-trust and government footprints; the combined entity would start with **>$10B ARR**.
- *Probability of a material delay (second request / Phase 2 / remedies): 60–70%.* A $44B horizontal-plus-adjacent deal between the #1 endpoint and #1 standalone zero-trust player, in the same quarter the DOJ/FTC have kept an aggressive posture, invites at minimum a US second request and an EU Phase 1→Phase 2 referral (both parties exceed EU turnover thresholds). UK CMA, Canada, Australia, Japan also likely.
- *Impact: Medium-High.* All-stock structure means 15.9% dilution is already priced; a blocked deal removes the $1B run-rate synergy case and the "cyber operating system" narrative. Shares fell ~10% on announcement. Watch for: HSR filing date, any FTC/DOJ second request, EU Form CO notification, and SAMR (China) — note Zscaler has greater China exposure than CRWD; SAMR jurisdiction is a tail risk I could not verify.
- Second-order political risk: a combined $10B+ ARR cyber vendor becomes a single point of failure for the US federal estate — expect Congressional/CISA scrutiny independent of antitrust.

**Risk 2 — EU digital-sovereignty procurement exclusion**
EMEA is 16.3% of revenue and the fastest grower, and the EU is actively moving from policy to procurement: the Commission's tech-sovereignty package (Chips Act 2.0 + **Cloud and AI Development Act**), the Cloud Sovereignty Framework, and reported discussions on restricting US cloud providers for sensitive government data (CNBC, 7-May-26; European Commission package). Reuters (23-Nov-26) reports the sovereignty push "gaining ground" in public-sector awards; trade press reports European buyers actively displacing US security vendors on sovereignty grounds.
- *Probability: 60% of measurable EMEA growth headwind within 3 years. Impact: Medium* (~200–400bps of group growth, concentrated in EU public sector and regulated financial services).
- Mitigants to verify: EU data-residency/sovereign cloud tenancy commitments and local hosting in Frankfurt/other EU co-location.

**Risk 3 — US–China tech decoupling and state-actor targeting**
CrowdStrike is the most prominent public attributor of China-nexus activity — its own 2026 Technology Threat Landscape Report puts China-nexus actors at **>58% of state-sponsored intrusions against the tech sector**. That is a commercial asset but also a geopolitical liability: elevated risk of Chinese counter-measures (procurement bans are already de facto in place for foreign security software), APAC data-localisation mandates, and retaliatory/espionage targeting of CRWD infrastructure.
- *Probability: Medium (40%) of a discrete APAC policy or incident event; Impact: Low-Medium* given APAC is only 10% of revenue and China is negligible.

*Also monitor:* US federal budget/shutdown and cyber-budget reallocation (public sector is CRWD's marquee vertical and CISA is a named customer); Gulf data-residency mandates in the 6.6% "Other" bucket; Middle East conflict escalation affecting regional deal cycles.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active litigation**
- **Delta Air Lines v. CrowdStrike** (Georgia state court, filed Oct-2024; Delta claims ~$500M revenue impact from the 19-Jul-2024 outage). In May 2025 the court allowed Delta's **gross negligence, fraud and computer-trespass** claims to proceed past dismissal (Reuters, 19-May-25); subsequent reporting indicates the judge narrowed/dismissed the bulk of the claims, and CrowdStrike's 2026 filings state the matter **remains in discovery** with no material accrual. Residual risk is precedent-setting exposure of contractual liability caps in enterprise SaaS — more consequential for the sector than for CRWD's P&L.
- **Federal passenger class action**: dismissed 19-Jun-2025 (W.D. Tex.) — largely resolved. Shareholder suits tied to the outage were also dismissed at the federal level. *Gap: I could not independently confirm the docket status of every 2024 securities/derivative action; treat as "no disclosed material exposure" rather than "closed."*
- **Zscaler transaction**: HSR plus multi-jurisdictional merger control (see Risk 1). Deal also triggers shareholder litigation risk (standard disclosure-based suits in all-stock mergers).

**Regulatory posture**
- **Privacy/cyber**: GDPR + EU–US Data Privacy Framework dependency is the key structural item — Falcon telemetry is processed centrally, so a Schrems-style invalidation of the DPF would force re-architecture of EU delivery, not merely paperwork. Also CCPA/CPRA and the ~20-state US patchwork, NIS2, and **DORA** (EU financial-services customers — CRWD's largest vertical).
- **AI regulation**: EU AI Act obligations attach to Charlotte AI / agentic security and to "SafeMind" frontier models post-Zscaler; safety-model reporting duties.
- **Federal contracting**: FedRAMP High / GovCloud authorisations, CMMC, FAR/DFARS flow-downs, and continuous-monitoring obligations — compliance failure here has revenue consequences, not just fines. CISA has directly selected CrowdStrike for endpoint and SIEMaaS programs.
- **SEC cyber disclosure**: as both a registrant and a cyber vendor, CRWD faces heightened 8-K Item 1.05 and Reg S-K Item 106 scrutiny; the SEC's 2024 enforcement actions against four RTX firms for misleading incident disclosures are the relevant precedent.
- **Export controls / sanctions**: encryption export classification, plus OFAC screening obligations across a 100+ country channel network.

**No disclosed DOJ, SEC, or EU investigation of CrowdStrike itself** in the filings reviewed. That is the single most important negative finding in this report.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# MEDIUM-HIGH

- **Pure geopolitical risk: MEDIUM.** ~83% of revenue in the US and EMEA, no Russia revenue, no material China revenue, no expropriation or currency-control exposure. This is one of the cleaner geographic books in large-cap tech.
- **Legal & regulatory risk: HIGH.** Three simultaneous stressors in the same quarter — a $44B merger review, an unsettled Delta tort case through discovery, and an EU procurement regime designed to favour domestic vendors — plus DPF transfer dependence on the EU revenue base.
- **Net: Medium-High**, and *rising* rather than falling. The rating is driven by regulatory concentration risk in the transaction and in EMEA, not by geopolitical flashpoints.

**Monitoring triggers (next 2 quarters):** HSR filing and any second request; EU notification/Phase 2 referral; Georgia rulings on summary judgment in *Delta*; final text of the EU Cloud and AI Development Act and whether cyber SaaS is captured by sovereignty procurement rules; outcome of the EU–US Data Privacy Framework review; CISA/FY27 federal cyber budget appropriations.

Zscaler's own geographic mix (relevant to combined-entity and SAMR exposure) was not verified.
