# CrowdStrike Holdings (CRWD) — ESG Analysis

**Company:** CrowdStrike Holdings, Inc. (Nasdaq: CRWD) · Technology / Software–Infrastructure · Austin, TX · 11,706 FTE · Market cap ≈ $214bn · Beta 1.15

**Material event at time of writing (23-Nov-2026):** agreed to acquire Zscaler in a **$43.9bn all-stock** transaction.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- Asset-light subscription model; primary footprint is Scope 2/3 (cloud hosting, purchased hardware, business travel). No owned manufacturing, no extraction, no remediation liabilities, no environmental fines identified.
- Disclosure quality is **above the US software median**: the FY26 Sustainability Report publishes **Scope 1, 2 and 3** GHG emissions with **third-party limited assurance** — the specific credibility marker US ESG buyers screen for, and notable given the post-2025 anti-ESG disclosure retreat.
- Regulatory exposure: EU **CSRD** is plausibly in scope via EU subsidiaries (likely above the €150m EU-turnover threshold for subsidiary-level reporting), which will force ESRS-grade E1 climate disclosure, including Scope 3 and a transition plan. Cost is disclosure/assurance opex (low single-digit $m), not capex. US SEC climate rule remains non-operative.
- **Gap:** no verified absolute emissions tonnage, science-based target, or RE100 commitment captured in this review — a mildly negative disclosure-quality flag, not a risk flag.
- **Verdict:** not a valuation factor. Cleanest E profile of the major cybersecurity names.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (elevated by a single tail risk)

**The dominant S factor is product safety — CrowdStrike's version of an airline's safety record.**
- **19-Jul-2024:** defective Falcon content update crashed ~8.5m Windows machines; ~7,000 flights cancelled; hospitals, banks and 1.4m Delta passengers affected. Remediation: **$60m customer-commitment credit package** and FY25 guidance cut. Reputational cost was real but *transient* — ARR kept compounding to $5.51bn, evidence that enterprise switching costs and trust recovery both behaved as the bull case assumed.
- **Privileged-access/data-privacy:** CRWD sits in the kernel of its customers' estates and serves federal / "Department of War" / healthcare / financial customers. A CRWD-origin breach would be a Category-1 social and regulatory event. This is a *concentration* risk, not a probability-weighted cost.
- **Labour:** 11.7k FTE, high-equity, high-turnover software labour market; ~$242m of the CEO's pay is equity, so broad-based equity participation is a retention lever. No union, no material labour dispute, no safety (industrial) exposure identified.
- **DEI:** CrowdStrike publishes UK gender pay gap reporting and diversity data; like most US tech it has softened DEI language through 2025–26 under US legal pressure — a low-materiality reputational issue with some institutional-investor and EU-tender (CSRD S1) friction.
- **Community/social licence:** no material community controversy; the *government/defence* customer mix creates activist-screening and ethical-use (surveillance-adjacent) exposure at the margin.
- **Verdict:** Medium — a structurally safe business with one low-probability/high-severity reliability tail that is now demonstrably better governed than in 2024.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.0 / 10**

**Positives (genuine, and better than the founder-led peer set):**
- **Dual-class structure has sunset.** All Class B shares (10 votes) converted to Class A on **11-Dec-2024**; CRWD is now **one share, one vote**. Kurtz's voting power fell from ~31% (2022) to ~2.5%, partly via >$1bn of share gifts disclosed in 2025. Index-exclusion and proxy-contest overhangs removed.
- **Independent Chairman** (Gerhard Watzinger) separate from the CEO — **no CEO/Chairman duality**.
- **9 directors, 7 independent** (78%); independent Audit, Nominating and Compensation Committees; Roxanne Austin chairs Audit (SEC "audit committee financial expert" archetype).
- Pay design has moved toward performance: the **April-2026 award is explicitly performance-based, with "no guaranteed value" and the potential to pay zero** if hurdles are missed — mechanically better-aligned than time-vested founder grants.
- Insider ownership ~2–3% of shares out — enough for alignment, too little for entrenchment.

**Negatives (why not 7.5+):**
- **CEO compensation of $247.6m in FY26** (base $1.1m + ~$242.1m stock) against $4.81bn revenue = ~5% of revenue and ~1.1% of market cap for a single year's grant, and a ~+86% YoY step-up. Even for a +22%-growth, $200bn+ company, this is a severe outlier and a live proxy-advisor and say-on-pay flashpoint. **Say-on-pay support level not obtained** — that is the missing datapoint that would move this score by up to a full point.
- **Disclosure opacity on control:** the >$1bn share gifts that cut voting power were initially "unexplained" by the company; the *outcome* is shareholder-friendly, the *process* is not best-in-class transparency.
- **Capital-allocation concentration risk:** the $43.9bn all-stock Zscaler deal is ~21% of CRWD's market cap and ~15% dilution (Zscaler holders to own ~15% of the combined company; ratio 1.135 CRWD shares per ZS share, 34% premium; CRWD fell ~8% on announcement). All-stock consideration defers cash risk onto existing holders, integrates two similar-sized security platforms with overlapping cultures, and triggers **antitrust review** ("antitrust scrutiny is certainly possible given the size" — Reuters) plus Zscaler shareholder approval, targeted close H2-2027. The board's approval of a premium, dilutive mega-merger *in a business whose core asset is reliability* is the single largest governance question in the name today.
- Founder-CEO with a long tenure and no visible CEO-succession disclosure — key-person risk flagged by the company itself.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU Cyber Resilience Act** — the most financially binding item. Vulnerability/incident **reporting obligations started 11-Sep-2026**; **full compliance by 11-Dec-2027**. CRWD becomes a "manufacturer" with mandatory secure-by-design, SBOM, and vulnerability-handling duties, plus potential penalties. Direct opex/engineering cost; also a **moat**, because smaller competitors face the same bar with fewer resources.
- **NIS2 / DORA** — demand tailwind (financial-services and critical-infrastructure customers must buy); raises CRWD's own vendor-diligence burden.
- **EU CSRD/ESRS** — E1 climate and S1 workforce disclosure via EU subsidiaries; assurance costs only.
- **US:** SEC cybersecurity disclosure (Item 1.05 / Reg S-K) is the operative rule; the SEC's climate rule is not in force and the 2025–26 deregulatory tilt lowers near-term US E-disclosure risk. State AG antitrust (and potentially FTC/DOJ and EC/CMA) review of the **Zscaler** deal is the live regulatory event, not any ESG rule.
- **No ESG-linked debt covenants identified** — CRWD is net-cash and has no sustainability-linked financing that could trip a margin ratchet.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (high-end average; structurally improving, governance still contested)

A genuinely clean E profile, a medium-severity S profile whose worst historical event is now legally and operationally contained, and a governance stack that has *improved* (dual-class gone, independent chair, 7/9 independent, PSU-based pay) but is pulled back by an extreme CEO pay package and a highly dilutive $43.9bn all-stock merger approved at a premium.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

ESG factors are **not the reason to own or avoid CRWD** — there is no stranded-asset risk, no carbon-tax exposure, no social-licence fracture, and no escalation in litigation. The two ESG-adjacent risks that *do* touch the cash-flow model are:

1. **Reliability/product-safety recurrence** — a second systemic outage would hit gross retention and invite CRA-era product-liability rules. Mitigant: post-2024 deployment architecture is now the tightest in the sector. **Neutral.**
2. **The Zscaler transaction** — ~15% dilution, a 34% premium, ~21% of market cap, and antitrust review, into a franchise whose only real asset is trust. This is the item to underwrite: a governance/capital-allocation risk, **not** an ESG scorecard issue, and arguably a mild negative for the equity thesis.

**Net: Neutral on ESG grounds.** Screenable for most ESG mandates (no exclusions triggered; one-share-one-vote restored; assured Scope 1–3 disclosure). For an ESG-integrated fund, the *only* materiality-weighted action is to monitor (a) say-on-pay support at the next annual meeting, (b) Zscaler deal terms/break fee and any governance concessions to Zscaler holders, and (c) CRA compliance milestones through Dec-2027.
