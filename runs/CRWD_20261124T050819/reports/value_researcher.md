# CrowdStrike Holdings (CRWD) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $208.97 | Market cap ≈ $210–214B | Diluted shares 1,052M | Net cash $4.41B | FY ends Jan 31**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW (widening)**

CrowdStrike sells cloud-native endpoint/cloud/identity security on its single-agent Falcon platform. The evidence of a moat in the numbers is genuinely strong:

| Evidence | Reading |
|---|---|
| Gross margin | 74.7% (FY26) → 75.9% (Q3 FY27). Flat-to-up for 5 years at rising scale = pricing power, no commoditization |
| Revenue CAGR FY22–FY26 | 40.6% ($1.45B → $4.81B); TTM now $5.75B |
| Reacceleration | FY26 +21.7% → Q1 FY27 +25.6%, Q2 +25.8%, Q3 +28.3%. ARR $6.26B, +27% YoY; Q3 net-new ARR $419M, Q2 +51% YoY |
| Retention | Management reports *increased* dollar-based gross and net retention. (Exact NRR % not available in my data — flagged as a gap) |
| Stress test | Revenue compounded straight through the July 2024 global IT outage — the classic "would customers leave?" test, answered |

Sources of the moat: (a) **switching costs** — the agent sits on every endpoint, feeding workflows, SOAR playbooks and a SOC that would have to be rebuilt; (b) **data/network effects** — the Threat Graph improves with each new customer and module; (c) **land-and-expand** — 30+ modules sold into one installed base, which is why net-new ARR is *accelerating* at $6B of ARR.

Where it falls short of "Wide": Microsoft bundles Defender at near-zero marginal price, Palo Alto's Cortex XSIAM is attacking the security-operations layer, and SentinelOne competes at the endpoint. Falcon is winning, but it is not indispensable the way a payments network or an exchange is. A narrow, widening moat is the honest verdict.

**The uncomfortable return-on-capital fact:** GAAP ROIC is **–5.5%**; ROE –3.6%. On a cash basis FCF/invested capital looks spectacular (~33%) — but that is largely funded by deferred revenue float (current liabilities $4.18B vs receivables $1.36B) and by paying 23% of revenue in stock instead of cash. Properly charged for SBC, owner earnings are ~$0.48B on ~$5.3B of capital ≈ **9% ROIC — roughly equal to cost of capital.** The moat is real operationally; the moat's *economics are currently being shared with employees.*

| Revenue | $3,056M | $3,954M | $4,812M | $5,745M |
| Gross margin | 75.3% | 74.9% | 74.7% | 75.9% |

| FCF | $929M | $1,068M | $1,310M | $1,778M |
| FCF margin | 30.4% | 27.0% | 27.2% | **30.9%** |
| **SBC** | $632M | $865M | $1,097M | **$1,299M (22.6% of rev)** |

Positives: fortress balance sheet (net cash ≈ $4.4B, gross debt $820M), negative working-capital model, no dividend obligation, FCF margin ~31% and rising, and 26–28% growth at $6B scale — rare.

Deductions: **SBC at 22.6% of revenue is the single largest flaw in the business.** FCF "conversion" is meaningless when net income ≈ 0; share count still grows ~2.5%/yr (diluted 1,032M → 1,052M) despite the buyback ($176M in Q1 FY27 — a rounding error against $1.3B of annual grants). Retained earnings are still **–$1.28B**; the company has never accumulated a GAAP profit in 15 years.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — Intrinsic value ≈ **$95/share (range $65–155)**

Current multiples on TTM: **36.5x sales, 32.7x ARR, 118x FCF, 437x SBC-adjusted owner earnings, 173x non-GAAP EPS ($1.21), 2,600x GAAP EPS, 46.9x book.**

I modeled owner earnings with SBC treated as the real cash-equivalent cost it is (base case: revenue grows from $6.07B FY27E to $21.4B by FY36 — a 12% CAGR; FCF margin 31%→37%; SBC declines to 10% of revenue from 21%):

| Scenario | Key assumptions | Intrinsic value/sh |
|---|---|---|
| Bear — SBC fully expensed, 9% WACC, 3% TG | revenue CAGR ~10% | **$64** |
| Base — SBC fully expensed, 8% WACC, 3.5% TG | as above | **$84** |
| Bull — SBC *ignored*, 24.8% FCF growth 10 yrs, 9% WACC | tool DCF on reported FCF | **$115** |
| Very bull — same, 8% WACC / 3.5% TG | | **$155** |

Even the *generous* models that pretend stock compensation is free land 27–55% below today's price. **Reverse DCF:** to justify $209 at 9% WACC, CRWD needs ~**$20B of year-10 owner earnings** — roughly $60–70B of revenue (10x today) at 30% margins *and* SBC near zero. The market is paying today for a decade of flawless 25%+ compounding plus full SBC elimination.

Cross-check: FY27 non-GAAP EPS ≈ $1.25. Five years at a heroic 30% CAGR → $4.64; at a premium 30x multiple that is $139/share. To earn even 10%/yr from $209, the multiple must still be ~47x in 2031.

*Margin of safety: at $209 there is none — you are paying ~2.2x my midpoint fair value. A disciplined entry price is **below ~$75**, which is 25–30% under base-case value.*

## 4. Management Quality: **7 / 10**

Founder-led (George Kurtz, CEO), technically credible, executes well and has built a genuine platform rather than a serial acquirer's roll-up — tuck-ons like Flow Security and Adaptive Shield were small and capability-filling, not empire building. Cash discipline is good; the balance sheet is unlevered.

Two blemishes: (1) **equity compensation of 23% of revenue** is shareholder value transferred to employees, and the modest buyback does not offset the ~2.5% annual dilution; (2) GAAP profitability remains aspirational after 15 years, which means the "profitable growth" story has always required adding SBC back. Insider ownership data was unavailable to me in this dataset — a gap, not a red flag.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (valuation-driven; the business is fine, the price is not)

This is a high-quality, still-accelerating franchise trading at a price that already discounts near-perfection. Under Buffett's framework: wonderful business, narrow-but-widening moat, strong balance sheet — and a price roughly twice a defensible intrinsic value with negative margin of safety. I would not own it here, and I would not short it either (momentum and a genuine 27% ARR growth engine make timing unknowable).

For an existing holder with a low basis, a tax-aware HOLD is defensible; for new capital, **avoid**.

**Buy zone: < $75/share** (≈ 40% below today), or re-evaluate if the price falls while ARR growth holds above 20%.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Durability of the reacceleration.** ARR +27% with record net-new ARR *and* rising retention is not the profile of a decelerating asset. If 30%+ growth persists into FY28–FY29 while FCF margins reach 35–40%, my base case rises toward $130–150 — still not $209, but the gap narrows.
2. **SBC discipline.** If management cuts grants toward 10–12% of revenue, owner earnings could triple within four years and the multiple becomes far less absurd. Watch this above all else.
3. **Duration/rate risk.** At 118x FCF, the valuation is acutely sensitive to the discount rate; a falling-rate regime can keep the multiple aloft regardless of fundamentals.
4. **Genuine new TAMs** — AI-security, identity, SIEM/log management, exposure management — could extend the runway and justify a "platform" terminal multiple rather than a security-software one.
5. **Competitive/pricing risk** is the flip side: Microsoft bundling and XSIAM consolidation could crack the 75% gross margin, which is the load-bearing pillar of my moat rating.
6. **Data limitations:** I could not retrieve the 10-K risk factors (tool returned "No 10-K found"), exact NRR, or a clean peer multiple set (the sector peer list returned mega-cap platform names, and the P/E comparison is meaningless with negative GAAP EPS). My conclusions rest on reported financials, ARR disclosures and price history, which are consistent internally.

**Bottom line: excellent company, narrow moat, weakening capital efficiency after SBC, and a price that requires everything to go right. No margin of safety — Sell.**
