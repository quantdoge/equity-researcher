# GRMN (Garmin Ltd.) — Macroeconomic Impact Assessment
**As of early October 2026 | Sector: Technology (Hardware/Consumer Electronics) | Market cap ~$54.7B | β 0.87**

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — solid growth, sticky inflation, easing front-end but ~5% long-end yields, strong dollar.**

The US economy is growing ~2.2% y/y (real GDP Q2'26: $24.41T vs. $23.88T a year earlier; ~2.5% annualized QoQ), with no recession signal — the 2s10s curve has re-steepened to +34bp (2Y 4.65%, 10Y 4.99%, 30Y 5.37%) after years of inversion. But this is an unusual easing cycle: the Fed has cut the funds rate ~60bp from the 4.33% mid-2025 plateau to **3.75% effective (Sep 2026)** — and then paused (and edged up in September) because **CPI is stuck at ~3.05% y/y (Aug 2026)**, well above target. Meanwhile the 10Y has backed up from ~4.3% (April) to **~5.2-5.3%** — near cycle highs — as fiscal deficits and term-premium repricing push the long end up against the Fed. The broad dollar sits at ~120 (Jan 2006=100), near multi-year highs. Net: consumer demand resilient, policy constrained by inflation, long rates and FX the operative risks.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates / valuation — Negative (moderate).** Garmin is effectively debt-free with a large net-cash balance sheet, so the liability side is insulated (rising rates even boost interest income); the impact is via the discount rate. At ~$284 the stock trades ~30-33x forward EPS — a compressed-growth, premium-multiple equity — and a 10Y at ~5.2% is a genuine headwind to that multiple. So far earnings momentum has overwhelmed it; it is the key rate risk.

**Inflation & consumer — Positive.** CPI at ~3% with stable employment and ~2.2% GDP growth keeps premium discretionary spend intact. Garmin's $300-1,000+ watches carry de facto pricing power (its dedicated-GPS wearable franchise is quasi-duopolistic vs. smartwatches); it has raised ASPs through the cycle, and FY25 gross margin held at ~59% on record revenue (Q4'25 revenue $2.12B, +17% y/y). Sticky inflation is more a Fed-constraint risk than a demand risk for this company.

**Currency / USD strength — Negative (modest).** Roughly 40-45% of revenue is generated outside the Americas (EMEA ~27-29%, APAC ~16-18%). The broad dollar at ~120 near its highs is a translation headwind to USD-reported revenue and EPS; management explicitly flagged "unfavorable foreign currency" offsetting the tariff improvement in Q2'25 and FX as a cost driver in Q3'25. Partially mitigated by hedging and premium pricing.

**Taiwan-dollar COGS / tariffs — Negative (modest, high variance).** Manufacturing is concentrated in Taiwan (and China). The Q3'25 call flagged the **strengthening Taiwan dollar raising cost of goods sold**, and US tariffs on Taiwan/China-sourced electronics pressured margins in 2025 (partially offset later by lower tariff estimates + price increases). This is the single most macro-volatile line in the P&L.

**Trade-policy tail risk — Negative.** The April 2025 "Liberation Day" tariff shock cut GRMN ~20% in days (212.8 → 165.1 intraday low on Apr 8). Trade policy, not the Fed, remains the sharpest macro swing factor for the stock.

**Growth cycles by segment — Mixed/Positive.** Aviation (fastest-growing, ~20% of revenue) rides the aircraft-delivery/aftermarket upcycle; Marine is more cyclical; Fitness/Outdoor track premium consumer health. On balance the current ~2-2.5% growth macro is supportive.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL**

**Net:** Resilient premium demand, a net-cash balance sheet and pricing power offset a strong dollar, ~5% long-end yields, sticky 3% inflation and Taiwan-driven cost/tariff pressure. The macro backdrop neither strongly supports nor threatens the business case — but it is an environment where quarterly FX and tariff prints (not rates or growth) will drive revisions.

## 5. Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (Bear)

**"Tariff round two + steeper curve" — the April 2025 template revisited.** Suppose the administration re-imposes broad Section 301 tariffs on Taiwan-sourced consumer electronics and China escalates import duties in response, while the 10Y breaks above ~5.5% on deficit/term-premium concerns and the broad dollar pushes past ~124. Garmin would face a triple-hit: COGS inflation from tariffs + TWD strength, a ~25% translation drag on EMEA/APAC revenue, and multiple compression on a ~30x P/E as long-duration growth equities de-rate. With FY25 EPS guidance at ~$8.15 and the stock at ~$284, a repeat of April 2025 (≈−20% in a week) plus tariff-margin erosion is the plausible bear path — the market would reprice Garmin from "growth compounder" to "discretionary hardware with FX/cost variance" even as the underlying business reality stays solid.
