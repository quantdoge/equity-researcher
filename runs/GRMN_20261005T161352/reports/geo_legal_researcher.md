## 1. Geographic Risk Map

Garmin is a Swiss-domiciled, US-listed designer/manufacturer of GPS hardware (Fitness, Outdoor, Aviation, Marine, Auto OEM segments). It reports revenue in three geography buckets — Americas, EMEA, and APAC — with ~23,000 employees and manufacturing/assembly in Taiwan (Garmin Corp.), China, the United States (Olathe, Kansas — aviation), and other sites. **Note:** Exact FY2025 geographic percentages come from Garmin's 10-K revenue tables, which I could not retrieve verbatim (the 10-K text pull failed); figures below are best-available estimates anchored to Garmin's recent annual-reports patterns (~55% / ~29% / ~16%) and should be validated against the FY2025 10-K before final use.

| Country / Region | Revenue exposure (est.) | Primary exposure type | Risk level | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **United States / Americas** | **~55%** | Consumer & aviation demand; US tariff policy; FAA certification | **Medium** | Largest single market; live US–China tariff regime directly hits COGS (Garmin booked ~$21M in *tariff refunds* in Q2 2026 alone, evidencing material tariff payments) |
| **Europe (EMEA)** | **~29–30%** | Fitness/outdoor/marine/auto revenue | **Medium** | GDPR health-data regulation, EU Cyber Resilience Act (connected devices), GPSR; Ukraine-war energy/trade spillovers; Russia exposure essentially nil after 2022 exit |
| **Asia-Pacific** | **~15–16%** | China consumer growth; Taiwan production base | **Medium-High** | Taiwan Strait tension = supply-chain tail risk; China market growth but rising PRC data-localization/compliance burden; US tariff-circumvention scrutiny on Asia supply chains |
| **Russia** | ~0% | — | **Low** | Operations/sales suspended since 2022; residual reputational/export-control optics (see §4) |

**Net assessment:** Demand is well-diversified across ~30%+ geographies with a neutral-country Swiss domicile, but the *supply chain* is concentrated in Asia (Taiwan + China) and the *biggest P&L line* (Americas) is exposed to US tariff politics. That combination — not country risk in the classic sense — is the core geopolitical exposure.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US–China tariff regime eroding gross margin
- **Probability:** High (ongoing, live)
- **Impact:** Moderate (margin, not demand)
- **Detail:** Consumer hardware with China/Taiwan production is squarely in scope of US Section 301 and IEEPA tariff actions (2025–2026). Evidence of materiality is direct: Garmin's Q2 2026 gross margin (62.4%) "benefited from mix and about **$21 million of tariff refunds**" (8-K, 29-Jul-2026). The company has guided gross margin up (59.7% for FY26), indicating it is currently *winning* the tariff fight via refunds, pricing, and mix — but refunds are one-time-ish and tariff policy under the Trump administration has been volatile (potential export bans, escalation with China). A reversal would pressure the ~59–62% gross margin that underpins the premium valuation (~$54.8B cap).
- **Mitigants:** Production diversification (Taiwan, US), premium pricing power, $4.4B cash.

### Risk 2 — Taiwan Strait tension → supply disruption
- **Probability:** Low (tail risk), but **Impact:** High if it materializes
- **Detail:** Garmin's durable manufacturing base is in Taiwan (Garmin Corp., Taiwan is a core operating entity; insider filings reference the "General Manager - Garmin Corp."). US–China friction over Taiwan continues to escalate rhetorically and legally (China's 2025/2026 counter-sanctions and blocking-statute expansions; US arms-sales pressure; ballistic-missile drills per CFR Global Conflict Tracker). A blockade/conflict would disrupt a meaningful share of Garmin's hardware output and its aviation/marine supply chains, with no near-term substitute capacity.
- **Mitigants:** US (Olathe, KS) and China plants provide partial redundancy; $4.4B cash buffer; multi-sourcing culture. Still, this is the single largest tail risk on the map.

### Risk 3 — US "tariff evasion / circumvention" scrutiny of Asia supply chains
- **Probability:** Medium (enforcement trend rising)
- **Impact:** Low-to-Moderate (compliance cost + reputational, not existential)
- **Detail:** A White House report (Aug 2026) named Taiwan among jurisdictions implicated in a China-linked tariff-evasion network, and 2025–2026 enforcement has centered on importers routing Chinese goods through third countries. Garmin's Taiwan operations are long-standing, legitimate, in-house manufacturing (not a transshipment scheme), so this is a watch item rather than an active allegation — but any US importer with China/Taiwan supply chains faces elevated IEEPA/FTC circumvention scrutiny, duty audits, and certification burdens. No Garmin-specific action has been reported.

**Honorable mentions:** Europe's regulatory stack (Cyber Resilience Act, GPSR, GDPR health-data rules) adds compliance cost to ~30% of revenue; Russia/Ukraine exposure is de minimis post-2022; Middle East/Africa and LatAm exposure is small and unremarkable.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active / recent litigation — watch list:**
1. **Strava v. Garmin (patent infringement) — FILED AND WITHDRAWN.** Strava sued Garmin on ~Sept 30, 2025 over "segments" and "heatmap" patents, seeking to halt sales of most of Garmin's fitness wearables (Android Police, Mondaq, DC Rainmaker coverage). **Strava withdrew the suit ~21 days later (Oct 2025)** amid industry backlash (athletechnews, road.cc). Net: resolved, non-event, but demonstrates patent-attack surface in the fitness wearables franchise.
2. **Silver State patent case — GARMIN PREVAILED.** A US jury found Silver State's asserted patents invalid; verdict subject to appeal (RTTNews). Defense wins of this kind are routine for Garmin, which holds thousands of patents and litigates IP (including counterclaims) as a normal cost of doing business.
3. **No material non-IP litigation, class actions, or government probes surfaced** in a broad search of Q1 2025–Q3 2026 (no DOJ/FTC/SEC enforcement actions against Garmin found).

**Important data gap:** Garmin's 10-K "Legal Proceedings" and full "Risk Factors" sections could not be retrieved (SEC EDGAR fetch failed; only the Articles-of-Association exhibit came back for the 10-Q). The litigation picture above is therefore based on public reporting through Q3 2026, not the company's own legal-proceedings disclosure — **validate against the FY2025 10-K Item 3 / Item 1A before final publication.**

**Regulatory environment by franchise:**
- **Aviation (high-margin, ~20%+ of revenue):** FAA/EASA type-certified products; certification is a moat but also a regulatory dependency — any certification slowdown (FAA shutdowns, China-market certification politics) affects the aviation and defense-adjacent roadmap.
- **Consumer electronics:** FCC (US) and CE/RED (EU) compliance; new EU **Cyber Resilience Act** will impose cybersecurity-by-design and vulnerability-handling duties on all connected devices, including wearables — a rising, sector-wide compliance burden from 2026–2027.
- **Data/privacy:** Fitness wearables collect health, biometric, and geolocation data. Exposure includes GDPR (special-category data, explicit consent), US state health-privacy laws (e.g., Washington's My Health My Data Act), and FTC Section 5 theory. No Garmin-specific enforcement found to date, but this is the most probable future regulatory front.

**Financial/legal context (from 8-K press releases):** FY2025 revenue **$7.25B (+15%)**, operating margin 25.9%, GAAP EPS $8.59; FY2026 guide raised after record Q2 2026 ($2.02B, +11%; operating margin 30.4%) to **~$8.05B revenue / ~$10.00 pro forma EPS**; ~$4.4B cash; $4.20 annual dividend and new **$500M buyback** (Feb 2026). Strong margin expansion and a fortress balance sheet are the credit-side offsets to the risks above. Acquisitions (TrainingPeaks, TrainHeroic, 2026) add new data/consumer surfaces worth monitoring from a privacy/antitrust view.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** (i) Demand is diversified across Americas/EMEA/APAC with a politically neutral Swiss domicile; (ii) fiscal strength ($4.4B cash, 30%+ operating margin, premium pricing) gives Garmin unusual resilience to tariff shocks — and it is *currently* net-benefiting from tariff refunds; (iii) the only headline IP fight (Strava) was withdrawn within weeks, and no government investigations were found. The rating stops short of **Low** because: US–China tariff policy is live and directly material to COGS; the Taiwan production base is a genuine, high-impact tail risk; and the EU's incoming cybersecurity/health-data regulation raises structural compliance costs. These are manageable, not existential — hence **Medium**.
