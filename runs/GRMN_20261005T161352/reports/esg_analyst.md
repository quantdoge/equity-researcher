## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **Medium**

- **Position:** Garmin is an electronics assembler/designer, not a heavy emitter; lifecycle footprint is dominated by **Scope 3** (purchased components, logistics, use-phase energy). This is a structurally low-carbon-intensity model.
- **The gap:** The 2025 PRE 14A discloses: *"Because our 2024 global GHG emissions will serve as the baseline, we have not yet established specific GHG targets or goals."* The 10-K confirms only a general commitment to *"direct carbon emissions reduction and elimination strategies."* **No SBTi-aligned target, no quantified trajectory** — clearly behind Apple and other consumer-electronics peers, though ahead of many small-cap hardware names that disclose nothing.
- **Physical risk:** Real but manageable via diversified sites (US/TW/PL/CN). Taiwan concentration is the standout exposure (typhoon, seismic, and the 2024 Taiwan earthquake that rattled electronics supply chains), plus tariff/geopolitical tail risk.
- **Regulatory:** CBAM exposure negligible (low embedded carbon). The binding vectors are **EU ESPR/ecodesign, battery pass-porting, and right-to-repair** for wearables — compliance, not existential.
- *Data gap: granular Scope 1/2/3 figures and Scope 3 methodology not retrieved from the report itself (Garmin does publish Corporate Sustainability Reports 2021–2024); treat specific tonnage claims as unverified.*

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **Medium**

- **Labour:** 23k employees; manufacturing in Taiwan/China/Poland/US with IATF 16949 quality certs (per 10-K). No major labour controversy surfaced in search. Asia supply-chain labour standards are inherent exposure; Garmin's responsible-materials program exists but I could not verify current RBA membership — flag as gap.
- **Product safety:** Aviation segment is safety-critical and FAA-supervised; no recent major AD/recall controversy found — a positive.
- **Data privacy (the core S issue):** Garmin collects continuous health, location, and biometric data (incl. inReach SOS safety data). Historical scrutiny under GDPR (health = special category) and ongoing user concern about health-data use. No enforcement fine located in this review, but this remains the most credible future S liability vector.
- **Community/brand:** Strong social licence (inReach rescue ecosystem, fitness community); minor IP litigation (Strava segments/heatmap suit) is competitive, not ESG-material.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **6.5 / 10**

**Strengths:** Single-class share structure (no dual-class/takeover entrenchment docs found); CEO/Chair split (Pemble CEO, Kao Executive Chairman); disciplined capital allocation and founder-aligned long-termism; transparent SEC disclosure as US-listed large accelerated filer.
**Weaknesses:** Executive (non-independent) Chairman in co-founder Kao + long-tenured insider management = limited board refresh/independence pressure; no climate targets and incomplete emissions accounting into 2024 (board recommending against early target-setting per PRE 14A context; SEC text blocked, so board's formal recommendation language not directly read); 2020 cyber event indicates incident-response readiness was then inadequate; ESG oversight is not visibly linked to executive compensation.
**Net:** Above-average basic governance, below-average ESG-specific governance for a $54.7B-cap company. **6.5/10.**

## 7. Overall ESG Rating — **Average (leaning Laggard on E)**

Solid S/G hygiene (no major labour/environment/safety scandals; no dual-class; decent disclosure discipline), but **materially behind on climate target-setting and full emissions disclosure**, with a material 2020 cybersecurity failure and persistent health-data privacy exposure. Garmin is *not* an ESG risk story; it is a middle-of-pack hardware name with a clear climate-credibility gap versus premium tech peers.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication — **Neutral**

ESG factors are not currently thesis-driving for GRMN: none of the identified risks threaten near-term cash flows, margins (~60% GM model), or the aviation/government franchise, and no active fines/litigation loom. Monitor triggers that would flip this negative: (1) a GDPR/privacy enforcement action or breach recurrence; (2) Taiwan-Strait escalation or a major typhoon/quake hit to Chung-Li production; (3) EU ecodesign/battery rules compressing wearable margins; (4) institutional pushback if the board continues resisting GHG target-setting. Disclosure momentum (baseline year set in 2024, formal sustainability reporting since 2021) is improving and offers a modest positive optionality should governments/enterprise procurement start requiring verified climate data.
