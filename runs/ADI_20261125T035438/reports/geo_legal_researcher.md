**Manufacturing/ops footprint matters more than the revenue map:** front-end fabs in the US (MA, OR, ME), Ireland; assembly/test in the **Philippines, Thailand, Malaysia (Penang — new plant)**, and Singapore. ADI is therefore simultaneously exposed to US inbound tariffs, Malaysian/Thai export-licensing rules, and China's retaliation list — a "three-sided" exposure most analog peers share but with unusually heavy Asian back-end concentration.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US–China tariff/export-control re-escalation around the truce expiry (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
- The Nov 2025 truce is fragile. As of **24 Nov 2026**, the US is "preparing to raise tariffs on China and other countries," with a **Section 301 probe into excess industrial capacity** expected to produce new duties "this week," and Trump "eyeing a tariff blitz on China, EU, Mexico and Japan" (*WSJ, Barron's, NY Post, 24 Nov 2026*).
- China extended only a **temporary suspension** of its gallium/germanium/antimony export ban to the US, **until January** (*Reuters, WSJ, 24 Nov 2026*) — a hard cliff for ADI's compound-semiconductor and specialty input supply.
- China has **activated its 2021 anti-foreign-sanctions "blocking rules"** and its Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law, which can penalise a company for complying with US controls (*MERICS, WION, Nov 2026*). With ~25% of revenue in China and 56% of revenue flowing through distributors, ADI sits directly in the compliance crossfire.
- **Quantify:** a 10% demand impairment in Greater China ≈ 2.5% of group revenue; a distribution-channel freeze (as seen in prior cycles) would hit harder given the $1.7B price-protection credit liability that magnifies channel-inventory shocks (auditor-designated critical audit matter, FY26 10-K).

**Risk 2 — Section 232 semiconductor tariffs plus derivative-product scope creep (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium)**
- A **25% Section 232 tariff on certain semiconductors, SME and derivative products took effect 15 Jan 2026** (*White House proclamation; EY, C.H. Robinson, ST&R guidance*). The initial scope is narrow (advanced computing chips/derivatives), which largely spares ADI's analog catalogue — but a **2026 Section 232 review / wider chip tariff** is live, and ADI's US-bound flows from Penang, the Philippines, Thailand and Ireland would be caught by any broadening (*chip-war.com*).
- Offsetting mitigant: ADI raised prices in 2026 and runs high gross margins (~65% GAAP, ~72.5% adjusted), giving pricing power to absorb a moderate tariff — but not a blanket one applied to Asian back-end output.

**Risk 3 — China revenue concentration meets a re-accelerating ADI (Probability: Medium / Impact: High)**
- China went from 18.1% (FY23) → 22.6% (FY24) → 25.9% (FY25) of revenue — a structural, not cyclical, increase in the single most policy-exposed geography, just as FY26 growth (+38%) is concentrated in data centre/AI power and industrial, both of which carry explicit US–China security framing (*Seeking Alpha, Nov 2026: "questions over China-related chip exports compounded the AI trade's liquidation"*).
- Secondary: **BIS now requires licences for advanced-chip exports within Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil** (*GHY*) — a direct constraint on ADI's own intra-Asian manufacturing and shipping logistics.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Cybersecurity incident / litigation (most material new item)**
- ADI detected **unauthorised access to company systems on 23 June 2026**; an 8-K disclosed that **"certain files were exfiltrated"** with scope then unknown. The intrusion was claimed by the **ExfilSquad** group (*SEC 8-K accession 0001193125-26-324223; The Record, SecurityWeek, SecurityAffairs, Aviatrix threat research*).
- **Class-action plaintiffs' firms are actively soliciting claimants** (*classaction.org, "Analog Devices July 2026"*). Expected exposure: consumer/employee privacy class actions (typically $5–50M for a chipmaker of this size), plus potential SEC scrutiny of disclosure controls and Item 1.05 timing — the latter is the more consequential tail risk. GDPR exposure exists if EU personal data was in scope.

**Patent litigation**
- **Bell Semiconductor §337 ITC complaint** naming Analog Devices alongside AMD, Marvell, Bose, Micron, NXP and Qualcomm, over "layered dummy fill / timing-aware dummy fill" semiconductor patents, with **parallel district-court suits** (*USITC, Federal Register, Legal500, McKool Smith*). ADI has been a Bell Semic target before (337-TA-1138 lineage), i.e., this is a repeat monetisation campaign, not a one-off. **Risk: an ITC exclusion order would bar importation of affected devices — a supply-chain remedy, not just damages.**
- **Mobility Workx, LLC v. Analog Devices, 7:2026cv00180 (W.D. Tex., filed 30 Apr 2026)** — patent case in a plaintiff-friendly venue (*Justia docket*).
- ADI's patent-litigation record totals roughly 11 cases (*PatSnap*); a design-tool infringement matter involving semiconductor design tools is reportedly seeking Supreme Court review (*VitalLaw, 16 Oct 2026*) — sector-wide precedent risk on damages for foreign sales.

**Regulatory / compliance posture**
- **Export controls (BIS/ECCN/EAR):** distributor-heavy model + China exposure = diversion-risk scrutiny. DOJ's 2026 enforcement theme is explicitly that "U.S. companies cannot assess diversion risk based on..." assumptions about intermediaries (*Pillsbury*).
- **FCPA/AML:** 56% of revenue via distributors, much of it in China and emerging Asia, is the classic third-party bribery/diversion profile. No FCPA action against ADI is in evidence, but disclosure controls over distributor rebates/price protection are the weak point.
- **Antitrust:** acquisitions are ongoing — **Empower Semiconductor (closed FY26)** and **Alif Semiconductor (closed 3 Nov 2026**, i.e., five days into FY27), $1.536B of FY26 acquisition payments. Alif (edge-AI MCUs) will have triggered HSR plus likely China SAMR and EU merger clearances; **SAMR is the realistic bottleneck** for any US chip acquirer in the current climate.
- **Tax/trade:** FY26 gross margin benefited from pricing; note the pending Section 301 action could reopen tariff-cost pass-through negotiations with distributors.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **China blocking-rules conflict (amber→red).** ADI is legally obliged to comply with US export controls while China's Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law purports to punish exactly that compliance. This is an unhedgeable legal conflict for any entity with 25% China revenue.
2. **Distributor concentration + $1.7B price-protection liability (amber).** The FY26 audit report flags distributor price-protection credits as a critical audit matter; geopolitically-driven demand reversals get amplified through this channel and can trigger restatement risk.
3. **Cyber incident disclosure quality (amber).** 8-K filed but scope "not yet known"; ExfilSquad is a known extortion actor. Watch for Item 1.05 amendment, customer notification obligations, and any CMMC/defence-customer contract issues given ADI's aerospace/defence exposure.
4. **ITC exclusion-order risk on imports (amber).** An adverse §337 ruling is a *supply* remedy — more operationally damaging than a damages award, and it lands on a company that imports finished product into the US from Malaysia/Philippines/Thailand.
5. **No evidence found** of DOJ/SEC/FTC/European Commission enforcement actions against Analog Devices, nor OFAC sanctions exposure. This is a clean record on the enforcement side, which is a genuine positive versus peers.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale.** ADI's ~75% non-US revenue mix is normal for the sector, but the *composition* is the problem: China at ~25% and rising, an Asian back-end manufacturing base squarely inside the tariff and export-licence perimeter, a distributor-mediated channel that concentrates diversion/anticorruption/inventory risk, and a fresh unresolved data breach with plaintiff firms mobilised. The company faces no existential legal threat — no monopolisation case, no sanctions designation, no criminal probe — and its margins provide real absorptive capacity. But three independent policy vectors (Section 301/232 tariffs, BIS controls, China's blocking/critical-minerals regime) can each move the model, and the January critical-minerals cliff plus the Section 301 determination due "this week" make Q1 FY2027 a concentrated event window. **Rate High, with the caveat that this is policy-driven and reversible — a truce extension would quickly move the rating to Medium.**
