## MACROECONOMIC ASSESSMENT — NET (Cloudflare, Inc.)

> **Instrument note:** Ticker NET resolves to **Cloudflare, Inc.** (Technology / Software–Infrastructure, San Francisco), market cap **$117.6B**, beta **1.65**, 5,156 employees. This is *not* Netflix. Cloudflare is a global cloud security/edge-computing platform — a long-duration, high-multiple growth asset with material non-US revenue. The macro read below is calibrated to that profile.

---

### 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: TRANSITIONAL — hawkish repricing / risk-off for duration assets.**

The US economy in October 2026 is in an unusual and hostile configuration for high-multiple software: **inflation is re-accelerating while the Fed has resumed hiking**, and long-end yields sit near 19-year highs.

- The FOMC **raised** the target range 25bp to **3.75%–4.00% on 16 September 2026** — a 12–0 vote and the *first hike since 2023*, explicitly framed as a commitment to the inflation fight. This marks the definitive end of the 2024–25 easing cycle.
- **September CPI came in hot**, pushing the 10-year yield to **5.30%** on the print. The 10Y has repriced ~80bp higher since June 2026 (~4.38% → ~5.0–5.2%), a **bear steepener** — term premium and inflation risk, not growth optimism.
- Consensus (Reuters, 9 Sep 2026) expects the Fed to **hold through year-end**, but a rising number of analysts now see **at least one more hike in 2026**. The market-implied path agrees: the 2Y at 4.65% sits ~75bp above the top of the funds range.
- Growth is decelerating but not contracting: real GDP **+2.2% YoY** in Q2 2026 (vs +2.6% in Q1), unemployment **4.2%** and drifting higher. Nominal consumer spending remains robust (+6.1% YoY), so the economy is *not* rolling over — this is an inflation-and-rates problem, not a demand problem.
- Equity market consequence: an **AI/tech selloff in October 2026 wiped roughly $1 trillion in value** on inflation and hike fears, followed by a sharp rebound as yields eased. High-beta growth is trading as a **rates proxy**.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact (Cloudflare)

| Macro Variable | Impact | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Fed re-hiking / 10Y ~5%** | **NEGATIVE (high)** | Cloudflare is a near-pure long-duration asset: $117.6B cap on a very high revenue multiple. A ~80bp move in the 10Y mechanically compresses terminal-value-heavy DCFs. The **October drawdown from $355 (6 Oct) to $292 (20 Oct), −17.8%** (intraday −23% from $370), tracks the 10Y spike to 5.21% almost tick-for-tick. This is the dominant macro channel. |
| **Inflation re-acceleration (+3.72%)** | **MIXED / mildly negative** | Cloudflare has genuine pricing power as a security/performance necessity, and contracts are largely subscription with annual escalators — revenue is inflation-resilient. But rising costs (compute, bandwidth, headcount) and *higher discount rates* outweigh the pricing benefit. Also pressures enterprise customers' discretionary IT budgets. |
| **Real GDP +2.2%, decelerating** | **NEUTRAL to mildly negative** | ~Half of revenue is enterprise IT spend. Security and network infrastructure are among the last budget lines cut, so revenue is defensive relative to discretionary software. But decelerating GDP slows large-deal expansion and lengthens sales cycles. Net new ACV, not churn, is the exposure. |
| **Labour market 4.2%** | **NEUTRAL** | Still-tight labour market keeps wage costs elevated for a 5,156-person high-comp engineering workforce. No demand destruction signal. |
| **USD +2.9% (broad)** | **NEGATIVE (moderate)** | Cloudflare derives roughly **half its revenue outside the US** (estimate; company discloses material international mix). A stronger dollar is a direct translation headwind to reported revenue and a tailwind to nothing on the cost side. The ~3% rise since May is a ~1–1.5pt revenue-growth drag if sustained. |
| **AI capex cycle** | **POSITIVE (structural, offsetting)** | The AI buildout is Cloudflare's macro trump card — bot verification, edge inference, zero-trust security, and AI-agent traffic all expand its TAM. The **October selloff rebound (+6% on 23 Oct)** and the BigCommerce/WebMCP AI-shopping partnership show company-specific momentum *overriding* the rate headwind. This is why NET is +52% over 12 months despite a 19-year-high 10Y. |
| **Trade/tariff exposure** | **LOW** | Software/edge services are not goods-flow exposed. Minimal direct tariff risk; indirect exposure via customer verticals (retail/industrial). |

**Net transmission:** The rate channel is the *primary* macro driver and it is pointing against the stock. The AI/security demand channel is the *primary fundamental* driver and it is pointing for the stock. **The two are in direct conflict**, which is precisely why NET exhibits violent 15–20% swings around every inflation print and Fed communication.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Cloudflare sits in the worst macro quadrant for its valuation style: **re-accelerating inflation + a Fed that has resumed hiking + long yields at 19-year highs + a strengthening dollar + decelerating GDP.** Every one of those variables except AI demand works against a 1.65-beta, ~$118B, long-duration software multiple. The 12-month +52% return is being earned *despite* the macro, not because of it — which makes the position fragile to any further hawkish surprise. The structural AI/security tailwind prevents a fully bearish call, but the macro backdrop is a clear headwind to valuation and a source of high realised volatility.

---

### 5. Surprise Scenario — **Hawkish Breakout (bear, ~25% probability)**

**The 10Y breaks decisively above 5.5% toward 5.75% and the Fed delivers a second hike at the December 2026 meeting** — driven not by growth but by a sticky-services CPI print above 0.5% MoM and rising inflation expectations de-anchoring the long end. In that world:
- Long-duration software de-rates another 20–30%; NET at beta 1.65 and an extreme multiple is among the most exposed names in large-cap tech.
- A retest of the **$250–275** zone (the July/August 2026 consolidation shelf) is the realistic downside; a break there opens **$180–200** (the November 2025 low).
- Watch trigger: **5.5% on the 10Y** and any hawkish dissent turning into a majority.

**Offsetting upside scenario (~20%):** a soft October CPI (≤0.2% MoM) convinces the market the September hike was a one-off; the 10Y retreats to ~4.4% and NET re-rates through the **$370** high toward $400+. NET's own earnings delivery, not the macro, would be the catalyst — which is why the macro rating should be read as a *valuation* headwind, not a thesis-breaker.
