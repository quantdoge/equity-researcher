## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation — **1.0% initial, 2.5% absolute ceiling**

**Recommended initial weight: 1.0% of portfolio. Hard ceiling: 2.5%.**

Applied against my sizing rubric:
- The franchise quality and growth momentum would ordinarily justify the **3–5% band**.
- But NET carries **six independent elevated-risk flags** (governance event, extreme valuation, margin trajectory, rates/duration, insider selling, 70% volatility). By the rubric, "multiple risk flags" → **0.5–1.0% or pass**. I split the difference upward to 1.0% because the growth evidence is stronger than a typical flagged name.

**Kelly check — and why it caps size rather than expanding it.** The distribution is unusually wide and genuinely two-sided:
- Upside path: consensus PT ~$385 (+17%); soft-CPI scenario → $400+ (+21% to +25%).
- Downside path: hawkish breakout → $250–275 (−17% to −25%); a break there opens $180–200 (−39% to −45%).
- Roughly 1:1 odds with wide dispersion → Kelly fraction approaches **zero to 1%**.

That is the honest arithmetic. A 1:1 payoff at ~50/50 does not support a 4% position regardless of how good the company is. **1.0% is the Kelly-consistent answer, not a compromise.**

**Why not larger:** a 1% position at 70% annualized vol contributes ~0.70% to portfolio volatility on its own — it is a meaningful risk-budget line item despite being a small weight. A gap through the stop to $180 would cost ~45bps at 1%, versus 135bps at 3%. The governance trial and the 5 Nov print are two near-term binaries inside an eight-session window; sizing must be set *before* those resolve, not after.

**Why not zero:** the growth, penetration, beat-rate and revision data are strong enough that an outright pass forfeits real alpha, and the low correlation to SPY (0.277) and to AKAM (0.261) means NET does contribute some genuine diversification benefit despite the headline beta.
