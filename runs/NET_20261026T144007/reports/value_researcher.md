## CLOUDFLARE, INC. (NYSE: NET) — Value Investment Analysis

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: NARROW

Cloudflare operates a global edge network (300+ cities) delivering security (WAF, DDoS, Zero Trust), performance (CDN, DNS), and developer compute (Workers, R2, AI inference). It is a genuinely differentiated asset with three real moat sources:

- **Network effects (moderate–strong):** Every new customer and every new city adds capacity and threat intelligence, improving performance and security for all users. This is a real flywheel, but it is a supply-side network effect, not the marketplace-style lock-in of a Visa or Moody's.
- **Switching costs (moderate):** DNS, CDN and WAF are individually easy to swap. Stickiness comes from platform breadth — Workers code, Zero Trust policies, and data egress economics become embedded in customer architecture. Genuine, but not deep.
- **Intangible assets/brand (moderate):** Best-in-class developer brand and a large free-tier funnel that drives low-CAC land-and-expand. No meaningful patent or regulatory protection.

**What the moat is not:** there is no cost advantage (capex is 15.8% of revenue and rising) and no efficient-scale protection — it competes directly with hyperscalers (AWS CloudFront/Shield, Azure, Google Cloud) and specialists (Akamai, Fastly, Zscaler, Palo Alto, CrowdStrike), all with greater scale or focused incumbency.

**The decisive test — has the moat produced economic profits?** No. ROIC has been negative every year: −9.1% (2022), −8.4% (2023), −6.2% (2024), −4.0% (2025). The trend is improving but the business has never earned its cost of capital. A moat that cannot yet generate excess returns is an *aspirational* moat. Product breadth is widening (Workers AI, R2, Zero Trust), but **gross margin fell ~280bp in FY2025 (77.3% → 74.5%)** — the first meaningful decline — suggesting the network buildout and AI mix are costing more than pricing power is recovering. Moat: **Narrow, directionally stable but unproven.**

### 2. Financial Quality Score: 4 / 10

| SBC ($M) | 203 | 274 | 338 | **451** |
| SBC % revenue | 20.8% | 21.1% | 20.3% | **20.8%** |

**Positives:** Exceptional top line — ~35% four-year CAGR (38.1% five-year CAGR), gross margins in the mid-70s, FCF now positive and scaling ($324M, 15.0% FCF margin), and a fortress liquidity position ($4.10B cash and short-term investments against ~$3.70B total debt, ~$0.4B net cash; current ratio ~2.0x). Recent quarters show consistent non-GAAP EPS beats (Feb, May, Aug 2026) and revenue beats — momentum is real.

**Negatives — and they are serious:**
- **Persistent GAAP losses.** Seven years post-IPO and still loss-making. Operating margin actually worsened slightly in FY2025 (−9.3% → −9.6%) as gross margin compressed.
- **FCF is not owner earnings.** SBC of $451M (20.8% of revenue) exceeds FCF of $324M. Adjusted for SBC, FCF is **−$127M** in FY2025 (and negative in every year: −$243M, −$155M, −$143M, −$127M). The owner-earnings tool confirms this: **−$255M in FY2025** and negative in all four years. The reported FCF is an artifact of adding back a very large, recurring, real compensation cost.
- **Rising capital intensity.** Capex jumped to $343M (15.8% of revenue) for AI/network buildout — a structural headwind to future FCF conversion.
- **Relentless dilution.** Diluted shares 312.3M (2021) → 348.4M (2025), ~2.8% per year. No buybacks ($0), no dividend. Owners are diluted ~3% annually to fund compensation.

Score: **4/10** — excellent growth and balance sheet, but the earnings/FCF quality is poor and ROIC is negative. A Buffett-style "financial quality" grade cannot be higher.

The peer-median P/E of 44x is meaningless against a loss-maker; on P/S, mature infrastructure/security peers trade roughly 10–20x, putting NET at 2.5–5x the group. The 0.28% FCF yield compares with a ~4%+ risk-free rate — investors are paying for growth and nothing else.

**Intrinsic value (my own DCF scenarios, 10-yr projection, net cash added):**

**Intrinsic value estimate: ~$50–$100/share (midpoint ~$75).** Even the super-bull case — a decade of 40% FCF compounding with an 8% discount rate — only *approaches* today's $330. In other words, the current price discounts a near-flawless decade in which Cloudflare simultaneously (a) sustains ~30–40% growth, (b) expands FCF margins to 25–45%, and (c) normalizes SBC — all while competing with hyperscalers. If SBC is treated honestly as a cash cost (as I believe it must be), current owner earnings are negative and no conventional DCF supports the price.

**Price vs intrinsic value: ~230–560% premium; margin of safety is deeply negative (−70% to −85%).** The stock trades near its 52-week high (~$367) after a violent 12-month ride ($160 low in Feb 2026 → $367 in Sep 2026). Sell-side targets ($340–$430 from Barclays, Oppenheimer, BTIG; $181 outlier from Bernstein) have been repeatedly chased upward as the price rose — classic anchoring, not independent valuation.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — B / Adequate but Shareholder-Dilutive

- **Founder-led and long-tenured:** Matthew Prince (CEO) and Michelle Zatlyn co-founded the company in 2009; founder-led insiders retain meaningful stakes (exact ownership not verifiable in this data set). Strategic vision is coherent: free-tier land-and-expand, platform consolidation, edge/AI compute.
- **Capital allocation:** No empire-building — goodwill and intangibles are only $268M after seven years as a public company. No dividends, no buybacks; all capital is reinvested in the network. That is defensible for a growth franchise.
- **The governance blemish:** Compensation is funded overwhelmingly through stock — SBC at ~21% of revenue and ~2.8% annual dilution. This is a real, recurring transfer of value from owners to employees, and it flatters reported FCF. "Non-GAAP profitability" while GAAP losses persist for seven years strains credibility; the profitability goalpost has moved repeatedly. Debt is modest and cheap (interest expense $8.8M), so there is no balance-sheet mismanagement.
- **Grade: B.** Trustworthy operators with a genuine long-term vision, but they have not yet demonstrated the capital discipline (positive ROIC, controlled dilution) that commands an A.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL**

This is a *valuation* verdict, not a business-quality verdict. Cloudflare is a high-quality, narrow-moat franchise with exceptional growth and a clean balance sheet — the kind of business worth owning at the right price. But at ~$330 (≈53x sales, 353x FCF, 1,078x EBITDA, 0.28% FCF yield), the stock embeds two decades of perfection and offers **no margin of safety whatsoever**. My intrinsic value range of ~$50–$100 (even a generous ~$140 under aggressive assumptions) sits 60–85% below the current price. ROIC has never been positive; reported FCF is negative once SBC is honestly charged; gross margin is now compressing. A Buffett-style investor does not pay 3–5x a generous intrinsic value for a business that has not yet proven it can earn its cost of capital. **Sell / avoid; revisit only on a 70%+ drawdown or on evidence of durable positive GAAP ROIC with SBC normalizing below ~10% of revenue.**

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (what could make the sell call wrong)

1. **AI at the edge becomes a genuine second act.** If Workers AI/inference becomes the default platform for AI applications, TAM expands dramatically and 40%+ growth could persist far longer than my base case — the super-bull scenario is not fantasy, merely improbable.
2. **Sharp operating leverage.** If SBC normalizes toward 10% of revenue and gross margin stabilizes, GAAP earnings could inflect violently; the market would re-rate on earnings power rather than sales, and my "no earnings" objection weakens.
3. **Momentum and flow dynamics.** Beta 1.65, heavy retail/options participation and index flows can keep a richly valued name elevated far longer than fundamentals justify — a short/underweight can be painfully early.
4. **SBC treatment disagreement.** If the market continues to treat SBC as non-cash (as many software investors do), reported FCF of $324M and its 15% margin look far healthier than my owner-earnings math implies — though even then the FCF yield is only 0.28%.
5. **Strategic scarcity value.** A unique global network with developer mindshare could attract acquisition interest, though a $117B price tag makes a takeout unlikely.

**Conversely, the risks to *owning* it at this price are severe:** deceleration to ~20% growth compresses a 53x sales multiple violently; hyperscaler bundling pressures pricing; further AI capex depresses FCF; and continued 3% dilution erodes per-share value. Asymmetry favors the sidelines.
