# Nebius Group N.V. (NBIS) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $88.22 (Nov 20, 2026) | Shares ~300M | Market cap ~$26.5B | Net debt $8.65B | EV ~$35B*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **None (unproven)**

Nebius is an AI neocloud: it buys GPUs, builds data centers (4+ GW contracted), and rents compute capacity under multi-year contracts. Customers include Microsoft ($17.4B / 5 yrs) and Meta (~$12B capacity, ~$15B over five years). Trailing RPO is $38.7B; ARR reached $6.4B at Sept 2026, up 113% *in one quarter*.

Against the five moat sources, I can find only one candidate, and it fails the durability test:

| Source | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Intangible assets | None durable — no proprietary chips, no licenses, no pricing power over Microsoft/Meta |
| Switching costs | Low. AI workloads are increasingly portable across clouds; the customer base builds its own data centers |
| Cost advantage | They design their own racks/servers, but so does every peer; no structural cost edge |
| Network effects | None |
| Efficient scale | None — this is a scale-*taker* industry with hyperscalers, CoreWeave, Oracle, Lambda all adding capacity |

**The core problem:** the $38.7B backlog is a *contract*, not a moat. Moore's-law-driven GPU depreciation and rival capacity additions mean the industry's incremental economics must eventually compress toward cost of capital. Scarcity today is a supply-cycle rent, not a durable franchise. **Moat has not widened in any demonstrable way over five years — it didn't exist five years ago (this was Yandex N.V.).**

**Circle of competence test: FAILS.** I can describe this business, but I cannot forecast 2031 GPU-hour pricing or residual values on $60B+ of depreciating silicon with the confidence a value investment requires. That alone disqualifies it.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **2 / 10**

| Metric | FY2025 | Q3 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $530M | $981M |
| Gross margin | 68.6% | **77.6%** |
| Operating margin | –115% | **+5.1%** |
| EBITDA | $493M | $405M (41%) |
| Net income | +$82.5M | –$37.5M |
| Capex | $4.07B | **$7.30B (one quarter)** |
| Free cash flow | –$3.68B | **–$7.57B** |
| ROIC | **–6.4%** | ~0–1% annualized |

What's genuinely good: gross margin expanded 52% → 78%, operating income turned positive in Q3 2026, and Q3 prepayments covered **>100% of associated capex** (customer-funded build). Revenue growth is real and enormous (~$3.2B FY2026E, $12–13B guided for 2027).

What's disqualifying:
- **ROIC is negative and has never exceeded WACC.** Invested capital is now ~$30B+ against ~$200M of annualized EBIT. The hallmark of a moated business — sustained ROIC > WACC — is entirely absent.
- **Owner earnings: –$3.6B (FY2025), and roughly –$11B for the first nine months of 2026.** FCF conversion is not poor; it does not exist. Every dollar of growth consumes several dollars of capital.
- **Balance sheet transformed from strength to leverage in 21 months:** net cash of $2.4B (YE2024) → net debt $8.65B (Q3 2026); total debt $16.1B; PP&E $0.9B → $21.5B. Interest expense ran $112M in Q3 alone (~$450M/yr and climbing).
- **Headline profits are accounting noise.** FY2025 net income of +$82.5M came against a –$612M operating loss; Q1 2026 "EPS $2.40" came against a –$128M operating loss. These are non-operating revaluation gains (ClickHouse-related), not earnings power. Any screen showing P/E of 267x is looking at fiction.
- **Dilution:** weighted diluted shares 248M → 298M in twelve months. Receivables of $4.0B (~124 days of revenue) also merit scrutiny.

Cash-flow noise from prepayments aside, **nine-month 2026 capex was $15.4B against $4.2B of operating cash flow.**

## 3. Valuation Analysis — Fair value ≈ current price; **no margin of safety**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / 2026E revenue (~$3.2B) | ~11x |
| EV / 2027E revenue ($12–13B) | **~2.8x** |
| P/B (on $12.3B equity) | ~2.1x |
| Peer median P/E | 28x vs. NBIS 267x (not meaningful) |

EV/Sales of 2.8x on 2027 revenue looks almost cheap for a triple-digit grower — which is exactly why I did the reverse DCF rather than dismissing it.

**Reverse DCF:** To earn a 10% annual return from a $35B EV, Nebius needs roughly **$5.5–6B of steady-state owner earnings by ~2035** — approximately $19–20B of revenue at a ~30% owner-earnings margin, with **no further dilution**. That is a demanding path but not a fantasy, given the $38.7B backlog and $12–13B 2027 guide.

**Scenario valuation (my estimates):**

| Scenario | Assumptions | Value/share |
|---|---|---|
| **Bear** (35%) | AI capex digests 2028; GPU-hour pricing deflates; 2028 revenue ~$14B at 25% EBITDA, financed with ~$30B net debt | **$15–25** (impairment risk; equity levered) |
| **Base** (45%) | 2027 revenue $12.5B; 2029 ~$22B at 30–33% EBITDA; 11–12x EV/EBITDA | **$85–100** |
| **Bull** (20%) | Scarcity persists, prepayments fund build, 2029 revenue $30B+ at 35% margin, 14x | **$150–190** |

Probability-weighted value: **~$75–90/share.** The stock at $88.22 sits *at* base-case fair value. Downside is ~75%; upside is ~100% but only on a flawless, dilution-free, glut-free execution of $40–45B annual capex by a company with a $26.5B market cap.

**Critically: FY2027 guided capex ($40–45B) is 1.5–1.7x the entire market capitalization.** That must be funded with debt, more prepayments, or equity. If the equity window shuts — as it did between April ($167) and today ($88) — the financing math gets ugly fast.

That is not a value investment. That's a levered, capital-cycle bet priced for a good outcome.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Mixed: high competence, unproven discipline**

*Positives:* Founder-led (Arkady Volozh, Yandex founder) — genuine owner-operator DNA, world-class engineering pedigree, credible execution (ARR $3.0B → $6.4B in one quarter; >half of Microsoft's New Jersey capacity delivered on schedule). Negotiating $30B+ of take-or-pay contracts with Microsoft and Meta is a real achievement, and the >100% prepayment coverage on Q3 deals shows commercial sophistication. No dividends, no buybacks — appropriate for the growth stage.

*Concerns:* (1) **Capital allocation is the entire investment case and has no track record through a downturn.** Committing $40–45B/yr in capex against a $26.5B market cap is not conservatism — it is a maximum-conviction bet funded with leverage (debt/equity ~1.3x). (2) Operating losses persisted for years before Q3 2026 breakeven. (3) Significant shareholder dilution (248M → 298M+ shares). (4) Governance overhang from the Yandex restructuring — a Dutch-domiciled entity with Russian roots, a complex asset split, and limited insider-ownership disclosure in the data available to me (**not verified** — a genuine gap in this analysis). (5) M&A: the ClickHouse stake revaluations that flatter reported earnings suggest a financial-engineering layer I would want examined line by line.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID** (not a value investment)

Not a Sell on business quality — the franchise is real, growing explosively, and now gross-margin-rich. A Sell because at $88 nothing in my framework is satisfied:

- **Moat:** none demonstrable; ROIC –6.4% and never above WACC
- **Financial quality:** negative owner earnings of ~$11B annualized; net debt $8.65B and rising
- **Margin of safety:** zero. Base-case intrinsic value is $85–100 — the price *is* the fair value on a good outcome
- **Circle of competence:** fails. I cannot underwrite residual GPU values and 2031 compute pricing
- **Asymmetry:** unfavorable — the downside requires ~$30B of debt to be serviced by assets that may be worth less than book

If you own it, this is a momentum/growth position and should be sized as such — not as a value holding. If the stock revisits **$45–55** (2x downside-weighted optionality, ~0.6x base-case value), the risk/reward would finally offer the 25–30% discount I require to look seriously. Until then: pass.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to owning it (why I'm bearish):**
1. **Financing risk (dominant).** $40–45B of 2027 capex vs. $26.5B market cap and $16.1B existing debt. A closed equity market forces expensive debt or stalled growth.
2. **Customer concentration.** Essentially all backlog traces to a handful of counterparties — Microsoft and Meta — who are simultaneously customers, competitors, and the largest buyers of GPUs. They hold the pricing power on renewal.
3. **Technological obsolescence / depreciation.** GPU useful lives are short and residual values illiquid. Depreciation ($355M/quarter and compounding) probably understates true economic wear; a Glut would force impairments on a $21.5B PP&E base.
4. **AI capex cycle reversal.** If hyperscaler spending digests in 2028, spot GPU pricing collapses and this equity is levered into exactly the wrong moment.
5. **Dilution and governance.** Share count +20% in a year; Yandex legacy structure.

**Risks to my thesis (what would make me wrong):**
1. Compute scarcity persists through 2029 and contract prices on renewals *rise* — in which case ROIC could exceed WACC and the moat argument becomes "efficient scale in a supply-constrained market."
2. Prepayments keep funding >100% of capex, making the model effectively customer-financed and dilution-free.
3. Management proves disciplined: capex moderated after 2027, margins expand to 35%+, owner earnings inflect.
4. Strategic acquisition of the platform at a premium by a hyperscaler or Nvidia.
