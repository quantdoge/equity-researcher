## SECTOR BRIEF — Nebius Group N.V. (NBIS)

**Classification caveat:** FMP tags NBIS as "Technology / Software – Application." That is wrong for investment purposes. Nebius is an **AI infrastructure / GPU-cloud "neocloud"** — it sells rented accelerated compute. The correct comparable set is CoreWeave (CRWV), IREN, Applied Digital (APLD), Core Scientific (CORZ) and Lambda/Crusoe (private), with Oracle OCI, Microsoft Azure and Amazon AWS as both customers and competitors. Mega-cap tech is not a valid peer group here.

---

### 1. Sector Overview

The neocloud sector rents GPU capacity to AI developers, model labs and hyperscalers that cannot build fast enough internally. Synergy Research forecasts the neocloud market approaching **~$400bn by 2031 (~58% CAGR)**, against Big-5 hyperscaler AI capex of roughly **$780–800bn in 2026**, with sell-side 2027 estimates of **$1.2–1.3tn** (Goldman baseline: ~$7.6tn cumulative 2026–31). Demand is genuinely contracted, not speculative — take-or-pay agreements with investment-grade tenants now run into the tens of billions (CoreWeave–Meta ~$14bn; CoreWeave backlog ~$55bn). But the sector is **brutally capital-intensive and increasingly credit-financed**: 2026–27 capex is being funded with debt, customer prepayments and vendor financing, and the sector's binding constraint has shifted from demand to **power, transformers and financing cost**. Equity markets re-rated the group violently in 2026 — NBIS is ~70% off its June high, CRWV ~70% off its May high - even as reported revenue kept accelerating.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Demand vastly exceeds deliverable capacity.** NBIS annualised run-rate revenue doubled sequentially to **$6.4bn** in Q3'26; management guides **$12–13bn revenue for 2027**, implying ~2x again. CRWV guides **$12.4–13.2bn** for 2026 from $5.13bn in FY25. Contracted backlog, not spot demand, is the driver.
- **Power scarcity is a moat, not just a cost.** Interconnection queues, gas-turbine lead times and grid constraints mean capacity already permitted and energised commands a premium. This supports multi-year pricing and long-dated take-or-pay contracts — and it structurally favours operators with secured power (IREN's owned power, APLD's campuses) over pure lessors.
- **Customer prepayments convert customer demand into zero-cost project finance.** NBIS states capex is ~100% covered by customer loans/prepayments on long-term contracts; deferred revenue rose **+57.4% QoQ** in Q3. This lets neoclouds grow without fully diluting equity — a genuine structural advantage over the last cycle's data-centre REITs.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Financing and credit risk is now the dominant variable.** NBIS carries **~$14bn of debt**; net debt rose >**1,200% QoQ** in Q3 to roughly **8x TTM adjusted EBITDA**, with negative operating cash flow. Interest expense reached **$112.5m in a single quarter** — larger than the entire quarterly net loss. Michael Burry's public short (Aug'26) and the November neocloud rout are a repricing of this, not of demand.
- **GPU depreciation and technology obsolescence.** D&A is already **$354.7m/quarter** at NBIS, ~36% of revenue. Each NVIDIA generation (Rubin cycle) shortens the economic life of installed Hopper/Blackwell fleets; if contract renewals reset to lower $/GPU-hour, high headline gross margins compress fast. T. Rowe and JPMorgan both flag accelerated depreciation as under-modelled.
- **Customer concentration and role conflict.** Hyperscalers are simultaneously the largest customers and the most credible long-run competitors — they can insource or reprice at renewal. This is a structural squeeze on terminal pricing power, exacerbated by physical build delays (NBIS narrowed 2026 guidance on colocation timing).

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position — **STRONG on economics, AVERAGE on scale/balance sheet (net: above average)**

**Rationale for strength:**
- NBIS is the **#2 neocloud globally** by contracted scale ($6.4bn ARR) with the **fastest growth in the peer set** and the **first positive operating-income quarter** in the group (Q3'26: +$50.4m, +5.1% margin) — a genuine inflection that CRWV, IREN and APLD have not yet shown.
- Unit economics are best-in-class: 47.5% adjusted EBITDA margin and 77.6% reported gross margin are at the top of the peer group on a normalised basis.
- Differentiated positioning: EU/Netherlands domicile with sovereign-AI and data-residency appeal, plus optionality from Avride (autonomy), Toloka (data) and TripleTen (edtech) — non-correlated assets most peers lack.

**Rationale for discount to CRWV / caution:**
- Revenue scale is **~2.5x smaller than CRWV** and capex budgets are correspondingly smaller ($35–39bn for CRWV in 2026), meaning NBIS has less ability to pre-buy GPU allocation and less pricing leverage with NVIDIA.
- Leverage is disproportionately high **relative to its own EBITDA base** (~8x), interest expense now exceeds quarterly revenue growth contribution to profit, and the company is not pre-tax breakeven until **Q4 2027** on its own guidance. This makes NBIS the higher-beta expression of the sector's financing risk.

**Those seeking the sector's upside should own NBIS over IREN/APLD/CORZ** (better margins, growth, and a real inflection) but with explicit acceptance of credit/concentration risk that CRWV carries at larger scale.

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL** (fundamentally attractive, tactically hostile)

- **Demand (attractive):** ~58% CAGR to 2031 with contracted, investment-grade-backed backlog. This is not a bubble in demand.
- **Economics (deteriorating):** Capital intensity, GPU obsolescence and rising funding costs are transferring value from equity to lenders and NVIDIA. Rolling hyperscaler FCF approaching zero by early 2027 (T. Rowe) caps the sector's ability to fund neoclouds at current terms.
- **Cycle position:** **Early-to-mid growth in the underlying demand cycle, but late-cycle in the equity/financing cycle.** The 2026 de-rating (NBIS -69%, CRWV -70%) is a rational repricing of leverage, not a demand signal — and it is not obviously finished while the credit markets remain the marginal funder.
- **Verdict:** Neutral. Attractive for **contract-backed, power-secured operators with manageable leverage**; unattractive for **pre-profit, prepayment-funded names** if AI capex guidance decelerates or credit spreads widen. Any negative revision to 2027 hyperscaler capex is the sector's single largest downside catalyst.
