## Nebius Group N.V. (NBIS) — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment
*As of 20 November 2026. Sources: SEC Form 6-K (12 Nov 2026), Form 20-F FY2025 references, Reuters, BIS, CNAS, company newsroom.*

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

Nebius is a **Dutch-domiciled (Amsterdam) N.V., NASDAQ-listed, foreign private issuer** — the renamed Yandex N.V. (rebranded Aug 2024). It derives essentially all revenue from GPU/AI cloud capacity, and its footprint is now a barbell of **US + Europe + Middle East**.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① Middle East war — Israel + Gulf data-centre exposure (Probability: High, Impact: High)**
The FY2025 20-F risk factors explicitly cite *"ongoing and potential escalation of geopolitical conflicts, including hostilities involving the United States, Israel, and Iran."* This is not hypothetical: the **2026 Iran–Israel war** has seen Iranian strikes on Gulf data centres, and analysts now describe AI data centres as military-relevant targets (MEI, Asia Society Policy, Feb–Mar 2026 reporting). Nebius has ~134MW+ of Israeli leases with Mega Or and is anchoring Saudi NEOM capacity. A single successful strike, or an insurance/force-majeure dispute, would strand capex against 7–12-year lease liabilities. *Note the Q3-26 guidance cut was already driven by third-party capacity delays (Reuters, 12-Nov-26) — the model has minimal slack.*

**② US export-control tightening targeted partly at non-US neoclouds (Probability: High, Impact: Medium-High)**
BIS published the **interim final rule "Addressing Remote Access to Advanced Semiconductors" on 16-Nov-26, effective 19-Nov-26** (comments to 19-Jan-27), and formally rescinded the AI Diffusion Rule. Critically, trade press states *"non-US neoclouds such as **Nebius** and Nscale would need a US licence to install NVIDIA systems in Johor or Singapore unless US-headquartered."* This (a) caps Nebius's Asia/India expansion, (b) forces US-licence dependency for its core product, and (c) hands a structural advantage to CoreWeave et al. Its **Dutch domicile is a regulatory handicap**, not just a tax/law choice.

**③ Russian legacy / "Russian-adjacent" perception risk (Probability: Medium, Impact: Medium-High)**
Founder-CEO Arkady Volozh was EU-sanctioned and **delisted in March 2024** after condemning the invasion; the Russia business was divested with the company claiming it severed all ties. But the tail is live: reporting indicates Nebius retained a **residual economic interest (~11% as of 31-Mar-26)** tied to the divested assets, and the TBIJ investigation into **Toloka's** Russian gig platform being used to train Moscow facial-recognition/surveillance tools remains an ESG and counterparty-diligence liability. **Concrete precedent of state action:** Italy's PM Meloni used **"golden power" to veto a Nebius/Yandex-linked acquisition of an Italian cloud provider** over Russia links — proof that European FDI screening treats this company as a national-security file. Expect repeat blocks on EU M&A.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Export controls & sanctions:** Nebius publishes a formal **Trade Controls Policy (Nov 2025)** covering BIS/OFAC/EU — a governance positive. **No OFAC, BIS or DOJ enforcement action against Nebius was identified** in this review.
- **EU regulatory stack:** as a Dutch cloud/AI provider it sits under **GDPR, the EU AI Act** (Article 99 fines up to €35m or 7% of turnover), **NIS2** (data centres are in-scope critical infrastructure), and the **EU Data Act** cloud-switching duties. The EU added **145 more Russia sanctions designations on 16-Nov-26**, raising ongoing screening burden.
- **FDI screening:** Italian golden-power veto (above); Dutch government engagement is favourable (PM Jetten met Volozh on compute access) — the Netherlands is a *supporter*, Italy is a *blocker*.
- **Litigation:** **No material active securities class action identified** (aggregator shows 0 active NBIS class actions). However, a **~60–65% drawdown from the 52-week high and a guidance cut** create textbook 10b-5 exposure; expect filers within 60–90 days. This is a *watch item*, not yet a reserve.
- **Subsidiary-specific:** **Avride** (autonomous delivery robots, Austin TX) carries NHTSA/state AV permitting risk; **TripleTen** (edtech) carries consumer-protection/refund risk.
- **Related-party / financial-integrity overlay:** Q3-26 revenue $980.8m of which **$325.1m from "strategic partnerships" that may be non-recurring**; deferred revenue +$7.8bn funded by customer prepayments; net debt up ~1,243% QoQ; contract liabilities of **$32.9bn beyond 12 months**. Lumpy, prepayment-funded revenue is exactly the profile regulators and short-sellers stress-test.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. 🚩 **Toloka/Russia surveillance linkage** (TBIJ 2023/24) — unresolved reputational and potential sanctions-adjacent exposure; now part-owned by Bezos Expeditions, raising LP-level scrutiny.
2. 🚩 **Golden-power veto precedent** — European governments will block Nebius M&A; growth-by-acquisition in the EU is effectively foreclosed.
3. 🚩 **Licence dependency on BIS for third-country NVIDIA deployments** — a compliance bottleneck embedded in the growth plan, not a one-off fine.
4. 🚩 **Revenue concentration in two US hyperscalers (~80% of commitments)** — not a compliance breach, but it converts a commercial issue into a *US policy* issue: any US restriction on Microsoft/Meta GPU-capacity off-take hits the entire revenue base.
5. 🚩 **War-zone asset concentration** (Israel/Gulf) with long-dated lease liabilities and no disclosed force-majeure/insurance adequacy.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not "Very High" — there is **no current Russia revenue, no disclosed enforcement action, and a real trade-controls compliance function**. But the combination of (i) *in-theatre* Middle East assets, (ii) a **newly effective US export-control rule that names Nebius's business model specifically**, and (iii) an unresolved Russian-origin perception problem that has already produced a European state veto makes this materially riskier than a US-domiciled peer such as CoreWeave. The domicile is the crux: **Dutch incorporation buys EU market access but now costs US licensing friction.**

**What would change the rating:** ⬆️ to Very High on any strike damage to Israeli capacity, a BIS licence denial, or a securities class action filing. ⬇️ to Medium on a firm US-headquartered licensing carve-out, or evidence of a definitive break with residual Russian economic interests.
