# Oracle Corporation (ORCL) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $103.88 (25 Nov 2026) | Market cap ~$303B | Diluted shares 2.914B | EV ~$428B (incl. $124.9B net debt)*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat Rating: NARROW — and rapidly NARROWING**

Oracle's historic moat was one of the best in software: the Oracle Database and Fusion ERP/NetSuite stacks carry brutal switching costs (a core ledger or database migration is a multi-year, career-risking project). That franchise still exists — Cloud Applications revenue grew 10% to $4.2B in Q1 FY27 and software/license support throws off high-margin annuity cash.

But the company is no longer primarily that business. The evidence:

| FY (May) | 2021 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 40.5 | 50.0 | 53.0 | 57.4 | 67.4 |
| Gross margin | 80.6% | 72.9% | 71.4% | 70.5% | **65.8%** |
| Operating margin | 37.6% | 26.2% | 29.0% | 30.8% | 30.6% |
| **ROIC** | — | 14.2% | 14.8% | 14.1% | **10.3%** |
| Capex ($B) | — | 8.7 | 6.9 | 21.2 | **55.7** |

Gross margin has fallen **~1,480bps in five years** and ROIC has fallen from 14.8% to 10.3% — now roughly equal to Oracle's cost of capital. In Buffett's terms, that is the definition of a moat being spent. The mix has shifted from ~80%-margin software to ~30–40%-margin racked compute sold to a concentrated set of AI customers. IaaS revenue grew 121% to $7.4B in Q1 FY27; that is a **commodity compute business priced on cost of capital**, where Oracle is a price-taker competing with Microsoft (unlimited balance sheet, zero incremental cost of capital), Amazon, and Google.

Concentration is the second moat problem: RPO is $664B (from $137.8B a year ago), of which **at least $300B is tied to OpenAI** — a cash-consuming private company. Oracle is effectively a leveraged lessor to one tenant.

**Honest assessment: the database/ERP franchise is a wide-moat asset. The consolidated entity is not.**

| Operating cash flow ($B) | 18.7 | 20.8 | 32.0 |

| D&A ($B) | 6.1 | 6.2 | 9.3 |

| Dividends paid ($B) | (4.4) | (4.7) | (5.8) |
| Net debt ($B) | 84.0 | 93.3 | **124.9** |
| Total equity ($B) | 9.2 | 21.0 | 43.1 |
| Goodwill + intangibles ($B) | 69.1 | 66.8 | 65.5 |
| **Tangible book equity ($B)** | **(59.9)** | **(45.8)** | **(22.4)** |
| Diluted shares (B) | 2.823 | 2.866 | 2.914 |

What the score reflects:

- **FCF conversion has broken.** FY26 net income of $17.1B produced negative $23.7B of free cash flow. Q1 FY27 FCF was negative $5.0B despite record $23.1B operating cash flow — of which **$11.4B was customer prepayments**, not earnings.
- **Owner earnings are approximately zero.** Net income ($17.1B) + D&A ($9.3B) − maintenance capex. On the tool's total-capex definition owner earnings are **−$29.3B**. Even on the generous "maintenance capex = D&A" assumption ($9.3B), the true figure is far lower than $17.1B, because D&A is set to triple: PP&E has gone from $21.5B to $129.6B in two years, so D&A will migrate toward $25–30B annually. I estimate **maintenance-adjusted owner earnings of roughly $0–3B today.**
- **Balance sheet has flipped from fortress to levered.** Net debt $124.9B and rising; tangible book equity is **negative $22.4B**. The reported 7.0x P/B is meaningless — there is no tangible book to anchor it. Retained earnings are still negative (−$4.3B); equity exists only because of share issuance.
- **The dividend is debt-funded.** $5.8B paid out against negative $23.7B FCF, while simultaneously raising $20B of ATM equity. Paying a dividend while issuing stock and borrowing to fund capex is circular capital allocation.
- **Dilution.** Share count has risen every year (2.766B → 2.914B, +5.4%) plus ~$20B of new equity in Q1 FY27. Shareholders are being diluted to fund depreciation-heavy assets.
- **One genuine positive:** revenue is accelerating (6% → 8% → 17% → ≥34% guided for FY27) with massive contracted backlog. GAAP earnings quality is decent ex-SBC (FY26 GAAP NI was flattered by a one-time Ampere investment gain in Q2 FY26, which is why Q2 FY27 EPS is guided down 18–19%).

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples at $103.88:**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E — FY26 GAAP ($5.83) | 17.8x |
| P/E — FY26 GAAP normalized ex-Ampere gain (~$5.00) | ~21x |
| **P/E — FY27E non-GAAP ($8.10 guided)** | **12.8x** |
| P/E — FY30E non-GAAP ($21+ target) | 4.9x |
| EV/EBITDA — FY26 ($33.4B) | 12.8x |
| EV/EBITDA — FY27E adj. (~$49B) | 8.7x |
| P/FCF | **Negative** |
| FCF yield | **−7.8%** |
| Dividend yield | 1.93% |
| Peer median P/E | 27.3x (ORCL at 34.7% discount) |

The 35% P/E discount to software peers is **not** a free lunch: peers are asset-light, net-cash, and convert >85% of earnings to cash. Oracle now consumes cash. On EV/EBITDA the discount largely disappears.

**Scenario-based intrinsic value (my own model, not the tool's DCF — which is unusable given negative trailing FCF):**

| Scenario | Prob. | FY30 assumptions | Owner earnings/sh | Multiple | Value today (9% disc.) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull — Oracle executes** | 40% | $225B rev, 40% non-GAAP op margin, interest $10B, SBC/amort adjusted | ~$15.50 | 18x | **~$182** |
| **Base — haircut to plan** | 30% | $170B rev, 35% margin, net debt $180B | ~$9.50 | 13x | **~$125** |
| **Bear — OpenAI stress / stranded capex** | 30% | $120B rev, 30% margin, $250B PP&E depreciating, interest $13B | ~$6.90 | 10x | **~$69** |

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $131/share.** Range: $69–$182.

At $103.88 the stock trades at a **~21% discount to this weighted estimate** — at the very bottom edge of my 20–30% margin-of-safety band, with dispersion roughly 2.6x between bear and bull.

**Reverse DCF check:** the current $428B EV requires Oracle to reach roughly $40–45B of sustainable owner earnings by FY31 — i.e., it prices in *most* of the FY30 plan being delivered, but assigns no terminal multiple for continued growth. The market is now roughly pricing the base case. That is "fair," not "cheap."

**Critically: growth at ROIC ≈ WACC creates no value.** Oracle is deploying $55B+/year at a 10.3% return against a ~9% cost of capital. Even if the $225B revenue target is hit, shareholders earn their cost of capital plus a thin spread — while bearing leverage and single-counterparty risk.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Mixed-to-poor on capital allocation, strong on alignment.**

- **Alignment:** Larry Ellison's ~40% stake remains the single best governance feature. Management owns the outcome alongside you.
- **Capital allocation record is the problem.** Oracle repurchased $3.2B of stock in FY24 and $1.5B in FY25 at average prices well above today's $104, then issued ~$20B of equity via ATM in Q1 FY27 — buying high and selling low is the cardinal capital-allocation sin. Buybacks have now essentially stopped ($206M in FY26).
- **Promotional targets.** The October 2026 Investor Day promised "$225B+ revenue (35%+ CAGR), 40%+ non-GAAP operating margin, $21+ non-GAAP EPS, positive FCF in 2H FY28, $30B+ FCF in FY29." Every one of these is a non-GAAP construct that excludes $5B+/yr of stock compensation and intangible amortization. Guidance credibility is now load-bearing for the equity.
- **Credibility stretch:** management "committed" to limiting additional capital raises to $20B through FY30 — *excluding* refinancing, and *after* $20B of equity and with a stated reliance on "vendor financing, customer-provided hardware and customer prepayments." That is off-balance-sheet-adjacent financing dressed as discipline.
- **The credit market is voting.** Oracle's 5-year CDS hit a record ~356bps in November, from below 80bps a year earlier. Bondholders — who get paid first — are far more nervous than equity analysts with $170–285 price targets.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD — not a value investment**

I cannot issue a Buy. Three independent tests fail:

1. **Circle of competence: partially outside.** I understand the database and ERP franchise. I do not have an edge in forecasting 5GW of AI datacenter leasing economics, depreciation schedules on GPU-heavy assets, or the solvency of OpenAI. Buffett's rule applies: no opinion is better than a confident wrong one on a business I can't underwrite.
2. **Business quality has degraded below the threshold.** ROIC 14.8% → 10.3%, gross margin −1,480bps, tangible equity negative $22.4B, FCF negative for two consecutive years and guided negative through FY28. Great businesses do not require $20B equity raises and record CDS spreads to fund growth.
3. **Margin of safety is insufficient for the risk.** Weighted intrinsic value ~$131 vs $103.88 = ~21% discount, but with a bear case near $69 and a bull case near $182. For a business of this quality and leverage profile I require a **30–40% discount** — i.e., roughly **$80–90/share**.

**Practical guidance:** Existing holders with a long horizon may reasonably hold a reduced position, because the base case is intact and FY27E 12.8x non-GAAP EPS is not demanding. I would **not commit new capital** here. A genuine margin of safety emerges below ~$85; conversely, if FY28 capex does not visibly step down or if RPO growth stalls, the bear case becomes the base case and the equity is worth materially less than today's price.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to the downside (dominant):**
- **Counterparty concentration.** ≥$300B of $664B RPO is OpenAI. A funding interruption, contract renegotiation, or shift to a cheaper provider would strand ~$130B of newly built PP&E.
- **Refinancing / credit spiral.** $156B debt today, heading toward $250B+. A downgrade below investment grade raises interest expense by billions and is explicitly the one thing management says it will protect. CDS at 356bps says the market doubts it.
- **Depreciation cliff.** D&A is $9.3B against $55.7B of annual capex. When the bill arrives, GAAP earnings — and the non-GAAP-to-GAAP gap — compress hard.
- **Obsolescence.** GPU and networking assets have short economic lives; a 6–7 year depreciation schedule may prove optimistic, requiring write-downs.
- **Dilution.** Further equity issuance at $104 is far more painful than the $150+ ATM done in Q1 FY27.

**Risks to the upside (what would make me wrong):**
- Oracle has beaten aggressive targets for a decade. If FY27 revenue lands above $95B and capex peaks in FY28 as promised, FCF inflects violently and the $30B FY29 target becomes a $60B+ run-rate by FY31 — at which point today's $303B market cap is ~5x FCF.
- If the SaaS/database annuity ($21B+ software + $17B cloud apps) is valued separately at 7x revenue (~$265B), the AI infrastructure build is being priced at close to zero — implicit optionality.
- Customer prepayments and vendor financing are shifting capex off Oracle's balance sheet more than the bears assume.

**Bottom line:** Oracle at $323 was a bubble. Oracle at $104 is a reasonably priced, highly levered, capital-consuming infrastructure bet with a shrinking moat and an unmeasurable tail. That is a trade, not an investment. **Hold / avoid; buy below ~$85.**
