**Alt-data read:** Operating momentum is genuinely strong — cloud infrastructure revenue **+121%** last quarter, RPO backlog **$664B**. Revenue should track at or above consensus. But three things cap the quality of the beat:
1. **FCF was -$5.4B** on **$28.5B** quarterly capex.

3. **EPS estimate ($1.89) sits below the year-ago actual ($2.26)** — suggesting the +37.8% Dec '25 quarter contained a one-off, and/or estimates have been cut.

**Verdict: In-Line to Slightly Above on revenue/EPS. Below on FCF.** The critical point: **the debate has migrated off the income statement entirely.** A clean EPS beat is unlikely to re-rate the stock while CDS spreads sit at records.

If the credit tape stabilises, this is where asymmetric upside lives. **That is a trade for confirmation, not for prediction.**

The **news sentiment score is definitively broken** (+1.00 on a wall of negative headlines) and required full manual override, meaning the sentiment pillar rests on 20 hand-read headlines, not a quantitative series.

**Why not Low:** the **price, Google Trends, earnings-surprise, and credit-market** streams are high-quality, recent (through Nov 26), and internally consistent. The credit/force-majeure evidence is primary-source and specific (named banks, named projects, exact spreads), and it is the most decision-relevant input in the packet.

**Data limitations & lag:** Google Trends is weekly and normalised (0–100 peak-relative), so it measures *relative attention*, not demand — a -76% decline from a hype peak overstates fundamental deterioration. Earnings-surprise averages are distorted by a single +37.8% quarter. News is a 10–14 day window only. **All alt data here is a leading indicator, not a verdict** — and in this case the leading indicator is a credit signal pointing somewhere the equity consensus has not yet gone.
