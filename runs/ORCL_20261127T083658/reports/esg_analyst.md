### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH** (rising)

- **Target architecture is weaker than it looks.** Oracle now frames climate goals as (i) net-zero by 2050, (ii) 100% carbon-free electricity at *custom AI data centres* by **2035**, and (iii) a **20% reduction in emissions per MW of IT capacity by 2030**. The former "100% renewable energy by 2025" ambition has effectively been superseded by an **intensity metric** — an intensity target is arithmetically compatible with a large *increase* in absolute Scope 1+2 emissions during an AI capacity ramp. This is the single most important greenwashing/credibility flag in the E pillar.
- **Absolute footprint is growing fast.** Data-centre energy commentary puts Oracle's data-centre energy growth at a ~24% CAGR, with Oracle mid-pack-to-weak on Scope 1+2 emissions *intensity* versus hyperscaler peers (~10th) and slightly better on Scope 3 (~8th) — directional third-party analysis, not audited.
- **Mitigants are real:** >1.7 GW of Texas wind; 2 GW carbon-free offtake request in New Mexico; closed-loop cooling; a **$50m** commitment to southern New Mexico water systems; 75% of the 1,000+ acre Michigan site preserved as farmland/wetland/open space; funding of utility infrastructure so other customers don't cross-subsidise.
- **Execution risk:** the New Mexico gas-turbine plan was abandoned for Bloom Energy **fuel cells** after the State Land Office denied pipeline-related permits; a ~400m cubic-foot/day gas pipeline remains contested with 12 states intervening. Fuel-cell power is materially more expensive per MWh than grid power — an unquantified operating-cost headwind at that campus, plus a **$1m** CO2-capture feasibility study that signals residual fossil emissions.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Restructuring at scale:** ~**21,000** positions eliminated over FY26 (~13% of workforce) with **$1.84bn** of severance paid — cash diverted from the AI buildout; some analyst estimates run to 30,000 (~18%), with further WARN notices filed into Nov-2026. Severance of up to 26 weeks is comparatively generous, which limits wrongful-termination and reputational tail risk, but attrition of institutional knowledge at an HCM-software vendor is a product-quality and sales-execution risk.
- **Social licence:** Project Jupiter drew packed, polarised public meetings; New Mexico officials have investigated whether pro-project public comments were orchestrated — an integrity/astroturfing allegation that raises regulatory-relations risk. Local water and air-quality opposition is organised and litigious (Food & Water Watch et al.).
- **Diversity/labour litigation overhang:** prior shareholder and diversity-related litigation (including demands to replace the chair) keeps Oracle on controversy screens.
- **Product safety/consumer protection:** no material cloud-outage or breach liability quantified in available sources — but note I could not review 10-K risk factors, so this is a **known gap**.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD:** Oracle has substantial EU operations; a large non-EU parent with significant EU turnover falls into the later CSRD waves (reporting roughly FY2028, subject to the Omnibus simplification/delay). **Exposure: Medium** — Oracle already publishes CSRD-aligned "cloud infrastructure" guidance, but limited-assurance-grade Scope 3 data for GPU/hardware supply chains is the hard part.
- **EU Energy Efficiency Directive, Art. 12:** mandatory data-centre energy-performance reporting for EU facilities — **exposure: High** on data granularity.
- **EU AI Act:** Oracle as both AI deployer and (via OCI/partners) supplier of AI capability — transparency, GPAI and, for financial-sector customers, DORA critical-ICT-provider obligations. **Exposure: Medium-High**, and rising.
- **UK:** Oracle publishes a UK Carbon Reduction Plan, so it is captured by PPN 06/21 in UK public procurement — a live revenue gate.
- **US:** SEC climate-disclosure rule remains effectively inoperative; state-level exposure runs through **grid interconnection and permitting (NM, MI, TX)** rather than disclosure. New Mexico's Land Office and pipeline permitting are the binding constraints.
- **CBAM:** not material.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD** (trending toward ESG Risk)

Oracle is not an environmental laggard by intent — it has credible renewables procurement and genuinely improved disclosure — but it is a **governance laggard under financial stress**, and the two live securities/bondholder class actions alleging disclosure failures around the AI buildout are exactly the kind of controversy that ESG risk models escalate to **"high/severe."** The combination of founder control, 413m pledged shares, a dual-CEO transition, an intensity-only climate target during an absolute-emissions ramp, and a 13% workforce reduction funds-shifting into capex is a textbook convergence of G and S risk. I could not retrieve an MSCI or Sustainalytics score to benchmark — treat the rating as evidence-based rather than score-anchored.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE** (for the thesis, as a risk overlay)

- **The binding constraint is Governance, not Environment.** Disclosure-litigation risk plus a controlling shareholder with pledged collateral is a **cost-of-capital** problem, and cost of capital is precisely what an AI buildout funded with $108bn of debt is most sensitive to. A 100bp penalty on incremental AI-buildout debt is worth more to the equity story than any plausible emissions fine.
- **Environmental risk is a rising opex/permitting tax, not an existential one.** Fuel-cell substitution, contested pipelines and water politics in New Mexico create schedule risk on revenue recognition. Watch: the shift from the 2025 "100% renewable" pledge to a 2030 *intensity* target — if Scope 1+2 rises in absolute terms while Oracle claims progress, expect index-level ESG downgrades and exclusion from Article 8/9 funds.
- **Social risk is transitionary but reputationally compounding** — and awkward for a company selling HCM software while cutting 13% of staff and opposing shareholder-access rights.
- **Positives that keep this from being an unmitigated negative:** single share class, EY audit, 82% say-on-pay, ~$1.7–2 GW of real renewable procurement, closed-loop cooling, and generous severance that caps employment-liability tails.

**Bottom line:** ESG factors here are a **material, quantifiable drag** on valuation through the discount rate rather than through fines. Any long thesis on Oracle should underwrite (i) litigation reserves of roughly $0.7–2.5bn, (ii) elevated new-issue spreads versus peers with cleaner disclosure, and (iii) permitting-driven capex slippage at New Mexico. Absent a governance catalyst — an independent chair, a cap or unwind of Ellison's pledge, restoration of in-person meetings, and absolute (not intensity) emissions targets — the ESG rating stays Laggard.
