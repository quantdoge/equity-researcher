# UBER — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**As of October 17, 2026 | Price $70.35 | Market cap $143.7B | 36,600 employees (pre-restructuring)**

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (trending weaker)

**Positives:**
- **Independent Chairperson** — Dr. Ronald Sugar (former Northrop Grumman CEO) has chaired since July 2018; bylaws require the chair be independent. Board includes credible independents (Ursula Burns, Revathi Advaithi). No founder-control pathology; Khosrowshahi is a professional CEO, not a controlling shareholder.
- **No dual-class super-voting structure** — one share, one vote, unusual for a 2019-vintage tech IPO.
- **A genuine insider-buying cluster** near the lows (see §3) — the strongest alignment signal in this file.

**Concerns:**
- **Board refreshment lag** — Sugar's tenure is ~8.3 years and he is 78; combined with two CEO-adjacent seats, the board is long-tenured through the most turbulent period in the company's history (AV pivot, 10% RIF, $1B EU fine).
- **Stock pledging is permitted** for directors and employees per the 2026 proxy. This is a real governance weakness: pledged shares can force sales at the worst moment and decouple insider incentives from shareholders.
- **CFO churn** — Prashanth Mahendra-Rajah transitioned out of the CFO role (8-K dated Jan 30, 2026; advisory role through July 1, 2026) and was classified a "former officer" in Form 4 filings by April 2026. A CFO exit mid-pivot to a $10B AV capital program is a watch item for financial-control continuity.
- **Conflict-of-interest allegations** — Consumer Watchdog has publicly demanded disclosure of an executive's personal financial interest in a contingency-fee ballot measure. Unresolved; small dollar impact, outsized optics.
- **Shareholder-pressure posture is reactive** — the company agreed to a third-party **civil-rights audit** and is facing investor demands for **driver-pay transparency** only after being pushed. ESG/governance engagement is defensive rather than proactive.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **AV partner dependency and disintermediation — the existential risk.** Waymo is reportedly exploring exit from its exclusive Uber partnership, with the Atlanta/Austin arrangement expected to end by 2028. Analysts estimate **~40% of Mobility bookings are exposed to AV substitution**. Uber is responding with a **$10B AV/AI commitment**, but its network-aggregator model is precisely what AV fleets can bypass. The brand also absorbs partner safety failures (Avride/NHTSA) it does not control.
2. **Execution risk from a simultaneous 10% RIF and capital pivot.** ~3,300 jobs cut Sept 2, 2026 — the largest since COVID — to consolidate management layers and fund AV/AI. Attrition, institutional knowledge loss, and morale damage compound with **key-person departures**: robotaxi lead **Sarfraz Maredia leaves Nov 16, 2026**, and the CFO seat changed hands during the same window.
3. **Regulatory licensing and platform-access concentration.** Uber's history in London (licence withdrawal and reinstatement) demonstrates that a single municipal authority can impair a major market. Layered on top: Chinese AV partner **Pony.ai's London robotaxi launch** introduces geopolitical/CFIUS-adjacent scrutiny, and Freight's turnaround rests on a single new leader (Rebecca Tinucci) with the segment still investing ahead of profit.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — MODERATE-HIGH

- **Driver-pay credibility gap.** A study published Oct 14, 2026 found Uber pay **falls below minimum wage in several major US cities**, directly contradicting Uber's "$30/hour average" claim. This is the same fact pattern that produced Lyft's $272.5M California wage-theft settlement. **Deceptive-earnings-claim exposure is live and underappreciated.**
- **Safety narrative is deteriorating** — 16 Avride crashes, "excessively assertive" regulator language, and a $28.5M settlement over safety advertising. Uber's core value proposition is "get home safe."
- **Institutional investors are agitating** on driver-pay transparency (Bloomberg Law) and civil rights, keeping ESG controversy scores elevated.
- **Mitigants:** Recent positive coverage on grocery expansion (Costco 47 states, Sur La Table), Uber Freight leadership credibility, and the Prove "Trust 25" digital-identity recognition suggest brand equity remains intact outside the labor/safety debate.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

The financial model is intact — Q2 2026 gross bookings rose 24% to $58.0B and the company beat by ~41% in August — but the stock is down ~30% from its $101.99 high (Sept 22, 2025) and touched $65.94 on July 24, 2026 precisely because **non-financial risks have become the valuation driver**. Four independent regulators (Dutch DPA, FTC + 22 states, NHTSA, and a set of labor authorities) are active simultaneously, the AV partnership that anchors the growth story may be unwinding, and the company is executing its largest restructuring since COVID. Governance is functional and insider alignment is real, which is why this is High rather than Critical.

---

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **€825M Dutch GDPR fine (Aug 2026)** — appealed, but the automated-decision precedent invites EU-wide replication against the algorithmic driver-management core. Ask management for the appeal timeline and whether other DPAs have opened parallel inquiries.
2. **FTC/state-AG Uber One litigation** — an injunction could force changes to subscription enrollment/cancellation, hitting the retention engine. Confirm litigation accrual and whether the board has disclosed a reserve.
3. **Waymo partnership exit risk** — request disclosure on the contractual term of the Atlanta/Austin exclusivity and what share of Mobility gross bookings run on partner AV supply. A 2028 termination is a structural, not cyclical, earnings risk.
4. **NHTSA ODI probe into Avride** — Uber bears brand and liability exposure for a third party's safety defect. Confirm indemnity terms with AV partners.
5. **Leadership continuity** — CFO transition completed within 12 months and the robotaxi head departs Nov 16. Two of the three roles that matter most for the AV pivot have turned over.
6. **Driver-pay disclosure gap** — the delta between Uber's public earnings claims and independent studies is the exact predicate for the Lyft California outcome. Escalate to legal/IR.
7. **Governance hygiene** — stock pledging permitted for directors and employees; ~8.3-year independent chair tenure; unresolved conflict-of-interest disclosure demand. Raise at the next engagement cycle.
