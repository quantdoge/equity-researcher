# UBER — Complete Financial Risk Assessment
**As of FY2025 (period end 2025-12-31) | Price $70.35 | Market cap ~$143.7bn**

---

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE**

Uber is a genuinely de-risked balance sheet. Net debt/EBITDA of **0.64x** and interest coverage of **12.65x** (EBIT $5,565M / interest $440M) are solidly investment-grade metrics, and the position is improving: net debt has fallen from $6.8bn (2022) → $4.4bn (2025) while EBITDA compounded from −$7.9bn to $7.0bn.

Corroborated externally: S&P assigned a **'BBB+'** issue-level rating to Uber's euro-denominated senior notes (Sept 2026), and Fitch affirms **'F1'** on the $2bn commercial paper program — both imply a BBB/Baa-equivalent IG issuer. No CDS spread data was retrievable (data gap).

**No refinancing cliff.** FY2025 short-term debt is **$0** (down from $1,461M in 2024), with all $10.5bn sitting in long-term maturities. Recent issuance is 4.15% senior notes due 2031 — the nearest meaningful maturity is ~5 years out. Against that sits **$7,633M cash** plus a $2bn CP program. The 10Y UST at 4.99% (curve as of 2026-10-18, 2s10s +34bp, no inversion) means refinancing will be modestly more expensive than the 2031 coupon, but at 0.64x leverage this is immaterial.

**Critical interpretive point on the Z-Score.** The reported 1.466 lands in the distress zone almost entirely because (a) x4 uses *book* equity of $28.1bn rather than the $143.7bn market cap, and (b) x2 carries negative retained earnings of −$10,628M. Both are legacy artifacts of the 2019–2022 loss years (retained earnings bottomed at −$32.8bn in 2022). Substituting market cap into x4 (143,694/33,719 = 4.26) lifts the Z-Score to **~3.52, the safe zone**. Retained earnings have improved $22.1bn in three years. **The Z-Score flag is a historical scar, not a live credit signal** — do not let it drive the rating.

**Piotroski 7/9** is a fair score. It loses points on F3 (ROA fell to 16.3% from 19.2%) and F9 (asset turnover slipped to 0.842x from 0.858x) — both reflecting a growing asset base outrunning returns, not deterioration in the business.

The headline insight: **Uber is not a high-beta stock — it is a high-total-risk stock.** Beta of ~1.0 vs. volatility of 37.6% (roughly 2x a typical market vol of ~16–18%) means the risk is overwhelmingly **idiosyncratic**, not systematic. A market drawdown alone will not break this equity; regulatory action, driver-reclassification rulings, insurance cost shocks, or autonomous-vehicle disintermediation will. Scenario work should therefore be stress-tested on company-specific shocks rather than market beta.

A Sharpe of **0.119** over three years is poor — holders have been paid almost nothing for 37.6% volatility, and a −34% drawdown is consistent with a range-bound, headline-driven tape. Sortino was not available (data gap), which matters here since downside-vol asymmetry is the key unknown.

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue −20%

Base FY2025: Revenue $52,017M, COGS $31,338M (60.2%), total costs $46,452M, EBIT $5,565M.

| Scenario | Cost flex | New EBIT | Margin | Int. Coverage | Net Debt/EBITDA | FCF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | — | $5,565M | 10.7% | 12.65x | 0.64x | $9,763M |
| **−20% rev, cyclical** | 85% variable | **$3,059M** | 7.4% | **6.95x** | 1.17x | ~$3.5–3.8bn |
| **−20% rev, harsh** | 65% variable | **$1,201M** | 2.9% | **2.73x** | 2.28x | ~$1.5bn |
| −20% rev, stressed | 50% variable | **−$193M** | −0.5% | breakeven | 8.0x | ~breakeven |
| −20% rev, zero flex (theoretical floor) | 0% | **−$4,838M** | −11.6% | none | n/m | ~−$2.7bn |

**The realistic case is the first row.** Uber's cost base is dominated by driver and courier payments, which flex almost mechanically with gross bookings — a 20% revenue decline does not produce a 20% decline in the cost base *without* commensurate variable-cost relief. Even the *harsh* case leaves coverage at 2.73x and leverage at 2.28x — still IG-compatible.

**Liquidity runway under the absurd zero-flex case:** annual cash burn of roughly −$2.7bn (EBIT loss less interest, adding back $747M D&A and $1,826M SBC), against $7,633M cash = **~2.8 years of runway on cash alone**, before touching the $2bn CP program or cutting the $6.5bn buyback. That buyback is the single largest discretionary lever — suspending it alone adds ~$6.5bn of annual liquidity.

**Conclusion: Uber survives a 20% revenue shock with room to spare. The equity is not at risk from demand cyclicality; it is at risk from regulatory reclassification liabilities, which are not quantifiable from the available data (see gaps).**

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **LOW**

Balance-sheet risk is the lowest it has been in Uber's public history. Leverage 0.64x, coverage 12.65x, $7.6bn cash, zero short-term maturities, IG ratings, $9.8bn FCF, and a downside scenario that never breaches covenant-equivalent thresholds. The two items that keep this from being an unqualified "very low" are earnings quality and the thin current ratio.

**For equity holders, financial risk ≠ investment risk.** The financial risk is Low; the *market* risk (37.6% vol, −34% drawdown, 0.119 Sharpe) is High. Those are different problems and should be sized separately in the memo.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Earnings quality — the biggest analytical trap.** FY2025 net income of **$10,053M is 1.81x operating income of $5,565M**. Roughly 45% of reported earnings are non-operating: deferred-tax valuation-allowance releases plus mark-to-market gains on equity stakes (Aurora, Didi, Grab, Joby). **Do not value UBER on GAAP P/E.** The EPS of $4.82 materially overstates recurring earning power; EBIT/EBITDA-based multiples are the correct lens.

2. **FCF is flattered.** The $9,763M FCF includes a **+$2,227M working-capital benefit** and excludes **$1,826M of stock-based compensation**, which is a real economic cost. Adjusted for both, FCF is ~$5,710M and FCF yield drops from 6.55% to **~3.8%**. Still healthy — but half the headline. In a downturn the working-capital benefit reverses, which would hit reported FCF harder than it hits EBIT.

3. **Altman Z-Score in the distress zone (1.466)** — will trip naive screens. Explain the book-equity/retained-earnings artifact; market-based Z is 3.52.

4. **Thin current ratio of 1.14x.** Improved from 0.96x in 2022, but the buffer is narrow. Note that quick = current (no inventory), so there is no inventory risk inflating the ratio — this is a genuine liquidity measure, not a padded one.

5. **Debt figure reconciliation gap.** `total_debt` is reported at **$12,080M**, but long-term debt ($10,521M) plus short-term debt ($0) sums to $10,521M — a **$1,559M unexplained difference**, likely operating lease liabilities folded into total debt. Similarly, the leverage tool's net debt ($4,447M) and the balance sheet's `net_debt` field ($4,975M) differ by $528M. I have used the leverage-tool figures ($4,447M / 0.64x); the discrepancy does not change the rating but should be reconciled against the 10-K before publication. **Data gap.**

6. **Piotroski F3/F9 failures** — ROA and asset turnover both declined. Combined with the leverage-tool's `operating_margin_pct` of 10.7% vs. the income statement's computed 10.70% (consistent), the operating story is fine; the issue is capital deployment, with $6,523M of buybacks in FY2025 shrinking the equity base used in ratios.

7. **Not quantifiable from available data — explicit gaps:** (a) driver/courier reclassification contingent liabilities and legal accruals — the single largest tail risk to this equity and absent from the financials provided; (b) debt maturity schedule by year beyond the 2031 notes; (c) CDS spreads and Moody's issuer rating (only S&P and Fitch surfaced); (d) Sortino ratio and downside beta; (e) **sector peer benchmarks are unusable** — UBER is misclassified as "Technology / Software – Application," returning NVDA/AAPL/MSFT as "peers." No valid comps were available, so all comparisons above are historical self-comparisons versus Uber's own 2022–2024 results rather than peer cross-sections.

8. **Terminal-value risk, not solvency risk:** autonomous-vehicle disintermediation. This does not appear in any ratio above and is not stress-tested here; it belongs in the qualitative section of the memo, not the credit section.

---

**Bottom line:** Uber's financial risk is **Low**. The balance sheet can absorb a 20% revenue decline with coverage still above 2.7x in a harsh case and ~2.8 years of cash runway in an implausible zero-flex case. The credit is investment grade on both metrics and external ratings. The risks that matter for the equity are *earnings quality* (45% of net income is non-operating), *FCF quality* (half the headline is working capital and SBC), and *idiosyncratic/regulatory tail risk* — not leverage, not liquidity, and not market beta.
