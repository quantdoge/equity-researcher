# Macroeconomic Assessment — UBER (Uber Technologies)
**As of 18 October 2026 | Price $70.35 | Market cap $143.7B | Beta 1.265**

## 1. Macro Environment Summary: **Risk-Off — "Energy Shock + Policy Reversal"**

The US economy entered Q4 2026 in a distinctly uncomfortable regime, and it is a regime change, not a wobble:

- **The Fed is hiking again.** On **16 September 2026** the FOMC raised the target range 25bp to **3.75%–4.00%**, the first increase since 2023, after cutting from 5.33% to 3.63% through 2025. The dot plot implies one more hike in 2026; MUFG assigns a 55–60% probability to a December move to 4.00–4.25%.
- **Inflation has re-accelerated.** Headline CPI is **+3.72% YoY (Sept 2026)**, up from a 2.44% trough in February — a 128bp re-acceleration. The 3-month annualized run-rate is ~4.6%. Driver: a Middle East conflict (Strait of Hormuz shipping attacks, Houthi strikes on Saudi) that has lifted **regular gasoline from $2.80/gal (5 Jan) to $4.33/gal (12 Oct), +54.9% YTD / +41.5% YoY**.
- **The long end has broken out.** 10Y UST **5.10%** (15 Oct), from 4.38% at end-June (+72bp), touching 5.31% on 5 Oct — a 24-year high. Curve is bear-steepening: 2Y 4.65%, 5Y 4.80%, 30Y 5.37%, 2s10s **+34bp**.
- **Growth is decelerating but not collapsing.** Real GDP +2.2% annualized in Q2 2026 (+2.5% Q1), down from the 3.9–4.0% pace of mid-2025. September manufacturing output rose but "lost considerable momentum in Q3" on energy and supply-chain headwinds; September import prices rose more than expected. WTO forecasts a **17% drop in Middle East exports**.
- **Labor market tight, household mood is not.** Unemployment **4.2%** (Sept). But University of Michigan sentiment is **51.7 (Aug 2026)**, versus 71.7 in Jan 2025 — a level historically seen only in recessions. Consumers are being squeezed by fuel and food costs while employment income holds up. That is *exactly* the setup where high-frequency discretionary spend gets traded down before payrolls break.

This is not a recession call — it's a **stagflation-lite, policy-tightening, energy-tax regime** in which the marginal consumer cuts small-ticket frequency purchases and the discount rate on long-duration equities grinds higher.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Context on the stock itself:** UBER is **$70.35, down 27.2% YoY** (from $96.61), roughly 30% below its 52-week high of $101.30, and has spent 2026 de-rating (Feb 4: −9.8% on results; Aug 5: −5% on weak Q3 profit guidance plus a $10B autonomy investment plan). Q2 2026 was actually *operationally* strong — **gross bookings +24% YoY to $58.0B (+22% constant currency), revenue +12% to $14.19B** — which makes the macro read-through unusually clean: the multiple, not the P&L, is taking the damage.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Three forces are hitting UBER simultaneously and they are correlated, not independent: **an energy tax on the consumer → falling sentiment and frequency risk; an inflation problem → a hawkish Fed and a 24-year-high long bond → multiple compression; and a stronger dollar → a 2pp translation drag.** The offsetting goods — full employment, ample driver supply, an asset-light model, and 22% constant-currency bookings growth that is still accelerating — are real, and they argue UBER *weathers* this better than a leveraged consumer-discretionary peer. But they do not offset the discount-rate channel, which is what has been driving the shares. Note that the *operational* macro backdrop is nowhere near as bad as the topline multiple move implies; the market is charging UBER for duration, not for demand. Next hard checkpoint: **Q3 2026 results, expected early November** — the key test is whether US Mobility trip frequency and Delivery order frequency held through the summer fuel spike.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Surprise: **A Bull De-Escalation**

**The setup:** Oil is currently carrying a war premium that has not yet produced actual supply loss — the Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia "did not cause any interruptions that markets had feared" (16 Oct). Sentiment at 51.7 and a 10Y at 5.10% are both priced for continuation of the shock.

**The path:** A Middle East ceasefire or Hormuz de-risking → crude gives back the war premium → gasoline from **$4.33 back toward $3.00** within a quarter (~30% relief, a large implicit tax cut for the marginal Uber rider) → headline CPI from **3.72% back under 2.5%** by Q1 2027 → the December hike is priced out and the 2027 dot flips → 10Y from **5.10% down to 4.30–4.40%**.

**The UBER impact:** three tailwinds compound. Frequency and take rate recover as fuel relief flows to the consumer; the FX drag flips to a tailwind as the dollar softens with lower US rates; and the multiple re-rates off a compressed base. Historically a 70–80bp round-trip in the 10Y plus a consumer sentiment snap-back is worth **25–35% on a high-beta platform name** with unchanged estimates — which, from $70, retests the $90s. This is also the scenario in which the market stops punishing Uber for its $10B AV spend and starts paying for it.

**The mirror-image bear case (smaller probability, larger magnitude):** the energy shock persists into winter, sentiment keeps falling from 51.7, the Fed delivers a December hike to 4.00–4.25%, and 10Y breaks 5.50%. Ride frequency rolls over at the same time the discount rate rises — a genuine double-hit that would take UBER below its July low of $65.94 toward the high-$50s. In that world, the asset-light model is a defence, not an offence, and Uber becomes a "beat and de-rate" story for another two quarters.
