# UBER — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Prepared from FY2025 filings data, Q2 2026 results (5 Aug 2026), and news through 17 Oct 2026. Note: SEC EDGAR direct fetches returned HTTP 403 and `fetch_filing_text` had no 10-K/10-Q indexed for UBER; geographic detail below relies on secondary reporting of the FY2025 10-K (SQ Magazine, updated 28 Sep 2026) and Uber's own IR releases.*

## 0. Scale & Shape of the Risk
Q2 2026: Gross Bookings $58.0bn (+24% YoY, +22% cc), revenue $14.2bn (+12%), GAAP income from operations $1.9bn, net income $2.4bn (incl. $1.6bn pre-tax gain on equity revaluations — **low-quality beat**), Adj. EBITDA $2.8bn, TTM FCF >$10bn, 208m MAPCs, 3.9bn trips, 10.2m drivers/couriers across 70+ countries (Uber Q2'26 release, 5 Aug 2026). FY2025: revenue $52.0bn, Gross Bookings $193.5bn. This is a **70-country regulated-license business** — every city is a permit, every driver relationship a legal construct. That is the core structural risk.

**The stand-out finding: the UK is ~20% of *revenue* but a far smaller share of *bookings*.** Uber books UK driver pay gross because of its agency/principal structuring post-*Aslam/ Farrar* (UK Supreme Court 2021 worker ruling). Consequence: UK revenue is a *policy variable*, not a demand variable. In Jan 2026 Uber re-cut UK driver terms (variable service fee from 3%, "accept new terms or be blocked", per TaxiPoint/PHTM reporting), and the change **removed $1.1bn of Q2 2026 revenue, an ~8pt hit to YoY growth** (Q2'26 prepared remarks). A 20%-of-revenue market that is regulator-manipulable is the single largest quantified legal exposure in the model.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Regulatory Risks

**R1 — Platform-work reclassification in UK & EU (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
The EU Platform Work Directive must be transposed into national law (Linklaters, Mar 2026), creating up to 27 divergent presumption regimes by end-2026. Outcomes are currently *diverging in Uber's favour at the courts but against it at the legislators*: France's Cour de Cassation (2025) and the Amsterdam Court of Appeal (Jan 2026) held drivers are independent contractors — yet Spain, Italy and the UK already impose employment-like costs, and UK/EU regulators keep pushing. **Watch:** 2026–27 transposition bills in Spain, Netherlands, Germany, Ireland; UK HMRC/VAT treatment of the new agency model. Impact channel: revenue de-grossing (the $1.1bn/qtr precedent), insurance/pension/paid-leave cost, and pricing-driven demand elasticity.

**R2 — Delivery Hero: a multi-jurisdiction FDI and merger-control gauntlet (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium-High)**
Uber launched a voluntary cash takeover at €41.50/share, $14.8bn equity value (16 Jul 2026); offer document published **after BaFin approval**, acceptance 27 Aug–5 Nov 2026; Uber already holds ~37% economic interest (~$4bn invested) and the combined group would span **99 markets**. The joint reasoned statement explicitly conditions the deal on **"merger control and foreign-investment clearances"** — meaning EU/German FDI screening plus national-security review in Delivery Hero's Middle East, Asian and LatAm footprint, and competition overlap in Germany, Spain, Korea and the Gulf. Each clearance is a lever for governments to extract rider-classification or data commitments. If clearances slip past Q1 2027, the deal becomes an overhang on capital allocation and an antitrust-covenant risk.

**R3 — US–China decoupling vs. Uber's Chinese AV supply chain (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium)**
Uber's robotaxi strategy leans on Chinese AV makers: Pony.ai (expanded partnership, **2,000 robotaxis in Europe plus Middle East**, CNBC 14 Aug 2026; London Gen-7 testing from Oct 2026 — TechCrunch), plus WeRide and Momenta. This sits directly in the path of US restrictions on connected/autonomous vehicles with Chinese components and of EU/UK security-of-supply scrutiny. A US Commerce-style rule, CFIUS touchpoint, or Chinese export/retaliation action could strand deployment timelines in exactly the geographies (London, Middle East) where Uber is racing Waymo and Tesla. Secondary: Chinese AV entrants (Didi, WeRide, Pony.ai) are simultaneously Uber's partners *and* potential competitors in the Gulf.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights
- **Driver classification (US):** Massachusetts AG settlement (2024, $175m framework, mass.gov settlement page); California AG + city attorneys' wage-theft/misclassification actions against Uber and Lyft remain the template; the earlier PAGA class win was gutted on appeal, leaving a narrow ~$20m settlement (ClassLawGroup).
- **Data breach legacy:** 2016 breach produced a **DOJ non-prosecution agreement** (N.D. Cal.) and a **$148m multistate AG settlement**; this is closed but is the anchor precedent for future cyber enforcement against Uber.
- **Privacy/GDPR:** the Dutch DPA's €290m GDPR fine (2024) over driver-data transfers to the US is the largest single privacy penalty against the company and remains the benchmark for EU transfer risk; Uber's ID-verification/biometrics (BIPA-style) exposure persists in Illinois-type regimes.
- **AV permitting:** Wayve obtained London private-hire vehicle licences in Aug 2026, enabling launch on Uber — AV rollout is gated by TfL-type permits, not just technology.
- **Tax/VAT:** UK and EU VAT characterization of the agency model is unsettled and is the mechanism by which R1 could re-penalize the 20% UK revenue base.
- **Non-litigation but material:** continuous securities/consumer class actions are a cost of scale; none individually material versus the $10bn TTM FCF, but they create disclosure drag.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags
1. **Classification-as-compliance-systemic risk:** the Jan 2026 UK term change ("accept or be blocked") is exactly the kind of conduct that invites worker-status and unfair-terms litigation. Documented, dated, and already quantified at $1.1bn/qtr.
2. **Sanctions/withdrawal perimeter:** Uber exited Russia (sold its Yandex JV interest, 2023) and does not operate in Iran/Cuba/NK — clean, but the Careem/MEA footprint and cash-heavy markets require ongoing AML/sanctions screening discipline; no current FCPA/OFAC action identified in the record reviewed.
3. **Data localization/transfer:** EU–US transfer mechanics under the DPA framework are the live enforcement frontier (Dutch DPA precedent).
4. **Deal-covenant compliance:** integrating a 99-market group means inheriting Delivery Hero's own labour, antitrust and sanctions tail — diligence/compliance integration cost is a real, under-modelled line item.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Justification:** Not because of expropriation or war risk (Uber is asset-light and its Russia exposure is gone), but because **~20% of reported revenue sits in a single jurisdiction whose regulator has already demonstrated it can move that number by 8 points of growth in one quarter**, and because a 27-country EU transposition cycle plus a 99-market acquisition with explicit FDI conditions converge in the same 12-month window. The bull case offset: French, Dutch and (post-appeal) US courts have lately validated the contractor model, and $10bn+ TTM FCF gives Uber the balance-sheet capacity to absorb settlements and re-engineer models market-by-market.

**What would upgrade to Very High:** an adverse EU transposition in Spain/Germany/Netherlands applying an employment presumption with retroactive liability; a US federal or multi-state reclassification push; or Delivery Hero clearances being blocked/delayed with conditions on rider status.

**What would downgrade to Medium:** a UK agency-model run-rate stabilizing without further revenue reset; Delivery Hero cleared with light conditions in H1 2027; and confirmation that the EU Directive's presumption remains rebuttable in the major markets.

Peer benchmark: Lyft (US-only) carries materially lower regulatory complexity — a reminder that Uber's global diversification is a *diversification of risk*, not a reduction of it.
