# UBER — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $70.35 (16-Oct-2026) · Market cap ~$143.6B · Net debt ~$5.0B · EV ~$148.6B · 2,042M diluted shares*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW (widening on operations, with a genuine structural question mark)**

Uber is the only truly global two-sided marketplace for local movement: Mobility, Delivery, and Freight, in 15,000+ cities. Latest quarter (Q2'26): **3.9B trips (+18% YoY), MAPCs +16%, Gross Bookings $58.0B (+24%, +22% cc), revenue $14.2B (+12%)**. Those are extraordinary growth rates for a platform of this scale.

**Sources of advantage**
- **Local network effects / density economics.** Liquidity is city-by-city: density → shorter ETAs → more riders → more drivers. This is a real barrier to entry in each metro, but it is a barrier to *new entrants*, not to existing multi-homers.
- **Platform cross-sell + subscription.** Uber One bundling Mobility + Delivery raises retention and increases share of wallet. Trips per MAPC still rising (+2%) shows engagement, not just user adds.
- **Cost advantage in asset intensity.** Capex is ~0.6% of revenue. Uber owns no cars, no kitchens, almost no trucks. Incremental gross bookings require nearly zero capital — a superb structural feature.
- **Intangible assets.** Brand, 15 years of pricing/insurance/regulatory data, and city-level regulatory relationships (a real, if unglamorous, moat).

**Why not Wide?**
1. **Consumer multi-homing is cheap.** Riders and eaters flip apps for a $3 coupon. There are no contracts, no data lock-in, no switching costs of consequence.
2. **Driver supply is not proprietary** — and this is where the AV question bites. If autonomous fleets (Waymo, Tesla, others) own the supply, the scarce asset in the value chain shifts from Uber's demand aggregation to the vehicle/fleet owner. Uber's strategic answer — become "the world's largest platform for autonomous vehicles" and aggregate AV demand via partnerships (Waymo, Lucid/Nuro, Volkswagen, WeRide, Aurora) — is credible and may preserve the toll-booth position, but it converts Uber from supply-independent aggregator to a party negotiating with concentrated, deep-pocketed suppliers. That is a narrower moat than the one it had in the human-driver era.
3. **Regulatory dependence** (driver classification, insurance mandates, minimum-pay rules) is an ongoing tax on returns that competitors like DoorDash/Lyft share, so it's an industry cost rather than a relative disadvantage.

**Moat trend (5 years):** materially **wider operationally** — from a $9.1B net loss (2022) to >$10B TTM free cash flow, GAAP operating income of $6.9B TTM, and 22% bookings growth at ~$190B annualized bookings. But the durability of the *terminal* moat is less certain than it was in 2021, because AV supply concentration is a new, exogenous variable.

\*Inflated: Q3'25 contained a ~$5B tax-valuation-allowance benefit and Q2'26 a $1.6B pre-tax net benefit. Underlying net income is closer to **$5B**.

**What's excellent**
- **Margin trajectory is the whole story**: operating margin went from −5.8% (2022) to ~12.5% TTM on essentially flat gross margin — pure operating leverage on a fixed-cost tech base.
- **ROIC 13.9% vs ~9% WACC**, and the trend is up (4.5% → 8.3% → 13.9%). This is now a value-creating business, which it was not in 2022.
- **Balance sheet**: net debt ~0.5x TTM FCF, $7.6B cash. No solvency risk whatsoever. No dividend, so no payout strain.
- **FCF conversion**: reported FCF exceeds GAAP net income, helped by ~$2.2B working-capital inflow in 2025 (Uber's float/insurance payables) — genuinely good economics, though the WC swing is not a repeatable earnings source.

**What keeps it from 9–10**
- **Reported earnings quality is muddy.** Headline P/E of 14.9x is an artifact: 2025 GAAP EPS of $4.73 includes roughly $2.40/share of one-off tax benefit. On a clean basis Uber trades at **~23x non-GAAP EPS** (TTM ~$3.05) and **~28x adjusted GAAP EPS**.
- **Stock-based comp ~$1.8B (3.5% of revenue)** is a real cost; it is excluded from non-GAAP and is only now being offset by buybacks. Note management disclosed in Q2'26 that "Adjusted EBITDA is no longer a key metric" and has pivoted to FCF/non-GAAP operating income — a reporting change worth monitoring, generally in the right direction.
- **Normalized FCF ≈ $7.5B** (stripping the WC tailwind), not the $10B headline.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Multiples today**

| Measure | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| EV / TTM revenue | 2.7x | cheap for 22% bookings growth |
| EV / TTM adj. EBITDA (~$10.1B) | ~14.7x | reasonable |
| EV / normalized FCF ($7.5B) | ~19.8x | 5.1% normalized FCF yield |
| EV / reported TTM FCF (>$10B) | ~14.5x | 7.0% yield, WC-flattered |
| P/E (clean, non-GAAP) | ~23x | on ~30–35% EPS growth |
| P/E (headline 2025 GAAP) | 14.9x | **misleading — do not use** |

Peer-median P/E of 43.7x from the sector screen is meaningless here — UBER is misclassified as "Software–Application" and the peer set is NVDA/AAPL/MSFT. True comparables (DoorDash, Lyft, Grab, Booking, Airbnb) trade in a wide 20–40x band; Uber sits at the cheaper end despite the fastest bookings growth in its history.

**Intrinsic value (owner-earnings DCF)**
Base case: normalized FCF $7.5B; growth 14%/yr years 1–5 (margin expansion on mid-teens bookings growth), 7%/yr years 6–10, 3% terminal; 9% WACC → **EV ~$234B, equity ~$229B ≈ $112/share**.

Conservative case: $7.0B FCF; 10% then 5% growth; 2.5% terminal; 9.5% WACC → **~$72/share**.

Bull case (AV aggregation works, margins → 6%+ of bookings): **$130–150/share**.

I weight these roughly 25/50/25, and shade the terminal value down for AV uncertainty → **intrinsic value ~$90/share, fair-value range $72–$112**.

**Implied expectations.** At $70.35 the market is paying ~19.8x normalized FCF and implying roughly 8–9% annual FCF growth for a decade — i.e., the price already assumes material deceleration from the current 20%+ bookings trajectory and gives Uber no credit for AV aggregation. That is not a demanding bar; the stock has de-rated from ~$100 (Sept-2025) to ~$70 despite accelerating results, which tells you the discount is about narrative (robotaxis, growth durability), not numbers.

**Margin of safety.** Price is ~22% below my ~$90 base-case value — *inside* my 25–30% required discount, not beyond it. My disciplined buy threshold is **≤$65** (a ~28% discount to base case, and roughly the conservative DCF value).

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Rating: Good — professional, competent, adequately (not deeply) aligned.**

- **Dara Khosrowshahi (CEO since 2017)** executed the hardest turnaround in consumer tech: from a culture crisis and $3B+ annual cash burn to >$10B FCF and 12%+ operating margins, without abandoning growth. The "profitable growth" playbook is being delivered quarter after quarter (non-GAAP operating income +40% YoY; non-GAAP EPS +35%).
- **Balaji Krishnamurthy (CFO)** communicates in FCF and share count, not adjusted metrics — the right language. Explicit buyback discipline: **$6.5B repurchased in 2025, $1.25B in 2024**, diluted shares down from 2,150M (2024) to 2,050M (Q2'26), ~3% annual shrink. Buybacks are being done at 15–20x FCF — value-accretive.
- **M&A discipline: good.** No empire-building mega-deals. Foodpanda Taiwan (~$950M), Trendyol GO, smaller tuck-ins. Contrast with the 2020–21 era (Postmates, Drizly, Transplace — ~$10B of goodwill sitting on the balance sheet, some of it impaired).
- **Strategic capital allocation into AV:** equity stakes and partnerships rather than building vehicles — capital-light and rational, though it concedes that Uber will rent, not own, the future supply stack.
- **Weaknesses:** no founder/owner-operator; insider ownership is modest (founders largely exited); the board is a standard large-cap tech board. SBC of ~$1.8B/yr is real dilution that only recently turned net-negative. Governance is clean (single share class), and management has been forthright about the AV threat rather than dismissive.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (accumulate below $65)

Uber is now a genuinely good business — narrow moat, capital-light, 13.9% ROIC and rising, >$10B FCF, net cash-ish balance sheet, 22% bookings growth at $58B/quarter, and a management team buying back stock at these levels. This is the rare "growth company that became a cash machine" and the transformation is real, not accounting.

But my job is price discipline, and the price is *fair*, not *cheap*: ~19.8x normalized FCF, ~23x clean earnings, a 22% discount to base-case intrinsic value — inside my required 25–30% margin of safety. And unlike a consumer-staples compounder, Uber's terminal value depends on an unresolved question: **does the demand aggregator keep the toll booth when the supply becomes autonomous fleets owned by a handful of trillion-dollar counterparties?** That uncertainty deserves a wider discount, not a narrower one.

**Action:** Hold existing positions; do not add at $70. Build the position at **≤$65** (roughly the conservative DCF value), add aggressively at **≤$58**. Sell/trim above ~$115–120 (near bull-case value, ~25x forward FCF with AV optionality fully paid for). Watch Q3'26 results (4-Nov-2026) for: bookings growth durability, non-GAAP operating income margin, TTM FCF ex-working-capital, and any change in AV partnership economics.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AV disintermediation (the big one).** If Waymo/Tesla/Zoox fleets go direct-to-consumer at scale in major metros, Uber loses supply scarcity and pricing power simultaneously. Uber's answer — being the demand aggregator and taking a lower but durable take rate — is plausible but unproven, and would compress the multiple structurally, not cyclically. Evidence to monitor: Waymo city count, whether Uber appears in AV apps, and take-rate trends in Austin/Atlanta/Phoenix.
2. **Earnings quality / normalization.** Headline GAAP EPS is inflated by tax benefits; investors anchoring on a 14.9x P/E are mispricing the stock. Conversely, if one-off benefits vanish and cash taxes normalize toward 23–25%, EPS growth decelerates sharply.
3. **Driver classification and minimum-pay regulation.** EU Platform Work Directive, state-level reclassification or pay floors (NYC, Seattle, California), and insurance mandates could add billions in cost or force a franchise/employee model.
4. **Delivery competition.** DoorDash and regional incumbents (Meituan, iFood, Delivery Hero) can compress delivery take rates; grocery/retail delivery is structurally lower-margin than restaurant delivery.
5. **Insurance and legal inflation.** Auto liability costs are the largest single variable cost in Mobility; adverse reserve development hits both earnings and FCF.
6. **Freight cyclicality** and a genuinely weak freight market remain a drag on consolidated margins and a distraction from the core.
7. **Consumer cyclicality** — a recession would hit discretionary mobility and delivery demand simultaneously; Uber One subscriptions soften but do not eliminate this.
8. **Concentration of counterparties** — strategic reliance on a small number of AV/platform partners creates negotiating risk that human-driver supply never did.

**Bottom line:** A high-quality, capital-light compounder with an improving moat and honest management, trading at a fair price. Own it; don't chase it.
