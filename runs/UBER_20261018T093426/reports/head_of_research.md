### Investment Memo: UBER — 18 October 2026

**Verdict**: HOLD (accumulate on weakness) | Conviction: Medium
**Price Target**: $90 (28% upside from current $70.35)
**Time Horizon**: 12–24 months

**Investment Thesis**
Uber is a genuinely transformed business — the only scaled platform in its sector converting 24% bookings growth into GAAP profit, with a 10.7% operating margin, >$10B TTM FCF, and an investment-grade balance sheet — yet the stock trades 30% below its 52-week high because the market is pricing two structural questions ahead of any evidence in the numbers: autonomous-vehicle disintermediation and take-rate decay. The earnings base is roughly half non-recurring (a $5.0B tax release inflated FY25 net income), so the honest entry multiple is ~22x EV/EBITDA and ~28x normalized EPS — fair, not cheap, and inside our 25–30% required margin of safety. Own a starter position; the risk/reward only becomes compelling below ~$65, and the thesis is re-underwritten on the Q3 print (4-Nov-2026).

**Key Findings by Domain**
- **Macro & Sector**: Macro is **unfavourable** — Fed hiking (3.75–4.00%), 10Y at 5.10%, gasoline +54.9% YTD, consumer sentiment 51.7; the damage is to the multiple, not the P&L (bookings still +22–24%). Sector structure is attractive (rational oligopoly) but constrained by the AV discontinuity and the EU Platform Work Directive; Uber is the strongest operator in a fairly-priced sector (10.7% op margin vs 6.6% peer median; 2.8x EV/Rev vs 3.5x).
- **Geopolitical & Legal**: **HIGH**. The UK is 20.4% of revenue and regulator-manipulable — a Jan-2026 model change already removed $1.1B/qtr (~8pts of growth). EU Platform Work Directive transposition deadline 2-Dec-2026; the $14.8B Delivery Hero acquisition runs a 99-market FDI/merger-control gauntlet into Q1-2027.
- **Equity Fundamentals — Value**: Narrow-but-widening moat; ROIC 13.9% and rising; normalized FCF ~$7.5B. Intrinsic value ~$90 (range $72–112; bull $130–150). At 19.8x normalized FCF the price is fair, not cheap — buy discipline says ≤$65.
- **Equity Fundamentals — Growth**: Real and durable: $58B quarterly bookings (+24%, 4th straight quarter >20%), Rule of ~41 on bookings, advertising scaling at high incremental margin, FY30 EPS consensus revised +12.6% in 3 months. Caveat: FY26 revenue growth only ~11% — a genuine quality-of-growth problem.
- **Quantitative Signals**: Neutral composite (+0.2): value +1.0, quality +1.5, but **worst momentum in peer set** (−30.9% peak-to-trough; unconfirmed +6.7% one-month bounce). R² of 0.084 means beta/alpha estimates are near-uninformative; correlation to SPY of 0.288 is the best diversification property in the group.
- **Financial Risk**: **LOW**. 0.64x net debt/EBITDA, 12.65x interest coverage, $7.6B cash, zero short-term maturities, S&P BBB+/Fitch F1. Survives a −20% revenue shock at ~7x coverage. The "distress-zone" Altman Z (1.47) is a retained-earnings artifact; market-cap Z ≈ 3.5.
- **Non-Financial Risk**: **HIGH**. €825M Dutch GDPR fine (Aug-2026), FTC + 22 state AGs over Uber One, NHTSA probe of partner robotaxis, CFO and robotaxi lead both turned over mid-pivot, Waymo exclusivity reportedly ends 2028 with ~40% of Mobility bookings AV-exposed. Offsetting: **CEO bought $10M and COO ~$5.3M of stock in September** near the lows — the strongest alignment signal in the file.
- **ESG**: Average overall; social pillar is the structural exposure (classification trajectory, €1.12B cumulative Dutch DPA fines, driver-pay-below-minimum-wage studies). Governance genuinely sound (single class, independent chair). Electrification ~20pp/yr behind its own 2030 target.
- **Alternative Data**: Neutral, tactically improving. Q3 search interest −7.4% QoQ argues for an in-line print at best on 4-Nov; but the series troughed mid-September (+6% off the low), delivery is holding share vs DoorDash, and retail sentiment is capitulated ("extremely bearish") — bad news is discounted.
- **Short / Contrarian View**: The credible short is valuation and duration, **not fraud** — Beneish −2.42, accruals ~zero, cash conversion 1.005 are all clean. The case: ~28x normalized earnings, 18.7x EV/(FCF−SBC), take rate −210bp YoY, flat gross margin, insurance +50%/trip, and consensus FY28 EBITDA requiring a near-double. Crowded thesis — the stock is already down 30%.

**Bull Case**
- The demand aggregator keeps the toll booth: Waymo, Nuro/Lucid, WeRide and Pony.ai all chose Uber distribution, AVs are <0.5% of trips, and 22–24% bookings growth with margin expansion makes ~$15B of FY30 FCF plausible (~$310B equity value at 20x, +116%).
- Take rate stabilizes while advertising ($1B+ run-rate, ~80–90% incremental margin), grocery/retail and Uber One re-rate the revenue mix — the exact combination the market says is impossible.
- A macro de-escalation (oil war premium unwinds, 10Y back toward 4.3–4.4%) is historically worth 25–35% on a high-beta platform with unchanged estimates — retesting the $90s.

**Bear Case**
- Take-rate compression is structural, not mix noise: 24.5% and falling turns 24% bookings growth into ~11% revenue growth permanently, capping EPS growth in the low teens with no multiple expansion.
- AV fleets (Waymo ~25% SF share, Tesla, Zoox) go direct-to-consumer and take the highest-margin urban Mobility trips first; Waymo's Uber exclusivity reportedly ends 2028, and Uber's $10B AV commitment converts an asset-light model into a capital-consuming one.
- The regulatory stack lands simultaneously: EU transposition applies employment presumptions in a top-5 market, the UK de-grosses another $1.1B/qtr, and the FTC enjoins Uber One mechanics — while a December Fed hike pushes the 10Y through 5.5% and the stock toward the high-$50s.

**Key Risks**
1. AV disintermediation of Mobility take rate (highest impact; monitor Waymo city count, take rates in Austin/Atlanta/Phoenix, partner contract terms)
2. EU Platform Work Directive transposition and UK/VAT model resets (hard catalyst 2-Dec-2026)
3. Take-rate trajectory vs bookings growth (Q3 print, 4-Nov; consensus rev $14.67B implies only +9%)
4. Earnings normalization: the $5.0B tax benefit lapses; cash taxes rising toward 23–25% sharply decelerates reported EPS growth
5. Energy-shock persistence: gasoline's +55% YTD taxes both the rider and the driver cost line
6. Delivery Hero clearances slipping past Q1-2027, creating a capital-allocation overhang

Do not add into the 4-Nov print — alt data says in-line at best and guidance is the swing factor. A hard stop-review triggers on a break below the March low of ~$65.40 *with* deteriorating bookings, or on evidence of take-rate collapse in AV-exposed metros.
