## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — EU Digital Sovereignty / Transatlantic Tech Decoupling (highest priority)
**Probability: Medium-High | Impact: Medium-High**

- The European Commission is considering rules that would **restrict use of US cloud platforms for sensitive government data** (CNBC, May 2026), formalized in the June 2026 European Technological Sovereignty Package and a September 2025 Cloud Sovereignty Framework (Atlantic Council).
- This directly threatens EMEA public-sector and regulated-enterprise addressable market — 16.3% of revenue and the second-fastest-growing region.
- Compounding friction: Washington is **linking tariff relief on European steel to softer DMA enforcement** (Politico), raising retaliation risk in both directions. The EU's "Brussels Effect" regulatory model (Cato Institute) is being applied aggressively to US tech.
- *Mitigant:* CrowdStrike is a security vendor rather than a hyperscale data host, and the sovereignty push targets infrastructure/cloud concentration more than endpoint security — but data-residency demands and "buy European" procurement preferences still bite.

### Risk 2 — US–China Cyber and Technology Escalation
**Probability: High | Impact: Medium**

- China-nexus adversaries accounted for **more than 58% of state-sponsored targeted intrusions against the technology sector**, and escalated state-sponsored cyber operations by **150%** (CrowdStrike 2025/2026 Global Threat Reports). China is explicitly targeting AI capability and IP theft.
- As a high-profile US cyber vendor with sensitive federal customers, CrowdStrike is itself a priority espionage target — IP/telemetry compromise risk is elevated.
- Export-control and entity-list churn could disrupt APAC channel/distribution and restrict sales to Chinese-affiliated customers. APAC = 10.3% of revenue.

### Risk 3 — US Federal Budget and Political Risk
**Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium**

- CrowdStrike is a significant federal contractor with FedRAMP authorization and CISA BOD-26-04 alignment. The administration's FY2027 budget **proposes cutting $707M from CISA** (Cloud Security Alliance), and government shutdown/CR risk persists.
- Political scrutiny of federal cyber spending and contractor consolidation could pressure public-sector renewals — a meaningful vertical within the 67% US revenue base.
- *Offsetting:* Rising threat environment and AI-attack proliferation are bipartisan budget tailwinds; the July 2024 outage's reputational drag on federal relationships is fading.

**Additional (lower-ranked):** Russia sanctions/export-control compliance — OFAC's IT and Software Services Determination (FAQ 1184) prohibits supplying IT/software services to Russia; EU has added 19th–21st sanctions packages. As a cyber-tooling provider, channel screening and denied-party diligence are ongoing obligations. Probability of violation: Low; Impact if violated: High.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active / Resolved Litigation**

Delta seeks compensatory + punitive damages for ~7,000 cancelled flights. CrowdStrike has filed a countersuit. | Highest-value open contingency. | **DOJ / SDNY investigation — distributor transactions** | **Closed July 2026, no criminal or civil action** (Bloomberg/Yahoo Finance, Sept 25, 2026). Probe examined a ~$32M distributor transaction and what executives knew. | Material positive. Removes a major overhang. |
| **SEC investigation — accounting for "transactions with certain customers"** | **Status unknown/unresolved** as of Sept 2026. Company acknowledged cooperating with DOJ and SEC information requests in an August regulatory filing. | **The principal remaining legal red flag.** Narrow scope but unresolved. |

**Regulatory Environment**
- **GDPR / EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework:** CrowdStrike self-certifies to the EU–U.S. DPF, UK Extension, and Swiss–U.S. DPF, with the **FTC** as the enforcing jurisdiction. Annual re-certification and FTC enforcement risk are ongoing compliance obligations.
- **EU AI Act / AI-agent security:** CrowdStrike is expanding into AI Detection & Response (Falcon Guardian, NVIDIA partnership announced at GTC 2026). AI-agent security products pull the company into the EU AI Act's high-risk and transparency obligations — an emerging, not-yet-quantified compliance cost.
- **Sector regulation:** CISA BOD-26-04 mandates and FedRAMP authorization are competitive advantages but also create dependency on federal policy continuity.
- **No antitrust action identified** against CrowdStrike. The company is a challenger, not a dominant platform — lower antitrust exposure than hyperscalers, though its consolidation of endpoint/cloud/identity modules could invite future scrutiny.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Unresolved SEC investigation into revenue accounting for certain customer/distributor transactions.** The DOJ closure is reassuring, but the SEC probe's status is unclear. Given CrowdStrike's ~95% subscription revenue model and high valuation multiple, any restatement or revenue-recognition finding would be severely punished. *Highest-priority monitoring item.*
2. **Channel/distributor model in emerging markets.** 33% international revenue, growing faster than US, intermediated through partners → **FCPA/UK Bribery Act** third-party risk and **sanctions/denied-party screening** gaps. The distributor transaction scrutinized by DOJ highlights precisely this vulnerability.
3. **Sanctions/export-control obligations in cyber.** OFAC's IT and Software Services Determination bars supplying IT/software services to Russia; cyber-tooling can be dual-use. Channel partners could re-export to denied parties. Low probability, high consequence.
4. **Data-transfer conflict (CLOUD Act vs. GDPR).** US legal compulsion over data stored in the EU collides with EU sovereignty requirements — an unresolved structural tension for all US security vendors, magnified by CrowdStrike's telemetry-heavy architecture.
5. **No FCPA enforcement action or AML finding identified** to date.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# MEDIUM

**Rationale for the rating:**
- **Favorable:** Two-thirds of revenue sits in the lowest-risk jurisdiction (US); the DOJ investigation closed with no action; the federal shareholder class action was dismissed; no sanctioned-country operations; no antitrust exposure; international revenue remains a minority of the base.
- **Constraining the rating to "Medium" rather than "Low":** The **SEC investigation remains unresolved**, Delta's fraud/gross-negligence claims survive dismissal and pierce liability caps, EMEA (16.3%) faces structural digital-sovereignty headwinds, APAC (10.3%) sits in the crosshairs of China-nexus espionage and export controls, and the federal budget vertical carries political risk.
- **Not "High":** No evidence of systemic sanctions violations, no criminal exposure after the DOJ closure, and jurisdictional concentration is in the US rather than in fragile or sanctioned markets.

**Key monitorables:** SEC investigation resolution (next 2–4 quarters); Delta trial/settlement outcome and any punitive-damages award; EU Cloud Sovereignty Framework final rules and whether endpoint security falls in scope; CISA/federal cyber budget outcomes; any revenue-recognition restatement.
