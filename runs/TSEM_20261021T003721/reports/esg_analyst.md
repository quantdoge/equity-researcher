### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Structural intensity, small absolute footprint.** 5–6 fabs, ~$1.5–2.0bn revenue → a mid-single-digit-Mt-scale Scope 1+2 profile at most. This is not a TSMC/Micron-scale carbon problem. *(Verified tCO₂e not obtained; company publishes a standalone **GHG Report for CY2024** on its Environment page — a real, if partial, disclosure.)*
- **Process-gas exposure** is contained and the company is a *specialty* rather than leading-edge node operator, so per-wafer energy is lower than leading-edge logic.
- **Water:** highest-conviction physical risk. Two Israeli fabs + Newport Beach, CA. Mitigants: Israel's desalination-heavy supply, fab-level UPW recycling, and the fact that no supply interruption is disclosed.
- **Environmental controversies: none identified.** No spills, no disclosed enforcement actions, no remediation liabilities found. Permits are stated as "obtained, maintained and kept current."
- **Product compliance programme is genuinely robust:** annual third-party audit by **SGS Taiwan** against **EU REACH, RoHS, California Prop 65 and the Montreal Protocol** — this is a verifiable, externally-audited control, not self-reporting.
- **Where it can bite:** Japan's **GX-ETS** (mandatory phase covering large emitters from 2026) applied to the TPSCo 300mm operations, and the **$3bn Japan expansion with ~$1bn of METI grants** announced July 2026 — subsidy conditions almost certainly carry emissions/reporting covenants. Grid carbon in Israel and Japan is fossil-heavy, so Scope 2 is the dominant lever and largely outside management's control.

**Rating: Medium** — high intensity ratios, immaterial absolute emissions, no controversy, but no public science-based target or net-zero commitment was found.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**The single hard-documented negative:** In a ruling dated 20 Feb 2023, the **Nazareth Regional Labour Court** found that Tower Semiconductor **"seriously violated the right of its employees to organise"** and ordered **NIS 550,000 compensation plus NIS 50,000 in court costs (~US$165k)**. The findings are specific and ugly: manager-led intimidation gatherings, defamatory statements about the Histadrut, WhatsApp/Facebook campaigns against organising, blocking union representatives from the plant, publicly humiliating a works-committee member, and managers assisting employees to sign membership-cancellation forms. The judge attributed the conduct to "the spirit of the commander" — i.e. management direction, not rogue actors. Prior 2021 injunctions had already ordered the company to admit union representatives, and the court found continued obstruction.

- **Direct financial impact: immaterial** (~$165k against $208m net income).
- **Indirect impact: material.** This is exactly the profile that triggers adverse findings in customer supply-chain audits (Tower is a long-standing **Responsible Business Alliance** member, so it is held to the RBA Code), and it is a discoverable fact for **EU CSDDD**-scoped customers. It also creates a durable collective-bargaining counterparty at the 2,000-employee flagship site.
- **Workforce/geopolitical:** ~2,000 of 5,613 employees at Migdal Haemek. Israeli reserve mobilisation and regional conflict are a genuine operational-social risk (absenteeism, fab continuity) — mitigated by the fact that most of Tower's fab footprint is now **outside** Israel (two US fabs, three Japan fabs via TPSCo, plus Agrate, Italy).
- **Positives:** strong safety-critical process culture required by automotive/aerospace/medical customers (IATF-style discipline), low-Turnover skilled workforce, and community presence in the Galilee periphery.

**Rating: Medium.**

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**Strengths (real, not cosmetic):**
- Board determined **independent under Nasdaq rules for all members except the CEO**; separate CEO and Chairman roles (Ellwanger / Elstein).
- Three fully independent committees (Audit, Compensation, CG&N); the **CG&N committee explicitly owns oversight of social and environmental matters**, with CEO succession planning assigned to it — an actual mandate, not boilerplate.
- **Single class of ordinary shares** — no dual-class super-voting structure identified. Shareholders elect directors annually.
- **Israeli "Special Majority" test** on compensation gives minority shareholders a genuine veto — evidenced by the fact that it was *used*.

**Weaknesses (why not higher):**
- **The compensation policy was voted down at the 2 July 2026 annual meeting.** A failed say-on-pay is the clearest possible market signal of misalignment. Management then engaged **11 shareholders representing ~29% of shares** and had to withdraw two amendments to get a revised policy to an EGM on 25 Nov 2026. Credit for responsiveness — but the initial rejection stands.
- **Pay quantum is aggressive:** proposed caps of **13× salary in annual CEO equity** and **7× for other officers**, with fixed-to-variable ratios of **1:20 (CEO) / 1:14 (others)**. Highly leveraged to equity, which is theoretically shareholder-aligned, but the absolute cap level drew the proxy objection.
- **Chairman tenure and lineage:** Amir Elstein has chaired the board **since January 2009 (~17 years)** — well beyond refreshment norms — and previously served as **Chairman of Israel Corporation**, the historical controlling shareholder. Residual related-party/independence optics.
- **ESG disclosure is stale and unevidenced:** the website still promotes the **2023** Corporate Sustainability (ESG) Report (published June 2024); no FY2024 or FY2025 full ESG report was found. No CDP disclosure, no SBTi-validated target located. An ESG-committed investor is buying 2-year-old data.
- **Litigation overhang:** **GlobalFoundries filed patent infringement suits on 26 March 2026** (11 patents; ITC action plus W.D. Texas), seeking profit-based damages and an **import ban**. This is IP, not ESG, but it is a governance/legal risk with a plausible route to cash flow and belongs in the risk register.

**Score: 6/10** — structurally sound, procedurally democratic, but with a live shareholder-rejection event, a 17-year chair, and lagging disclosure.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Tower is neither a sustainability leader nor a laggard. It has genuine governance architecture (independent board, single share class, special-majority protection, externally audited product-compliance programme, RBA membership, published GHG inventory) offset by three concrete defects: a **failed say-on-pay vote**, a **judicially confirmed union-busting episode**, and **ESG disclosure that has fallen ~2 years behind**. Environmental exposure is real but small in absolute terms and clean on controversy. It would **pass** most ESG inclusion screens; it would fail a "best-in-class" or "net-zero-aligned" screen for absence of a validated target.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**ESG is not a driver of the TSEM thesis in either direction.** At a ~$20.7bn market cap:

- Carbon and water costs, even under aggressive carbon pricing, are a low-single-digit percentage of EBIT. **No stranded-asset risk.**
- The labour ruling is a **sub-$0.2m** item — reputationally awkward, financially noise.
- The governance friction (comp rejection, 17-year chair) is a **real but bounded** discount-rate consideration, partly offset by demonstrable shareholder responsiveness.

**What to actually underwrite is ESG-adjacent, not ESG:** (1) the **GlobalFoundries ITC action** — an exclusion order would directly impair US-bound revenue and is the only item here that can move the equity; (2) **geopolitical concentration** in Israel for the flagship fab; (3) **Japan GX-ETS + METI grant covenants** as the $3bn expansion ramps, which convert a soft carbon issue into a hard contractual one.

**Action:** No ESG-driven position change. Add the labour and disclosure items to the engagement agenda for the **25 Nov 2026 EGM**; monitor the ITC docket as a fundamental risk, not an ESG one. For ESG-mandated mandates, TSEM is **eligible but unexciting** — not a name to build a sustainability narrative around.
