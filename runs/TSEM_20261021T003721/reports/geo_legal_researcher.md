### 1. Geographic Risk Map

**Manufacturing footprint (hard assets):** one operating fab in Israel (200mm), two in the U.S. (200mm), two in Japan (200mm and 300mm), plus 300mm capacity in Italy — company language from its results releases, corroborated by Reuters ("analog semiconductor wafers … in the U.S., Israel, Japan and Italy"). Reuters (14-Jul-2026): Tower will invest **$3B in Japan, backed by ~$1B in METI grants**, repurposing its Arai facility (ex-Fab 6) for 300mm silicon photonics and taking **full ownership of the 300mm TPSCo facility, targeted to close 1-Apr-2027** (it currently owns TPSCo 51%).

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Israel operating-base concentration (Probability: Medium / Impact: High)**
Tower's corporate domicile, headquarters, R&D and one of its five fabs sit in Migdal Haemek, Israel. This carries: (a) direct kinetic/war risk and repeated employee reserve call-ups (5,613 employees globally); (b) a deteriorating *trade* environment — in Sept–Oct 2026 the UK, France, Canada and nine other states announced restrictions on Israeli-origin trade, and 61 economists publicly warned of broader sanctions risk. These measures are aimed at West Bank settlement goods, not semiconductors, so direct financial impact is *low* — but the direction of travel matters for an Israeli-domiciled issuer's cost of capital and for customer/supplier country-of-origin optics. **Note the offsetting positive:** Israel's Innovation Authority andMETI-style grant regimes (Japan's $1B) are actively subsidising the company's expansion.

**Risk 2 — U.S. Section 232 semiconductor tariffs (Probability: High — already law / Impact: Medium)**
A Presidential proclamation of **14-Jan-2026** imposed a **25% Section 232 tariff on certain advanced semiconductors**, effective mid-January 2026, deliberately narrow and with carve-outs for certain end uses. Tower ships wafers from Israel, Japan and Italy to U.S. customers — i.e., it is an *importer* of covered goods with no U.S. tariff-free alternative at scale (its two U.S. fabs are 200mm legacy). Margin absorption or price pass-through on a ~42% U.S. revenue base is a live, quantified risk that the sell-side 2028 "$3.6B revenue / $1.2B net profit" model likely underweights.

**Risk 3 — U.S.–China export-control and retaliation exposure (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium)**
China is embedded in the ~39% "Asia ex-Japan" revenue line, largely via distributors and Asian-based customers of U.S. fabless firms. Two specific markers: (i) **precedent** — Intel's $5.4B acquisition of Tower collapsed in Aug-2023 after China's SAMR declined to clear it, proving Beijing will use Tower as a lever in chip geopolitics and costing the deal; (ii) in **June 2026 U.S. lawmakers urged tighter rules on contract chipmakers supplying Chinese firms' overseas units** — a direct regulatory-channel threat to foundry order books. Tower's older 20-Fs referenced a *planned* 200mm JV in Nanjing, China; its current releases describe no operating Chinese fab, so the exposure is commercial rather than asset-based. Beijing's rare-metal export controls add input-cost risk.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active litigation — GlobalFoundries v. Tower Semiconductor (the single largest legal overhang):**
- Filed **26-Mar-2026**: multiple U.S. suits alleging infringement of **11 GlobalFoundries patents** covering core chipmaking process technologies, plus a **Section 337 complaint at the ITC (Inv. No. 337-TA-1500)**.
- Venue: **W.D. Tex. (Waco), Case 7:26-cv-00109**, Judge David Counts; defendants' response deadline was set for **20-Jul-2026**.
- Relief sought: **lost profits damages and a total U.S. import ban** on infringing products. Parties include **Tower Partners Semiconductor Co. Ltd.** (the Japanese subsidiary), reflecting that the accused processes are used in Japan.
- **Assessment:** the ITC remedy — an exclusion order — is the tail risk, because the accused technology (RF/SiGe/BiCMOS process flows) sits in Tower's highest-growth AI/optical portfolio. Base case remains a paid licence/settlement, consistent with how GF has historically monetised its portfolio (cf. GF v. TSMC, 2019). Watch for an ITC initial determination in 2027 and any customer indemnification demands.

**Other legal/regulatory items:**
- **Regulatory history:** the aborted Intel transaction (2023) is a demonstrated binary regulatory risk event, settled with a $353M termination payment; Intel subsequently took a $300M stake — a strategic relationship worth monitoring for change-of-control optionality.
- **Israeli Innovation Authority grants:** Tower has historically received IIA funding carrying **royalty obligations and know-how/transfer restrictions** — i.e., contractual limits on moving Israeli-developed process IP offshore, which constrains "fab-light" or relocation strategies in a war scenario.
- **U.S. DoD "trusted foundry" status** at the Newport Beach fab: accreditation is an asset but imports ITAR/export-control compliance obligations and restricts who can access certain process flows.
- **Foreign private issuer** (20-F filer, CIK 0000928876): disclosure cadence is semi-annual plus 6-Ks, so legal contingencies surface with a lag versus a domestic 10-Q filer.
- **No evidence found** of FCPA/AML/OFAC enforcement actions, securities class actions, or active SEC/DOJ investigations against Tower.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Rationale.** Tower is not a "sanctions story" — it has no Russia/Iran/BIS-entity exposure and its domicile, while conflict-adjacent, has not materially disrupted output. The rating is driven instead by three converging, identifiable events: (1) an **active ITC + federal patent campaign by GlobalFoundries seeking a U.S. import ban** on the company's core process technologies, filed March 2026 and unresolved; (2) **Section 232 tariffs already in force since January 2026** on advanced semiconductor imports into a market that is ~42% of revenue, with no tariff-free manufacturing alternative at scale; and (3) **single-site production concentration in northern Israel** combined with IIA rules that legally restrict offshoring that IP. Layer on a Chinese regulatory apparatus that has already demonstrated willingness to block a Tower transaction, and a European trade-policy drift away from Israel.

**What would move the rating:** *to High* — an ITC exclusion order or a preliminary injunction touching SiGe/silicon-photonics flows; a renewed Israel-front conflict affecting Migdal Haemek throughput. *To Medium* — a licensed settlement with GlobalFoundries, a tariff carve-out for wafer-level imports, or a Japan/U.S. capacity footprint large enough to re-route Israel output.
