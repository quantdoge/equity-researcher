# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Microsoft Corporation (MSFT)
**Sector:** Technology (Software – Infrastructure) | **Market cap:** ~$3.84T | **HQ:** Redmond, WA | **Assessment date:** Oct 2026

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate (with material concentration concerns)**

**Evidence:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality confirmed** — Satya Nadella holds both roles, the first combined CEO/Chair arrangement at Microsoft in two decades. This concentrates board agenda-setting and management oversight in one person and **bucks prevailing governance trends** at this scale. It is the single largest structural governance weakness.
- **Material insider selling, CEO-led.** Nadella has executed repeated large share sales: ~$6M (Aug 2024), **~$75.3M (149,205 shares ~$503–505)** and **~$43M (86,525 shares)** in subsequent tranches — flagged by financial media as "offloading amid the AI boom." Form 4 volume for MSFT spiked in Sept 2026 (20+ filings), consistent with a broad insider-selling pattern; no insider *buys* were identified in the window.
- **Positives:** No SEC enforcement actions on record for MSFT (EDGAR search returned empty); no fraud/accounting-regularity indicators; institutional 13F activity is mixed (both trims, e.g., KBC −14.4%, and adds) with no panic-level exit; sponsored proxy-data unavailable (gap: DEF 14A and 10-K text not retrievable through the filing tool, so board independence ratios and pay-alignment details could not be independently verified).

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis — **Net selling, CEO-led; signal: negative**

- CEO stock sales totalled roughly **$124M across disclosed tranches** during the AI-bull-phase window — the largest insider activity by dollar volume (per insider-trade trackers).
- Dense Form 4 volume Sept 2026 (multiple filings on 9/11, 9/15, 9/17) suggests routine scheduled selling (e.g., 10b5-1 diversification), but the **absence of insider buying and the CEO's scale of exits** temper the "scheduled diversification" interpretation.
- Institutional ownership drift is mixed — no single-holder red flag.
- **Assessment:** Not evidence of impropriety, but a confidence signal worth weighting against frothy AI sentiment. A "rare insider sale" was literally the news hook — insider demand for MSFT paper is not apparent.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Systemic cybersecurity exposure (High).** Microsoft is simultaneously a repeated *victim* and an implicated *vector* of state-sponsored intrusions: Storm-0558 (2025 emails across 25 US agencies, 2023), China-linked OAuth/Exchange compromises of senior US officials, **Salt Typhoon's exploitation of Microsoft identity/OAuth systems** in the telecom breach wave, and the Jan 2025 *source-code* ("crown jewel") exfiltration campaign by Chinese-state actors. With ~223,000 employees, an enterprise-critical OS/cloud stack, and an AI-infrastructure mandate, Microsoft's security posture is itself the company's biggest single non-financial risk.
2. **Concentrated AI dependencies (High).** Multi-billion-dollar exclusive-compute commitment to OpenAI (a supplier **and** increasingly a competitor-customer), plus NVIDIA GPU dependence for Azure AI capacity, concentrate both supply and earnings on two counterparties. Any disruption or antitrust reconfiguration of the OpenAI relationship directly hits the AI growth narrative.
3. **Business-continuity fragility of the platform (Moderate–High).** The July 2024 CrowdStrike incident (8.5M Windows systems BSOD — largest IT outage in history) plus Microsoft's own Azure configuration-change outages demonstrate that a single third-party or backend fault can cascade globally through Windows/AWS… i.e., **Windows/Azure** estates — an enterprise-trust and liability-exposure risk for a platform company.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment

- **Re-litigating the "monopoly" narrative:** With the revived FTC probe and EU cloud attention, Microsoft is squarely inside the post-Google-ruling Big Tech antitrust backlash. Google's EU complaint over the OpenAI deal adds an active adversarial campaign.
- **Enterprise security trust:** Repeatedly being the named vendor in state-sponsored compromise disclosures corrodes the "if you're on our platform you're protected" brand promise — precisely while Microsoft markets itself as a cybersecurity leader.
- **AI-era ethics exposure:** AI copyright litigation (OpenAI co-defendant exposure), state AG subpoena activity around AI training data, and content-policy controversies keep an ESG/ethics tail risk alive.
- **CEO-selling headlines** ("Nadella offloads $75M amid AI boom") generated negative consumer/retail-investor sentiment, though coverage was noisy rather than systemic.
- **Net:** Reputation is resilient (entrenched enterprise/consumer relationships) but continuously stressed by antitrust and cyber narratives.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Diversity of exposure drives the rating: **multiple active antitrust fronts** (FTC cloud/AI probe; EU cloud/DMA expansion; OpenAI exclusivity complaints), **a governance concentration flag** (CEO/Chair duality) coinciding with **CEO-scale insider selling**, and a **repeat-victim cyber record** on the most systemically important software estate in the world. No fraud indicators, no SEC enforcement, and a settled EU Teams case are the mitigating offsets. Were the FTC probe to convert to litigation or fate the OpenAI arrangement, this would escalate toward **Critical**.

---

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **CEO/Chairman duality persisting** into the next proxy year — a concentration-of-power structure inconsistent with board-independence best practice at $3.8T scale. *(High priority)*
2. **~$124M of CEO insider selling** in a compressed window with **zero insider buys** — flag for compensation-committee review and monitoring of upcoming Form 4s for acceleration. *(High priority)*
3. **Multi-front antitrust exposure concentrated on the two highest-growth segments** (Cloud + AI) — FTC probe, EU cloud/DMA designation risk, and OpenAI exclusivity complaints jointly threaten the business model, not just fines. *(High priority)*
4. **Recurring state-sponsored intrusions through Microsoft identity and source-code assets** (Salt Typhoon OAuth; Jan 2025 source-code theft) — elevate to board-level cyber-oversight; these are cross-product systemic liabilities. *(Medium-high priority)*
