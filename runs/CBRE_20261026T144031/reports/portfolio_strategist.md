## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT (2 bullish / 4 neutral / 5 cautious-negative)**

This is not a close call once evidence quality is weighted — it is a **split where the bearish side is fact-based and the bullish side is narrative-based.**

**The consensus negative (independently reached by three agents):** Sector Researcher and Short Analyst — working from separate datasets — arrive at the *same* damaging fact: gross margin 22.2%→15.0%, operating margin down 171bp over three years, operating profit declining while revenue compounds. Fin Risk and Non-Fin Risk add leverage (3.18x) and legal/alignment flags. This is a **high-confidence, verified** finding.

**The consensus positive (weaker evidence):** Growth Researcher and Alt Data both cite the beat rate and rising estimates. But three caveats degrade this signal: (a) the "100% beat rate" is an *EPS* beat — Q3'26 **revenue missed** by 2.2% and Q4'25 missed too, so beats come from cost control and buybacks, not demand; (b) the +17.75% revision is measured against an **FY2030** estimate, an unusually long-dated and weakly predictive horizon; (c) Growth's own base case ($170) rests on a multiple re-rating that requires the AI-disintermediation narrative to repair — an event no agent can falsify.

**Conflict resolution — the decisive tension:** Value says intrinsic ≈ $115–125 (0% MoS); Growth says $170 (+37%). I resolve this by probability-weighting rather than averaging: **40% base ($125) / 25% bull ($170) / 35% bear ($95)** = **~$126 expected value** against a $123.67 price. That is a **~2% edge with 36.6% annualized volatility and a 28% realized drawdown** — mathematically indistinguishable from zero edge. Per Kelly logic (f* ≈ edge/variance), the correct fraction is **≈0**. This is why the split resolves to *pass*, not to a small buy.

**Where the sector researcher is most persuasive:** the relative call. Within CRE services, CBRE is the *diluting* operator and JLL the *expanding* one — JLL at 18.5x P/E with +33bp margin trajectory vs CBRE at 32.1x GAAP with −171bp. Paying a 37% EV/EBITDA premium for a worse margin trajectory is the single least defensible thing in this file.

**Factor exposure added:** negative momentum, negative value (richest P/FCF and EV/EBITDA in the peer group), deteriorating quality trend (ROIC 12.5%→6.6%), plus a small-capitalization-of-risk flavor (high idiosyncratic variance). Beta 0.93 understates the risk — the R² of 0.11 means beta is a poor risk descriptor; volatility of 36.6% is the honest number.
