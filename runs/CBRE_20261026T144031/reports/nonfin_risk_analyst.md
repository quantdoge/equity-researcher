## CBRE GROUP, INC. (NYSE: CBRE) — NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT

*Data caveat: the SEC filing-text tool had no DEF 14A/10-K coverage for CBRE (10-Q returned only an exhibit), so governance detail was sourced from CBRE's IR-hosted 2026 proxy, and insider detail from Form 4 filing metadata plus secondary aggregation. Gaps are flagged where relevant.*

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (trending weaker)

**Positives**
- **Lead independent director in place**, elected by the independent directors; board is overwhelmingly independent with the CEO as the sole management director (2026 proxy, annual meeting May 21, 2026).
- Board declassified/smaller: **10 nominees** in 2026 (down from 11), suggesting refresh and tightening.
- Proxy includes CD&A performance-based equity (PSU) disclosure covering 2022–2025 performance periods — pay is largely equity-linked, aligned with TSR.

**Negatives / evidence**
- **CEO/Chairman duality.** Robert E. Sulentic has been President & CEO since December 2012 and **assumed the Board Chair role in 2024**, succeeding Brandon Boze. He is now Chair, President and CEO — 14 years of tenure with combined chair/CEO authority. The lead independent director partially offsets this, but it is a structural concentration-of-power flag.
- **Key-person concentration with no disclosed succession plan.** Sulentic is ~64 and there is no public CEO succession framework; the board instead *added* the chair title. This is a live governance continuity risk for a business whose strategy (data centers/infrastructure pivot, M&A) is closely identified with him.
- **Legacy dual-class history.** CBRE was IPO'd with Class B shares carrying 10 votes/share (Blackstone-era structure). While the class structure is no longer a live voting distortion, its history plus a PE-sponsor-influenced board lineage (Boze) is a residual investor-perception issue.
- **Compensation/insider alignment signal is weak** — see §3; senior executives sold into strength near $148–149 while the shares now trade ~$123.58.

**Verdict:** Not a broken board — independent majority, lead director, equity-weighted pay — but combined chair/CEO plus succession opacity keeps this below "Strong."

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**Active / pending (highest severity)**
- **Antitrust class action, filed June 12, 2026, N.D. Illinois (FitFactariDC v. CoStar, CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, Colliers, Newmark).** Alleges a "hub-and-spoke" conspiracy in which brokers exchanged current, non-public, deal-specific lease data through CoStar's comparables database, inflating office/industrial/retail rents in Denver and 49 U.S. metros since June 2022. Class certification sought. This is the single largest non-financial exposure: it targets the core brokerage revenue model (information asymmetry), invites follow-on suits and state AG action, and could force changes to data-sharing practices industry-wide. **Status: early-stage, unquantified, but potentially multi-hundred-million-dollar and structurally disruptive.**

**Historical enforcement (Violation Tracker aggregate)**
- **29 penalty records, ~$7.38M total since 2000.**
- **SEC, Sept 2023:** $375,000 civil penalty (cease-and-desist) for conditioning separation pay on releases that impeded whistleblowers — a *culture/compliance* failure, not a technical one. Remedial steps and a procedure change were credited.
- **Employment discrimination, 2010:** $4.15M (private federal suit, CB Richard Ellis).
- **Price-fixing/anti-competitive:** $2.375M across 2 records — including a **$2.125M 2026 federal private settlement involving Trammell Crow Residential** and a 2020 Colorado AG action ($250K). Note the pattern: antitrust exposure recurs across both the brokerage and development arms.
- **OSHA/safety:** 18 records, ~$330K — consistent with a 155,000-person building-operations workforce (low severity, high frequency).
- **SEC EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned no additional actions** — no open SEC/DOJ investigation disclosed in the sources reviewed.
- **Regulatory backdrop:** SEC proposed rescission of its 2024 climate-disclosure rules (July 2026), reducing U.S. reporting burden but creating fragmentation versus EU/APAC/Australia mandatory regimes — CBRE must satisfy client-driven and jurisdictional disclosure demands regardless.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Interest-rate / capital-markets cyclicality is the earnings fulcrum.** Q3 2026 revenue of $11.5B **missed consensus by 2.4%** despite +12.5% y/y growth; operating margin flat at 4.9%; management explicitly tied the outlook to **rate stabilization** and the stock sold off on that commentary. Transaction/brokerage revenue is high-beta to cap rates (Q2 2026: mortgage rates 5.7%, cap rates 6.3% and rising) with a self-forecast U.S. GDP slowdown to 2.0% in 2026. This is a structurally cyclical, low-margin, commission-heavy model (4.9% operating margin) — small revenue shortfalls swing earnings hard.
2. **M&A integration and data-center concentration.** The **Onnec Group acquisition (£460M / ~$610M cash, plus a multi-year earn-out, announced Oct 20, 2026)** plus prior acquisitions are now the growth engine (infrastructure services +50% y/y to $4.5B TTM; ~$7B targeted for 2027). Guidance explicitly depends on continued M&A — serial-deal integration, purchase-accounting/earn-out valuation, and concentration in a single hot asset class (data centers, itself facing power/permitting and AI-capex-cycle risk) are compounding execution risks. Management is *already* restructuring "problematic" enterprise facilities-management contracts — evidence that integration/contract underwriting has been imperfect.
3. **Labor intensity, contract underwriting, and key-person dependency.** 155,000 employees (mostly non-exempt, high-turnover, client-site staff) create wage/union, safety (18 OSHA records), and service-quality exposure; large enterprise facilities contracts carry repricing/collection risk. Overlaying this, strategy, capital allocation and client relationships are heavily identified with Sulentic, with no disclosed succession plan.

*Secondary:* Cyber — CBRE's 10-K discloses real-time board escalation for material breaches and third-party assessors; a 2024 "IntelBroker/TheSupremeGodKing" breach claim circulating on social media is **unverified** and should be treated as rumor, not fact. However, CBRE holds highly sensitive tenant/lease/valuation data — precisely the dataset at issue in the antitrust suit — making it an elevated-value cyber target.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: MODERATE (rising)

- **Antitrust narrative is the dominant brand risk.** Being named alongside CoStar and four peers in a rent-inflation conspiracy suit cuts directly at CBRE's "trusted advisor" positioning and its ESG/"responsible landlord" marketing. Adverse coverage is compounding: Bisnow, Facilities Dive and trade press have already framed the industry as a "data cartel." Class certification in 2027 would generate sustained negative headlines and client-side scrutiny from tenants (CBRE's own occupier clients).
- **Culture/compliance stain** from the 2023 SEC whistleblower case — a *deliberate* practice of conditioning separation pay on releases — remains a citable governance-oversight failure and a template for plaintiff/regulator narrative.
- **Segment pressure and "restructuring contracts"** language risks signaling execution problems to enterprise clients evaluating renewals.
- **ESG exposure is two-sided**: climate-disclosure regime flux creates compliance and reputational inconsistency risk; CBRE's large data-center buildout invites energy/water/permitting criticism from ESG-focused clients and investors.
- **Mitigants:** Strong Q3 EPS beat ($2.05 vs. $1.97), 26% GAAP EPS growth, $1.8B TTM free cash flow, and a generally positive franchise reputation — reputational damage is currently legal-driven rather than product/safety-driven.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: A material, unquantified **antitrust class action attacking the core brokerage information model**, layered on a **recurring antitrust pattern** (Trammell Crow 2020 and 2026), **combined Chair/CEO with no succession plan**, **net insider selling into a 17% decline**, and **margin-flat, rate-sensitive earnings with an integration-heavy M&A strategy**. Governance mitigants (lead independent director, independent-majority board) and a clean recent SEC/DOJ record keep this from "Critical."

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **[HIGH] FitFactariDC v. CoStar et al. (N.D. Ill., filed 6/12/2026)** — hub-and-spoke rent price-fixing via CoStar lease data; 49-metro class sought. Escalate for legal reserve analysis and scenario modeling of class certification; monitor for additional AG/DOJ involvement and for co-defendant settlement dynamics that could leave CBRE exposed.
2. **[HIGH] Recurring antitrust pattern** — $2.125M 2026 Trammell Crow Residential settlement plus 2020 Colorado AG action plus the brokerage suit: this is a systemic business-model issue, not an isolated event.
3. **[MEDIUM-HIGH] CEO/Chair duality and succession opacity** — Sulentic holds Chair + President + CEO after 14 years; no disclosed succession plan while strategy depends on him. Escalate as a board-engagement item and a continuity-risk disclosure question.
4. **[MEDIUM-HIGH] Insider alignment** — ~$3.3M sold, CFO ~$1.4M at ~$149, no open-market buys, stock subsequently −17%. Request 10b5-1 plan adoption dates to distinguish scheduled from opportunistic sales.
5. **[MEDIUM] Earnings quality of the growth story** — Q3 revenue miss, flat 4.9% margin, "problematic contract" restructuring, and guidance contingent on M&A + data-center demand. Ask management for organic-vs-acquired revenue split and Onnec earn-out/valuation assumptions.
6. **[MEDIUM] Culture/whistleblower history** — 2023 SEC order ($375K) for impeding whistleblowers via separation agreements; confirm current separation-agreement language and ethics hotline metrics post-remediation.
7. **[LOW/UNVERIFIED] Cyber breach claim** — unverified 2024 threat-actor claim against CBRE; verify via security team before citing in the memo. Data sensitivity (lease/valuation databases) makes this a standing watch item.
