## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment

**Rating: MEDIUM-LOW (deteriorating)** — not fraud, but reported headline growth is materially lower-quality than it appears. The single most important finding: **CBRE's operating profit has fallen for three years while revenue rose 32%.**

**The earnings bridge is the smoking gun.** 2025 net income rose 19.5% while *operating income fell 8.4%*. The entire increase came from non-operating "other income," which swung from **+$37M in 2024 to +$523M in 2025** (EBIT less operating income) — real-estate co-investment/equity-method gains. Strip those out and 2025 pre-tax profit was *down*. Management's own Q3-2026 release confirms the reversal: Real Estate Investments operating profit fell to **$38M, "lower co-investment returns."** The 2025 beat is being handed back.

Cash conversion looks fine (OCF/NI 1.35x, FCF/NI 1.03x), but **absolute FCF fell 20%** ($1.49B → $1.19B) and OCF fell 13%. Composite earnings-quality score: **57/100** — mediocre. Accruals are genuinely negative (see §3), so this is *not* an accrual-fraud case; it is a **mix/composition and capital-allocation** case.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

1. **Gross margin collapse: 21.5% → 15.0% over three years, −466bp in 2025 alone.** Gross profit *fell* in dollars ($7.02B → $6.07B) on 13.4% revenue growth. Beneish GMI of **1.311** quantifies this — the model's second-largest red flag. This is the signature of buying revenue through lower-margin facilities-management/GWS contracts and acquisitions, not pricing power.
2. **Receivables growing 2.3x faster than revenue.** AR +30.3% vs revenue +13.4% in 2025 (and +22.2% vs +3.6% in 2023). Days sales outstanding: **74 → 87 → 83 → 95 days**. Classic channel-stuffing / collection-deterioration signal (Beneish DSRI **1.15**).
3. **Payables stretch is masking the receivables build.** AR rose $2.46B but reported working-capital change was only −$67M — because current liabilities rose $3.03B. Cash flow is being propped up by paying suppliers slower. That flatters OCF and is why the accruals ratio looks benign.
4. **Debt up 75% in one year** ($5.69B → $9.99B); net debt **$8.13B**, net-debt/EBITDA **3.2x** (vs 2.1x in 2024, 2.0x in 2023). Interest expense quadrupled 2022–25 ($69M → $223M) while operating income fell.
5. **Goodwill ($10.02B) now exceeds total book equity ($9.63B)** — 32% of assets. Acquired at a moment when acquired margins are visibly compressing. Impairment risk is live, and any write-down takes equity below goodwill.
6. **Revenue growth decelerating sharply:** +18.2% (Q1'26) → +15.1% (Q2'26) → **+12.3%** (Q3'26). The tool labels this "stable"; the series says otherwise.
7. **Per-share earnings have gone nowhere for three years** despite the roll-up: diluted EPS $4.29 (2022) → $3.85 (2025), **−10%**, and that is *after* ~$4.1B of buybacks shrank the diluted count from 327.7M to 300.8M. Ex-buyback, per-share earnings are down roughly 17%.
8. **ROIC ≈ 5.3%** (NOPAT $939M / invested capital $17.75B) against a cost of capital near 8% — the roll-up is destroying value, not creating it.
9. **Thin liquidity:** current ratio 1.10; Altman Z **2.274 — grey zone**; Altman X1 (working capital/assets) only 0.038.
