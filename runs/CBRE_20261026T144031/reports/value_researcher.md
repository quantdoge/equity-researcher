## CBRE Group, Inc. (NYSE: CBRE) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price basis: $123.67 (last close 2026-10-26); market cap ≈ $37.2B (300.8M diluted shares); EV ≈ $45.3B. Stock is 29% below its 52-week high of $174.27.*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: NARROW

CBRE is the world's largest commercial real estate services franchise: 2025 revenue of **$40.6B** across advisory/leasing/capital markets, Global Workplace Solutions (GWS) facilities outsourcing, project management (Turner & Townsend), and investment management (Trammell Crow, CBRE IM) — vs. JLL (~$24B) and Colliers (~$5B). Scale here is real: multi-year global outsourcing contracts require a footprint no challenger can replicate quickly, and ~95% GWS renewal rates create genuine switching costs. The brand and Trammell Crow/Turner & Townsend franchises are legitimate intangible assets.

But the moat does **not** extend to the highest-margin activity — transaction brokerage — which is cyclical, commission-based, and fundamentally talent-driven (brokers can walk). Nor does the financial evidence support a wide moat:

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROIC | 12.5% | 8.0% | 9.5% | **6.6%** |
| ROE | 16.4% | 10.9% | 10.5% | 12.0% |
| EBITDA margin | 6.6% | 5.6% | 6.0% | 6.3% |
| GAAP diluted EPS | $4.29 | $3.15 | $3.14 | $3.85 |

The moat test Buffett applies — ROIC consistently above WACC — **fails on the recent record**. Four-year average ROIC ≈9.2%, essentially equal to my ~9% WACC estimate. Note this is partly an artifact of capital allocation: $10.0B of goodwill/intangibles (32% of assets) from acquisition premiums depresses book returns. On tangible capital the business earns high returns, but shareholders paid those premiums, so consolidated returns are what count. Margins have narrowed structurally as revenue mix shifted toward pass-through, lower-margin outsourcing (gross margin 22.2% → 15.0% since 2021), even though EBITDA margin held ~6%. Verdict: **Narrow moat — best-in-class operator in a cyclical, capital-light, low-margin, people-dependent industry.**

### 2. Financial Quality Score: 6 / 10

**Positives**
- Revenue predictability: 9.2% five-year CAGR ($23.8B → $40.6B), only one down year (2020, −0.3%); recurring GWS/IM revenue cushions cyclicality.
- Cash conversion: 2025 FCF $1.19B vs. net income $1.16B = **103%**; owner earnings $1.52B (131% of GAAP NI). Asset-light: capex $366M vs. D&A $729M.
- Balance sheet: $1.86B cash, $9.99B debt, net debt $8.13B — investment-grade and manageable.

**Negatives**
- **Quality-of-earnings drift**: revenue +46% since 2021 while GAAP EPS fell from $5.41 to $3.85 (−29%). The GAAP-to-"core" gap has widened materially (2026 Q2: GAAP $0.69 vs. core $1.56) — heavy acquisition amortization/integration charges are recurring, not one-off.
- **Leverage rising with M&A**: net debt/EBITDA 3.2x vs. 2.1x in 2024; interest expense tripled to $223M from $69M in 2022. Goodwill up $2.1B, total assets up $6.5B in one year.
- **FCF volatility**: 2021 $2.23B → 2022 $1.46B → 2023 $0.23B → 2024 $1.49B → 2025 $1.19B. FCF is flat-to-down over four years despite 40% revenue growth.
- Negative tangible book (equity $9.63B vs. goodwill/intangibles $10.02B) — no asset floor.
- No dividend; SBC modest ($120M).

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — Good, with recent concerns

**Strengths**: CEO Bob Sulentic (since 2012) converted a debt-laden local brokerage roll-up into a global services and investment platform; long-run TSR has been excellent. Capital returns are shareholder-friendly and value-accretive: **$4.1B of buybacks over 2021–2025** (2022 $1.85B, 2023 $665M, 2024 $627M, 2025 $968M), shrinking the diluted share count 11.4% (339.7M → 300.8M) at average prices well below today's. No dividend, which is correct for a reinvestment-led compounder.

**Concerns**: The recent M&A cadence has been aggressive — $6.5B of assets and $4.3B of debt added in 2025 — pushing leverage to 3.2x and ROIC down to 6.6%, and interest expense tripled. The growing reliance on "core" EPS framing to bridge to GAAP is a mild yellow flag; Buffett-style analysis should discount non-GAAP reconciliation. *Data gap: I could not retrieve the 10-K (unavailable) or verify insider ownership/skin-in-the-game from the proxy — an unassessed variable I would want before committing capital.*

### 5. Investment Verdict: HOLD

**Not a Buy at $123.67.** This is a genuinely good, scale-advantaged business with sticky recurring revenue and excellent cash conversion — but the price already reflects a full, optimistic scenario, ROIC sits at WACC, and the margin-of-safety threshold is absent. **Accumulate below ~$100; add aggressively below ~$88** (25–30% discount to base intrinsic value). A HOLD is the honest verdict; a SELL is not warranted given the franchise quality and near-52-week-low valuation.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI disruption of transaction brokerage** — the core overhang. AI-driven underwriting/comparables/leasing tools could compress commissions in Advisory (the highest-margin segment). This fear knocked CBRE 12.8% in a single session (Feb-2026) and again 8.4% after Q3-2026. Raymond James calls the fears "misplaced," but the risk is real and unquantifiable — precisely why a discount is required.
2. **Cyclicality** — transaction volumes track rates, credit availability, and office demand. A downturn hits the highest-margin revenue first.
3. **M&A integration and leverage** — 3.2x net debt/EBITDA with $10B goodwill; further deals at peak-cycle prices could keep ROIC at or below WACC and dilute per-share value. Negative tangible book removes any downside cushion.
4. **Margin structure** — 6.3% EBITDA margin leaves little cushion; mix shift toward pass-through outsourcing caps upside.
5. **Talent leakage / disintermediation** — brokerage is a people business with no contractual lock-in; top producers can leave or be hired away.
6. **Earnings-quality credibility** — if "core" adjustments prove to be recurring cash costs, the effective multiple is higher than headline core EPS implies.
