### 1. Credit Risk Assessment
**Preliminary Rating: Sub-Investment Grade (Monitoring)**

While CBRE's debt servicing capacity appears robust based on its interest coverage, the company’s leverage profile and the thinness of its liquidity metrics place it in a cautious category.

**Analysis:**
*   **Leverage Sustainability:** The Net Debt to EBITDA ratio of **3.18x** is elevated but within the realm of tolerance for the institutional Real Estate sector, which is capital-intensive. However, a ratio over 3.0 warrants close monitoring of future earnings stability.
*   **Debt Servicing (Interest Coverage):** With an Interest Coverage Ratio of **8.27x** (EBIT/Interest Expense: $1.82B / $0.22B), CBRE has a significant buffer against default risk, suggesting strong operational cash flow coverage for its interest payments.
*   **Financial Health Indicators (Altman/Piotroski):** Automated calculation tools failed to retrieve scores due to data unavailability. **[Data Gap Flag]** However, based on profitability (positive net income) and manageable debt, preliminary assessment suggests a relatively stable financial position, likely scoring in the *Grey Zone* for Altman/F-Score.

### 2. Liquidity Risk
**Rating: Moderate**

*   **Current Ratio:** **1.09:1** (Current Assets: $13.49B / Current Liabilities: $12.32B). This is tight for a large enterprise.
*   **Quick Ratio:** **1.09:1**. Since inventory is negligible, this is equivalent to the current ratio.
*   **Assessment:** CBRE's liquidity position is adequate for normal operations but leaves little margin for error. A sustained contraction in rental income or a shift in debt covenants could quickly compress this coverage. The existing cash and short-term investments ($1.86B) provides a cushion, but the company relies heavily on asset management and recurring fee-based revenue.
*   **Covenant Risk:** No immediate covenant breach is apparent based on current ratios, but the tight liquidity requires vigilance.

*   **Stress Test (Revenue -20%):**

    *   **New Net Income (Estimate):** Drops to approximately **$0.93B**.

    *   **Credit Stress:** The Net Debt/EBITDA ratio would deteriorate significantly to an estimated **~5.6x** ($8.13B / $1.45B), placing CBRE firmly in a **Distress** territory regarding leverage coverage.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **High**

While CBRE possesses strong operational resilience and high interest coverage, the combination of **thin liquidity (1.09:1)**, **elevated leverage (3.18x Net Debt/EBITDA)**, and a **very poor risk-adjusted return (Sharpe Ratio: -0.852)** indicates a high risk profile. The company is highly susceptible to revenue shocks, as demonstrated by the stress test.

### 6. Key Red Flags and Vulnerabilities

*   **Liquidity Tightness:** The current and quick ratios (1.09:1) are thin. Any sudden dip in fee income or asset valuations could trigger a liquidity squeeze.
*   **Poor Risk-Adjusted Return:** The negative Sharpe ratio is the most concerning metric, signaling that the current equity risk premium demanded by investors is not being sufficiently rewarded by the company's historical performance.
*   **Reliance on RE Cycle:** As a service provider to a cyclical industry, CBRE is vulnerable to broader economic contractions and changes in commercial real estate trends.
*   **Data Gaps:** The lack of definitive Altman Z-Score and Piotroski F-Score prevents a complete, quantitative measure of immediate bankruptcy probability.
