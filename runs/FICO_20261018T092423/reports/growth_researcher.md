## FICO — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $771.19 (16-Oct-26) · Mkt cap ~$17.0B · Source data: FMP fundamentals, FY ends 30-Sep*

---

### 1. Growth Story

FICO is not a frontier innovator — it is a **regulated network monopoly whose pricing engine has just been structurally attacked**. For two decades the growth algorithm was simple and near-perfect: raise the wholesale mortgage score price annually (to $4.95/score), collect on both purchase and refinance volume, and recycle the ~40% FCF margin into buybacks. That algorithm broke between 4-Sep and 28-Sep-2026, when the FHFA directed Fannie/Freddie to accept **VantageScore 4.0**, then announced a **unified pricing grid** eliminating FICO's structural differential — and Rocket Mortgage (the largest U.S. originator) named VantageScore its preferred model. The stock fell ~60% from its $1,476 March high to $586 (30-Sep) before rebounding 30% to $771 on FICO's counter-moves (Mortgage Direct License Program; ~15% RIF).

The forward thesis is now a **share-defense-and-reprice story, not a share-gain story**: FY2026 is a blowout (+27% revenue, ~+55% EPS) precisely because FICO re-contracted mortgage scores at a higher *effective* per-loan economics ($0.99/score + $65 funding fee vs $4.95 + $33), and FY2027 is where the durability question gets answered.

### 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **U.S. consumer scoring pool:** ~$2B (FICO ≈ $1.6–1.7B of it). U.S. mortgage score revenue ≈ **$450–550M, ~20% of total revenue but the majority of incremental growth.** Penetration is ~90%+ — this is a **saturated, price-only market**, which is why VantageScore (priced at a fraction of FICO, and *owned by Equifax/Experian/TransUnion*) is such a clean attack.
- **The real growth TAM is elsewhere:** FICO Platform decisioning/fraud/compliance (~$12B TAM, FICO ~$850M software) and international scoring (India, LatAm, SE Asia — genuinely low penetration). Management's own messaging shifts there.
- **Structural conflict undermining the moat:** FICO's distribution channel (the three CRCs) jointly owns its only competitor. Every incentive alignment runs against FICO — this is a classic incumbent-disruption setup, not a moat-widening one.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **4-Nov-26: Q4 FY26 + first FY27 guide** (cons. $658M rev / $10.86 EPS). *The* binary event — FY27 revenue guide vs the stale ~$2.78B consensus.
- **Q4-FY26 → FY27: Mortgage Direct License Program adoption** (Xactus multi-year partnership live; $0.99/score + $65 funding fee) — evidence lenders will re-contract before switching costs reset.
- **FY27: FHFA unified pricing grid implementation** — clarity on whether any FICO differential survives at the GSEs.
- **FY27: restructuring harvest** — 15% RIF, ~$27M pre-tax charges → opex offsets that partially cushion a revenue reset.
- **Capital allocation**: $2B buyback exhausted; watch for new authorization vs. debt paydown (a deleveraging pivot would signal management expects lower cash flow).

### 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Google Trends "VantageScore" (US, 5-yr):** baseline 20–30 in late 2025 → 44–75 Feb–Jun 2026 → **peak 100 (wk of 19-Apr-26)** → faded to 18–27 by mid-Aug → spiked 54/49 on the Aug-30 and Sep-27 FHFA news → **now 25 (still ~2.5x the 2025 baseline)**. Competitive awareness is not decaying.
- **Google Trends "FICO score":** holds 60–90 vs VantageScore's 25 — **FICO retains ~2.5x consumer mindshare**, but interest has drifted from 85–100 (Mar–Jun) to 58–65 (Sep–Oct). Brand default intact; growth in *searches* is flat-to-down.
- **Hiring/headcount:** the ~15% workforce reduction is a decisive *negative* growth signal — the first RIF in memory at a company that grows revenue per employee. (Job-posting feed not retrieved; headcount action is the stronger signal.)
- **Volume vs price:** Q3 mortgage revenue +97% on **flat origination volumes** — confirming growth is 100% price/contract structure, 0% market. That is the thesis's central fragility.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

This is the rare case of a **great business with a broken growth algorithm at a genuinely cheap price**. At $771: **EV ~$21.5B / FY26E revenue 8.5x; P/E 18.0x FY26E, 15.7x FY27E; ~14.5x FY26E FCF (~6.9% FCF yield); PEG ~1.0**. Post-collapse analyst targets average **~$1,070** (range $700 BofA → $1,230 Raymond James) — but that ~$1,070 dispersion is itself the tell: the market has no idea what the terminal economics are.

The Cathie Wood test — *is growth durable at 20%+ with an expanding moat?* — fails. Growth is decelerating to high-single digits by design, the moat is being opened by regulators at the behest of the company's own distributors, and the buyback that was supposed to compound the story ($2B repurchased near $1,200–1,400, ~2x today's price) instead added leverage (interest expense +110% YoY to $240M annualized; **book value −$71/share**). You don't pay up for 10% growth with negative equity and a regulator holding the pricing pen.

**Why not Sell:** 80%+ gross margins, ~45% FCF margins, Rule of 40 ~72, a still-dominant consumer brand, and a valuation that already assumes a severe outcome. Sum-of-parts: Software + non-mortgage Scores alone (~$2B revenue, high-teens margins) plausibly support ~$600–650 of the current price, meaning the market is charging very little for mortgage scoring optionality.

**Upgrade triggers → Buy:** (1) FY27 guide ≥$2.75B revenue with mortgage revenue retention >70%, (2) two of the top-10 originators publicly re-committing post-Direct-License, (3) evidence VantageScore adoption stalls at pilot stage. **Downgrade trigger:** FY27 guide <$2.6B or a second top-5 originator defect.

### 7. Key Risks

- **Distribution-channel conflict (highest).** The three CRCs that distribute FICO also own VantageScore. Rocket's endorsement creates a demand-side cascade that FICO cannot price-defend without destroying its own revenue base.
- **Price/volume decoupling.** Nearly all recent growth is price. Any unified GSE grid that equalizes score economics cuts FY27 EPS by an estimated 25–35% — consensus has likely not marked this.
- **Leverage and capital misallocation.** ~$4.5B net debt, negative book equity, $2B of buybacks executed into a thesis break. A downgrade or covenant tightening would force a growth-investment retreat.
- **Regulatory/political risk.** FHFA (Pulte) has shown willingness to intervene directly in score pricing; CFPB scrutiny of consumer score costs is a live tail risk.
- **Execution risk on the pivot.** Simultaneously re-platforming mortgage delivery, cutting 15% of staff, and defending software share against incumbents — with a 3,876-person org and a sales force built around a single price-escalator.
- **Software segment softness.** If Platform growth is truly "softer," the offset thesis (software + international replacing mortgage price) is weaker than the bull case requires.
