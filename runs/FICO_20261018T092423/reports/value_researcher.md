# Fair Isaac Corporation (FICO) — Value Investment Analysis

**Current price: ~$771** (last reliable market print, ~Oct 2026; the stock has fallen ~60% from its ~$2,000 peak in late 2024). **Market cap ~$17.3B; net debt ~$2.9B; EV ~$20.2B.**

> *Data note:* My price feeds were internally inconsistent — daily history ended Feb-2026 at ~$1,350 while the valuation model and the most recent analyst notes (targets posted at $646–$763 in Oct-2026) point to ~$770. I have anchored on ~$770, corroborated by three independent data points. Fiscal 2025 is the last complete audited year; I have reconstructed an FY2026 trailing-twelve-month (TTM through Q3-FY26) from quarterly filings.

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **WIDE, but narrowing at the edge**

FICO is two businesses under one roof:

- **Scores (~$1.17B FY25, +27% y/y):** The FICO Score is a de facto standard — used by "90% of top lenders," embedded in Fannie/Freddie underwriting, securitization and regulation. This is a near-monopoly with extraordinary pricing power and ~virtually 100% incremental margins. This is the crown jewel.
- **Software (~$820M):** FICO Platform and decisioning/analytics software — a sticky, subscription-like annuity with high switching costs (mission-critical decision engines in banks, insurers, telcos).

**Moat sources:** intangible asset/brand (the score itself is a regulated standard), high switching costs (Software), and scale/regulatory entrenchment (Scores).

**The critical development:** In **April 2026 the FHFA began allowing VantageScore 4.0 to compete for GSE mortgage underwriting**, and the market reacted violently — Rocket Mortgage announced a switch at "$0.99" pricing and TransUnion extended VantageScore mortgage pricing through 2028. This is the **first credible structural threat to FICO's mortgage-score pricing power in decades**, and it directly attacks the highest-margin, fastest-growing part of the franchise. The moat in *mortgage scoring* has narrowed; the moat in *Software and non-mortgage Scores* remains wide. Net: a wide moat with a visible crack.

**Moat trajectory check:** ROIC has *risen* steadily — 48.9% (FY22) → 53.0% → 57.3% → **69.6% (FY25)** — far above any reasonable WACC (>15 pts), the textbook signature of a moated business. But this very prosperity is what invited the regulator to open the market.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **Roughly fair; no margin of safety**

**Multiples at ~$770:** ~22x TTM EPS (~$34.6), ~20x FY26E, **~16x EV/EBITDA**, **~19–21x P/FCF**, **~5% FCF yield**. (The tool's 24.6x P/FCF and 23x EV/EBITDA are on stale FY25 figures; the TTM base is materially higher.)

**Intrinsic value (owner-earnings DCF, SBC treated as a real cost):**

| Scenario | Assumptions | Intrinsic value/sh |
|---|---|---|
| **Bear** — moat cracks | $820M owner earnings; 6% growth fading to 2% terminal, WACC 10% | **~$450** |
| **Base** — share loss contained, Software durable | $820–900M; 12% fading to 3% terminal, WACC 9% | **~$850** |
| **Bull** — FICO retains mortgage pricing | 15%→4%, WACC 8.5% | **~$1,100** |

**Probability-weighted fair value ≈ $810** (25% bear / 50% base / 25% bull) — i.e., the stock is trading **at, not below, intrinsic value.** Even the generous base case offers only ~10% upside — well short of the **20–30% margin of safety** I require.

*Cross-check:* Analyst targets were slashed but cluster at **$875–$1,230** (median ~$1,100), implying 15–60% upside — consistent with "overdone sell-off, but not yet a fat pitch." The generic peer screen (median P/E 43.7 vs FICO 29) is **not a valid comp** — my peer set was the mega-cap semis/platform names, not information-services peers (TRU, FDS, NDAQ, DB1), so I discount it.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **B+ (strong operator, one capital-allocation blemish)**

**Positives:** CEO Will Lansing has run the "raise price, harvest margin, buy back stock" playbook brilliantly for a decade. ROIC ~70% reflects genuine operating discipline. Share count cut from 29.9M (FY20) to ~22.7M (Q3-FY26) — **~24% retired** — a powerful per-share compounding engine. No dividend (fine — retained capital earns 70% ROIC).

**The blemish:** In FY2025 FICO spent **$1.41B on buybacks** (and $822M in FY24, $1.10B in FY22) — much of it **debt-funded and at average prices of ~$1,500–2,000**, i.e., roughly twice today's price. That is meaningful value destruction at the top of the cycle, made worse by levering up to do it. Buffett tolerates buybacks only below intrinsic value; FICO's timing was poor. *(I could not verify current insider ownership from available feeds — a gap to confirm from the proxy.)*

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (Buy trigger: **≤ ~$600**)

This is unambiguously a **wonderful business** — 80%+ gross margins, ~70% ROIC, >100% FCF conversion, a genuine standard-setting franchise. It is also now trading near **fair value (~$770 vs. ~$810 probability-weighted intrinsic)** after a 60% drawdown.

But two things stop me from calling it a Buy:
1. **No margin of safety.** At ~22x TTM earnings and 16x EV/EBITDA, the price roughly equals intrinsic value. "A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy."
2. **The moat is being tested in real time.** FHFA-sanctioned VantageScore competition attacks the highest-margin pillar. FY2026's stunning results may be a **price-hike harvest ahead of the competitive wave** — note Q3-FY26 revenue ($674M) already ticked *down* sequentially from Q2 ($692M). If mortgage Scores pricing compresses in FY2027–28, the bear case (~$450) is live, and the debt load removes the cushion.

**Discipline:** I will not pay full price for a franchise whose pricing power is under structural review. For a patient investor, the entry point is a **25% discount to base intrinsic value — roughly $600/share (~15x normalized earnings, ~6.5% FCF yield)** — which would properly compensate for the binary competitive risk. Existing holders can hold; new money should wait for either a lower price or evidence that FICO is *retaining* mortgage share against VantageScore.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **VantageScore adoption outpaces expectations** — Rocket's "$0.99" pricing and TransUnion's 2028 commitment could seed a genuine price war in mortgage scoring, the segment that generated the FY25–26 margin surge. *This is the thesis-breaker.*
2. **Growth cliff / "harvest" reversal** — FY2026's +25% revenue may front-run price increases; a FY2027 Scores decline would expose a much lower normalized earnings base.
3. **Leverage & negative equity** — $3.1B gross debt, ~$180–200M rising interest expense, negative book equity; limited room to maneuver if cash flow stalls. Buybacks at 2x today's price worsened this.
4. **Regulatory/political** — Credit scoring is politically charged; FHFA and consumer-advocacy pressure could further commoditize the Score or cap pricing.
5. **Mortgage-volume sensitivity** — B2B/B2C Scores and myFICO are levered to origination volumes and rates; a weak housing market compounds the pricing threat.
6. **Software segment concentration** — must carry more of the growth burden if Scores softens; watch Platform bookings as the key offset.

**Bottom line:** A great business at a fair price with a cracked—not broken—moat. Hold, watch the VantageScore share data each quarter, and be ready to buy aggressively if Mr. Market offers ~$600.
