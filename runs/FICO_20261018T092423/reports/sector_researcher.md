# Sector Brief — FICO (Fair Isaac Corp.) | Credit Analytics & Decisioning

**Sector classification note:** FICO sits in the *credit data, scoring & decision analytics* sub-sector — economically far closer to the bureaus (TransUnion, Equifax, Experian) and to data-analytics compounders like Verisk and Moody's Analytics than to the mega-cap semiconductors that dominate the GICS "Technology" peer list returned by the screen. Peer set below is hand-constructed accordingly. *Data gap: the sector-aggregate financials endpoint returned only single-company rows and was unusable; Experian is not in the dataset.*

## 1. Sector Overview

Credit scoring and decision analytics is a ~$25–30B high-margin oligopoly built on three pillars: regulated consumer credit files (Equifax, TransUnion, Experian), a scoring layer that converts files into a portable, investor-accepted risk measure (FICO historically ~90%+ of GSE-eligible mortgage scores), and decisioning software sold to lenders. The defining characteristic has been *regulatory or contractual* barriers to entry rather than technology — a score is valuable only because the GSEs and secondary-market investors accept it. That barrier just moved: the FHFA began a limited VantageScore 4.0 rollout on 22 Apr 2026, extended access to all approved enterprise lenders on 9 Sep 2026, and aligned upfront fees across Classic FICO and VantageScore 4.0 on 30 Sep 2026. The sector is therefore mid-transition from a **monopoly scoring layer to a two-player market**, while the underlying demand pool is cyclically soft (MBA projects originations falling 5% to ~$1.99T in 2027, with 30-yr rates above 7%). Structurally attractive economics; a business model in the middle of a re-rating.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Recurring, mission-critical data rent outside mortgage.** FICO's non-mortgage B2B scores (bank card, auto, personal loans, collections) remain effectively uncontested and were the engine of Q3 FY26 Scores revenue of $458.9M, **+41% y/y** — driven by contractual pricing escalators, not volume.
- **Software/decisioning platform as a second franchise.** Q3 FY26 Software revenue $215.3M (+2%), FY26 guided revenue raised to **$2.53B** (~+27% y/y vs FY25's $1.99B). Sector-wide, demand for explainable AI/model governance is rising (FICO's own 2026 survey: only 5.2% of tech leaders can fully explain their AI decisions).
- **Data-moat consolidation.** Verisk (43.7% operating margin) and Moody's (44.8%) demonstrate that proprietary-data franchises can expand margins for years; the bureau oligopoly plus scoring layer is one of the few software-adjacent pools with 80%+ gross margins and genuine regulatory protection.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Loss of the mortgage-score monopoly — the single largest risk.** Bureaus are undercutting aggressively: TransUnion extended a **99¢ mortgage price for VantageScore 4.0 through December 2028**, versus FICO's multi-dollar wholesale royalty. VantageScore claims the market saves **>$600M in the first 12 months** of competition. FICO's stock is **-59.7% YTD** (to $681.77 on 7 Oct 2026 vs $1,690.62 at YE25); FICO 10T is approved but **not yet GSE-eligible**, so FICO cannot respond with its newest model.
- **Cyclical mortgage volume + rates above 7%.** Score revenue is levered to origination units; a 5% volume decline in 2027 compounds the pricing reset. Equalised upfront fees removed the last practical friction to lender substitution.
- **Balance-sheet strain from debt-funded buybacks.** FICO repurchased **$3.1B** in the first nine months of FY26 — **4.0x operating cash flow** — lifting total debt **+82.7% to $5.58B** (from $3.06B) and interest expense to $133.6M in FY25. It simultaneously announced a **15% workforce reduction** (6 Oct 2026) and faces securities-firm investigations. Leverage removes the flexibility to defend price.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position — **Strong, but eroding at the core**

**Rationale for "strong":** FICO's margin structure is not merely sector-leading, it is a different business model — 82.2% gross and 46.4% operating margins versus a peer median of 18.7% operating, with gross margin up **+750bps since FY2021** (74.8% → 82.2%) and operating margin up **+805bps**. Growth outruns the peer median by ~1.8x, and the 25.4x trailing P/E is now *below* the 28.7x peer median despite superior quality — the market is discounting permanent mortgage-score share loss, not just cyclicality.

**Rationale for "eroding":** The entire margin premium was a regulatory rent on the mortgage score, and that rent is now being competitively priced away (99¢ for three years). The first evidence will be Scores revenue growth decelerating from +41% toward the high single digits as volumes migrate, likely visible in FY27. Meanwhile, FICO funds defence with 82.7% more debt and a 15% headcount cut — the classic pattern of a franchise under attack. Outside mortgage, FICO remains a top-tier franchise; inside it, FICO is a former monopolist in a price war.

## 6. Sector Rating: **Neutral**

The credit-data and decision-analytics sector retains genuinely attractive structural economics — recurring revenue, 56–82% gross margins, regulatory barriers, and a long runway to sell decisioning software and AI governance. But the sub-sector is in a **late-mature-to-disrupted phase**: the scoring layer is transitioning from monopoly to duopoly, the demand pool (mortgage units) is contracting ~5% in 2027, and the pricing umbrella that supported 46% operating margins is collapsing by an order of magnitude. **Selectivity matters more than sector beta:** the bureaus (TRU, EFX) are the structural *beneficiaries* of score competition because they own the file and capture the VantageScore volume; FICO is the incumbent being repriced. FICO-specific risk is elevated and event-driven, not cyclical — the next catalysts are lender adoption disclosures, FICO 10T GSE eligibility, and FY27 guidance.
