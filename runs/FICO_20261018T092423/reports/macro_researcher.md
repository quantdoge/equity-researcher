# FICO — Macroeconomic Environment & Impact Assessment
**As of:** October 18, 2026 | **Price:** $771.19 (Oct 16 close) | **Market cap:** $16.66B | **Beta:** 1.34

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: TRANSITIONAL — a bear-steepening, sticky-inflation regime that is specifically hostile to long-duration, consumer-credit-volume assets.**

The US is in a "full-employment, above-target inflation, rising long-rate" configuration. Real GDP grew 2.5% (Q1'26) and 2.2% (Q2'26) — no recession. Unemployment is 4.2% (Sep'26), *down* from 4.5% in Nov'25. But CPI has re-accelerated to **3.72% YoY in September** (+0.65% MoM, ~8% annualised), up from a 3.30% trough in July. That has closed the easing cycle: the Fed Funds rate bottomed at 3.63% (Jan–Aug 2026) and ticked **up** to 3.75% in September.

The critical signal is the curve. The **2Y at 4.65% sits ~90bp ABOVE the effective funds rate** — the market is pricing *hikes*, not cuts. The 10Y has risen from 4.39% (May 1) to 5.31% (Oct 5), now 4.99%, with the 30Y at 5.37% and 2s10s at +34bp. This is a fiscal/term-premium-driven **bear steepener**, the worst possible shape for equity multiples.

Simultaneously, credit markets are euphoric: **HY OAS at 3.23%** (near cycle tights, though +58bp off the 2.65% Sep 11 low), VIX ~15. So we have a K-shaped divergence — **capital markets risk-on, households miserable** (UMich sentiment 51.7 in Aug, versus 71.7 in Jan'25). For FICO, that divergence is the whole story: lenders are *willing* to lend (tight spreads, improving card delinquencies at 2.85%), but the consumer is frozen, and the mortgage channel is shut by 7.40% mortgages.

**Vital nuance for FICO:** the mortgage-Treasury spread is ~241bp (7.40% mortgage vs 4.99% 10Y) versus a ~170–180bp norm. MBS market capacity is impaired — **mortgage rates will lag any decline in Treasuries.** FICO's highest-value score vertical is therefore suppressed by both the level *and* the spread of rates.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

FICO has two engines: **Scores** (per-transaction royalties — volume-levered to consumer credit originations) and **Software** (subscription/platform — more resilient). Roughly 35–40% of revenue is international.

| Macro Variable | Impact | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **30Y mortgage at 7.40% (+142bp)** | **Strongly NEGATIVE** | Mortgage is FICO's highest price-per-score vertical (tri-merge pulls on every application). A 142bp move kills refi entirely and guts purchase volume. This is the single most important macro variable for FICO. |
| **10Y at 5.0–5.3% / bear steepener** | **NEGATIVE** | Two channels: (i) discount-rate compression on a long-duration, terminal-value-heavy equity; (ii) suppresses mortgage volume. FICO's ~60% YTD decline is *partly* re-rating, not purely the FHFA shock. |
| **Market pricing Fed hikes (2Y 90bp over FF)** | **NEGATIVE** | Removes the "rates fall → refi wave → score volume surge" catalyst that normally makes FICO convex to easing. |
| **CPI 3.72%, sticky** | **Mildly negative / neutral** | Low direct cost exposure (software, 3,876 employees, ~80%+ gross margins). Inflation *does* raise nominal loan balances (score volume is somewhat nominal-GDP-linked). But it keeps the Fed from cutting. The 15% workforce cut (Oct 6) is a genuine cost offset. |
| **HY OAS 3.23% / card delinquencies improving (2.85%)** | **POSITIVE** | Lenders are open for business in cards, auto, personal loans — the non-mortgage score verticals should keep growing. Normalising delinquency = no credit tightening cycle. |
| **Unemployment 4.2%, GDP 2.2%** | **NEUTRAL / mildly positive** | Origination volumes track employment and income. No recession = no volume collapse. |
| **UMich sentiment 51.7** | **NEGATIVE** | Consumer demand for new credit is weak even where supply exists; also pressures myFICO B2C subscription conversions. |
| **Broad USD +3% since May** | **MILDLY NEGATIVE** | Translation headwind on ~35–40% international revenue (EMEA/APAC/LatAm). Modest, not thesis-changing. |
| **Tariffs / trade policy** | **NEUTRAL** | Software/analytics — no physical goods, no direct tariff exposure. Indirect via lender IT budgets. |

**Decomposition — macro vs. idiosyncratic (important for honesty):** FICO's ~60% YTD drawdown is *primarily regulatory*, not macro. The Dec 31, 2025 close was $1,690.62; it is now $771.19. The decisive events were the April 22 FHFA/Fannie/Freddie acceptance of VantageScore 4.0 and FICO 10T, then late September's FHFA directive opening mortgage underwriting to a direct competitor — with VantageScore 4.0 reportedly priced at **$0.99 per mortgage origination score**, establishing a hard price ceiling against FICO's mortgage royalty. The stock gapped from $840.89 (Sep 28) to $617.87 (Sep 29), −26.5% on 4.9M shares. It has since rebounded +30% off the $592 low to $771, aided by UWM's public reaffirmation of FICO scores and the restructuring announcement.

**The macro overlay is best characterised as an amplifier, not the trigger:** the pricing umbrella was removed at precisely the moment the addressable mortgage pool was shrinking. That is the worst possible sequencing. Multiple compression now sits on a much smaller earnings base.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Four reinforcing negatives outweigh two genuine positives:

**Negatives:** (1) 7.40% mortgage rates destroying the highest-value score vertical; (2) a 5%+ long bond compressing a duration-heavy multiple; (3) sticky 3.7% inflation with the market pricing *hikes*, removing the easing catalyst FICO is normally most levered to; (4) a strengthening dollar on ~35–40% international mix.

**Positives:** tight credit spreads and improving delinquencies mean non-mortgage score volumes (cards, auto, personal) are healthy; 2.2% GDP and 4.2% unemployment preclude a volume collapse.

At $16.66B, the shares have de-rated by roughly two-thirds from 2025 peak multiples even on a still-growing EPS base — so much of the macro discount-rate damage is already in the price. The residual macro risk is *not* valuation; it is that **the 2027 origination recovery keeps getting pushed out** by the mortgage-UST spread.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (BULL)

**A growth scare in H1 2027 that cracks this bear steepener.** My base case is that inflation stays ~3–4% and the 10Y grinds at 5%+, keeping mortgages near 7% and FICO's mortgage vertical moribund. The genuinely surprising outcome is the opposite: if Q4'26/Q1'27 GDP prints near zero (note 2025 showed two sub-0.3% quarters — this economy *can* stall) and term premium compresses as fiscal supply concerns ease, the 10Y could fall from 5.1% toward 4.0% and mortgages from 7.40% toward 6.0%.

FICO's per-score economics on mortgage pulls are so high that this is a violent operating lever: a refi wave could lift mortgage score volume 40%+, and — critically — a competitor priced at $0.99 wins on *price*, not on *throughput capacity*, so volume recovery accrues disproportionately to FICO in the short run. A stock already down 60%, with a 15% cost base removed and sentiment at 51.7, would re-rate hard on that combination. **The convexity here is to rates falling, and almost nobody is positioned for it because the market is pricing hikes.**

**The symmetric bear risk:** the 10Y breaks 5.50% on inflation persistence, the Fed hikes, mortgage rates go to 7.75%, origination volumes fall further — while FHFA extends VantageScore parity into more channels. That combination takes the multiple toward mid-teens. I weight the bull scenario slightly higher than market pricing implies, but it is a **timing call on the long bond, not a call on FICO's franchise** — and that franchise is now demonstrably no longer a regulated monopoly in mortgage.
