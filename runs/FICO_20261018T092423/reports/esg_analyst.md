# FICO (Fair Isaac Corporation) — ESG Analysis

**Company:** Fair Isaac Corporation | Bozeman, MT | Technology / Software–Application | ~3,811–3,876 FTE | Market cap ~$16.7B | Price $771.19 | Beta 1.34 | CEO William J. Lansing | Chairman Braden R. Kelly (independent)
**Business:** Two segments — **Scores** (B2B FICO Score royalties via the three bureaus; B2C myFICO subscriptions) and **Software** (FICO Platform, decisioning, fraud/financial-crime). The ESG thesis for FICO is *not* an environmental story. It is a **market-conduct / licence-to-operate story that has already converted into a financial event.**

---

## 1. ESG Materiality Map

| Factor | Pillar | Materiality | Why it hits cash flows / cost of capital |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Market conduct & pricing power** | S | **Critical** | Near-monopoly product used by ~90% of lenders. Price increases invite antitrust, Senate/FTC action, and customer substitution. Already crystallised. |
| **Product access / financial inclusion** | S | High | FICO score gates mortgage access; pricing flows to borrowers → political salience, reputational exposure. |
| **Human capital (2026 restructuring)** | S | High | −15% of positions, ~570 roles, ~$27M pre-tax charge; execution + retention risk during a price war. |
| **Data privacy & AI model governance** | S / G | High | Credit scoring is **Annex III high-risk AI** under the EU AI Act; explainability obligations. |
| **Board independence & comp alignment** | G | Medium | Split Chair/CEO, majority-independent board — a genuine strength. |
| **Regulatory competition policy (GSE rules)** | G/Econ | Critical | FHFA parity for VantageScore 4.0 reset the moat in Sept 2026. |
| **Carbon / climate** | E | **Low** | Asset-light software; Scope 1&2 immaterial; Scope 3 = data centres/cloud only. |
| **Physical climate risk** | E | Low | No operations materially exposed; no water/heat-intensive assets. |

**Read-through:** ~85% of FICO's financially material ESG risk sits in **Social (market conduct + human capital)**. Environmental is a rounding error. This is the opposite of a typical "asset-heavy" ESG assessment and is why generic ESG scores (which weight E heavily) will mislead on FICO.

---

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- Asset-light analytics model: two segments, ~3,800 employees, cloud-hosted platform. Scope 1 (offices) and Scope 2 (purchased power) are de minimis relative to ~$2B+ revenue. Carbon intensity per revenue dollar is among the lowest in the Technology sector.
- **No CBAM exposure**, no carbon-tax-exposed processes, no stranded-asset risk, no remediation liabilities identified.
- **Genuine gap:** FICO's corporate responsibility disclosures are behind a restricted portal (incapsula-blocked) and I could not retrieve third-party-verified Scope 1/2/3 figures, a TCFD index, or CDP submission. No science-based target was confirmed. This is a **disclosure weakness, not a risk** — but it means FICO screens poorly on ESG *scoring* frameworks that penalise non-disclosure, creating a false negative.
- Data-centre/cloud Scope 3 is the only future E vector, and it is a supplier-selection lever, not a cost-material risk.

---

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

Three live issues, in order of financial materiality:

**(a) Market conduct / pricing — the dominant ESG risk.** FICO raised its per-score wholesale mortgage royalty from **$0.60 to $10.00 over five years**, and doubled it from **$4.95 to $10.00 for 2026**. Senator Josh Hawley (Chair, Senate Judiciary Subcommittee on Crime; member, Antitrust Subcommittee) opened an investigation in **March 2026** and separately urged the **FTC to investigate**, estimating the increase could cost the mortgage industry **~$500M**. The complaint is explicitly about *how* the franchise was earned ("cemented by a regulatory framework that, for nearly three decades, required… exclusive use of FICO scores") — i.e. a **social-licence critique, not a competition critique**.

**(b) The licence was revoked by policy, not by a competitor.** In **July 2025** FHFA permitted VantageScore 4.0 as an alternative; by **September 2026** Fannie/Freddie placed VantageScore 4.0 on the **same loan-level pricing adjustment grid as Classic FICO**, and Equifax/TransUnion began pricing VS 4.0 at **$0.99** (complimentary in some channels). FICO cut its own mortgage score price to **$0.99**. The stock fell **~26.5–27% in a single session** (30 Sept 2026) and FICO was the **worst performer in the S&P 500 for Q3 2026**. This is the textbook ESG transmission mechanism: a stakeholder/political grievance became a repricing of the Scores segment margin (~90%+ historically).

**(c) Human capital.** On **1–6 October 2026** FICO approved elimination of **~15% of positions (~570 roles)**, citing reduced organisational layers and AI-driven product development; affected employees notified week of 5 Oct 2026; completion targeted by **end of Q3 FY2027**; **~$27M pre-tax Q4 FY2026 charge**, substantially all cash. Cutting 15% of the workforce *while* defending a price war and *while* marketing itself as the authority on "Responsible AI" (its own Oct 2026 survey found only 5.2% of tech leaders can fully explain AI decisions) is a **cultural and execution risk**, and raises attrition risk among the engineers who built the Scores IP.

**Mitigants:** CFPB's **April 2026 rewrite of Regulation B abandoned disparate-impact ("effects test") enforcement** — a material reduction in US fair-lending litigation risk for algorithmic scoring. **Offsetting risk:** **Illinois enacted a state disparate-impact standard for credit decisions (Aug 2026)**, creating a state-by-state patchwork.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.5 / 10**

**Positives (+):**
- **Chair/CEO separation** — Braden R. Kelly serves as **Independent Chairman**; William Lansing is **CEO and a director**. No duality.
- Board includes clearly identified independent directors (Kelly, Joanna Rees, Fabiola Arredondo, H. Stansbury) — consistent with a majority-independent, largely non-executive board.
- **Single class of common stock** — no dual-class super-voting structure, no evidence of a poison pill. One share, one vote.
- Long-tenured chairman (13+ yrs) provides continuity; director pay (~$360–520k) is conventional.

**Negatives (−):**
- **The central governance failure is strategic, not structural.** Monetising a government-conferred near-monopoly to $10/score — a 16x increase in five years — without securing political cover was a **foreseeable stakeholder-management failure** that destroyed roughly a quarter of market value in one day and reset the segment's terminal economics. Good boards stress-test regulatory and political durability of pricing; this one did not visibly do so before FHFA forced the issue.
- **Sequencing of capital/people decisions:** a 15% RIF announced days after the pricing collapse reads as reactive cost-cutting rather than a pre-planned operating model.
- **Disclosure posture:** restricted ESG portal, no accessible proxy/10-K via standard retrieval, no verified emissions data. Investor-relations accessibility is a modest governance negative.

**Scored 6.5 rather than 8+ because structural governance is good but *risk governance* — the part that actually protects long-term value — failed on the single largest value driver.**

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (with a flagged **Social / licence-to-operate** weakness)

- **E: Leader-by-default** (low impact, but weak disclosure — not verified).
- **S: Laggard** on market conduct; **Average** on human capital; **Above-average** on AI-explainability thought leadership (FICO publishes responsible-AI research and is well positioned on AI Act documentation).
- **G: Above-average structurally** (split Chair/CEO, single share class, independent majority), **below-average on risk governance**.

The composite lands at **Average**, but the *dispersion* is what matters: FICO is a company whose ESG profile looks benign on every dimension except the one that actually drove a ~27% drawdown.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE for the thesis**

**Reasoning:**
1. **The material ESG risk has already crystallised — and it is now a margin story.** FICO's ESG exposure is not a contingent liability; it is a realised reset of the Scores-segment mortgage royalty from $4.95–$10.00 to $0.99. The terminal-value question for FICO is now "what is a politically durable price for a government-legitimised score?" — and the answer just moved down.
2. **The antitrust/political overhang is not closed.** A live Senate Judiciary investigation plus an FTC referral means headline risk persists into FY27, with tail risk of rulemaking that further standardises score-model choice (FHA/GSE adoption of VantageScore, or mandated multi-model disclosure).
3. **Management credibility is impaired at the margin.** Raising price 16x in five years, then cutting 15% of staff within a week of the repricing, suggests the board did not model the political response. That is a governance discount, not a governance premium.
4. **Genuine offsets — do not over-penalise.** (a) The Software segment (fraud, financial-crime compliance, decisioning) carries no such political baggage and benefits from AI-regulation-driven demand for explainable, auditable decisioning; (b) EU AI Act high-risk obligations create a compliance moat favouring established vendors; (c) the CFPB's retreat from disparate-impact enforcement lowers US litigation risk; (d) environmental risk is genuinely negligible, so ESG-driven exclusion pressure is low.

**Net:** For an ESG-integrated long-only mandate, FICO should be treated as **carnage in the S pillar with a real, if smaller, franchise underneath** — an **Average-rated name with a Negative ESG trajectory**, not an ESG-risk exclusion. The ESG signal was, unusually, *early* and *loud*: the Hawley letter (March 2026) preceded the FHFA parity ruling (Sept 2026) by six months. That is the lesson to record in the memo — **for regulated-monopoly business models, political/antitrust controversy belongs in the valuation model, not the appendix.**
