### 2. Key Red Flags Found

1. **The moat was just broken by the moat's landlord.** On 29-Sep-2026 FHFA Director Bill Pulte announced Fannie and Freddie would treat **VantageScore on equal footing with FICO** in mortgage pricing. The stock fell 26.5% ($840.89 → $617.87) on 4.9M shares — worst single day since 1989. FICO's US mortgage scoring franchise was a regulator-granted toll booth; the regulator revoked the exclusivity.
2. **Rent extraction invited the expropriation.** Mortgage score pricing was reportedly raised **~1,800% since 2020**. That is the single most politically attackable profit pool in US consumer finance during a housing-affordability cycle. Near-monopoly pricing into a populist policy environment is a classic failure mode — exactly the "too-profitable toll booth" pattern.
3. **Share loss is already visible, not hypothetical.** Rocket Mortgage (the largest originator) named **VantageScore 4.0 its preferred** model; TransUnion reported VS 4.0 in **~30% of its Q2 mortgage credit pulls**. This is adoption, not a press release.
4. **Management conceded the pricing model within 48 hours.** The 1-Oct-2026 "FICO Mortgage Direct License Program" — letting bureaus distribute scores directly, bypassing the royalty structure — is a unilateral price/terms concession dressed as a product launch.
5. **Leverage and negative equity, built deliberately into the shock.** Total debt went **$2.24B (Sep-24) → $3.07B (Sep-25) → $5.60B (Jun-26)**; shareholder equity is **−$4.10B**; net debt **$5.35B**; cash only $248M; current ratio 1.18. Interest expense went from $32.9M to **$59.9M per quarter** (+82% YoY), i.e. a ~$240M annual run-rate against ~$1.4B of EBITDA.
6. **Peak-cycle earnings base.** FY2025 EPS +30% on revenue +15.9% — margin expansion from price, plus a shrinking share count (26.0M → 21.6M since FY2022). Consensus FY2027 EPS of $49.14 and $1.46B EBITDA assumes essentially no VantageScore impact.
7. **FY2026 revenue acceleration (+16% → +39% → +26% YoY) is the bullish-looking data point that is actually the bearish one** — it reflects aggressive score repricing, which is what triggered the regulatory response.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment — **SEVERE, and structural rather than cyclical**

- Threat is regulatory-backstopped: the GSEs define what score can be used in conforming mortgages. Once FHFA permits parity, FICO's pricing is capped by a competitor (VantageScore, owned by the three bureaus — who are also FICO's distribution channel and now its rival).
- The bureaus have the distribution, the data and the incentive. FICO has the incumbent model and lender integration (10T), which buys time measured in quarters, not decades.
- Jefferies' estimate of **~20% adjusted EBITDA impact** if VantageScore gains real traction is the right order of magnitude: if mortgage score revenue is ~$400-500M at ~90% incremental margin, a 30-40% share/price loss removes roughly $150-200M of EBITDA (~11-14% of total) — before knock-on pricing pressure on the much larger card/auto score book.
- Counterweight (steelman): consumer-lending scores for cards/auto/personal loans remain FICO-entrenched, VantageScore has minimal share there, and GSE implementation of a new pricing grid is slow and reversible by politics. Software/FICO Platform ARR is growing double-digit and is unaffected.

### 5. Management Red Flags

- **Capital allocation is the standout.** FICO bought back $1.41B (FY25) and roughly $2B+ in FY2026 — including a large June-2026 quarter — funded with debt, at prices between ~$995 and ~$1,800, weeks before a 47% collapse. Value destroyed for the benefit of an EPS line while equity went to −$4.1B. Retained earnings of $5.2B now sit behind $5.6B of debt.
- **Framing after the fact:** a 15% workforce cut (~570 jobs, ~$27M pretax charge) announced 6-Oct-2026 is described as "AI-driven restructuring." Read plainly: cost-cutting in response to a revenue shock. Promotional language outrunning delivery is a pattern.
- **No insider buying during the collapse.** Form 4 cadence is heavy and clustered (9 filings 11-Dec-2025; 7 on 6-Mar-2026; last filing 25-Aug-2026) — consistent with programmatic exercise-and-sell. Notably, the filing record shows **no Form 4 in September–October 2026** while the stock made three-year lows. I could not verify transaction direction from the available feed, so I flag this as an inference, not a proven net-sell number.
- No auditor changes, no related-party issues, no restatements found. Governance is conventional; the offence is aggressive financial engineering, not fraud.

### 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, and it is a good one — but it has already been monetized.** The bear case is:

> FICO's mortgage score is a regulated monopoly toll booth whose toll was raised ~1,800% since 2020. The landlord (FHFA) has now invited a subsidised competitor with superior distribution (the bureaus themselves) and a marquee adopter (Rocket). Peak-priced earnings, an $5.35B net-debt load, negative equity, and a share count propped up by debt-funded buybacks leave no balance-sheet cushion for an EBITDA reset. Consensus FY27 ($49.14 EPS) embeds none of it.

That is a Strong Short **at $1,800–2,000**. At $771 it is a different trade.

**The other side (and why I will not pound the table short here):**
- The stock has already fallen 68%; the Sept-29 capitulation ($592 intraday low) is roughly a 12x multiple on consensus FY27 EPS.
- The forensic quality is genuinely excellent: 82-87% gross margin, 1.2x cash conversion, negative accruals, 46% operating margin pre-shock. Franchises like this rarely trade at trough multiples for long.
- The *non-mortgage* Scores business and Software segment are intact; FHFA grid details are unknown and implementation is likely 2027+ and politically reversible.
- On EV/EBITDA (~$22.9B EV vs ~$1.4B FY26E EBITDA ≈ 16x) it is **not** statistically cheap — but on P/E (~15.7x FY27 consensus) it screens cheap enough to attract value buyers and a squeeze.
- Consensus PT ~$1,204 and "Moderate Buy" ratings are stale and will be marked down, but BofA already went Neutral with a $700 target — the downgrade cycle is underway, not ahead.
