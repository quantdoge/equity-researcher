### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate** (with two caveats)

**Evidence supporting the rating:**
- **Chair/CEO separation:** Braden R. Kelly serves as independent Chairman (former partner, Health Evolution Partners); Lansing is CEO and a director. Power is not formally concentrated in one seat — a genuine positive.
- **Small, presumably independent-majority board:** 8 directors, elected annually (2026 proxy proposes eight one-year terms). No classified board.
- **Pay structure appears shareholder-aligned:** Lansing total comp ≈$36.05M, of which **~97.9% is non-salary** (equity/performance-based) — high pay-at-risk, consistent with a founder-like operating mandate.
- **No accounting red flags:** Q3 FY26 10-Q certifications (Lansing, July 29, 2026) are clean and unqualified on disclosure controls and ICFR; no restatement, no material weakness disclosed, no auditor change identified.
- **Strong reported operating results through the crisis:** Q3 FY26 revenue +25.7% y/y, EPS +41.2%; FY26 guidance ~$2.53B revenue / ~$42.43 adj. EPS; TTM FCF ≈$961M. This is not a business in financial distress — it is a business whose *moat* was repriced.

**Caveats pulling the rating down from "Strong":**
- **Key-person dependency on Lansing** — CEO since January 2012 (~14.7 years). He is the public face of the pricing strategy that regulators and legislators have now targeted. No disclosed succession plan.
- **No insider open-market buying into a 60–65% drawdown** (see §3). Boards that believe their own regulatory-risk narrative is intact typically see at least one director buy. None did.
- **Disclosure-timing exposure** — multiple firms are investigating whether FICO misstated the regulatory risk to its mortgage scoring business. That is a governance *and* control question, not just a legal one.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — **This is the dominant risk and it has already crystallized**

| Matter | Status | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| **FHFA mortgage credit-score repricing (Sept 28–30, 2026)** | FHFA Director Bill Pulte announced Fannie/Freddie would simplify mortgage pricing and align **Classic FICO and VantageScore 4.0 under the same credit-fee framework**; Freddie Mac confirmed fee alignment Sept 30. FHFA has also published FICO 10T and VantageScore 4.0 historical data (FICO 10T on July 1, 2026). | **Critical** |
| **DOJ Antitrust Division civil investigation** | Opened into "potential exclusionary conduct"; FICO publicly states confidence it will be cleared. Still an *investigation*, not a filed complaint as of my data. | **High** |
| **Florida AG civil subpoena** (AG James Uthmeier, July 2026) | Alleged anticompetitive practices harming Florida consumers. State-level discovery = document-production burden and political template for other states. | **Moderate** |
| **U.S. Senate investigation** (Sen. Josh Hawley, R-MO, launched Mar 23, 2026) | Formal inquiry into FICO score pricing; Hawley publicly urged the **FTC** to investigate as well. Congressional pressure sustains the DOJ/FTC pipeline. | **Moderate–High** |
| **Private antitrust litigation** | Long-running *In re FICO Antitrust Litig.* (1:20-cv-02114); credit-bureau claims dismissed, remaining claims partially survived. | **Moderate** |
| **Securities litigation risk** | Levi & Korsinsky, Rosen, SueWallSt, Bronstein Gewirtz all publicly soliciting FICO shareholders over the **~20% overnight/premarket drop** on the FHFA announcement, focused on what FICO disclosed about regulatory risk. I could not confirm a filed complaint — treat as *investigation-stage* with high conversion probability. | **High** |

**The economic substance of the regulatory hit:** FICO's mortgage score was effectively a regulatory *mandate*, not merely a product. Pricing parity plus single-score-per-loan acceptance removes both (a) the mandatory-usage moat and (b) part of the per-loan pull volume (tri-merge → single score). Rocket Mortgage has adopted VantageScore 4.0 as its default, and VantageScore claims all top-30 U.S. mortgage lenders now use it. **UWM publicly reaffirmed FICO commitment (Oct 6, 2026)** — evidence the moat is contested, not gone. The regulatory risk is bidirectional from here (Pulte could tighten or soften), which raises volatility without resolving the question.

**Insider-adjacent market evidence:** The stock fell **-26.5% on Sept 29, 2026 on 4.9M shares (≈16x normal volume)** — the worst single day since 1989 — and is still ~54% below its Dec 31, 2025 close of $1,690.62. A separate **-17% drop on July 30, 2026** followed FY26 guidance despite guidance *rising* to $2.53B — the market is now pricing the durability of the Scores segment, not near-term earnings.

### 3. Insider Activity Analysis — **Net selling; zero insider buying** (12 months)

**Form 4 clustering (from SEC submission metadata):**
- **Dec 11, 2025** — 8 separate Form 4s in a single day (a classic coordinated 10b5-1/option-exercise-and-sell cluster, likely tied to a vesting or plan window), plus Dec 5, 8, 16, 19 filings.
- **Mar 6, 2026** — 7 Form 4s in one day, again a cluster consistent with annual equity vesting plus same-day sales.
- **Feb 18–27, 2026**, **May 19–27, 2026**, **Jul 7, 2026** (2), **Jul 31, 2026**, **Aug 25, 2026** (2) — steady cadence of routine filings.
- **Aug 22–24, 2026** — 6 conversion transactions by 2 directors, 4 of them reported at $0.00 (awards/conversions, not open-market activity).

**Directional evidence:** EVP **Mark R. Scadina** — 6 transactions over five years, **0 buys, 6 sells**, ~34,812 shares for ≈$39.4M, including a ~$6.16M sale. This is the disclosed pattern for the executive team: **monetization without accumulation.**

**Assessment:**
- **Net selling, decisively.** No director or officer open-market purchase appears anywhere in the trailing-twelve-month Form 4 record — including through a 65% peak-to-trough collapse. That is the single most telling insider datapoint in this file: management has been *publicly confident* ("we support FHFA's announcement") while *privately* selling into strength and declining to buy the dip.
- **Mitigant:** the selling is programmatic, spread across many insiders on scheduled dates, and pre-dates the September shock. This looks like **routine 10b5-1 diversification / compensation monetization, not informed dumping** ahead of the FHFA decision. I found no evidence of unusual selling *immediately before* the Sept 28 announcement — the last Form 4 before it is Aug 25, 2026. If a cluster had appeared in mid-September, that would be an escalation-grade red flag; it did not.
- **Caveat:** without per-transaction share counts and 10b5-1 adoption/termination dates, I cannot fully exclude a plan modification ahead of the news. Recommend pulling the raw Form 4s on the Oct 6 8-K / Sept 29 window before clearing this.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Simultaneous moat erosion and 15% headcount reduction (announced Oct 6, 2026).** FICO is cutting ~580 of 3,876 employees and taking ~$27M pretax severance in Q4 FY26, framed as "AI-driven restructuring" and "simplifying operating structure." **Cutting 15% of a 3,876-person organization while your core product is under competitive attack is the highest-risk form of restructuring**: it removes institutional knowledge in exactly the sales, regulatory-affairs, and mortgage-relationship functions needed to defend share. Execution risk is elevated and the market's reaction was negative ("Why Investors Aren't Buying It" — Barron's).
- **Revenue concentration in the Scores segment / mortgage channel.** Scores (B2B scoring + myFICO) carries disproportionate margin versus the Software segment. The regulatory change attacks the *price* and the *volume* (single-score vs. tri-merge) of mortgage scoring simultaneously. Software's offset — FICO Platform ARR reportedly +62% — is real but smaller and lower-margin; the transition window is when the company is most fragile.
- **Key-person and key-relationship dependency.** A 14.7-year CEO is executing a contested strategy through a hostile regulatory environment with a reduced team. Separately, FICO's economics depend on contractual arrangements with the three national credit bureaus (Experian, Equifax, TransUnion — the latter now reportedly engaged in aggressive mortgage-score pricing) and on a handful of large lender relationships. The UWM reaffirmation shows these relationships are now **individually negotiated and individually announceable** — i.e., winner/loser, not structural.
- *(Secondary)* **Cybersecurity / data-handling:** FICO processes credit and consumer data at scale for 90% of top U.S. lenders and operates myFICO B2C. No incident identified in this review, but the breach consequence is asymmetric given regulatory scrutiny already in place.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **High and rising**

FICO's reputational position has deteriorated from "expensive but essential utility" to **"monopoly rent-extractor"** in the political narrative:
- **Congressional framing is explicitly adversarial.** Senator Hawley's stated premise: "FICO dominates the credit scoring market" — an allegation, published by a sitting U.S. Senator, that FICO overcharges consumers. That framing is now in the public record and is being amplified by the FTC referral.
- **State AG action** (Florida) converts a federal narrative into consumer-protection branding for elected officials — a template likely to be copied by other states ahead of 2027.
- **Mortgage-industry and consumer backlash** is structural: VantageScore markets itself on "could reduce mortgage costs by nearly $1 billion." FICO's brand is now the *cost* in that comparison.
- **Shareholder-relations damage:** four plaintiff firms are competing for FICO shareholders. Securities-investigation solicitations generate their own negative press cycle and invite institutional investors to reassess.
- **Counterweight:** FICO retains genuine reputational assets — the "State of Responsible AI" and fraud-research franchises, the Decision Awards, and a 30-year analytic credibility with lenders. Nothing suggests brand damage in the *software/analytics* business; the damage is concentrated in Scores and is political, not product-quality, in origin.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH** (trending toward **Critical**)

**Rationale:** The regulatory risk here is not prospective — it is **realized and structural**. A single FHFA policy decision removed the regulatory mandate underpinning FICO's most profitable franchise, and it took ~$1,000/share of market value in one session. That is layered on top of a live DOJ antitrust investigation, a state AG subpoena, a Senate inquiry, a four-firm securities-investigation cluster, a 15% workforce reduction, and a complete absence of insider buying. Offsetting strengths — separated Chair/CEO, clean ICFR certifications, 97.9% performance-based CEO pay, ~$961M TTM FCF, and a credible software growth engine — keep this from **Critical**. It would become Critical on any of: a filed DOJ complaint, a formal securities class action with a defined class period, an SEC formal order of investigation, or a second major lender defection.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **⚠️ HIGHEST — Disclosure-timing / securities-fraud exposure.** Four firms investigating whether FICO **misrepresented the regulatory risk** to its mortgage scoring business before the FHFA announcement, with the stock down ~20% premarket. If a class action is filed, expect the class period to reach back into 2025–2026 statements where FICO characterized its score position as durable. **This is a potential fraud indicator, not merely litigation risk, and should be escalated to the GC of the investment process.** *Caveat: no filed complaint confirmed in my data.*
2. **⚠️ Zero insider buying through a 65% drawdown** — including zero by the Chairman or any independent director. Management's public confidence and its private capital allocation are inconsistent. Pull the raw Form 4s for Aug–Oct 2026 to confirm no 10b5-1 plan adoptions/terminations.
3. **⚠️ Layoffs and moat defense occurring simultaneously.** A 15% cut announced four business days after the regulatory shock, framed as "AI-driven," reads as **cost reaction dressed as strategy** — and it degrades the exact functions needed to defend share. Escalate the Q4 FY26 charge and whether further restructuring is coming.
4. **⚠️ Multi-front regulatory convergence** — DOJ (federal antitrust) + Florida AG (state) + Senate Hawley/FTC referral + private MDL + securities plaintiffs, all on the same underlying conduct narrative. Coordination among these is the classic precursor to a material adverse settlement. FICO's EDGAR record shows **no SEC enforcement action** to date — that is the one piece of genuinely good news and should be monitored for change.
5. **Monitor:** Whether the FHFA framework is finalized and irreversible (statutory/rulemaking vs. Director discretion — Director Pulte's social-media-driven announcements suggest discretion, meaning the risk could also *reverse* favorably). And whether UWM's reaffirmation is followed by other large-lender commitments or by further defections.

---

**Bottom line for the memo:** FICO is not financially impaired — it generated ~$961M TTM FCF and grew revenue 25.7% in its last reported quarter. What has changed is that its most valuable asset was a *regulatory franchise* that the government has now revoked, and the non-financial risk stack (antitrust, securities, reputational, restructuring execution) all points the same direction with no insider conviction offsetting it. **Treat FICO as a high non-financial risk position where the investment case must now rest entirely on the Software segment's ARR trajectory and the durability of non-mortgage Scores revenue — not on the mortgage monopoly.**
