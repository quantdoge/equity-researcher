# Adobe Inc. (ADBE) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**As of:** October 17, 2026 | **Price:** $245.20 | **Market cap:** ~$95.4B | **Sector:** Technology / Software–Application | **Employees:** 31,360

**Context:** The stock is down ~32% YTD 2026 and ~29% from $345 in Sept 2025, with a 52-week range of roughly $190–$370. The decline reflects a re-rating of the software sector after generative-AI launches (Claude-class models), plus company-specific governance and leadership shocks. Non-financial risk is therefore *not* a side issue here — it is the primary driver of the de-rating.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

| Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **2026 say-on-pay passed with only 50.65% support** (down from 84.86% in 2024) per the Item 5.07 8-K tally | Near-failure. Historically a sub-60% result signals a board that has lost the confidence of a large block of holders on pay. This is the single most important governance datapoint in the file. |
| **CEO/Chair concentration** — Shantanu Narayen served as Chair *and* CEO; effective Dec 1, 2026 he becomes **Executive Chair and remains Chair of the Board** while Anil Chakravarthy becomes CEO | An incoming CEO reporting to a board chaired by the 18-year predecessor is a classic shadow-authority structure. Mitigant: a genuine Lead Independent Director (Frank Calderoni) exists and is empowered. |
| **Succession handled properly but slowly** — Narayen announced transition Mar 12, 2026; special committee (chaired by Calderoni, considering internal and external candidates) named an internal successor Sept 3, 2026; effective Dec 1, 2026 | ~9-month interregnum, board process was orderly and unanimous, no activist pressure evident. Credit due. |
| **Simultaneous CFO vacated** — Dan Durn resigned effective June 15, 2026 to become CFO of Marvell; Steve Day (SVP Corporate Finance) is **interim CFO**. As of this writing no permanent CFO has been disclosed | Adobe operated for months with a lame-duck CEO *and* an interim CFO. That is a control-environment gap, not merely an optics issue. |
| **Retention RSUs + severance protections** granted to executives during the CEO search (10-Q Ex-10.1), sun-setting 12 months after a permanent CEO starts | Rational retention tool, but it is additional "pay for staying" immediately after a 50.65% say-on-pay vote — poor optics and a dilution cost. |
| 11 directors reelected 2026; single class of common stock (no dual-class super-voting) | Standard-shareholder structure is a positive. |

**Verdict:** Process is defensible (independent lead director, special committee, single share class), but outcomes — a 50.65% pay vote, an Executive-Chair handoff, and a concurrent CFO vacancy — justify a **Weak** rating.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **FTC/DOJ "negative option" enforcement — RESOLVED VIA SETTLEMENT, WITH ONGOING OBLIGATIONS.** Filed June 2024 against Adobe and two named executives (Maninder Sawhney, David Wadhwani), alleging hidden early-termination fees and cancellation hurdles. Reuters/DOJ (Mar 13, 2026): **$150M total — $75M civil penalty plus $75M in free services, plus an injunction.** Key residual risks: (a) the injunction imposes conduct remedies on checkout/cancellation flows that must be audited, (b) named-executive liability normalizes individual accountability, and (c) the case is a template for state AG copycats and private consumer class actions.
- **AI copyright class actions — ACTIVE, EARLY STAGE.** Proposed class actions filed Dec 2025 and Feb 2026 allege Adobe trained generative models on roughly 200,000 pirated books. This is an existential-adjacent IP exposure for a company whose brand is "creator-safe" stock and Firefly.
- **Securities/derivative overlay — ACTIVE.** Investor suits against executives over prior statements that Adobe did not train AI on copyrighted material (Courthouse News). Separately, the Figma-competition 10b-5 class action's amended complaint was **dismissed**; an appeal is pending — a favorable but not final outcome.
- **SEC enforcement:** EDGAR full-text search returned **no SEC enforcement actions citing ADBE**. No FCPA/anti-corruption or whistleblower-retaliation matters surfaced. *Negative finding, material for a name of this size.*
- **Antitrust/M&A:** Semrush acquisition closed April 2026 (plus prior Workfront). No open antitrust challenge identified, but the combined SEO/AI-search-visibility data position merits monitoring.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Creative Cloud franchise cannibalization by generative AI.** Wells Fargo double-downgraded ADBE to Underweight on Oct 12, 2026 ($225 PT), citing a CIO survey showing Adobe **losing AI budget share**; a separate analyst cut followed. Seat-based growth is structurally exposed to AI-native creation tools. This is the risk that actually sets the share price.
2. **Two-seat C-suite vacuum / key-person and execution risk.** No permanent CFO and an incoming CEO who has never run the whole company, while the prior CEO stays on as Board Chair and Executive Chair. Institutional knowledge, capital-allocation discipline (including a ~$25B buyback) and M&A integration (Semrush, Workfront) all run through this gap. The retention letters confirm the board itself sees elevated flight risk.
3. **AI-era supplier and IP dependency.** Expanded NVIDIA partnership (Oct 15, 2026) improves performance but deepens dependence on a single compute/model partner and public cloud concentration; in parallel, training-data provenance is now a *legal* input to product design — Firefly's "commercially safe" positioning is only as good as undocumented training pipelines.

---

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Elevated**

- **"Dark patterns" branding is now permanent.** The FTC's framing — "trapped customers," hidden termination fees — attached a consumer-hostile label to a brand that sells to creators and small businesses. The $150M settlement keeps that story in the press cycle.
- **Creative-community trust is the crown jewel and it is under strain.** Allegations of training on pirated books cuts directly at Adobe's core constituency, which has already shown willingness to mobilize (the 2024 terms-of-service backlash). This is the highest-severity reputational vector; a adverse ruling or damaging discovery would amplify it.
- **Sell-side narrative has flipped** from "AI winner" to "AI casualty." "AI cannibalization," "losing budget share," and "no longer bullish" headlines are now the default framing.
- Partial offsets: record Q2 FY26 revenue ($6.62B, +13% YoY) with beats on revenue and EPS; customer-facing data PR (holiday e-commerce forecast) and AI-visibility index presence show the franchise is still monetizing.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** No fraud indicators, no SEC enforcement, no FCPA issues, and an orderly board-run succession process argue against *Critical*. But three factors compound: (i) governance legitimacy is impaired (50.65% say-on-pay) exactly while (ii) the two most important executive seats are in transition, and (iii) active AI copyright litigation plus an FTC injunction touches the core product and core customer relationship. These risks are correlated, not independent — leadership distraction raises the odds of execution missteps in the AI transition, which is the very thing the market is already punishing.

**Watch items that would move this to Critical or down to Moderate:** adverse ruling in the AI-training class actions (→Critical); a permanent CFO of high credibility plus a clean first year for Chakravarthy and a >70% say-on-pay in 2027 (→Moderate).

---

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Say-on-pay at 50.65%** — near-failure; the compensation committee's 2027 responsiveness filing must be reviewed closely. Escalate to governance/proxy-voting desk.
2. **CEO and CFO vacancies overlapping for a multi-quarter period** — elevate to a formal control-environment query: who owned financial reporting tone and M&A pipeline discipline through Q2–Q3 FY26? Request detail on the interim CFO's authority.
3. **Executive Chair structure with the departing CEO retaining the Board Chair** — confirm charter-level safeguards: independent-chair vote thresholds, executive-session practice, and whether Narayen recuses from CEO evaluation.
4. **AI training-data provenance litigation** — obtain the actual legal-proceedings footnote language (10-K FY25 / 10-Q Q3 FY26). Quantitative reserve disclosure and any DOJ/copyright-holder demand letters should be treated as material unknowns.
5. **Zero insider buying at multi-year lows, ~$45M of CEO sales in 2026** — flag as sentiment signal, not fraud evidence.
