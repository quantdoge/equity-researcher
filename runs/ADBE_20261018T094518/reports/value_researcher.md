# ADBE — Adobe Inc. | Value Investment Analysis

**Price ~$245** (last available tape $258.61 on 2/20/26; Oct-2026 analyst notes posted at $238–248) · Market cap ~$95–100B · FY2025 ended 11/28/25

*Data note: my price feed truncates at 2/20/26 while the earnings calendar and analyst targets extend into late FY2026. I use $245 as the working price and FY2026 run-rate estimates (Q1–Q3 actuals + Q4 guidance) alongside audited FY2025 financials. Adobe pays no dividend; peer cohort returned by the screener is semiconductor-heavy and not a valid multiple benchmark — I rely on Adobe's own history and absolute cash-flow math instead.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Narrow** (was Wide; now contested)

Adobe is three businesses with very different economics:

**Document Cloud (Acrobat/PDF) — Wide moat.** PDF is a de facto global standard; Acrobat sits inside corporate document workflows, e-signature, and compliance processes. This is a toll road. Switching costs are institutional, not just individual.

**Creative Cloud — Narrow and narrowing.** The 5-year evidence was excellent: 89% gross margins held across 2020–2025 (86.6% → 89.3%), pricing power exercised annually, and a subscriber base locked in by file formats, team libraries, plugin ecosystems, and a decade of trained-user habit. But the *direction* has changed. Total ending ARR growth guided to **10.2% for FY2026**, down from the mid-teens of FY2021–22, with management deliberately trading near-term ARR for AI monetization. Generative models (OpenAI, Google, Midjourney, Runway) and lower-cost design tools (Canva, Figma) attack the casual/prosumer tier — the seat-expansion engine. AI ARR has passed ~$500–650M, real but only ~2.5% of revenue and not yet proven to be incremental rather than cannibalizing credit-based upsell.

**Digital Experience — Weak.** Competing head-on with Salesforce, Microsoft, HubSpot in marketing automation. No durable pricing power. ~25% of revenue with structurally lower returns.

**Moat verdict:** the switching costs are real and the installed base is enormous, but the *sources* of the moat (per-seat pricing against headcount, professional tooling complexity) are precisely what AI compresses. A business whose returns on capital are this high but whose growth is decelerating into a technological shift gets **Narrow**, not Wide. This is the single most important judgment in the thesis, and it is why I do not call ADBE a "wonderful company at a fair price" without qualification.

**Evidence the moat is still functioning:** ROIC of **47.7%** in FY2025 (vs. ~32–33% in FY22–24) — rising, not falling; gross margin at a five-year high; revenue +12% in each of the last three reported quarters *while* the stock fell 31% YTD. Erosion shows up in the multiple, not yet in the P&L.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **9 / 10**

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 17.61 | 19.41 | 21.51 | **23.77** |
| YoY growth | 11.5% | 10.2% | 10.8% | **10.5%** |
| Gross margin | 87.7% | 87.9% | 89.0% | **89.3%** |
| Operating margin (GAAP) | 34.6% | 34.3% | 31.4% | **36.6%** |
| FCF ($B) | 7.40 | 6.94 | 7.87 | **9.85** |
| FCF / Net income | 155% | 128% | 142% | **138%** |
| ROIC | 32.6% | 32.3% | 33.4% | **47.7%** |
| Owner earnings ($B) | 5.17 | 5.94 | 6.23 | **7.77** |

- **FCF conversion of 138%** is exceptional even for software (capex is only 0.8% of revenue). Earnings are real cash.
- **Balance sheet:** $6.60B cash vs. $6.65B debt — net debt $1.22B, ~0.1x EBITDA. Effectively neutral; no solvency risk. Note net debt swung from −$3.1B (net cash) in FY23 to +$1.2B in FY25 purely from buybacks, not operations.
- **One real blemish: stock-based comp of $1.94B (8.2% of revenue), up from $1.07B in FY2021.** Owner earnings net of SBC are ~$5.8B, not $7.8B. Buybacks are 6x SBC, so net share count still shrinks — but SBC is a genuine transfer to employees, not a non-cash footnote.
- Goodwill + intangibles of $13.4B (56% of assets) is the residue of Marketo/Magento/Workfront/Frame.io. Digital Experience has not obviously earned its purchase price; I treat this as an option value of zero.

The one-point deduction: revenue growth is drifting to 10–12% with ARR decelerating, and SBC is high. Otherwise this is a top-decile financial profile.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**At $245:** trailing GAAP P/E **14.7x**; FY2026E GAAP P/E ~**12x**; FY2026E non-GAAP P/E ~**10x**; EV/EBITDA ~**8.5x** forward; trailing FCF yield **9.4%**, forward ~**11%**. Peer-median P/E of 43.7x is meaningless here (semiconductor cohort) — Adobe is being priced as a *declining* asset, not as software.

**Intrinsic value — three scenarios** (FCF base ~$10.8B FY2026E, ~389M shares, no net cash adjustment):

| Scenario | Assumptions | Equity value | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Moat holds** | FCF +8%/yr yrs 1–5, +5% yrs 6–10, 3% terminal, 9% WACC | $236B | **~$605** |
| **B. Slow erosion** | FCF +4%/yr yrs 1–5, +2% yrs 6–10, 1% terminal, 9.5% WACC | $150B | **~$385** |
| **C. AI disruption** | FCF −5%/yr yrs 1–5, flat thereafter, 0% terminal, 10% WACC | $87B | **~$225** |

**Blended (25/45/30): ~$392/share — a ~37% discount to the current price.** Even the slow-erosion case, which assumes permanent deceleration to low single digits, is worth ~$385. Only outright FCF *decline* justifies $245.

**What the price implies:** ~0–1% perpetual FCF growth. That is the market's bet. Against it: 12% revenue growth, 10.2% guided ending ARR growth, 47% ROIC, and a buyback running at **~11% of market cap per year**. That last point matters enormously and is underappreciated: if Adobe simply holds FCF flat and keeps repurchasing 10% of itself annually at 9x cash flow, per-share value compounds at a low-double-digit rate with *zero* growth. The margin of safety here is being manufactured by the capital return program at these prices.

## 4. Management Quality: **Good — B+**

**Positives:** Shantanu Narayen has run Adobe since 2007, architecting the single greatest SaaS transition in software history (perpetual license → subscription, 2012–2014) — a genuinely owner-minded, long-horizon decision that most CEOs avoided. Capital allocation is shareholder-first: **$11.3B repurchased in FY2025**, $9.5B in FY2024, share count down from 481M (FY2020) to ~389M (−19% in five years) with zero dilution net of SBC. And the **Figma acquisition was abandoned** in 2023 when regulators balked — an accidental blessing that avoided $20B for a competitor now attacking from below.

**Deductions:** (1) *Buyback timing.* The bulk of FY2024–25 repurchases occurred at $450–600, and FY2025's $11.3B was struck at an average well above today's $245. That is capital destroyed relative to waiting — a textbook failure of price discipline in favor of a formulaic program. (2) **CFO turnover** — a CFO departure was cited in the Q2-FY26 selloff, an unwelcome signal mid-transition. (3) Insider ownership is low (~<1%); this is a hired-management company, not an owner-operator one. (4) No dividend — fine, given the buyback, but it means shareholder returns depend entirely on management's buyback judgment, which has been mediocre on price.

Honest, competent, strategically shrewd — but not a capital allocator I'd trust to be price-disciplined without prodding.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **BUY** (not Strong Buy)

**The setup:** a 47%-ROIC, 89%-gross-margin, net-cash-neutral franchise trading at ~10x forward non-GAAP earnings and ~11x FCF, down 60% from its peak, with a 37% discount to a *conservatively weighted* intrinsic value that already assumes meaningful deceleration. Every dollar of bad news about AI has been marked into the price twice over.

**Why not Strong Buy:**
1. The moat question is genuinely unresolved. I can construct a defensible case for $225. A value investor must respect that this is not a case of "temporarily depressed earnings at an intact business" — it is a case of *possibly* impaired terminal value, and terminal value is 60%+ of the DCF.
2. The one thing that would break the thesis (sustained ARR deceleration below ~8% with flat net-new Creative seats) is observable only quarterly, and current guidance already points that direction.
3. My own blended IV of ~$392 is not so far above price that I'd deploy a full position at once.

**What I would require as proof over the next 4–6 quarters:** (a) total ending ARR growth staying ≥9%; (b) AI-influenced ARR converting into *net-new* revenue rather than credit repricing of existing seats; (c) buybacks continuing at ≥7% of shares annually at these prices; (d) no further senior finance departures.

**Position sizing:** Buy in tranches. This is a 5–10 year holding — the compounding mechanism here is buyback-driven per-share FCF growth, which requires patience and does not need a re-rating to work.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI commoditization of the prosumer tier (existential).** If generative tools make "good enough" creative output free, Adobe loses its seat-expansion engine and pricing power. Mitigant: brands need rights-cleared, audit-traceable assets (Firefly), and enterprise creative ops remain complex — but this is a real, permanent risk.
2. **Seat-model structural exposure.** Adobe prices per seat against knowledge-worker headcount. AI-driven efficiency gains at customers can *reduce* seats even if content volume rises. A pricing model mismatch, not a product failure.
3. **Digital Experience is a value trap inside the company.** ~25% of revenue with no moat; Salesforce/Microsoft compete with bundling. Continued low growth there drags total ARR and invites write-downs on $13.4B of intangibles.
4. **Terminal-value sensitivity.** At 9% WACC, each 100bp change in assumed terminal growth moves per-share value by roughly $60–80. Valuation precision is low; only the margin of safety is wide.
5. **Capital allocation risk.** If management accelerates buybacks at higher prices (as it did in 2024–25) or attempts another large AI-era acquisition, per-share compounding deteriorates sharply. The Figma escape was luck, not policy.
6. **Regulatory/legal.** Subscription cancellation and auto-renewal practices have drawn FTC/DOJ scrutiny historically; adverse outcomes would pressure the consumer funnel.
7. **Multiple already broken — no re-rating needed, but no re-rating to hope for either.** The thesis must work on cash flow and share count alone. If it works, that's a ~12–15% annual return; if the moat truly fails, the downside is ~10–20% from here, not 60%.

---

**Bottom line:** Adobe is a high-quality, cash-generative franchise whose stock is priced for decline. The financial quality is near-perfect (9/10); the moat is narrower than it was (Narrow, contested); management is good but imperfect on buyback price discipline. At ~10x forward earnings and ~11% FCF yield against a blended intrinsic value of ~$390, the market is offering a **~35% margin of safety on a business that doesn't need growth to compound — only survival.** **Buy, in tranches, with the ARR trend as the thesis monitor.**
