## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Regulatory Risks

### Risk 1 — Contagion of subscription-practice enforcement across US, UK and EU
**Probability: High (~75%) | Impact: Low–Medium | Timeframe: 0–18 months**

- The **DOJ settled its ROSCA case on 13-Mar-2026**: Adobe pays **$75M civil penalties plus $75M in free services ($150M total)** and accepts an **injunction** requiring clear pre-enrolment disclosure of any Early Termination Fee, trial-conversion reminders for trials over seven days, and easy cancellation paths. Two named employees (Sawhney, Wadhwani) were defendants — individual-accountability signal. *(Source: DOJ Office of Public Affairs, Release 26-252, 13-Mar-2026; Reuters 13-Mar-2026; Compliance Week)*
- **The UK CMA opened its own investigation on 19-Mar-2026** — six days after the US settlement — into whether Adobe's early cancellation fees are unfair and misleading. Adobe is reported to be the **ninth business** in this CMA consumer-enforcement programme (CMA opened a parallel Microsoft 365 probe on 29-Jul-2026). No conclusions reached yet. *(Source: gov.uk CMA press release; Bristows; Lexology)*
- **Why it matters:** the same conduct is now under live scrutiny in Adobe's second-largest regional market, and the US injunction creates a *documented compliance benchmark* that UK/EU/state enforcers can cite. Fines are immaterial relative to $23.8B revenue, but mandated friction in cancellation flows could compress conversion/renewal economics — precisely the metric the sell-side is already penalising (Wells Fargo double-downgrade to Underweight, 12-Oct-2026, citing "loss in pricing power").

### Risk 2 — AI regulation and generative-AI copyright exposure (EU AI Act + output provenance)
**Probability: High for compliance cost (~85%); Medium for material litigation (~35%) | Impact: Medium | Timeframe: ongoing**

- EU AI Act transparency obligations (Art. 50) became applicable **2-Aug-2026**; high-risk obligations phase in on the same date, with some high-risk deadlines reported delayed in 2026. As provider *and* deployer of **Firefly**-based generative features embedded across Creative Cloud and Digital Experience, Adobe carries labelling, disclosure and documentation duties across its 27% EMEA revenue base. *(Sources: European Commission AI Act page; CSA research note; artificialintelligenceact.eu)*
- **Mitigant:** Firefly was trained on Adobe Stock, licensed and public-domain content, and Adobe ships **Content Credentials** (C2PA) provenance metadata — a structurally stronger position than peers facing training-data suits. This is the single biggest reason Adobe's AI copyright risk is *lower* than the sector's.
- **Residual:** third-party claims over AI-generated output, plus DSA/Behance content-moderation obligations (Adobe files EU DSA transparency reports and has removed content for IP violations). Compliance build-out is a cost, not a solvency issue.

### Risk 3 — US–China tech decoupling and export-control friction
**Probability: Medium (~45%) | Impact: Low | Timeframe: 12–36 months**

- APAC is 14% of revenue and **China is not separately disclosed, implying it is immaterial** (<2%). Adobe has minimal mainland manufacturing or chip supply-chain dependence (the NVIDIA partnership on agentic creation is a US-to-US relationship). *(Source: Adobe/NVIDIA release, 15-Oct-2026)*
- Real exposure is *second-order*: (a) US software/AI export controls and Chinese counter-measures (CSL/PIPL) complicating deployment for multinational customers operating in China; (b) Chinese domestic substitutes compressing a small, already-thin revenue line.
- **Not a thesis-driver.** Rated Low impact deliberately — this is the least binding of the three.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **🔴 Consumer-protection compliance failure, now adjudicated.** A federal court found basis to impose a ROSCA injunction — with two *named individuals* as defendants. This is not a nuisance suit; it is a finding-adjacent consent decree on core monetisation mechanics. Escalation is now cross-border (UK).
2. **🟠 Live copycat regulatory exposure.** The CMA probe mirrors the exact US allegations. Assume EU/state AG follow-on interest. Remediation in the US should be exported globally *before* regulators require it.
3. **🟡 AI Act / DSA documentation obligations** — new, dated (Aug-2026), and applicable to 27% of revenue. Compliance maturity unverified from public filings.
4. **🟢 Clean on sanctions, FCPA, AML, and data-breach litigation.** No red flags identified. Russia exit is complete and documented.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale for Medium (not Low):** revenue is 86% concentrated in high-rule-of-law, low-expropriation jurisdictions with no sanctioned-country exposure and a clean FCPA/AML record — that argues for Low. But it is pushed to **Medium** by three factors: (i) an **active, unresolved UK CMA enforcement investigation** into the same conduct the DOJ just settled; (ii) a **brand-new federal injunction** constraining core subscription mechanics; and (iii) live **EU AI Act** obligations over 27% of revenue, coinciding with a **CEO transition five weeks out**.

**What would move it to High:** CMA issuing a breach finding with mandated pricing/refund remedies; any EU Commission or multi-state AG action; or a material AI-training-data lawsuit naming Firefly.
**What would move it to Low:** a closed CMA file with no fine, and 12 months of clean injunction compliance.

**Not thesis-defining.** For the investment case, the operative risk in ADBE is **AI-driven creative cannibalisation and growth deceleration** (stock −32% YTD 2026), not geopolitics. Legal and regulatory matters here are a **low-single-digit-percent drag on earnings at worst** — a governance and sentiment irritant that compounds the growth narrative rather than a balance-sheet threat.
