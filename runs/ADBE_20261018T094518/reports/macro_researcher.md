## 1. Macro Environment Summary — Stance: **Risk-off / "hawkish re-acceleration"**

As of **18 October 2026**, the US economy is in an unusual and hostile configuration for long-duration equities: **growth is re-accelerating, inflation is re-accelerating, and the Fed's easing cycle has gone into reverse** — while credit markets remain calm. This is not a recession regime; it is a *reflation-with-a-hawkish-Fed* regime in which the discount rate, not the earnings stream, is doing the damage.

**The decisive fact is the composition of the yield move.** The 10Y nominal yield has risen from **4.44% (30 Jun 2026)** to **5.10% (15 Oct)** — a +66bp backup that took it to a multi-year high of 5.31% on 5 Oct. Over the identical window, **10Y breakevens were flat** (2.24% → 2.35%), while the **10Y TIPS real yield rose from 2.20% to 2.78%** — and from **1.91% on 1 May**, a +87bp move in five months. **100% of the recent yield rise is real rates / term premium**, not inflation expectations. Term premium is expressing itself at the long end (30Y at 5.37%, 2s10s at **+34bp**, a bear steepener). For an equity whose value is weighted toward distant cash flows, a real-rate shock is precisely the wrong kind of tightening.

The inflation data justify it. **September CPI rose 0.65% m/m — an 8% annualised pace — lifting headline YoY to 3.71%** (336.298 vs 324.245), up from 3.30% in July. Core CPI came in **above consensus** (Reuters, 14 Oct), and market-implied odds of an **October Fed hike moved to roughly 50-50**. The 7 October FOMC minutes stated officials expect to **raise rates again before year-end**. Policy has pivoted from easing (200bp of cuts from 5.33% to a 3.63% trough in Feb–Jun 2026) to a **hiking bias** (effective fed funds back up to 3.75%).

Yet there is **no recession signal anywhere**: real GDP grew **2.2% annualised in Q2 2026** (+2.19% YoY) after a near-stall in Q4 2025 (+0.2% ann.), unemployment has *fallen* to **4.2%** from 4.5% in Nov 2025, and **HY OAS at 323bp** (15 Oct) is near historic tights — though +58bp off the 265bp low of 11 Sep, the first genuine crack. Tight spreads + 5.1% nominal yields = the market believes in nominal growth, not default risk.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact (ADBE)

Adobe is a **~$95.4bn market-cap, beta-1.415, net-cash software compounder** with roughly **40–45% of revenue outside the Americas** (approximate; segment disclosure). At **$245.20 (16 Oct)** the stock is **-50.5% over two years** (from $494.90) and **-55% from its $549.93 peak (11 Dec 2024)** — valuation compression has already done most of the work.

| Macro variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **10Y real yield 2.78% (+87bp/5m)** | **NEGATIVE (high)** | Primary driver. Equity duration: even after a ~55% de-rating, ADBE's ~7–8% FCF yield vs a 5.10% risk-free rate leaves a **thin ~200–300bp equity risk premium**. Each +50bp in real yields mechanically clips ~7–10% off a long-duration multiple. The tape confirms it: ADBE fell **-19%** from $292.79 (31 Aug) to $235.47 (25 Sep) *exactly* as real yields ran from 2.44% to 2.93%. |
| **Fed hiking bias / Oct hike ~50-50** | **NEGATIVE** | Caps any multiple re-rating and raises the hurdle for the AI-capex and enterprise-IT budgets Adobe sells into. Also weighs on the whole high-multiple software complex, which drags ADBE on beta (1.415) regardless of its own cheaper multiple. |
| **Re-accelerating CPI (3.71%)** | **NEGATIVE, partially offset** | Cost side: headcount is ~80% of opex (31,360 FTE); 3.5–4% wage inflation against 2.2% real GDP growth squeezes margins. **New and underappreciated:** AI inference/compute is an inflation-exposed COGS line (see the 15 Oct NVIDIA partnership expansion) that does not deflate as fast as licence gross margins did. Offset: subscription pricing power allows partial pass-through. |

| **Tight credit (HY OAS 323bp)** | **POSITIVE** | SMB and mid-market customers — a meaningful Creative Cloud cohort — retain cheap credit access. No demand destruction from financing stress. **Watch item:** the +58bp widening since 11 Sep is the first sign of risk repricing. |
| **USD +3.1% vs mid-May (index 121.6)** | **NEGATIVE (modest)** | High real yields are importing dollar strength. With ~40–45% non-US revenue, a further 5% broad USD appreciation is a ~2% reported-revenue headwind and a bigger EPS headwind given US-dollar cost base. |
| **Refinancing / leverage risk** | **NEUTRAL–POSITIVE** | Adobe carries only ~$5.5–6bn of senior notes against a large cash balance — effectively net cash. **Negligible refinancing risk.** Higher rates actually help via interest income on cash, and make the aggressive buyback *more* accretive at ~12–13x FCF. **The key asymmetry:** ADBE is *less* rate-fragile than it was in 2021–24 because the multiple has already collapsed ~60%, and it is net cash with a large buyback. But it is *more* exposed to the **direction of real yields** than a value stock, and its recent price action shows duration sensitivity is fully intact.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Justification: (i) a Fed that is debating *hikes*, not cuts, with ~50% October odds; (ii) a **+87bp real-yield shock in five months** that is the exact variable that prices Adobe's cash-flow stream; (iii) a **thin equity risk premium** even after the de-rating; (iv) USD strength translating ~40–45% of revenue. The mitigants — net cash, ~12–13x FCF, heavy buyback, tight credit spreads, +6.7% nominal consumer spend — prevent this from being *severely* unfavourable and explain why the stock has bottomed and bounced (+20% off the 18 Jun low of $195.16) rather than broken down. Net: **the macro is a headwind to the multiple, not to the business.**

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**Bear surprise (highest-impact, underpriced): "Two hikes and a 3%+ real yield."**

The market prices ~50-50 for *one* October hike and essentially assumes the Fed stops there. Suppose September's 0.65% m/m CPI print is not an outlier, the Fed hikes in **October and December to ~4.25%**, and 10Y real yields break **3.00%** with the nominal 10Y through **5.50%**. HY OAS widens from 323bp to 450bp+, and — crucially — enterprise buyers respond to a 5.5% cost of capital by stretching software renewal cycles and pushing AI-budget consolidation. In that world ADBE's ~12–13x FCF does *not* protect it: a 3.0% real rate plus a de-rating of the whole software complex on beta 1.415 puts the **June 2026 low of $195 (-20%)** firmly back in play, and a break there opens the $170s. *Trigger to watch: the 28–29 October FOMC and the 10Y TIPS yield crossing 3.00%.*

**Bull surprise (equally plausible, and the reason not to be structurally short): "Disinflation shock."** If shelter and energy roll over and the October/November CPI prints come in at 0.2% m/m, the hike is priced out, the real 10Y retreats from 2.78% to ~2.2%, and a net-cash software name at 12x FCF with an aggressive buyback re-rates 20–30% on multiple alone — without any change to Adobe's underlying AI narrative.
