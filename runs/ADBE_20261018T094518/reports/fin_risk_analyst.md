## ADBE — Complete Financial Risk Assessment
*(Data as of FY2025 10-K (period end 2025-11-28); market data through profile date; Q3 FY2026 results included)*

---

### 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE (high quality)**

Adobe is effectively debt-free on a net basis. Net debt is **$53M** against **$9.82B EBITDA** (0.01x). S&P rates Adobe **A+ / stable** (confirmed via S&P Global Ratings commentary on its senior unsecured notes). All debt is long-dated with **no short-term debt** on the FY25 balance sheet, and $6.60B cash covers gross debt almost 1:1. This is a cash-generative, negative-working-capital subscription model — the classic profile of a high-quality credit.

**Important caveat:** the credit is strong; the *equity* is not. Credit risk and equity risk have fully decoupled here. The bond market prices Adobe as a fortress; the stock market prices it as an AI casualty. This assessment must be read with that split in mind.

Not a solvency issue at these levels — but a shareholder-return policy funded with debt at a 9% FCF yield is a capital-allocation question, not a balance-sheet strength.

### 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue –20%

Base FY25: revenue $23.77B, gross margin 89.3%, opex $12.51B, EBIT $9.00B, interest $263M.

| Line item | Base FY25 | **–20% revenue** | Stress (–35%, margin compression) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $23.77B | $19.02B | $15.45B |
| Gross margin | 89.3% | 88.0% | 84% |
| Gross profit | $21.22B | $16.74B | $12.98B |
| Opex (assume 15% cut) | $12.51B | $11.39B | $10.63B |
| EBIT | $9.00B | **$5.35B** | **$2.35B** |
| Operating margin | 37.8% | **28.1%** | 15.2% |
| Interest coverage | 33.1x | **~20x** | **~9x** |
| EBITDA | $9.82B | ~$6.4B | ~$3.3B |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 0.01x | **~0.4x** (if buybacks paused) | **~0.8x** |
| FCF | $9.85B | **~$5.5B** | ~$2.5B |
| FCF yield (at $95B cap) | 10.3% | 5.8% | 2.6% |
| Altman Z | 4.35 | ~3.4 (safe) | ~2.4 (grey) |

**Conclusion: a 20% revenue decline is a profitability event, not a solvency event.** Adobe remains comfortably investment grade, coverage stays above 20x, and net leverage stays well under 1x — even with buybacks continuing. Only a ~35% revenue collapse *combined with* severe margin compression pushes the Z-score toward the grey zone, and even then the company generates positive FCF and services debt.

**The real transmission mechanism is equity valuation, not credit:** at a 9–10% FCF yield the market is already discounting a permanent decline. Further downside comes from further multiple compression, not from covenant breach. Notably, Q3 FY2026 came in at record revenue **$6.76B, +13% YoY**, non-GAAP EPS $6.13 (beat by ~5%), with FY26 guidance **raised** — so the 20% decline scenario is not currently being observed in the operating data.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Split it explicitly:

- **Balance-sheet / credit risk: LOW.** Zero net leverage, 33x coverage, A+ rated, Z-score 4.35 in the safe zone, $6.6B cash, $9.85B FCF.
- **Equity / market risk: HIGH.** 37% vol, –67% max drawdown, –0.85 Sharpe, event-driven repricing on every print.
- **Business-model risk (not captured by ratios): HIGH and rising.** AI disintermediation of the Creative Cloud franchise is the single largest risk to this company, and it is a risk that no leverage ratio will detect. Every prior "AI loser" re-rating in software has hit the terminal multiple long before it hit the income statement.
- **Goodwill impairment risk (balance sheet): MODERATE.** Goodwill + intangibles of **$13.35B = 45% of total assets and 115% of book equity**. In the –35% scenario, a Digital Experience impairment charge could wipe out a large fraction of book equity and mechanically spike D/E — flag as a non-cash but optically severe risk.

---

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Buybacks funded beyond FCF** — $11.28B repurchased vs $9.85B FCF; net debt flipped from –$1.56B to +$0.05B in one year. Gross debt +63% in two years. Sustainable only while FCF holds.
2. **Interest expense +60% YoY**; coverage down from ~61x to 33x (still excellent, but the direction is wrong).
3. **Goodwill/intangibles at 115% of book equity** — impairment risk if Digital Experience growth stalls.
4. **Current ratio at exactly 1.00x with negative working capital** — fine for a deferred-revenue model, but leaves no liquidity cushion from the balance sheet; liquidity depends entirely on continued cash collections.
5. **SBC of $1.94B = 8.2% of revenue** — GAAP FCF is flattered relative to true economic compensation cost; adjust FCF to ~$7.9B and FCF yield to ~8.3% (still high).
6. **Q3 FY26 EPS beat was on non-GAAP $6.13 vs GAAP $4.62** — a $1.51/share (~$600M/quarter) gap, driven largely by SBC and amortisation. GAAP earnings quality is materially below headline.
7. **Beta/risk-model instability** — calculated beta 0.74 vs vendor 1.42 signals the stock has decoupled from the market; standard beta-based risk budgeting is unreliable here.

**Bottom line:** Adobe is not a credit risk — it is a high-quality, near-net-debt-free annuity with a 41% FCF margin trading at a 9–10% FCF yield because the market is pricing existential AI disruption. The downside case is real and violent in equity terms (–67% already realized), but the balance sheet can absorb a 20–35% revenue decline without threatening debt service or ratings. The correct framing for the investment committee: **this is a duration/narrative risk with an intact balance sheet, not a leverage problem.** Watch the buyback-versus-FCF gap and goodwill, not the debt ratios.
