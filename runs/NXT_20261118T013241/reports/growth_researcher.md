# Nextpower Inc. (NASDAQ: NXT) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price: $78.48 | Market Cap: $11.8B | EV: $10.9B | Date: Nov 17, 2026**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Nextpower (formerly Nextracker) is the dominant global franchise in solar trackers — the steel-and-software systems that tilt utility-scale PV panels toward the sun — with roughly 30% global share and a commanding US position. The company is now executing a deliberate platform expansion from a single-product hardware vendor into an integrated "clean power technology" supplier: foundations (NX Earth Truss), electrical balance-of-system (eBOS), power conversion/inverters (NX Power Conversion), energy storage, and NX PowerMerge. Non-tracker products already account for ~22% of Q2 FY27 revenue and management targets **>50% by 2030**, which is the entire basis for the raised 2030 target of **>$7.5B revenue and >$1.6B adjusted EBITDA**.

The disruptive thesis is real but the *quality* of the growth has deteriorated: the platform expansion is being bought with gross margin. The stock has fallen ~52% from its May 2026 high of $156 to $78, and ~26% in the two sessions around the Nov 16 Capital Markets Day, as the market repriced NXT from a high-multiple growth compounder to a cyclical, capital-intensive hardware platform.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

**Core tracker TAM:** ~$10–13B in 2026, growing 15–17% CAGR to ~$23B by 2029–31 (multiple third-party estimates cluster here). NXT at $3.56B FY26 revenue holds ~30% global share and an estimated ~50%+ of the US utility-scale market — already a dominant position, so **core tracker share gains are limited; growth must come from market growth and TAM expansion.**

**Expanded TAM (the actual thesis):** By adding foundations, eBOS, inverters/power conversion, storage, and data-center power infrastructure, management is chasing a SAM that plausibly exceeds **$40–50B** — roughly 3–4x the tracker-only market. This is the legitimate Cathie Wood-style argument: NXT is not trying to win a bigger slice of a $12B pie, it is trying to redefine the pie as "everything on a utility-scale power plant except the module."

**Penetration math:** 2030 target of $7.5B revenue against a ~$45B expanded SAM implies ~17% penetration — low enough to be credible. Non-tracker revenue must go from ~22% to >50% of a $7.5B business, i.e. from ~$0.8B to ~$3.8B — a **~48% CAGR in non-tracker products over four years.** That is the single most important number in the story, and it is unproven.

**Differentiation:** NX Horizon + independent-row tracking software, NX PowerMerge (patented, >2 GW in backlog), and now a bundled "design-build-operate" platform. The moat is integration and installed-base software, not hardware IP — and ~75% of revenue flows through cost-sensitive EPC contractors with **low brand stickiness**, which caps pricing power. That is the structural weakness in the moat.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q3 FY27 earnings — ~Jan 26, 2027.** The single most important datapoint. Guidance embeds Q3 revenue growth of only ~10% YoY and H2 adj. EBITDA margin in the **high teens** (vs. 23.6% in H1). Any stabilization above 19% is a re-rating event; a miss toward 17% confirms structural margin erosion.
- **December 2026 tariff determinations.** Q2 FY27 included ~$99M of IRA 45X vendor rebates and tariffs, net. Tariff timing/rate changes flow directly to gross margin and are the swing factor in the FY28 bridge.
- **IRA 45X / domestic-content rule clarity.** Policy durability underpins both the rebate stream and US demand; adverse rulemaking is the largest single downside risk to the 2030 target.
- **Data-center power infrastructure entry.** Management has flagged expansion into data-center power — a genuinely large, high-growth adjacent TAM that is not in consensus numbers. First commercial wins would be a meaningful optionality unlock.
- **Zimmermann integration + European ramp.** Adds 15 countries (68 total) and fixed-tilt/structural products; European utility-scale is earlier in its adoption curve than the US.
- **Buyback cadence.** ~$162M repurchased in Q2 FY27 alone with >$1B net cash — at 13.6x forward earnings, aggressive repurchase is highly accretive.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (not Strong Buy)

**The bull case is arithmetic.** At $78.48 you are paying **16.6x FY27 adjusted EPS, 13.6x FY28, and 9.8x management's 2030 target of >$8.00** — for a business growing revenue 20%+ with a $6B backlog, >$1B net cash, no debt, a 100% seven-quarter beat rate, and a PEG under 0.8. If even 80% of the 2030 target is achieved, the stock is worth $120–140 on a 15–17x exit multiple. The average analyst target of ~$129 is not fantasy; it is roughly the 2030 target discounted back.

**But this is not a Strong Buy, because the growth quality has broken down.** My framework demands expanding unit economics alongside revenue growth, and NXT is delivering the opposite:

- Gross margin peaked at 33.7% in FY25 and has compressed to 32.6% (FY26) and 32.0% (Q2 FY27 adj.), with Q2 GAAP GM at 31.1% vs. 32.4% a year ago.
- Operating margin has fallen three straight years: 23.5% → 21.6% → 19.6%.
- Management's own 2030 target of $1.6B EBITDA on $7.5B revenue implies a **~21.3% margin — roughly 250–300bp *below* FY26 levels.** The dilution is structural (lower-margin eBOS, storage, inverters, foundations), not a one-quarter blip. FY28 is explicitly an "investment year."
- Rule of 40 sits at **34.7** — below the 40 threshold, and FCF margin fell from 21.0% to 14.4% in FY26 despite 20% revenue growth.

**The honest framing:** you are buying a 20%-growth revenue story at a 13x forward earnings multiple, where the earnings compound at maybe 12–15% because margins leak. That is a good risk/reward — roughly 2:1 to 3:1 upside/downside from here — but it is a *value-plus-growth* setup, not a disruptive-compounder setup. The market has correctly stopped paying a growth multiple; the question is whether it has overcorrected. At 12x EV/EBITDA and 6.8x the 2030 EBITDA target, I believe it has.

**Position sizing implication:** initiate at current levels, add on confirmation of H2 FY27 margin stabilization at the January print, and add materially below $70 (near the $72 low-end analyst target and the $75 52-week low).

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Structural margin dilution (highest conviction risk).** The mix shift into eBOS, storage, and power conversion is margin-dilutive by design. If non-tracker revenue scales faster than management plans, consolidated gross margin could fall below 30%, breaking the earnings model entirely. Q2's 31.1% GAAP GM is a warning shot.
2. **Policy dependence — IRA 45X and tariffs.** ~$99M per quarter of net 45X vendor rebates and tariff effects flow through gross profit. A change to 45X transferability, domestic-content rules, or tariff rates could remove a mid-single-digit percentage of gross profit with little notice. This is a binary, non-diversifiable risk.
3. **Customer concentration and weak pricing power.** ~75% of revenue comes from utility-scale EPC contractors with low brand stickiness. In a downturn these buyers extract price concessions and push out deliveries — exactly the dynamic that produced the FY26 FCF decline.
4. **Competition from Chinese and European trackers.** Arctech, PV Hardware, and Chinese entrants price ~10% below US competitors pre-tariff. Tariffs currently protect NXT's US position; any easing of trade barriers would compress both share and price.
5. **Execution risk on the platform pivot.** Non-tracker revenue must compound ~48% annually to hit >50% of a $7.5B business by 2030. Consensus FY2031 revenue of $7.11B already sits *below* the 2030 target — the Street does not believe the number, and the burden of proof is on management.
6. **Rate sensitivity / macro.** Beta of 1.93, Treasury yields near 5%, and a possible December hike directly raise the cost of project financing for NXT's customers. Utility-scale solar demand is a financing-spread derivative as much as a technology story.
7. **Backlog conversion timing.** $6B of backlog is only valuable if it converts on schedule. Grid interconnection bottlenecks and permitting delays can push revenue between quarters, and this stock has shown it will trade ±12% on a single print.

---

**Bottom line:** NXT is a high-quality franchise trading at a cyclical multiple because its margin story broke. The revenue growth, backlog, balance sheet, and beat history are all genuinely strong; the unit economics are not. At 13.6x FY28 earnings with 19% growth and a $6B backlog, the risk/reward is favorable enough to own — but the thesis now rests on margin *stabilization* rather than margin *expansion*, which caps the multiple and the conviction. **Buy, with the January 2027 print as the key checkpoint.**
