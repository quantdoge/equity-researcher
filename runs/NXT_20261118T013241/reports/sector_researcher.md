### 1. Sector Overview

NXT sits in **solar balance-of-system (BOS) equipment** — principally single-axis trackers, plus an expanding power-conversion/inverters and battery-storage (BESS) platform. The global tracker market is ~$8.8–11.2bn in 2025–26, growing ~12–17% annually, and is structurally oligopolistic: NXT, Array Technologies, PV Hardware (Gamesa), Soltec and a rising tier of Chinese entrants. The demand driver is no longer the module — it is **US utility-scale project economics plus AI/data-centre load growth**, with US solar+storage supplying >90% of new grid capacity additions in Q1 2026. The sector is **late-cycle in the US policy sense**: Q2 2026 US installs were 11.4 GWdc, +45% YoY, but Wood Mackenzie estimates 216–240 GWdc has been safe-harboured ahead of the OBBBA cliff — pulling 2027–28 demand forward and creating a visible air pocket thereafter. Internationally the cycle is earlier and more competitive.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Safe-harboured pipeline converts to tracker revenue through 2028.** 216–240 GWdc begun-construction before the OBBBA deadline implies ~6–7x annual US install volumes already contracted; NXT's own backlog exceeds **$6bn** (≈1.7x FY26 revenue), giving unusually high forward visibility versus industrial peers.
- **Revenue-per-project expansion (the real re-rating driver).** BESS + power conversion + software now ~22% of revenue, letting NXT cross-sell into an installed base it already won on trackers. Management's FY30 target is **>$7.5bn revenue / >$1.6bn adj. EBITDA / >$8.00 adj. EPS** — the revenue goal was raised ~45% from last year's $5.2bn plan.
- **Data-centre load growth and storage attach are demand-multipliers**, not substitutes: each GW of new firm capacity pulls trackers + BESS + interconnection equipment, and storage attach lifts content per MW with structurally higher ASPs.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Policy cliff, not a slope.** OBBBA (July 2025) shortened 45Y/48E qualification windows; projects not begun by the deadline face accelerated credit phase-out. Wood Mackenzie's own view is that **2026 US installs look flat despite the record pipeline** — the risk is a 2028–29 demand trough as safe-harboured projects complete.
- **Steel and tariff-driven COGS inflation.** Trackers are steel-intensive; Section 232 steel/aluminium duties plus FEOC domestic-content rules raise landed cost and force supply-chain re-sourcing. This is the direct cause of NXT's gross-margin compression (33.7% → 32.6% FY26, and 35.9% → 31.1% sequentially in the two most recent quarters).
- **Price competition is intensifying outside the US.** Chinese tracker makers and PV Hardware are gaining share in EMEA/India/LatAm, capping international pricing power precisely where US-exposed players must redeploy capacity post-2028. Adjacent peer performance confirms how violent this can be — ARRY's revenue fell 41.9% in 2024, ENPH's 41.9% in the same year.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **Scale and share leadership:** #1 global tracker position (~25–30% share), roughly 2–3x Array's installed base; the only BOS vendor with a credible multi-product (tracker + BESS + inverter + software) platform.
- **Best-in-class unit economics:** 19.6% operating margin on 32.6% gross margin is the widest GM→OM conversion in the peer set, evidencing pricing discipline and a genuinely asset-light model.
- **Growth consistency is the differentiator:** NXT has grown revenue every year of the series (21.9%, 30.5%, 31.4%, 18.4%, 20.3%) while ARRY, ENPH and SHLS each posted a >18% *decline* within the same window. In a policy-shock sector, that consistency is the moat.
- **Caveats:** (i) gross margin is trending down as lower-margin BESS/inverter mix rises — FY30's >21% EBITDA margin target assumes this stabilises; (ii) the stock's **-12% reaction on 17 Nov to a Capital Markets Day that *raised* FY30 revenue 45%** is the market explicitly refusing to pay for long-dated targets and instead pricing near-term margin compression and post-2027 policy risk; (iii) beta of 1.93 and a ~50% drawdown from the May 2026 peak (~$163 → $78.48) confirm this is a high-torque policy-beta name, not a defensive compounder.

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Structural growth (trackers + storage attach + data-centre demand) and a ~$6bn backlog argue for *Attractive*. But three things cap it at Neutral: a **quantifiable 2028+ US policy air pocket**, **input-cost inflation with limited pass-through**, and **eroding international pricing power**. Within that neutral sector, NXT is the highest-quality way to own the theme — best margins, most consistent growth, cheapest relative to its own long-range plan (FY30 EPS target of >$8.00 implies ~10x at $78.48) — but the peer group's history of 40% revenue drawdowns means the policy cliff should be underwritten explicitly, not dismissed.
