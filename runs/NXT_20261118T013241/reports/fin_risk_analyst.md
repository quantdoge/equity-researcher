**Company:** Nextpower Inc. (formerly Nextracker) · Solar trackers, eBOS, foundations, energy storage · Fremont, CA · FY ends March 31 · IPO Feb 2023 · Market cap ~$11.8B · Price $78.48 (feed)

### 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE (BBB-)**

| Agency | Rating | Outlook | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fitch | BBB- (first-time IDR) | Stable | Fitch, 20-Jan-2026 |
| S&P | BBB- | **Negative** | Argus/Fidelity market update, Oct-2026 |

The balance sheet is effectively debt-free: total debt **$52.9M** (short-term $11.9M, **long-term $0**), against **$1.095B cash** → **net cash of $1.04B**. Net debt/EBITDA **−1.39x**. FY26 interest expense was only **$2.6M** on $716M EBIT → **interest coverage 266x**, and with net-interest *income* running positive the company has no debt-service dependence on operating performance whatsoever. Liquidity is supplemented by an **undrawn $1,000M senior revolver** (per Fitch), putting total liquidity near **$2.1B** — ~4.1x annual capex. Fitch notes management intends to hold >$250M cash at all times; current cash is 4x that floor.

**No refinancing cliff:** there is essentially no maturity wall to breach. The only credit-relevant constraint is a ratings-agency expectation of conservative leverage, not a covenant likely to bind.

**Conclusion:** Not investment-grade by luck of an undrawn revolver, but by capital structure. The only caveat is the **S&P negative outlook** diverging from Fitch stable — worth monitoring, most plausibly driven by policy/tariff exposure rather than leverage.

FCF conversion is high (FCF/NI ≈ 0.87), and capex is minimal at 1.3% of revenue (asset-light contract manufacturing), so FCF tracks earnings closely.

### 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue −20%

**Base case:** FY27 guidance midpoint — revenue **$4.325B**, adjusted EBITDA **$915M** (21.2% margin), adj. EPS $4.74, FCF >$500M.

**Assumptions:** contribution margin ≈ gross margin of **31–32%** (latest quarter: 31.1%); SG&A (~$410M, largely fixed) flexes only ~10%; D&A ~$65M; cash tax rate 20%; capex ~$60M; net interest income remains positive on the cash pile.

| Line item | Base (FY27E) | Shock (−20% rev) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $4,325M | **$3,460M** | −$865M |
| Gross profit (32%) | $1,384M | $1,107M | −$277M |
| SG&A | $410M | $369M | +$41M relief |
| **Adj. EBITDA** | **$915M** | **~$665M** | **−27%** |
| EBITDA margin | 21.2% | **19.2%** | −200bp |
| Interest coverage | >250x | **>400x** | Improves |
| Net debt / EBITDA | −1.1x | **−1.6x** | Net cash *grows* |
| FCF (incl. WC release ~$250M) | $500M | **$500–700M** | Flat to up |
| FCF (ex-WC release) | $500M | ~$430M | −14% |

**Key finding: a 20% revenue decline is an earnings event, not a solvency event.** EBITDA falls ~27% to ~$665M but stays comfortably positive; the balance sheet *strengthens* because inventory and receivables unwind (~$250M cash release) and there is no debt to service. FCF is roughly flat.

**Severe stress (−35% revenue, US tax-credit shock):** revenue ~$2.8B, EBITDA ~$230–450M depending on decremental margin. Even at the low end, the company remains **EBITDA-positive, FCF-positive, and net cash**, with $1.1B cash plus a $1B undrawn revolver. **Net cash would not be consumed.** There is no realistic operating scenario in which NXT requires external capital — the distress path here is an *equity* de-rating, not a credit event.

**Equity downside translation:** at 66% vol and ~13.5x EV/EBITDA (TTM) / ~11.8x FY27E adj. EBITDA, a −20% revenue year with multiple compression to 8x could imply a 40–55% further equity decline. NXT has already demonstrated a 51% drawdown is achievable.

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Policy/tariff concentration — the #1 risk.** ~**82% of quarterly revenue is US-sourced**, and demand is levered to 48E/45X ITC-PTC economics, FEOC/foreign-entity rules, and anti-dumping duties on imported modules/steel. The S&P **negative outlook** most plausibly reflects this. Management cites persistent **steel, diesel and freight cost inflation**, partially offset by localized manufacturing. This is a risk the balance sheet cannot hedge.
2. **Receivables quality and DSO.** Net receivables of **$1.30B = 36% of annual revenue (DSO ≈ 134 days)**, growing faster than revenue (+19% vs +20% revenue FY26, but +$209M). Counterparties are project developers — the weakest link in the solar value chain. A customer insolvency cycle would hit cash and require allowances. This is the most under-appreciated liquidity risk on the sheet.
3. **Gross margin erosion.** 33.7% (FY25) → 32.6% (FY26) → **31.1% (Q2 FY27)** — down ~260bp even as revenue grew 23%. Mix shift into lower-margin eBOS/storage/foundations (non-tracker now 22% of revenue) plus cost inflation. Watch whether 21%+ EBITDA guidance holds.
4. **Negative retained earnings (−$1.97B)** and **$567M of goodwill/intangibles** (14% of assets). The deficit is a structural legacy but it distorts Z-score screening and signals acquisitions (Zimmermann, €230M cash, post-quarter) are now part of the growth model — a new capital-allocation risk vector.
5. **Ongoing dilution.** Diluted shares up ~2%/yr from SBC (145.9M → 155.1M); F-Score penalizes it. Not credit-relevant, but it quietly erodes per-share FCF.
6. **Accruals above cash.** FY26 OCF ($556M) < net income ($586M) — modest, but combined with the working-capital build it's worth tracking quarterly.

### Bottom Line

**Credit: safe. Equity: volatile.** NXT cannot be stressed into distress on its current capital structure — $1.04B net cash, zero long-term debt, 266x interest coverage, and a $1B undrawn revolver mean a 20% or even 35% revenue decline is absorbed without external financing. The Z-score grey-zone reading is a retained-earnings/market-cap artifact and should be dismissed; the F-Score of 4 is real but reflects growth-stage dilution and margin normalization rather than deterioration.

The genuine risks are (a) **66% volatility, 1.3–1.9 beta, and a −51% drawdown with the stock near its 52-week low while fundamentals hit records** — the market is pricing policy risk the financials don't show, and (b) **$1.3B of receivables from leveraged developers** in a subsidy-dependent value chain. Rated **MODERATE overall**, with credit risk **Low** and market risk **High**.
