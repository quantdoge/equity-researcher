# Nextpower Inc. (NXT) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $78.48 (Nov 17, 2026) · Market cap ~$11.9B · Net cash ~$1.0B · No debt · FY ends March 31*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW**

Nextpower (formerly Nextracker) is the world's largest supplier of solar trackers — the steel-and-motor structures that tilt utility-scale panels toward the sun. It shipped into 68 countries, holds the #1 global share (~30%), and has deliberately expanded into foundations, eBOS, power conversion/inverters, and energy storage. Trackers are still the foundation (~88% of FY26 revenue), but non-tracker lines are targeted at >50% of FY30 revenue.

**Sources of advantage (real but not durable enough for a Wide rating):**
- **Scale + manufacturing cost position.** FY26 revenue of $3.56B against ~1,993 employees; gross margin went from 15.1% (FY23) to 32.6% (FY26). That expansion is the single best piece of evidence that the moat *widened* — but it was substantially subsidy-assisted (see below).
- **Intangible/bankability.** Utility-scale buyers (IPPs, EPCs) spec products with 25-year warranties and lender-approved track records. A new entrant cannot easily get banked on a $1B project. This is genuine stickiness, though per-project rather than per-customer.
- **Software attach.** TrueCapture/NX Navigator create modest switching costs and a higher-margin revenue layer.
- **Regulatory moat (time-limited).** Domestic steel manufacturing qualifies for Section 45X advanced-manufacturing credits. Q2 FY27 gross profit included ~$99M/quarter (~$400M annualized) of "IRA 45X vendor rebates and tariffs, net." That is roughly **43% of guided FY27 adjusted EBITDA** sitting on a statute. The phase-down begins after 2029 (75% in 2030, 50% in 2031, 25% in 2032).

**Why not Wide:** trackers are fundamentally fabricated steel plus electronics, and the FY23 15% gross margin is the cautionary proof of how fast pricing can collapse when competitors (Arctech, Trina, GameChange, Soltec, PVH) get aggressive. Steel is 25–30% of cost and largely passes through. Revenue is driven by utility-scale solar construction, which is policy-determined and cyclical. **5-year moat trend: widened, but the widening is partly rented from the tax code and likely narrows from 2030.**

**Circle-of-competence note:** I understand this business, but it is a capital-light equipment manufacturer whose demand curve is set in Washington rather than by pricing power. That is inherently a lower-quality Buffett business.

**Strengths:** 25% four-year revenue CAGR; ROIC of 29% (FY26) versus a ~10% WACC — consistently value-creating; FCF conversion of 87–138%, comfortably above the 80% excellence threshold; net cash of ~$1.04B and *zero* long-term debt after funding the €230M Zimmermann acquisition and $162M of buybacks in Q2 FY27. Interest coverage is effectively infinite.

**Deductions:** (a) **Working capital is heavy** — receivables of $1.30B equal ~134 days of sales, a structural feature of selling to leveraged IPPs and international EPCs; (b) **stock-based compensation of $119M in FY25** (~23% of net income) is real dilution — the FY26 feed reports $0, which I treat as a data gap, not a fact, and I haircut owner earnings accordingly; (c) margins are now *contracting* (32.6% → guided lower) even as revenue grows 20%; (d) owner earnings of $572M (FY26) are flattered by the 45X credits; (e) negative retained earnings (−$1.97B) is cosmetic, from pre-IPO distributions, not an economic problem.

Recent TTM (Q3 FY26–Q2 FY27): revenue $3.83B, net income $613M, diluted EPS $3.97. Q2 FY27 delivered record revenue of $1,109M (+23%) and record bookings, with backlog above **$6B** (~1.4x annual revenue).

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples (FY27 guidance midpoint: revenue $4.33B, adj. EBITDA $915M, GAAP EPS $3.75, adj. EPS $4.74):**

| Measure | NXT | FSLR | ENPH | SHLS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P/E | 20.4x (TTM) | 10.8x | 17.9x | 35.2x |
| EV/EBITDA | ~11.9x (adj.) | 6.5x | 12.4x | 18.7x |
| P/FCF | 23.4x | 13.9x | 32.4x | n.m. |

FCF yield 4.3%; earnings yield 4.8% on adjusted EPS. NXT is the highest-quality name in a badly derated solar-equipment complex (FSLR at 6.5x EBITDA tells you what the market thinks of solar policy risk), yet it trades at a meaningful premium to it.

**My own DCF (base case):** normalized owner earnings of ~$500M (FY27 GAAP net income of $578M, adjusted for SBC and a partial 45X haircut), growing 12% for five years (deliberately half the company's ~20% target, reflecting the policy air-pocket and 45X decay), then 4%, terminal 2.5%, discounted at 10%:

- PV of years 1–10 FCF: ~$4.96B
- PV of terminal value: ~$5.65B
- Enterprise value ~$10.6B **+ $1.0B net cash = ~$11.6B equity → ~$76/share**

- **Bull case** (company hits FY30 targets of $7.5B revenue, $1.6B EBITDA, >$8.00 EPS; 15% FCF growth through year five): ~$99/share
- **Bear case** (OBBBA begin-construction deadline pulls 2028–29 demand forward into 2027, revenue declines, 45X decays, 11% WACC): ~$52/share
- **Probability-weighted: ~$76**

**The tool-assisted DCF returned $142/share** using the trailing 3-year FCF CAGR of 30% — I reject it. That growth rate is an artifact of the 2023 IPO base and will not recur; using it produces a terminal value that is 80% of the answer. Do not anchor on it.

**Verdict on price:** at $78.48 the stock trades at roughly a **0–3% premium to intrinsic value**. There is no margin of safety. Note also that the stock is −50% from its May 2026 high of $156 and −25% in the week since the Nov 16 Capital Markets Day — but a falling price is not a discount if the earnings base is also falling.

## 4. Management Quality: **B+ / A−**

- **Dan Shugar (founder-CEO)** built the category leader and still runs it — owner-operator temperament, deep technical credibility. CFO Chuck Boynton is a credible, disclosure-forward operator (quarterly 45X quantification is unusually candid).
- **Capital allocation is disciplined and shareholder-friendly:** no dividend (correct — retained capital earns 29% ROIC), $552M and $694M of repurchases in FY24/FY23, $162M in Q2 FY27 alone, and the Zimmermann deal done for €230M with **no stock and no earnout** — a cash deal that does not dilute. That is the opposite of empire-building by share issuance.
- **Two caveats.** First, the pivot into energy storage and power conversion puts NXT against CATL, Sungrow, Tesla and Fluence — arenas with weaker returns on capital than trackers. The FY30 framework already concedes this: revenue +45% versus the prior plan, but adjusted EBITDA margin of only ~21%, *below* FY27's ~21%. Growth is being bought, not earned at higher returns. Second, insider ownership could not be independently verified in this data set; the former PE sponsor's sell-down has been the dominant supply of shares, and my buyback figures may partially reflect repurchases from that holder.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

This is a genuinely good company — #1 share, 29% ROIC, a fortress balance sheet, and a founder who compounds capital sensibly — trading at approximately its intrinsic value. Under my framework, **a business that trades at a fair price is not a buy.**

**To become a Buy, I need $58 or below** (~25% discount to my $76 base case). At that price the bear case is more than priced in and the FCF yield would exceed 7%.

What would make me wrong to wait: US utility-scale interconnection and data-center-driven load growth proving durable well past the 2026–27 tax-credit deadlines, and storage/power conversion scaling to billion-dollar lines at >20% margins. If FY28 guidance in April 2027 shows continued growth *through* the policy cliff, the base case moves toward $95–100 and I would upgrade at today's price.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Policy cliff (the dominant risk).** OBBBA's begin-construction/placed-in-service deadlines pull US solar demand forward into FY27–FY28, risking an air pocket in calendar 2028–29. A 20% US volume decline would cut EBITDA by ~$250–300M.
2. **45X dependency and decay.** ~$400M annualized of gross-profit benefit phasing to 75%/50%/25% in 2030/31/32. Stripping it out takes FY27 EV/EBITDA from ~11.9x to ~15x. Earnings quality is subsidized.
3. **Commoditization.** FY23's 15% gross margin is the historical proof that tracker pricing can collapse. Chinese and Indian competitors are well capitalized.
4. **Customer credit and working capital.** 134 days of receivables against leveraged IPP counterparties; a single large EPC or developer failure would be material.
5. **Diworsification.** Storage and inverters are lower-return, more competitive, and capital-intensive (10 GW of US inverter capacity by end-2027). Watch for ROIC erosion below 20%.
6. **Volatility and timing.** Beta 1.93; the stock has fallen 40–50% twice in three years. Position sizing must assume that.
7. **Backlog conversion risk.** Fixed-price contracts on $6B of backlog expose NXT to steel and tariff cost inflation between booking and delivery.
