### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US trade policy stack (Section 232 polysilicon derivatives + metals) compressing US demand and margin. Probability: High (policy already in force) | Impact: High**

- A Section 232 proclamation establishes a **15% ad valorem duty plus a minimum import price (~$0.38/W) on polysilicon and covered derivative solar products, effective December 4, 2026**, replacing the solar cell/module safeguard that **expired February 2026** (Federal Register 2026-16400; Norton Rose Fulbright; Hunton; EY Tax News 2026-1695; Sheppard Mullin).
- Metals Section 232 has been restructured to apply to the **full customs value of imported product including derivative articles** (White & Case; Phillips Lytle) — directly relevant to tracker steel content.
- **Tariffs are already a material P&L item:** Q2 FY26 results include **~$99M of tariffs, net**; Q3 FY26 ~**$53M**; the Q2 FY27 release references **$99M / $99M / $67M** tariff-related amounts across periods. FY27 guidance is **$4.2–4.45B revenue**, with gross margin sensitivity to "steel, freight and diesel costs," partly offset by "tariff recoveries" (Q1 FY27 shareholder letter).
- Transmission channel: higher module + steel costs raise installed system cost, which pressures US project FIDs and therefore tracker bookings — a demand risk even where NXT successfully passes through cost.

**Risk 2 — Middle East conflict escalation vs. the Saudi JV and MENA backlog. Probability: Moderate (conflict is active) | Impact: High**

- Geopolitical news (mid-Nov 2026) confirms an **active US–Iran conflict** and a broader Middle East war; VP Vance has stated the US does not intend to remain "for years," while Tehran keeps diplomacy open (Bloomberg, 11/17/26; CNBC 11/16/26 on the Trump–Xi–Putin summit agenda including "the Middle East war" and trade policy).
- NXT's MENA growth strategy is **physically anchored in Saudi Arabia** (Jeddah plant, Abunayyan JV), with counterparties that are substantially **government-linked (PIF-backed) developers**. Exposure runs through: project scheduling, Red Sea shipping/insurance, personnel safety, and payment/force-majeure risk.
- Mitigant: localization reduces freight and tariff exposure and aligns with Saudi local-content mandates — the same JV that raises geopolitical risk also reduces trade-policy risk.

**Risk 3 — US tax-credit / regulatory regime risk given 77% US revenue concentration. Probability: Moderate–High | Impact: High**

- Utility-scale solar economics depend on §48E/§45Y credits and **domestic-content / foreign-entity-of-concern (FEOC) thresholds**, which determine whether customers can claim the full credit and thus their appetite for a given tracker supply chain. Any tightening of FEOC rules, guidance, or credit value hits NXT's order book with a lag of 2–4 quarters.
- Management's FY30 targets (**>$7.5B revenue, >$1.6B adj. EBITDA, >$8.00 adj. EPS**) assume substantial non-US growth, i.e., the company is implicitly betting that international diversification outruns US policy risk (Capital Markets Day, 11/17/26).

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation**
- **Securities class action (N.D. Cal., No. 5:24-cv-09467)** — filed 2024 alleging §§10(b)/20(a) violations over project delays and revenue guidance. **On March 9, 2026 the Court granted defendants' Rule 12(b)(6) motion to dismiss the amended complaint; the action has concluded** (Kessler Topaz case page; govinfo court order PDF). **Resolved in the company's favor — this is a positive, not a live contingency.**
- **Product warranty** remains the principal recurring contingency (warranty reserves per 10-Q notes), plus ordinary-course contract/construction claims from EPC customers.
- **No evidence found** of DOJ, SEC enforcement, FCPA, or AML investigations against NXT in available sources.

**Regulatory / trade administration**
- Section 232 duty **classification and origin determinations** for tracker components and the **recovery/drawback claims** NXT has been booking (tariff recoveries benefited Q1 FY27 gross margin) are an audit-sensitive area — CBP review of recovery claims is a realistic downside.
- **Domestic content certification** and FEOC supply-chain traceability for customers' tax-credit eligibility: a documentation failure becomes a customer dispute and reputational issue.
- **UFLPA / forced-labor diligence** remains a live diligence standard for any solar supply chain (steel, electronics, cells), particularly for product flowing through third-party contract manufacturers in Asia.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags (watch items, not findings)

1. **FCPA / anti-bribery (Medium):** Saudi JV with a local partner selling to government-linked developers and sovereign-backed utilities — third-party/intermediary risk is structural. No disclosed issue.
2. **Sanctions screening (Medium, rising):** Active Iran conflict and expanding US Treasury campaign against Iranian networks (OFAC "Operation Economic Outcast") raise counterparty-screening obligations across MENA and East Asia procurement. NXT has no disclosed Iran operations.
3. **Customs/tariff recovery (Medium):** Meaningful tariff-recovery income in gross margin is inherently a regulatory judgment.
4. **Export controls (Low):** Tracker and software hardware is largely non-controlled technology; limited ITAR/EAR exposure.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Legal risk is *below average* for the sector — the only material securities matter was dismissed in March 2026, and no government investigations were surfaced. The elevated component is **policy and trade concentration**: 77% of revenue sits in a single jurisdiction undergoing the most active tariff restructuring in decades (Section 232 polysilicon + minimum import price regime effective 12/4/26, restructured metals tariffs), with quantified tariff drag already near $99M in a single quarter. Layered onto that is a **fresh, capital-intensive MENA bet (Saudi JV, Jeddah plant) executed into an active regional war**. Diversification into India, LatAm and MENA lowers long-run trade-policy risk but raises near-term execution and country risk.
