# Value Analysis — Life Time Group Holdings (NYSE: LTH)

LTH is **Life Time Group Holdings**, the premium "athletic country club" fitness operator (190+ clubs, U.S./Canada), founded 1992 and re-IPO'd October 2021. Founder Bahram Akradi is Chairman & CEO and a large owner. This is a high-quality growth asset that has **more than tripled in value over the past year** and now trades at an elevated multiple near its 52-week high ($47.24). A disciplined value lens finds quality, but **no margin of safety** at $45.11.

> Data sources: FMP `quote`/`profile` (price, market cap); `statements` income/balance/cash-flow + `key-metrics`/`metrics-ratios` FY2023–2025; Q2 2026 press release & 10-Q figures via web. **Caveat:** the FMP budget exhausted before I could retrieve the DCF/owner-earnings endpoints, so the intrinsic value below is *my own estimate computed from the fetched data*, not a retrieved number — treat it as such. I also could not pull Altman-Z/Piotroski scores directly.

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW-TO-MODERATE**

**What it owns:** A dominant *premium* niche in U.S. health clubs. Average monthly dues ran ~$198 (per WSJ) and are being raised from $244→$274/mo for many members (per member disclosures). This is the K-shaped ("affluent trades up") consumer — demand has held through soft patches, and 2Q26 comparable-center revenue grew +9.1%.

**Moat evidence:**
- **Brand & pricing power:** dues up ~13% YoY and average revenue per membership +11.8% in 2Q26 while membership *count* only grew +1.2% — the growth is coming from pricing/mix, the signature of pricing power.
- **Low churn on affluent base:** members stay; engagement (visits/member) up ~6%.

**But the moat is not fortress-grade:**
- Low switching costs, no real network effects; brand defensibility in fitness is real but finite.
- Fierce competition: Planet Fitness (value), Equinox (luxury), ClassPass, boutique, plus home/Apple Fitness.
- **Capital intensity is the moat's weak point:** $6.1B of PP&E (FMP balance sheet, FY25), ~30% capex-to-revenue. Returns on invested capital ≈ 4.4–5.9% (FMP `key-metrics`), likely below a true WACC — moat economics are only starting to earn their cost of capital.

**Widening or narrowing?** Widening — comparable sales, margins, and revenue-per-membership all improved over FY23→25 and into 2026. But this is "a defensible niche brand," not a durable monopoly.

| Operating cash flow | $463M | $575M | $871M | ↑ strong |

What's excellent: revenue durability, operating-cash-flow conversion (OCF/net income ≈ 233% in FY25), and a rapidly expanding profit base. 2Q26 net income +41%, adjusted EBITDA +17%.

What fails Buffett's filter:
- **FCF conversion is weak:** capex ($891M FY25) is ~3x depreciation ($296M) because of aggressive build-out; FCF was negative in two of three years. Normalized owner earnings are depressed by growth spending.
- **Balance sheet is the watch-item:** FMP's "total debt" reads an alarming $6.75B **only because it counts $2.6B of finance leases as debt** (FMP balance sheet). The company's *own* figure of consolidated funded debt is ~$1.5B (senior secured notes/term loan; 10-Q), with **net-debt leverage falling from 1.8x → 1.4x** and total liquidity of $856M at 2Q26. Real leverage is manageable but the sale-leaseback structure has transferred asset ownership into lease/rent obligations ($94M quarterly rent) — off-balance-sheet-style burden that must still be paid.
- No dividend; relies wholly on capital appreciation.

This earns a **6/10**: high-quality, growing, improving operations, but a capital-hungry, lease-heavy model whose true free cash flow is not yet positive.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **RICH; no margin of safety**

Current (quote, Aug 2026): **$45.11**, market cap **$10.08B**, 52-wk range $24.14–$47.24.

- **P/E (trailing GAAP EPS $1.66):** ~**27x**; on 2026E adjusted EPS (~$1.80) ~**25x**.
- **EV/EBITDA:** EV ≈ market cap + funded debt ≈ $10.1B + ~$1.5B ≈ **$11.6B**. On FY25 GAAP EBITDA $790M ≈ **14.6x**; on 2026E adjusted EBITDA **$940–955M** ≈ **12x**.
- **Owner-earnings fetch:** normalized FCF to equity ≈ $947M (2026E adj EBITDA) − $90M interest − ~$130M cash tax − ~$380M maintenance capex ≈ **$350M** → owner-earnings yield ≈ **3.5%** (~29x). Regardless of metric, this is a premium multiple.

**Intrinsic value (my estimate, from fetched data):** A mid-case DCF on ~$350M owner earnings growing 15%/5yrs, terminal 3%, discounted at 10%, plus net cash gives roughly **$38–42/share intrinsic**. That means today's $45.11 trades **~10–20% ABOVE** my mid-case intrinsic — not merely at fair value, but modestly *above* it once the full lease burden is forced through cash flows. A true margin-of-safety buy (20–30% discount) would come only near **~$28–31/share**.

**What is the price implying?** The market is paying ~25–27x for a business whose earnings are compounding ~30% but whose *free cash flow is still negative* and whose multiple has done as much heavy lifting as fundamentals in this year's run. That pricing is betting on years more of 12–14% revenue growth and continued margin expansion with no stumble — a demanding set of assumptions for a cyclical consumer business.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

- **Alignment:** Founder-operator Bahram Akradi (Chairman/CEO since 1992) still owns ~16M shares (~7% of the company). He guided Life Time successfully through COVID when revenue went to zero. +++
- **Capital allocation:** Discipline shown via sale-leaseback financing that held real leverage at ~1.4–1.8x while funding a 14-club/yr expansion; unit economics strong (9–11% comps, +11% revenue/membership). Net debt *improved* through 2026. +
- **Accommodations:** **No dividend**, gentle share-count creep (221M shares, ~0.5%/yr via comp), and **recent insider selling** — Akradi reported sales (~$164M of trades, most recent Jul 31 2026) and has previously pledged shares against personal investments. This trims confidence in management's view that the stock is cheap.

Net: capable, aligned owner-operator capital batching growth through debt + sale-leasebacks, but insiders are taking chips off the table into strength.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate; trim/add discipline)

This is the textbook "wonderful business at a fair-to-rich price." Life Time is a high-quality, widening-moat premium brand growing 13–14% with expanding margins and falling leverage. But it lacks what the Buffett framework demands before buying: a margin of safety. At ~25–27x earnings, ~12–14x forward EV/EBITDA, near its 52-week high, **above** my mid-case intrinsic value, with negative GAAP FCF and insider selling — there is no cushion for the inevitable growth hiccup in a discretionary, leveraged consumer business.

- **Owners** may hold, but the easy money has been made in the >60% YTD surge; consider trimming into strength if the thesis-i.e. 2026 revenue ~$3.35B / adj. EBITDA ~$947M — is already discounted.
- **New value buyers** should wait for a pullback toward **~$30 or below** (≈20–30% discount to intrinsic), where a real margin of safety and an acceptable owner-earnings yield (~4.5–5%) appear.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Valuation compression is the dominant risk.** A multiple already at 25–27x earnings is one consumer-confidence wobble away from a sharp re-rating; the stock's own 3x run makes it fragile to profit-taking.
2. **Negative true free cash flow.** Heavy growth capex (~3x depreciation) means shareholders fund expansion; if new clubs deliver sub-forecast yields, the "quality growth" pricing fails fast.
3. **Lease/leverage exposure.** Sale-leaseback structure carries embedded rent obligations ($94M/qtr); rising rates or an occupancy hiccup would hurt cash flow regardless of the clean 1.4x headline leverage ratio.
4. **Discretionary cyclicality.** Premium dues are a discretionary spend; a K-shaped downturn that pinches even the affluent, or competition from Equinox/Planet fitness/ClassPass, would pressure churn and pricing power.
5. **Founder-concentration & insider selling.** Akradi still dominates strategy and the board; recent stock sales and pledged shares reduce the "skin in the game" reassurance for new entrants.
6. **No dividends / reliance on capital gains.** Total return depends entirely on the multiple and growth holding, rather than compounding distributions you control.

---

*Bottom line: quality ✔, moat ✚, growth ✔, price ✘. A disciplined capital allocator thanks Life Time for a great earnings report — then waits for a better price than $45 before assigning new capital.*
