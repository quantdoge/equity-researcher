## Non-Financial / Governance Risk Assessment — Life Time Group Holdings (NYSE: LTH)

**Company:** Life Time Group Holdings, Inc. — premium athletic country club / fitness operator, founded 1992, HQ Chanhassen, MN, ~190 clubs across the U.S. and Canada, ~52,000 employees, consumer-cyclical leisure. Market cap ≈ **$10.1B**, price $45.11, beta 1.5 (source: FMP `company/profile-symbol`).

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Weak-to-Adequate** (leaning Weak)

**Evidence of concentration:**
- **Founder/Chairman/CEO duality** — Bahram Akradi is simultaneously founder (1992), Chairman, and CEO, and has held combined roles since 1996, a 34.5-year tenure. This is the classic power-concentration risk (FMP `company/company-executives`; Life Time IR board page). He is **also Chairman of Northern Oil & Gas (NYSE: NOG)** — a second public chairman post (company profile / NOG board page).
- **Heavy, long-tenured sponsor presence on the board.** Multiple multi-year directors are active private-equity principals of the pre-IPO sponsors: John G. Danhakl (Managing Partner, Leonard Green & Partners — LGP, director since 2015), Kris/John Galashan (LGP partner, director since 2015), Jonathan Cosset (senior/CIO of TPG, director since 2015), Paul Hackwell (TPG consumer lead, director since 2015), and David Landau (LNK Partners, director since 2015). Sponsor-affiliated seats have been held ~11 years (FMP IR board page).
- **Defensible positives:** A reasonable cohort of independent non-sponsor directors with relevant credentials exists (e.g., a former PwC partner and former SEC accountant, a global digital-retail executive, a family-office/fitness-operator background). Gender diversity is not a differentiator. Several directors hold multiple outside public board seats (Danson IQVIA/Mister Car Wash; Galashan Mister Car Wash; Laschi NOG; Hackwell VIK; Alben ABG), which raises an overboarding/attention concern.

**Net:** Governance is dominated by a founder-CEO who also chairs a second public company, with a deep PE-affiliated board presence. Not fatally weak — independence exists and there is a reasonable audit/nominating cohort — but concentration is material.

---

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **No SEC/DOJ enforcement action** was confirmed for LTH in my research. This is the honest position; I found no open federal securities-enforcement matter.
- **Active civil litigation — child safety (HIGH).** A Dallas family sued Life Time on **March 27, 2026** (Dallas County District Court) alleging their child (under 6) was sexually abused over an extended period at the **Kid's Academy childcare** at the Highland Park, TX club; relief sought **in excess of $1M**; a 12-year-old was arrested in connection. Life Time has not commented beyond "safety... is part of the company's culture" (Sorley-Dallas News, Apr 3, 2026).
- **Civil-rights complaint (active).** **May 12, 2026**, the Council on American-Islamic Relations (CAIR Maryland) publicly called on Life Time to investigate after a ~18-year Muslim Arab member of the Columbia, MD club reported anti-Muslim threats, harassment, and retaliatory suspension; CAIR demanded an external investigation and reinstatement (CAIR press release, May 12, 2026).
- **Historical resolved matters:** TCPA text-messaging class actions settled for up to ~$15M (2015); EEOC pregnancy-discrimination suit settled for $86,000 plus relief (2017). These indicate recurring regulatory/consumer and employment-exposure patterns.
- **Financing/filing activity:** Life Time's SEC filing history shows repeated debt/credit activity — the IR filings page and 8-K search confirms **34 Form 8-K filings** and a **Aug 13, 2026 8-K (sixteenth amendment to its credit facility)** — a leveraged financing cadence worth monitoring for covenant/refinancing risk (FMP `secFilings/search-by-symbol`; IR filings page).

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Children-in-care (Kid's Academy / child care) operations.** Childcare is a core member-facing product across 190+ clubs and now sits at the center of a high-profile criminal-linked sexual-abuse lawsuit. A single event materially threatens reputation, litigation spend, and insurance/regulatory status across a network. **Highest severity.**
2. **Key-person / succession dependence on the founder-CEO.** Akradi is the company's identity, controller of strategy, and also chairman at another listed issuer; there is no disclosed transition plan. Succession is a real single-point-of-failure for strategy and brand.
3. **Heavy real-estate/lease and debt structure.** Historically ~40% of its footprint was owned/ground-lease vs. leased, and the constant high cadence of credit-facility amendments (→ the "sixteenth" amendment in Aug 2026) points to a high-fixed-cost, refinancing-concentration balance sheet and concentration depending on rent/sales maintained.

(Secondary: 52,000-person workforce → labor, supervision, and wage exposure, given the pending discrimination matters; and a digital layer — Life Time Digital — that adds data-privacy/consent exposure, with GDPR/TCPA claims already in its past.)

---

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **High**

- The **Dallas Kid's Academy sexual-abuse lawsuit** and CAIR's public discrimination/threat allegations landed in the same 2026 spring window. Both target the member-family and minority-community trust that the brand sells. Public-document litigation is ongoing and unresolved.
- **Member-sentiment risk** — the fitness-brand is consumer-premium; childcare-safety and discrimination incidents hit exactly the "family-friendly premium" promise (income/trust) Life Time trades on. Social-messaging and media forward flow around a case like this is disproportionately damaging because it references children.
- Offsetting: strong national brand, positive event portfolio (Leadville Trail 100 returned to a community recovering from the Willow Fire), and strong Q2 2026 results (revenue $866.0M, +14%; net income $101.4M, +41%; EPS $0.46–$0.48) will sustain goodwill in the short-term. That does not cancel the child-safety overhang.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: The combination of (a) an active, high-visibility child-safety / sexual-abuse civil matter naming the Company's flagship childcare product, (b) a concurrent public discrimination/harassment complaint, (c) founder-CEO-key dismissal + heavy sponsor sitting and ongoing PE exit selling, and (d) a high-fixed-cost, finance-heavy balance sheet flags a net **High** (not Critical) — there is no confirmed regulatory enforcement, fraud indication, or cyber-event; liquidity, Q2-2026, and an independent-board cohort provide offsetting strength.

---

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Child-safety litigation — Kid's Academy (Highland Park).** Active, unresolved, directly tied to a criminal arrest and >$1M claim against the Company for extended child abuse in Company childcare. Escalate for legal, insurance, and disclosure implications; monitor for follow-on claims at other facilities.
2. **CAIR discrimination complaint (Columbia, MD).** Public call for an external investigation into alleged anti-Muslim harassment and retaliatory suspension of an 18-year member — a title-VII/public-accommodation and member-retention event the company is still responding to.
3. **Extreme insider-sale concentration** — ~$110M+ of sponsor/director shares sold in the last two quarters (Q2-Q3 2026), including 3.38M-share Form 144 — alongside unchanged sponsor board control and a post-IPO financing replenishment cycle. Watch for succession timing / financing stress correlation.
4. **Succession concentration** — founder-CEO age (~64), dual-chairman roles, and no public transition plan: key-person discontinuity risk.
