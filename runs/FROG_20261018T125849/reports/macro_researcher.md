*Data as of Oct 16–18, 2026. All rates/CPI from FRED.*

### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional, tilting risk-off (rate-shock regime)**

The regime has changed materially in the last six weeks. The Fed cut the funds rate to **3.75%** (Sept 2026), down from the 5.33% cycle peak — but the market has since moved *against* it. The **2Y at 4.65% now sits ~90bp ABOVE the fed funds rate**, meaning the forward curve is pricing **hikes, not cuts** ("markets out-hawking the Fed"). The **10Y has risen ~71bp since May 1 (4.39% → 5.10%)**, peaking at 5.31% on Oct 5.

The critical diagnostic: **10Y breakevens are 2.35%, down from 2.50% in early May.** Essentially the *entire* rise in nominal yields is a **real-rate shock** — the 10Y TIPS real yield has gone from **1.91% (May 1) to 2.78% (Oct 15), +87bp**. This is a pure discount-rate event, not an inflation-expectations event. That distinction matters enormously for a company like FROG: rising breakevens are survivable for a firm with pricing power, but rising *real* rates cut the present value of every dollar of 2032 earnings.

Meanwhile the **curve is bear-steepening** (30Y 5.37% > 10Y 4.99% > 2Y 4.65%, 2s10s +34bp, no inversion). Upward-sloping = no recession signal from the curve, but the term-premium expansion and rising oil point to fiscal/credibility concerns rather than growth optimism.

**The real economy is fine:** Real GDP Q2 2026 = $24,408bn, **+2.2% YoY** with QoQ SAAR of ~2.2%, and unemployment at **4.2%** (range 4.1–4.5% for a year). This is a "no landing," not a downturn. The problem for FROG is not demand — it's the price the market pays for that demand.

Its equity value is overwhelmingly terminal-value-weighted — the textbook definition of a long-duration asset. Beta is only 1.24, but **rate duration is far higher than beta implies.**

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL (negative skew)**

The two channels diverge cleanly:
- **Operating channel: FAVOURABLE.** 2.2% growth, 4.2% unemployment, no recession, AI tailwind, NDR 121%, guidance raised.
- **Discount-rate channel: UNFAVOURABLE.** +87bp real yields, curve pricing hikes, 30Y at 5.37%.

For a 14–15x EV/sales name the discount-rate channel wins near-term price action — which is exactly what the last week demonstrated. I rate it **Neutral** rather than Unfavourable only because the fundamental momentum (+29% growth) is strong enough to offset a portion of multiple compression, and because FROG's net-cash, ILS-cost-shielded model is more rate-resilient than a levered peer. **If the 10Y clears 5.50%, this rating flips to Unfavourable.**

### 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**BEAR — "The Fed hikes into a 15x-sales multiple" (higher probability than the market assigns).**
The Sept CPI re-acceleration to 3.72% proves sticky; the market's hawkish repricing is validated when the Fed *actually hikes* in H1 2027. Real 10Y goes to 3.3%, nominal to 5.75–6.0%, breakevens unchanged. FROG de-rates from ~14.6x to ~9x EV/sales **even while beating and raising on Nov 5**. Math: $650m × 9x + ~$0.7bn net cash ≈ **$6.5bn, or ~$54/share, −36%**. The danger is that this happens *despite* flawless operating execution — the operator will feel the business is going well while the stock halves. Watch the 30Y: a move through 5.60% would signal a term-premium/fiscal event rather than a cyclical one, which would hit long-duration software indiscriminately.

**BULL — "September CPI was an oil/tariff blip."**
If Oct/Nov CPI rolls back toward 3.2% and the 10Y retraces to 4.50% (real 2.25%), FROG's Nov 5 print (guide $164–166m, likely beat-and-raise) re-rates the stock to 17–18x forward sales → **$115–125, +35–45%**. FROG is a high-beta call option on the front end of the yield curve.
