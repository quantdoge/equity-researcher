## ESG ANALYSIS — JFrog Ltd. (NASDAQ: FROG)
**Sector:** Technology / Software–Application (DevOps & software supply chain platform) | **Market cap ~$10.2–10.4B** | FY2025 revenue $531M (+24%) | ~1,800 FTE | Israeli-incorporated, US-listed | Data as of most recent filings (FY2025 10-K, FY2025 Sustainability Report, 2026 Proxy)

---

### 1. ESG Materiality Map

For an asset-light, high-multiple SaaS platform, traditional "E" metrics are largely immaterial; the financial stakes sit in **product security, talent, and board accountability**.

| Factor | Materiality | Why it moves cash flow / cost of capital |
|---|---|---|
| **Product & supply-chain security** (S/G) | **Critical** | JFrog sits in the CI/CD path of ~7,000+ enterprise customers. A vulnerability or compromise in Artifactory is a *systemic* trust event → churn, federal procurement bans, litigation, multiple compression. |
| **Board independence, chair/CEO duality, board classification** (G) | **High** | Entrenchment features raise the governance discount applied to founder-led names; classified boards blunt activism/M&A optionality. |
| **Talent retention & Israel concentration** (S) | **High** | ~all core R&D in Israel/India; IDF reserve mobilisation and AI-driven developer-tool disruption are live operating risks. Revenue/employee ≈ $295K leaves little slack for attrition. |
| **Compensation alignment / SBC dilution** (G) | **High** | FY2025 GAAP operating loss $91.9M vs non-GAAP operating income $92M — a ~$184M SBC wedge. Dilution is the primary governance-to-valuation transmission channel. |
| **Scope 3 cloud/energy emissions** (E) | **Low–Medium** | Emissions concentrated in AWS/Azure/GCP purchased compute; cost-optimisation already managed, so E-risk and margin risk are aligned. |
| **Data privacy / cyber regulation** (S/G) | **Medium** | GDPR, DORA/NIS2 exposure via regulated customers, EU Cyber Resilience Act (2027). |
| **Direct emissions, water, biodiversity, community** (E/S) | **Low** | Office + cloud footprint only; <5 tCO2e per $M revenue (analyst est.). |

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- **Footprint:** Asset-light. Sunnyvale HQ, Israeli and Indian R&D centres, plus public-cloud hosting. Company states it conducts an **annual GHG inventory covering Scope 1, 2 and 3** across operations and value chain; **absolute tonnage figures were not retrievable within this review's data budget** — a disclosure gap relative to CDP-grade peers (no TCFD-indexed quantitative table published in the web-accessible report).
- **Estimated intensity:** Order of magnitude 1,000–3,000 tCO2e Scope 1+2, i.e. **<5 tCO2e per $M revenue** — among the lowest intensities in any sector.
- **Transition risk:** No carbon-tax or CBAM exposure (no physical goods); no stranded assets; no EU ETS liabilities.
- **Physical climate risk:** Minimal — no manufacturing, no water-intensive operations; residual risk is data-centre siting/energy price, largely passed through by hyperscalers.
- **Controversies:** None identified (no spills, fines, or remediation liabilities).
- **Offsetting positive:** Product-level efficiency story (public-package caching, storage de-duplication, compute optimisation) reduces customers' upstream footprint; credible but unquantified "avoided emissions" claim — treat as marketing until MRV'd.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Negatives / material exposures**
1. **Product security event (the dominant S risk).** CVE-2026-82329 — critical authentication bypass in Artifactory, **CVSS 9.8**, default configuration allows an unauthenticated network attacker to obtain admin privileges. **CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog (3 Sep 2026)** with a federal patch deadline of 5 Sep 2026; honeypot telemetry shows attackers minting admin tokens, enumerating federated access topologies and, in some cases, **creating backdoor users**. This directly threatens JFrog's "source of truth" positioning with federal, financial-services and healthcare accounts.
2. **Geographic workforce concentration.** Primary R&D in Israel; prior 10-Ks disclose **IDF reservist call-ups** disrupting operations. Renewed conflict escalation is an unhedgeable continuity risk for product velocity.
3. **Disclosure gaps.** No public turnover rate, no EEO-1-style diversity metrics, no gender pay-gap data. Human-capital narrative is qualitative only — weak for a $10B company whose only real asset is engineers.
4. **AI-era talent substitution.** Agentic coding tools compress the addressable DevOps seat count; the 2026 supply-chain report itself flags **451% growth in AI-agent skills as a new attack surface** — reputationally favourable but signals customer workflow change.

**Positives**
- 99% security-awareness training completion; 420,000 malicious open-source packages flagged (2025) — genuine public-good contribution rooted in open-source heritage.
- >2,700 employee volunteer hours; employee-led CSR committees; flexible-work policy reducing commuting emissions.
- No layoffs disclosed at JFrog (peer Israeli SaaS names cut 15–20% in 2025–26); workforce stable at ~1,800.
- Zero identified labour-relations, safety, discrimination, or supply-chain-labour controversies; no unionised workforce issues.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.0 / 10**

| Item | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Chair/CEO duality** | **Negative.** Co-founder **Shlomi Ben Haim is both Chairman and CEO** — confirmed in the 2026 DEF 14A. Mitigant: **Yossi Sela serves as Lead Independent Director.** |
| **Board independence** | Reasonable. Estimated 6 of 9 directors independent; three founder seats (Ben Haim, CTO Yoav Landman, co-founder Frederic Simon). Audit and Compensation Committees described as fully independent. |
| **Board structure** | **Negative.** **Classified (staggered) board** — only four Class III directors stand in 2026, for terms to 2029. Entrenches founders; reduces M&A optionality. |
| **Share class** | **Positive.** Single class of ordinary shares (NIS 0.01); **no dual-class super-voting structure** — unusual and creditable among founder-led IPOs. No poison pill identified. |
| **Compensation alignment** | **Mixed.** Cash STI 0–200% payout range (good); new **Stock Ownership Guidelines effective 10 Feb 2026** with a five-year compliance window (good). But 2026 replaced one-year PSUs with **time-based RSUs vesting ratably over five years** — improved horizon, **reduced performance-linkage**. SBC of ~$184M against $531M revenue remains the core dilution governance issue. |
| **Shareholder rights** | **Above US norm in one respect:** Israeli Companies Law requires **binding shareholder votes** on CEO and CTO compensation (Proposals 5 & 6, 2026 AGM) and on director pay — structurally stronger than a US advisory say-on-pay. |
| **Audit & ethics** | **Strong.** EY (Kost, Forer, Gabbay & Kasierer) as auditor; quarterly ethics/integrity audits by Internal Audit under the CLO, quarterly anonymous-hotline testing, annual Code of Conduct training, Audit Committee report to the full Board quarterly. |
| **Refreshment** | Positive — Sigal Zarmi (ex-Berkshire Hathaway Specialty Insurance CIO) added Nov 2025. |
| **External grade** | Boardroom Alpha board grade **B−**. |

**Verdict:** institutionally sound controls and unusually shareholder-friendly comp votes, but offset by combined Chair/CEO and a staggered board — a structure that in a founder-led, high-multiple name is worth a real, if modest, discount.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **Israel:** Israeli Companies Law governs — binding shareholder approval of CEO/CTO pay, no US-style proxy access, staggered boards permitted. Also Israeli Privacy Protection Law for domestic data.
- **EU CSRD:** **Currently outside mandatory scope** — the non-EU parent test (EU turnover >€150M) sits above JFrog's estimated EMEA revenue; the practical pressure is *customer-driven* Scope 3/security questionnaires, not statutory. Watch item if EU revenue scales.
- **EU Cyber Resilience Act (from 2027)** and **NIS2 / DORA** (financial-services customers): JFrog's self-hosted products fall in scope of CRA "products with digital elements" → **certification and documentation cost**, partly offset by the AppTrust/SBOM compliance franchise.
- **US SEC climate rule:** stayed April 2024; SEC moved to withdraw its defence in 2025 — **de minimis near-term exposure**. California SB 253/261 thresholds (>$1B revenue) not yet met.
- **US federal procurement:** **CISA KEV listing is the binding regulatory consequence** — exploited-vulnerability remediation deadlines for FCEB agencies cascade into contractor requirements; EO 14028 software supply-chain attestation (SBOM) is simultaneously a tailwind for the Curation/Xray suite.
- **CBAM / EU Taxonomy / ESG-linked debt covenants:** **Not applicable** — no exported carbon-intensive goods and no material debt (net-cash balance sheet), hence **zero sustainability-linked loan covenant risk**.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Assessed against large-cap software peers: environmental risk is best-in-class low; social risk is above-average *because of product-security and human-capital concentration*, not because of labour or community conduct; governance is a split verdict — strong committees, ethics infrastructure, single-class shares and binding comp votes, weakened by Chair/CEO duality and a staggered board. No material environmental or social liability, no enforcement history, and a published (if quantitatively thin) sustainability report with Board-committee oversight and a Chief Sustainability Officer reporting to the CEO keep it out of Laggard territory. It is not a Leader: no TCFD-quantified emissions table, no turnover/diversity data, no third-party assurance.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

- **Not a thesis driver either way.** FROG will never screen out of an ESG mandate on carbon (intensity is negligible, no CBAM/ETS/stranded-asset exposure) and carries no sustainability-linked financing or environmental remediation tail. On the exclusion side, the only live trigger is **controversy-based** (a KEV-listed exploited vulnerability in the core product) — and that is priced as cybersecurity/operational risk by the market, not as ESG.
- **The real ESG-to-valuation channel is governance and trust, and it is asymmetric.** A ~$10B market cap on $531M revenue with +97% 1Y TSR means the equity is a *trust multiple*; a second exploited-Artifactory CVE, a federal agency breach traced to the platform, or a founder-succession shock would compress that multiple faster than any fundamental deterioration. In that scenario ESG screens (MSCI controversy penalties, Sustainalytics incident-driven risk ratings) *mechanically amplify* the de-rating via passive and ESG-constrained flows. This is the key monitoring trigger.
- **Governance discount is real but bounded:** combined Chair/CEO plus a staggered board argues for a modest structural discount and for engagement/voting against Proposal 1 (or at minimum abstention) at the 20 May 2026 AGM; conversely, binding Israeli-law comp votes and a single share class are better than most US founder-led peers.
- **Recommendation to the investment committee:** treat ESG as **Neutral with a downside tail**, do not adjust the target multiple for environmental or social factors, but (a) add "Artifactory CVE / KEV listing" and "founder succession" to the risk register as non-financial triggers with 2–3x normal weighting, and (b) monitor the promised CSRD-ready emissions quantification and human-capital disclosure — a move to third-party assured, quantified disclosure would be a mild positive catalyst for ESG-constrained ownership.
