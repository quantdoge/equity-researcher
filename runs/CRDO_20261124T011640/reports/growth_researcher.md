# CRDO — Credo Technology Group
### Growth Investment Analysis | Price $122.46 | Market Cap $23.0B | Nov 23, 2026

---

## 1. Growth Story

Credo is the picks-and-shovels monopoly of AI cluster connectivity. Its proprietary SerDes/DSP architecture powers Active Electrical Cables (AECs) that carry the bulk of scale-out traffic inside AI data centers — roughly **88% AEC share** in AI data-center interconnect — and it is now extending from copper into optical DSPs, PCIe/Ethernet AECs, and SerDes chiplets. Revenue *tripled* to $1.34B in FY26 (+206%) with non-GAAP net income up 5x to $662M, and management is guiding **>85% revenue growth in FY27** — yet the stock has collapsed 60% from its June peak of $302.52 to $122.46, leaving it at ~16x trailing sales and ~12.6x FY28 consensus EPS. This is a violent multiple de-rating against *rising*, not falling, estimates — the classic dislocation this framework is built to exploit.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

**The opportunity:** Every AI rack is becoming a wiring problem. As clusters scale to 100K+ GPUs, the electrical/optical connectivity bill of materials per rack is rising faster than compute content. Credo's served market stacks four layers:
1. **AECs** (current hero product) — scale-out and scale-up copper, moving from 400G→800G→1.6T
2. **Optical DSPs / SiPh** — management now describes a portfolio "from millimeters to kilometers"
3. **PCIe / Ethernet AECs** — adjacent sockets in storage and front-end networks
4. **SerDes IP & chiplets** — high-margin licensing to hyperscalers designing custom silicon

**Penetration:** Credo captures ~88% of AI AEC share, yet at ~$2.47B FY27E revenue it is still a sub-$3B vendor in an AI infrastructure capex pool running into the hundreds of billions. The incremental TAM is not share-gain — it is **socket expansion**: 1.6T AECs, optical DSP attach, and the shift of copper from rack-to-rack into rack-internal scale-up. Management has highlighted five hyperscaler relationships plus a broadening neocloud base, which is precisely the customer set that determines whether the H2 FY27 ramp exists.

**Honest caveat:** I could not verify a hard third-party SAM figure in this budget window. The bull case does not depend on a specific TAM number — it depends on AEC attach rates per GPU holding through the 1.6T generation.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Dec 1, 2026 — Q2 FY27 results (binary).** Consensus $531.8M revenue / $1.29 EPS, against guidance of $525–535M. The single number that matters is the **Q3 guide**. Credo's >85% FY27 growth target requires ~$1.46B in H2, i.e. **~$700-730M per quarter** vs. a $530M Q2 guide. A Q3 guide of $650M+ validates the ramp; anything near $600M breaks the annual target and the stock retests $100.
- **Dec 1, 2026 — Marvell (MRVL) reports same day.** Marvell's commentary on copper vs. optics is the cleanest read on competitive intent. Post-settlement, Marvell appears to be pivoting toward optics (its $115B all-stock Lumentum deal), which is bullish for Credo's near-term AEC dominance.
- **Nov 17, 2026 — Marvell patent settlement (already occurred, underappreciated).** Credo settled its AEC patent dispute with Marvell, ending Marvell's patent-office challenges to Credo's IP. Terms undisclosed, but the overhang on the business that drives >90% of growth is removed. **The stock has not re-rated for this.**
- **H2 FY27 — 1.6T AEC qualification and ramp.** MaxLinear is publicly angling for 20-30% of the 1.6T generation; Credo's ability to hold ~80%+ share at 1.6T is the durability test for FY28-29 estimates.
- **H2 FY27 — optical DSP/SiPh revenue contribution.** Management's new "millimeters to kilometers" framing implies M&A or internal ramp into optics. Note: cash fell from $1.4B to $764M in Q1 — **verify what that capital bought**; an optics acquisition would extend the TAM, a buyback would be less strategically valuable.
- **Ongoing — Nvidia leadership resolution.** The AI complex is trading on Jensen Huang's departure and a board CEO search. A credible successor normalizes AI capex sentiment and lifts high-beta connectivity names indiscriminately.

**Why not Strong Buy:** the Dec 1 print is genuinely binary. **Positioning:** Accumulate here. Add aggressively on a Q3 guide above $650M; a sub-$600M guide likely takes the stock to $90-100 where the risk/reward on FY28 EPS is ~10x and I would add again.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Customer concentration (highest-probability killer).** Top 4 customers = 84% of Q1 FY27 revenue; largest = 33%. A single hyperscaler pausing or dual-sourcing a rack program removes a third of revenue overnight. This is the risk that justifies the multiple compression.
2. **Copper-to-optics structural displacement.** AECs are a copper-transition technology. At 1.6T and beyond, co-packaged optics (CPO) and silicon photonics could compress the copper socket. Credo's optical DSP/portfolio expansion is the hedge — but it is unproven and, worse, *unverified* given the unexplained $636M cash decline.
3. **Growth deceleration is real.** Sequential growth has collapsed from 52% to ~10%. If Q3 disappoints, FY27 growth lands near 60-70% rather than 85%, and the "cheap on FY28" math gets pushed out a year.
4. **MaxLinear at 1.6T.** MaxLinear is openly targeting 20-30% share of the next-generation AEC socket. Even partial share loss against a 88% base is a meaningful revenue and pricing headwind.
5. **Gross margin mix erosion.** GAAP GM fell 68.2% → 64.5% in Q1 as lower-margin bundled AEC revenue outgrows high-margin IC/IP revenue. Non-GAAP GM holds ~68%, but the direction of travel caps operating leverage.
6. **AI capex/sentiment cycle + extreme beta (3.29).** Nvidia is searching for a CEO. Any hyperscaler capex digestion headline moves CRDO 15-20% in a session regardless of company fundamentals. The stock fell 53.5% in three months on essentially no company-specific bad news.
7. **Not absolutely cheap.** 16x trailing sales and 57x trailing FCF require the growth to continue. This is a *relative* value argument, not a deep-value one — a further 30-40% multiple compression is entirely possible in an AI bear market.

**Data gaps:** peer multiples are trailing-basis only (ALAB FY ends Dec-25, MRVL Jan-26); headquarters-level job posting data was unavailable (proxy returned an unrelated company); long-dated consensus EPS (FY30, FY31) rests on 1-2 analysts and is not reliable.
