## CRDO — Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd.
**Price $122.46 (Nov 23, 2026) · Market cap $23.05B · EV ~$21.6B (net cash ~$1.14B) · Beta 3.29**

*Fiscal year ends late April/early May. FY2026 ended May 2, 2026. Latest reported quarter: Q1 FY2027 (ended Aug 1, 2026).*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW** (currently widening, durability questionable)

Credo sells high-speed connectivity into AI/cloud data centers: Active Electrical Cables (AECs), SerDes chiplets/retimers, optical DSPs, and IP licensing. The technology rests on proprietary SerDes and DSP designs that deliver high signal integrity at low power — a genuine engineering asset, not a commodity.

**Evidence of a moat:**
- **Intangible assets / IP:** 15+ years of SerDes development; the low-power advantage is the explicit reason hyperscalers qualify Credo AECs in multi-year platform designs.
- **Switching costs:** Design-in cycles for a hyperscaler rack architecture run 12–24 months and are re-qualified only grudgingly; once inside a generation, the vendor typically holds it.
- **Financial proof of pricing power:** Gross margin rose from 57.7% (FY23) → 61.9% (FY24) → 64.8% (FY25) → 68.0% (FY26, GAAP), with non-GAAP GM ~68%. Operating margin went from –19% to **+33.3%** in two years on 7x revenue growth. That is operating leverage from real pricing power, not just volume.
- **ROIC crossed WACC:** 21.3% in FY26 vs. negative in FY23–FY24. This is the first year the business has earned its cost of capital — one year of evidence, not five.

**Why the moat is only Narrow:**
- **The moat has not been tested across a full cycle.** Copacetic margins arrived at the peak of an AI capex boom. There is no evidence yet that Credo can hold 68% gross margins when a hyperscaler dual-sources or a customer insources.
- **Customer concentration is extreme.** Top 10 customers ≈ **90% of revenue**; FY2026 effective revenue was reportedly ~84% from three customers, with four hyperscalers each >10% in Q4 FY26. This is the opposite of pricing power in a negotiation — it is dependence. (Improving directionally: four >10% customers vs. two earlier.)
- **Technology-substitution risk is existential, not marginal.** AECs are copper. Every speed node (448G, 1.6T) shortens copper's reach and improves optics' economics. Credo is hedging with its optical DSP (management "reaffirmed the optical ramp"), but the company is defending a toll road that may be bypassed.
- **Well-capitalized competitors:** Broadcom, Marvell, Astera Labs, and in-house hyperscaler ASIC teams all attack the same socket.

**Verdict on moat trend:** Widened over the last 5 years on execution and IP; the *durability* over the next 5 is the single most uncertain variable in this thesis.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples (on TTM / FY26):** P/E ~46x GAAP (48.8x on FY26 EPS of $2.51), P/B 11.2x, EV/EBITDA ~38x, P/FCF ~50x, **FCF yield 1.8%**. Peer median P/E in the (mega-cap-dominated) tech screen is 27.4x — a ~78% premium, though that peer set is of limited use (note: I could not retrieve Astera Labs / Marvell multiples within budget — a genuine gap).

**Tool output ignored deliberately:** the mechanical DCF returned an intrinsic value of **$6.67B (~$35/share)** because it applied a 5% growth rate to trailing FCF. For a company that just tripled revenue, that model is worse than useless. I substituted a scenario DCF below.

**My scenario DCF** (base owner earnings $520M = TTM GAAP net income + D&A – capex, which properly charges SBC; WACC 10%, given beta 3.3 and cyclicality):

**Sanity check from the forward multiple:** FY27 revenue guidance implies **>85% growth (~$2.47B)**; with Q1 non-GAAP EPS of $1.20 and Q2 consensus of $1.29, a full-year non-GAAP EPS of ~$5.75–6.25 is plausible → **~20–21x FY27E adjusted EPS**. That is not an extreme multiple *if* the guide is met. The stock has already fallen ~47% from its Oct 2, 2026 intraday peak of $231.59 (to $99.86 on Nov 20) — the de-rating has done real work.

**Conclusion:** The price is now approximately **equal to my probability-weighted intrinsic value (~$118)**, or ~4% above it. For a 25% margin of safety, I need ~$88–90 — which is roughly the bear case. **There is no margin of safety at $122.46.**

### 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Positives:**
- Founder-led (CEO William Brennan, since 2008) — owner-operator DNA, deep domain expertise, not a hired-gun turnaround.
- **Zero debt** and a $1.4B cash pile; capital allocation so far has been conservative (bolt-on acquisition creating $122M of intangibles, disciplined in size).
- **Conservative guidance culture:** beat consensus EPS in each of the last six quarters by widening margins (e.g., $0.49 est → $0.67 actual; $0.91 → $1.07), then raised FY27 revenue growth guidance to >85%. That pattern earns credibility.
- Initiated a small buyback ($19M FY26, $26M Q1 FY27) — sensible but token, and arguably irrational while the stock traded above intrinsic value.

**Negatives:**
- **Compensation is the largest capital-allocation decision management makes, and it is being made generously.** SBC of ~$350M annualized against $519M of TTM GAAP net income is a substantial annual transfer from owners to employees. A buyback of $26M against $350M of SBC is a rounding error — net share count still rises ~4–5%/yr.
- Management's own enthusiasm for dilution tells you the equity is viewed as currency, not as something to be purchased cheaply.
- Insider ownership figures were not available to me — a gap I flag rather than guess at.

**Grade: B+.** Honest operators who are excellent technologists and disciplined on the balance sheet; mediocre on owner-oriented compensation.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate; trim oversized positions)

This is a genuinely good business — real IP, 68% gross margins, 33% operating margins at scale, net cash, and a widening moat inside a secular buildout. It is not a bargain.

Applying the discipline that matters: **a fair price is not a buy.** My probability-weighted intrinsic value of ~$118 sits 4% *below* the current $122.46. The bull case ($161) requires 30% FCF growth for five years, flawless optical execution, and no material share loss to optics or Broadcom/Marvell. The bear case ($90) is entirely plausible on a single AI capex digestion year. You are being paid nothing to choose between them.

**Action framework:**
- **New money:** wait. Buy zone **≤$90** (~25% discount to weighted IV; also near the bear case, so you are protected if the cycle turns).
- **Existing holders:** hold a position sized so a 50% drawdown is survivable — this stock has already moved from $232 to $100 in seven weeks and carries a 3.3 beta.
- **Sell triggers:** (a) two consecutive quarters of GAAP gross margin below 62%; (b) a top-3 customer publicly shifting an AEC socket to optics or in-house silicon; (c) FY27 revenue tracking below ~$2.2B; (d) SBC remaining above ~15% of revenue while net share count grows >5%.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Copper-to-optics substitution (highest severity).** AECs are a copper solution in a market where every speed node favors optics. Credo's optical DSP is the hedge, but it competes against Broadcom/Marvell in a market where Credo has less incumbency. If AEC revenue rolls off faster than optical ramps, both my growth and margin assumptions break simultaneously.
2. **Customer concentration.** ~90% of revenue from ten customers and ~84% from three. One hyperscaler re-architecting or dual-sourcing is a 20–30% revenue event, not a 5% one. Pricing power is structurally limited regardless of technology merit.
3. **AI capex cyclicality.** Revenue went +4.8% in FY24, then +206% in FY26. That amplitude cuts both ways. A digestion year would compress both earnings and the multiple (a 20x forward multiple on cut numbers is a very different price).
4. **Gross margin trajectory.** GAAP GM 68.2% → 64.5% → guided ~63.9%. Some of this is acquired-intangible amortization (non-GAAP held at 68%), but mix shift toward AECs and optical modules could be structurally lower-margin than chiplets/IP. Watch the GAAP/non-GAAP gap widening as a sign of serial deal-making.
5. **Dilution.** ~$350M annualized SBC against a $23B market cap is 1.5% of value per year handed to employees, plus 4–5% share count growth. Reported per-share growth materially lags business growth.
6. **Valuation risk asymmetry.** At 11x book and ~46x GAAP earnings, a mere normalization to 30x would be a 35% decline with fundamentals fully intact. The stock's own history ($232 → $100 in 7 weeks) is the cleanest evidence of this risk.

**Data gaps acknowledged:** insider ownership percentages; Astera Labs/Marvell/Broadcom forward multiples for a proper comp table; full details of the FY26 acquisition. None changes the conclusion that the price already reflects the good news.

---

**Bottom line:** Wonderful technology, narrow moat, good-but-not-owner-friendly capital allocation, and a price set at the exact midpoint of a wide range of outcomes. Buffett's rule applies — the first loss is buying a great business at a full price. **Hold, with a $90 buy target.**
