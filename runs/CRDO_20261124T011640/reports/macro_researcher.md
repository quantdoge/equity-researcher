## 1. Macro Environment Summary — Stance: **RISK-OFF / Transitional (late-cycle tightening)**

The US is in an unusual and hostile configuration for long-duration growth equities: **growth is fine, but the Fed has restarted hiking into an energy-driven inflation reacceleration, and the entire rise in long yields is *real*, not inflationary.**

- **Policy has turned hawkish.** Effective fed funds has risen from 3.63% (Jun–Aug 2026) to **3.88% (Oct)**, with the target now 3.75–4.00%. Markets price a **~90–95% probability of a 25bp hike at the Dec 9 FOMC** (CME FedWatch, per AP/Admirals). This is a *re-tightening* cycle, not the easing cycle markets discounted a year ago.
- **The yield rise is a real-rate shock.** 10Y TIPS real yield went **2.20% (Jun 30) → 2.77–2.92% (Nov 16–20), +57bp**, while the 10Y breakeven sits near **2.2%**. Nominal 10Y is **5.13%**, 30Y **5.47%**. Inflation expectations are *anchored*; the discount rate is not. This is the worst possible composition of rate move for a 3.3-beta, high-multiple compounder.
- **Curve is bear-steepening, not signalling growth.** 2s10s at **+39bp** (2Y 4.63%, 10Y 5.13%) — positive slope driven by **term premium/fiscal supply** at the long end (30Y 5.47%), not by an easing-driven bull steepener. Elevated long-end term premium is precisely what compresses long-duration equity multiples.
- **Credit is the live wire.** HY OAS has widened **2.65% (Sep 1) → 3.76% (Nov 20), +111bp**, and **+56bp in the eight days from Nov 12**. Spreads are still historically tight in absolute terms, but the *velocity* of widening is a genuine financial-conditions tightening signal.
- **Growth/labour: no recession.** Real GDP **+2.1% YoY** (Q3'26), QoQ saar ~3.4%; unemployment **4.1%** (Oct), down from 4.5% a year ago. This is what permits the Fed to hike.
- **Inflation is a supply/energy story, not demand.** Headline CPI **3.82%** vs core **~2.6% YoY** (Sep) — a ~120bp gap. But 3-month annualised core is running **~3.7%**, so core momentum is *re-warming modestly* rather than falling. That combination keeps the Fed hawkish even as goods disinflation persists.
- **USD firming.** Broad dollar index **118.69 (Sep 14) → 123.60 (Nov 20), +4.1%**.
- **Trade policy is an active headwind.** A **25% Section 232 tariff** on specified advanced computing chips and *derivative products* was proclaimed **Jan 14, 2026**, layered on China export-licensing cross-currents — directly relevant to a company that designs in San Jose and builds AECs/ICs in China and Mexico.

**CRDO's own tape confirms the macro transmission.** The stock peaked at **$302.52 on Jun 22, 2026** — almost exactly at the trough in 10Y real yields (2.20%) — and is now **$122.46, −59.5%** from that peak (−67.6% intraday at the Nov 20 low of $97.92). It is **only −14.5% YTD** (from $143.22 on Jan 2), i.e. the entire drawdown is a *second-half de-rating*, coincident with a ~100bp rise in the 10Y and a ~57bp rise in real yields. Last week alone: **−15.2% on Nov 20, then +22.6% on Nov 23.** That is a stock trading on macro/liquidity impulse, not on its fundamentals.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

CRDO's business model is a **pure derivative of hyperscaler AI capex**, financed increasingly with debt. That gives four transmission channels, ranked by materiality:

**Channel 1 — Real rates → AI capex financing capacity: NEGATIVE (highly material)**
AI-related debt issuance reached roughly **$500bn by late July 2026** (Goldman Sachs, via bfinance), with Big-5 hyperscaler capex widely forecast **>$600bn in 2026 (+36% YoY)**. A meaningful share of that buildout — particularly neoclouds, data-centre developers and levered GPU lessors — is **credit-financed**. With 10Y at 5.13%, real yields at 2.77% and HY spreads +111bp in eleven weeks, the marginal cost of funding AI infrastructure has risen sharply. CRDO sells AECs and SerDes into exactly these racks. **The macro variable that most determines CRDO's 2027 revenue is not GDP — it is the price and availability of AI project debt.**

**Channel 2 — Discount rate on a long-duration asset: NEGATIVE (highly material)**
Beta **3.29**, market cap $23B on ~807 employees. This is close to the maximum-duration equity profile: cash flows are back-end loaded into 2027–2030 AI ramp expectations. Each 25bp on real yields mechanically compresses the multiple. The empirical linkage is stark — 10Y TIPS bottomed at 2.20% within a week of CRDO's all-time high, and real yields +57bp have coincided with a ~60% drawdown. **CRDO is effectively a levered short position on long-duration Treasuries, wrapped in an AI growth story.**

**Channel 3 — Credit spreads → customer concentration risk: NEGATIVE**
CRDO's revenue is concentrated in a handful of hyperscalers/programmes. That concentration means **one customer's capex revision is a P&L event**, not a sentiment event. Widening spreads raise the probability that second- and third-tier AI buyers defer or re-phase orders — and CRDO has no diversification cushion.

**Channel 4 — Tariffs & trade policy: NEGATIVE (dated, quantifiable)**
The **25% Section 232 tariff on covered advanced computing chips and derivative products (proclaimed Jan 14, 2026)** plus China export-licensing rules sit directly on CRDO's cost architecture: AEC assembly in **China and Mexico**, IC sourcing, and US-bound imports. AECs are plausibly "derivative products" within scope. Impact path: margin compression or price pass-through to hyperscalers, both of which hurt a company already being de-rated. Tariff escalation into 2027 is a live policy risk.

**Channel 5 — USD strength: NEUTRAL-to-NEGATIVE (minor)**
Broad USD +4.1% since mid-September. CRDO bills US hyperscalers largely in USD but carries opex in CNY/MXN/ILS and reports in USD. Translation effect is modest; the bigger issue is relative competitiveness vs non-US connectivity vendors. **Low materiality** compared with Channels 1–3.

**Channel 6 — No refinancing risk: POSITIVE (structural offset)**
A material *offset* to the rate story: CRDO is a **net-cash, essentially debt-free** balance sheet. It has **zero refinancing risk** and no floating-rate interest burden. Its rate sensitivity is **entirely valuation-driven (discount rate + customer financing), not solvency-driven.** This is the key differentiator vs levered AI-adjacent names — and the reason a rates-driven de-rating is a *multiple* problem, not a *survival* problem. Working capital and opex are also largely insensitive to inflation given gross-margin structure and pricing power in a supply-constrained niche.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BEAR: an AI-financing credit accident**

**The setup:** HY spreads have widened 111bp since September and 56bp in eight days while absolute levels remain tight (~3.76%). The AI buildout has become a **credit-financed** story (~$500bn of AI-related issuance by July 2026). Spreads have not yet priced stress — they have only priced *caution*.

**The surprise:** A leveraged data-centre developer or neocloud fails to refinance into a 5.13% 10Y / 5.47% 30Y market. HY OAS breaks through 4.5–5.0%, and prime brokers and private-credit vehicles mark down AI infrastructure exposure. Two to three hyperscalers respond by *re-phasing* (not cancelling) 2027 capex — a mere 10–15% trim to the marginal programme is sufficient. For CRDO, whose revenue is concentrated in a few programmes and whose multiple embeds uninterrupted hypergrowth, the combination of a demand air-pocket *plus* a higher discount rate produces an **outsized second leg down — a further 40–50% from $122, with the Nov 20 intraday low of $97.92 unlikely to hold.** The 3.29 beta cuts both ways: the Nov 23 +22.6% single-day rally is the same reflex in reverse, and it demonstrates how violently this stock re-prices on financial-conditions headlines rather than fundamentals.

**Why it's under-priced:** consensus is anchored on the *demand* story (>$600bn 2026 capex, +36% YoY) and treats AI capex as policy-insulated. It is not — it is the most rate- and credit-sensitive capex cycle in the S&P, and it is being funded at the long end of a curve where term premium is rising on fiscal supply.

**The bull mirror-image (secondary):** If the energy shock that drives headline CPI (3.8% vs 2.6% core) fades and the Dec 9 hike proves the terminal one, real yields retracing 2.77% → 2.40% plus HY spreads back to 3.0% would re-rate CRDO 60–80% in a quarter without any change to estimates. The asymmetry is real, but the *macro path of least resistance* over the next one-to-two quarters runs against the position.
