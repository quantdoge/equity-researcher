# SECTOR BRIEF — CRDO (Credo Technology Group)

**Sector:** Technology / Semiconductors — sub-industry: AI data-centre connectivity (AECs, SerDes/DSP retimers, optical DSPs, IP licensing)
**Market cap:** $23.0B | **Price:** $122.46 | **Beta:** 3.29 | **Employees:** 807 | **FY end:** late April/early May

---

## 1. Sector Overview

Credo competes in AI infrastructure connectivity — the "plumbing" that moves data between accelerators inside and between racks. The addressable pool is set by hyperscaler capex, which the five US hyperscalers are on track to fund at **$800B+ in 2026** (BCG; Morgan Stanley puts global AI capex at ~$900B). Within that, the active electrical cable (AEC) market is roughly **$1–1.2B in 2026** growing at ~45% CAGR through 2028 (LightCounting), with the 800G AEC segment projected to reach $3.9B by 2034. The sector is an oligopoly at the silicon layer — Credo, Marvell (Alaska AEC DSP), Broadcom, Astera Labs, MACOM — sitting underneath a concentrated customer base of ~5 hyperscalers. This is a **late-stage hypergrowth sector**: demand is still accelerating in absolute dollars, but the *rate of change* in capex is decelerating and the market has begun compressing multiples aggressively.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Hyperscaler capex still compounding in absolute terms.** $800B–900B of 2026 AI capex, with Microsoft alone guiding ~$170B calendar-year. Every incremental rack requires more copper/optical connectivity per unit of compute as cluster sizes scale.
- **Copper's cost/power advantage persists to 1.6T.** AECs remain materially cheaper and lower-power than optics for in-rack and short-reach links. LightCounting's ~45% AEC CAGR to 2028 implies the copper window is not closing imminently, and Credo's 224G/lane SerDes roadmap extends it.
- **Sector-wide margin expansion from operating leverage.** Credo's gross margin rose 57.7% → 68.0% in three years; Astera 68.9% → 75.7%; MACOM 50.9% → 58.0%. Mix shift to higher-value silicon plus fixed-cost absorption is lifting the whole connectivity complex, not just Credo.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Capex deceleration scare is the dominant sector risk.** CNBC (29 Oct 2026) reported Microsoft and Alphabet capex *below* estimates, with Microsoft trimming its outlook on data-centre delays. Any plateau in hyperscaler spend hits connectivity names first and hardest — these are derivative, high-beta plays (CRDO beta 3.29).
- **Architectural substitution: CPO/NPO.** Copper hits physical limits at 200G–400G/lane. Broadcom is actively pushing co-packaged optics, and Marvell is investing in CPO as "the physical limitations of copper" bind. Credo is dominant in AEC but a *small player* in optical transceivers — an adverse mix shift is a genuine medium-term threat.
- **Extreme customer concentration across the peer group.** Credo's top three customers were 34%/27%/16% of revenue (~77%) in the last reported quarter, with four hyperscalers each ≥10%. Hyperscalers are simultaneously designing in-house silicon, which caps pricing power and creates cliff risk on any single socket loss.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

**Rationale:**
- **Best-in-class growth-margin combination.** Only company in the set growing >200% while also posting a 33% operating margin and expanding gross margin. Astera grows fast but at half Credo's operating margin; Broadcom/Arista have margins but a quarter of the growth.
- **Entrenched technology moat.** Proprietary SerDes/DSP IP with 224G/lane capability, and a licensing arm (IP) that monetises the same core technology independently of hardware cycles. Only 807 employees producing $1.65M revenue/head — extremely asset-light and scalable.
- **Near-term risk resolution.** The Marvell AEC patent settlement (17 Nov 2026) removes a material overhang on its core product line.
- **Caveats that keep it from "exceptional":** (i) ~77% of revenue from three customers; (ii) the optical transition requires Credo to win share where it is currently subscale; (iii) 3.29 beta and 17x sales mean the equity is a leveraged bet on capex sentiment, not a defensive compounder.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE** (high-conviction demand, late-cycle risk)

**Cycle position: late-stage hypergrowth — absolute demand still rising, growth *rate* decelerating, multiples compressing.**

Porter's Five Forces: **Supplier power LOW** (TSMC/SMIC foundry capacity ample for mature nodes; 807 employees, fabless). **Buyer power HIGH** (5 hyperscalers, 77% concentration, in-house silicon programmes). **Threat of new entry MEDIUM** (SerDes IP is a decade-long build, but well-funded entrants and Chinese connectivity vendors are active). **Rivalry HIGH** (Marvell, Broadcom, Astera, MACOM all targeting the same sockets; patent litigation is routine). **Substitutes MEDIUM-HIGH** (CPO/NPO and linear pluggable optics over time).

Net: the demand tailwind ($800B+ capex, 45% AEC CAGR) comfortably outweighs these forces over the next 12–18 months, justifying an Attractive rating. But this is an **early-in-the-de-rating, late-in-the-fundamentals** setup — the sector's risk is not demand disappearance, it is that 2027 capex growth decelerates toward 20% while these stocks still carry 17–23x sales.
