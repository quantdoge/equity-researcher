## CRDO — Geopolitical, Geographic & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company:** Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd — Cayman Islands incorporated (ISIN KYG254571055), HQ San Jose, CA; fabless semiconductor (AECs, SerDes/DSP chiplets, optical DSPs, IP licensing). FY2026 revenue **$1.335B (+205.7%)**, net income $472.3M / diluted EPS $2.51. Market cap ~$23.0B, 807 FTEs, beta 3.29.

**Key structural finding:** Credo has *already* de-risked direct China revenue — mainland China fell from 23.2% to **0.2% of revenue** year-over-year, while Malaysia ramped from 2.9% to 15.5%. This is a supply chain/customer-driven China+1 pivot that has largely played out. Note the FY2025 10-K reported only **15% North America / 85% "rest of world, primarily Asia"** — geography of *revenue recognition* historically tracked where ODMs/contract manufacturers take title, not end-demand. The FY2026/Q1 FY27 shift to 53% US recognition suggests bill-to entities are migrating onshore (Sources: [Q1 FY27 10-Q geographic note](https://d18rn0p25nwr6d.cloudfront.net/CIK-0001807794/27bb6ffb-1050-483b-bfd8-880b44d5621c.html); [InsightDeck CRDO map](https://insightdeck.jp/en/maps/CRDO/2026-08-01/)).

**Operational footprint:** subsidiaries in **China, Hong Kong, Singapore, Canada, Israel, and the US**, plus **Mexico**. Fabless model — wafer fabrication and assembly/test are concentrated in the **Pacific Rim, principally Taiwan** (named OSATs have included Amkor, ASE, KYEC). **Israel** was added via the **$1.25B DustPhotonics acquisition** (Modi'in, Israel; closed May 2026), bringing silicon-photonics R&D into a conflict-affected jurisdiction.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Taiwan Strait / Pacific Rim manufacturing concentration. Probability: Low-Medium (12–24 mo); Impact: Severe**
Credo is fabless with dependency on Taiwanese foundry and Asian OSAT capacity. Non-cancelable purchase commitments are **$222.2M, of which $212.7M is to manufacturing vendors and foundries**, plus **$88.4M refundable capacity deposits and a further $102.4M committed in FY2027**. This is a *prepaid, non-diversifiable* bet on a specific supply geography — a blockade, quarantine, or PLA escalation would halt AEC ramps with no near-term second source. Mitigant: management is deliberately locking capacity, and Malaysia assembly is scaling.

**Risk 2 — US–China export controls and Chinese counter-measures. Probability: High (ongoing); Impact: Medium**
The macro backdrop as of late Nov 2026 is fragile: the US–China trade truce is expected to be extended, but Eurasia Group's Ian Bremmer warns *"tighter chip restrictions could prompt measured Chinese retaliation"* ([CNBC, 11/20/26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kXcA3CYZXjU)). Beijing has activated its **2021 blocking regulations** and issued new rules targeting "long-arm jurisdiction" of Western countries — creating genuine legal-conflict exposure (complying with BIS can violate Chinese law and vice versa) for any entity with HK/China contracting parties. The FY2025 10-K explicitly flags that *"U.S. tariffs and trade restrictions, particularly those affecting China, may increase costs and limit component availability."* Credo is not itself a leading-edge AI GPU exporter, which caps direct hit risk, but **19% Hong Kong revenue** sits squarely in the jurisdiction BIS scrutinizes most for end-use diversion.

**Risk 3 — Hyperscaler customer concentration as a policy transmission channel. Probability: Medium; Impact: High**
Top 10 customers ≈ **90% of FY2026 revenue**; two customers >10%. FY2025 had **one customer (Amazon AWS) at 67% of revenue**; in Q1 FY2027 **Customer A was 43% of quarterly revenue and 57% of accounts receivable**. This is normally framed as commercial risk, but it is *geopolitical* here: a single US hyperscaler's procurement decision, or a shift in its own China/export posture, can move Credo's revenue by tens of percent. It also means Credo has almost no pricing power over tariff and compliance-cost pass-through. Secondary: **Israel** operations (DustPhotonics) add Middle East conflict and call-up/continuity risk to a $1.25B optical bet.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **AEC patent war — now fully resolved.** Credo settled with **Marvell** on **Nov 17, 2026** on confidential terms, covering both parties' AEC patent rights *and* resolving **Marvell's pending USPTO challenges to Credo's patents** ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20261117966700/en/Credo-Reaches-Settlement-in-Patent-Dispute/); [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/trading-ideas/movers/26/11/63372948/credo-stock-pops-after-reaching-patent-agreement-with-marvell)). Credo had earlier settled AEC disputes with **TE Connectivity and Molex in March 2026**, also confidential. This removes a major overhang — the stock popped on the news.
- **Current litigation status: clean.** The Q1 FY27 10-Q states Credo **"is not subject to material litigation"** and that there were **no material changes to previously disclosed risk factors**.
- **No disclosed DOJ, SEC, FTC, or EU investigations** into Credo. Note the Nov 2026 *Nvidia* 10-Q delay and reported SEC accounting inquiry is a **sector-level sentiment event**, not a Credo matter — but it has driven semiconductor multiple compression (Nasdaq −10% in one week).
- **Regulatory regimes in scope:** BIS/EAR export licensing (AI-networking DSPs/SerDes), Section 301/232 tariffs, FCPA/UK Bribery Act (HK, China, Malaysia, Israel, Mexico ops), GDPR + Israeli Privacy Law + China PIPL (residual), and CFIUS-adjacent considerations on future US/EU optical acquisitions.
- **Cayman domicile:** no operational substance in KY; principal exposure is to US tax-reform changes on foreign-held IP and to the FY2026-27 integration of three acquisitions (Hyperlume, Comira, DustPhotonics) with **contingent earn-out consideration**.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Hong Kong revenue (19%) vs. mainland China (0.2%).** A near-total collapse in PRC-recognized revenue alongside persistent HK bill-to revenue is the exact pattern BIS diversion-enforcement teams screen for. Most likely explanation is legitimate — HK/Singapore entities are the contracting parties for Asian ODMs building for US hyperscalers — but Credo does not publicly disclose end-user due diligence. **Watch item, not an allegation.**
2. **Prepaid capacity deposits ($88.4M + $102.4M committed) to Taiwanese/Asian foundries.** These are refundable only under narrow conditions; they are effectively unhedged geopolitical exposure sitting on the balance sheet.
3. **DustPhotonics (Israel) integration.** Israeli Innovation Authority grant conditions and Israeli export-control rules on photonics now apply to a core technology roadmap.
4. **Tariff pass-through opacity.** With ~90% of revenue in ten customers, Credo cannot easily pass through Section 301 cost increases — margin risk is absorbed, not transferred.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale for not higher:** direct mainland China revenue is now immaterial (0.2%), the multi-front AEC patent litigation that dogged the name through 2023–2026 is **fully settled** across Marvell, TE, and Molex, and there are no government investigations or material pending suits. The company is a US-HQ, US-demand-driven business with a strengthening onshore revenue mix.

**Rationale for not lower:** a **beta of 3.29** and ~90% top-10 customer concentration mean any geopolitical shock transmits violently; **Taiwan/Pacific Rim supply concentration with $222M of non-cancelable commitments** is an unhedged tail; **Hong Kong at 19%** keeps Credo inside the most enforcement-active export-control jurisdiction; and the **Israel** footprint plus a deteriorating US–China tech détente (Nov 2026) leave multiple live vectors.

**Upgrade to Medium-High if:** Malaysia/Taiwan capacity commitments grow further without a second-source qual, HK concentration rises back above ~30%, or BIS extends licensing to AI-networking interconnect beyond GPUs/memory.
