## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① U.S. tariff and trade policy on instruments, consumables and components — Probability: High (already in effect) | Impact: Medium**
Tariffs are explicitly named in IDEXX's own forward-looking risk list ("changes in U.S. and other countries' tariff and trade policies… impact of tariffs"), and management's 2026 outlook explicitly incorporates a tariff impact. Notably, **reported operating-margin guidance was trimmed to 32.1–32.3% from 32.3–32.5%** in the Q3 2026 release even as revenue and EPS guidance rose — consistent with cost pressure (tariffs, mix, FX) offsetting operating leverage. IDEXX manufactures much of its instrument/consumable volume in the U.S. (Maine, Memphis) but sources components and sells into tariff-affected markets; a re-escalation of U.S.–China or U.S.–EU tariffs is the single most likely source of a guidance reset. *Caveat: no company-disclosed dollar tariff figure was retrievable; the causal link to the margin trim is inference, not company attribution.*

**② Antitrust and channel-practice legal risk in the U.S. — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium–High**
This is the most distinctive legal exposure. The FTC's 2010–2013 consent order bars IDEXX from concurrent exclusive distribution arrangements with the three national point-of-care distributors. Private plaintiffs have repeatedly tested the same conduct: a 2022 pet-owner class action alleging inflated prices was **largely dismissed in March 2025** (Reuters), but a **new antitrust suit was filed by veterinarians** alleging IDEXX pivoted to exclusive agreements directly with vets after regulators intervened (Bloomberg Law). IDEXX also recorded a **discrete litigation expense accrual in 2024 that cut EPS by $0.56** (reversed $0.08 in Q1 2025) — evidence that these matters can be financially material, not nuisance-level. Rising private-equity ownership of vet clinics and political scrutiny of veterinary pricing raise the odds of continued plaintiff activity and possible state/FTC inquiry.

**③ FX and European/APAC regulatory drift — Probability: Medium | Impact: Low–Medium**
With ~37–39% of revenue outside the U.S., currency is a recurring swing factor (Q4 2025 EPS included a **$0.09 positive FX impact**; FY2025 included **$0.10**). Beyond translation, IDEXX must maintain country-by-country registrations for diagnostic tests and instruments, and its water-testing franchise (Colilert, Enterolert, Legiolert) depends on regulatory acceptance of its methods (e.g., EPA and international equivalents) — a change in approved-method lists is a low-probability but high-consequence event. EU/UK divergence on veterinary diagnostics and data rules adds ongoing compliance cost.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **FTC consent order (2010 matter, settled 2013):** prohibits concurrent exclusive distribution agreements with the three national POC distributors — a standing conduct constraint and the template for private suits.
- **Consumer class action (2022, antitrust):** most claims dismissed March 2025; residual claims/watch for appeal.
- **Veterinarian antitrust suit (Bloomberg Law):** alleges exclusive dealing with practices post-FTC intervention — active.
- **Trade secrets:** IDEXX v. Covetrus / Vets First Choice matter settled (voluntary dismissal with prejudice of claims against individual defendants).
- **Patent:** IDEXX v. Charles River Laboratories (D. Del., 2015, sample-collection patents) — part of a long-running IP posture; IDEXX is both plaintiff and defendant in veterinary-diagnostics IP.
- **Regulatory bodies:** FDA-CVM (animal drugs/devices adjacent), USDA-APHIS (animal biologics/livestock testing), EPA (water methods), EU competent authorities, and state veterinary practice boards. No DOJ/SEC/FCPA enforcement action identified in the search record.
- **Governance note:** CEO transition during 2026 — Jay Mazelsky is quoted as President & CEO in the Feb 2026 FY2025 release, while **Michael G. Erickson signed the Q3 2026 10-Q as President & CEO**. Leadership change mid-year is worth monitoring for strategy/guidance continuity.
- **M&A:** 2026 CoVet acquisition ($5M contribution in guidance) — small, but adds integration and data-handling obligations.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **LOW** (with a Medium sub-rating on U.S. legal/antitrust)

**Rationale:** Revenue is concentrated in the U.S. and Western Europe — the lowest-geopolitical-risk jurisdictions — with no meaningful exposure to sanctioned states, nationalisation risk, or capital controls. The balance sheet and cash generation (9M 2026 FCF $977.6M) make any plausible legal settlement absorbable. The two things that could actually move the stock are (a) a tariff-driven margin reset, already partly visible in the trimmed operating-margin guide, and (b) an adverse antitrust outcome or FTC re-engagement that constrains distribution practices. Neither is currently priced as a base case.

**What would change the rating:** a formal FTC/state AG investigation into direct vet exclusive-dealing; a class certification in the veterinarian suit; new U.S. tariffs on veterinary instruments/consumables or EU retaliation; or a disclosed material (>$100M) litigation accrual.
