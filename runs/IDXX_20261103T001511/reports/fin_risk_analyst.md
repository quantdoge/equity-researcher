### 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE**

IDEXX is a conservatively financed, high-margin, cash-generative business. Absolute debt is small ($1.08B) against $1.51B of EBITDA and $1.05B of annual FCF. Leverage of **0.6x net debt/EBITDA** and **35.5x interest coverage** place it comfortably in solid investment-grade territory; on leverage alone the metrics are consistent with A-category. Reported ratings could not be confirmed from the search (results returned IDEX Corp., a *different* company — see Data Gaps); the widely indicated level is BBB+/Baa1, which I treat as inferred, not verified.

Leverage did **rise** in FY2025 — net debt from $699M → $897M and short-term debt from $418M → $500M — not because of operating stress but because **buybacks ($1.22B) exceeded FCF ($1.05B)**. That is a capital-allocation choice, not a solvency signal, and it is the sole reason the Piotroski F-score lost its leverage point.

**Break-even analysis:** with a roughly 54–55% contribution margin and ~$985M of structural fixed cost, revenue could fall approximately **58%** before operating income reaches zero. Debt service does not become a binding constraint in any plausible demand scenario.

**Conclusion on the downside:** a 20% revenue decline is an *earnings* event, not a *solvency* event. Leverage stays under 1.0x, coverage stays above 20x, and FCF remains solidly positive. The genuine risk is equity-side: at ~28x EV/EBITDA and 40x earnings, a −20% revenue scenario would likely compress the multiple far more than it impairs the balance sheet. Credit is insulated; the share price is not.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **LOW**

Balance-sheet and cash-flow risk is objectively low — sub-1x leverage, 35x coverage, Z-score 5.9, F-score 8, 99% FCF conversion, high ROIC, no dividend obligation, and a massive revenue buffer to break-even. The constraint is not survivability but **valuation and shareholder-return policy**.

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Buybacks exceeding FCF** — $1.22B repurchased vs $1.05B FCF in FY2025, funded by debt drawdown (net debt +$198M). Sustainable only while FCF grows; a downturn plus continued buybacks would lever up faster than the model above assumes.
2. **Liquidity mix deteriorating** — current ratio 1.57x → 1.17x, quick 1.17x → 0.84x, cash 453.9M → 180.1M over two years. Short-term debt of **$500.1M now exceeds cash of $180.1M by 2.8x**, creating recurring refinancing dependence (mitigated by ~$1.05B FCF and, per filings, an unsecured revolver).
3. **Receivables outrunning revenue** — receivables +23.5% y/y vs revenue +10.4%; DSOs 52.6 → 58.8 days. Working capital was a $227.5M cash drag in FY2025 vs $133.2M in FY2024. Worth monitoring for collection quality or channel-stuffing pressure, particularly with corporate veterinary consolidators as customers.
4. **High realised volatility and event gap risk** — 39.9% annualised, 34% drawdown, 27% single-day moves. Position sizing should reflect earnings-gap risk, not beta.
5. **Valuation-driven fragility** — 2.5% FCF yield at 28x EV/EBITDA leaves no cushion; credit quality is decoupled from equity downside.
