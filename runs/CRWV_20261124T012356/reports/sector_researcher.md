## SECTOR BRIEF — CoreWeave (CRWV)
*Sector classification: Technology / "Software – Infrastructure" (FMP), but economically the **AI neocloud / GPU-as-a-Service (GPUaaS)** segment. Data as of 23 Nov 2026.*

---

### 1. Sector Overview

CoreWeave competes in the **merchant AI compute ("neocloud") market** — specialised, GPU-dense cloud capacity rented by AI labs, hyperscalers and enterprises, as distinct from general-purpose cloud. The segment is being pulled by a demand shock rather than a normal cycle: global data-centre power demand is forecast to rise ~50% by 2027 and up to 165% by 2030 (Goldman Sachs), PwC sizes the AI/data-centre build-out at **$31.6trn of cumulative capex**, and SRG forecasts the neocloud market alone approaching **~$400bn by 2031**. The structural opening exists because traditional cloud supply cannot keep pace with GPU-accelerated demand — but the segment's economics are those of a **capital-intensive asset lessor**, not a software business: GPU/servers turn over every 4–6 years, GPU capital costs can run ~3.7x hosting costs, and by 2027 a handful of GPU providers are expected to control 80%+ of share (Vultr). The sector is in **hypergrowth on revenue but has already rotated into a credit-and-financing phase in equity pricing**.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Contracted demand exceeds the industry's ability to build.** CRWV's revenue backlog reached **$132.5bn at 30 Sep 2026, +138% YoY** (from ~$104bn two quarters earlier) — multi-year, largely take-or-pay visibility that is ~10x FY26E revenue and underwrites debt-financed capacity.
- **Supply constraint is power and cooling, not demand.** Cooling (49%) has now overtaken power availability (44%) as the top scaling constraint (AlixPartners 2027 outlook). Scarce energised shell capacity at scale — which CRWV, Nebius and IREN hold — commands pricing power that general-purpose cloud lost long ago.
- **Re-contracting of depreciated fleets is extending asset life.** Older GPUs are being re-leased short-term at *higher* prices, accelerating payback and lifting ROIC on installed capacity — a genuine second-life tailwind that de-risks the 4–6 year depreciation cycle.

---

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Single-supplier dependency on NVIDIA.** NVIDIA is simultaneously supplier (near-monopoly on accelerated compute), investor/backstop and ecosystem kingmaker — evidenced by CRWV moving ~22% on the news of Jensen Huang's reinstatement as CEO. Supplier power is the sector's defining structural weakness; HBM/memory tightness (SK hynix, Micron) compounds it.
- **The "capital wall."** CRWV guided **$37–39bn of 2026 capex after $30.65bn in the first nine months** — roughly 2.6–3x revenue. Sector-wide, 2027 capex models are increasingly capital-constrained, and multiple sell-side/specialist views flag **2027 as the potential peak** of the build. Rising interest cost is the transmission mechanism: CRWV's interest expense hit **$846m in Q3'26 alone** (~$3.4bn annualised) against $2.02bn for 9M'26.
- **Concentrated buyers with a build-your-own option.** Hyperscalers (Microsoft historically the largest customer) and AI labs are both the biggest customers *and* the most credible competitors via Azure/AWS/GCP/OCI plus custom silicon (TPU, Trainium, MTIA). Buyer power is rising, not falling. Add policy drag — new US export curbs threatening neocloud business in Southeast Asia (Nov 2026).

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG — with a fragile balance sheet**

**Rationale for "strong":**
- Clear **#1 pure-play** in merchant GPU cloud: $14.6bn annualised revenue run-rate vs ~$4bn for NBIS and ~$0.9bn for IREN, with a **$132.5bn backlog** no peer matches.
- Differentiated on **NVIDIA allocation priority** (exemplar partner status), energised capacity pipeline, and a software stack (Mission Control, Tensorizer) that is real but not a true switching-cost moat.
- Structural edge in **financing at scale** — the actual barrier to entry in this sector is access to capital, and CRWV is the sector's reference credit.

**Why not "very strong":**
- **Economic moat is capital, not technology.** GPU-hours are close to a commodity; switching costs are contractual, not technical.
- **Gross margin compressing** (74%→66–68%) and interest coverage deteriorating: FY25 EBITDA $2.47bn / interest $1.23bn = 2.0x; 9M'26 EBITDA ~$2.7bn / interest $2.02bn = **~1.3x**. Net loss widened to **–$2.08bn for 9M'26** from –$1.17bn for all of FY25.
- **Customer concentration** in exactly the counterparties best able to substitute in-house capacity.
- Stock at $48.33 vs $126.32 a year ago and a ~$138 May-2026 peak — **down ~62–65% while revenue tripled**. The de-rating is a multiple/credit repricing, not a demand problem: market cap (~$26.9bn) is now **~1.9x implied FY26 revenue (~$12.5–13bn)** and **less than 0.7x one year of capex**. Beta of 7.7 makes this a leveraged, reflexive vehicle on AI sentiment.

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

- **Demand: Attractive.** Backlog conversion, power scarcity and re-contracting give the sector multi-year visibility that few industries can match.
- **Structure: Unattractive.** Porter's Five Forces is hostile on three axes at once — extreme **supplier power** (NVIDIA, HBM), rising **buyer power** (hyperscalers as customers *and* competitors), and low entry barriers for anything except capital. Rivalry is intensifying toward 2027 consolidation, with **substitute risk from custom ASICs** the medium-term wildcard.
- **Cycle position: late-stage hypergrowth.** Revenue is still early-innings, but *capacity build* is mid-to-late cycle and capital availability is the binding constraint — the sector has shifted from a growth-scarcity trade to a **financing-and-depreciation trade**.
- **Verdict:** Neutral overall. The neocloud layer is structurally the highest-beta, lowest-return-on-capital slice of the AI stack; better risk-adjusted exposure to the same demand sits **upstream (NVDA, memory)** or **with the vertically integrated hyperscalers (MSFT, ORCL)**. CRWV is a strong operator in a structurally difficult segment — own it for backlog-conversion and re-rating optionality, size it for credit risk.
