# NRG Energy (NYSE: NRG) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $99.38 | Market cap ~$20.9B | Data through Oct 19, 2026*

---

## 1. Growth Story

NRG is executing the most aggressive repositioning in the utility space: it has converted a commodity retail-electricity business into an **integrated "supply + load" power platform** — ~25 GW of dispatchable generation (doubled via the $12B LS Power deal closed Jan 30, 2026), ~6–7M retail customer relationships, and now a **"Bring Your Own Power" (BYOP)** model where hyperscalers fund new-build gas plants behind long-term contracts (first project: 1.2 GW CCGT in Texas, ~$3.2B, GE Vernova turbines / Kiewit EPC, with capacity reserved for **5.4 GW** of further builds). The thesis is that AI data-center load growth creates a decade of contracted, capital-light-return generation demand — and that NRG, uniquely, already owns the customer relationship, the interconnection queue position, and the turbine slots. Management targets **14%+ adjusted EPS growth through 2030**.

The problem is that the market has stopped paying for the story: the stock is **–47% from its Feb 2026 peak of $188** and –41% YoY, after two consecutive adjusted-EPS misses.

## 3. TAM and Penetration

- **US retail electricity: ~$500B+/yr.** NRG serves ~6–7M customers of ~130M US households → **~5% penetration**, with record new customer adds and strong retention (FY2025) — but retail is a low-margin, churn-prone, price-competitive business (Reliant, Direct Energy, Green Mountain).
- **Data-center load is the real TAM.** US data-center demand is projected to roughly double by 2030 (order of +35–50 GW). NRG's 25 GW fleet is ~2% of the ~1,200 GW US fleet, but its **5.4 GW of reserved turbine/EPC capacity** is the scarce asset — turbine slots through 2029 are effectively sold out industry-wide. Converting 5.4 GW at ~$2.7B/GW implies **~$15B of contracted-margin capex** — a deployment pipeline equal to ~70% of today's market cap.
- **Differentiation:** ownership of both generation and the retail customer (affordability/reliability politics), plus interconnection positions at existing sites. **Weakness:** no proprietary technology or data network effect — the moat is contracts and siting, which peers (VST, CEG, TLN) can replicate.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Q3 2026 earnings — Nov 5, 2026** (consensus EPS $3.24, revenue $9.17B). Third consecutive miss would be a credibility break; a beat resets the narrative. *~2 weeks out.*
2. **Definitive 1.2 GW hyperscaler BYOP agreement / financial close** — expected by YE2026; converts the flagship project from announcement to backlog.
3. **Additional BYOP contract signings** off the 5.4 GW reserved slot book — 2026–2027; each 1 GW ≈ a re-rating event.
4. **2027 guidance + reaffirmation of 14%+ EPS CAGR** — Feb 2027. Street needs proof that 2026's ~8% growth is a one-year deal-dilution trough.
5. **Remaining Texas Energy Fund new-builds reaching COD** (after T.H. Wharton's 415 MW in Q2'26) + ~$1.0B buyback completion and ~8% dividend growth ($1.90 annualized).

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

NRG fails the growth-quality test that matters most to me: **~4.5% revenue CAGR, a Rule of 40 in the low-20s on the company's own non-GAAP basis, inorganic growth, and EPS growth that depends entirely on management's ability to deploy ~$15B of capital at attractive contracted returns while servicing a materially larger debt load.** Two consecutive misses, with the miss driven by deal-related interest and D&A, is exactly the execution risk that accompanies acquisition-led growth. There is no innovation moat here — only contract and siting advantages.

That said, the *valuation* has become genuinely interesting: **11.2× 2026E and 9.0× 2027E adjusted EPS**, ~7× forward EV/EBITDA versus **CEG at 15.6× and VST at 13.6×**. If the 14%+ EPS CAGR is delivered, the PEG is ~0.6. I am not willing to underwrite that today, but I am also not willing to sell a franchise asset at 9× forward with a signed data-center demand wave behind it.

**Upgrade to Buy on:** (1) a Q3'26 EPS beat with reaffirmed guidance, **and** (2) a definitive 1.2 GW BYOP contract signed with the counterparty disclosed — together those would validate both the margin trajectory and the growth engine.
**Downgrade to Sell on:** a third consecutive miss, a guidance cut, or net leverage above ~4×.

## 7. Key Risks

- **Execution/credibility risk (highest):** 2026 guidance midpoint ($8.90) implies only **+8%** growth against a **14%+** CAGR target — the back-end years must carry ~18%/yr. The Street has already begun discounting this.
- **Earnings quality:** GAAP EPS $4.01 (2025) vs. adjusted $8.24 — a >100% gap driven by derivative fair-value marks that management explicitly excludes and does not guide to. GAAP net income can swing to a loss in a single quarter (Q2'25: –$104M).
- **Leverage and rate sensitivity:** LS Power added meaningful debt and project-level obligations; interest expense is up ~85% since 2022 and rises with refinancing. This is the direct cause of the recent misses.
- **Commodity/weather exposure:** ERCOT merchant margins and East capacity prices drive results; "milder weather and higher supply costs" was already cited in Q2'26. Earnings are not contracted like a regulated utility's.
- **Competitive/structural:** if hyperscalers shift to behind-the-meter self-supply, the BYOP model's economics compress; VST, CEG and TLN compete for the same turbine slots and contracts.
- **Regulatory:** ERCOT/SB6 market redesign, PJM capacity market rules, and large-load interconnection tariffs could change who bears new-build risk — the very premise of BYOP.
