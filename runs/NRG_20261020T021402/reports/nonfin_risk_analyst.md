## NRG ENERGY, INC. (NYSE: NRG) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
*As of market data through 19-Oct-2026 (close $99.38). Sector: Utilities / Independent Power Producers. Mkt cap ≈ $20.9bn.*

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (improving, but with churn)**

**Evidence for:**
- **Chair/CEO separation restored 30-Apr-2026**: Dr. Lawrence Coben stepped down as President (earlier) and then as CEO/Chair; **Robert J. Gaudette** became CEO and **Antonio Carrillo** (Lead Independent Director) became Chair. A clean, well-signposted succession rather than an abrupt exit.
- Board is ~11 strong, and **all directors other than the CEO are independent**. Two new independent directors added in 2026 — **Sanjay Kapoor** (3-Feb-26, Audit Committee) and **Glenn Wright** (26-May-26, power sector experience) — a deliberate refresh plus audit/financial expertise.
- DOJ antitrust and FERC/NYPSC approvals for the LS Power transaction were obtained cleanly (DOJ 23-Jan-26; close 30-Jan-26) — no political/regulatory friction on the largest deal in company history.

**Evidence against:**
- **Combined Chair + CEO from Aug-2024 to Apr-2026** — precisely the window in which NRG underwrote and announced the ~13 GW LS Power portfolio, CPower and the $3.2bn hyperscaler project. Concentration of power during peak balance-sheet risk-taking.
- **Three CEOs in ~24 months** (Gutierrez abrupt departure Oct-2024 → Coben interim-turned-permanent → Gaudette Apr-2026). Coben remains an **advisor to the company through 2026**, creating at least the optics of a shadow executive while a first-time CEO executes the largest capital programme in NRG's history.
- Long-tenured directors (Carter, Cox, Donohue, Fawaz) sit alongside the new arrivals; no evidence yet that refresh has changed capital-allocation discipline.

### 4. Top Operational Risks
- **Single-counterparty, single-site concentration in the growth story.** The 1.2 GW Texas CCGT (~$3.2bn, ~$2,670/kW, 15-yr minimum term, >95% capacity-based cash flow) rests on **one unnamed hyperscaler** and a parent guarantee. If Texas interconnection terms harden, the counterparty re-trades, or Phase 2 (to 2.4 GW) slips, the equity narrative and a large slice of committed capex are impaired. Management explicitly declined to say how Abbott's pause affects the deal ("I think we're OK" is not disclosure).
- **Integration, leverage and execution.** 13 GW LS Power + CPower absorbed in one year; Fitch **BB+ (stable)**, S&P **BB** — sub-investment-grade issuer ratings with a stated 3.5x downgrade threshold against a <3.0x target. Q2-26 EPS missed on **higher interest expense and LS Power-related costs**. Turbine slots/EPC depend on **GE Vernova and Kiewit** (5.4 GW through 2032) — vendor concentration on a compressed 2029 COD.
- **Physical, cyber and key-person.** ERCOT-concentrated generation and ~8m retail accounts (Reliant, Direct Energy, Green Mountain, XOOM, Stream) expose NRG to extreme-weather and hurricane risk; T.H. Wharton (415 MW, May-26) is its first new-build in a decade, so commissioning muscle is untested at scale. No cybersecurity breach located in the public record, but a customer-data/smart-home platform is a high-value target. Key-person risk is elevated by CEO turnover and a first-year CEO running peak capex.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **ELEVATED**
- **Affordability politics is the dominant vector.** Texas residential bills and the "data centres raise your power bill" narrative are now a national political story; NRG is simultaneously a large Texas *retail* brand and a data-centre power developer. The BYOP/"we're paid for megawatts, not run-hours" framing is a genuine defence, but it protects the P&L, not the brand.
- **Chronic retail-conduct friction**: Direct Energy's $12m Illinois AG settlement (2025) plus repeated TX-PUC/PA-PUC consumer-protection penalties indicate a persistent marketing/complaints problem across the retail portfolio.
- **M&A diligence reputation**: the Vivint smart-home chapter (FTC $20m, DOJ $3.2m FCA) remains the reference case for value-destructive, reputational M&A — an awkward precedent while management runs its most aggressive acquisition programme ever. The RGGI "not in our underwriting" admission echoes it.
- **Press tone has turned**: "misses estimates as interest costs rise", "costs related to LS Power assets", −16% single-day move (4-Aug-26), stock −46% from the Feb-26 high of $188.72. Potential bidder for a **bankrupt West Virginia coal plant** would invite fresh ESG/climate scrutiny.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**
*Driven by regulatory/political concentration and governance churn, not by integrity or fraud concerns.* Integrity signals are clean (no SEC/DOJ action, no restatement, no whistleblower disclosure located, Uri liability extinguished). The offsetting negatives are a sub-investment-grade balance sheet, a core-market policy binary in Texas, a single-counterparty $3.2bn bet, three CEOs in two years, and a one-sided insider tape.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation
1. **Gaudette sold 41% of his holdings for $7.1m fifteen weeks before becoming CEO**, with no insider purchase since — request the 10b5-1 adoption dates for all 2025-26 officer sales.
2. **LS Power exited 67% of its NRG stake (16.1m shares) in March 2026**, with an anomalous Form 4 price field — verify the true disposition price and remaining overhang/sell-down plan.
3. **"RGGI was not included in our underwriting"** — a $70m unforecast cost on freshly acquired assets; test what else was excluded from the LS Power underwriting model (CCR liabilities, capacity-market rules).
4. **Texas political binary** — Abbott's interconnection audit and PUCT Project 58480 could re-price or delay the BYOP pipeline; management gave no quantification. Escalate for direct diligence on the hyperscaler contract's Texas-policy conditions precedent.
5. **Combined Chair/CEO during 2024-26 deal-making, and a departed CEO retained as 2026 advisor** — confirm scope of Coben's advisory role and whether it covers capital allocation.
6. **Sub-investment-grade ratings against a $3.2bn+ growth capex plan with a 3.5x downgrade trigger** — governance oversight of the leverage covenant is the single largest value-at-risk item.
