## Airbnb, Inc. (ABNB) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price:** $181.13 (Nov 11, 2026) · **Diluted shares:** 597M · **Market cap:** ~$108B · **Net cash:** ~$4.5B · **EV:** ~$104B · **52-wk range:** $110.81–$197.87

> *Data note:* The price-history feed I received was stale (ended Feb 2026 at $127.89). I confirmed via live quote sources that ABNB trades near **$181** and used that throughout. Peer valuation comparison (peer median P/E 24.6x) is based on a mega-cap consumer-cyclical basket (AMZN, TSLA, HD, MCD) and is not a meaningful read-across for a travel marketplace — I have de-emphasized it.

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow (widening)**

Airbnb is a two-sided marketplace with ~5M+ listings across 220+ countries. The bull case for a moat rests on genuine network effects: more hosts → more unique inventory → better guest experience → more guests → more hosts. The result is visible in the unit economics — **$13.96B TTM revenue on 8,200 employees (~$1.7M revenue/employee)**, and capex that rounds to zero.

**Evidence the moat is real:**
- ROIC of **24.9%** in FY2025 (23.9% FY2024, 22.8% FY2022) — consistently well above any reasonable WACC, the hallmark of a moated business.
- ROE 30%+, achieved despite a *negative* book equity position.
- Revenue CAGR of 17% from 2020–2025 through a pandemic, a war, and a travel downturn.
- Brand intangible: "Airbnb" is a verb in the way "Google" is. That is not replicable with ad spend.

**Evidence the moat is narrower than it looks:**
- **Switching costs are near zero on both sides.** A guest can check Booking.com/Vrbo in a second tab; a host can list on four platforms simultaneously. Multi-homing is the norm, not the exception.
- **Supply is not proprietary.** The inventory is owned by third parties who owe Airbnb no loyalty.
- **Regulation is a direct assault on the supply side** — New York City, Barcelona, Amsterdam, Paris and others have imposed caps and de facto bans. This is the single most credible threat to the franchise, and it is not something management controls.
- **AI disintermediation risk** is real: if travel discovery shifts to AI agents and hosts can be reached directly, the marketplace's toll-booth position thins.

**Verdict on moat:** the brand, global liquidity, and efficient scale are real and have *widened* over five years (ROIC up, margins up, revenue up 4x). But the absence of switching costs and the regulatory overhang cap this at **Narrow** — a high-quality narrow moat, not a Wide one. I would not underwrite this as a forever-compounder the way I would a Moody's or a See's.

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **8 / 10**

| Metric (TTM to Q3'26) | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $13.96B | +17% YoY; **re-accelerated** from +10.8% in FY2025 |
| Operating income | $3.16B | 22.6% margin |
| Net income | $3.21B | Diluted EPS $5.33 |
| Operating cash flow | $5.01B | **156% of net income** |
| Capital expenditure | ~$0 | Asset-light to an extreme |
| Stock-based comp | $1.77B | 12.7% of revenue — the one blemish |
| **Owner earnings (FCF − SBC)** | **$3.24B** | ~101% of net income — clean conversion |
| Net cash | ~$4.5B | No leverage risk |
| ROIC | 24.9% | Well above WACC |

**What's genuinely excellent:** FCF conversion, balance sheet, capital intensity, revenue visibility. Working capital is a *source* of cash (guests prepay, hosts are paid after the stay) — a genuine, growing float.

**What keeps this from a 9 or 10:**
1. **SBC of $1.77B/yr** is a real economic cost, whether or not it appears in FCF. Management partly neutralizes it — share count has fallen from 680M (2022) to 597M (Q3'26), roughly −3.5%/yr — but it is a persistent tax on owners.
2. **FY2025 showed margin compression**: operating margin fell from 23.0% to 21.2%, and reported gross margin dropped from 83% to 72%. The gross-margin drop is a *reclassification* (cost-of-revenue line changed), not a deterioration in economics — but SGA did rise 17.9% against 10.8% revenue growth. TTM operating margin has since recovered to 22.6%.
3. **Negative retained earnings (−$5.5B)** and negative tangible book value are IPO-era accounting artifacts, not distress — but they mean book value is useless as a valuation anchor.
4. **Cyclicality**: Q1 is structurally loss-making (Q1'26 operating income just $84M on $2.68B revenue). Travel demand is discretionary and beta is 1.27.

### 3. Valuation Analysis — Intrinsic Value ~$165–175; **no margin of safety**

The provided DCF tool mis-scaled WACC (read 9.5 as 950%), so I built the model directly.

**Multiples at $181:**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E (TTM GAAP, EPS $5.33) | **34.0x** |
| EV / reported FCF ($5.0B) | **20.7x** |
| EV / owner earnings ($3.24B, SBC-adjusted) | **32x** |
| FCF yield (reported) | 4.6% |
| FCF yield (SBC-adjusted) | **3.0%** |

**Two-stage owner-earnings DCF.** Base = $3.24B SBC-adjusted owner earnings. Stage 1: 15% growth for 5 years (consistent with the 17–19% revenue growth of the last three quarters). Stage 2: fade to 4% by year 10. Terminal growth 3%.

| Discount rate | Value/share |
|---|---|
| 9.0% | ~$183 |
| **9.5%** | **~$168** |
| 10.0% | ~$155 |

If one instead ignores SBC as non-cash (which I do not endorse, but many do), intrinsic value shifts to roughly **$200–205**. Taking a blended, conservative base case: **intrinsic value ≈ $165–175**, or about **$170**.

**The conclusion is stark and worth stating plainly:** at $181, the market is discounting Airbnb's future owner earnings at roughly **9%** while assuming 15% growth fading to 4%. That is a perfectly reasonable set of assumptions — but it is *the* reasonable set, with nothing left over for the owner. You are being paid nothing for the regulatory risk, the AI disintermediation risk, or the SBC drag.

**What growth is implied by $181?** Roughly 15–16% owner-earnings CAGR for five years *and* a 9% hurdle rate. To justify $181 on a 9.5% hurdle you need growth to hold near 17–18% for a decade. That is a demanding ask for a company whose revenue growth was 10.8% just last fiscal year (though it has since re-accelerated).

**My buy price** — applying a 25% margin of safety to a $170 fair value — is roughly **$125–130**. The stock traded there in April 2025 and again in February 2026; it has since appreciated ~40%. The opportunity was real and has been harvested by others.

### 4. Management Quality — **Above average, with one caveat**

- **Founder-led**: Brian Chesky remains CEO and retains a substantial personal stake. Owner-operator alignment is genuine.
- **Capital allocation is disciplined and rational.** No dividend (correct — capital compounds better inside this business). Buybacks have scaled: $1.5B (2022) → $2.25B (2023) → $3.43B (2024) → $3.79B (2025) → ~$3.95B TTM 2026. Share count is down 12% in four years. No dilutive empire-building M&A — goodwill and intangibles are just $770M.
- **The caveat**: buybacks are running at an average price well above my estimate of intrinsic value. Repurchasing stock at 34x earnings is not the same as repurchasing it at 22x. A management team buying aggressively at $110–125 in early 2025 was compounding; one buying at $180+ is largely offsetting SBC dilution rather than creating value. I would prefer they let cash build if the price stays here.
- One earnings-quality flag: FY2024 net income was flattered by a ~$1B deferred-tax valuation-allowance release, which is why FY2025 net income "fell" despite revenue growth. Ignore the optics; the operating trend is fine.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

This is a genuinely good business — a Narrow, widening moat, 25% ROIC, negative working capital, essentially no capex, and net cash. It belongs on a watchlist and in a portfolio at the right price.

But my principles are not negotiable on price. **$181 against intrinsic value of ~$170 means the stock trades at a premium to fair value.** There is no discount, let alone the 20–30% margin of safety I require. The market has already paid Airbnb for the re-acceleration in growth and the AI narrative.

- **Existing holders:** Hold. Do not sell a high-return-on-capital franchise with a growing moat over a 6% valuation premium; taxes and opportunity cost likely exceed the benefit.
- **New money:** Do not buy here. Set a limit order near **$130** and be patient. If ABNB revisits $110–130 — entirely plausible given a 1.27 beta and a 52-week low of $110.81 — it becomes a **Buy**.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Regulatory restriction of supply (highest severity).** City-by-city caps and bans directly reduce the addressable inventory. A coordinated EU-wide tightening would compress both growth and the take rate. This risk is structural, not cyclical, and cannot be diversified away within the position.
2. **AI disintermediation / channel shift.** If travelers transact through AI agents or hosts cultivate direct booking, Airbnb's position as toll-taker erodes. Conversely, if Airbnb becomes the default inventory layer for AI agents, this could be upside — but I do not pay for that optionality at 34x.
3. **Growth deceleration.** The DCF is highly sensitive to Stage-1 growth. At 10% instead of 15%, fair value falls to roughly $125 — in which case today's price is a 45% premium. FY2025's 10.8% growth is a reminder that 17–19% is not guaranteed to persist.
4. **Margin pressure from brand/performance marketing.** SGA growth ahead of revenue in FY2025. If management spends to defend against Booking.com, Expedia/Vrbo, and AI entrants, the 22–23% operating margin is at risk.
5. **SBC dilution.** At 12.7% of revenue, persistent SBC would erode per-share value if buybacks are ever curtailed.
6. **Travel cyclicality.** Discretionary, high-beta exposure. A recession would hit nights booked and, because costs are largely fixed in the short run (8,200 employees), operating income would fall faster than revenue.
7. **Valuation risk itself.** At 34x GAAP earnings and 32x SBC-adjusted owner earnings, there is minimal multiple support if growth disappoints. The stock has de-rated ~35% twice in the last two years from similar levels.

**Bottom line:** a high-quality franchise, correctly but fully priced. I will not pay $181 for $170 of value. **Hold; buy below $130.**
