# Zebra Technologies (ZBRA) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price: $332.44 | Market cap: ~$15.8B | Net debt: ~$2.0B | EV: ~$17.8B**

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW**

Zebra is the global leader in Automatic Identification & Data Capture (AIDC): thermal barcode printers, rugged handhelds/tablets, scanners, RFID readers, plus the software and services layer on top. FY2025 revenue $5.40B; FY2026 run-rate ~$6.35B.

**Sources of advantage (real but not impregnable):**
- **Installed-base annuity (the best part).** Printers lock customers into proprietary label/ribbon consumables — high-margin, recurring, and sticky. Consumables + services now represent a large share of gross profit. This is a genuine razor/razorblade economics.
- **Switching costs.** Rugged mobile computers run customer-specific enterprise workflows (warehouse management, inventory, proof-of-delivery). Re-platforming a 5,000-device fleet plus retraining and re-certifying applications is a multi-year project. Zebra's Savanna data platform and Workforce Connect software deepen this.
- **Intangible assets / efficient scale.** #1 global share in AIDC, roughly peer to Honeywell and well ahead of Datalogic; a certified channel partner network of thousands that low-cost entrants cannot replicate quickly.

**Why not Wide:**
- **Still a hardware business.** Over half of revenue is cyclical capex-driven equipment tied to retail, e-commerce, logistics and manufacturing spending. FY2023 proved the point brutally: revenue **−20.7%**, operating cash flow **−$4M**, FCF **−$91M**. A wide-moat business does not go cash-flow negative in a normal inventory correction.
- **Returns are merely adequate.** GAAP ROIC 12.5% (2025), 12.5% (2024), 8.8% (2023), 10.4% (2022) vs. a ~9.5–10% WACC. The spread is thin — the hallmark of a good, not great, business. Adjusted ROIC (adding back acquired-intangible amortization) is perhaps 15–16%, better but still short of true wide-moat economics.
- **Growth has required acquisitions.** 5-year revenue CAGR is just **3.1%**, and the portfolio (Motorola Enterprise, Reflexis, Fetch Robotics, Matrox, and now **Elo Touch + Photoneo for $1.365B**) was assembled by checkbook. Goodwill + intangibles are **$5.54B = 65% of total assets and 154% of book equity**.

**Moat direction over 5 years: roughly stable, arguably slightly narrowed.** Organic growth has lagged nominal GDP, Asian hardware competition has intensified, and margin expansion in 2026 is partly cyclical recovery plus acquisition mix rather than pure pricing power. The rising software/services mix is the one clear positive.

## 4. Management Quality — **Adequate, with blemishes**

- **Capital allocation is mixed.** The 2022 buyback of **$751M** was executed at prices near $300–400 before the stock fell into the $200s — value-destructive timing. The 2025 repurchase of $587M was better timed. M&A has been disciplined on price but is now the primary growth engine, and the Elo Touch deal pushes goodwill to ~2x equity.
- **Operating execution has been strong.** Adjusted EBITDA margin has climbed from ~21% to ~23.5%, and 2026 EPS guidance has been raised repeatedly (Q2 beat of $6.35 vs. $4.36 est.; Q3 $4.95 vs. $4.89). Management guides conservatively and beats.
- **No dividend; buybacks are the only shareholder return.** Reasonable given the reinvestment opportunity, but buybacks should be stepped up meaningfully below $270 and curtailed above $400.
- **Insider ownership is modest** — a founder-operator mentality is not present; this is a professional-management company. Governance is fine, not exceptional.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

Zebra is a **good business at a fair price** — and a fair price is not a buy.

The bull case is legitimate: an installed-base annuity, stable gross margins, ~2x cash conversion, the AIDC cycle turning up (+11.3% organic in Q3 2026), and Elo Touch adding a POS/touchscreen adjacency. FY2026 adjusted EPS of $21.20 at 15.7x is not expensive for that profile.

But my discipline says: no purchase without a 20–30% discount to intrinsic value. Base-case intrinsic value is ~$330 — essentially today's price. The stock has already re-rated from $215 in May 2026 to $332, capturing the recovery. Downside is real if the hardware cycle rolls over as it did in 2023, when revenue fell 21% and free cash flow went negative.

**Action:** Hold existing positions. **Buy aggressively below $260** (≈25% discount). Trim above $400. Revisit the thesis if organic growth sustains double digits for four or more quarters *with* expanding gross margin — that would justify lifting the moat rating toward Wide and the intrinsic value toward $400+.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Cyclicality (the dominant risk).** Hardware capex tied to retail/logistics/manufacturing. FY2023 demonstrated a −21% revenue and negative-FCF outcome. One bad inventory cycle erases two years of earnings growth.
2. **Goodwill impairment.** $5.5B+ of goodwill and intangibles (and rising post-Elo) against $3.6B of equity. Any Elo or Fetch Robotics writedown hits book value directly.
3. **Earnings-quality gap.** Adjusted EPS of $21.20 vs. GAAP likely ~$12–13. Investors buying the "15.7x P/E" are buying an adjusted number that excludes ~$250M of amortization and $163M of SBC. True owner-earnings multiple is closer to 19x.
4. **Competitive intensity.** Honeywell, Datalogic, and low-cost Asian hardware vendors compress hardware pricing; Zebra's defense is consumables and software, both of which require continued R&D spend.
5. **Tariff and supply-chain exposure.** Significant China-based manufacturing; trade policy shifts hit gross margin directly.
6. **Integration risk on Elo Touch/Photoneo.** A $1.365B deal in an adjacent, lower-margin category (POS/touchscreens) at a time when leverage is already ~1.4x EBITDA.
7. **Valuation risk.** At ~12x EV/EBITDA and ~16x FCF with no margin of safety, any guidance miss is punished severely — as the −20% single-day drop on 2025-10-28 showed.

---

**Bottom line:** A narrow-moat, well-run AIDC leader with excellent cash conversion but sub-WACC-plus returns and a history of cyclical drawdowns. At $332 it is priced for its base case. **Hold, and wait for Mr. Market to offer it near $260.**
