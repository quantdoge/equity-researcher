## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US trade policy volatility / tariff re-routing (Probability: High; Impact: Medium-High)
The single largest realised geopolitical hit has been tariffs, not litigation.
- Zebra historically warned of "reliance on third-party manufacturers and suppliers, particularly in China" and "increased uncertainty and volatility in global trade policy" (FY2024 10-K risk factors).
- Management has **shifted ~80% of manufacturing out of China** (CEO Bill Burns, Morgan Stanley conference) — a genuine mitigant, but ~20% dependence plus Tier-2 component sourcing (memory, semiconductors) remains tariff- and policy-exposed.
- The **US Supreme Court invalidated IEEPA-based import tariffs**, producing a **$73M pretax refund benefit** in Q2 2026 ($46M Connected Frontline, $27M AVA); only $14M collected by Jul 4 and a further $27M by Jul 31, with the remainder a receivable subject to the **CBP refund process** (Q2'26 10-Q, via StockTitan). This creates a genuine two-way risk: refunds are not fully monetised, and replacement tariff authorities (Section 301/232, forced-labour tariffs) could reimpose cost.
- **Watch item:** the Supreme Court ruling struck down IEEPA tariffs but did *not* eliminate exposure on Chinese-origin goods (Feb 2026 ruling analysis, Gembah/Baker Botts tracker). Any adverse refund determination would reverse part of the $73M.

### Risk 2 — Memory/component supply chain squeeze with geopolitical roots (Probability: High, already materialising; Impact: High on margins)
- Q3 2026 gross margin fell 220bp to 45.7% on **memory component costs**; management flagged a **>$50M YoY Q3 headwind, ~$145M FY26 increase (up $25M from prior estimate)**, and warned **memory prices keep rising into 2027** (Zebra Q3'26 release and earnings slides; Benzinga, "Zebra warns memory prices will keep rising into 2027").
- Driver is structurally geopolitical: AI datacentre demand crowding out DRAM/NAND allocation. This is the most important *non-tariff* transmission channel from geopolitics to ZBRA earnings, and it produced the "2027 margin warning" that drove a >7% share drop despite a beat (Motley Fool, Benzinga).
- Mitigants are limited (pricing, dual sourcing); this is an input-cost exposure Zebra largely cannot hedge.

### Risk 3 — China regulatory counter-measures and IP/technology-transfer friction (Probability: Medium; Impact: Medium, tail risk High)
- China's **April 2026 State Council regulations** broadened blocking/counter-sanctions tools against foreign legal actions with extraterritorial effect, and it has begun using them (Morgan Lewis, May 2026; MoFo, July 2026).
- **Draft PRC trade-secret rules (Nov 2026)** would let Beijing restrict a foreign company's trade in goods/technology with China where foreign entities are accused of misappropriating Chinese trade secrets (Steptoe sanctions update, 2 Nov 2026; Geopolitechs). For a company with machine-vision, RFID and software IP, operating manufacturing in China and competing with domestic AIDC vendors, this is a live IP-protection and forced-localisation risk.
- Secondary: APAC carries $72M of Zebra's $519M long-lived assets — meaning physical exposure in the region is materially larger than the 12% revenue share implies.

**Lower-probability but worth naming:** USMCA review (Mexico nearshoring node) and EU AI Act compliance costs for Zebra's AI machine vision / prescriptive analytics / workforce-management software; both are second-order versus 1–3.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation (no material pending matter disclosed):**
- The FY2025 annual report states the company is "subject to a variety of investigations, claims, suits, and other legal proceedings that arise from time to time in the ordinary course" — i.e., **no quantified material contingency**.
- Patent history is the recurring theme: **Intellectual Tech LLC v. Zebra** — Federal Circuit reversed the district court on Constitutional standing (opinion 1 May 2024); the **Supreme Court declined to hear the case** (JD Supra), leaving unresolved patent-assertion risk from patent-holding entities. Patsnap records **14 patent cases** involving Zebra, by far the largest legal category.
- Historical precedent for IP settlement size: **Paxar Americas settlement — $63.75M** paid by Zebra plus a perpetual cross-licence (Justia contract archive). Useful as a severity anchor for a single IP dispute (~1.2% of annual revenue).
- A 2021 federal securities class action concluded (KTMC). No new securities or derivative suit identified.

**Regulatory / antitrust:**
- **Elo Touch Solutions acquisition ($1.3B, signed 3 Aug 2025, Kirkland & Ellis advised)** closed and is now consolidated — evidence that multi-jurisdiction merger control (US HSR, EU, others) cleared without disclosed remedy. This establishes a track record but also means Zebra now carries integration-related regulatory exposure in retail/kiosk end markets.
- Divestiture of the **Robotics Automation** business (Q1 2026, per 10-Q taxonomy) — reduces one regulatory surface (automation/robotics safety standards).
- Ongoing routine regulators: FCC/CE/product safety, conflict-minerals and supply-chain due-diligence rules, healthcare product rules for temperature-monitoring labels, and **data privacy/cybersecurity** in workforce-management and RTLS software (flagged in the 10-K as an area where "compliance with evolving laws and regulations… could increase operational costs and legal liabilities").

**Financing/documentation contingency (legal, not geopolitical):**
- Total debt **$2,776M**, of which **$2,275M is current**, tied to 2027 Credit Facility maturities management intends to refinance in 2H 2026 — unexecuted, therefore a live execution risk (Q2'26 10-Q).
- The **Fourth Amendment to the Receivables Financing Agreement (20 July 2026, PNC as agent)** granted *temporary* concentration-percentage relief for the June–August 2026 fiscal months, reverting automatically from September 2026. Read plainly, this is a lender concession on receivables concentration — worth monitoring as a signal of customer/channel credit concentration, though immaterial in size ($180M facility).

## 5. Overall Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Zebra is geographically diversified (no single non-US country above ~1/3 of revenue), has no material pending litigation or government investigation, cleared its largest-ever acquisition without disclosed antitrust remedy, and has already absorbed (and partially recovered) the tariff shock — with the Supreme Court's IEEPA ruling producing a $73M benefit rather than a cost. The rating is held at Medium rather than Low by three structural factors: (i) **~20% residual China manufacturing** inside an escalating PRC counter-sanctions/trade-secret regime, (ii) a **memory-cost shock that is geopolitical in origin and explicitly guided to worsen into 2027**, compressing gross margin 220bp, and (iii) a **refinancing-dependent balance sheet** ($2,275M current debt) that limits capacity to absorb a simultaneous trade or supply shock.

**Monitoring triggers:** reimposition of tariffs under non-IEEPA authority; adverse CBP refund determination; 2027 memory guidance revisions; PRC enforcement of new trade-secret/counter-sanction rules against US technology firms; outcome of the 2H26 credit facility refinancing.
