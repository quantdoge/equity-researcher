### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW–MEDIUM**

- **Carbon intensity: low.** Asset-light, assembly-outsourced model; emissions dominated by Scope 3 purchased goods and use-of-sold-products. A $100/t shadow carbon price on Scope 1+2 (order of 10⁴–10⁵ tCO₂e) yields single-digit-$M exposure — immaterial against ~$5B revenue.
- **Targets are real and validated:** SBTi-validated (Aug-2022) — **−50% absolute Scope 1+2 by 2030** (2020 base, 1.5°C-aligned) and **−15% absolute Scope 3** (purchased goods & services + use of sold products). Note these were validated under SBTi's pre-Net-Zero-Standard criteria and are *below* the ~42%/25% near-term threshold now expected; expect a target refresh/resubmission requirement — a modest disclosure-integrity flag, not a financial one.
- **Third-party read:** EcoVadis **Silver**; DitchCarbon climate score ~49 vs industry average ~38 (better than ~62% of industry) — mid-pack. Climate Action 100+ and The Climate Pledge: **not** participants. UN Global Compact: participant.
- **Real E risk is product/regulatory, not operational:** battery passport & replaceability, e-waste/circularity (device take-back exists but is not revenue-scale), paper/liner and label-stock sourcing (EU Deforestation Regulation), and a possible EU universal PFAS restriction affecting coatings/electronics.
- **Controversies:** no environmental fines, spills or remediation liabilities identified.

**Verdict:** operational E risk is genuinely low; the *regulatory product* E risk is the highest environmental line item and is manageable but under-provisioned in disclosure.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Supply chain (highest S risk).** Manufacturing is outsourced to Asian EMS partners. Zebra publishes a Supplier Code of Conduct that explicitly bars **forced labour, worker-paid recruitment fees, child labour and harassment**, and runs **RMI-aligned conflict-minerals due diligence** — the right architecture. But architecture ≠ assurance: no evidence found of RBA-validated audits, SMETA coverage or a supplier list, which is exactly the transparency gap that converts into **UFLPA customs detentions** or a customer-led audit failure. This is the single most plausible tail risk in the S pillar.
- **Human capital.** Headcount down materially from the 2022 peak, plus the **Fetch Robotics AMR wind-down** (announced 2025, most unit employees out by Dec-2025) and a Dec-2025 California WARN notice. Socially defensible as strategy correction, but it reverses a 2021 acquisition narrative and carries retention/severance cost and goodwill-impairment risk. Elo Touch integration (Oct-2025) adds culture/benefits harmonisation exposure.
- **Product/data social risk.** Zebra's RTLS and workforce software *track workers and assets* — increasingly a **privacy and AI-governance** issue in the EU (worksite monitoring, emotion-recognition prohibition, works-council consultation). No litigation found, but this is the fastest-rising S/G vector.
- **Product safety:** lithium-ion powered devices and healthcare temperature-monitoring labels; no recalls or safety actions identified.
- **Diversity:** board and workforce D&I metrics exist in sustainability reporting but not verified at this pass.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (mildly positive on governance)

**For the thesis: ESG is not a valuation driver for ZBRA, and that is itself the finding.** Quantitatively, identifiable ESG-driven cash-flow drag is small: carbon cost exposure is single-digit-$M; incremental EU compliance (CSRD assurance + battery/ESPR/DPP + AI Act conformity) is plausibly **$15–35M cumulative through 2028** — roughly low-single-digit % of EBITDA and defensible as largely *pass-through* into product pricing, with DPP compliance even monetisable as a customer offering.

**Where ESG could bite, and what to monitor:**
1. **UFLPA/forced-labour event in the Asian supply chain** — the one credible binary risk (revenue interruption, customer audit loss). Watch for supplier-list disclosure and RBA/SMETA audit publication.
2. **EU Battery Regulation replaceability** — if Zebra must redesign sealed devices, expect 2026–27 product-transition friction and possible share loss to Honeywell/Zebra's own channel. This is a *product-cycle* risk that equity analysts are currently modelling as normal refresh, not compliance-forced.
3. **EU AI Act high-risk classification of workforce software** — modest revenue line, but a compliance-gated market entry risk in Europe from 2026–27.
4. **Governance credibility** — the disclosure-litigation history plus a 34-year Lead Independent Director means activist/ISS pressure is conceivable; a shareholder proposal on board refreshment or an independent chair would be a low-probability, low-impact event.

**Positioning conclusion: ESG does not change the investment case — it does not raise cost of capital or threaten licence to operate.** The defensible ESG positives (clean share structure, equity-heavy pay, no live environmental or safety controversies, SBTi-validated targets, negligible carbon cost) neutralise ESG screens and support index inclusion; the negatives (supply-chain assurance gaps, board staleness, product-regulation transition) are second-order relative to the demand cycle in AIDC, retail/logistics capex and the Elo Touch integration. **Rate Neutral; do not underweight on ESG grounds, and do not pay an ESG premium.**

**Explicit gaps:** FY2024/25 Scope 1/2/3 tonnage, board diversity statistics, say-on-pay support %, and current chair identity were not verifiable from primary filings within this budget — all flagged above rather than estimated.
