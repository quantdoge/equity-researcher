### 1. Macro Environment Summary: **RISK-ON ASSETS / RISK-OFF RATES ("Transitional-Hawkish")**

The US is in a **re-tightening regime**, not an easing one. After cutting the funds rate ~170bp from the 5.33% 2023–24 peak down to 3.63% by mid-2026, the Fed — under new Chair Kevin Warsh — **hiked 25bp on 16 September 2026 to a 3.75–4.00% target** (IORB 3.90%) as inflation re-accelerated. September CPI rose **+0.6% m/m / +3.7% y/y**, with core firmer; three-month annualised inflation is running **~4.6%**, versus ~3.3% y/y in July. October's FOMC is expected to hold.

The critical — and unusual — feature is a **bear steepener**: the 10Y sits at **~5.0%** (5.31% on 5 Oct, a 24-year high) and the 30Y at **5.37%**, while the 2Y at **4.65%** trades ~90bp *above* the effective funds rate. That means the market is pricing either further hikes or a durable term-premium/fiscal-deficit premium. Financial conditions are still accommodative in *credit* — HY OAS at just **330bp** — so this is a rates shock, not a credit event. Real growth is intact (~2.2%), unemployment is low and falling (4.2%), and nominal GDP is compounding near **6%** — a genuinely good environment for a lender's nominal revenue, and a genuinely bad one for long-duration equity multiples.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

SoFi is a **deposit-funded consumer lender with a long-duration growth equity attached** — it is exposed to rates through four distinct channels.

**Negative:**

- **Discount rate / valuation (highly negative).** Beta 2.33, most earnings value in out-years. A 10Y moving 4.5% → 5.0% in three months mechanically compresses the multiple ~10–15%. This is the single best explanation for the stock falling from ~$18.60 (9 Jul) to $14.22 (14 Oct) *despite* improving fundamentals, and for it being down ~37% YTD even after Tuesday's 13% pop. It is a multiple de-rating, not an earnings problem.
- **Funding cost and deposit beta (negative).** A hiking Fed keeps high-yield savings competition elevated. SoFi's NIM expansion (NII $929.5M, +58.8% y/y in Q3) has come from asset repricing outrunning deposit costs — that lever gets harder to pull if the Fed hikes again and peers reprice deposits.
- **Loan sale / ABS economics (negative).** Wider long-end spreads and higher term premium raise the discount on whole-loan sales and securitisations, pressuring gain-on-sale revenue and fair-value marks.
- **Mortgage/HELOC and student-loan refi (negative).** 30Y mortgage near 7% and a 5% 10Y suppress origination volumes in exactly the segments a rate-cut cycle would unlock. The refi business remains structurally impaired.
- **Refinancing / capital cost (modest negative).** Terming out debt or convertibles at 5%+ is far more expensive than 2021-vintage paper.

**Positive:**

- **Nominal GDP ~6% (positive).** Personal-loan demand, member growth and balances expand with nominal income. Q3 originations and revenue ($1.39B vs $1.25B est) confirm this — high nominal growth is a tailwind for a lender's top line.
- **Credit quality (positive).** With unemployment at 4.2% and falling, personal-loan net charge-offs improved to **3.4%, down 80bp y/y**, and management reaffirmed a 7–8% maximum cumulative net-loss assumption. Tight HY/ABS spreads corroborate a benign consumer-credit backdrop — the macro risk most feared in 2023–24 has not materialised.
- **Front-end real rates (mild positive).** If the Fed holds at 3.75–4.00% while asset yields reprice higher, NIM can still drift up.
- **Fee-based revenue mix (natural hedge).** The Loan Platform Business and Tech Platform (Galileo, Apex, Technisys) are largely rate-insensitive, so an increasing share of revenue is insulated from the rate channel.
- **Currency (neutral).** Revenue is overwhelmingly domestic; USD strength is a minor translation drag on the small international tech-platform book, not material. Tariff exposure is indirect (consumer goods inflation squeezing borrower disposable income).

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL (with a negative skew)**

Nominally supportive (6% nominal GDP, 4.2% unemployment, 330bp spreads, credit improving) but **multiple-hostile** (5% 10Y, 24-year-high long yields, a Fed that just hiked, 2Y pricing more). The operating environment is good for SoFi's *income statement* and poor for its *P/E*. Net neutral — and the asymmetry is negative because the market is currently pricing the discount-rate channel, not the earnings channel. If the Fed hikes again, the balance tips to **Unfavourable**.

### 5. Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (Bear)

**A hawkish policy error into a stalling consumer.** Consensus assumes the September hike was the last and the Fed holds through 2027. The realistic surprise is the opposite: sticky core CPI near 3.5% plus deficit-driven term premium pushes the 10Y through 5.50% and the Fed to 4.25–4.50% by mid-2027. Unemployment breaks 4.8% as real incomes compress, personal-loan charge-offs revert from 3.4% toward 5%, ABS spreads widen from 330bp to 500bp+, and gain-on-sale revenue stalls. For a 2.33-beta name, the multiple compresses to ~8–9x and the stock revisits **$10–11 (−35%)** even if revenue guidance is met. Note the stock's own history: it fell 15% on 29 Apr and 9% on 29 Jul 2026 on modest disappointments, and 7% on 14 Oct as the 10Y pushed to 5.3% — the tape is already highly sensitive to this channel.

*Upside mirror case:* an inflation rollover (shelter + tariff unwind) lets the Fed cut back to 3.00% by mid-2027, the 10Y falls to 4.2%, and the multiple re-rates to 25–28x — **$25–28 (+55–70%)**. Both tails are wide; that is the point.
