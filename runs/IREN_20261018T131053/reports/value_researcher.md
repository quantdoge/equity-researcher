## IREN Limited (NASDAQ: IREN) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price:** $26.99 (16-Oct-2026) | **Market cap:** ~$10.6bn | **EV:** ~$12.6bn (net debt $1.94bn) | **52-wk range:** $14.00 – $76.87 (−65% from high)

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **None today, Narrow/emerging at best**

IREN (formerly Iris Energy) is a vertically integrated owner of powered land, data-centre shells and compute. It is executing one of the most dramatic pivots in the market: from Bitcoin miner to AI cloud lessor.

**What the moat is supposed to be:** 2.15GW of secured grid interconnection, owned (not leased) substations and shells in BC and Texas, an NVIDIA 5GW strategic partnership (5-yr warrant on 30M shares at $70, up to $2.1bn), a $9.7bn Microsoft AI cloud contract, $2.8bn of incremental contracts with AI developers, and a ~$4bn contracted ARR target on 480MW of AI cloud capacity by year-end 2026.

**Why it is not yet a moat by Buffett standards:**
- **Bitcoin mining (~80% of FY26 revenue) is a pure commodity.** No pricing power, no switching costs, no customer relationship. Cost-curve position is real but competable, and hashprice/BTC ($81.8k) sets the return.
- **AI cloud is a capital lessor with no switching costs.** Customers (Microsoft plus a handful of "leading AI developers") can and will multi-source, in-source, or move at contract end. IREN's durable asset is the *interconnection queue position* — a genuine local efficient-scale advantage, but regulatory and time-limited, not a franchise.
- **ROIC has never exceeded WACC.** ROIC: −15.8% (FY23), −2.5% (FY24), +0.8% (FY25), −8.7% (FY26). A moated business earns above its cost of capital *consistently*. IREN has not done so in any year as a public company.
- **Moat trend:** has *improved* — the pivot is real, the Microsoft contract is with an AA credit, and NVIDIA's warrant is meaningful third-party validation. But the moat is being purchased with $4.3bn of annual capex and 8x leverage growth. A moat you must buy at any price is not a moat.

**Conclusion:** Outside my circle of competence. The five-year economic outcome depends on 2030 GPU rental prices, AI capex cycle duration, and financing terms — none of which are knowable with the precision a value investor requires.

**Positives:** revenue CAGR ~160%; gross margin structurally improved from 28% to 69%; FY26 Adjusted EBITDA of $245.7m (first materially positive EBITDA year); $5.9bn cash on hand.

**Serious negatives:**
- **Cumulative four-year FCF of −$3.9bn. Owner earnings negative in every year** (−$257m, −$458m, −$1,104m, −$4,618m). The business has consumed, not produced, capital for its entire listed life.
- **FY26 operating cash flow of $2.10bn is an illusion.** It contains a **+$1.82bn working-capital inflow** — largely customer prepayments/deferred revenue, mirrored in $3.8bn of non-debt liabilities. Ex-WC, OCF is ~$275m, consistent with Adjusted EBITDA. The "cash generation" is customer money parked on the balance sheet, not earnings power.
- **$638.8m of non-cash impairments in FY26 ($450.4m in Q4 alone)** — a direct admission that prior mining-fleet capital was mis-sized or mis-timed. Equity is now $4.19bn against $15.79bn of assets, with $6.76bn of PP&E exposed to further write-down.
- **Leverage:** debt from $0.96bn to **$7.84bn in one year**; net debt $1.94bn. Convertibles at ~1.00% coupons were cheap, but the ~$51.40 conversion strike is now ~90% out of the money — that is debt to refinance, not equity.
- **Dilution:** share count up ~9.6x in four years (41m → ~394m), plus 30m NVIDIA warrants.

### 3. Valuation Analysis — Intrinsic value ~$30/share; **no margin of safety**

The standard DCF tool is unusable here (negative trailing FCF base produces a nonsense −$196m output). Valuation must be scenario-based.

**Current multiples:** EV/Sales 17.8x trailing; **EV/Adjusted EBITDA ~51x**; EV/contracted ARR ~3.1x; P/B ~2.5x on 394m shares (BVPS ~$10.6). No dividend, no buyback. Peer median P/E of 43.7x is meaningless (data feed returns mega-cap semis as "peers"; the true comps — CoreWeave, Nebius, Applied Digital, Cipher, TeraWulf — were unavailable, an acknowledged gap).

**Scenario build (FY2030 steady state, discounted at 12%):**

| | Prob. | EBITDA | EV/EBITDA | Net debt | Equity value | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear** — AI capex digests, GPU rental −40%, distressed asset sales | 30% | $1.2bn | 6x | $8bn | ~$0.5bn | **~$2** |
| **Base** — 480MW–1GW delivered, contracts perform | 45% | $2.6bn | 8x | $5bn | ~$16bn | **~$27** |
| **Bull** — 2–5GW NVIDIA build-out, pricing holds | 25% | $6.0bn | 10x | $10bn | ~$50bn | **~$80** |

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $32/share**, range $2–$80.

**Critical cross-check on the base case:** at 480MW and $4bn ARR, GPU-heavy assets depreciate over 6 years against a 2–3 year economic life. Blended D&A on ~$14bn of installed capital is roughly $1.7bn against ~$2.5bn EBITDA — pre-tax earnings of only ~$500–700m. **Adjusted EBITDA massively overstates owner earnings here.** On that math, the $10.6bn equity is being asked to pay ~15–20x earnings that have not yet been earned, for a business that has consumed $3.9bn of cash.

**Verdict on price:** at $27 the stock sits ~15% below my probability-weighted value — *inside* the estimate's error band, not at a 25–30% discount to it. Analyst targets ($40 Redburn to $100 Bernstein; median ~$85) reflect growth-multiple faith, not owner-earnings math. I would not begin to be interested above $20, and only with a basket approach.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Mixed (6/10)**

**Strengths:**
- Genuine owner-operators: co-founders Will and Dan Roberts hold B-class shares carrying **15 votes per share** via Awassi Capital. Interests are aligned with the equity, and they have repeatedly sold equity at high prices ($50–70) rather than at the lows — opportunistic, shareholder-conscious financing.
- Execution speed is real: 480MW of AI cloud stood up in roughly 18 months, major hyperscaler contract landed, NVIDIA partnership signed.
- Financing creativity: 1.00% converts, a $2.1bn NVIDIA warrant struck at a 23% premium. Cheap capital and third-party validation.

**Concerns:**
- **Capital allocation record is poor on the numbers:** $638.8m of FY26 impairments, ROIC never above WACC, 9.6x dilution since IPO, zero return of capital.
- **Governance:** dual-class control means minority holders have essentially no influence; there is no dividend or buyback discipline to check empire-building.
- **The 2022 episode is the cautionary template:** when BTC fell, two non-recourse SPVs were handed to lenders and equity in those vehicles was wiped out. The capital structure today ($7.84bn of debt against an unproven revenue ramp) can transfer value away from shareholders with similar speed.
- **Disclosure complexity** (adjusted EBITDA, impairments, prepayment-funded OCF) demands a sceptic's discount.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

This is a leveraged, venture-style bet on AI infrastructure demand and financing conditions — an excellent *trade* for a growth-mandate investor, but not a value investment.

The disqualifiers, in Buffett terms:
1. **No demonstrable moat** — no year in which ROIC exceeded WACC.
2. **No reliable owner earnings** — negative in all four years; cumulative FCF −$3.9bn; Adjusted EBITDA overstates cash economics given 6-yr GPU depreciation against 2–3 yr economic lives.
3. **No margin of safety** — ~$32 probability-weighted intrinsic value versus $27 price, with a bear case near zero. An estimate whose range is $2–$80 cannot anchor a 30% discount.
4. **Dependence on the kindness of capital markets** — the value accrues to whoever funds the next $10bn of capex, and the NVIDIA warrant signals that even partners price that risk at a 2.6x premium to today.

If already owned after the 65% drawdown, I would use strength to exit rather than average down. Any position should be sized as a lottery ticket (≤1%), held in a speculative bucket, and explicitly excluded from the value portfolio. Revisit only below ~$20/share *and* with proof of positive owner earnings and ROIC > 10%.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (both directions)

**Risks that would make me wrong (upside):**
- NVIDIA's 5GW partnership converts into firm, funded capacity contracts; AI compute pricing proves stickier than feared.
- Sustained 60%+ EBITDA margins at scale with 15-year leases, converting to genuine ROIC > 15%.
- Bitcoin rallies hard, restoring high-margin mining cash flow to fund capex without dilution.

**Risks to the equity (downside):**
1. **GPU obsolescence/pricing deflation** — the single largest risk; depreciation schedules assume lives the market may not grant.
2. **Refinancing wall** — $7.84bn of debt, converts ~90% out of the money at $27; further equity issuance at depressed prices is the rational path.
3. **Customer concentration** — Microsoft dominates contracted revenue; hyperscalers increasingly in-source capacity.
4. **Bitcoin exposure** — BTC $81.8k; ~80% of FY26 revenue is still mining, and falling hashprice hits both revenue and the mining fleet's carrying value.
5. **Further impairments** on $6.76bn PP&E; FY26 already took $638.8m.
6. **Rates** — 10-yr at 5.10% compresses long-duration, no-earnings equities; IREN's beta of 4.27 magnifies this.
7. **Execution** — transformer, turbine and grid-interconnect bottlenecks on a 480MW year-end deadline; contract penalties for delay.
8. **Governance** — dual-class control with no shareholder check.

**Bottom line:** a real business with a real strategic position, priced as though the hard part is already done — while the balance sheet ensures the hard part is still ahead. **Not a value investment. Sell/Avoid.**
