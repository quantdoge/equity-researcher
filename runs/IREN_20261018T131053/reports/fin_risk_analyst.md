# IREN Limited (IREN) — Financial Risk Assessment

**Company:** IREN Limited (formerly Iris Energy) · Sydney, AU · Technology / "Software–Services" classification (in reality: vertically integrated Bitcoin mining + AI/HPC data centers)
**Fiscal year end:** 30 June · Latest reported period: **FY2026 (ended 30-Jun-2026)**, filed 27-Aug-2026
**Market cap:** ~$10.6bn (profile); ~$8.5bn implied by the FCF-yield tool — **inconsistent, see data gaps**
**Business model:** Owns data centers, electrical infrastructure and compute hardware in Australia/Canada. Revenue is dominated by Bitcoin block rewards, with a large capital pivot toward AI/HPC cloud hosting under way.

> ⚠️ **Framing note:** IREN is not a balance-sheet-clean software company. It is a capital-intensive, commodity-price-exposed compute landlord that has just **8x'd its debt and 5x'd its asset base in twelve months while revenue was falling**. The risk profile must be read through that lens.

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: SUB-INVESTMENT GRADE (high-yield / deeply speculative, ~CCC+ profile)**

The paradox: **solvency on a liquidity basis is genuinely strong; solvency on an earnings basis is close to non-existent.**

| Dimension | Reading | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Net debt / EBITDA | **55.98x** | Effectively unserviceable from operations |
| Interest coverage (EBIT/interest) | **−17.67x** | Negative — interest paid from cash, not earnings |
| Debt / equity (book) | **1.87x** | Manageable |
| Net debt / market cap | ~0.18x | Low on market terms |
| Equity / assets (book) | **0.27x** | Thin; retained earnings −$1.30bn |
| Altman Z-Score | **0.497 — Distress zone** | x2 (RE/TA) −0.082, x3 (EBIT/TA) −0.024 |
| Piotroski F-Score | **4/9 — Weak** | Fails on ROA, leverage, dilution |

**Why not outright "Distress":** the debt is **long-dated and cheap**. Long-term debt is $7.42bn against only **$169m of short-term debt**; the convertibles carry coupons as low as **1.00% due 2033** (plus a $500m tranche at 3.50%). There is **no refinancing cliff** inside 24 months. Cash of **$5.90bn** covers current liabilities ($2.22bn) 2.65x.

**Why not Investment Grade:** FY26 EBITDA was **$34.7m** on $7.84bn of debt, and the *most recent quarter* printed **negative EBITDA (−$32.8m)**. Interest expense is ramping fast — $9.3m (Sep-25) → $14.8m (Mar-26) → **$24.5m (Jun-26)**, an annualised run-rate of ~$100–160m. The company does not earn its cost of debt; it *funds* it.

**Altman Z caveat:** the 0.497 reading is structurally biased against a company in pre-revenue-ramp heavy capex, but the two genuinely diagnostic components (retained earnings and EBIT relative to assets) are both negative — the signal is real, not just a model artefact.

**Capex has grown 9x in two years while revenue turns down.** FCF margin on FY26 revenue is **−316%**.

**Cash runway (base case):** $5,896m cash ÷ $2,233m annual FCF burn = **~2.6 years** at the current rate with no new financing — *but* the Jun-26 quarter alone consumed **$1.98bn of capex**. Sustaining that quarterly rate implies ~$7.9bn/yr and would exhaust cash in **under 9 months** absent further raises. The genuinely discretionary lever is capex; the non-discretionary one is interest (~$120–160m/yr run-rate).

**Important mitigation:** the equity and convertible markets are demonstrably open to this name (see dilution below). Funding access — not solvency — is the binding variable.

## 7. Key Red Flags

1. **🔴 Revenue down 43% from its Sep-25 peak while debt rose 8x.** The core operating business is contracting *into* a historic leverage ramp. This is the dominant risk.
2. **🔴 Negative interest coverage (−17.7x) and negative EBITDA in the latest quarter.** Debt service is funded by the balance sheet, not operations. Warranted coverage requires EBITDA >$1.2bn — roughly **35x** the FY26 figure.
3. **🔴 Altman Z 0.497 (Distress) and Piotroski 4/9 (Weak).** Both independent models agree the fundamental quality is poor.
4. **🔴 FCF −$2.23bn, FCF yield −26.2%, capex up 9x in two years.** Cash generation capacity is deeply negative and worsening.
5. **🔴 41% share count growth in 12 months**, with a further $3bn convertible overhang. Per-share value is being structurally eroded.
6. **🔴 Beta 3.0–4.3, vol ~110%, max drawdown 65.6%.** In a genuine market or crypto drawdown, expect a 60–80% loss of equity value. Sortino unavailable — tail-risk quality cannot be formally confirmed.
7. **🟠 SG&A +253% YoY to $449m (63.5% of revenue)** with revenue falling — classic late-cycle cost indiscipline in a capex boom.
8. **🟠 Revenue/contract concentration.** Bitcoin block rewards plus a small number of large AI/HPC counterparties; single-customer failure would be material.
9. **🟠 Data-integrity flags in reported figures** (below) mean the FY26 picture cannot be taken fully at face value.

| **Dilution risk** | **High** | +41% shares in 12m, $3bn convert overhang |
| **Credit rating view** | **Sub-Investment Grade** | Not a default candidate; a structurally subordinated, cash-funded, dilution-dependent credit |

**Bottom line:** IREN will **not run out of money in the next 24 months** — the $5.9bn cash pile, 2033 convert maturities and 1% coupons see to that. The risk is not bankruptcy; it is **permanent capital impairment through dilution and multiple compression**. The company is asking investors to fund a $4.3bn/yr capex program out of a business whose revenue has fallen 43% in three quarters and whose EBITDA is now negative. On a market cap of ~$10.6bn against $707m of *declining* revenue, the equity is priced for a hyperscale AI hosting outcome that has not yet appeared in the numbers. **Any 20% revenue shock, or a market drawdown of 20%, implies a 50–80% equity decline and a high probability of a further dilutive raise.** Risk is asymmetric and skewed heavily to the downside.

**Key monitors:** (1) quarterly revenue trend and whether the AI/HPC pivot offsets mining decay; (2) whether quarterly capex decelerates from the $1.98bn Jun-26 run-rate; (3) interest expense trajectory vs EBITDA; (4) any new equity or convertible issuance; (5) share count each quarter; (6) the reconciliation gap between operating cash flow and net income.
