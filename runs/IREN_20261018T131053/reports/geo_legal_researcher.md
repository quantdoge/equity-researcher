### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Texas/ERCOT political intervention on large-load interconnections (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
Governor Abbott's **3-Aug-2026 directive** ordered the PUCT and ERCOT to conduct a "comprehensive verification audit" of every data-centre project in the ERCOT interconnection queue and **paused approvals** until completion (Akin Gump; White & Case; HK Law). This sits on top of **SB 6 (signed 20-Jun-2025)**, which imposed new large-load interconnection standards, and PUCT financial-commitment requirements. IREN's growth engine — Childress phase roll-outs underpinning the Microsoft contract and the $25–30bn FY27 capex plan — depends on Texas load approvals and transmission timing. Risk vector: schedule slippage, higher security deposits/credit support, mandated capex on generation or "behind-the-meter" self-supply. Already-energised capacity is largely insulated; incremental phases are not.

**Risk 2 — British Columbia electricity re-regulation (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High)**
BC's **Bill 31 (Energy Statutes Amendment Act 2025)** (Royal Assent 27-Nov-25) replaced first-come-first-served grid access with government allocation by industry priority. The **Supply Regulation (in force 1-Feb-26)** caps new BC Hydro capacity at **100MW conventional + 300MW AI data centre** for two years, **max 145MW per project**, halving from Feb-2028, with a proposed **permanent ban on new crypto-mining grid connections** (BLG; DCD; Bloomberg). Implications: (a) BC expansion is effectively closed beyond existing capacity — all incremental growth must go to Texas/Spain/Australia; (b) the residual Bitcoin mining footprint in BC faces obsolescence risk, consistent with FY26's **$638.8m impairment** and commencement of mining-hardware decommissioning. Water-use rules under the Water Sustainability Act add a second provincial front.

**Risk 3 — AI compute policy, GPU export controls and tariffs (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium-High)**
IREN's value proposition is NVIDIA GB300 allocation resold to hyperscalers. US BIS export-control rules, Section 232/semiconductor tariff actions, and any tightening of allied-country compute policy can raise GPU landed cost, constrain allocation, or impose contractual compliance reps with termination/cure rights. Because all current GPU deployments are in the US and Canada, direct export-control exposure is low today — but the **Bundey (Australia) and Spain** campuses will require clean re-export authorisations, and US policy on allied data-centre compute is a live variable. Secondary transmission channel: the Microsoft contract ties ~$9.7bn of contracted revenue to the US hyperscaler AI capex cycle — a US policy/antitrust shock to that cycle is IREN's largest single demand risk.

*Honourable mentions:* Australian approvals at Bundey (state development consent, transmission, and FIRB/ACCC overlay); Spanish grid permitting and EU energy-price politics; counterparty/sovereign none (no Russia, China, or sanctions-exposed revenue).

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action (active/recent, EDNY):** Following the **11-Jul-2024 Culper Research short report** ("A Prius at the Grand Prix"), multiple firms (Rosen, Kessler Topaz, Kaplan Fox, Levi & Korsinsky, Robbins) solicited lead plaintiffs for a putative class action in the Eastern District of New York, with a **6-Dec-2024 lead-plaintiff deadline**. Allegations centre on misrepresentation of asset strength/potential. I could not confirm dismissal or settlement in available sources — treat as an **unquantified contingent liability** (typically covered by D&O, but a discovery/costs and headline overhang). A **prior putative class action was filed 14-Dec-2022** in the same district per the FY24 10-K. Pattern of shareholder litigation around short reports is a governance signal.
- **Australian corporate-law architecture:** Incorporation under the Corporations Act means FIRB approval thresholds (A$1,464m for US acquirers, A$339m general), the **20% takeover prohibition**, and a **new mandatory and suspensory ACCC merger-clearance regime effective 1-Jan-2026** — any notifiable acquisition put into effect without clearance is **void by operation of law**. This applies to M&A and, per the company's own EX-4.1, potentially to acquisitions of its notes. Practically: caps on takeover optionality/premium, and execution friction on any IREN acquisition strategy.
- **Dual-class control:** Two B Class shares (Awassi Capital trusts / founder-affiliated) carry **15 votes per ordinary share** plus the right to designate Founder Directors. Effective founder control constrains activist and M&A outcomes — a governance discount item.
- **Multi-jurisdiction tax and equipment financing:** Operations span Australia (parent), Canada (Dell Canada leasing, Canada Revenue Agency interactions) and the US — transfer-pricing, withholding and permanent-establishment exposure across three regimes; the heavy use of vendor leasing (Dell) and debt to fund GPUs adds covenant/cross-default sensitivity.
- **Regulatory perimeter:** SEC/Nasdaq reporting as a foreign private issuer filing 10-K/10-Q; ASIC and ASX-adjacent obligations as an Australian public company; BCUC in Canada; PUCT/ERCOT in Texas.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Sanctions/AML via mining pools (Low-Medium):** Bitcoin mining revenue is settled through pools whose other participants can include sanctioned or tainted hashrate flows. Absent robust OFAC/sanctions-screening evidence, this is a standing (sector-wide) enforcement touchpoint.
2. **Export-control compliance representations (Medium):** Contracts with NVIDIA/Dell and the Microsoft services agreement will carry ECCN 3A090/4A090 and end-use/end-user covenants; breach triggers cure/termination rights over the crown-jewel contract.
3. **Data privacy/EU AI Act (Medium, forward):** AI Cloud hosting for frontier labs implies GDPR (Spain), PIPEDA (Canada), Australian Privacy Act and EU AI Act customer-obligation pass-throughs; a cross-border data-transfer incident would be a first-order reputational and contractual event.
4. **Founder-controlled capital structure + short-seller litigation history (Governance flag):** Reduces minority influence and increases headline/reputational volatility.
5. **No evidence found** of DOJ/SEC/FTC/ACCC enforcement actions, FCPA matters, or sanctions designations against IREN or its subsidiaries.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Basis:**
- **Geopolitical: Medium.** Every operating jurisdiction is a developed, allied, rule-of-law market; there is no sanctions, expropriation, currency-control, or EM exposure. Risk is *policy* risk, not sovereign risk.
- **Regulatory: High.** Two of IREN's three growth jurisdictions (Texas, British Columbia) have *already enacted or ordered* interventions specifically targeting energy-intensive data centres within the last 12 months. This is not tail risk — it is the base case operating environment, and it directly gates the ~5GW pipeline that the equity story is priced on.
- **Legal: Medium.** One unquantified securities class action with a documented short-seller trigger; no known government investigation; a corporate-law framework (FIRB, ACCC suspensory regime, 20% threshold, dual-class) that materially restricts strategic optionality.

**What would move this to High/Very High:** a Texas audit outcome that suspends Childress/Sweetwater phases beyond contracted delivery dates; a BC order impairing existing (not just new) capacity; new US export-control or tariff action on NVIDIA hardware hitting contracted GPU economics; or an adverse ruling/certified class in the EDNY action.
