### 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **LOW**

| $M | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 187.2 | 501.0 | 707.0 |
| Gross profit (marginal) | 82.1 | 342.0 | 487.3 |
| SG&A | 50.0 | 127.2 | **449.1** |
| Operating income | (27.3) | 22.1 | **(1,046.7)** |
| Net income | (28.9) | 86.9 | **(702.6)** |
| EBITDA | 25.1 | 285.7 | **34.7** |
| EBITDA margin | 13.4% | **57.0%** | **4.9%** |
| Capex | 479.9 | 1,372.6 | **4,333.1** |

Reported revenue grew 41% while EBITDA collapsed 88%. The gap between gross profit ($487M) and operating loss ($1,047M) is **$1,085M — larger than SG&A**, driven by **$638.8M of non-cash impairments in FY26 ($450.4M in Q4 alone)**, "primarily related to" the mining fleet. The company has already written off a large slice of the asset base the legacy business was built on.

**The single profitable quarter of FY26 was not operational.** Q1 FY26 net income of **+$384.6M** came against an operating loss of **–$76.4M** — a **$461M non-operating swing** (digital-asset/warrant fair-value items). Strip it and every quarter of FY26 lost money. That is the textbook pattern: the headline EPS print is an accounting event, not a cash event.

**Gross margin is an artifact.** Depreciation of $417.7M is nearly **2x the entire cost of revenue ($219.7M)** — i.e., D&A sits largely below the gross-profit line. IREN's "68.9% gross margin" is not comparable to any peer and should not be used in valuation work. The honest margin is EBITDA margin: 4.9%.

### 2. Key Red Flags

1. **Revenue is rolling over.** Q1 FY26 $240.3M → Q2 $184.7M → Q3 $144.8M → **Q4 $137.2M**. Peak-to-trough **–43%**; Q4 YoY **–26.8%**. The Bitcoin mining base is shrinking while the company spends $4.3B/yr of capex on the next business.
2. **The FY26 operating cash flow of $2,100M is not earnings — it is customer money.** OCF/revenue = 297%. Q4 alone generated **$1,811M of OCF against a $684M net loss**, with only $36.8M of working-capital movement identified — the residual is **customer prepayments** (the Microsoft contract), reported as operating inflow. Adjusted operating cash flow is roughly **$280–300M**, against $4.3B capex → a true FY26 FCF deficit of **~$4.0B**, funded by debt (net debt $400M → $1,944M; gross debt $0.96B → **$7.84B**) and equity.
3. **Dilution is structural, not tactical.** Weighted shares: 99.6M (FY24) → 214.6M (FY25) → 316.1M (FY26) → 362.0M (Q4 FY26) = **+263% in 24 months**. SBC alone ~$173M in FY26 (24% of revenue).
4. **Asset productivity is collapsing.** Total assets went $2.94B → $15.79B (+$12.85B) to generate **$206M of incremental revenue**. Asset turnover fell from 17.0% to **4.5%**. Cumulative FY24–FY26 capex of $6.2B has produced $1.45B of cumulative revenue and $3.79B of cumulative negative FCF.
5. **Receivables +134.8% vs revenue +41.1%** (AR $25.0M → $58.7M). Small in absolute terms, but the wrong direction, and a repeat offender (FY24: AR +348.8% vs revenue +147.9%).
6. **Balance-sheet risk:** total liabilities $11.60B / assets $15.79B (73.5%); equity/assets 26.5%; debt/equity 1.87x. Ex-debt liabilities of **$3.77B** now contain the customer prepayments — cash on hand is partly refundable customer money, not shareholder capital.
7. **Altman Z-Score 0.497 = Distress zone** (x2 retained earnings/assets –0.082; x3 EBIT/assets –0.024; x5 asset turnover 0.045). **Piotroski F-Score 4/9**, with failures on ROA improvement, leverage, dilution and asset turnover.
8. **Sector misclassification:** IREN is tagged **"Technology / Software – Services."** It is a capital-intensive data-centre/commodity-compute operator. Screening it against software multiples flatters it.

Note also that the tool's negative cash-conversion figures are themselves a signal of how useless headline OCF has become here: it is a financing line item wearing an operating label.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

- **Legacy moat: none.** Bitcoin mining is a pure commodity conversion of electricity + hashprice. IREN's cost position is not proprietary, and its own impairment charge is management's admission that the ASIC fleet lost value faster than modelled.
- **Emerging moat: unproven and sub-scale.** The AI pivot is real (Microsoft contract, prepayments as validation) but IREN is a **price-taking landlord** competing against CoreWeave, Crusoe, TeraWulf, Hut 8 and the hyperscalers' own build programmes — all with cheaper capital and longer customer relationships.
- **Technology obsolescence risk is double-counted.** GPU/ASIC depreciation cycles are 3–5 years; IREN is levering 8x into assets that deflate. That is the classic boom-era error.
- **Consensus already encodes the bear case.** Analyst FY30 estimates: revenue **$25.3B** but net income only **$421M — a 1.7% net margin** (and *below* the FY29 net income estimate of $639M on $13.2B of revenue). The sell-side's own model says this becomes a gigantic, low-return capital business. Dispersion is enormous (FY30 revenue range $15.4B–$37.0B, 1–2 analysts). Meanwhile the stock trades at **15x trailing sales** on today's $707M. You are paying a software multiple for a business whose own bull case implies commodity economics.

### 5. Management & Governance Red Flags

- **Dual-class control:** 2 B-Class shares held by the founders' Awassi Capital trusts carry **15 votes per ordinary share held**. The Roberts brothers control the company with a negligible economic stake — a 75% special-resolution threshold is effectively unattainable without them.
- **Insider selling at the top:** co-CEOs **William and Daniel Roberts** and Christopher Guzowski executed "substantial share sales" in **September 2025**, with the stock $30–47 and en route to $76. A cluster of **seven Form 4s filed 1 July 2026** immediately after fiscal year-end. **Zero insider purchases on record** during a period in which the company asked shareholders to fund a multi-billion-dollar build-out. Insiders sell into strength; they do not buy the 65% drawdown.
- **Narrative-vs-delivery gap:** promotional "AI cloud" language against revenue that fell 43% sequentially, a $639M impairment, and a 263% increase in share count.
- **Not fraud — but promotional.** No auditor change or related-party issue surfaced in my work; the accounting concerns here are classification and presentation (prepayments in OCF, D&A outside COGS, non-operating gains inside net income), not fabrication.

### 6. Short Thesis Summary — **Yes, there is a credible bear case**

1. **Valuation held up by a revenue number that is falling.** $10.6B market cap on $707M of trailing revenue that declined 27% YoY in the latest quarter, with EBITDA margin down from 57% to 5%.
2. **Cash flow is optically excellent and economically weak.** ~$2.1B OCF is customer prepayment, not earnings; true FCF is ~–$4.0B/yr; funded with 8x more debt and 3.6x more shares.
3. **The clean-numbers story is an artifact.** Beneish looks "low risk" only because prepayments corrupt the accruals input; adjusted, it lands in warning territory. Altman 0.497 = distress. Piotroski 4/9.
4. **The bull case requires flawless, rapid, cheap execution** of a $4B+/yr capex programme in a commoditising market, while consensus itself models a 1.7% terminal net margin.
5. **Asymmetric downside:** at a 4.27 beta, a single quarter of AI-contract slippage, a financing window closing, or another mining impairment compresses the equity violently. The $5.9B cash pile is a genuine mitigant, but a meaningful part of it is refundable customer prepayment.

**The other side, stated honestly:** this is *not* a fraud short. If the Microsoft contract is real and prepaid, IREN has genuine contracted revenue and one of the better power portfolios in the sector; $5.9B of liquidity removes near-term solvency risk, and the AI-revenue ramp from a $707M base to multi-billion dollars, if delivered, would justify a much higher price. Shorting a high-beta name with a signed hyperscaler contract and a funded balance sheet is how short sellers get carried out. Position accordingly: size small, prefer put spreads or a long-peer/short-IREN pair over naked short, and use the FY27 quarterly AI-revenue disclosure as the trigger.

### 7. Contrarian Rating: **SHORT** (moderate conviction — not "Strong Short")

Quality of earnings is genuinely poor and the multiple is indefensible against the company's own decelerating fundamentals. But the balance sheet is funded, the contract is real, and the fraud signal is absent. This is a **valuation-and-capital-allocation short**, not a solvency or accounting-fraud short — which caps conviction one notch below the maximum.
