## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Current stance: TRANSITIONAL — "higher-for-longer on the long end, easing on the short end."**

The macro regime is defined by a striking disconnect in the US rate structure. The Fed has delivered an easing cycle — the effective federal funds rate fell from a 4.33% peak (March–July 2025) to **3.63–3.75%** by Q3 2026 — yet long-term yields have moved *against* the Fed: the 10-year Treasury backed up from ~4.3% in late April 2026 to **5.24–5.28%** in early October 2026. The curve is positively sloped (+34bp 2s10s as of Oct 6) but *steepening from the top*, i.e., a term-premium/sovereign-supply repricing rather than a cyclical one. This is the single most important macro fact for IBM: a low-beta (~0.71), bond-proxy equity with heavy cash-flow duration is on the wrong side of a ~100bp backup in long rates within six months.

Growth remains acceptable — real GDP +2.2% YoY in Q2 2026 — but inflation is sticky (headline CPI +3.0–3.4% mid-2026; core CPI +2.4–2.5% YoY), with a tariff/energy-inflected spike in headline CPI between Feb–May 2026 that confirms upside risks are live. Unemployment has drifted from 4.0% (early 2025) to 4.2–4.3% — a normalizing labor market, not a recession signal. Investment-grade credit spreads (Baa ~6.5% vs. 10Y ~5.3%, ~120–150bp) are stable: no credit stress. The broad dollar index sits at **121.4 (Oct 2)** — near multi-year highs and *rising* (~+2.7% YTD 2026) — a direct headwind to IBM's heavily international P&L.

**Net:** risk-off in flavor for long-duration, ex-US revenue tech names. IBM is being compressed by discount-rate and currency channels at the same time its own guidance is being cut (July 2026, to 4–5% constant-currency growth). The stock's path — ~$306 in January 2026 → ~$250 in April → ~$220 in October — tracks the backup in the 10Y almost tick-for-tick in its drift phases.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Weighing the channels: positive on growth/labor/credit, but **negative on the two variables that move IBM's equity value most** — long-term real/nominal rates (discount-rate compression) and the dollar (FX translation drag) — with inflation sticky enough to keep long yields bid. The market is already telling us which channel wins: IBM drifted ~12% lower from April to October 2026 while the 10Y backed up ~100bp. This is **unfavourable but not acute** — no recession, no credit stress, growth positive — so the call is a cautious "unfavourable, driven by rate + FX," not a risk-off blanket.

## 5. Material-Surprise Scenario (BEAR)

**"Term-premium breakout + dollar squeeze" (bear, ~20–25% probability):**
Inflation re-accelerates (the Feb–May 2026 headline spike showed the mechanism: tariffs plus energy), core CPI pushes back toward 3%, and the market concludes the Fed cut too early. The 10Y breaks higher from ~5.3% to **5.75–6.0%** as term premium re-prices (sovereign supply + curve steepening), even as the short end stalls. Two amplifiers hit IBM simultaneously: (1) a further multiple compression on its bond-proxy equity (10Y +50–70bp ≈ another 8–12% de-rating, toward high-$180s/low-$200s); and (2) safe-haven flows push the dollar from 121 toward 125, deepening the FX drag exactly as IBM guides 4–5% growth and clients keep prioritizing AI chip/infrastructure capex over IBM consulting. The result compounds: **weaker reported USD growth than guided, margin pressure on labor, and a de-rated multiple** — the full stack of IBM's macro risks firing together. This is the scenario to hedge against, and it is the direction the market is already drifting.

*Bull-side counterpoint for balance:* if instead disinflation reasserts (core <2.2%), the 10Y fades to ~4.3%, and the dollar reverses 3–4% on a EUR/GBP recovery, IBM's constant-currency growth would translate ~1:1 into reported USD with a re-rating toward the high-$200s — a genuine double-barreled recovery. But that is the *lower* probability path today.
