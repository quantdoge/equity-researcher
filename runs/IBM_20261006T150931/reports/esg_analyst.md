## 1. ESG Materiality Map

For an IT-services business (~264k employees, ~$63B revenue, services-heavy mix), financial materiality is concentrated in **human capital (S), client-confidentiality/government-contracting conduct (G), and energy/carbon of AI and data-center operations (E)** — not in manufacturing footprint.

| Factor | Financial Materiality | Channel to Value |
|---|---|---|
| **S: Workforce composition & litigation** | **High** | Age-discrimination class actions and DEI-related False Claims Act settlement; legal expense, government-contract risk, talent cost |
| **G: Government-contracting compliance** | **High** | Federal contractor revenue; DOJ "Civil Rights Fraud Initiative" enforcement now targets exactly IBM's conduct |
| **G: AI ethics & oversight** | **High** | EU AI Act exposure as AI provider/deployer (watsonx); enterprise clients in banking/airlines buy on trust |
| **E: Energy intensity of AI/quantum** | **Medium-Rising** | $10B+ quantum commitment and AI growth raise electricity demand, emissions trajectory, client pressure |

---

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **Low–Medium**

- **Carbon intensity:** Low (services-dominant). IBM targets **net-zero by 2030** — ahead of the 2050 norm — with GHG reduction of ~65% vs. 2010 baseline and **>90% renewable electricity** achieved (2022 ESG data; 2025 CDP-style status unverified).
- **Transition risk:** Low. No stranded-asset or carbon-tax exposure; CBAM immaterial for services exports.
- **Physical risk:** Low-Medium. Data centers/offices vulnerable to extreme weather and water stress in arid regions; mitigated by global footprint diversification.
- **Key watch item:** Gen-AI and quantum compute growth is the one vector that could bend IBM's emissions curve upward — this is the flagship E-risk to monitor, and IBM itself monetises it (Envizi, Cloud Carbon Calculator; "State of Sustainability Readiness" products).

---

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **Medium (leaning High on workforce conduct)**

- **Age-discrimination litigation (chronic):** Multiple class actions (Liss-Riordan filings; 2018 ProPublica/Mother Jones investigation) alleging systematic displacement of workers 40+. Ongoing litigation exposure and reputational drag — the single largest S risk.
- **DEI False Claims Act settlement:** $17.1M settlement (April 2025) with DOJ over claims IBM's career-development program discriminated on race/sex — resolved but signals enforcement exposure for a major federal contractor.
- **Mitigants:** strong human-capital programs (SkillsBuild, New Collar), union-relations track record, and product safety risk near-zero for enterprise software/services. Supply-chain labour standards monitored but moderate risk.

---

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

- **−** **CEO/Chairman duality:** Arvind Krishna holds both roles — a governance negative.
- **+** Single-class share structure (one share, one vote); no poison pill.
- **+** Long-tenured, majority-independent board; say-on-pay passed at 2025 AGM; compensation is performance-linked (relative TSR/operating metrics).
- **+** One of the earliest formal **AI Ethics Boards** (2018) with published principles — mature for the AI regulatory era.
- **−** Disclosure gap: 10-K/DEF 14A text unavailable in this research pass; rely on public ESG reporting (CDP-disclosing, ISO/sustainability reporting).

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US:** SEC climate disclosure rule (currently stayed/litigated) — IBM already discloses beyond its scope; **DOJ Civil Rights Fraud Initiative** and state anti-DEI laws are the live, higher-stakes exposure given IBM's federal-contractor status.
- **EU:** **CSRD** applies to IBM's EU operations; **EU AI Act** binds IBM as AI provider/deployer (watsonx) — mitigated by mature AI governance; **CBAM** negligible for services.
- **Indirect:** Clients in regulated industries (banks, airlines, government) increasingly pass through ESG procurement requirements (e.g., German Supply Chain Act due diligence).

---

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **Average** (Leaning Leader on E/G disclosure; S-dimension drag)

IBM scores *Leader* on environmental ambition/transparency and mature AI-governance structure, but the combination of **chronic age-discrimination litigation and a 2025 DEI-related federal settlement** caps the rating at Average. The S-dimension — not E or G — is the binding constraint on a higher rating.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **Neutral (slight Positive tilt)**

- **Positive:** Net-zero-by-2030 commitment lowers transition risk; AI-governance maturity is a differentiator as EU AI Act bites; ESG/Sustainability software (Envizi, Carbon Calculator) is a genuine **revenue tailwind** from ESG regulation, not a cost.
- **Negative/watch:** Employment-litigation overhang and federal-contractor enforcement risk could raise legal spend and threaten government revenue in a worst case; AI compute energy growth may pressure the E narrative.
- **Verdict:** ESG factors do not undermine the long-term thesis; monitor the age-discrimination docket and DOJ contracting posture. **Neutral for the thesis, with upside optionality from ESG software growth.**
