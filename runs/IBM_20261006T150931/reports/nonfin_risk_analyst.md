# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — International Business Machines Corporation (IBM)
**Prepared for investment memo | Risk horizon: next 12 months | Data through Oct 2026**

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate (with concentration flags)**

**Evidence:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality confirmed**: Arvind Krishna serves as *Chairman & CEO* — a concentration of power that makes CEO-level execution and disclosure failures a direct board-governance issue (relevant to the July 2026 events below). IBM otherwise maintains a large, majority-independent board; **Frank Baker was elected to the board effective Oct 1, 2026**, signaling refresh activity.
- **Compensation/alignment**: 31 consecutive annual dividend increases, FCF guidance ~$15.5–15.7B for FY26 — payout policy favors shareholders. No compensation red flags surfaced.
- **Related-party transactions**: none identified in coverage.
- **Insider behavior**: Insiders registered *modest net buying* — ~$417K of insider buys over the trailing three months and director Howard's 50-share open-market purchase (Feb 2026). However, Form 4 volume spiked in batches on **Jul 2, Aug 28, and Oct 1, 2026 (13 filings)** — the Oct 1 cluster smells like compensation-linked sales/RSU events, and the timing straddles the July 14 crash. Transaction-level buy/sell attribution was not retrievable from this feed; **this is a data gap requiring disclosure-level review** (see Red Flags #2/#3).

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **ACTIVE — Securities-law investigation (highest-profile item)**: Shareholder-rights firm **Hagens Berman is investigating potential violations of U.S. securities laws** after CEO Krishna "previewed" disastrous Q2 2026 results on **July 14, 2026**; the stock fell ~25% in one session (close $288.16 → $215.52, volume 10× normal, **$68B market cap erased** — worst one-day crash in IBM history per CNBC). Q2 revenue came in at $17.2B vs ~$17.9B expected, with **mainframe/Z infrastructure sales collapsing 42%**, and FY26 constant-currency growth guidance was cut to 4–5% from >5%. Core allegation: **selective disclosure / Regulation FD and Rule 10b-5 exposure**.
- **No SEC/DOJ securities enforcement** found in EDGAR full-text search for IBM (search_regulatory_actions returned zero hits).
- **DOJ employment resolution**: DOJ announced (Apr 10, 2026) that IBM agreed to pay **>$17M** to resolve allegations it violated certain employment laws; specific statutory basis unconfirmed from available sources.
- **Employment-discrimination litigation cluster**: ongoing multi-year **age-discrimination** litigation (ex-IBM employees; two federal appellate courts have held IBM severance releases defective, letting age claims proceed; 29 former employees have sought Supreme Court review). America First Legal has also sued IBM and subsidiary Red Hat (2024) over alleged race/sex discrimination. Cumulative exposure is real but long-running and case-by-case.

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis (trailing 12 months)
- **Aggregate signal: neutral-to-modestly-buying.** Small open-market insider buys (incl. director Howard, Feb 2026) and ~$417K insider purchases over three months are positive signals and **contradict an insider "dump before the crash" narrative** at the aggregate level.
- **Caution**: 13 Form 4s filed Oct 1, 2026 (one day after the crash quarter closed) — likely pre-scheduled compensation/RSU transactions, but **no pre-crash insider selling pattern is evident from available data**. Institutional holders trimmed (Neville Rodie & Shaw −3.7%, Envestnet −4.1%) while Nykredit built a new ~261K-share position.
- **Verdict**: no fraud signal; moderate transparency gap on Form 4 transaction detail.

## 4. Top Operational Risks
1. **Mainframe (IBM Z) concentration risk** — a single product family drove a 42% revenue collapse in Q2-2026 and guided the stock crash. "Z shortfall" (demand timing/supply) makes Infrastructure revenue lumpy and cyclical; a miss in the mainframe refresh cycle now materially moves the whole P&L.
2. **Recurring large-scale restructuring risk** — ~14,000 layoffs in Oct 2025, further UK/global cuts and "workforce rebalancing" in H1 2026 (with the "8,000 out, 6,000 openings" skills-rebalancing narrative). Repeated reductions create execution, knowledge-transfer and key-person attrition risk in a consulting-heavy business where delivery capacity = revenue.
3. **AI-hype dependency / key-person execution risk** — IBM's turnaround thesis (watsonx, AI consulting book reported at $5.5B, IBM Bob agentic platform) hinges on credibility that the July crash damaged; guidance credibility is now itself an operational asset at risk. No material cybersecurity incident against IBM's own systems was found (it is the industry's breach-cost benchmark), though its reach into banking/airline/retail critical systems makes it a high-value target.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment
- **Damage is material but containable.** The July crash generated sustained negative financial press ("worst crash in its history"; Motley Fool: "I still wouldn't buy it today"), and the combination of mass layoffs + discrimination litigation + DOJ settlement keeps a steady employment-practices narrative alive. On the positive side: the Bob self-hosted/AI-sovereignty pivot (Oct 1) and Accenture-driven consulting-sector tailwinds generated favorable coverage; board refresh and dividend consistency support institutional trust. No ESG controversy (pollution, supply-chain labor) surfaced.
- **Rating: Moderate-High**, driven primarily by guidance-credibility and workforce-morale headlines rather than product or ESG brand damage.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Drivers: (a) an active securities-law investigation tied to a one-day $68B wipeout; (b) cumulative employment litigation + DOJ resolution; (c) severe single-product (Z) revenue concentration; (d) reputation/guidance credibility under strain. Not rated *Critical* because: no SEC/DOJ securities enforcement yet filed, no fraud/accounting-irregularity evidence, governance is mature and boards are being refreshed, and insider behavior shows no dumping.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation
1. **Hagens Berman securities investigation (Reg FD / Rule 10b-5)** — track for: class-action filing, SEC subpoena, and DOJ securities-fraud referral. This is the report's #1 tail risk and is unresolved.
2. **Timing of CEO's July 14 preview vs. guidance cut** — the company's disclosure process around the Z shortfall needs scrutiny; if insiders or the CEO communicated selective detail pre-earnings, exposure is direct.
3. **Form 4 cluster (Oct 1, 2026) and any July insider sales** — require transaction-level verification that no executives sold into or before the crash window (10b5-1 plan or otherwise).
4. **Forecast integrity** — FY26 guidance was cut, then re-affirmed (per Oct 2026 coverage); a second cut or miss would compound litigation and credibility damage.
5. **Employment-practices tail** — appellate rulings voiding IBM severance releases extend age-discrimination exposure; monitor for a consolidated class action and further DOJ action.
