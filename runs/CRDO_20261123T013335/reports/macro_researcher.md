# Macroeconomic Assessment — Credo Technology Group (CRDO)
**As of 20–23 November 2026**

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Risk-Off for duration assets — a hawkish re-tightening regime.**

The US economy is in an unusual configuration: **solid real growth, sticky-to-rising inflation, and a Federal Reserve that has pivoted back to *hiking*.** The Fed cut to a 3.63% effective rate by mid-2026, then delivered its first hike since 2023 on 16 September 2026 (+25bp to 3.75–4.00%), held on 28 October, and the 18 November minutes showed "almost all officials" expect further hikes "in the months ahead." October CPI at **3.82% YoY** is a three-year high; core PCE is **+3.12% YoY** and running ~3.5% annualized over the last two months — re-acceleration, not decay.

The single most important fact for CRDO: **the entire 69bp rise in the 10-year yield since 30 June (4.44% → 5.13%) is real.** 10Y breakevens are unchanged at ~2.24–2.25% (4.44 − 2.20 in June vs 5.13 − 2.88 in November). The 10Y TIPS real yield has gone from **2.20% to 2.88%** — a pure discount-rate/term-premium shock, not an inflation-expectations shock. Real GDP grew **+3.37% SAAR in Q3 2026** (+2.07% YoY), unemployment is **4.1%** (down from 4.5% a year ago), and credit is *not* stressed — HY OAS at **3.52%** remains ~100bp below its long-run median despite widening 87bp from the 2.65% September low.

This is a "bear steepening, no landing" backdrop: 2Y 4.74% → 10Y 5.13% → 30Y 5.47%, 2s10s at **+39bp**. The curve is not signaling recession; it is pricing term premium and fiscal supply. For a 3.3-beta, long-duration growth equity, this is the worst possible macro mix short of an actual downturn.

**CRDO has already paid the price.** The stock is **−67% from its 22 June 2026 peak of $302.52**, closing at **$99.86** on 20 November ($18.77B market cap, ~188M shares). Critically, this is *not* fundamental deterioration: Q1 FY27 (reported 1 September) delivered **$479M revenue, +115% YoY**, and EPS of $1.20 vs. $1.12–1.17 consensus. On a ~$1.92B revenue run-rate the stock has de-rated from roughly **29.7x sales at the June peak to ~9.8x today** — about 20 turns of pure multiple compression against accelerating fundamentals.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The macro variables that matter most to CRDO — real yields, Fed policy direction, and the financing cost of AI infrastructure — are all pointed the wrong way simultaneously. A 3.29-beta, 25x-forward-growth semiconductor cannot hold a premium multiple against a Fed that has resumed hiking into 3.8% CPI with real 10Y yields at 2.88%. That is precisely what the −67% drawdown reflects.

**Important nuance, and where I differ from a pure bear read:** the macro risk is *substantially priced*. At ~9.8x run-rate sales (~5x plausible FY28 revenue) with revenue growing 115% and no recession signal from the curve, labor market, or credit spreads, the multiple has already absorbed most of a 2.25% → 2.88% real-yield move. The macro backdrop is unfavourable but no longer obviously *expensive* — the asymmetry has shifted toward the upside, contingent on the rates path.

**Note on positioning:** CRDO reports **1 December 2026** (~1 week out). Macro regime will not decide that print; AI capex orders and guidance will. But in this tape, a beat may not be rewarded and a miss will be punished at 3.3 beta.

## 5. Surprise Scenario (Bear, with quantified impact)

**The scenario that would materially surprise: a term-premium/fiscal-supply shock that pushes the 10Y to ~6% *without* a growth scare.**

The setup is already visible: 30Y at 5.47%, breakevens anchored at 2.25%, and the entire move in long yields coming from real yields rather than inflation expectations. That is the signature of duration/fiscal risk, not demand risk. If heavy Treasury supply and a hawkish Fed (say, two more hikes to 4.25–4.50%) drive the 10Y to 6.00% and 10Y TIPS to ~3.4%, then:

- CRDO's multiple compresses from ~9.8x to **6.5–7.5x** run-rate sales → **$65–75 per share (−25% to −35%)**, despite unchanged revenue estimates.
- The secondary transmission is AI financing: with 35% of 2027 hyperscaler capex debt-funded, a 100bp+ rise in corporate funding costs is exactly the sort of event that triggers capex *guidance* revisions — which would hit CRDO's revenue line, not just its multiple.
- Why it counts as a surprise: consensus assumes the Fed is near terminal and that AI capex is a secular, rate-insensitive constant. Both assumptions break at the same time in this scenario.

**The mirror (bull) case is equally sharp:** one soft December CPI print that takes the Fed's remaining hikes off the table and pulls 10Y TIPS back to 2.40% would re-rate CRDO toward 14–16x run-rate sales — **$140–165 (+40% to +65%)** — with zero change to fundamentals. That convexity is the honest summary of CRDO's macro position: **the business is executing, the macro is the swing factor, and the payoff is now two-sided.**
