# CRDO — Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd
**Price $99.86 (Nov 20, 2026) · Mkt Cap $18.77B · EV $17.38B · Beta 3.29**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Credo owns the physical layer of the AI cluster. Its proprietary SerDes/DSP architecture gives it roughly 70%+ of the merchant Active Electrical Cable (AEC) market — the low-power copper links that stitch together GPU racks at 400G/800G today and 1.6T tomorrow — and it is now converting from a copper pure-play into a vertically integrated copper **plus** optical stack via the $1.25B DustPhotonics (silicon photonics) and Hyperlume (microLED active LED cable) acquisitions. Revenue has compounded **88.6% since FY2022** and more than tripled to $1.34B in FY2026, yet the stock has collapsed **67% from its $302.52 June peak** on a single unresolved structural question: does copper AEC survive the 1.6T/co-packaged-optics transition, or is this a peak-cycle franchise?

That question — not the fundamentals — is what is for sale at 4.5x FY2028 sales.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

**What's supported by third-party data:** the merchant AEC market is roughly $1.2B today (2025) and third-party forecasters model a ~61% CAGR as 800G gives way to 1.6T. Credo holds ~70%+ of merchant AEC (some buyside estimates 90%+) and, critically, is the *qualified* second source inside multiple hyperscalers — a qualification moat that takes 12–24 months to replicate.

**Why the revenue already exceeds headline TAM estimates:** those "market size" figures undercount hyperscaler-direct copper programs, and Credo's $1.34B includes SerDes IP licensing, retimers, PCIe and optical DSP alongside AEC cables. Credo's real serviceable pool — AI datacenter interconnect spanning copper AEC + optical DSP/retimer + SerDes IP + optical transceivers — is best framed as **$10B+ by FY2030**, on which consensus FY2031 revenue of $6.25B implies only mid-single-digit share. That is the bull case in one line: the company does not need to win the market, only a slice of a very large one.

**Penetration today vs. potential:** rack-scale copper attach is still early — copper content per AI rack rises with scale-up domain size, and every doubling of radix expands the AEC count. Credo is <15% penetrated against its own FY30 SAM.

**The genuine bear counterpoint:** Broadcom's Tomahawk 6 CPO switch carries a **>$10B AI switch backlog**, and CPO is designed precisely to displace pluggables/copper at the switch face. Management's answer — that CPO is costlier and less reliable, and that near-package optics with microLED (their Hyperlume bet) is 1/3 the power — is technically credible but unproven at scale.

- **Dec 1, 2026 — FQ2'27 earnings (the single biggest near-term event).** Consensus $531.8M revenue (+98% YoY) / $1.29 EPS. A 7th consecutive beat plus a raise would be the first fundamental data point to break the downtrend; a guide-down breaks the thesis.
- **1.6T AEC qualification announcements (FQ2–FQ3'27).** Confirmation of a 1.6T copper socket at a top-3 hyperscaler is the direct refutation of the CPO-displacement bear case.
- **Hyperscaler capex guides, late Jan–Feb 2027** (MSFT/AMZN/GOOGL/META Q4 prints). CRDO is a high-beta derivative of four capex budgets; this is the swing factor.
- **DustPhotonics optical revenue contribution (FY2027–FY2028).** First meaningful optical DSP/transceiver revenue would prove the copper→optical bridge is real, expanding TAM rather than defending it.
- **TE Connectivity settlement / licensing.** Removes a legal overhang and could establish a royalty stream — modest but a clean-up catalyst.
- **Customer diversification** — any named second hyperscaler or neocloud win disclosed at >10% of revenue would materially de-risk the concentration discount.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (high conviction, size for volatility)

**The arithmetic:** the market is capitalizing CRDO at 3.0x consensus FY2031 revenue. To justify $99.86 you must believe revenue plateaus near $3–4B or gross margins structurally break. Consensus says $6.25B of revenue and $13.45 of EPS in FY2031 — a 7.4x P/E on a company with a 70% share of a growing interconnect standard. My base case: FY2028 revenue $3.5–3.9B, ~25% net margin, 20–22x earnings → **$190–210**, versus a bear case of ~$40–50 (CPO wins, revenue peaks at $2.5B, 3x sales) and a bull case of $300+ (1.6T copper + optical both land).

Risk/reward on those numbers is roughly 2.5:1 in favor, which justifies a Buy but not a Strong Buy — because the CPO question is a genuine technology-outcome risk, not a sentiment problem, and one customer at 33–43% of revenue means a single socket decision can invalidate the model overnight.

**What upgrades this to Strong Buy:** a Dec 1 beat-and-raise *plus* a named 1.6T AEC win. **What kills it:** gross margin below 62% with decelerating AEC orders, or a hyperscaler disclosing CPO-first scale-out.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Customer concentration (highest-order risk).** One customer = **33% of FQ1'27 revenue (43% per 10-Q detail) and up to 57% of receivables.** Dependence on 3–4 hyperscaler capex budgets means a single procurement shift is existential, not merely painful.
2. **CPO / optical displacement.** Broadcom's >$10B TH6 CPO backlog is the clearest evidence that the industry's largest switch vendor is building the replacement for copper at the switch face. Credo's response (microLED ALC, DustPhotonics silicon photonics) is a hedge that must execute — the company is spending $1.25B to buy optionality against its own core product.
3. **Gross margin compression.** 68.0% → 64.5% in one quarter. Mix shift to AEC/optical hardware plus acquisition amortization means the Rule of 40 of 236 is a peak, not a plateau. Model 62–66% forward.
4. **Balance sheet degradation.** Cash fell to $466.9M from $1.16B to fund DustPhotonics; goodwill-heavy $3.01B asset base; **$222.2M of non-cancelable purchase commitments** and inventory of $313.1M — if demand normalizes, that inventory and those commitments hit margins simultaneously.
5. **Competitive intensity.** Marvell, Broadcom, MaxLinear, Astera Labs, plus the permanent threat of hyperscalers in-sourcing their own SerDes. Credo's 70% share is precisely what invites well-funded attack.
6. **Estimate reliability.** FY2030–FY2031 consensus rests on **1–2 analysts**. The long-dated numbers I used above are directionally useful, not bankable — treat the "7x FY31 earnings" as illustrative.
7. **Volatility/beta 3.29.** Expect 40%+ drawdowns as routine. This position must be sized for a 50% permanent-loss scenario, not a 15% stop.

---

**Bottom line:** The operating business is executing at the top of the entire semiconductor universe — 115% growth, 64% gross margins, 30% FCF margins, a 100% beat rate. The stock's 67% collapse is a sentiment-and-structure event (Nvidia leadership shock, AI-trade unwind, VIX 36), occurring while estimates went *up*. That is the dislocation a growth investor is paid to buy. The two things that keep me at Buy rather than Strong Buy are the 33–43% single-customer exposure and the unresolved question of whether copper AEC is a multi-decade standard or a two-generation bridge to optics. Dec 1 answers more of that than any model can.
