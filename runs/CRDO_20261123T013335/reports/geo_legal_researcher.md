# CRDO — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company:** Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd (Cayman Islands exempted company; HQ San Jose, CA; fabless AI-connectivity semis). FY2026 (ended 2 May 2026): revenue $1.34B, +206% y/y; net income $472.3M; 807 FTE (477 in Asia, 330 North America).

*Note on data sourcing: the SEC filing-text tool returned no documents for CRDO (Cayman filer / accession mismatch), so geography and legal detail below are sourced from the FY2026 10-K summary, third-party filing aggregators, ITC/agency notices and company press releases. Segment percentages should be re-verified against the 10-K before client distribution.*

**Greater China (HK + mainland + Taiwan) = 36.1% of revenue (~$482M).** Hong Kong alone is a quarter-plus of the business and is, in substance, a booking channel into PRC AI/ODM customers rather than a domestic market — sell-side commentary describes HK revenue as flowing "directly into Chinese AI clusters." That single line item is the largest identifiable geopolitical concentration in the P&L.

**Operational footprint:** subsidiaries in mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore, Canada, Israel and the US; offices also in Taiwan. Manufacturing is fully outsourced: **TSMC is the exclusive wafer foundry**, with a small group of Asian OSATs and cable partners. The May 2026 **DustPhotonics acquisition (~$750M cash + ~1M shares, closed 28 May 2026)** added Israeli silicon-photonics operations and a Tower Semiconductor foundry relationship — a new Middle East conflict/export-licensing vector in the optical roadmap.

**Customer concentration (the real tail risk):** top 10 customers ≈90% of FY26 revenue; two customers ≥10% each; Q4 FY26 top three at 34%/27%/16%. Note: a subsidiary-share pledge covering entities in "mainland China, Hong Kong, Singapore, Canada, Israel and the United States" appears in the filing set, implying secured debt covenants that could constrain cross-border asset transfers/repatriation — verify against the credit agreement.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US–China export-control escalation hitting the HK/China channel — Probability: Medium-High | Impact: High**
Macro backdrop as of Nov 2026: Trump–Xi summit produced a truce expected to be extended, but Eurasia Group's Ian Bremmer warns "tighter chip restrictions could prompt measured Chinese retaliation." The BIS Affiliates Rule is only **suspended through 10 Jan 2027** — a hard cliff edge that re-imposes broad end-use/affiliate licensing. Credo's products sit in AI-cluster interconnect; progressive controls on AI infrastructure plus end-user screening of HK distributors could impair 25–35% of revenue. Mitigant: US revenue grew 1,080% y/y, mechanically diluting Greater China from ~76% of revenue in FY25 to ~36% in FY26 — the mix is improving fast, but the absolute HK dollars are still growing (+55% y/y).

**② Taiwan Strait / single-foundry chokepoint — Probability: Low (disruption) | Impact: Very High**
Every Credo wafer comes from TSMC. No second source at leading-node SerDes. A blockade, quarantine, or even a prolonged cross-Strait exercise would halt shipments within a quarter. This is a low-probability/high-severity tail, not a base case, but it is unhedged — note Credo's own 10-K flags "trade restrictions (especially involving China), tariffs or supply disruptions."

**③ Section 232 semiconductor tariffs + Mexico/Asia assembly — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium**
A Section 232 semiconductor investigation has been running (referenced in peer Marvell's FY2026 10-K and in buy-side commentary). Credo's cost of goods flows through TSMC (Taiwan), Asian OSATs and Mexican assembly. Tariffs on semis or on Mexican content would compress gross margin, and the 2026 USMCA review adds renegotiation risk. Credo likely has limited pricing power against hyperscalers to pass through tariffs.

*Honourable mention:* China's expanding tech-export and critical-minerals/rare-earth controls (Pillsbury, Nov 2026) could disrupt cable/connector/optical component inputs independent of US action.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **AEC patent war — effectively resolved (a material de-risking event).** Credo filed an ITC Section 337 complaint on **13 March 2025 (Inv. No. 337-TA-1446)** against Amphenol, Molex, TE Connectivity and Volex, with parallel district-court suits. Settlements on confidential terms: **Molex** (joint motion to terminate, 26 Mar 2026), **TE Connectivity** and **Amphenol**, and finally **Marvell (announced 17 Nov 2026)** covering mutual AEC patent rights. This removes the largest identifiable legal contingency and validates Credo's IP estate. Residual exposure: possible open Volex/remaining respondents and **USPTO PTAB/IPR petitions** challenging Credo patents (e.g., petition 1557707) — an invalidation would narrow the moat even without damages.
- **No confirmed securities class action.** Multiple plaintiff-firm "shareholder alerts" (e.g., Holzer & Holzer) following the post-earnings drawdowns exist, but I found no filed, docketed class action. Treat as noise unless a complaint is filed; the stock's −15.6% move on 20 Nov 2026 and −20% post-Q4 print were driven by concentration/valuation, not disclosure failures.
- **M&A regulatory:** DustPhotonics required customary approvals (HSR/Israel) and closed 28 May 2026 — no CFIUS friction reported. Future acquisitions remain exposed to CFIUS given the Cayman/China subsidiary structure and Chinese-linked shareholder base (e.g., Lip-Bu Tan's stake).
- **Regulatory posture:** No known DOJ/BIS/SEC enforcement action. Cayman incorporation provides no US-tax shield for the operating subsidiaries; IRS transfer-pricing scrutiny of China/HK/Singapore IP ownership is a standing (low-visibility) tax controversy risk.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Hong Kong distribution/end-use diligence (elevated).** 28% of revenue booked in HK with PRC end-customers is precisely the profile BIS examines for diversion and unverified end-users. Rank-and-file compliance risk is low today, but reputational/enforcement optionality is non-trivial and rising post-Jan 2027.
2. **FCPA/AML:** Low — customer base is concentrated, named hyperscalers, and direct-sales. Not a red flag.
3. **Data privacy:** Low — PILOT diagnostic software has limited PII surface.
4. **No sanctions nexus identified** (no Russia/Iran/Syria exposure disclosed).

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** ~36% Greater China revenue centered on a Hong Kong booking channel, unhedged single-foundry (TSMC) dependence, and added Israel/optical supply-chain complexity post-DustPhotonics. It is **not "Very High"** because (a) mainland China direct revenue is now only 6%, (b) US revenue has become the dominant and fastest-growing region, and (c) the multi-front AEC patent litigation — the single biggest legal overhang — was fully settled between March and November 2026, converting a binary damages/injunction risk into a confidential, closed item.

**Key monitoring triggers:** reinstatement of the BIS Affiliates Rule (Jan 2027); any HK revenue deceleration or distributor de-registration; Section 232 semiconductor tariff determination; a filed securities class action; PTAB institution decisions on Credo AEC patents.
