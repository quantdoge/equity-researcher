## SECTOR BRIEF — CRDO (Credo Technology Group)
**Sector:** Technology / Semiconductors — AI Data-Center Connectivity & High-Speed Interconnect (SerDes, AEC, optical DSP)
**Report date context:** last close $99.86, market cap $18.77B, beta 3.29

---

### 1. Sector Overview

Credo competes in the AI data-center **high-speed connectivity** niche — active electrical cables (AECs), SerDes/DSP chiplets, retimers and optical DSPs that move 100G–1.6T signals between GPUs, switches and memory inside hyperscale clusters. Demand is a direct derivative of hyperscaler AI capex, which we size at **$700–725B in 2026 and ~$1.0–1.125T in 2027** (H1-2026 alone ran $301B across MSFT/META/AMZN/GOOGL; sell-side 2027 forecasts have been revised up ~66% since January). The underlying cable/interconnect hardware TAM is far smaller but growing fast: data-center cabling ~$13.6B (2025) at ~12.7% CAGR, active optical cable $5.0B (2025) → $13.4B (2033), and AEC specifically forecast at a **23.1% CAGR 2027–2035**. The sub-sector is **oligopolistic and consolidating** — Broadcom, Marvell (+Lumentum, ~$115B pending deal), Arista, Astera Labs, Coherent and Credo — with the top handful of vendors controlling the overwhelming majority of AI networking silicon content, and hyperscaler customers holding countervailing power through dual-sourcing and in-house silicon.

**Cycle position:** *Fundamentals in late-stage hyper-growth; equity multiples in a de-rating/mean-reversion phase.* These two facts are diverging sharply, and that divergence is the single most important thing to understand about this sector today.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Hyperscaler capex is still accelerating, not decelerating.** Group AI capex of ~$700–725B (2026) → **>$1T (2027)** implies ~40–55% growth at the top of the funnel; networking/content-per-accelerator rises faster than capex itself as cluster sizes scale (more nodes ⇒ super-linear cable and SerDes content).
- **Copper-to-optics runway plus copper's persistence in short-reach.** AEC remains the lowest-power, lowest-cost option for intra-rack and rack-to-rack at ≤2–3m; 1.6T upgrades reset the speed node and re-open the socket for a new SerDes generation, which is exactly where Credo's IP is strongest. Optical DSP/AOC expansion (23%+ CAGR) gives Credo an additive second leg.
- **Sector profitability has inflected structurally.** Peer-set median gross margin 61% and operating margin ~22%, with the leaders (Arista 42.8% OM, Broadcom 39.9%) proving this is a genuine IP-margin business, not commodity optics. Credo's own OM went from −19.2% (FY24) to **+33.3% (FY26)** in two years — operating leverage in this niche is real and violent in the right direction.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Co-packaged optics (CPO) and linear pluggable optics (LPO) are an architectural substitute, not a cyclical dip.** Nvidia's accelerating CPO deployments are the clearest signal that optics are moving onto the switch/GPU package — directly threatening the pluggable/AEC sockets that generate most of Credo's revenue. This is the primary reason the whole group de-rated in 2026 despite record results.
- **Consolidation is raising competitive intensity against a sub-scale player.** Marvell's ~$115B combination with Lumentum creates a single vendor offering custom AI silicon *plus* electrical *and* optical interconnect — precisely the bundle Credo sells à la carte. Broadcom ($230B AI revenue targeted by FY28) and Astera Labs (37x sales, 115% growth) squeeze from both ends.
- **Extreme customer concentration and hyperscaler in-sourcing.** CRDO's **top-3 customers ≈88% of revenue**, with one hyperscaler reported at >50%. Hyperscalers are simultaneously the buyers, the dual-sourcing agents, and increasingly their own chip designers — the worst possible combination of buyer power (see Porter's below). A single architecture decision or digestion quarter can move the model by 30%+.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (with a narrow moat)**

**Rationale for "strong":**
- **Best-in-class unit economics for its scale.** 68% GM / 33% OM at $1.3B revenue in a hardware-adjacent category, with gross margin up 10.4pts in two years and operating margin swinging 52pts — evidence of pricing power and SerDes IP value, not price-taking.
- **Share gainer, demonstrably.** Growing 206% while the median peer grows 33% means Credo is taking share inside a growing TAM, not just riding capex. Seven consecutive quarters of triple-digit growth.
- **Efficiency model:** $1.65M revenue/employee on 807 staff, a fabless IP-led structure that scales without proportional capex.
- **Near-term overhang removed:** the Nov 2026 patent settlement with Marvell eliminated a binary legal risk in its core AEC product line.

**Why not "very strong":**
- **Moat is narrow, not wide.** The moat is SerDes mixed-signal IP + 12–18 month hyperscaler qualification cycles. Both are real but erodable — Astera, Marvell, Broadcom and Chinese entrants all possess credible SerDes capability, and qualification moats reset at each speed node (1.6T).
- **Structural vulnerability to architecture shift.** AEC's value proposition is short-reach copper economics; CPO/LPO attacks it at the margin. Credo's optical DSP portfolio is the correct hedge but is a later, less defensible position against Broadcom/Coherent/Marvell-Lumentum.
- **Concentration is a competitive weakness, not just a financial one.** With 88% of revenue in three customers, Credo has minimal negotiating leverage on price and roadmaps versus a vertically integrating buyer.

**Porter's Five Forces (sector):** Buyer power **HIGH** (hyperscaler oligopsony, dual-sourcing, in-house silicon) · Supplier power **MODERATE** (TSMC leading-edge dependency) · Threat of substitutes **HIGH** (CPO, LPO, retimed optics) · Threat of new entrants **MODERATE** (high IP barrier, low capital barrier — fabless) · Rivalry **HIGH and rising** (Marvell-Lumentum consolidation).

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE** — *operating fundamentals only; equity risk elevated*

**Justification:** The demand backdrop is the strongest in the entire semiconductor complex — $1T+ hyperscaler capex in 2027, content-per-accelerator rising faster than capex, and a genuine 23%+ structural CAGR in AEC/interconnect. Median peer revenue growth of 33% and median gross margin of 61% are not cyclical-peak numbers; they are the signature of a technology transition with years of runway. On fundamentals this is a top-decile sector.

**The critical caveat — and the reason this is not a "high conviction" attractive:** the sector's **equity** performance has decisively decoupled from its **operating** performance. CRDO trades **67% below its June 2026 peak of $302.52** and **−44.7% over twelve months** while posting +206% revenue growth — and it is down ~42% in the last month alone. That is not a growth scare; it is the market repricing terminal value for CPO substitution and customer concentration. With beta of 3.29 (highest in the peer set) and multiples still at 14x sales, this remains a **high-torque, architecture-risk sector where the multiple — not the revenue line — is the dominant driver of returns.** Investors should underwrite a CPO transition scenario, not just a capex-growth scenario.

**Sector stance:** Overweight on fundamentals / Underweight on duration. Favour names with optical *and* electrical exposure (the Marvell-Lumentum logic) over single-architecture pure plays.
