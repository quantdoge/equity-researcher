# CRDO — Credo Technology Group Holding Ltd.
**Price: $99.86 (20-Nov-2026) | Market cap ~$18.8B | EV ~$17.4B | FY26 revenue $1.34B**

*Fiscal year ends late April/early May. Latest reported period: FQ1 FY2027 (ended 1-Aug-2026). Next print: 1-Dec-2026.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW**

Credo sells the plumbing that moves data between AI accelerators: **Active Electrical Cables (AECs)**, retimers, optical DSPs, SerDes chiplets/IP. Its edge is a proprietary SerDes + DSP design that delivers signal integrity at materially lower power and cost than alternatives for short-reach (in-rack / rack-to-rack) links. That is genuine intangible-asset IP, built since 2008.

**Evidence for the moat:** 68% non-GAAP gross margins, 33% GAAP operating margin, and revenue up 6.9x in two years ($193M FY24 → $1,335M FY26). A commodity component supplier cannot earn 68% gross margins. Design wins are sticky — hyperscaler qualification cycles run 12–18 months, and AECs are specified into rack architectures.

**Evidence against a wide moat:**
- **Extreme customer concentration: the top three customers were 74% of revenue** (FQ1 FY27), with AECs shipping to five hyperscalers. These are the most sophisticated, price-aggressive buyers on earth, and they routinely dual-source and in-source.
- **Technology transition risk.** AECs are copper. Copper's reach shrinks with every speed node; the long-term direction of travel is optics (co-packaged optics / silicon photonics), where Credo is a challenger to Broadcom and Marvell rather than the incumbent. Credo's optical business is growing fast, which mitigates but does not eliminate this.
- **Margins already wobbling at the peak of the cycle.** GAAP gross margin fell from 68.2% in FQ4 FY26 to 64.5% in FQ1 FY27 (−2.9pp YoY), and GAAP operating margin fell from 35.7% to 25.2% sequentially. Non-GAAP GM held at 68.0%, so much of the delta is stock-compensation-in-COGS and mix rather than pure pricing — but the direction is a warning, not a comfort.
- ROIC 21.3% (FY26) is inflated *downward* by $1.4B of idle cash; on operating capital alone returns exceed 70%. Excellent — but only two years old.

**Verdict:** a real, narrow, eroding-at-the-edges moat. Wide-moat businesses do not depend on three customers for 74% of revenue.

I ran a ten-year owner-earnings DCF, discounting at 11% with 3% terminal growth, using economic earnings that deduct SBC:

- **Base case** (revenue $2.25B FY27 → $4.4B FY31, ~21% owner-earnings margin): intrinsic value **~$62/share**.
- **Bull case** (revenue compounds >20% for a decade, 24% owner-earnings margin, share gains in optics): **~$106/share** — *barely above today's price*.

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value: ~$68/share.** At $99.86 the stock trades at a **~45% premium to that estimate**, and — critically — **today's price already discounts something close to the bull case.** The implied expectation is a decade of >20% compounding with no execution error, no customer loss, and no copper-to-optics disruption. That is not a forecast; that is a hope with a spreadsheet.

Note the built-in tool's 5%-growth DCF output (~$27/share) is not usable here — it grossly understates growth — which is exactly why I hand-built the scenarios above.

**Sanity check on the drawdown:** the stock is down 67% from its June 2026 high of $302. It was absurd then. It is still expensive now. A falling price is not the same thing as a margin of safety. Sell-side targets ($235–$310) have been cut three times since June ($350→$310, $325→$292) and remain anchored to momentum, not to discounted cash flow.

## 4. Management Quality — **Good, with one real blemish**

- **Founder-influenced and technically deep:** CEO William J. Brennan (co-founder era leadership) and a lean 807-person organization generating $1.6B of revenue — capital-light and focused. Impressive.
- **Balance sheet discipline:** no meaningful debt; $1.4B net cash; the FY26 raise (~$700M of equity) plus a modest $122M tuck-in acquisition looks prudent rather than empire-building.
- **Capital return:** first-ever buyback in FY26, but only $19M — a token. No dividend, appropriately.
- **The blemish: SBC at 13.7% of revenue.** Management is paying itself largely in shareholder dilution (5–6%/yr) while buying back a trivial amount. Net, shareholders are being diluted ~6% annually. I want to see this fall below 8% of revenue as the company matures; if it doesn't, per-share compounding will be materially worse than revenue compounding.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID at $99.86**

This is a genuinely good business — narrow moat, 68% gross margins, 86% FCF conversion, net cash, hyper-growth — and it is priced as though none of the risks exist.

- **Quality:** above average (Narrow moat, 8/10 financial quality).
- **Price:** roughly **45% above** my probability-weighted intrinsic value of ~$68.
- **Margin of safety:** negative. I require a 25–30% *discount*; the market is asking a 45% *premium*.
- **Where I would buy:** **~$50–55/share** (30% discount to base intrinsic value), which also happens to be near where a reasonable bear case stops being catastrophic.
- **Action:** if held, trim or exit and revisit after the 1-Dec-2026 print. Put it on the watchlist — this is exactly the kind of business that becomes a wonderful purchase when a capex-digestion scare takes it below $60. It is not that today.

The core asymmetry: the bull case returns ~6% a year to a long-term holder from here; the bear case costs ~55%. Bad odds for a value investor.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to owning it (the bear case):**
1. **Customer concentration — the single biggest risk.** Top-3 = 74% of revenue. One hyperscaler pausing, re-architecting, or dual-sourcing is a $400M+ revenue hole. Any exposure to financially fragile neocloud buyers amplifies this.
2. **Copper-to-optics substitution.** AECs' addressable reach shrinks at each speed node; if co-packaged optics arrives faster than expected, the flagship product's TAM compresses.
3. **Competition:** Broadcom, Marvell, Astera Labs, MACOM and hyperscaler in-house ASIC teams all target the same socket.
4. **Cyclicality + reflexivity.** Beta 3.29; the equity is a levered bet on AI capex sentiment, not just on Credo's execution. The 52-week range ($86–$309) is a 3.6x spread.
5. **Margin normalization.** If 68% gross margins drift to 55–58% as competition matures, the earnings power implied by today's price evaporates.
6. **Continued dilution** at 5–6% per year.

**Risks to my "avoid" call (what would make me wrong):**
1. Credo converts its AEC lead into a durable optical/DSP franchise and grows revenue past $5B by FY30 at 25%+ net margins — the bull case.
2. Hyperscaler capex accelerates again and AEC content per rack rises faster than optics substitution.
3. Two or three new large customers diversify the base below 50% concentration while growth holds — that would widen the moat and justify re-rating my intrinsic value upward.
