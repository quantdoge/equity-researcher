# Rivian Automotive (RIVN) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Prepared for the investment memo. Data as of late November 2026.*

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

Rivian is one of the most geographically **concentrated** large-cap industrial stories in the US market: it manufactures only in the United States and sells almost exclusively into the US and Canada. There is no China revenue, no Russia/CIS exposure, no emerging-market manufacturing, and no material European revenue today. The concentration is simultaneously a mitigant (low cross-border expropriation/currency risk) and the core vulnerability (100% of the business sits inside one policy regime).

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | **~90% of deliveries** (FY25: ~38,000 of 42,200 units, Tridens) | Manufacturing (Normal, IL; Georgia under construction), R&D (Palo Alto, Irvine), all sales | **Medium–High** | Single-policy-regime dependency: Section 232 tariffs, repeal of 30D credit, DOE loan discretion, 50-state franchise-law patchwork, NHTSA scrutiny |

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US EV policy reversal is now structural, not cyclical (Probability: ~90% already realized; Impact: High)
The **Section 30D $7,500 consumer credit was terminated effective September 30, 2025** under the "One Big Beautiful Bill," removing the primary demand subsidy for Rivian's core addressable buyer. Rivian guided lost regulatory-credit sales falling from **$300M (2024) to ~$160M (2025)** (evworld). Critically, gross margin is still subsidy-adjacent: Q3 2026's 8% gross margin was aided by **~$80M of regulatory credits** — i.e., roughly 4 points of a 8% margin is credit income, not vehicle economics.

Management commentary signals no expectation of restoration: at CNBC's Nov 2026 coverage, **Rivian, Lucid and Scout CEOs said they "aren't counting on Democrats to restore federal EV support"** even after midterm wins. This is the correct read — the credit is gone, and any reinstatement would be partial and slow. **The geopolitical/political risk here has already converted into a P&L risk**: Rivian's path to profitability now depends on R2 cost-down and Amazon/VW revenue, not policy tailwinds.

Second-order: Canada's federal GHG credit program is a smaller but real analogue — Rivian stated it "will continue to earn federal GHG credits in Canada in 2025," and that regime is also politically contestable.

### Risk 2 — Tariff and export-control exposure via an import-dependent bill of materials (Probability: 80%; Impact: Medium–High)
Rivian assembles in Illinois, but an EV is not "made in America" at the sub-component level. Two distinct exposures:

- **Section 232 auto/auto-parts tariffs (25%)**: USMCA-qualifying content is currently exempt, but trade counsel flag that **this exemption is likely to end around July 2026** with a new valuation methodology rollout (thepeopleseconomist/CRS IN12545). Broader industry context: by March 2026 US automakers had absorbed **up to $35B in tariff-related costs**, still growing (RapidRatings Sept 2026). Rivian is better positioned than legacy OEMs (fewer imported complete vehicles, no Mexico/Canada assembly), which is a genuine relative advantage — several analyst notes cite "limited dependency on imports."
- **China's rare earth and magnet export controls**: China has imposed its most stringent controls yet, covering products with even **trace Chinese content**, with only ~60% of licence applications approved as of early July 2026 (CSIS, IEA). This is a direct threat to motor magnets, sensors and power electronics. Rivian names it explicitly in its 10-Q risk factors. This is a *hard* chokepoint: it is not price risk, it is availability risk, and it sits outside Rivian's control.

**Assessment:** Tariffs are a manageable margin headwind; China's magnet controls are a low-probability/high-severity supply interruption risk that could force line stoppages at Normal or delay the R2 ramp.

### Risk 3 — Politicization of the $4.5B DOE ATVM loan (Probability: 40% of adverse modification/delay; Impact: Medium–High)
Rivian originally secured a **$6.6B** DOE ATVM loan in the final weeks of the prior administration to build the Georgia plant. In **April 2026 the loan was restructured down to ~$4.5B**, with a smaller first tranche available **sooner** and production now targeted for **late 2028** (Reuters, TechCrunch, Yahoo Finance, Apr 30 2026; DOE/Rivian newsroom).

**Read:** The downsizing is itself evidence of political risk being priced. A DOE loan to a cash-burning EV startup is an inherently discretionary, administratively revocable instrument that is now the funding backstop for Rivian's single largest capacity expansion. Adverse scenarios: further tranche conditions tightening, milestone renegotiation, or a policy-driven freeze. Mitigant: Rivian had **no near-term debt maturities** and ~$7.1B liquidity at Q3'26, so a delay is survivable — a cancellation after capital is committed is the tail risk.

*Honorable mention — customer concentration as quasi-sovereign risk:* Amazon now represents roughly **52% of Rivian's Automotive revenue**, up from ~11% (eletric-vehicles.com), with $1.6B booked from Amazon in 2026. Amazon holds **12.28%** of RIVN. This is not a geopolitical risk in the classic sense, but it is a governance/counterparty risk that behaves like sovereign concentration: one counterparty with leverage over pricing, volume and the EDV roadmap.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation (largely resolved — a genuine positive)**
- **Crews v. Rivian Automotive, Inc.** (securities class action, class period Aug 2022–Feb 2024, IPO/pricing disclosure claims): **settled for $250M**; preliminary approval **Dec 18, 2025**; **final approval May 2026** (Kessler Topaz, claimdepot, Verita). The Feb 2025 8-K announcing the settlement is on EDGAR. This removes the largest known legal overhang and puts a hard ceiling on that contingency — but it cost ~1.6% of current market cap.
- **Tesla v. Rivian** (trade secrets / employee-poaching, filed July 2020): after a California judge cleared it for a **March 2025 trial**, the parties reached an **out-of-court settlement** (Proskauer, JD Supra, Legal500). Resolved, but the reputational/IP narrative (allegations of taking battery trade secrets) lingers.

**Product safety / NHTSA — the live legal risk**
- **Open NHTSA investigation into ~115,000 Rivian vehicles over a rear suspension issue** (cbtnews) — unresolved, and the single most material open regulatory matter.
- 2026 recall cadence is elevated: **26V-625** (R2 HV battery under-torqued fasteners, remedy available 10/02/26), **26V597** (software, suspect period Aug 2021–Aug 2026), **~34,824 EDV** seat-belt pretensioner cable recall, and an earlier **~20,000 R1S/R1T** rear-suspension recall. Recall volume at a company still ramping quality systems is a genuine execution/liability signal and raises the probability of future product-liability claims as the R2 fleet scales.

**Regulatory / legislative**
- **State franchise laws**: Rivian's direct-to-consumer model is illegal or restricted in a patchwork of states. It won a **Washington state dealer licence in March 2026 after a ~$5M campaign**, with conditions that it cannot complete transactions or disclose pricing in-store (cbtnews, Washington State Standard). This is an ongoing, state-by-state political expenditure — a structural cost of the model.
- **CAFE/GHG credit dependency**: federal (NHTSA/EPA) and CARB-state credit markets plus Canada are a revenue line that is being deliberately shrunk by policy. California's ACC II waiver status is the single biggest swing factor for that ~$80M/quarter.
- **NHTSA/ODI, EPA, CARB, FTC** (advertising/pricing), and state AGs are the relevant regulators; no DOJ/SEC enforcement action beyond the settled private class action is disclosed.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

| Area | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **FCPA / AML** | **Low.** No non-US government sales, no agents/intermediaries in high-corruption jurisdictions. Minimal FCPA surface. |
| **Sanctions / export controls** | **Low direct, Medium indirect.** No Russia/Iran/etc. revenue. But Chinese-origin content in the BOM means exposure to *US inbound* restrictions (Section 232, UFLPA forced-labour traceability for graphite/anode) and to *Chinese outbound* controls. Supply-chain traceability documentation is the pressure point. |
| **Data privacy** | **Medium.** Vehicles are increasingly data-generating (ADAS fleet data, in-cabin sensing, OTA). US state privacy laws plus EU GDPR exposure if Europe launch proceeds. No disclosed enforcement. |
| **Related-party / governance** | **Medium — flag.** Amazon is simultaneously largest customer (~52% of Automotive revenue), a 12.28% shareholder, and a cloud services vendor. VW is the 15.9% top shareholder, a JV counterparty, and a licence customer. Dual-class structure (3.9M Class B vs 1.44B Class A) concentrates control with RJ Scaringe. Governance screens are poor — **ISS Governance QualityScore of 10 (highest risk decile)** as of Nov 1, 2026. This is a disclosure-quality issue for the memo, not a legal violation. |
| **Green-bond use-of-proceeds** | **Watch.** Rivian has 2029/2030 Convertible Green Notes (4.625%) and 2031 Secured Green Notes. Green-bond frameworks require allocation reporting; misallocation would be a reputational/regulatory irritant, not currently an issue. |

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

### **MEDIUM**

**Reasoning.** Rivian scores unusually well on the classic geopolitical axes — no China/Russia/EM revenue, no expropriation or currency-repatriation exposure, US-domiciled manufacturing, and its two largest legal contingencies ($250M securities class action; Tesla trade secrets) are now **settled and quantified**. The residual legal risk is product-safety and NHTSA-driven, which is idiosyncratic rather than geopolitical.

The rating is held at Medium rather than Low because **the concentration that removes cross-border risk concentrates policy risk into a single, actively hostile vector.** Three policy facts dominate the investment case: the 30D credit is permanently gone, Section 232's USMCA carve-out looks set to erode from mid-2026, and the Georgia expansion depends on a $4.5B discretionary federal loan that has already been cut 32% once. Add Amazon at ~52% of Automotive revenue and VW at 15.9% ownership, and Rivian's counterparty and policy dependence both sit at the extreme end of the auto sector.

**What would change the rating:**
- **To High:** termination or material re-scoping of the DOE loan after Georgia capex is committed; a rare-earth-driven production stoppage at Normal; an adverse NHTSA finding escalating the 115,000-unit suspension probe into a mandated recall with a costly remedy; or loss/repricing of the Amazon EDV relationship.
- **To Low:** commercial-scale R2 profitability without reliance on regulatory credits, sustained R2 Europe launch diversifying revenue, and a multi-year track record of clean safety investigations.
