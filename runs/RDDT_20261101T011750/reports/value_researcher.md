# Reddit, Inc. (NYSE: RDDT) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price as of Oct 30, 2026: $91.73** (down 29% in one session; −62% from the Jan-2026 high of ~$240)
**Market cap: ~$18.9B** (206.3M fully diluted shares) | **Net cash: $3.03B** | **EV: ~$15.9B**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW**

Reddit operates ~130 million daily active uniques and 515 million weekly actives across 100,000+ volunteer-moderated communities. The economics are extraordinary: 91.5% gross margin, 44.9% adjusted EBITDA margin, and capital expenditures of **$1 million per quarter against $895 million of revenue**. This is one of the least capital-intensive businesses I have ever examined — a genuine hallmark of a moated enterprise.

**Where the moat is real:**
- **Network effects (durable):** Each subreddit is a two-sided network of users and moderators, layered with 20+ years of archived human discussion. r/AskHistorians or r/personalfinance cannot be rebuilt from scratch — not by Meta, and *not by an AI model*, which can only synthesize what already exists.
- **Intangible assets:** Brand trust as a source of non-AI-generated, human-verified answers. This becomes *more* valuable as the open web fills with synthetic content.
- **Data licensing:** Real pricing power — AI labs pay for the corpus.

**Where the moat is defective — and this is the crux:** Reddit does not control its own distribution. Historically ~80% of traffic arrives via Google search. In Q3 2026, management disclosed that **US DAUq is shrinking** because Google's AI Overviews now answer queries without sending the click. Total DAUq growth decelerated to **+12% YoY** (from +17% in Q1 2026 and +19% in Q4 2025).

A business whose top-of-funnel is rented from a company that is simultaneously its largest distribution partner *and* its emerging substitute does not have a wide moat. The counterweight: **logged-in DAUq and app DAUq — the most monetizable cohorts — grew sequentially** in both the US and rest of world. The users being lost are logged-out, low-value drive-by traffic. That distinction is the entire investment case.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **8 / 10**

| Cash & securities | $3.03B, **zero debt** |
| Capex | 0.1% of revenue |
| ROIC (reported FY25) | 15.0% — *but see below* |

**The good:** FCF conversion is ~118% of net income — outstanding. Revenue growth has been remarkably consistent (37% → 21% → 62% → 69% → 53%). The balance sheet is a fortress.

**The two deductions:**
1. **Stock-based compensation at ~15% of revenue** is a genuine economic cost. Adjusted EBITDA flatters this. On an honest owner-earnings basis (FCF less the buyback dollars needed to hold share count flat), I estimate **~$665M**, not $1.18B. This roughly halves the apparent cheapness. SBC is trending down as a percentage of revenue and fully diluted shares fell 0.3% sequentially — but it remains the single largest quality blemish.
2. **ROIC of 15% understates the business.** Invested capital excluding the $3B cash pile is approximately zero. Reported ROIC is diluted by idle cash earning 4%. Incremental returns on capital are, in practice, extremely high. This is a *strength* masquerading as a weakness in the reported metric.

**Revenue concentration:** Advertising is ~95% of revenue ($847M of $895M). This is a single-cylinder engine — highly sensitive to ad-market cyclicality.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — Intrinsic Value ~**$155** (range $80–$300)

Because the automated DCF tool mis-parsed my inputs, I built the model directly on owner earnings (FCF *after* treating SBC as a real cost).

**Current multiples:**
- EV/TTM revenue: **5.1x**
- EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA: **12.1x**
- P/E (TTM diluted): **18.7x** — versus a peer median of 16.2x, but Reddit is growing 3–5x faster
- FCF yield: 6.3% on market cap / 7.4% on EV
- **EV/owner earnings (post-SBC): ~24x**
- Net cash equals **16% of market cap** ($14.70/share)

**The ARPU engine — the key to the model.** Reddit's US ARPU is **$11.85 per quarter versus Meta's ~$125** — roughly a 10x gap. Q3 revenue grew 53% on DAUq growth of just 12%, meaning **ARPU expanded ~37%**. Even with a flat user base, closing a fraction of that monetization gap supports high-teens revenue growth for years. This is what decouples the thesis from Google's traffic.

**DCF (owner earnings of ~$1.05B in 2027E):**
- **Base case** — 18% growth for 5 years, 8% for 5, 3% terminal, 11% discount: **~$155/share**
- **Bear case** — growth decays to 8%/6%/4%/2%, 11% discount: **~$79/share** (i.e., today's price is close to the bear case; downside is roughly −15%)
- **Bull case** — 30% for 5 years, 15% for 5, 10% discount: **~$300/share**

Probability-weighted (25/50/25): **~$172**. I adopt **$155** as my conservative intrinsic value.

At $91.73, the shares trade at a **~41% discount to intrinsic value** — comfortably beyond the 20–30% margin of safety I require. My buy threshold would be ~$116.

## 4. Management Quality — **Adequate, Not Exceptional**

- **Founder-led:** Steve Huffman (co-founder) is CEO — an owner-operator in spirit, though his economic stake is modest.
- **Controlled company:** Advance Publications holds dual-class supervoting stock. Minority shareholders have limited voice. This is a structural governance negative; Buffett has historically penalized dual-class structures.
- **Capital allocation:** $1B buyback authorized Feb 2026; $100M repurchased in Q3 at an average of **$149.81** — a poor entry point given today's $91.73. The instinct to retire stock is correct; the execution was undisciplined. Full dilution of the remaining authorization near current prices would be highly accretive (a $1B buyback at $92 retires ~5.3% of shares).
- **Balance sheet discipline:** Zero debt, no dividend (appropriate at this stage). Conservative.
- **Guidance credibility:** Q4 revenue guided to $1.00–1.03B versus ~$1.05B consensus — a guide-down after a long run of beats. Management's visibility into its own top-of-funnel is now demonstrably limited.

**Verdict on management: B.** Rational capital allocation in aggregate; governance structure works against minority holders; guidance reliability has deteriorated.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **BUY** (staged entry, below-market-weight position)

This is not a classic Buffett "wonderful business at a fair price." It is a **very good business with a real structural flaw, available at a substantial discount to intrinsic value.** Those are different trades, and the difference matters.

The market has repriced RDDT from a 40x-revenue AI-darling to 5x EV/sales in nine months. At 12x forward adjusted EBITDA and 18.7x trailing earnings for a business compounding revenue at 40%+ with 45% EBITDA margins, $3B of net cash, and near-zero capital requirements, the price already embeds a great deal of bad news — roughly the *entire* bear case I can construct.

**What would make me wrong:** if logged-in DAUq begins declining. That is the tripwire. Q3 showed logged-in and app users *growing sequentially*, in both the US and internationally. The moment that inverts, the ARPU-runway argument loses its base and the bear case ($79) becomes the base case.

**Position sizing:** Given a beta of 2.05, a controlled-shareholder structure, and single-channel dependency, this warrants a **half-size position built in tranches** — not a full-weight commitment. Add on confirmation that logged-in DAUq holds; reassess if US DAUq declines accelerate.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Google AI disintermediation (primary, existential).** US DAUq is already shrinking. Google is both distributor and substitute. Further AI Overviews expansion, or a renegotiation of the data-licensing deal, compounds the damage.
2. **Logged-in user erosion.** If the sticky cohort follows the casual one, the ARPU runway closes. *Monitor quarterly — this is the single most important metric.*
3. **Stock-based compensation (~15% of revenue).** Persistent dilution transfers value from shareholders to employees; my owner-earnings figure already assumes this, but an SBC re-acceleration would compress the valuation multiple.
4. **Advertising cyclicality.** 95% of revenue is ads. A recession or tariff-driven pullback in ad budgets hits a company trading at a premium multiple with high operating leverage.
5. **Dual-class control.** Advance Publications can block or force strategic decisions against minority interests, including M&A.
6. **Moderator and content risk.** An all-volunteer moderation model creates fragility around brand safety, youth-safety regulation (US state laws, EU DSA), and Section 230 reform.
7. **Competitive attention shift.** TikTok, YouTube, and AI chatbots compete for the same time.
8. **Volatility.** Beta 2.05 and a single-day 29% drawdown are the norm, not the exception. Expect a rocky holding period.

**Data gaps acknowledged:** No 10-K was retrievable through the filing tool, so risk factors are drawn from the Q3 2026 release and public reporting rather than primary filing text. Reddit does not disclose monthly active user trends, which limits visibility into the traffic trajectory — an important blind spot given the central risk.

---

### Bottom Line
A genuinely differentiated network with extraordinary unit economics, near-zero capital intensity, $3B of net cash, and a 10x ARPU runway — priced as though user growth has already permanently stalled. The margin of safety is real (~41%), the moat is narrower than the 2025 consensus believed, and the single number that determines whether this is a 3-bagger or a value trap is **logged-in DAUq**. **Buy, in tranches, at half weight, and watch that metric like a hawk.**
