## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT** (structurally, not randomly)

Clear two-axis split that resolves cleanly:

- **Fundamental axis (Value, Growth, Short): bullish / not-short.** Three independent analysts using *different* methods converge: a 41% discount to intrinsic value, a deflated 4.6x forward sales multiple, and **no forensic fraud signal after a deliberate hunt.** This is the strongest signal in the memo — the fraud short is dead, which removes the only "pass" argument.
- **Risk axis (Non-Fin, Geo Legal, ESG, Fin-Risk equity, Alt Data, Macro): negative.** Not one bears a solvency claim; all six are *terminal-value and equity-price* claims.

**Resolution rule applied:** risk flags take precedence over valuation enthusiasm, **but only where they threaten the enterprise**. Here the Short Analyst and Fin Risk Analyst both explicitly confirm the enterprise is safe (net cash, no debt, positive FCF even at −40% revenue) while the *stock* can halve. That downgrades RDDT from "buy at size" to **"buy small, in stages, with pre-committed invalidation triggers"** — not to "pass."

**The one genuine disagreement to flag:** Alt Data is **bearish** on demand while Value and Growth are **bullish** on price. These are not in conflict — Alt Data's own authors concede Google Trends will *misread* the logged-in pivot (declining external search interest can coexist with rising logged-in DAUq). The two views reconcile into exactly one question: **does logged-in ARPU compound faster than logged-out DAUq leaks?** Q3 said yes (logged-in and app DAUq grew sequentially in US and RoW). That is a one-quarter sample.

## 6. Risk Management Rules

**Stop-loss — deliberately NOT a tight percentage stop.** At 78.5% vol, a 1-sigma month is ±22%; a conventional 15% stop would be triggered by noise. Use a two-layer structure:
- **Soft review at −25% from average cost (~$69):** halve the position. This sits below the bear-case IV of $79, so it is evidence the *thesis*, not the tape, is deteriorating.
- **Hard exit at −45% (~$50):** full exit unless logged-in DAUq has demonstrably inflected upward — because below ~$50 the market is pricing the Fin Risk de-growth path ($35–41), which is self-fulfilling if guidance follows.

**Thesis invalidation (fundamental — exit on any *two*):**
1. **Total DAUq declining >10% YoY for two consecutive quarters.**
2. **Logged-in / app DAUq inverts from sequential growth to sequential decline** (Value's "tripwire" — the entire ARPU-runway argument loses its base).
3. US DAUq decline accelerating beyond −5% YoY.
4. Q4'26 revenue below the **$1.00B guide floor**, or any FY27 guide below **$4.0B**.
5. Google AI licensing deal **not renewed** *combined with* Google referral traffic down >25% YoY (the two bad outcomes are correlated — they arrive together).
6. SBC re-accelerating above ~17% of revenue (reverses the owner-earnings math the whole valuation rests on).
7. Moderator revolt, or a measurable advertiser brand-safety pullback (watch Q1'27 ad revenue two quarters later).
8. EU DSA VLOP non-compliance finding or a US COPPA/FTC action.

**Trim trigger (valuation):**
- **At $124–140 (80–90% of $155 IV):** trim one-third.
- **At $150+:** trim to a core stub or exit — >7.5x forward sales with no DAUq re-acceleration is where the short case re-arms.
- **Mechanical:** any single position exceeding **3%** of portfolio is trimmed back, regardless of price.

**Positive tell to monitor (do not ignore):** the **absence of a single insider open-market purchase after a 65% drawdown** is a genuine negative. **The first insider buy would be a high-quality signal to accelerate Tranche 3.**
