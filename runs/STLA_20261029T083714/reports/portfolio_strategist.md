## 2. Consensus Assessment: **HIGH agreement on direction, SPLIT on actionability**

- **Unanimous absence of a bull.** Not one of eleven analysts recommends buying. That is itself the single most important output of this aggregation.
- **Consensus (high):** balance sheet is impaired, cash generation is broken, the moat is absent, and the recovery is a management-execution bet against a hostile macro. Fin Risk, Quant, Non-Fin, Growth, Sector all independently land on "avoid."
- **Contested (split):** *whether the bad news is priced.* Value says IV $6.0 vs $4.68 (+28%) — close but insufficient. Short Analyst says the decline is exhausted. Macro says "heavily discounted" with a genuine oil-driven double in the tail. Alt Data says the bounce is relief, not recovery.
- **Conflict resolution — fraud risk:** The Short Analyst's forensic work is the highest-weight input on tail risk, and it clears STLA of fraud. **This removes the binary catastrophic case but does not create a buy.** A low-quality, honestly-disclosed, cash-burning cyclical is a valuation question, not a solvency question — but it is still a *low-quality* business.
- **Conflict resolution — the bogus inputs (all three agents flagged them independently):** the **16.5% dividend yield** is stale (2026 dividend suspended — model 0%); the **"+16.5% earnings upgrade"** compared FY2030 to FY2030 on n=1 analyst (actual revision direction is Piper Sandler $38→$4, MS $10.90→$5.20); the **Beneish "low risk"** reading is a mechanical artifact of the loss. I have discarded all three.
- **The one live disagreement I must resolve:** Value's $6.0 IV assumes **no dilution**. The Board has authorised a **€5bn issuance** (reported variously as hybrid-bond and equity authorisation — unreconciled across two reports). Against a $13.5bn market cap, an equity raise would be ~40% dilutive. **This single unknown is worth more than the entire 28% valuation gap**, and it is unresolvable from the evidence set.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation

**Core diversified portfolios: 0.0% — PASS.**
**Distressed / event-driven sleeve only: 0.5% maximum, and not at this price.**

**Rationale via scenario EV and Kelly:**

| Scenario | Prob. | Value/sh | Return |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bear (turnaround stalls, dilution, net debt >€25bn) | 30% | ~$1.50 | −68% |
| Base (4.5% AOI by 2030, FCF €4bn) | 50% | ~$6.00 | +28% |
| Bull (2030 plan delivered, oil mean-reverts) | 20% | ~$15.00 | +220% |
| **Probability-weighted** | | **~$6.45** | **+38%** |

Full Kelly on this distribution optimises at ~50% of capital — an obviously unusable output that tells you the inputs are too uncertain to trust. Applying **1/8 fractional Kelly** for model uncertainty, then constraining by risk budget: at **53.5% annualised volatility**, a 1% position contributes ~54bp of portfolio tracking error — roughly the same risk as a **3% position in an 18%-vol name**. A 3% "cheap cyclical" position here is functionally a 9% risk-equivalent bet on a BBB− credit with negative FCF.

**Therefore: 0.5% is the ceiling, not the target.** The sizing is capped by *risk contribution and balance-sheet fragility*, not by the +38% expected return.

**What would move me to 1.5%:** (a) two consecutive quarters of **positive industrial FCF**; (b) AOI margin >4%; (c) the €5bn issuance resolved as **debt, not equity**; (d) entry at ≤$3.50. All four together justify 1.5–2%.
