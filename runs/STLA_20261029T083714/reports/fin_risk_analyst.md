## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: Sub-Investment Grade profile (borderline IG / one notch above junk)**

Stellantis is a fallen angel in progress. The company holds the *lowest* investment-grade ratings — **Moody's Baa3** (downgraded, stable outlook), **S&P BBB-** (A-2 short-term), **DBRS BBB** — but its FY2025 credit metrics are not investment grade by any conventional test. In February 2026 the company booked **~€22.2bn of H2-2025 charges** (a ~$26.5bn EV/portfolio write-down), driving a **€22.4bn net loss** and **negative EBITDA of €15.3bn**.

The critical distinction: the loss is **impairment-driven, not cash-driven** (goodwill & intangibles fell €9.5bn; retained earnings fell €24bn). Cash flow, however, was also negative — **FCF of –€16.2bn** on operating cash flow of –€5.5bn and capex of €10.7bn. Interest coverage is **–17.5x** on a reported basis (EBIT –€22.2bn / interest €1.27bn), though underlying EBIT is roughly breakeven once the €22.2bn of excluded charges are added back.

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue –20%

Start point: FY2025 underlying EBIT (AOI) ≈ **breakeven** after adding back the €22.2bn of excluded charges.

| Line | Base (FY2025A) | –20% revenue stress |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | €153.5bn | €122.8bn (–€30.7bn) |
| Variable cost savings (@78% of lost revenue) | — | +€24.0bn |
| **EBIT impact** | — | **–€6.7bn** |
| EBIT (underlying) | ~€0.0bn | **–€6.7bn** |
| D&A | €7.0bn | €6.5bn |
| **EBITDA** | ~€7.0bn | **–€0.2bn** |
| OCF | –€5.5bn | **~–€12.0bn** |
| Capex (discretionary cut) | €10.7bn | €8.0bn |
| **FCF** | –€16.2bn | **~–€20bn** |

**What breaks:**
- **Net debt → ~€35bn** (€14.6bn + €20bn burn), against **near-zero EBITDA**. Net-debt/EBITDA becomes effectively infinite; any covenant keyed to EBITDA is breached outright.
- **Downgrade to high yield is near-certain**, triggering a step-up on €45.9bn of debt and closing the commercial-paper market — the usual first liquidity domino for an OEM.
- **Cash bridge:** €31.3bn cash + €5bn authorised equity issuance + facilities ≈ €36bn+, versus €20bn annual burn → **~18–21 months of coverage**. Survivable *only* because asset-backed financing (dealer/lease receivables) and inventory liquidation are available levers.

**Mitigants that prevent a "Critical" call:** ~€31bn cash, deep asset base (€43bn PP&E), a €5bn equity backstop the Board has already authorised, no 2026 dividend obligation (suspended — saves ~€2.3bn), and demonstrable 2026 improvement. **But note the equity raise is itself a warning:** the Board pre-arranged dilution capacity because it does not trust the balance sheet on its own.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating

# **HIGH**

| Pillar | Score | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Credit risk | **High** | Negative EBITDA, negative coverage, Z=1.03, F=3, one notch from junk |
| Liquidity risk | **Low–Moderate** | €31.3bn cash, 2.4x coverage of ST debt, S&P "strong" |
| Market risk | **High** | 50% vol, 1.53 beta, –66.5% drawdown, Sharpe –0.90 |
| FCF sustainability | **Critical** | –10.6% FCF margin, negative for two years running |

**Verdict:** Stellantis passes the *solvency* test on liquidity and asset coverage but fails the *earnings-quality and cash-generation* tests decisively. The balance sheet can absorb the current shock for roughly two years without new capital — that is the definition of a "High," not "Critical," risk. The trigger to "Critical" would be a second consecutive year of negative FCF *without* the 2026 recovery materialising.

**The forward datapoint matters:** Q1-2026 returned to a **$440m net profit**, and Q3-2026 narrowed its loss **90% YoY to €0.5bn** with guidance reaffirmed. The trajectory is stabilising from a very low base — but the 2026 improvement is off a collapsed base and does not yet restore investment-grade metrics.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **€22.2bn of H2-2025 charges** — goodwill/intangibles cut €9.5bn; management concedes it "over-estimated the pace of the energy transition." A strategy reset of this size implies further risk to the €44.9bn of remaining goodwill & intangibles.
2. **Negative gross profit (–€4.2bn, –2.7% margin)** — the first negative gross margin in the company's history; operating deleverage is structural, not cyclical.
3. **Net debt swing of €28.8bn in 24 months** (net cash → €14.6bn net debt) — the fastest balance-sheet deterioration in the peer group.
4. **Negative EBITDA (–€15.3bn) and FCF (–€16.2bn)** — both coverage and leverage ratios are uncomputable on a trailing basis.
5. **Altman Z of 1.03 in the distress zone** and **Piotroski F of 3/9** — only accruals quality, absence of dilution, and asset-turnover improvement score positively.
6. **Two downgrades in five weeks to the lowest investment grade** (Moody's Baa3, S&P BBB-, DBRS BBB) — the rating agencies are moving faster than the fundamentals.
7. **Dividend suspended for 2026 and up to €5bn of equity issuance authorised** — an explicit admission that internally generated cash is insufficient.
8. **Revenue down four consecutive years, –19% from the 2023 peak**, with working capital compressed to €1.8bn (current ratio 1.02x) — minimal buffer for a demand shock.
9. **Single-day –24% share move (2026-02-06)** and –71% peak-to-trough — the market has already repriced existential-tail risk.
10. **Sortino ratio, CDS spreads, and the granular debt-maturity ladder are unavailable** — if credit spreads are widening materially, that would be the earliest confirmation of the downgrade thesis and should be sourced before any position is sized.
