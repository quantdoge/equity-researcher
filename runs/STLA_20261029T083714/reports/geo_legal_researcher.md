## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US tariff architecture (Section 232 / USMCA 2026 review) — Probability: certain (already in force); Impact: high**
- Passenger-vehicle duty on non-US content went from 2.5% MFN to 27.5% under Section 232 (CRS IF12082); USMCA-qualifying parts partially exempt pending rulemaking.
- Management quantified **€1.5bn (~$1.7bn) 2025 tariff hit**, guided *higher* H2-25 impact, and now guides **~€1.0bn net tariff cost for FY2026** (Q3-26 6-K). Tariffs were a named driver of the H1-25 $2.7bn loss (Reuters, 21-Jul-2025).
- Mitigant/response: **$13bn four-year US investment pledge** (FT) — capital reallocation risk as much as relief.
- Live catalyst: the **2026 USMCA joint review** (USTR 2026 USMCA Autos Report; Baker Donelson: "Section 232 tariffs, ADCVD actions converge"). Any tightening of rules-of-origin or loss of the Mexico vehicle exemption hits Ram/Jeep sourcing directly.

**② EU CO₂ / 2035 powertrain regime — Probability: medium (policy is shifting in company's favour, but compliance gap is real); Impact: medium-high**
- Stellantis **pooled with Tesla for 2025** and, per EU filings dated 27-Feb-2026, is **not in the Tesla pool for 2026** — a materially thinner compliance cushion (Reuters 07-Jan-2025; Autonews/Electrek 03-Mar-2026). Company says it could still join later; that optionality is a live fine-risk variable.
- Partial offset: the Commission's Dec-2025 reversal/rethink of the 2035 ban (90% tailpipe cut from 2035, 3-year 2030–32 averaging window; ACEA "Automotive Package") reduces prospective penalty exposure — but adds multi-year regulatory uncertainty on product/capex planning.

**③ Italy/home-market political risk — Probability: high (ongoing); Impact: medium**
- Meloni government publicly attacked Stellantis for shifting output to low-cost countries, demanding **1m units/yr in Italy**, accusing "French bias"; Italian autoworker strikes followed (Bloomberg, Courthouse News, Autonews). Stellantis responded with a **€2bn Italy investment pledge** (FT) — subsidy-for-production bargaining that recurs annually and constrains restructuring speed.

Runner-up: **South America/MEA FX & trade policy** — Q3-26 MEA revenue −19%, APAC −7%; Brazil facing a surge of Chinese-built imports that Stellantis says imperil local jobs (Bloomberg, 07-Oct-2026) — a tariff/safeguard ask that could cut either way.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **UK "dieselgate" collective action (Pan-NOx, ~1.6m claimants) — largely resolved in company's favour.** High Court judgment 10-Jul-2026 found *mostly for* the manufacturers; Stellantis states **only one allegation was upheld against it** and it was considering appeal (Reuters). Claimants' permission to appeal refused 8-Oct-2026 (Cockerill J). This removes the single largest identified contingent liability.
- **NHTSA:** long-running probe into **~7.4m US vehicles closed 26-Feb-2026 without a recall** (Reuters) — favourable. However FCA US carries a history of enforcement (prior **$70m cash + $20m spend consent order** over 23 recall campaigns), and recall/quality frequency remains elevated (AutoNews quality "war rooms").
- **US securities fraud class action** (Rosen Law; Bernstein Litowitz), lead-plaintiff deadline **8-Jun-2026**, arising from the H2-2025 multi-billion charge/guidance disclosures. H2-2025 net loss of **€20.1bn** and ~**€1.6bn of remaining cash outflows** tied to those charges (Q3-26 6-K) keep disclosure-quality litigation live. Size not quantified publicly — treat as a modest but real contingent item.
- **China re-entry:** $1.2bn Dongfeng JV to build Peugeot/Jeep in China from 2027 (Reuters, 15-May-2026); **DPCA shareholder-loan-to-equity conversion completed Sept-2026** (Q3-26 6-K). This reopens China-related compliance surfaces: connected-vehicle/ICTS rules, potential export-control and outbound-investment scrutiny, and JV partner risk.
- **Regulators to watch:** European Commission (CO₂, state aid), Italian government, UK OZEV/DfT, US EPA/NHTSA, USTR/Commerce (232, ADCVD), and SEC (disclosure).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **No active FCPA/UK Bribery Act or sanctions enforcement action against Stellantis was identified** in this review — absence of evidence, not a clean bill of health. Residual exposure is structural: 250k+ employees, JVs in Türkiye, Morocco, Algeria, China, Argentina — high-corruption-perception jurisdictions where third-party/intermediary controls matter.
- **Russia:** no material disclosed Russian operations identified post-divestment; sanctions exposure appears low/indirect (component supply chain).
- **Data privacy:** EU GDPR + connected-vehicle data rules and US state privacy/telematics rules — routine but growing cost.
- **Flag:** the CO₂ pooling withdrawal (① above) is the most plausible route to a *quantifiable* regulatory penalty in the next 12–18 months (EU excess-emissions premium of €95/g·km per vehicle).

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** ~75% of revenue sits in two jurisdictions (US, EU) both actively rewriting automotive trade and emissions rules in real time, with quantified tariff costs (~€1.0bn FY26) already flowing through the P&L and a 2026 USMCA review as a binary-style catalyst. Offsetting this, the *litigation* tail has shortened materially — the UK Pan-NOx ruling and the NHTSA probe closure both went the company's way in 2026 — so legal risk is **Medium** while **geopolitical/policy risk is High**.
