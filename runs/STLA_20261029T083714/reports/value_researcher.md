# Stellantis N.V. (STLA) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price: $4.68 (~€4.27) | Market cap ~$13.5bn / €12.3bn | Shares ~2.90bn | Net debt €18.4bn (30 Jun 26) | EV ~€30.7bn**
*EUR/USD ≈ 1.10. FY2025 charges were largely non-cash; 2026 is the first "clean" year of the reset.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — **Moat rating: NONE**

Stellantis is a levered bet on a 14-brand OEM portfolio (Jeep, Ram, Peugeot, Fiat, Citroën, Opel, Alfa, Maserati) with ~259k employees and €154bn revenue. It has scale and brand recognition, but **no durable economic moat at the group level**, and the last 24 months is the evidence.

| Evidence | Observation |
|---|---|
| ROIC (approx.) | 21.2% (2022), 20.6% (2023), **4.6% (2024), -22.2% (2025)** |
| Gross margin | 18.6% (2022) → 19.0% (2023) → **11.7% (2024) → -2.7% (2025)** |
| Operating margin | 11.7% → 12.1% → 3.5% → **-14.5%** |
| Revenue | €179.6bn → €189.5bn → €156.9bn → €153.5bn |

That is a ~2,100bp gross-margin collapse in 24 months. A business with pricing power does not experience that. The 2022–23 returns were **cyclical windfall** (chip-shortage pricing, inventory restocking), not moat economics — which is precisely why a 20%+ ROIC evaporated to zero in two years. The moat has clearly **narrowed**: brand overlap is severe (Fiat/Opel/Citroën/Peugeot fight in the same mass-market segments), European capacity is structurally over-built, the company is effectively absent in China (the one profit pool that matters), and North America — the historic profit engine — required a **~$26bn restructuring charge** in FY2025.

Narrow brand-level intangible assets (Jeep, Ram loyalty; Ram/Jeep pricing in US trucks) exist, but they cannot protect consolidated returns on capital. This fails the "ROIC > WACC consistently" test decisively.

## 2. Financial Quality Score — **3 / 10**

| Metric | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | H1 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue (€bn) | 179.6 | 189.5 | 156.9 | 153.5 | 81.6 |
| EBIT (€bn) | 21.1 | 23.0 | 5.4 | -22.2 | 1.4 |
| Free cash flow (€bn) | 10.9 | 12.3 | **-7.1** | **-16.2** | -1.8 |
| Owner earnings (€bn) | 14.6 | 16.0 | 1.6 | -26.1 | n/a |
| Net debt / (cash) (€bn) | (19.3) | (14.2) | 3.1 | 15.8 | **18.4** |
| Tangible equity (€bn) | 21.6 | 30.5 | 27.8 | 9.1 | **16.0** |

**Score justified by three structural flaws:**

1. **FCF conversion is broken.** Cumulative 2021–25 FCF of just **€8.5bn** against cumulated net income of €32.7bn — conversion of ~26%, far below the >80% threshold of a quality business. Capex of €10–11bn/yr now exceeds D&A (~€7bn) perpetually: this industry must out-invest its depreciation just to stand still.
2. **Balance sheet has deteriorated violently.** Net cash of €14.2bn (end-2023) became net debt of €18.4bn by June 2026 — a ~€33bn swing in 30 months, driven by concurrent buybacks, dividends and cash burn. **Net debt now exceeds tangible equity.** Goodwill and intangibles of €45.8bn are **74% of the €61.7bn equity base**, so the headline 0.25x P/B is largely an accounting artifact — tangible book is only ~€5.5/share. A current ratio of 1.04 is thin for a cyclical.
3. **Earnings are not predictable.** Two consecutive years of negative FCF, a €20bn half-year operating loss, and a suspended dividend are not the profile of a business whose earnings can be discounted with confidence.

One genuine positive: liquidity is adequate — €34.4bn cash/short-term investments at H1 2026 vs €15.0bn short-term debt. Solvency is not imminent. But it is below the board's own policy threshold (liquidity > 25% of revenue ≈ €40bn), which is why the dividend is gone.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**What the price implies.** At EV €30.7bn on ~€161bn of 2026E revenue, the market is capitalizing this business at roughly a **permanent ~2% EBIT margin** (€30.7bn × 10% = €3.1bn). Management's mid-cycle ambition (4–5% AOI) and its 2030 target (7% AOI on €190bn revenue) imply €55–80bn of EV. That gap *is* the entire investment case — and it is a bet on execution, not on an asset bargain, because 60% of today's EV is debt.

**Normalized owner earnings.** Mid-cycle 4–4.5% AOI on €160bn = €6.4–7.2bn EBIT → ~€4.0–4.8bn net income (EPS ~€1.40–1.65) after ~€1.2bn interest and 25% tax. Normalized FCF after €11bn capex and restructuring is far lower — realistically **€2.5–4.0bn**.

**DCF / scenario range** (10% WACC, net debt €19.5bn at end-2026, shares 2.90bn):

| Scenario | Assumptions | Equity value | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear (30%)** | Turnaround stalls; FCF ~€0 to 2028, ~€2bn terminal, 0% growth | €0–7bn | €0–2.5 (~$0–2.7) |
| **Base (50%)** | 4.5% AOI by 2030 on €175bn; FCF €4bn terminal, 0% growth | ~€16bn | **~€5.5 (~$6.0)** |
| **Bull (20%)** | 2030 plan largely delivered; FCF €7bn, 2% terminal growth | ~€32–50bn | €11–17 (~$12–19) |

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ €5.4/share (~$6.0).** Current price €4.27 sits at roughly a **20–25% discount to central IV — at the edge of, but not inside, a proper 30% margin of safety**, given how wide the distribution is.

**Multiples vs the only valid comparables:**

| | P/B | P/Tangible B | P/FCF | EV/EBITDA | Div yield |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **STLA** | 0.25x | ~0.75x | n.m. (neg.) | n.m. (neg.) | 0% (suspended; the 16.5% feed yield is stale) |
| GM | 1.27x | ~1.3x | 7.3x | 9.9x | 0.9% |
| Ford | 1.32x | ~1.4x | 3.8x | n.m. (neg.) | 5.0% |

STLA is genuinely the cheapest of the three on tangible book — but it is also the only one with **negative free cash flow and net debt exceeding tangible equity**. The discount is compensation for risk, not a free option. *Data gap: Toyota (TM) returns a JPY-denominated feed and is unusable as a comparator; the "peer median P/E of 28.4x" is meaningless here (it includes Tesla/Amazon) and was discarded.*

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Weak, improving at the margin**

- **Leadership churn:** CEO Antonio Filosa replaced Carlos Tavares after a 2024 board revolt — a public admission that the prior strategy failed. Chairman John Elkann; controlling shareholder Exor (~14%), with the Peugeot family and French state holding meaningful stakes. Owner-aligned in structure, not in outcome.
- **Capital allocation has been value-destructive, and this is measurable.** Stellantis returned roughly **€25bn** in dividends and buybacks over 2021–25 while generating only **€8.5bn of cumulative FCF** — funding the gap by converting €14bn of net cash into €18bn of net debt. Worse, ~€6.3bn of buybacks were executed in 2022–24 at prices near $20–25/share; those shares are now worth $4.68. That is a ~€5bn realized loss on shareholder capital.
- **The reset itself is defensible:** suspending the dividend on an FY2025 loss, taking the $26bn North American charge in one hit rather than dribbling it out, and targeting €6bn of structural cost savings (€2.4bn from 2027) are the actions of a management being honest about the problem.
- **Credibility is the issue.** Guidance has been cut repeatedly since 2023; the €190bn/7% 2030 plan and the €60bn investment programme now carry the burden of proof. No insider buying of note has been disclosed; no buyback at these prices — which is either prudence (given cash burn) or a lack of conviction.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (avoid new capital; not a Buy)

**Reasoning.** Stellantis is not a Buffett business: no moat, cyclical returns on capital that swing from +21% to -22%, a balance sheet that has flipped from net cash to net debt above tangible book, and a decade-long record of sub-WACC economics for the industry as a whole. The stock is optically cheap at 0.25x book — but strip €45.8bn of goodwill/intangibles and the discount largely disappears.

My central intrinsic value is **~€5.5/share (~$6.0)**, with a genuinely wide **€2–11** range. At $4.68 the shares trade at a ~20–25% discount to that midpoint. That is *interesting*, but it is **not** the 30%+ margin of safety I require — particularly when the central case itself depends on management delivering a five-year turnaround in a structurally impaired industry, and when the bear case (permanent capital loss) carries ~30% weight.

**What would make it a Buy:**
1. **Price below ~€3.2 (~$3.5)** — roughly 1x tangible book ex-goodwill, where the debt and execution risk are fully paid for; or
2. **Evidence of inflection:** two consecutive quarters of positive **industrial free cash flow**, falling net debt, and AOI margin above 4% — the 2027 promise delivered rather than restated.

**What would make it a Sell:** a further equity raise/dilution, another goodwill impairment, net debt above ~€25bn, or 2027 industrial FCF guidance being withdrawn.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Cash burn persists longer than guided.** Industrial FCF was -€0.9bn in H1 2026 and -€4.0bn in Q3 (a €4.5bn quarterly drain). If 2027 is not FCF-positive, the balance sheet — not the P&L — becomes the story, and equity issuance at €4 would be brutal.
2. **Goodwill impairment.** €45.8bn of intangibles against €61.7bn of equity is a second large write-down waiting to happen if North American recovery or the 2030 plan slips.
3. **North America concentration.** ~60% of the €36bn investment plan goes to the US, where tariffs, the $26bn reset, dealer inventory and rising competition make the profit engine the risk centre.
4. **China/EV structural disadvantage.** Effectively no China franchise; BYD/Xiaomi/Geely cost curves compress the addressable profit pool. Stellantis carries European overcapacity and union constraints its Chinese competitors do not.
5. **Pension and captive-finance obligations** are not visible in the extracted data (funded status and financial-services debt are combined into reported net debt). *Data gap — unquantified, and a genuine swing factor in a stress case.*
6. **Political/ownership complexity:** Dutch-domiciled, French-Italian-American operations, Exor plus the French state as anchors — decisions may optimize for stakeholders other than the residual shareholder.
7. **Value trap mechanics.** A 0.25x P/B OEM can stay cheap for years; without a cash-return catalyst (the dividend is suspended until liquidity exceeds ~25% of revenue) there is no obvious re-rating trigger short of demonstrated FCF.

**Bottom line:** the market is pricing Stellantis as a permanently 2%-margin business; it may prove better than that. But an investor should be paid for that uncertainty, and at $4.68 he is being paid only partially. **Hold — revisit below ~$3.5 or on proof of positive industrial FCF.**
