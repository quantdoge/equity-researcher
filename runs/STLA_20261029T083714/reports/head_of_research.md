### Investment Memo: STLA — 29 October 2026

**Verdict**: SELL / PASS — no new capital; reduce existing positions | Conviction: **Medium-High**
**Price Target**: $5.25 (+12% from $4.68) — central case; scenario range **$1.50–$15.00**
**Time Horizon**: 12–24 months

**Investment Thesis**

Stellantis is a subscale, 14-brand OEM in mid-turnaround after a self-inflicted FY2025 collapse: a €22.4bn net loss on €25.4bn of EV-reset charges, −€16.2bn FCF, and a €30bn balance-sheet swing from net cash to €15.8bn net debt in 24 months. The 2026 inflection is genuine — H1 net profit of €656m, Q3 losses narrowed 90%, guidance reaffirmed, and at 0.25x book / 0.07x sales the equity already discounts near-failure. But every demand-side leading indicator is still deteriorating, the recovery is a pure management-execution bet against a hostile macro (10Y ~5%, Brent $123, record-low consumer sentiment, no policy put), and an unresolved €5bn issuance authorisation hangs over a $13.5bn market cap. This is a legitimate distressed/event-driven candidate **below ~$3.50 with FCF proof in hand** — not an investable position at $4.68 on hope.

**Key Findings by Domain**

- **Macro & Sector**: Macro rated **UNFAVOURABLE** — the worst configuration for this model: 10Y 4.99%, CPI 3.72% and rising, Fed on hold with hike bias, Brent $123 (+75% since June), UMich sentiment at a record-low 48.1. Sector rated **NEUTRAL** with negative skew for subscale OEMs; STLA's competitive position rated **WEAK** (op margin −14.5% vs peer median +4.6%; 5.5m units vs Toyota's ~11m). The one asymmetric upside: an oil mean-reversion toward $80–85 unwinds CPI, unfreezes the Fed, and could plausibly double the stock. Monitor **Brent first, 10Y second, UMich third**.
- **Geopolitical & Legal**: **HIGH** policy risk — ~75% of revenue sits in the US and EU, both rewriting auto rules in real time; €1.0–1.2bn FY26 US tariff cost guided; the 2026 USMCA review is a binary catalyst; EU CO₂ pooling cushion withdrawn for 2026. Legal tail, by contrast, **shortened materially**: UK Pan-NOx ruled mostly for manufacturers, the 7.4m-vehicle NHTSA probe closed without recall. Residual: an active US securities-fraud class action (modest, unquantified).
- **Equity Fundamentals — Value**: **HOLD / avoid new capital**. No moat (ROIC +21% → −22% in three years proves the 2022–23 returns were cyclical windfall); financial quality 3/10; FCF conversion ~26% over five years; €25bn returned to shareholders against €8.5bn of FCF. Probability-weighted IV ≈ **€5.5 (~$6.0)**, range €2–11. At $4.68 the stock sits at a 20–25% discount to central IV — *at the edge of, but not inside*, a proper margin of safety. Buy triggers: **≤$3.50** (~1x tangible book ex-goodwill) or two consecutive quarters of positive industrial FCF.
- **Equity Fundamentals — Growth**: **SELL** on any growth mandate. Fails every test: forward revenue CAGR ~2.4% (consensus explicitly models share loss), Rule of 40 = **−12.7**, no innovation moat — the €25.4bn write-down is an admission it cannot compete in EV/software, the segment where future industry value accrues. Explicitly *not a short call*: it's a distressed trade misfiled in a growth book.
- **Quantitative Signals**: **STRONG SELL**. Four of five factors decisively negative — 12m momentum −57% (bottom decile), ROIC −22.2%, F-Score 3/9, 53% volatility, max drawdown 60%. The value factor is disqualified: negative P/E and EV/EBITDA are arithmetic artifacts of impairment, and P/B 0.25x is a melting-ice-cube signature. R² of 0.08–0.14 vs SPY means this is an idiosyncratic event name: **position sizing, not stops, is the only real risk control**.
- **Financial Risk**: **HIGH**. Altman Z **1.03** (distress zone), Piotroski 3/9, EBITDA −€15.3bn, interest coverage −17.5x, rated BBB−/Baa3 — one notch above junk, two downgrades in five weeks. Liquidity is the sole strong pillar (€31.3bn cash, 2.4x coverage of ST debt, ~1.9 years of runway at FY25 burn). FCF sustainability rated **CRITICAL**; a −20% revenue stress pushes net debt to ~€35bn against near-zero EBITDA and forces a junk downgrade.
- **Non-Financial Risk**: **HIGH** (upper end). Executive-chairman structure with Exor/Peugeot binding nominations; CEO Filosa personally carrying North America after two years of management churn (key-person concentration); the securities-fraud action alleges *disclosure-timing* failure, not just a business miss; Jeep fire recalls strike the franchise brand; **zero insider buying through a ~65% drawdown** — no internal vote of confidence at any price.
- **ESG**: **LAGGARD**, but with an important nuance — the negativity is largely *realised*, not incremental. The transition risk already crystallised in the €22.2bn charge; governance scored 4/10 (executive chair, ~53% say-on-pay opposition in 2025); diesel litigation tail €1–3bn; €458m EU recycling-cartel fine. An ESG/quality mandate should not own this name.
- **Alternative Data**: **NEUTRAL, negative tilt**. Every measurable demand leading indicator is decaying sequentially: Ram search −30% from August, Jeep −9% from September, NA shipments −12%, **dealer inventory 130+ days vs ~82 industry average**, incentives at 10% off MSRP plus bonus cash. The +13% post-earnings pop is a relief rally on cost saves and reaffirmed guidance — not a demand recovery. (The "1.00 positive" news-sentiment score is a methodology artifact; disregard.)
- **Short / Contrarian View**: **NO EDGE — do not initiate a short.** Crucially, forensic review finds **no fraud**: the Beneish "clean" reading is a loss-year artifact, but the losses are honestly and fully disclosed; this is capital destruction, not concealment. After an ~85% decline from 2023 highs, the easy short money is made; shorting tangible book into a turnaround print with government-adjacent shareholders invites a squeeze. The genuine forensic tells to watch: receivables +17.8% against −2.2% revenue, and €44.9bn of remaining goodwill (83% of equity).

**Bull Case**
- **Asymmetry from a distressed base**: at 0.07x sales and 3.5x 2028E consensus EPS, mere delivery of the low bar (€1.35 EPS 2028, 6x multiple) implies ~$8.80; full 2030 plan delivery implies $12–19. Probability-weighted value ~$6.00–6.45 vs $4.68.
- **The inflection is real and accelerating**: H1-26 swung to €656m profit, Q3-26 loss narrowed 90% with AOI margin +50bps and revenue +1%, NA share recovering (+60bps H2-25), €6bn cost programme running, guidance reaffirmed, and the litigation tail (Pan-NOx, NHTSA) cleared in 2026.
- **The oil mean-reversion option**: Brent at $123 is the upstream cause of the hostile macro; a reversion to $80–85 cuts CPI, unlocks Fed easing, restores financed-vehicle affordability, and mean-reverts record-low sentiment — a three-way multiplier (cost cuts × volume recovery × lower discount rate) on a 53%-vol equity.

**Bear Case**
- **Broken cash engine and balance sheet**: FCF negative two consecutive years (−€7.1bn, −€16.2bn), −€4.5bn industrial FCF in Q3-26 alone, net debt €18.4bn exceeding tangible equity, dividend suspended, BBB−/negative — one notch from junk, where €45.9bn of debt reprices.
- **Dilution overhang**: the board-authorised **€5bn issuance** is unreconciled (hybrid debt vs equity) across reports; an equity raise would be ~40% dilutive against a $13.5bn cap and would vaporise the "0.25x book" thesis outright. This single unknown is worth more than the entire valuation gap.
- **Structural decline disguised as cyclicality**: revenue −19% from the 2023 peak (share loss, not mix), Europe structurally loss-making, China effectively forfeited, North America supplying >100% of group profit while NA shipments fall 12% and dealers sit on 130+ days of inventory — with a possible US tariff step-up on 1-Jan-2027 landing inside the Feb-2027 print.

**Key Risks**
1. **Dilution** — equity issuance at ~$4–5 (primary kill-switch; destroys the asset-bargain case).
2. **FCF promise fails** — 2027 positive-industrial-FCF guidance withdrawn or FY2026 still negative → balance sheet, not P&L, becomes the story; junk downgrade follows.
3. **Tariffs/USMCA** — €1.0–1.2bn guided cost could double on a 2027 step-up or adverse USMCA review.
4. **Goodwill round two** — €44.9bn of intangibles (83% of equity) against a plan that must not slip.
5. **Receivables divergence** — AR +17.8% vs revenue −2.2% is the tell for whether "recovery" is shipped, not sold.
6. **Macro stays hostile** — December hike, 10Y to 5.5%, Brent >$130: the bear case (−30 to −68%) activates.
7. **Key person / governance** — Filosa departure or an Exor-level event; minority holders have no recourse mechanism.
