# ESG ANALYSIS — STELLANTIS N.V. (STLA)
*Auto Manufacturers | Netherlands-domiciled / Euro-American operating footprint | ~258,700 employees | Market cap ≈ US$13.5bn (€12bn), price US$4.68 — ~90% below 2024 levels*

**Data note:** Price series retrieved through 2026-02-20 (US$7.73); profile price US$4.68 and news coverage through 2026-10-28 — evidence set spans both. Stellantis files as a foreign private issuer; **10-K, 20-F and DEF 14A were all unavailable in the filing tool** (only 6-K / AGM materials on EDGAR), so governance and carbon data below come from AGM/press materials and third-party reporting.

## 1. ESG Materiality Map (Auto OEM)

Sector-level, the "E" factor *is* the business model; for Stellantis the "G" factor has, in our view, become equally dominant.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH** (crystallised, not prospective)

- **The €22.2bn charge (6 Feb 2026) was a transition-risk event, not an operational one.** Stellantis booked ~€22.2bn of H2-2025 charges — largely asset impairments and contract provisions tied to scaling back EV/battery capacity — guided to a **H2-2025 net loss of €19–21bn**, suspended the dividend, and spread cash payments over four years. Shares fell ~24–30% on the day (largest one-day drop on record; ~€6bn of market cap lost) to 2021 lows. This is the clearest ESG-cash-flow transmission in European autos this cycle: an "E" strategy (Dare Forward 2030, 100% BEV in Europe by 2030) reverse-engineered into a balance-sheet loss.
- **Forward stranded-asset risk is now partially de-risked** (capacity written down), but regulatory convexity remains: EU fleet target of **93.6g CO₂/km for 2025–27** (three-year averaging granted under the 2025 flexibility), 49.5g for 2030–34, and a Commission proposal (Dec 2025) to soften 2035 to a 90% cut. Industry adjusted fleet was ~94g/km, i.e. **~1g/km above target even after credits** — a structural fines/credit-purchase line.
- **Credit strategy shift:** Stellantis did **not** join Tesla's 2026 EU CO₂ pool (Toyota and Subaru also exited), stating it could rejoin later. This signals either confidence in own compliance or a higher cost of compliance — flag as a swing factor on the "E" P&L.
- **Own-footprint performance is genuinely good:** −26% absolute GHG (Scope 1+2+3) vs 2021 and −43% Scope 1+2 vs 2021 (company disclosure). Low base emissions are not the problem; product mix is.
- **Recycled-content/ELV regulation** and EU Batteries Regulation raise compliance cost in the 2027+ window.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **US labour: contract breach risk.** Belvidere Assembly has been idled since 2023; the 2023 UAW contract committed to reopening it. In August 2026 the restart was pushed to pilot build H1 2028 / retail H2 2029. UAW locals have prepared grievances with strike authorisation as the stated next step — a direct, quantifiable cost (strike pay, lost North American production, Ram/Jeep margin).
- **Europe: social licence erosion.** Italian production has fallen sharply (reports of 1950s-level output), with repeated voluntary-exit packages (thousands of Italian exits since 2023), extended layoff schemes at Mirafiori and Cassino downtime. Each restructuring round carries political risk in France/Italy where the state (Bpifrance) is a shareholder.
- **Consumer protection:** the diesel "defeat device" matter (below) is as much a social/product-integrity issue as a legal one.
- **Supply chain:** battery-chain due-diligence programme exists and CSRD/CSDDD reporting is in place, but external human-rights benchmarks (e.g. Amnesty's *Recharge for Rights*) place OEMs like Stellantis in the "policy without verified outcomes" bucket. **No independent audit evidence of child/forced-labour remediation was obtainable** — treat company self-reporting with a discount.
- Positive: 258,700 employees retained through the reset (so far); redundancies largely voluntary in Europe.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10**

Rationale (each item evidenced):
- **Executive Chairman / non-standard chair structure (−):** John Elkann serves as **executive** Chairman (re-elected April 2026, two-year term) while Henri de Castries sits as Senior Independent Director acting as board *voorzitter* — a workaround for Dutch law that leaves two centre-of-gravity figures and dilutes the independent chair check.
- **Anchor-shareholder control (−):** Exor, the Peugeot family and Bpifrance dominate; loyalty/double-voting rights widen votes over capital. Minority holders cannot realistically discipline the board.
- **Say-on-pay (−, improving):** ~**53% of votes cast opposed the 2025 remuneration report** — a de facto rejection. By the 2026 AGM the report received **93.17% support**, following engagement (Phitrust publicly objected that the structure was unchanged and that CEO Filosa's package could reach ~$23m/yr from 2028 vs $6.37m in 2025). One good year does not cure a two-strikes pattern (52% opposed in 2022).
- **Capital-allocation credibility (−):** the 2025–26 guidance restatement and a **securities class action** (class period 26 Feb 2025 – 5 Feb 2026, lead-plaintiff deadline 8 June 2026) alleging misleading 2025 earnings trajectory. Regardless of merits, this is a governance signal, and the abrupt Dec-2024 removal of CEO Tavares after a public board conflict shows a board that sets strategy late and changes it expensively.
- **Positives (+):** functioning **ESG Committee** (de Castries chair; Dufourcq, Parzani, Cicconi, Ribadeau-Dumas), independent-chaired Audit (Godbehere) and Remuneration (Cicconi) committees, two-year director terms, CSRD-grade disclosure with expanded sustainability statement, and a new operating team (Filosa/Laranjo) that is at least marking assets to reality.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU:** CSRD/ESRS (FY2025 report filed; Omnibus "stop-the-clock" has softened scope and delayed the assurance roadmap — lower reporting cost, lower comparability); EU CO₂ standards 2025-27/2030/2035 (incl. possible 2035 softening); EU Batteries Regulation + ELV/recycled-content rules; **CBAM** (exposure via steel/aluminium input costs — second-order but real); CSDDD (national transposition delayed to ~2027).
- **Italy/France:** golden-power/plant-level political involvement, state shareholding (Bpifrance), EU state-aid scrutiny of any EV incentives.
- **UK:** ZEV mandate (a hard annual percentage, unlike the EU's averaging) — Stellantis has UK van/car exposure via Vauxhall, historically its most compliance-constrained market.
- **US:** CAFE/EPA tailpipe rules materially loosened in 2025-26 and the federal EV credit removed — *favourable* to near-term cash but raises the risk of a state-level (CARB) patchwork and of a future regulatory snap-back.
- **ESG-linked debt:** Stellantis has used sustainability-linked facilities historically; **we could not verify current covenants/margin step-ups from filings** — flag for the credit team to confirm.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD** (borderline "ESG Risk")

Not because its footprint is dirty or its disclosures poor — they are above-average for the sector — but because the two factors that matter most in autos, **transition strategy** and **governance of capital allocation**, both failed visibly and expensively within twelve months. Social (US labour) and legal (diesel, cartel) overhangs are unresolved.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE** — but the negativity is now largely *realised*, not incremental

- **For the thesis:** an ESG/quality-tilted mandate should not own STLA at this stage. The governance discount is structural (executive chairman + anchor control + a rejected pay report and an ousted CEO inside 18 months), and the securities class action and diesel litigation create an unquantified, multi-year overhang that mechanically raises the cost of equity.
- **Contrarian counterpoint (worth stating):** the classic "E" risk — over-investment in unprofitable EVs — has just been written off in one €22bn hit. On a forward basis Stellantis is *less* exposed to a 2035 regulatory cliff than peers still carrying un-impaired BEV plans, and it retains flexibility to rejoin CO₂ pools or buy credits if enforcement tightens. If the FaSTLAne 2030 plan delivers, the ESG-attributable discount could compress.
- **What would change the call to Neutral:** (1) a genuinely independent chair or separation of chairman/CEO roles; (2) a settled UAW/Belvidere outcome; (3) reversal of the 1g/km CO₂ gap without heavy credit purchases; (4) clean, quantified diesel-disclosure resolution.
- **What would change it to ESG Risk:** a UAW strike, an adverse UK/French diesel judgment in the €1bn+ range, or a further CO₂-driven write-down of the remaining ICE/battery footprint.

**Gaps disclosed:** no third-party ESG score (MSCI/Sustainalytics) or peer ESG data could be retrieved; the €22.2bn charge composition and the specific Stellantis share of the €458m EU fine are unverified from primary filings; ESG-linked covenant terms unknown.
