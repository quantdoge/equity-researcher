## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

### Rating: INVESTMENT GRADE (lower tier) — but deteriorating

External ratings place LPL's senior unsecured debt at the bottom of investment grade: **Fitch BBB** (LPL Holdings, affirmed Apr-2026) and **S&P BBB-** (assigned to the 2025 new-issue senior notes). No CDS spread data was retrievable — **gap flagged**.

The credit story is a tug-of-war between genuine scale-driven cash generation and an aggressively M&A-funded balance sheet:

| Signal | Direction | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue scale | ✅ Strong | $8.6B (2022) → $17.0B (2025), +98% |
| Operating income | ✅ Growing | $1,238M → $2,285M, +85% |
| Net income | 🔴 **Falling** | $846M → $1,066M → $1,059M → **$863M** (2025) |
| Net margin | 🔴 **Compressing** | 9.8% → 10.6% → 8.5% → **5.1%** |
| Interest expense | 🔴 **+220%** | $126M → $403M (2022→2025) |
| Gross debt | 🔴 **+83% in 2 yrs** | $3.96B → $7.26B |
| Tangible equity | 🔴 **Negative** | Equity $5.34B vs goodwill+intangibles $5.98B = **–$631M** |

**The central credit anomaly:** FY2025 operating income rose 37% ($1,667M → $2,285M) yet net income *fell* 18.5% and diluted EPS fell 22% ($14.03 → $10.92). With pretax income of ~$1,882M (operating income less $403M interest) against $863M net income, the **implied effective tax rate is ~54%** — roughly **$600–700M of unexplained below-the-line charges** (M&A/severance/integration, likely tied to the Atria and Commonwealth transactions). This is a genuine earnings-quality flag, not a rounding artifact: it is corroborated by Q3-2025, when operating margin collapsed to **1.6%** (operating income $72M on $4,552M revenue) and net income went **negative (–$29.5M)**.

**Mitigant — the trend has inflected.** The trailing four reported quarters (Q4'25–Q3'26) show a rebound: revenue $20.4B, operating income $2,617M, net income $1,393M, interest $413M. On that LTM basis **interest coverage improves to ~6.3x and net leverage to ~1.9x**, materially better than the FY2025 as-reported figures. *(Caveat: these quarterly periods are dated after the final price observation in the feed — see §6 data-integrity note.)*

**Refinancing cliff: none near-term.** Identified instruments are the 4.900% 2028 Notes (Apr-2028), $750M 6.75% notes (Nov-2028), 5.099% notes (May-2029), and 5.150% 2030 Notes, plus a Credit Agreement term loan/revolver. **First material maturity is 2028** — a ~2-year runway. FY2025 short-term debt is **$0** (versus $1,047M in FY2024, since refinanced). The full maturity ladder and covenant thresholds could not be retrieved (SEC and IR pages returned 403) — **gap flagged**.

## 4. Downside Scenario: –20% Revenue Shock

Applied to LTM revenue of **$20,402M** (–$4,080M). Assumption: advisor commission payouts (~72% of revenue sits in cost of revenue) flex at 85% pass-through; SG&A 20% variable; D&A fixed.

| Line item | Base (LTM) | –20% revenue | Δ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $20,402M | $16,321M | –$4,080M |
| Gross profit (31.2% → 31.2%) | $5,704M | $5,092M | –$612M |
| SG&A | $2,708M | $2,600M | –$108M |
| D&A | $731M | $731M | — |
| **Operating income** | **$2,617M** | **$1,761M** | **–33%** |
| Interest expense | $413M | $413M | — |
| **Interest coverage** | 6.33x | **4.26x** | –2.1x |
| EBITDA | $3,348M | $2,492M | –26% |
| **Net debt / EBITDA** | 1.86x | **2.50x** | +0.64x |

**Verdict: survivable with room.** A 20% revenue decline still leaves **4.3x coverage and 2.5x net leverage** — comfortably inside a typical 4.0x maintenance covenant. LPL's payout model is structurally self-protecting: the bulk of its cost base is advisor compensation that falls with revenue.

**The cliff is much lower than 20%.** Re-running with pessimistic flex (50% pass-through, zero SG&A flex): gross profit $3,664M, operating income **$225M**, **coverage 0.54x** — covenant breach and a dividend/buyback halt. **The true distress threshold is roughly a 30–35% revenue decline, or a ~35–40% fall in client assets**, roughly double the standard stress case. Note the stock has already delivered a –33% *price* drawdown without any corresponding revenue shock, so the equity can re-rate hard well before the credit is impaired.

**Second-order risk the revenue model misses:** LPL earns meaningful spread income on client cash sweep balances. A scenario combining a 20% asset-value decline with **200bp of Fed easing** would compress sweep revenue *and* asset-based fees simultaneously — worse than a pure revenue haircut. Symmetrically, current high short rates have been a tailwind that will reverse in a cutting cycle. This rate sensitivity is not quantifiable from the available data (no sweep-balance or rate-sensitivity disclosure retrieved) — **gap flagged**.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating

# MODERATE

*(Creditors: Low-to-Moderate. Equity holders: Moderate-to-High.)*

**Justification:** LPL is a scale operator with investment-grade ratings, no debt maturing before 2028, a large and largely self-hedging variable cost base, and an LTM-leverage profile near 1.9x. A –20% revenue shock is absorbed with 4.3x coverage intact. These are genuinely resilient credit characteristics.

What keeps this from "Low" is the *direction of travel* and the *quality* of the balance sheet: net income down 18.5% on 37% revenue growth, net margin halved from 10.6% to 5.1% in two years, interest expense tripled, coverage down from 9.8x to 5.7x, goodwill now exceeding total equity, reported free cash flow negative in three of four years, a 3/9 Piotroski score, and a 37% realised volatility with two distinct –25%+ drawdowns in three years. The company is buying growth with debt and equity issuance (+5.3% share count dilution) and has not yet demonstrated that the acquired revenue converts to bottom-line cash.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **🔴 Earnings quality — unexplained ~$600–700M below-the-line charge (FY2025).** Operating income +37% but net income –18.5%; implied ~54% effective tax rate. Q3-2025 operating margin of 1.6% and a net loss corroborate a large one-off. *Needs segment-level / non-GAAP reconciliation — not retrievable here.*
2. **🔴 Negative tangible book equity: –$631M.** Goodwill and intangibles ($5,975M) now equal **112% of total equity** ($5,345M). Goodwill rose **63% in a single year** (+$2,320M) on the Atria/Commonwealth transactions. Any impairment directly hits regulatory and rating-agency capital.
3. **🔴 Coverage downtrend: 9.81x → 8.73x → 6.08x → 5.66x.** Interest expense +220% since 2022 while EBIT grew 85%. Debt +83% in two years.
4. **🔴 Reported FCF negative 3 of 4 years, cumulative –$1,030M (2022–2025).** Distorted by broker-dealer working capital, but capex has nearly doubled (+86%) and the reported FCF yield is –4.67%.
5. **🟠 Piotroski F-Score 3/9 — "Weak."** Fails on operating cash flow, accruals, ROA trend, gross margin, asset turnover, and dilution.
6. **🟠 Share dilution of 5.3%** (74.7M → 78.7M shares) despite $133M of buybacks — buybacks are not offsetting issuance.
7. **🟠 Altman Z-Score 2.22 (grey zone)** — but this metric is **not calibrated for broker-dealers** (it assumes a manufacturing working-capital structure, and LPL's 2.42x current ratio is a client-funds artifact). I assign it low weight and do not treat it as a bankruptcy signal.
8. **🟠 Market risk — 33.1% max drawdown and 37.5% vol.** Two separate –25%+ episodes in three years; the equity is currently –23.6% off its Feb-2026 high.

Monitor three things: (1) whether the FY2025 below-the-line charge is genuinely one-time, (2) tangible equity, which is already negative, and (3) whether the –$2.6B working-capital outflow reverses. If all three resolve favorably, this re-rates to **Low-Moderate**. If goodwill growth continues on the same trajectory, the ratings trajectory is negative.
