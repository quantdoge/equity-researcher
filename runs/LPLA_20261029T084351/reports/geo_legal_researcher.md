## 1. Geographic Risk Map

LPLA is, in substance, a **single-jurisdiction business**. Multiple 10-Ks state plainly that "our revenues for the periods presented were derived from our operations in the United States," and the company profile describes services delivered to advisors "across the United States" (SEC EDGAR 10-Ks, FY2016/FY2015 language; LPL FY2025 10-K, CIK 0001397911).

**Key insight:** LPLA carries essentially **no classic geopolitical risk** — no tariffs, no currency repatriation controls, no export controls, no nationalisation exposure. Its risk is **domestic US political-regulatory risk**, which is idiosyncratically high for a firm of its supervisory surface area ($2.6T client assets, ~24,000+ advisers, Q3 2026 release).

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**Risk 1 — US advice-standard and retirement-rule pendulum (Probability: High; Impact: Medium)**
The regulatory baseline is shifting deregulatory in 2026: DOL's EBSA **removed the Biden-era 2024 "Retirement Security" fiduciary rule from the CFR** and is consulting on a narrower DC-plan investment-alternatives proposal (EBSA Technical Release 2026-01; regulations.gov EBSA-2026-0166; Ascensus industry note). This eases near-term compliance cost but entrenches **state-level divergence** — NASAA, the NY AG, and state securities administrators have repeatedly acted where federal policy loosens (NASAA settlements with LPL on non-traded REITs and unregistered securities, nasaa.org/37204, /44990; NY AG settlement agreement with LPL). Any future administration flip re-imposes the rule and re-prices the IRA/annuity distribution model.

**Risk 2 — Cash-sweep economics and political scrutiny of sweep yields (Probability: Medium; Impact: Medium–High)**
Sweep revenue is LPL's most rate- and politics-sensitive line. Client cash was **$57B (2.2% of client assets)** in Q3 2026, down from 2.4% a year earlier (GlobeNewswire, 2026-10-27) — a declining mix into a Fed-easing cycle compresses revenue. The **SEC closed its cash-sweep investigation on 23 January 2026 with no enforcement recommended** (wealthmanagement.com; Yahoo Finance), materially de-risking the federal path; but the **private class action survives** (see §3 section).

**Risk 3 — Cyber / state-actor and third-party data risk as a political and disclosure risk (Probability: Medium; Impact: Medium)**
A November 2025 phishing/malware incident compromised adviser devices and led to unauthorised client transactions, with **1,581 clients affected** and notifications to state AGs (Maine) in April 2026 (wealthmanagement.com; ThinkAdvisor 2026-04-23; UpGuard). This is not geopolitically motivated per public reporting, but it sits inside SEC cybersecurity-disclosure and Reg S-P expectations and is now a recurring pattern of regulatory-exam exposure.

*(Honourable mention, non-geopolitical but material: the Commonwealth Financial Network conversion due Q4 2026 — asset retention guided to ~83%, run-rate EBITDA ~$415M — requires FINRA Rule 1017 material-change approval and carries integration/continuity risk; leverage 1.93x, corporate cash $563M.)*

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Enforcement history — a persistent pattern of supervisory/AML findings:**
- **SEC, January 2025:** AML program failures; **$18M civil penalty** plus mandated remediation (SEC press release 2025-17).
- **SEC, 2024:** **~$50M** for off-channel communications / recordkeeping failures (whitesecuritieslaw.com; widely reported).
- **FINRA, September 2025:** censure, **$5.5M fine + $651,374.51 restitution (>$6M total)** for Reg BI and transaction-supervision lapses, including failure to properly record **more than 830,000 transactions** (Barron's; Compliance Week; FINRA SD-2425_LPL_Financial_LLC_20250911). Note: the FINRA docket is styled as a **statutory-disqualification (SD) matter** under Exchange Act §3(a)(39)(F) — worth confirming in the 10-K whether this triggers any membership/eligibility conditions.
- **State regulators:** multi-state non-traded REIT settlement (**$1.425M** civil penalties across 48 states + DC, plus investor remediation); earlier **$26M** multistate settlement over unregistered, non-exempt securities (2018); a small 2026 Missouri Securities Division penalty ($98,920 per Violation Tracker).
- BrokerCheck third-party aggregators cite **~245 disciplinary events** for the firm — unaudited, but directionally consistent with the pattern.

**Material litigation:**
- ***In re LPL Financial Cash Sweep Litigation*, S.D. Cal. No. 24-cv-1228 (TWR/AHG)** — consolidated putative class action filed July 2024. On **30 June 2025** the court **granted in part** defendants' motion to dismiss: **breach of fiduciary duty and breach of contract claims dismissed; unjust enrichment claim survived**. Still pending; the surviving theory is quasi-contractual (fee/spread disgorgement), so the tail is monetary, not injunctive. (FindLaw caselaw 117528560; rainintelligence.com.)
- **Securities class action / plaintiff-firm investigations** arising from the same cash-sweep disclosures (Rosen Law "investigation of potential securities claims") — investigation stage, no confirmed filed securities class action found.
- **CUSO Financial Services data-breach class action** — **$1.75M settlement**, preliminary approval 16 July 2026, claim deadline 16 November 2026 (Bloomberg Law; settlementinsight.com).
- **PHL Variable Insurance Co. class action** — client losses tied to a carrier now in **rehabilitation/liquidation in Connecticut**; reputational and suitability exposure rather than a direct balance-sheet item (edwardstonelaw.com).

**Regulatory posture:** multiple regulators with overlapping reach — SEC, FINRA, CFTC/NFA (LPL Enterprise introducing BD), state securities administrators, DOL/EBSA, and state AGs. Note Q3 2025 recorded a **net loss of $30M** versus Q3 2026 net income of $357M (GlobeNewswire) — the cause of that one-time charge (legal accrual or deal cost) should be confirmed; it is not disclosed in the release excerpt.

## 5. Overall Rating

**Geopolitical risk: LOW.** Effectively a US-only revenue base; no sanctions, FX, expropriation, or trade-war exposure. Any "geopolitical" shock transmits only through US equity markets and Fed policy (asset-based fees, sweep revenue), not through jurisdictional operations.

**Legal & regulatory risk: MEDIUM–HIGH.** This is the binding constraint. LPL has paid well over $100M in aggregate penalties across SEC, FINRA, and state settlements in recent years, with a repeat-failure pattern in supervision and AML that raises the probability of escalating penalties (including potential business restrictions) rather than purely monetary outcomes. Offsets: the **SEC's January 2026 closure of the cash-sweep investigation** removes the largest single federal tail risk; the cash-sweep class action has been narrowed to unjust enrichment; the 2026 federal rulemaking environment is deregulatory.

### **Composite Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: MEDIUM**
*(driven almost entirely by domestic US regulatory/enforcement risk, not by geography)*

**What would move it to HIGH:** a FINRA or SEC action imposing business limitations or a supervisory-capacity mandate; an adverse class certification or large settlement in the cash-sweep matter; a Commonwealth conversion that fails on retention (~83% guided) or draws FINRA conditions; or a second material cyber incident in 2026–27.
