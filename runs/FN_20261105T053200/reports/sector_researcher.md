## SECTOR BRIEF — Fabrinet (NYSE: FN)
*Sector: Technology / Hardware, Equipment & Parts — sub-industry: outsourced optical packaging & precision electro-mechanical manufacturing (EMS for optical/datacom). Fiscal year ends late June. Latest data: FY2026 (ended 26-Jun-2026) plus Q1 FY2027 (ended 25-Sep-2026).*

---

### 1. Sector Overview
Fabrinet sits at the intersection of two sectors: **contract manufacturing (EMS)** and the **optical datacom component supply chain** that AI clusters depend on. The demand driver is optical interconnect intensity inside AI data centres — Yole estimates datacom optical component revenue exceeded **$18bn in 2025, up >70% YoY**, with 800G shipments expected to **more than double in 2026** (LightCounting) and 1.6T ramping from a small base in 2025 into a multi-million-unit market by 2027. The binding constraint is not demand but **supply**: McKinsey estimates 800G transceiver production running **40–60% below demand through 2027**, with 1.6T shortfalls of 30–40% — a classic mid-to-late-stage shortage upcycle with high utilisation and real pricing power for qualified capacity. Structurally the industry is **moderately concentrated at the module level** (Innolight, Eoptolink, Coherent, Lumentum, Hisense, AAOI) but **highly concentrated in merchant outsourced optical packaging**, where Fabrinet is the scaled, qualified supplier of choice.

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Optical intensity per AI cluster is compounding.** Each GPU generation lifts port count and speed (800G → 1.6T → 3.2T); Fabrinet's data centre revenue grew **+78% YoY** in Q1 FY2027, driving total revenue **+51% to $1.47bn** with Q2 FY27 guided to **$1.60–1.65bn (+43% YoY)**.
- **Structural component/assembly shortage = multi-year backlog and pricing power.** NVIDIA's reported ~$4bn laser/component lock-up and persistent EML/CW-laser scarcity mean qualified assembly capacity (Fabrinet's Bangkok/Chonburi footprint) is being pre-bought by customers, often with capex support and longer visibility.
- **Outsourcing penetration + non-datacom diversification.** OEMs continue to shift labour-intensive optical/electro-mechanical assembly to low-cost ASEAN; FN pairs the datacom boom with automotive sensors, industrial lasers, medical and semiconductor-processing laser programmes that hedge the eventual AI capex digestion.

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Extreme customer concentration.** FY2026 10-K shows **four customers each >10% of revenue** (largest 19.9%, second 16.3%; FY2025 was NVIDIA 27.6% and Cisco 18.2%); >10% customers together were **~59% of revenue**. A single programme re-allocation or customer in-sourcing (Innolight/Eoptolink and hyperscalers increasingly assembling in-house) is a material revenue event.
- **Structural margin erosion from the cost-plus model.** Contractual annual price-downs plus mix: gross margin has drifted **12.71% (FY23) → 12.35% (FY24) → 12.09% (FY25) → 11.99% (FY26)**. Operating margin (9.97%) is held only by SG&A discipline (SG&A just **2.0% of revenue**). Also a **new global minimum tax** kept GAAP EPS growth well below sales growth in Q1 FY27.
- **Cycle, capex and geopolitical risk.** Heavy building/equipment investment creates fixed-cost risk if hyperscaler capex digests in 2027–28; exposure to US tariffs on Thai/Malaysian output, Thailand's border tensions, and longer-term CPO (co-packaged optics) substitution risk for pluggable module assembly.

**Read-across:** FN grows **~1.6x the peer median** and above every other EMS name. Its 12.0% gross margin is structurally EMS-like (not comparable to Coherent/Lumentum's 37–42%, which own the optical chips), but within EMS it is **top-of-class**, and its **10.0% operating margin and 10.2% net margin are the best in the peer group** — CLS 8.6%/6.7%, JBL 4.7%/2.9%, SANM 4.4%/3.0%. FN converts ~2.3x Jabil's revenue into a similar absolute operating margin percentage with one-third the SG&A ratio. Only Lumentum is out-growing FN, and Lumentum is capturing the same 1.6T cycle at much higher gross margin but with far greater earnings volatility.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**
- **Scale/qualification moat:** the largest merchant provider of advanced optical packaging; customer qualification cycles of 12–18 months and co-developed process IP make switching costly.
- **Best-in-class EMS profitability:** 10.0% operating margin vs 4–9% for Jabil/Sanmina/Celestica, achieved on a cost-plus model — evidence of genuine value-added process content rather than pure box-build.
- **Growth, not share loss:** 4-year revenue CAGR **19.8%** with acceleration to +35.7% (FY26) and +51% (Q1 FY27) — FN is gaining share of outsourced optical capacity, not losing it.
- **Balance-sheet/valuation context:** $12.2bn market cap, FY26 diluted EPS $13.21 → **~25.9x trailing**, ~21x on FY27 GAAP guidance (~$15.5–16), with essentially no debt (interest expense $84k) — cheap relative to 35–50% growth, which is the market pricing concentration and cycle risk, not margin risk.
- **Deductions from "strong":** gross margin trending down four consecutive years; Porter buyer power is **HIGH** (top-4 ≈ 59% of revenue); supplier power **HIGH** (laser/chip allocation gates throughput); threat of new entrants **MEDIUM-LOW**; substitutes **LOW-MEDIUM** (copper for short reach, CPO long term); rivalry **MEDIUM**. Net: strong position, weak bargaining architecture.

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**
Demand is supply-constrained through 2027, optical content per AI system is rising through at least two speed generations (1.6T, 3.2T), and the outsourced-assembly layer is thin on qualified capacity — a favourable backdrop for a scale incumbent. The rating is capped below "highly attractive" by (i) late-cycle positioning: Yole/LightCounting growth rates of +60–70% in 2025–26 are peak-ish and decelerate thereafter, (ii) customer concentration that is worse than the EMS peer group, and (iii) a cost-plus margin structure that caps incremental gross margin even in a boom. **Sector cycle position: mid-to-late expansion of an AI-capex-driven upcycle — maximum current earnings momentum, rising 2027–28 digestion risk.**
