# Forensic Short-Seller Analysis: Fabrinet (FN)

**Price $341.64 | Market cap $12.24B | TTM P/E (GAAP) 25.8x | TTM FCF negative | -54% from May-2026 high of $746**

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **LOW**

FY2026 was a year in which revenue grew 35.7% and GAAP EPS grew 42%, while **free cash flow fell to $3.0 million** and the income statement stopped converting to cash altogether. Q1 FY2027 got worse, not better: revenue +50.7%, GAAP net income +7.9%.

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 | Q1 FY27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue growth | +9.0% | +18.6% | +35.7% | **+50.7%** |
| Gross margin | 12.35% | 12.09% | **11.99%** | 11.79% |
| GAAP net income growth | +19.5% | +12.3% | +42.2% | **+7.9%** |
| Operating cash flow | $413.1M | $328.4M | **$256.7M (-22%)** | $21.3M (-79%) |
| Capex | $47.5M | $121.8M | **$253.7M** | $158.7M |
| Free cash flow | $365.6M | $206.5M | **$3.0M** | **-$137.4M** |
| OCF / Net income | 139% | 99% | **54%** | **21%** |
| FCF / Net income | 123% | 62% | **0.6%** | negative |

**This is the crux of the bear case: the P&L and the cash flow statement have decoupled.** Over three years the company went from converting 123% of earnings into free cash to 0.6%. TTM free cash flow is approximately **negative $192 million**.

The reason is not fraud — it is a working-capital and capex binge: FY26 absorbed $259.9M of cash into working capital and $253.7M of capex. But it means every dollar of the "42% EPS growth" narrative is currently being funded by the balance sheet, and increasingly by debt.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**Red Flag 1 — GAAP earnings have stopped growing despite 51% revenue growth.** Q1 FY27: revenue $1,474.5M (+50.7%), GAAP operating income $146.0M (+55%), but **GAAP net income of $103.5M was up only 7.9%** ($95.9M). GAAP net margin collapsed from 9.8% to 7.0%. Something below the operating line is eating 100% of the incremental profit.

**Red Flag 2 — The tax holiday is over.** Income tax expense went from **$5.5M to $26.0M** year-over-year in Q1 FY27. For three consecutive years Fabrinet's net income *exceeded* its operating income (FY26: net income $473.0M vs operating income $462.9M), implying an effective tax rate of roughly **0–5%** courtesy of its Cayman/Thailand structure. Q1 FY27 implies roughly **20%**. If that normalization is structural — as Pillar Two minimum-tax rules would suggest — it is a **permanent ~15-point haircut to net income**, i.e., FY26's $13.05 EPS was really ~$11.00 on a normalized rate. The stock's multiple has been applied to a tax artefact.

**Red Flag 3 — $54M per quarter of non-GAAP add-backs.** Q1 FY27 non-GAAP net income of $157.4M vs GAAP $103.5M; non-GAAP EPS $4.34 vs GAAP $2.86 (**+52%**). Q2 guidance: GAAP $3.81–3.96 vs non-GAAP $4.77–4.92. Management is guiding the market to a number 25% above what it will actually report. The gap is not just stock comp ($9M/qtr) — it includes a **$21.5M loss on non-marketable equity securities**.

**Red Flag 4 — Fabrinet is taking equity risk in its own customer ecosystem.** The $21.5M mark-to-market loss on non-marketable equity securities is new and is the single largest contributor to the Beneish AQI blowout (see §3). A contract manufacturer booking losses on private-company equity stakes is taking principal risk alongside its customers, at a 12% gross margin. There is no disclosure in the data I could access on who the counterparties are — that opacity is itself a flag.

**Red Flag 5 — Inventory days up 21 days in one year.** 70.5 → **91.3 days**. Inventory rose 75.8% to $1,021M against revenue growth of 35.7%. That is ~$235M of inventory financed beyond the growth rate.

**Red Flag 6 — Negative free cash flow funded by debt and buybacks.** In Q1 FY27 Fabrinet drew **$74.8M of long-term borrowings** — a company that was debt-free at $4.0M total debt in June — and spent **$29.1M on buybacks**. Borrowing to repurchase stock while free cash flow is negative $137M is a capital-allocation signal, not a shareholder-return signal.

**Red Flag 7 — Insider selling, no insider buying.** Director H. Bahrami sold 2,500 shares at ~$711 on 22-May-2026 (within 4% of the all-time high); Director T. Kelly sold $1.15M on 27-Feb-2026; an officer sold 2,500 shares at ~$386 on 3-Sep-2026. The August 2026 Form 4 cluster is director RSU grants, not purchases. **Zero open-market insider buys** at any point in the data set. Individually modest and plan-consistent; collectively a one-way signal.

**What I did NOT find (credit where due):** No receivables red flag — AR grew 34.1% vs revenue 35.7% in FY26, i.e. collections kept pace. No auditor change, restatement, or short-seller allegation on record. No inventory-obsolescence charge. **The DSRI of 0.988 in the Beneish model confirms there is no channel-stuffing signature.** This is not a Hindenburg situation.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

**This is where a genuine moat-erosion argument exists, and it is the most durable part of the bear case.**

- **Gross margin is structurally capped at ~12% and shrinking slowly.** 12.35% → 12.09% → 11.99% → 11.79%. Fabrinet has *negative* pricing power: it cannot pass through. Its FY26 operating margin (10.0%) and net margin (10.2%) were almost identical — meaning the entire bottom line was the tax line, not the business.
- **Customer concentration is extreme and rising: four customers = 57.4% of FY26 revenue** (Cisco ~20%, NVIDIA ~16%, Nokia ~11%, plus a fourth at ~10%) — up from two 10%+ customers in FY25. NVIDIA is the fastest-growing relationship. A 16%-of-revenue customer who is the largest company in the world, with infinite resources to dual-source or in-source, is not a moat — it is a negotiating counterparty.
- **Technology displacement: co-packaged optics (CPO).** Fabrinet's value pool is the *pluggable* transceiver assembly. CPO moves the optical engine into the switch package, structurally shrinking the merchant pluggable module market that Fabrinet assembles. Management's Q1 FY27 answer — that pluggables and CPO "will coexist as complementary architectures for the foreseeable future" — is exactly what every incumbent says before its value pool is re-based. Add LPO (linear pluggable optics), which strips out the DSP content, and the assembly value per port falls further.
- **Zero switching costs.** Build-to-print contract manufacturing. The customer owns the design, the IP, and the demand signal. Fabrinet owns the building and the working capital.

**Competitive Threat Rating: HIGH.** Not imminent, but terminal-value-relevant, and it lands precisely in the FY2028–2030 window that consensus multiples are being applied to.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — Is There a Credible Bear Case?

**Yes, but it is a quality-and-valuation short, not a fraud short.** The one-sentence thesis:

> Fabrinet is a 12%-gross-margin, price-taking contract manufacturer whose reported earnings growth has been manufactured by a ~5% tax rate, whose cash conversion has collapsed to 21% with negative free cash flow, and whose single-analyst long-dated estimates require an arithmetic near-impossibility — gross margin expansion of 350–425bps — from a business with no pricing power and 57% customer concentration.

**The arithmetic that consensus requires:**
- FY2030 estimate: $11.33B revenue, $1.286B net income = **11.4% net margin**, from an analyst base of **one**.
- To earn an 11.4% net margin at a normalized 20% tax rate, pre-tax margin must be 14.3%. With opex at ~2% of revenue, **gross margin must rise from 12.0% to ~16.2%**.
- Even the FY2028 estimate ($7.87B revenue, $849.6M net income) requires gross margin of ~15.5%.
- **Consensus is double-counting:** it assumes revenue almost triples *and* gross margin expands 400bps *and* (implicitly) the tax holiday persists. Fabrinet has never earned a 15% gross margin in the data available to me.

**Valuation:** 25.8x TTM GAAP EPS of $13.25, 2.4x TTM revenue, and an **indefinitely negative free cash flow yield**. A business with no positive FCF trades on an EBITDA-ish multiple and a narrative.

**Short-side catalysts:**
1. Q2 FY27 (Dec-2026): does GAAP EPS of $3.81–3.96 versus $4.77–4.92 non-GAAP widen the credibility gap further?
2. Any further non-marketable equity markdowns — these are marked through a "non-GAAP" line, so the market is being trained to ignore them.
3. Full-year tax rate guidance / Pillar Two disclosure in the next 10-Q.
4. First negative inventory revision or days-inventory charge.
5. A CPO design win at a hyperscaler that excludes merchant pluggables.
