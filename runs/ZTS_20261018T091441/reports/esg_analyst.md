## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives (verified from company disclosure):**
- **29% reduction in Scope 1+2 emissions** vs 2021 base year; 37 energy-reduction projects completed in 2025; on-site solar at eight facilities.
- **85% renewable electricity** (RE100-aligned), up from 80% in 2024, targeting 100% by 2030; **carbon-neutral own operations by 2030** on track.
- **Transportation emissions intensity −36%** vs 2019 — target (25% by 2025) over-delivered.
- **Disclosure quality improving:** obtained **first-time independent third-party limited assurance over Scope 1+2** in 2025 — a genuine governance-of-data positive that many mid-cap pharma peers still lack.

**Deductions:**
- **Base-year flattering:** the 2024 rebasing to strip out the divested Medicated Feed Additives (MFA) business means the 29% figure partly reflects **portfolio shrinkage, not absolute decarbonisation**.
- **Boundary gap:** emissions from the 2025 Veterinary Pathology Group acquisition are *excluded* from reported totals — a recurring pattern that erodes comparability.
- **No Scope 3 reduction target identified** in the reviewed disclosures, despite Scope 3 dominating a pharma-style value chain (API suppliers, contract manufacturing, cold-chain distribution, customer use).
- **PiE** is the material environmental risk: antibiotic/parasiticide residues and manufacturing effluent face tightening EU scrutiny (EEA One Health analysis of veterinary antimicrobials in the environment). For animal health this is a *product-approval* risk, not just a reputational one.
- Physical climate/water stress at manufacturing sites not assessed in retrieved data — **〔unverified〕**.

**Verdict:** Below-average carbon intensity for a manufacturer, credible and improving disclosure, but the material environmental risk is regulatory-chemical (PiE/AMR), not carbon.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

**The Librela (bedinvetmab) product-safety controversy is the single most financially material ESG item at ZTS.** Documented sequence:
- **FDA Dear Veterinarian Letter** warning of adverse events in dogs including **ataxia, seizures, paresis, recumbency and other neurologic signs**; FDA continues to encourage AE reporting.
- **US label update (2025)** for Librela — a safety-driven labeling change on a flagship companion-animal monoclonal antibody.
- **FDA cited Zoetis for misleading claims about Librela efficacy** — a *promotional-compliance* failure, which is more damaging than the safety signal itself because it goes to internal controls over marketing (a Governance issue).
- **Class action litigation filed April 2025** alleging safety risks were not adequately disclosed; **one federal class action in New Jersey was dismissed**, but **new US test cases are reported as pending** — the litigation is not resolved.
- **Activist campaign** (stopzoetis.org) claims **>12,000 AE reports and 1,851 deaths**; company counters that **no single AE exceeds "rare" (<10 per 10,000 dogs treated)**. Treat activist figures as unverified advocacy statistics; FDA's own actions, however, are verified and independent. Peer-reviewed pharmacovigilance work broadly supports the "rare/very rare" characterization — so the *scientific* risk is modest while the *litigation and reputational* risk is real.

**Other social factors:**
- **AMR/antibiotic stewardship:** EU has banned growth promoters (2006), restricted prophylactic use (Reg. 2019/6), and targets a **50% reduction in livestock antimicrobial use vs 2018 by 2030**; EU-27 use already fell ~28% 2018–2022. Zoetis's livestock anti-infective book faces structural volume headwind. **Mitigant:** the **MFA divestiture** removed the most AMR-sensitive product line — a quiet, sensible ESG de-risking.
- **Workforce:** ~14,500 employees; reports of incremental layoffs (~100 staff) surfaced in 2026 — **single-source, unverified**, directionally consistent with a revenue-guided-down year. Turnover/industrial-action risk: low.
- **Positive social capital:** Zoetis Foundation **$3.1M in vet scholarships (460+ students)**, **20,700 volunteer hours (104% of target)**, **482,000 pet owners** reached via access programmes, and the **A.L.P.H.A.** Africa initiative (100,000+ trained; **19.9M cows and 391.3M chickens treated**, both ~doubling targets). This is substantive and supports emerging-market tender access.

**Assessment: HIGH** — driven by an active, regulator-confirmed product-safety and promotional-compliance episode plus structural AMR policy pressure.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Supporting the score:**
- **Single class of common stock, one vote per share** — no dual-class structure, no unequal voting rights.
- Board appears **majority-independent with a separated Chair/CEO structure historically** (CEO Kristin Peck sits on the board but an independent non-executive chaired the board under Michael McCallister, who has since **retired from the board**) — **〔chair independence post-transition unverified〕**.
- **Active board refreshment:** Stephanie Tilenius added Oct 2025; Iris Knobloch added Oct 13, 2026 — signals genuine renewal rather than entrenchment.
- **ESG explicitly linked to executive pay:** Executive Team members carry shared and individual objectives in the annual incentive plan, one of six strategic priorities being *"Advance Sustainability in Animal Health for a Better Future."* Sustainability is also embedded in strategic planning and resource allocation (company reports this aspiration as "Achieved").
- **No restatement, material weakness, or control failure** disclosed in the reviewed 10-Q certifications.

**Deductions:**
- **Promotional-compliance failure** (FDA citation for misleading Librela efficacy claims) indicates the marketing-review control environment did not perform — a governance-of-conduct weakness, in a company whose licence to operate rests on veterinary trust.
- **ESG-pay linkage is qualitative and unquantified** — no disclosed weighting, no disclosed Scope 3 target. Easy to satisfy, hard to verify.
- **Peer-relative pay, say-on-pay support levels, and shareholder proposal outcomes could not be verified** (proxy unretrievable) — a 1-point haircut applied for disclosure opacity.
- Board oversight of a high-profile product-safety controversy with continuing litigation raises a question the audit/regulatory-compliance committee should be answering publicly.

**Score: 7/10 — solid structural governance, blemished by a conduct/compliance event and unverifiable compensation alignment.**

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (slight negative skew)

**For the thesis:**
1. **Not a valuation driver — a cost-of-capital and tail-risk driver.** At ~13x earnings the market has already de-rated the franchise heavily; ESG factors are *not* the marginal price-setter, but they **cap the re-rating case**: an open Librela litigation docket plus an FDA promotional-compliance record plus unquantified Scope 3 and pay-linkage disclosure gives a generalist PM no ESG-supported reason to underwrite multiple expansion.
2. **The cleanest ESG-positive signal is governance, not environment.** Single share class, board refreshment (Tilenius 2025, Knobloch Oct 2026), ESG in the incentive plan, and — most concretely — **first-time third-party assurance on Scope 1+2**. That is a management genuinely tightening its own reporting standards, which correlates with better disclosure discipline generally.
3. **The MFA divestiture is underappreciated as ESG de-risking.** Removing medicated feed additives materially reduces exposure to the EU's most aggressive antimicrobial-restriction regime. Combined with A.L.P.H.A.'s emerging-market access work, the livestock franchise is being repositioned away from the highest-regulatory-risk products.
4. **Watch items that would flip this to NEGATIVE:** (a) an adverse Librela verdict or a **new FDA/EMA safety action** (class-wide label restriction); (b) emergence of **Scope 3 targets or CSRD-grade reporting failures** as an EU compliance issue; (c) discovery that chair independence was *not* preserved after McCallister's retirement — a proxy-advisor flashpoint; (d) evidence of a formal restructuring with meaningful severance charges, which would convert the unverified layoff chatter into a real social and margin story.
5. **What would make it POSITIVE:** resolution/dismissal of the Librela docket with no new safety action, restoration of a credible 2030 carbon-neutral path *with* a Scope 3 target, and quantified ESG weighting in executive pay.

**Bottom line:** ZTS is an **Average** ESG profile whose material ESG risk is concentrated in one place — companion-animal product safety and the promotional controls around it. That risk is *priced but unresolved*; it does not by itself justify a position, and it argues for sizing the name as a value/turnaround rather than a quality compounder until the Librela litigation and regulatory record are closed out.
