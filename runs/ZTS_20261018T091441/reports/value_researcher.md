# Zoetis Inc. (ZTS) — Value Analysis

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW — and narrowing over the last 24 months (was a credible "Wide" as recently as 2024).**

What is genuinely good:
- **Intangible assets / regulatory barriers.** Animal drug and vaccine approvals are slow and expensive; Zoetis is the #1 global animal health company (~$9.5B revenue, operations in ~100 countries) with a portfolio of trusted brands: Apoquel, Cytopoint, Simparica Trio, Revolution/Stronghold, Vanguard, Dectomax, Cerenia, Convenia, plus a diagnostics franchise.
- **Switching costs are real but shallow.** Vets embed products in clinic protocols and livestock producers embed vaccine regimens, but prescribers will substitute when a pet owner flinches at price — which is exactly what happened in 2026.
- **The ROIC test passes decisively.** ROIC of 28.1% / 29.5% / 26.1% / 23.4% (2025/24/23/22) against a WACC of ~7.5–8%. A ~20‑point spread, sustained for years, is the signature of a moated business.

Why the moat has narrowed:
- **U.S. companion animal organic revenue fell 11% in Q2 2026** after years of mid‑to‑high single‑digit growth. Management attributes it to lower clinic visits, pet‑owner price sensitivity, "heightened competitive pressure" in dermatology and Simparica Trio, **generic competition on Cerenia and Convenia**, and lower Librela sales.
- Pricing power is the core of a moat. A business that raised price annually for a decade and suddenly cannot is telling you something about its bargaining position.
- The offset is real but less durable: U.S. livestock +23% in Q2 2026 (beef cattle economics, poultry disease outbreaks) and International +6% organic. Livestock is cyclical and outbreak‑dependent — it is not a moat, it is a tailwind.

Net: a high‑quality franchise in a genuine cyclical/competitive air pocket, not a broken business — but the era of 35x earnings on 8% organic growth is over.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

Current multiples: **12.8x 2026E adjusted EPS ($6.20)**, 13.2x FY25 GAAP EPS, **EV/EBITDA ~10.5x**, **FCF yield 6.5%**, **dividend yield 2.67%** ($2.12/yr). Peer median P/E is ~30x, a 56% discount — but the peer set is specialty/generic pharma with different growth, and ZTS has historically traded at 30–45x. The de‑rating is rational, not a mispricing accident.

**Intrinsic value (own DCF, normalized FCF ~$2.2–2.3B, WACC 8.5%, terminal 2.5%):**

| Scenario | Assumptions | Value/share |
|---|---|---|
| Bear | Organic stays negative 2 yrs, then ~2%; FCF ~$1.9B; WACC 9% | **$47** |
| Base | 5% FCF growth yrs 1–5, 3.5% yrs 6–10, 2.5% terminal | **$87–92** |
| Bull | Demand normalizes + pipeline delivers; 7% then 4%; WACC 8% | **$115** |
| Probability‑weighted (25/50/25) | | **~$86** |

**Intrinsic value ≈ $88. At $79.38 the stock trades at roughly an 10% discount — inside fair value, not at a margin of safety.**

Useful cross‑check: a 6.5% FCF yield against a 9% required return implies the market is capitalizing only ~2.5% perpetual FCF growth. That is a genuinely undemanding assumption for the global leader with two‑thirds of revenue outside the U.S. growing 6% organic. So the price is not heroic — it is simply *not cheap enough*. I require a 25% discount to $88, i.e. **≤ $66–67**.

Note: the built‑in DCF tool returns $175B of value because it extrapolates a 19.9% three‑year FCF growth rate from a COVID‑recovery base. That output is unusable and I have discarded it.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment: **B− (adequate, not owner‑operator)**

**Positives**
- Kristin Peck (CEO since 2020) grew revenue from $6.7B to $9.5B (7.2% CAGR) with 400bp of operating margin expansion and ~185 product innovations in 2025.
- Dividend raised every year since IPO ($0.25 → $0.53 quarterly); share count cut from 479M (2020) to ~413M today (~14%), so per‑share value creation is real.
- Pipeline is substantive: long‑acting OA pain mAbs (Lenivia/Portela) approved in EU/Canada/GB, plus CKD, oncology, cardiology, anxiety and obesity candidates.

**Blemishes — and they matter**
- **Capital allocation error:** $3.24B of buybacks in 2025 plus $1.86B in 2024, largely at prices between $120 and $190, partly debt‑funded. That is roughly $5B of shareholder money repurchased at ~1.5–2.4x today's price. Buffett's test — "buy back stock only below intrinsic value" — was failed.
- **Guidance credibility:** FY26 adjusted EPS guidance cut from $7.00–7.10 (Feb) to $6.15–6.25 (Aug), a 12% cut, with two consecutive quarterly misses. Management appears to have been slow to recognize the change in U.S. pet‑owner behavior.
- Insider ownership is modest (2013 spin‑off legacy), and the multi‑year ERP/fiscal‑year‑alignment transition will inject accounting noise through 2027.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (accumulate on weakness)

This is a good business at a fair price — and a fair price is not a buy. Three things keep me from committing capital today:

1. **No margin of safety.** ~10% discount to an $88 intrinsic value versus the 25–30% I require.
2. **Unresolved trajectory.** Is 2026 a cyclical trough in vet visits, or the start of structurally slower companion‑animal growth? Q3 2026 results (5‑Nov‑2026) will not settle a multi‑year question, and a third guidance cut is plausible.
3. **Moat direction uncertain.** Generic erosion on Cerenia/Convenia is permanent; the dermatology and parasiticide competitive pressure is new and untested.

**Actions**
- **New money: wait.** Buy zone **$60–67** (25–30% discount to IV). At $65 the FCF yield is ~8%, which compensates for the uncertainty.
- **Existing holders: hold.** Do not sell into a 12.8x multiple on a 28%‑ROIC franchise with a 2.7% dividend; the downside is largely priced, and the option value of the pipeline is free at this multiple.
- **Upgrade triggers:** (a) U.S. companion animal organic growth returning to positive with stable gross margin; (b) evidence Lenivia/Portela plus CKD/oncology launches are adding >$500M of incremental revenue; (c) management suspending buybacks to delever — a signal of discipline.
- **Downgrade triggers:** a third FY26 guidance cut, net debt/EBITDA above 2.5x, or gross margin compression (the last remaining proof of pricing power).

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Demand model break.** U.S. clinic visits and pet‑owner price sensitivity may be structural (pet‑care cost fatigue, slowing pet population growth), not cyclical. Bear case is $47.
2. **Generic and brand competition.** Cerenia/Convenia erosion is irreversible; Elanco's Zenrelia, Credelio Quattro, Boehringer's NexGard and Merck's Bravecto are pressing the highest‑margin franchises. Apoquel/Cytopoint loss of exclusivity later this decade is the single biggest long‑term risk.
3. **Librela/Solensia.** Safety perception has already dented adoption; a further regulatory or label setback would remove a key growth pillar.
4. **Leverage and payout discipline.** $4B+/yr of dividends and buybacks against $2.2B of FCF on a $3.3B equity base. A deeper EBITDA decline forces either a buyback halt (credibility hit) or higher leverage.
5. **Pipeline concentration.** "12 potential blockbusters" is a promise, not revenue. R&D productivity in animal health is lumpy.
6. **Livestock cyclicality and FX.** The current growth engine (cattle, poultry, International) is exactly the part most exposed to producer economics and currency.
7. **Accounting noise.** ERP transition and fiscal‑year alignment will make 2026–2027 comparability poor, raising the risk of mispricing in both directions.

**Bottom line:** ZTS is a narrow‑moat, high‑ROIC compounder trading at a cyclical‐trough multiple. The market is asking you to believe almost nothing good happens again. I think something good probably does happen — but at $79 I am paid fairly, not generously, for that uncertainty. **Hold; buy at $67 or below, or on proof of a companion‑animal inflection.**
