## 1. Credit Risk Assessment: **INVESTMENT GRADE**

Zoetis is a high-quality, cash-generative issuer with comfortable debt service capacity. Balance-sheet credit metrics are solidly investment grade: net debt/EBITDA **1.76x**, gross debt/EBITDA 2.33x, EBIT/interest **14.8x**, Altman Z **3.14** (Safe zone). The debt is almost entirely long-dated (long-term $9,238M vs. short-term $53M) — there is **no near-term refinancing cliff**. In Aug-2025 the company issued $1.85B (4.150% due 2028, 5.000% due 2035) and used proceeds to retire $1.35B of 2025 maturities — orderly, well-executed liability management that *extended* duration but raised the average coupon.

**The credit quality is not in question. The trajectory is.** Leverage rose from 1.23x to 1.76x net in one year (+51% in net debt dollars) while shareholder returns exceeded free cash flow, and management has now guided FY2026 revenue *below* FY2025. S&P has publicly referenced an expectation that Zoetis holds long-term leverage below 3.0x; at 1.76x there is ~1.2x of EBITDA headroom before that marker is tested (~$2.9B of EBITDA cushion).

*Gap: no agency rating, CDS spread, or covenant threshold is available from the data tools. Rating is inferred from balance-sheet metrics, not confirmed.*

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue −20%

**Anchor: FY2026 guidance is already negative.** Management guided FY2026 revenue to **$9.12–9.32B** (−1.6% to −3.7% vs. FY2025's $9,467M) and cut adjusted EPS to $6.85–$7.00, citing **price-sensitive pet owners reducing veterinary visits**. So the −20% case is a *further* ~17% deterioration beyond the guided base — i.e., a genuine companion-animal demand reset, not a mild miss.

**Base → −20% revenue ($7,574M):**

|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 9,467 | 7,574 | −20.0% |
| Gross profit (68.0%, −250bp) | 6,673 | 5,150 | −22.8% |
| Opex (65% fixed; 5% fixed cut) | 3,076 | 2,761 | −10.2% |

| **EBIT / interest** | 14.8x | **9.8x** | Still IG-comfortable |
| Net income (21% tax) | 2,673 | 1,695 | −36.6% |
| **Net debt / EBITDA** | 1.76x | **2.50x** | Below 3.0x marker |
| FCF | 2,283 | **~1,630** | −29% |
| Dividend cover (FCF/div) | 2.57x | **1.83x** | Covered; buybacks stop |

**Verdict on −20%:** survivable with no covenant stress. Leverage rises to 2.5x — still inside the 3.0x marker S&P references — coverage stays near 10x, and the dividend remains ~1.8x covered. The realistic casualty is the **buyback**, which would be suspended (freeing ~$3.2B of annual liquidity). EBITDA could fall **~50%** (to ~$2.05B) before net leverage hits 3.5x.

**Severe case — revenue −35%** (a Librela-class safety event, or a full channel destocking cycle): revenue ~$6,154M, gross margin 66%, EBIT ~$1,360M, EBITDA ~$1,850M → **net leverage 3.88x (breach territory)**, coverage 5.6x, FCF ~$600M → **dividend not covered (0.7x) and would need to be cut.** This is the true tail, and it is a *plausible* tail for a company with ~65–70% companion-animal revenue concentration and a documented history of safety-signal scares in the category.

**Liquidity runway is not a constraint in any scenario:** $2,312M cash, 3.03x current ratio, $53M of short-term debt, and a 24% FCF margin mean Zoetis does not have a liquidity problem — it has an *allocation* problem.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

*(Credit risk: Low. Liquidity risk: Low. Market/equity risk: High. Capital-allocation risk: Moderate-High. Blended: Moderate, on a deteriorating trajectory.)*

The balance sheet is genuinely strong and would withstand a severe downturn. What keeps this from "Low" is the combination of (a) a rising leverage path funded by buybacks rather than cash generation, (b) an openly declining revenue base, and (c) extreme idiosyncratic equity volatility with a negative three-year Sharpe. The risk here is *not solvency* — it is that financial flexibility is being spent on buybacks precisely as the top line turns negative.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Revenue inflection is negative and guided.** FY2026 guide of $9.12–9.32B is the first decline in the visible history; driver is demand-side (price-sensitive pet owners, lower vet-visit volume), not a one-off — harder to fix than a cost problem. Guidance was *cut* (EPS $7.00–7.10 → $6.85–7.00).
2. **Shareholder returns exceed FCF for a 4th straight year.** 181% of FY2025 FCF returned ($4,124M vs. $2,283M); total debt +41% and net debt +51% YoY. Buybacks are debt-financed at 4.15–5.00% while FCF is flat — value-destructive if the equity is de-rating.
3. **FCF has stopped growing despite revenue growth.** $2,298M → $2,283M (−0.7%) with a −$408M working-capital drag on top of −$512M in 2023. Cash conversion (FCF/NI) fell to 85%.
4. **Thin and shrinking equity cushion.** Equity $4,770M → $3,331M; equity/assets 22%; book D/E 2.85x. Not a solvency issue, but it removes a shock absorber and largely explains the 5/9 F-Score.
5. **Piotroski deterioration in exactly the wrong places** — leverage up, ROA down, asset turnover down, equity change negative.
6. **Undiversifiable equity risk.** Beta 0.61 but 30% vol, −64% peak drawdown, negative Sharpe, and four −9% to −14% single-day earnings gaps in 24 months. Beta-hedging provides no protection.
7. **Concentration.** ~65–70% companion animal revenue ties earnings to discretionary consumer spending and veterinary-clinic economics — both flagged as under pressure.
8. **No refinancing cliff** (a positive): $53M short-term debt, duration extended to 2028/2035. The offset is a step-up in coupon and a higher fixed-charge base.
