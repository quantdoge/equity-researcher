### 1. Geographic Risk Map

Country-level figures below are **annualised from the country revenue tables in the 2025 quarterly releases** (e.g., Q4'25: Brazil $112M, Canada $80M, China $50M, France $52M) — treat as ±0.5pp estimates, not audited FY splits.

Geopolitical risk here is therefore **policy risk transmitted through U.S. trade and pricing decisions**, not emerging-market expropriation.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs — animal-health carve-out uncertainty (Probability: 50–65% of material cost impact; Impact: High)**
The April 2026 presidential proclamation imposes Section 232 duties of **up to 100% on patented pharmaceutical articles and ingredients**, with a first tranche effective **31 Jul 2026** and a second **29 Sep 2026**. Multiple trade-law analyses (PwC Ireland, Livingston, Business Sweden) state **animal-health drugs are exempt *only if* sourced from "trade-deal countries"** (EU/Japan/Switzerland-style agreements) and meeting stated conditions. Zoetis imports into the U.S. from Ireland, China, Australia and Brazil; management itself has publicly said *"it's unclear if animal health will be a part of pharmaceutical tariffs"* (Zoetis CEO interview). Because guidance was cut twice on demand (not tariffs), the tariff line is **not yet fully reflected in consensus** — a negative read-through of several hundred $M of gross duty exposure is plausible, partly mitigable via onshoring/pricing.

**② Brazil & LatAm trade/FX shock (Probability: 40%; Impact: Medium)**
A **25% Section 301 tariff on most Brazilian goods has applied since 22 July 2026**, and Brazil's October 2026 runoff plus a right-leaning Congress is a live macro/FX event (FTI, PIIE). Brazil is Zoetis's largest international market and its livestock franchise there is price-elastic; BRL depreciation hits both translation and local demand.

**③ China revenue erosion inside a deteriorating trade relationship (Probability: already occurring; Impact: Medium)**
China revenue fell from $65M to $50M (−23%) in Q4'25 and −26% in Q3'25, reflecting weak livestock economics and local competition. Zoetis operates an integrated **Suzhou manufacturing + R&D complex** — an asset exposed to export-control escalation, data-localization rules and reciprocal tariff action. Direction of travel is adverse but the revenue base is small (~2% of sales).

*Watch item:* **USMCA 2026 review** (Canada/Mexico ≈4–5% of sales) and EU retaliatory measures on U.S.-origin goods.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Librela/Solensia product-liability wave (the single most under-modelled legal risk).** A U.S. class action alleging Zoetis failed to adequately warn of neurological adverse events was **dismissed at the district-court level**, but reporting indicates a **new U.S. test case is looming** and UK claimant firms have threatened group litigation; UK VMD FOI disclosures (Jan 2026) and an AVMA-published study critical of AE labelling have kept the issue salient. FDA has issued Dear-Veterinarian communications on neurologic signs. Librela/Solensia were a >$1B peak franchise now declining — legal cost is manageable against $32.8B market cap, but **reputational and label-change risk is real**.
- **IP / generic erosion.** Q2'26 U.S. companion animal sales fell 11%, explicitly including **generic competition on Cerenia® and Convenia®**. Apoquel and Simparica patents run to ~2030/2032 — the two largest single-product cliffs in the portfolio and the core of the bear case.
- **Regulatory approvals as a dependency.** Growth now leans on EU/Canada/GB approvals (Lenivia®, Portela™, Poulvac® Procerta®), Chinese approvals (Bonqat®), Japanese approvals (Fostera® Gold) and FDA conditional licences (Dectomax®-CA1 for New World screwworm). Any regulator-driven delay is a guidance risk; conversely, U.S. screwworm emergency-use authorisations are a near-term tailwind.
- **Internal-control / disclosure risk from the ERP & fiscal-year transition.** The multi-year ERP replacement and the "Expected Fiscal Year Alignment" (elimination of the one-month international reporting lag) already shifted Q4'25 international revenue by 2.5–3.5pp — a genuine §404/internal-control and restatement-risk item to monitor.
- **Compliance programme items:** FCPA/UK Bribery Act exposure via tender-driven emerging-market livestock sales; GDPR/EU AI Act exposure via the expanding diagnostics/telehealth stack (VPG, VitalRADS teleradiology, Vetscan algorithms); sanctions exposure legacy in Russia (site still listed, essential-products carve-out). **No disclosed DOJ/SEC/FTC investigation or FCPA action was found in available sources** — but note the 10-K text was not retrievable, so absence of evidence here is not conclusive.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

None confirmed. Three **monitoring flags**:
1. **Animal-health treatment under Section 232** — classification/valuation risk on imports; CBP reporting and duty-payment obligations from 31 Jul 2026.
2. **Emerging-market distribution chains** (Brazil, Mexico, China, Africa) — third-party distributor and tender integrity risk under FCPA; standard for the sector, not company-specific evidence.
3. **Pharmacovigilance adequacy** — the Librela matter is fundamentally a labelling/surveillance-governance dispute; a second U.S. case or a regulator-mandated label change would validate the plaintiffs' theory.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Revenue is spread across ~100 countries with no meaningful expropriation, currency-control or sanctions-belt concentration; the EU/Japan/Australia/Canada exposure (~15–18%) is low-risk and benefits from trade-deal tariff carve-outs. The rating is held at Medium rather than Low by three live overhangs: (i) **unresolved whether animal health falls inside the 100%-cap Section 232 pharma tariff** — the largest binary in the file; (ii) **Brazil/LatAm tariff-plus-election risk** in the top international market; and (iii) **an active, well-organised Librela litigation campaign** with a new U.S. test case expected.

**Bottom line for the memo:** this is a *policy-risk* story, not a country-risk story. The dominant geopolitical variable is U.S. tariff classification of Zoetis's imported animal-health products — a single administrative determination that could move gross costs by hundreds of millions — layered on top of a demand-driven downgrade cycle already visible in guidance ($9.12–9.32B revenue, $6.15–6.25 adj. EPS as of Aug 2026). Legal risk is a slow-burn rather than an acute threat.
