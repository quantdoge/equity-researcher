# Nutanix, Inc. (NTNX) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price: $46.30 (Nov 25, 2026 close) | Market cap: ~$12.5B | Net cash: ~$0.9B | EV: ~$11.6B**
*Stock is −34% from its October 2026 high (~$70) and −15% on the most recent session, driven by a CEO succession announcement and a fiscal-2027 revenue guide that implies further deceleration.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat Rating: NARROW** (trending: stable, not yet widening)

Nutanix sells the software layer that converts commodity x86 servers into a private cloud — the AHV hypervisor, distributed storage, networking and management plane — plus Kubernetes and database services on top. It is, functionally, the principal alternative to VMware's vSphere stack.

**What the moat is built on:**
- **Switching costs (genuine but bounded).** A hyperconverged platform underpins production workloads; rip-and-replace is a multi-quarter, high-risk project. Over 32,000 end customers, ~89% of revenue recurring, deferred revenue of $2.42B (+14.7%). This is real stickiness.
- **Intangible assets / ecosystem.** Software ships on Cisco, Dell, HPE, Lenovo and Fujitsu hardware; 8,350 employees; 16 years of engineering into a hypervisor that is now genuinely production-grade. AMD took a $150M strategic stake (4.1M shares at $36.26, May 2026) — a modest but real endorsement.
- **Cost advantage: none.** It is a software-only model riding partners' hardware, which is precisely why it is exposed to server component inflation.
- **Network effects: none.** No marketplace or data flywheel.

**The decisive evidence against a wide moat:** fiscal 2026 was supposed to be the harvest year — Broadcom's VMware repricing (VMware infrastructure software +29% in a recent Broadcom quarter) was the once-in-a-decade catalyst. Instead **revenue growth decelerated from 18% (FY25) to 12.4% (FY26)**, and FY27 guidance calls for **11–13%**. The 10-K itself is candid: customers are locked into multi-year VMware commitments, are waiting for hardware refresh cycles, or "elect to continue operating VMware environments," and migration opportunities "may arise in multiple waves over an extended period." That is not a company describing a stampede.

**ROIC — the acid test.** FY26 NOPAT ≈ $278.9M operating income × 0.79 ≈ **$220M** against gross invested capital of ~$2.22B (debt $1.52B + equity $0.70B) ≈ **10%**. The reported 12.55% ROIC is flattered by the now-positive equity base and a negative-cash invested-capital calculation. A business earning roughly its cost of capital is, by definition, not yet a moated compounder. Gross margin expansion (79.7% → 86.8% over five years) is genuinely impressive and shows pricing power in the software layer — but it is being spent on SBC and go-to-market rather than dropping to the bottom line.

**Verdict: a good, improving, narrowly-moated challenger — not a franchise.** The moat is real enough to protect the installed base; it is not yet strong enough to force migration.

**Strengths:**
- **Revenue CAGR FY22–FY26 of 15.9%** with exceptional consistency; ~89% recurring. ARR $2.55B (+16%), Q1 FY27 ARR $2.63B (+15%), deferred revenue +14.7% — all running *ahead* of the 11–13% revenue guide, which suggests the guide is conservative or back-end loaded.
- **Operating leverage is real and accelerating.** Operating margin went from −29% to +9.8% in four years; Q1 FY27 GAAP operating margin hit **18.5%** ($142.6M on $772.4M). Gross margin 87.5% in that quarter.
- **Balance sheet is fortress-like.** $2.36B cash and short-term investments vs. $1.35B of convertibles ($500M due 2027 at 0.25%, $862.5M due 2029 at 0.50%), plus an undrawn $500M revolver. Net cash ~$0.9–1.0B, ~8% of market cap. Debt is nearly free; no maturity cliff.

**Deductions — and they are significant:**
- **Earnings quality is poor in FY26.** Reported net income of $1,507M is an artifact: $1,179.7M of it is a non-cash tax benefit from releasing $1,208.2M of valuation allowance against U.S. deferred tax assets (booked in Q4). Pre-tax income was only $327.1M; cash taxes paid were $30.3M. **The reported 8.97x P/E is meaningless — ignore it.** The tax release *is* informative, though: management and auditors now expect sustained U.S. profitability.
- **FCF is heavily subsidized by stock compensation.** SBC was $357.7M (12.5% of revenue). Strip it out and economic FCF is **~$483M**, not $840.7M — a 16.9% margin, not 29.5%.
- **Working capital is a recurring tailwind, not a durable one.** +$144.8M in FY26 and +$169.4M in FY25, driven by deferred revenue growth. If ARR growth slows to 10%, this contribution shrinks and FCF growth decelerates faster than revenue.
- **FCF conversion is fine but not clean:** FCF / non-GAAP net income ≈ 1.4x; FCF / operating income ≈ 3.0x — the gap is SBC and D&A.
- **Revenue concentration:** two distributors are 39% of revenue. Channel dependency is a structural vulnerability.

**Score rationale:** 6.5 rather than 8+ because the headline FCF and earnings both require heavy adjustment, and because the growth trajectory is decelerating at the exact moment the thesis needed it to accelerate.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Normalized earnings power (the honest base):** pre-tax income $327.1M taxed at 21% ≈ **$258M** → **~49x** at $12.5B market cap. On non-GAAP EPS (~$2.04 for FY26; Q1 FY27 actual $0.73 vs. $0.61 consensus), the stock is **22.7x trailing and ~19x forward** (~$2.45 FY27E). GAAP-basis, it is not cheap. Cash-flow-basis, it is.

| Multiple | At $46.30 |
|---|---|
| EV / FY26 FCF ($840.7M) | **13.8x** |
| EV / FY27E FCF ($900M guide midpoint) | **12.9x** |
| EV / FY26 FCF **ex-SBC** ($483M) | **24.0x** |
| EV / FY27E FCF **ex-SBC** (~$525M) | **22.1x** |
| P / FCF (FY26) | 14.9x → **6.7% yield** |
| FCF yield ex-SBC | 3.9% |
| P / non-GAAP EPS (FY27E) | ~19x |
| Net cash / market cap | ~7% |

**Intrinsic value (my own DCF — the provided tool returned a corrupted result, WACC mis-scaled to 900%, so I modeled it manually):**

*Base case:* $525M year-one owner earnings (FCF less SBC), 12% growth for five years fading to 3% terminal, 9.5% WACC, plus ~$0.9B net cash → **≈ $55/share**.
*Bull case:* 15% growth sustained five years, SBC declining to ~10% of revenue as revenue scales, 9.0% WACC → **≈ $72/share**.
*Bear case:* growth fades to 6% immediately (VMware migration stalls, hardware cost inflation persists), 10.5% WACC → **≈ $35/share**.

The unadjusted "company FCF" DCF (treating SBC as free) yields ~$78 — but that is the wrong way to value a business whose compensation is 12.5% of revenue and whose share count *rose* despite $483M of buybacks. **The SBC-adjusted figure is the economically correct one.**

**Cross-check against NetApp**, the closest listed infrastructure comparable: NetApp trades at ~23.3x EV/FCF and ~20.3x forward earnings. At NetApp's cash-flow multiple NTNX would be ~$76; at its forward earnings multiple, ~$47. Note that this apparent 45% discount to NetApp's EV/FCF **vanishes** once NTNX's SBC is treated as a cost — 22.1x vs. 23.3x. The two companies are roughly equally priced on economic cash flow. (The tool's "peer" set returned Apple, Microsoft and NVIDIA — useless as comparables; I have used NetApp and my own model instead.)

**What the price implies:** at $46.30 the market is capitalizing roughly **12% FCF growth fading to 3%** — modestly below the 15% ARR growth actually being delivered. That is not an aggressive assumption. The stock is not expensive. It is also not cheap.

**Margin of safety at $46.30: ~16% below base-case intrinsic value.** Buffett's threshold is 25–30%. **This does not clear the bar.**

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Rating: Adequate, with a governance question mark.**

- **Capital allocation — mixed.** FY26 FCF of $840.7M was spent as follows: $483.5M buybacks (9.4M shares at a $51.24 average — above today's price, so value-destructive in hindsight), $195.5M of taxes on net share settlement of employee awards, and **zero dividends**. That is 81% of FCF, and yet **Class A shares outstanding still rose from 269.0M to 270.8M** because SBC issuance and 4.1M new shares sold to AMD at $36.26 outran the repurchase. The buyback is running to stand still. A buyback that fails to reduce the share count is not a return of capital — it is a compensation expense paid in cash.
- **Operating discipline — good.** Management has delivered gross margin from 79.7% to 86.8% and swung operating margin from −29% to +9.8% while growing revenue 16% annually. FY27 FCF guidance of $850–950M on $3.18–3.23B revenue implies ~29% FCF margin — held, not expanded. Credible.
- **Transparency — genuinely above average.** The 10-K openly discloses that the VMware opportunity may not materialize within expected periods, that hardware component constraints began in Q2 FY26 and extended lead times, and that two distributors are 39% of revenue. Honest disclosure is worth a lot.
- **Succession risk — the live issue.** CEO Rajiv Ramaswami announced he will step down once a successor is found, no later than April 30, 2027, then serve as a senior advisor. This is an orderly, board-managed transition, not a rupture — but it arrives at the exact moment the strategic question (how aggressively to pursue VMware displacement versus defending the base) needs answering. A new CEO may reset strategy, guidance, or capital allocation. **Insider ownership and detailed compensation data were not available to me — an honest gap in this assessment.**
- **M&A discipline — clean.** No transformational or empire-building acquisitions. Goodwill and intangibles are only $187M against $5.07B of assets. The AMD share sale is a sensible strategic alignment rather than a dilution event for its own sake.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

**Not a buy at $46.30; a compelling buy below ~$40.**

The case for owning it: an 86.8% gross margin, 89%-recurring software business with 32,000 customers, $0.9B of net cash, FCF of $840M growing ~15%, a 6.7% FCF yield, an EV/FCF of 12.9x on forward guidance, and a genuine multi-year secular catalyst in VMware displacement. The market has de-rated the stock 34% on a CEO transition and a supply-chain-driven guidance cut — neither of which is a permanent impairment of the franchise.

The case against paying today's price:
1. **Growth is decelerating in the harvest year** (18% → 12.4% → 11–13% guided), which suggests the moat is not converting VMware's pain into Nutanix's gain as fast as the bulls assumed. The 10-K says so explicitly.
2. **ROIC of ~10% is only now reaching cost of capital.** The Buffett hallmark — consistently earning above WACC — is not yet established. Narrow moat, not wide.
3. **Economic FCF is $483M, not $840M**, once SBC is treated as the real compensation cost it is. At 22x economic FCF for a 12–15% grower, the stock is fairly valued, not discounted.
4. **The buyback is not reducing the share count**, so per-share value is not compounding the way headline FCF suggests.
5. **Hardware component inflation** (memory/server pricing) is outside management's control and directly delays software deployments — a structural headwind for a software-only model.

**Action:** Hold existing positions. Do not add. Revisit at **≤$40** (a 27% discount to my $55 base intrinsic value, which would also put the stock at ~19x economic FCF and ~5.5x EV/revenue). Trim above **$68–72**, where the market would again be paying for a VMware migration that the filing says will be slow. Analyst price targets of $62–75 (BofA cut to Neutral/$62; Piper $67; Rosenblatt $70; Needham $75) imply ~35–60% upside, but analysts are systematically optimistic about the timing of this specific catalyst — the same optimism that produced a 34% drawdown.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **The VMware migration never arrives in force.** Broadcom retains customers through multi-year commitments and aggressive repricing; customers migrate "only a portion of their workloads" or defer to refresh cycles. If revenue growth settles at 8–10% rather than 12–15%, my base case falls to ~$40 and the bear case to ~$28.
2. **Hardware supply and pricing.** Memory and server component inflation extends lead times and raises appliance costs, delaying software deployment. Management has guided a full year of these headwinds. If they persist beyond FY27, the 11–13% revenue guide is at risk.
3. **CEO transition.** A new CEO may reset strategy, re-base guidance, or change capital allocation. The search concludes by April 30, 2027. Strategic drift during a transition is a real risk in a market where the competitive window is now.
4. **Competitive intensity.** Broadcom is "continuing to compete aggressively" by Nutanix's own admission; Microsoft (Azure Stack HCI, Hyper-V) and public cloud hyperscalers absorb workloads; Dell, HPE and VMware sell integrated stacks. Nutanix is the challenger with 32,000 customers against an incumbent with hundreds of thousands.
5. **Channel concentration.** Two distributors = 39% of revenue. Loss or de-prioritization by either is a material revenue event.
6. **Compensation-driven dilution.** SBC at 12.5% of revenue, with a buyback that fails to offset it. If SBC stays at 12–13% of revenue rather than declining, economic FCF per share grows materially slower than headline FCF.
7. **Convertible debt.** $500M due 2027 at 0.25% — well covered by $2.36B of cash, but a refinancing event within twelve months.
8. **Multiple compression.** As a mid-cap infrastructure software name with a 0.64 beta, NTNX is exposed to any de-rating of software multiples; the Nov 25 move (−15% in one session) shows how quickly sentiment can reprice it.

---

**Bottom line:** Nutanix is a genuinely improved business — better margins, real FCF, a repaired balance sheet, and honest disclosure — but it is a *narrow*-moat challenger earning roughly its cost of capital, growing 12–13%, whose reported earnings and FCF both require material adjustment. At $46.30 it trades at a modest 16% discount to base-case intrinsic value of ~$55. That is a fair price. **A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy.** Hold, and wait for Mr. Market to offer the 25–30% discount that makes the risk worth taking.
