**Key observation — risk is migrating, not shrinking.** The US share has fallen from 55.8% (FY23) to 53.6% (FY26), and the two fastest-growing theaters are **EMEA (+21.3%)** and **Other Americas (+47.1%)** — i.e., the highest-growth incremental revenue is coming from the *higher*-risk jurisdictions. EMEA alone contributed **46% of the total dollar revenue increase** in FY2026. Meanwhile the lowest-risk geography (US) grew slowest at +8.6%. This is a slow, structural drift up the risk curve.

Revenue mix is **95% subscription/term-based licenses** ($2.71B) + 5% professional services — recurring revenue is generally sticky, which mitigates abrupt geopolitical revenue loss, but the underlying deployments run on **hardware sourced through OEM partners**, which is where trade policy bites.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk #1 — US tariff / trade policy on server hardware supply chains
**Probability: High · Impact: Medium · Timing: Ongoing**

Nutanix is a software company that sells through OEM appliance partners (Dell, Lenovo, HPE, Cisco, Fujitsu and its own NX line). Management explicitly flagged on the Q3 FY2026 call that *"supply chain challenges continue to drive higher prices and generally longer lead times for server hardware,"* and the 10-K risk factors warn that *"evolving US trade policy and tariff regimes (and reciprocal actions) could disrupt the global hardware supply chains and cross-border commerce"* that the NX appliance line depends on. With 46% of revenue outside the US and reciprocal tariff regimes active in 2026, this is the single most tangible geopolitical cost line. Impact is margin/pricing rather than existential — Nutanix has repeatedly demonstrated pricing power — but it pressures the hardware-attached portion of the model and can lengthen sales cycles.

### Risk #2 — US–China tech decoupling & export controls
**Probability: Medium · Impact: Low-Medium**

Direct China revenue is not disclosed as material, so direct exposure is limited. The transmission channel is **indirect**: (a) component and server supply via Chinese-manufactured hardware, and (b) the **Lenovo OEM relationship**, a US-listed but China-rooted partner. Escalating entity-list or end-use restrictions could constrain partner supply or force customer-segment exclusions. APAC (14.6% of revenue) includes China/HK alongside the much larger Japan, India and ANZ markets — the non-China APAC book is the bulk of the exposure. This is a tail risk, not a base case.

### Risk #3 — EMEA/Middle East volatility and FX translation
**Probability: Medium-High · Impact: Medium**

EMEA is 29.1% of revenue and the largest growth contributor, spanning the UK, Eurozone, **and the Middle East & Africa** (the company's own description cites Middle East and Africa operations — implying Gulf states, Israel, Turkey, South Africa). That concentration brings: EUR/GBP/TRY/ZAR translation and repatriation friction, sanctions/export-control screening obligations on Gulf and Turkish end-customers, and the risk of a single large sovereign or telco deal slipping a quarter. A 10% adverse EUR/USD move on the EMEA book is roughly a 2.9% revenue headwind — material but manageable. **This is where I'd focus diligence on customer concentration by country**, which the 10-K does not break out.

*Honest gap: the 10-K discloses only four regions. Country-level revenue (Japan, UK, Germany, Saudi, India, Israel) and any sanctioned-country exposure is not publicly disaggregated, so the table above is regional, not country-level.*

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**A. Nutanix v. Tessell — IP theft & patent litigation (active, escalating)**
- March 2024: Nutanix filed a federal IP-misappropriation and patent infringement suit in N.D. Cal. (**3:24-cv-01729**) against Tessell, Inc., founded by three former Nutanix employees (Bala Kuchibhotla, Kamal Khanuja, Bakul Banthia) who developed the Era database product. Nutanix publicly characterized the conduct as *"too nefarious to ignore."*
- The district court granted a motion to compel and **stayed the case** pending related proceedings (N.D. Cal. Doc. 63, 2025).
- Tessell filed **inter partes reviews** at the PTAB challenging Nutanix patents on which Tessell's own founders are named inventors. The PTAB issued **discretionary denial** invoking **assignor estoppel**; Tessell sought Federal Circuit mandamus, which was **denied in February 2026** (*In re Tessell*, No. 26-117) — a favorable outcome for Nutanix.
- **New front (2026):** *Tessell, Inc. et al v. Nutanix, Inc. et al* (**3:26-cv-02306**, N.D. Cal., filed **March 17, 2026**) — docketed as **assault/libel/slander**, i.e., a defamation-flavored counter-action likely tied to Nutanix's public statements about the alleged IP theft.
- **Assessment:** Nutanix is winning on the substantive IP merits so far, but the dispute is widening rather than resolving. Financial exposure is not quantifiable from public filings; the reputational and management-attention cost is real. **Low probability of material monetary loss; medium probability of continued headline noise.**

**B. Securities litigation / disclosure scrutiny (overhang)**
- On **Nov 25, 2025**, Nutanix reported Q1 FY2026 and **lowered its FY2026 revenue outlook**, prompting the stock to fall from a $58.77 close on Nov 25 to materially lower on Nov 26, 2025.
- **Rosen, Bragar Eagel & Squire, Kaplan Fox, Kirby McInerney, Schall, and Levi & Korsinsky** all announced shareholder investigations, with Rosen referencing a **filed securities class action**. The central allegation is **incomplete or misleading disclosure regarding a shift of revenue into later periods** — i.e., revenue-timing transparency, not accounting fraud.
- **Important distinction:** These are **plaintiff-firm investigations and private class actions**. I found **no evidence of a formal SEC or DOJ investigation, subpoena, or Wells notice** disclosed by Nutanix in its FY2026 10-K. Do not conflate the two in the memo.
- **Compounding factor:** On **Nov 24, 2026**, Nutanix announced **CEO Rajiv Ramaswami is stepping down** while simultaneously guiding conservatively; the stock fell ~15–19% to $46.30 on Nov 25, 2026 — its worst day in over a year. A CEO transition announced alongside a guidance reset is a **classic event-driven litigation trigger**, raising the probability of an expanded or newly filed class action covering the FY2027 class period.

**C. Regulatory environment — no adverse posture**
- No disclosed FCPA, OFAC, BIS, or AML enforcement actions. Nutanix's Code of Business Conduct explicitly requires compliance with **US export controls and sanctions laws** and mandates personnel *"monitor continued compliance with the Foreign Corrupt Practices Act."* Standard controls; no red flags surfaced in public filings or news.
- Product-level regulation is a secondary but rising channel: as Nutanix repositions toward "agentic AI" infrastructure, **EU AI Act** obligations and **NIS2** cybersecurity requirements become relevant for EMEA customers (29.1% of revenue), and **GDPR** applies to the data its platform stores. These are compliance-cost and product-feature requirements, not revenue-threatening.
- Antitrust tailwind, not risk: the Broadcom/VMware disruption continues to drive competitive displacement in Nutanix's favor across US and EMEA — a rare case where a competitor's regulatory problem is Nutanix's growth engine. Note the flip side: this tailwind is finite and is precisely what the market is now worried is fading (see the "slow VMware migration" theme in FY2026 10-K coverage).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

**None material.** Specifically checked and cleared:
- ❌ No FCPA/DOJ/SEC enforcement, no disclosed government investigation
- ❌ No OFAC/sanctions violations or Russia-exposure wind-down disclosed
- ❌ No export-control (BIS/entity list) violations
- ❌ No data-breach or GDPR enforcement actions disclosed

**Watch items (not flags):**
1. **Revenue-recognition disclosure quality** — the core allegation in the securities matters. Even if legally defensible, it invites ongoing plaintiff scrutiny and audit-committee attention. Escalation risk is highest around the CEO transition.
2. **Distributor concentration** — the **top two distribution partners accounted for ~39% of FY2026 revenue**. This is a channel/partner-governance risk with an anti-bribery dimension (third-party/intermediary liability under FCPA) that warrants diligence given EMEA and Middle East sales through indirect channels.
3. **Hardware OEM partner concentration** — export-control and tariff liability flows through partners Nutanix does not control.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Nutanix's jurisdictional footprint is fundamentally benign — 54% US, 29% allied EMEA, 15% APAC — with **no sanctions-exposed revenue, no hostile-jurisdiction operations, and no government enforcement exposure**. It is not a China-supply-chain story, not a Russia story, not an FCPA story. That alone keeps it out of Medium-High territory.

What pushes it to **Medium** rather than Low:
1. **Legal overhang is live and growing**, not legacy — an active and *widening* IP/defamation war with Tessell, plus shareholder litigation tied to revenue-timing disclosure that has a fresh trigger in the Nov 2026 CEO transition.
2. **Trade-policy cost inflation** on hardware is already visible in management commentary, and reciprocal tariffs are an ongoing 2026 condition.
3. **Growth is concentrating in higher-risk theaters** (EMEA +21%, LatAm +47%) even as the safe US base decelerates — the risk mix is deteriorating slowly.

**Bottom line for the investment memo:** Geopolitical and legal risk is **not** a thesis-breaker for NTNX and should not drive position sizing. The real risk here is **governance and disclosure credibility during a CEO transition** — and that is a legal/regulatory risk that could get worse before it gets better, particularly if a consolidated class action is filed covering the November 2026 guidance event. Rating would move to Medium-High on any of: a disclosed SEC subpoena, an adverse Tessell ruling with damages, or a formal consolidated class action naming the CEO transition.
