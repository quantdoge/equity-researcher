**Supply-chain geography matters more than revenue geography.** HP is one of the most China-assembly-dependent US hardware names: historically ~70–80% of notebook assembly in China, with plans (since 2019) to move up to 30% of notebook output out of China (Reuters). Critically, in **Oct-2026 HP, Asus and Acer began shifting *some* production back to China** because AI-driven memory/storage inflation made Chinese ODM clusters the cheapest option (Semafor, Nikkei Asia, TechSpot). Memory has gone from ~15% to ~40% of a mainstream PC bill of materials in a year (24/7 Wall St, 25-Nov-2026). **HP is therefore re-concentrating tariff-exposed manufacturing into the highest-tariff jurisdiction at exactly the wrong moment.**

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Tariff escalation on China/Vietnam/India (Probability: High | Impact: High)**
The White House proposed new Section 301 duties of up to **20% on China plus rates on 13 other partners, explicitly naming Vietnam and India among the steepest** (Proactive Investors, CNBC, 25–26-Nov-2026); for some Chinese goods these would stack on ~25% from the first term. Effective US tariffs on Chinese imports are running ~33% in 2026 (MS Advisory). HP already books tariff effects: FQ3'26 EPS included an **$0.11 tariff refund benefit** (TradingKey Q3'26 call), and management guided FQ4 revenue +9.3% to $16.0B while warning component costs and soft PC demand pressure FY27 (MarketBeat, Zacks, 24-Nov-2026). HP said in Feb-2026 it did not expect immediate impact from new tariffs and expects double-digit PC unit decline in 2026 (Reuters) — i.e., it is absorbing cost rather than fully passing it through. Every dollar of margin at HP (~5–6% operating) is highly levered to landed-cost changes; a 20% incremental duty on China-origin PCs is a multi-hundred-million-dollar gross exposure.

**Risk 2 — Component/memory shock + Taiwan Strait concentration (Probability: Medium | Impact: High)**
The DRAM/NAND squeeze is a structural, geopolitically-driven shock (AI datacenter demand diverting supply). HP is reportedly even considering Chinese memory suppliers to secure supply (TonerNews, 2026) — which would create a direct conflict with prospective US sourcing restrictions/entity-list rules. A Taiwan Strait disruption hits both silicon supply and ODM logistics; HP carries limited ability to dual-source advanced memory.

**Risk 3 — China market access & retaliation (Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium)**
China is ~10% of revenue and HP's largest single non-US market. Beijing has previously ordered government agencies and state firms to replace foreign PCs (2022 directive), a structural headwind that continues to favour Lenovo. A US-China deal collapse (talks between Trump and Xi are live, per Bloomberg 26-Nov-2026) raises the risk of Chinese regulatory action (SAMR/cybersecurity review) or rare-earth/component export controls against US hardware brands.

*Honorable mention:* EMEA exposure to a possible EU–US tariff round (EU among the 14 partners named) and FX translation on ~34% of revenue.

**Important correction for the memo:** the **Autonomy / Mike Lynch litigation is Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE), not HP Inc.** The March-2026 UK ruling (£920M / ~$1.24B awarded to HPE, Lynch estate refused permission to appeal — Reuters, Guardian, FT, 24-Mar-2026) must **not** be attributed to HPQ. Similarly, several "HPQ" news hits (ENDURA+ MiniMax power systems) are **HPQ Silicon**, an unrelated issuer — a data-hygiene trap in any HPQ screen.

**Government investigations:** I found **no active DOJ, SEC, FTC or EU enforcement action** disclosed against HP Inc. in the sources reviewed. Note the gap: direct SEC 10-K retrieval was blocked (403) and the Q4-CDN PDF was machine-unreadable, so I could not read the FY2025 "Legal Proceedings" footnote in primary form. The 10-Q pull returned only an exhibit (director RSU agreement).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **Russia/Belarus diversion — monitor, not alarm.** HP requires customers in high-diversion-risk countries to certify products will not be re-exported to Russia/Belarus (Business & Human Rights Resource Centre); Russia sanctions were guided at ~$0.03 EPS in 2022. No enforcement action against HP Inc. found. However, printers/PC components are recurring "common high-priority items" in Russia evasion cases (Global Trade & Sanctions Law, 2025–26), and HP's heavy distributor/ODM channel model raises third-party diversion risk.
- **Export controls / FCPA:** standard risk-factor disclosure across filings (2023 10-K cites FCPA, export control and sanctions compliance). No disclosed violations.
- **Emerging flag:** reported willingness to source Chinese memory (TonerNews) creates prospective exposure to US entity-list/sourcing rules.
- **Governance:** interim CEO Bruce Broussard (CEO transition mid-year) plus a thin-margin, cost-cut-driven plan — historically a combination that raises execution/disclosure risk.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM–HIGH**

Driven by (a) a manufacturing base that is being pulled *back into* China while US tariff policy escalates against China/Vietnam/India, (b) an AI-driven memory cost shock it cannot price away, and (c) a mid-single-digit-margin P&L with almost no buffer. Legal risk is **Low–Medium**: the securities and ink-lock matters have largely resolved in HP's favour or at modest cost, no government investigation is disclosed, and the headline-grabbing Autonomy liability sits with HPE, not HPQ.

**Gaps/limitations:** country-level revenue splits (China ~10%, Germany ~7%) are third-party estimates, not filing-verified; FY2025 10-K "Legal Proceedings" and tariff-risk-factor language could not be read directly (SEC 403 / PDF parse failure); the specific jurisdiction of the reported consumer-protection fines is unconfirmed.
