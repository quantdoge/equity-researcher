## HPQ — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**HP Inc. | Technology / Computer Hardware | US | Mkt cap ~$28.1B | 55,000 employees | FY ends Oct 31**
*Assessment date: late Nov 2026. Note: FY2026 10-K and 2026 proxy text were not retrievable via filing tool (returned "no filing found"); governance and legal detail below is sourced from SEC Form 4/8-K metadata, company releases, and press coverage. Gaps flagged.*

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE** (with meaningful caveats)

**Positives**
- **Independent chair tradition preserved.** Chip Bergh (director since 2015, Chair since July 2017, ex-CEO Levi Strauss) has chaired as a non-executive. 2026 proxy added **four independent directors**; board now 12 members — genuine refresh.
- **CEO succession was run through a proper process.** After Lores' abrupt exit, the board formed a **CEO Search Committee** and ran a ~9-month external search, landing **Emma Walmsley** (ex-GSK CEO), effective **Jan 4, 2027**. No insider-only coronation; no nepotism.
- **No dual-class structure, no controlling shareholder** — one share, one vote.

**Caveats / concerns**
- **Abrupt, unexplained CEO exit.** Enrique Lores stepped down as President, CEO and board member **effective end of day Feb 2, 2026**, "to pursue another professional opportunity" — he became CEO of PayPal. A CEO leaving mid-fiscal-year to a peer company, with no transition period, is a genuine signal. The company's own framing was thin.
- **Chair will be the just-departed CEO.** Bruce Broussard, interim CEO from Feb 3, 2026, becomes **Board Chair the same day Walmsley starts (Jan 4, 2027)**. Legal separation of chair/CEO is maintained, but the chair is the person who ran the company for 11 months — this weakens the counterweight to a first-time-in-industry CEO. Walmsley is also a **three-industry, two-time CEO** with no PC/printing background; her GSK tenure was contested by activists.
- **An 11-month interim CEO is a long interim.** Strategic decisions (the 4,000–6,000 job restructuring, memory-cost pricing) were made by a caretaker. Accountability for those choices is diffuse.
- **Compensation alignment unverified** — DEF 14A text unavailable; cannot confirm pay-for-performance structure or whether Lores' exit package was accelerated.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Memory/DRAM cost shock — the single largest earnings risk.** Memory and storage went from **15–18% of PC bill-of-materials to ~40% within one year** (CFO Parkhill). HP cannot fully pass this through in a commodity PC market; Q4 FY2026 revenue rose 9.3% to $16B but guidance was set "prudently" and shares fell. HP itself guides the **calendar 2027 PC market to shrink high-single to low-double digits**. AI-PC premium mix is the offset strategy, and it is unproven at scale.
- **Supply-chain and tariff concentration.** ~90% of China manufacturing for North America is being relocated — a multi-year, capital-intensive shift executed *during* a margin crisis. Component sourcing (memory, panels) is concentrated in a handful of Asian suppliers with no pricing power for HP.
- **Workforce restructuring execution + cyber exposure.** 4,000–6,000 jobs (~10% of workforce) cut by FY2028, ~$650M charges ($250M in FY2026) for ~$1B savings — institutional knowledge loss while simultaneously hiring for AI. Separately, **Poly (an HP subsidiary) was allegedly breached by the Everest ransomware gang, with 90GB of data claimed stolen from legacy Polycom systems**, and **CVE-2026-91097** is an RCE in HP Linux Imaging and Printing. HP is a hardware vendor whose brand promise is device security; a confirmed subsidiary breach plus an unpatched print-stack RCE is a material continuity and liability exposure.
- **Key-person risk:** interim CEO running the company 11 months, new CEO with no sector experience arriving Jan 2027, CFO Parkhill relatively new. Thin bench at the top during the hardest operating environment in years.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **ELEVATED**

- **Consumer boycott pressure on the printer ecosystem is real and durable.** "HP boycott" content circulates persistently on Reddit and YouTube over ink lock-in, cartridge DRM and Instant Ink subscription billing. A **viral May 2026 social media incident** renewed criticism of subscription printing after a user's complaint. This is a slow-burn brand tax on the highest-margin segment.
- **The 10% layoff announcement is being framed as "AI replaces workers."** Headlines ran "AI Is Coming for Even More Jobs" and "HP to cut 10% of its global workforce in AI push." Employer-brand damage in a tight technical talent market, and a ready-made ESG/social controversy narrative for proxy advisers.
- **Mitigants:** the Robinson ink case was dismissed with prejudice (Sept 2026); the securities matter is settled and closed; no product-safety recall or environmental disaster identified; no confirmed data-privacy regulatory fine. HP's 2021 BDS statement is old and not an active controversy.
- **Not assessed:** no ESG controversy score or social-sentiment index was available from the data feeds — a genuine gap.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not *Critical* — there is no live enforcement action, no going-concern question, no evidence of accounting fraud, and the two largest legal overhangs are resolved or dismissed. But three independent high-severity risks stack on top of each other: a **CEO transition inside a CEO transition**, a **40%-of-BOM memory cost shock in a shrinking market**, and a **10% workforce reduction being executed simultaneously**. Governance is adequate but the chair will be the interim CEO, and insiders have sold exclusively for twelve months. The compounding, not any single item, drives the rating.

---

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Abrupt CEO departure with no transition and no substantive explanation** (Lores → PayPal, effective Feb 2, 2026), followed by an 11-month interim CEO. Ask management directly what the board's concerns were and whether Lores' exit was voluntary in substance.
2. **Chair and CEO roles collapsing into the same person's recent orbit** — Broussard becomes Chair on Jan 4, 2027, the day Walmsley starts. Weakens board challenge of a first-time-in-industry CEO during a restructuring.
3. **Zero insider purchases across 12 months, including during the Feb 2026 leadership shock.** Selling by both segment presidents and the CCO into strength. Confirm 10b5-1 status and plan adoption dates for McQuarrie's five sales.
4. **$39M securities settlement (final approval Feb 13, 2026) over printing-supplies inventory and channel disclosures** — a prior disclosure-control failure in the highest-margin segment. Verify whether the same channel-inventory practices persist; the printing segment is now also under consumer-litigation and boycott pressure.
5. **Poly/Everest ransomware breach (90GB claimed) and CVE-2026-91097 HPLIP RCE** — confirm whether the breach is verified, whether customer data was involved, and whether notification obligations (GDPR/CCPA/state breach laws) have been triggered. Material unquantified liability.
6. **Memory cost at ~40% of PC BOM with a double-digit PC market decline guided for 2027** — confirm hedging/contract coverage and whether the AI-PC premium mix can actually offset. This is the risk most likely to force a guidance reset.
