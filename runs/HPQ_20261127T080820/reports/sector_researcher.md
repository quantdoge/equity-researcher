## SECTOR BRIEF — HP Inc. (HPQ)
**Sector:** Technology / Computer Hardware (PCs, peripherals, printers & supplies)

### 1. Sector Overview

The PC hardware sector is in a sharp **volume downturn driven by supply-side cost inflation**, not demand collapse. Memory is the swing factor: DRAM/NAND prices rose roughly 130% during 2026 (JPMorgan estimates DRAM +400% from start-2024 to end-2026; 16GB DDR5 moved from ~$8.70 in Oct-2025 to ~$48 by Sep-2026) as AI datacenter demand crowds out conventional memory supply. Gartner recorded Q3-2026 worldwide PC shipments of 55.4M units, **−22.4% YoY**, and forecasts a **−10.4%** full-year 2026 unit decline (IDC: −11.3%). Critically, **revenue is holding up while units fall** — IDC expects PC revenue +1.6% in 2026 — because component cost pass-through is inflating ASPs (PC prices +17% per Gartner). Profit is being redistributed up the value chain from OEMs to memory suppliers. Structurally this remains a mature, moderately concentrated market (Lenovo ~24%, HP ~20%, Dell ~15%; CR3 ≈ 60%, HHI roughly ~1,600), with low capital intensity for assembly, high working-capital intensity, brutal component-price cyclicality, and thin single-digit operating margins as the norm.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **AI PC mix shift is real and fast.** AI-capable PCs reached ~**60% of HP's Q4 FY26 shipments** and Gartner expects AI PCs at **54.7% of 2026 global shipments** (up from ~31% in 2025). Richer silicon/content per unit lifts ASP and partially offsets unit declines — this is the primary margin-recovery lever HP is leaning on.
- **Revenue/ASP inflation is masking unit weakness.** Cost pass-through means nominal revenue growth is achievable even in a −10% to −11% unit market (IDC: revenue +1.6%, Gartner: ASPs +17%). OEMs with procurement scale and commercial contract pricing power (HP, Dell, Lenovo) can defend revenue lines far better than whitebox/no-name assemblers, which should accelerate consolidation.
- **Commercial refresh and Windows-10 end-of-support hangover demand.** Enterprise/commercial demand for local AI PCs is explicitly cited as offsetting consumer weakness in HP's Q4; commercial mix is more stable and less price-elastic than consumer, supporting the FY27 "quarterly PC margin recovery" plan HP laid out.

---

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Memory cost inflation is the dominant margin destroyer.** Conventional DRAM contract prices still rising 10–15% QoQ and NAND 15–20% in 4Q26 (TrendForce), with supply partners warning of 10–20% *per month* increases. HP's gross margin fell from 22.1% (FY24) → 20.0% (FY25) → ~**19.2%** (FY26E) and operating margin from 7.1% → 6.6% → ~**5.1%** — a ~200bp peak-to-trough compression on a business with only ~6% starting margins.
- **Consumer PC demand is weak and being actively destroyed by price.** A −22.4% quarterly unit decline is partly demand destruction as ASPs rise; the budget segment is the most exposed. Printer supplies — HP's historical profit engine — face structural (not cyclical) erosion as page volumes decline, so it cannot fund the PC margin gap indefinitely.
- **Tariffs, policy uncertainty and cash-flow quality.** Shrinking free cash flow and a leadership transition (new CEO) coincide with a period requiring heavy working-capital investment to secure memory allocation. Buyback capacity — HP returned >$2.3B in FY26 — is now in tension with inventory/prepayment needs. Analysts are already flagging the FY27 beat as "low quality."

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **AVERAGE (defensible, structurally margin-constrained)**

**Rationale.** HPQ holds a genuine #2 global PC position (~20%) with the sector's best commercial channel and installed base, and it is demonstrably **outgrowing the underlying unit market** — Q4 FY26 revenue +9.3% against industry units −22.4% is real relative strength, and AI PCs at ~60% of shipments is ahead of the 54.7% industry average. But the franchise is caught in a pincer: it lacks Apple's vertical integration and 48% gross margin to absorb memory inflation, lacks Dell's AI-server growth engine to diversify the P&L, and its printer-supplies annuity — historically the margin ballast — is in structural decline as page volumes fall. Result: HPQ is a **price-taker on input costs and a share-defender on output prices**. FY26's ~19% gross margin is near a decade low, and the FY27 recovery plan explicitly depends on cost cuts plus premium mix rather than any structural pricing power. ROIC and FCF are adequate but deteriorating, and the equity has re-rated on guidance beats of a few cents — a low bar. Position: average, with an improving competitive trajectory if AI-PC mix holds and memory normalizes.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **UNATTRACTIVE (cyclically, near trough)**

Structurally, PC hardware is a mature, low-margin, high-working-capital industry with limited pricing power and a commoditising core — this alone justifies a below-market rating. Tactically, the sector is in the **worst phase of a cost-driven downcycle**: unit volumes −10% to −22%, input costs up 130%+, and profit accruing to upstream memory (MU, SK hynix) rather than OEMs. The 2026 "revenue growth" is largely price inflation and should not be mistaken for demand. Sector economics improve only when memory supply normalises (a 2027 event at the earliest, given AI datacenter demand) or when demand destruction clears the ASP overhang. **Bottom line: an UNATTRACTIVE sector where the only justifiable holdings are share gainers with a non-PC profit engine — HPQ is a share gainer without one, which is why it screens as a value/income trade rather than a compounder.**
