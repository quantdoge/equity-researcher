## ESG ANALYSIS: HP INC. (NYSE: HPQ)
**Sector:** Technology — Computer Hardware | **Market cap:** ~$28.1B | **Employees:** ~55,000 | **FY25 revenue:** $55.3B | **FY end:** Oct 31

> **Data note:** The SEC filing tools returned no 10-K/DEF 14A for HPQ in this environment (and direct SEC.gov access returned HTTP 403), so filing-level detail below is sourced from HP's own disclosures, press releases, and third-party reporting. Peer carbon intensity benchmarking was not obtainable — flagged as a gap rather than estimated.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Strengths**
- **SBTi-validated net zero by 2040** — 10 years ahead of sector norm; near-term target: −50% absolute Scope 1+2+3 by FY2030 vs FY2019 base.
- **Total footprint 16.51 Mt CO2e (FY25)**, down ~28% vs FY2019 — on track against the FY2030 target.
- **Scope 1+2: 120,500 tCO2e** across 179 sites in 54 countries (−5.6% YoY); −69% vs 2015.
- **Circularity is genuinely differentiated:** 1.2M+ tons of hardware/supplies/accessories recycled since 2016; >30% post-consumer recycled plastic in PS and print portfolio; 47% of product and packaging materials from recycled/reused/renewable sources.
- **EcoVadis Platinum for the 16th consecutive year** (top 1% of ~100,000 companies assessed).

**Weaknesses / watch items**
- **Carbon intensity ≈ 298 tCO2e per $1M revenue** (Scope 1+2+3), dominated by Scope 3 — high absolute reliance on supplier decarbonisation it does not control. Supplier capability-building is the binding constraint, not HP's own execution.
- **Renewable electricity at only ~62%** of global operations (2024 procurement, ~298,000 MWh). HP's earlier 100%-renewables ambition has effectively been pushed to 2040 — a goal that was softened rather than met. This is the clearest gap between HP's stated ambition and delivery.
- **Reversal in manufacturing geography:** HP accelerated exit from China (2025) under tariff pressure, then in Oct 2026 shifted *some* production back to China as memory/component costs spiked. Repeated re-routing raises supplier-audit and emissions-accounting volatility.
- **No CDP Climate disclosure** per KnowESG tracking — a notable transparency gap for a company with SBTi-validated targets, and a friction point with ESG-screened institutional holders.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

| Issue | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Workforce restructuring** | **The single largest social risk.** Nov 2025 "Fiscal 2026 Plan": **4,000–6,000 job cuts (~10% of workforce) by FY2028**, ~$650M restructuring charges (~$250M in FY26). Follows earlier multi-year reductions. Elevated attrition, morale, severance-litigation and execution risk — and it is happening *simultaneously* with an interim CEO and an AI-driven operating-model overhaul. |
| **Supply-chain labour** | High structural exposure (China, Vietnam, Thailand, India, Mexico). Mitigants: Supplier Code of Conduct updated Jan 2025 to RBA Code v8.0; supplier scorecards; audit program reaching ~95,000 workers (2024). Residual risk: UFLPA entity-list and EU Forced Labour Regulation (applies Dec 2027) create strict-liability-style import detention risk. |
| **Diversity & inclusion** | HP has maintained its DEI commitments and reporting amid the 2025 US corporate rollback wave — a relative positive, but it is swimming against political current and faces shareholder pressure from both directions. |
| **Social licence / digital equity** | 82M people reached through social impact programs; target 150M by 2030 (>halfway). Low-risk, reputationally additive. |
| **Product safety & consumer protection** | **Weakest social sub-factor.** The Dynamic Security firmware/ink-lock episode generated sustained consumer backlash and litigation. |

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **5.5 / 10**

**Positives**
- **Independent Chairman** (Chip Bergh, ex-Levi Strauss CEO) separate from CEO — no duality.
- **Single-class share structure**; no dual-class supervoting stock, no poison pill identified. Shareholder rights are structurally sound.
- **12 directors**, average board tenure ~5.8 years — reasonable balance of continuity and refresh.
- **Say-on-pay passed with ~92% support** (602.7M for / 52.1M against / 2.7M abstentions) — solid but not resounding; ~8% dissent is a yellow flag on pay design.
- **E&Y** auditor ratification; conventional, uncontested.

**Negatives — and why the score is only mid-range**
1. **Abrupt CEO exit, Feb 3 2026.** Enrique Lores departed with immediate effect after 7 years as CEO (36 years at HP) to become CEO of PayPal. The "pursue another professional opportunity" framing is a *poaching*, not a misconduct event — an important distinction that keeps this out of scandal territory. But the departure was overnight, with no internal successor ready, and **the company has been run by an interim CEO (Bruce Broussard, a sitting director since 2021) for ~10 months** with the permanent search still outstanding. That is a live succession and key-person risk, and it means strategy (AI PCs, cost program, capital allocation) is being executed by a caretaker.
2. **Interim CEO is a sitting board member** — his independence is suspended while in the role, temporarily thinning board independence.
3. **Dilution ask.** Shareholders approved expanding the 2004 Stock Incentive Plan by **73.6 million shares** — roughly 8% of shares outstanding. Approved, but a large ask at a ~$28B market cap; watch for compensation creep.
4. **Recurring shareholder proposal for a fully independent Chairman**, which the Board recommends against. Signals a persistent minority view that governance could be tighter.
5. **Interim leadership + $650M restructuring + a CEO search** is a combination that historically correlates with weak cost discipline and strategic drift.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

| Jurisdiction / Rule | Exposure | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **EU Right to Repair Directive (2024/1799)** | **HIGH** | Applies from **31 July 2026**; repair priority, spare parts up to 10 years. Directly challenges HP's supplies-led profit pool and replacement-cycle assumptions. |
| **EU Ecodesign / Energy Labelling (printers, computers, displays)** | **HIGH** | Repairability, disassembly, spare-part availability, energy and consumable-waste requirements built into market access. |
| **EU CSRD (as amended by 2025 Omnibus)** | **MEDIUM-HIGH** | HP is a non-EU parent with large EU turnover; in scope on a delayed timeline (FY2028/FY2029 reporting). Requires value-chain (Scope 3) assurance HP does not yet publish at CDP. |
| **California SB 253 / SB 261** | **HIGH (near-term)** | HP is HQ'd in Palo Alto and far exceeds the $1B revenue threshold → mandatory Scope 1/2/3 disclosure and climate risk reporting. *HP currently does not file CDP Climate — this is a concrete compliance gap to close.* |
| **UFLPA (US) + EU Forced Labour Regulation (2024/3015, applies Dec 2027)** | **MEDIUM-HIGH** | Import-detention risk on China/SE Asia-sourced components; no presumption-of-guilt safe harbour. |
| **Conflict minerals (Dodd-Frank §1502) / Form SD** | **MEDIUM** | Routine but recurring cost; smelter-validity disclosure required. |
| **EU Batteries Regulation; WEEE / e-waste take-back** | **MEDIUM** | Producer-responsibility fees scale with volumes. |
| **CBAM** | **LOW** | Hardware not a covered sector; indirect via steel/aluminium in packaging and racks only. |
| **ESG-linked debt covenants** | **None identified** | No sustainability-linked bond or ESG-linked revolver covenant found in available disclosures — a positive: no mechanical cost-of-capital penalty if targets slip. |

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LEADER** (environmental) / **AVERAGE** (governance) → **Composite: LEADER, with a governance discount**

HP is a genuine ESG management leader for its industry: SBTi-validated 2040 net zero, −28% absolute footprint since 2019, EcoVadis Platinum 16 years running, and best-in-class circularity. Third-party risk scoring agrees — Sustainalytics ESG Risk Rating of **11.0 (Low Risk)**, coverage from all three major providers.

The discount is entirely in **G**: an overnight CEO departure, a 10-month interim CEO, a ~8% dilution ask, and an ~8% say-on-pay dissent. None of this is a scandal — but all of it is a governance *stability* problem, and governance is the pillar that most reliably converts into valuation risk.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (mild negative tilt)

**Why neutral, not positive:** HP's ESG profile is *well-managed but not a value driver*. There is no unmanaged ESG liability large enough to move the equity story, and no ESG tailwind — such as a premium multiple or green-financing advantage — that the market is paying for. ESG is not where the HPQ thesis lives or dies; **memory-cost inflation, PC margin recovery, and AI PC mix** are.

**What would change the call:**

- **→ Negative:** a permanent CEO appointed without hardware/AI credibility; the EU Right to Repair rules (live 31 July 2026) proving to compress supplies attach rates faster than modelled; or an SB 253/CDP disclosure failure that triggers ESG-screened holder selling. Note the supplies annuity is the profit engine — regulatory erosion of it is the one ESG pathway that *is* financially material.
- **→ Positive:** a credible permanent CEO with a clear AI-PC and cost plan; restoring 100% renewable electricity progress and filing CDP Climate; and holding the restructuring to the stated $650M without a second round.

**Bottom line for the memo:** ESG is a **governance-overlay risk**, not a thesis driver. The environmental franchise is real and defensible; the governance story is temporarily hollowed out by caretaker leadership. Underwrite HPQ on the margin-recovery thesis, but treat the ongoing CEO search and the July 2026 EU repair regime as discrete, dated event risks — and do not pay for the ESG leadership.
