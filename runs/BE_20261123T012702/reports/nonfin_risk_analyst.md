## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Bloom Energy Corporation (NYSE: BE)

**Snapshot:** $142.39/sh, ~$42.1B market cap, beta 3.64, ~2,214 FTE, San Jose CA. Founder-Chairman-CEO K.R. Sridhar. Stock ~-60% from its high. FY2026 revenue tracking >$5B (Q3'26 revenue $1.2B, +138% y/y). *Analysis window: filings/news Feb–Nov 2026.*

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK** (adequate on paper, compromised in practice)

**Strengths:** Nine of ten directors are independent (2026 proxy). Genuinely credentialed board — Jeffrey Immelt (ex-GE), John Chambers (ex-Cisco), Michael Boskin, Mary K. Bush, Cynthia Warner, Jim Snabe, Eddy Zervigon. Audit committee is substantive, not ceremonial.

**Impairments that drive the Weak rating:**
- **Founder Chairman + CEO duality.** Sridhar has held both roles since 2002 with no independent chair.
- **Dual-class control.** Class B carries 10 votes vs. Class A's 1. Sridhar holds ~5.39M direct shares (post-Aug 2026 GRAT transfers) plus high-vote stock — minority shareholders have limited ability to check management.
- **Related-party revenue at scale.** Brookfield JV revenue was **$892.0M, or 44.1% of FY2025 revenue** (Q4'25 alone: $574.2M of $778M = **74%**); Q3'26 related-party concentration was **~55%**. FY2025 top three customers = 43%/13%/12%, and the 43% customer is a related party. Bloom's own footnotes concede these counterparties "may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user" — i.e., the financier is being booked as the customer.
- **Disclosure quality on the headline metric.** Management markets an **unaudited ~$20B "backlog"** against **$492.6M of audited RPO** (3/31/26) — a ~40x gap where Hunterbrook's 10-peer comp set maxed out near 2x. That backlog reportedly includes non-obligatory service revenue and tax credits not yet granted.
- **CFO turnover:** Simon Edwards installed April 2026 — a new CFO through the most aggressive growth and disclosure period in company history.
- **Legacy credibility issue:** the Delaware tariff episode (ratepayers obligated for two decades at >5x projected cost, ~600 jobs short of projections) is the template short-sellers are reusing.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Scandium single-point-of-failure and a contradicted public narrative.** Sridhar stated ≥5 times since Feb 2025 that Bloom has "no China supply chain" and is "not dependent on China for scandium." Hunterbrook (7/8/26) traced **four China-linked routes** — direct oxide to the Delaware plant, plus scandium-bearing ceramics via Thailand, Japan and Korea — and named Hunan Oriental Scandium ("we are also BE's largest supplier"). Follow-up (10/30/26) documented **~20 metric tons of electrolyte membranes re-entering Oakland in Sept–Oct 2026** via Thai subsidiary Glory Winner (parent: China's Three Circle Group) after a nine-month hiatus, with shipper/consignee fields blanked but "BE PALLET" markings intact. Bloom's rebuttal claims supply for **25 GW/year**; Hunterbrook's model shows ~220t required by 2028 against ~240t global supply already contested by Lockheed (F-35). Either the ramp is physically constrained or the guidance is not achievable — the supply risk and the disclosure risk are the same risk.
2. **Related-party / circular financing concentration.** With 44–74% of revenue flowing through Brookfield-affiliated JVs whose anchor tenant is Brookfield's own captive cloud company, revenue growth may measure a financier's willingness to fund Bloom's balance sheet rather than end-customer adoption. Recognized revenue ahead of deployment would make this a revenue-recognition problem, not merely a concentration problem.
3. **Cash conversion and execution.** Q3'26 FCF was **negative $181M** despite record revenue and a third guidance raise — scaling manufacturing consumes cash faster than revenue arrives, into a permitting-gated backlog. New CFO, 3.64 beta, no margin for a stumble.

*Key-person risk is also elevated:* Sridhar is founder, chair, CEO, and the sole public voice on the scandium question.

---

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

Three Hunterbrook publications, one titled "Bloom's Big Lie," have converted a technical sourcing question into a credibility narrative that now anchors two securities class actions. The story travels well because it has a villain (China), a hero-deflection (a CEO's repeated denials), and a numeric hook (the 40x backlog/RPO gap). Retail-heavy ownership and the AI-trade unwind have amplified drawdowns — the stock fell 7 consecutive sessions in November *despite* record results and a BMO upgrade to Outperform ($230 PT). Positive offsets (TIME Top Manufacturers 2027, community donations, grid-independence positioning that appeals to ratepayer politics) are real but thin relative to the negative narrative mass. A single corroborating disclosure event — an SEC inquiry, a delayed 10-K, or a restatement of backlog definitions — would be severely value-destructive at this multiple.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical: revenue and volume are genuinely growing, the board is numerically independent, no enforcement action or restatement has occurred, and the scandium allegations remain contested rather than adjudicated. But four risk vectors point the same direction — an unverified public claim contradicted by third-party trade data, revenue concentration in affiliated financiers, a headline metric 40x its audited analog, and $81M of insider selling with no offsetting purchases.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🔴 Contradicted public statements (highest priority).** "No China supply chain" (CEO, repeated 2025–26) vs. Sept–Oct 2026 bills of lading. Potential Rule 10b-5 exposure; the 8-K of 7/9/26 is now itself part of the class period's evidentiary record. **Action:** independent supply-chain diligence; monitor for SEC informal inquiry or 10-K language change.
2. **🔴 Backlog ($20B unaudited) vs. RPO ($492.6M).** ~40x vs. ~2x peer maximum. **Action:** do not underwrite backlog; model only on RPO and firm POs.
3. **🔴 Related-party revenue 44–74% with "project-finance affiliate" counterparties.** **Action:** obtain revenue-recognition policy detail and JV-level deployment data; test whether revenue precedes energization.
4. **🟠 Directors and officers sold ~$81M with zero buys**, including the two directors best positioned to know about scandium sourcing.
5. **🟠 Flagship backlog is permit-gated, not demand-gated.** Jupiter pipeline denial + air-permit waiver + AEP slippage to 2030.
6. **🟠 Structural control:** CEO/Chair duality plus 10-vote Class B; a shareholder resolution or proxy fight cannot discipline management.

**Data gaps (honest disclosure):** FY2025 10-K and the DEF 14A were not retrievable via the filing tool (the 10-Q endpoint returned only an exhibit), so legal-proceedings language, exact board-vote percentages, and compensation metrics are inferred from secondary sources. EDGAR full-text returned no enforcement actions against BE — treat as no *known* action, not as exoneration.
