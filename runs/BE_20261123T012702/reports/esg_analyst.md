## 1. ESG Materiality Map

Bloom is a **policy-dependent, critical-minerals-exposed manufacturer** whose ESG risks are unusually concentrated in three places — not in the usual "carbon footprint" bucket.

| Rank | Factor | Pillar | Why it moves cash flows / cost of capital |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Critical mineral supply chain (scandium, China)** | S (supply chain) / G (disclosure) | China requires an export licence for *every* scandium shipment; supply concentration = revenue-miss and licence-to-operate risk. Already produced a securities class action. |
| **2** | **Disclosure integrity & related-party revenue quality** | G | 44% of FY2025 revenue ($892M) was related-party (Brookfield JVs); $20B "backlog" is unaudited vs. $492.6M audited RPO (~40x gap vs. ~2x peer norm). This is the single largest governance discount in the name. |
| **3** | **Policy/tax-credit dependence (48E ITC)** | E (transition) / G | Fuel cell economics are subsidy-derived; the credit is a tax attribute, not a customer order. Credit change = price/margin shock. |
| **4** | **Local air permit & siting opposition** | E / S (social licence) | Non-combustion stack avoids NOx/PM — real — but projects are being litigated on permitting (Oracle Project Jupiter, NM). |

**Immaterial / low priority:** water stress (near-zero water use is a genuine differentiator), physical climate risk (indoor manufacturing), community philanthropy (noise, not signal).

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives (real, verifiable):**
- Electrochemical, **non-combustion** process → near-zero NOx, SOx, PM vs. gas reciprocating engines/turbines; Utah DEQ and NY DEC permits on file support materially lower criteria-pollutant rates.
- **Minimal water consumption** — a genuine siting advantage in water-stressed data-center and Western US markets.
- Fuel-flexible (gas / biogas / hydrogen blends) and **CCS-amenable**; Nov 2026 non-binding MOU with California Resources/Carbon TerraVault targeting ≥1 GW of fuel cell + carbon capture.

**Negative / contested:**
- **On natural gas, CO₂ intensity is roughly at parity with the US grid average (~0.3–0.4 tCO₂/MWh)** — Bloom's ~1 GW of 2026 deployments imply an estimated ~2–3 Mt CO₂/yr of new on-site fossil generation. The "clean" claim is a *criteria-pollutant and water* claim, not a *carbon* claim, unless biogas/H₂/CCS is used. Most AI data-center volumes are plain pipeline gas.
- **"Avoided emissions" accounting is the load-bearing marketing asset** and is methodologically aggressive (it assumes displacement of marginal fossil peakers). I would not underwrite a carbon credit or green premium on it.
- Absolute Scope 1/2/3 inventory, SBTi-aligned target, and third-party assurance: **not verifiable in accessed materials** — a disclosure gap that matters more as CA SB 253 bites (below).

**Net:** medium — the environmental *product* is better than a diesel genset and better than its carbon story implies; the risk is greenwashing/litigation and permitting, not physical climate.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Supply-chain traceability is the defining social issue.** Hunterbrook's 8 July 2026 report traced four China-linked scandium routes (direct oxide to the Delaware plant; ceramics/powders via Thailand, Japan, South Korea), citing trade data and a Hunan Oriental Scandium sales rep calling itself Bloom's largest supplier. China reinforced extraterritorial re-export restrictions on scandium on **10 November 2026**. This is simultaneously a *human-rights/due-diligence* exposure (no evidence of forced labour, but no verified chain-of-custody either) and a *revenue continuity* exposure. Bloom "categorically rejects" the report and stock rebounded 6% — unresolved, not disproven.
- **Material scarcity:** Hunterbrook's model has Bloom needing ~220t of Sc₂O₃ for a 5 GW ramp vs. ~240t global supply — and Lockheed (F-35) is a competing buyer. Physical constraint on the growth narrative.
- **Social licence / community:** The Delaware 20-year ratepayer obligation that ran **>5x original cost estimates** and missed job projections by **600 jobs** is the reference case utility commissions and community groups now cite. Expect contested permitting (New Mexico) and ratepayer-intervention risk on new utility deals.
- **Positive offsets:** 36:1 CEO-to-median pay ratio is low; 2,214 employees carrying ~$4B revenue implies high-skill job creation; genuine community/disaster-relief programme activity.
- **Unquantified:** no disclosed lost-time injury rate or turnover metric in accessed materials — a data gap for a company scaling manufacturing headcount fast.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10**

**Rationale.** The formal architecture is respectable; the substance is not yet investment-grade.

*Positives:* 10-person board with **9 independent directors (90%)**; independent Lead Independent Director; Deloitte as auditor (up for ratification); advisory say-on-pay; CEO total pay of only ~$3.5M in 2025 (36:1 ratio) — unusually modest; new 2026 PSU grant (271,076 target, up to 300% earn-out on 2026–2029 revenue and 2029 non-GAAP product gross margin) is at least long-dated and metric-linked.

*Negatives:*
- **Founder Chair/CEO duality** — Dr. K.R. Sridhar is Founder, Chairman *and* CEO (25+ year tenure). 90% independence is nominal if the same person sets the agenda; no independent chair.
- **Related-party revenue concentration** — ~**44% of FY2025 revenue ($892M)** and ~74% of Q4'25 revenue from partly-owned Brookfield JVs, where Bloom's own footnotes concede the counterparty "may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user." Circular-financing optics are a governance problem regardless of legal fairness.
- **Disclosure quality** — a ~**$20B unaudited "backlog"** (including service revenue customers need not pay and tax credits not granted) against **$492.6M** audited RPO is the kind of gap that converts into securities litigation. It did: **securities class action filed 30 July 2026, N.D. Cal.** (class period 27 Feb 2025 – 8 Jul 2026, §§10(b)/20(a)); parallel **derivative/D&O demands** (Kahn Swick & Foti, Bragar Eagel, Pomerantz, Rosen, Levi & Korsinsky). Lead-plaintiff deadline was 28 Sept 2026 — expect this overhang for 2–3 years.
- **Legacy dual-class** structure (10-vote Class B) was created at IPO; even where substantially converted, founder control culture persists through the chair.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US — §48E ITC (tailwind, but a cliff):** Fuel cell property beginning construction after 31 Dec 2025 gets a **fixed 30% ITC**; critically, fuel cells were **not** caught by the 4 Jul 2026 wind/solar termination. This is a genuine, underappreciated policy advantage for BE (JPMorgan upgraded on exactly this). **Counter-risk:** it is a *tax-credit* business — the credit drives customer economics, and the credit's value is being capitalised into Bloom's pricing. Post-2027 legislative reversal is the single largest policy risk in the thesis.
- **US — California SB 253/261:** Bloom is a California-domiciled company with >$1B revenue → **in scope for mandatory Scope 1/2/3 GHG reporting** (and SB 261 climate risk reporting). Combined with a thin public emissions inventory, expect a costly disclosure build-out and higher greenwashing-litigation surface.
- **US — SEC climate rule:** effectively stayed/rescinded; low direct exposure. **Federal permitting reform** and the administration's pressure on data centers to self-generate power are net *supportive* of on-site generation demand.
- **China — export controls:** Scandium export licensing plus **Nov 10, 2026 extraterritorial re-export restrictions** = direct, named geopolitically-driven supply risk (the exact fact pattern in the securities suit). This is now a *disclosed* risk, which caps liability but not the operating risk.
- **EU — CSRD/CBAM:** No current direct CSRD obligation (US-centric revenue, below EU turnover thresholds), but EU customers will push value-chain data demands (ESRS S2/C1). CBAM does not cover fuel cells, though steel/aluminium inputs and any EU "AI factory" projects (Brookfield) would import compliance friction.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified as material; capex is being funded with a large liquidity build rather than sustainability-linked instruments. **Low exposure** — a positive.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD (on governance/disclosure), approaching "ESG Risk"**

This is not an environmental laggard — the technology has genuine air-quality and water advantages and the 48E credit is a policy tailwind. It is a **governance and disclosure laggard wearing a clean-tech label**. My composite view: **E: Medium / S: High / G: 4/10 → Overall: Laggard**, one notch from "ESG Risk" pending resolution of the scandium question and the class action.

Critically, **external ESG ratings are likely to overstate Bloom** here: MSCI/Sustainalytics-type scores weight emissions intensity and governance architecture (90% independent board, Deloitte audit, modest CEO pay) and under-weight the two things that actually matter — related-party revenue circularity and critical-mineral chain-of-custody. The market has already begun pricing this (governance-driven de-rating from the peak).

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE (as a risk overlay) — but conditionally Neutral for the thesis**

**For the thesis:** ESG factors are **not** the reason to own or avoid BE — the AI power-demand story and 138% revenue growth are. But three ESG items are genuine *valuation* inputs, not footnotes:

1. **Cost of capital:** beta 3.64, plus a live 10b-5 class action and D&O overhang, justifies a **discount-rate premium of roughly 100–200bp** versus a clean industrial. On a long-duration growth name, that alone is worth a mid-teens percentage haircut to fair value.
2. **Revenue quality must be re-underwritten:** 44% related-party revenue and a $20B unaudited backlog mean the *order book is not an independent confirmation of demand*. Until Brookfield JV volumes convert to named end-customer deployments, treat backlog as an option, not a receivable.
3. **Supply-chain optionality is mispriced both ways:** China's scandium licence regime is a **hard, dated, non-diversifiable** risk to the 5 GW ramp. Conversely, if Bloom verifies ex-China scandium (Australia/Canada processing) or demonstrates scandium thrifting, that is a legitimate re-rating catalyst the market currently gives no credit.

**Recommendation to the desk:** Flag BE as **excluded from ESG-mandated and sustainability-labelled mandates** (fails standard governance and supply-chain due-diligence screens; likely to fail EU SFDR Article 8/9 "good governance" tests on the related-party and disclosure record). For generalist long books, treat as **position-size-constrained**: the ESG issues are idiosyncratic, resolvable, and already partly discounted — but they raise the probability of a second negative disclosure event, and the securities-fraud class period (through 8 Jul 2026) means *new* adverse disclosure has a litigation amplifier.

**What would move me to Neutral/Positive:** (a) third-party audit of scandium chain-of-custody with ex-China offtake contracted; (b) conversion of Brookfield JV "backlog" into named hyperscaler end-customer PPAs; (c) an independent-chair structure or a genuinely independent board committee reviewing related-party transactions; (d) published, assured Scope 1/2/3 inventory ahead of CA SB 253.

**Data gaps (honest):** no 10-K/DEF 14A text retrievable via filing tool (SEC 403 on direct fetch); absolute Scope 1/2/3 figures, injury rates and turnover not verified; 2026 say-on-pay vote outcome not confirmed. Findings on emissions intensity are modelled estimates, not company-reported.
