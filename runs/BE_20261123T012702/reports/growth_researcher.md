# Bloom Energy (BE) — Equity Growth Research

**Price $142.45 (Nov 20, 2026) · Market cap ~$42.0B · EV ~$44B · Sector: Industrials / Electrical Equipment**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Bloom Energy has crossed from a subsidy-dependent science project into the **default supplier of firm, on-site power for AI data centers**. Its solid-oxide fuel cell platform delivers grid-independent electricity in 9–12 months versus 4–7 years for utility interconnection — the single scarcest input in the AI buildout. Revenue has inflected violently (+138% in Q3'26, 122% guided for FY2026), gross margins have expanded ~2,100bps from the 2022 trough, and operating leverage has flipped the model profitable. The stock is simultaneously **59% below its June 2026 high** — a de-rating driven by an AI-infrastructure rotation and a short-seller report, not by a deterioration in fundamentals. That divergence is the opportunity.

## 3. TAM & Penetration

- **Market share:** Bloom holds roughly **75% of the global SOFC market** — a genuine technology monopoly position within its niche, protected by ~20 years of materials science IP, a 2,214-person manufacturing organization, and installed-base service annuities.
- **Capacity is the constraint, not demand:** ~2 GW annualized entering 2027, expanding toward **5 GW+** with the Fremont footprint buildout. Capex has stepped up to **~$153M/quarter** to fund it.
- **Backlog >$20B** — roughly **4.4x FY2026 revenue**, providing unusually high visibility for a company this young. Anchored by Oracle's **2.8 GW** order and the **$2.65B AEP** agreement, with **Brookfield** providing customer-side project financing.
- **Penetration is low single digits.** Bloom's own 2026 Data Center Power Report projects one in five data centers will exceed 1 GW of consumption by 2030. Even at $10B+ of revenue, Bloom would be capturing a low-single-digit share of cumulative AI-era data center power spend. The **utility channel** (selling to utilities rather than around them) is an entirely new, unmodeled TAM layer that opens the second act.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **FY2026 results & initial FY2027 guide — Feb 4, 2027.** Consensus Q4 EPS $1.07 on $1.42B revenue. The guide is the single largest re-rating lever: anything ≥$7.3B with ≥30% gross margin validates the consensus curve.
2. **5 GW+ capacity milestone disclosure** — management has declined to date the expansion; a firm timeline would de-risk the FY2028 $10.7B consensus.
3. **Oracle 2.8 GW order conversion** from framework to firm purchase orders — the test case for contract terms at scale.
4. **Utility partnership announcements** — the newest growth vector, currently zero in models.
5. **Resolution/clarity on the Hunterbrook short report and related class action** — a clean audit cycle and clean 10-K would remove the credibility discount.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Credibility / accounting overhang (highest severity).** Hunterbrook's July 8, 2026 report *"Bloom's Big Lie"* alleges accounting and financial-performance misrepresentation; securities class actions followed. Bloom "categorically rejects" the claims (8-K, July 9). The GAAP-to-non-GAAP gap is wide — Q3'26 non-GAAP EPS $0.94 vs. GAAP $0.78, with **$57M of quarterly SBC** (~$230M annualized) excluded. If the bears are right, the entire model is void. Position sizing must reflect a binary tail.
2. **AI capex single-variable dependence.** Nov 2026's 59% drawdown was a *sector* rotation, not company news. A hyperscaler capex pause would compress both the growth rate and the multiple simultaneously.
3. **Customer concentration.** A handful of hyperscalers plus Oracle/AEP likely drive most of the $20B backlog. Contract terms at 2.8 GW scale are untested.
4. **Manufacturing execution.** Scaling from 2 GW to 5 GW+ in ~24 months is a step-function increase in process control. Fuel cell defects are catastrophic for unit economics and warranty reserves; note "manufacturing defects" is a named risk factor.
5. **Working capital / financing dependency.** Customers need financing (Brookfield mitigates). Negative FCF in Q3'26 despite record profit shows how quickly growth consumes cash. Rising rates or a financing-market freeze would slow bookings.
6. **Competition.** GE Vernova gas turbines and reciprocating engines are the incumbents for firm power and are ramping their own backlog; Doosan and Plug Power compete in fuel cells. Bloom's moat is real but the grid-connection alternative gets better every year a utility catches up.
7. **Policy and dilution.** IRA / One Big Beautiful Bill Act changes affect customer economics. Diluted shares (324M) run ~10% above basic (295M) due to converts.

---

**Bottom line:** This is a textbook Cathie Wood setup — a monopoly-positioned platform technology at the epicenter of the largest infrastructure buildout of the decade, growing 122% with a Rule of 40 score above 100, trading at 26.7x forward earnings and 0.43 PEG after a sentiment-driven halving. The TAM is not the question; **execution, accounting credibility, and AI-capex durability are**. Buy, sized for a 3.6-beta name with a binary risk in the tail.
