**Split / contested:**

### 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector concentration — additive, not diversifying.** MKC correlates **0.63 with GIS, 0.60 with CPB, 0.59 with KHC, 0.54 with MDLZ** (252d). Any portfolio already carrying packaged-food exposure is buying a sixth version of the same volume-reset trade. Adding MKC raises sector concentration without adding a differentiated driver.

**Factor exposure — the critical misfit.** MKC is **+value / −momentum / −low-volatility**. Correlations confirm it is a leveraged proxy on a *cheap-staples reversal that has not arrived* (0.49–0.58 vs KO/PEP/PG/MDLZ). Two specific harms:
1. **It breaks the defensive sleeve.** A staples holding carrying **32.9% annualised vol vs a 21.8% peer median** and a **−36.7% drawdown** is not defence. If the sleeve exists to cushion equity risk, MKC imports idiosyncratic risk instead of removing it.
2. **The negative beta is a data artifact — do not size on it.** Beta vs SPY of −0.21 with **r² ≈ 0.009** means the market explains <1% of variance; the tool returns similarly negative betas for KO, PEP and MDLZ, so this is a benchmark-mapping error, not a hedge. Use the 5-year **0.67**. MKC offers no genuine market-neutrality.
3. **Negative carry on momentum.** Adding MKC to a momentum- or quality-tilted book adds the worst 12-1 name in the staples group (−29.6% vs PG +12.4%, KO +6.7%).

**Correlation/diversification:** effective diversification benefit is minimal — the average pairwise correlation to the staples complex is ~0.54, and to SPY it is statistically zero with no reliable sign.

**Income role — weaker than the yield suggests.** 4.11% looks attractive, but **Q3 FY26 FCF ($112M) was less than dividends ($129M)**, the FY26 payout is ~80–90% of FCF, and Fin Risk's $1.5–2.0B deleveraging plan needs ~$750M–1B/yr against ~$100–200M/yr of actual post-dividend surplus. In a 5.13% 10-year environment, a 4.1% yield with underfunded deleveraging and 4.2x pro forma leverage is **not a bond substitute**.

**Portfolio-level risks I would escalate:** (a) if the fund already owns GIS/KHC/CPB, MKC is not a new idea but the same impairment-cycle bet; (b) the deal converts the holding into a 35% minority in an entity whose board and capital allocation the buyer no longer controls — a governance discount that should persist absent a dual-class sunset; (c) EUDR/CSDDD compliance (ESG) is a market-access cost, not a virtue signal — low-hundreds of $m of European revenue at risk if the no-deforestation gap is not closed.
