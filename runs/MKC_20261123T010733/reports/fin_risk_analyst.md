## McCormick & Company (MKC) — Financial Risk Assessment

**Company:** McCormick & Company, Inc. | Consumer Defensive / Packaged Foods | US | Market cap ≈ **$12.77B** (269.4M diluted shares × $47.40) | Fiscal year ends Nov 30

**Critical context up front:** MKC is not a standalone story right now. On 31 March 2026 it agreed to **combine with Unilever's Foods business** (Unilever's own release states combined-company net leverage "expected to be 4.0x or less" at close). Separately, the January 2026 McCormick de Mexico buy-in (+25% stake to 100%) added ~13 points of FY26 sales growth and took on debt. My metrics below cover both the FY2025 baseline and the latest reported quarter (Q3 FY26, ended 31 Aug 2026); the merged entity is not yet funded in the reported balance sheet. *(FY2026 ends Nov 2026 — full-year actuals not yet available.)*

### 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: Investment Grade — lower tier (BBB/Baa), with negative momentum.** Not "Sub-Investment Grade" yet, but the trajectory is deteriorating and the pro forma deal profile sits at the edge of the BBB band.

- S&P published a research update on MKC estimating **adjusted pro forma debt/EBITDA of ~4.2x for fiscal 2026, up from ~2.9x previously expected** — a ~130bp jump in leverage versus prior expectations. The specific rating letter/outlook in that update was not captured in my search results; **verify the outlook (stable vs. negative) directly**.
- Standalone leverage has risen from **2.91x (FY25)** to **~3.27x** on Q3 FY26 annualised EBITDA ($3,915M net debt / $1,198M).
- Interest coverage has compressed from **5.46x (FY25)** to **3.26x in Q3 FY26**; 9M FY26 coverage was 4.14x. Interest expense is running ~$274M annualised vs. $196M in FY25 (**+40%**), driven by acquisition debt and refinancing at 5%+ rates.
- Mitigants: non-cyclical category, brand strength (French's, Frank's RedHot, Cholula, OLD BAY), and a stated **$1.5–2.0B debt paydown plan within two years**. That plan is the swing factor for the rating.

### 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue −20%

Base case assumed: FY26E revenue ~$7.9B, gross margin ~38.5%, EBITDA ~$1.20B, interest ~$274M, net debt ~$3.92B. A 20% revenue shock would hit through **Flavor Solutions** (industrial/foodservice) first — the cyclical segment — while Consumer holds better.

| Scenario | EBITDA | Net debt/EBITDA | EBIT | Interest coverage | Dividend coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | $1,200M | 3.3x | $890M | 3.3x | ~80–90% of FCF |
| −10% revenue, no cost action | $884M | 4.4x | $600M | 2.2x | FCF ~$350M vs $516M div → **shortfall** |
| −20% revenue, $200M cost mitigation | $770M | **5.1x** | $480M | 1.8x | **Dividend must be cut** |
| −20% revenue, no mitigation | $558M | **7.0x** | $280M | **~1.0x** | **Dividend eliminated; revolver drawn** |

At a 20% revenue decline, leverage breaches any plausible 4.0x covenant test, coverage falls below 2.0x, and the dividend is unfunded. Critically, the **$1.5–2.0B debt paydown plan does not appear fundable from current FCF**: FY26 FCF looks like ~$600–700M against ~$516M of dividends, leaving only ~$100–200M/yr of surplus — one-tenth of the required pace. In Q3 FY26 alone, **FCF of $112M was less than dividends of $129M**. The plan therefore depends on EBITDA growth, working-capital release, and/or divestitures — not on the current cash engine.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Standalone this is a **Moderate-to-High** credit (stable brands, 2.9–3.3x leverage, adequate FCF), but the combination of (a) pro forma leverage ~4.0–4.2x from the Unilever Foods transaction, (b) negative tangible equity, (c) sub-1.0x current ratio with $1.34B of short-term debt against $331M cash, and (d) coverage already down to 3.26x pushes the overall assessment to **High**. Not Critical — the category is non-cyclical, cash generation is positive, and there is a stated deleveraging commitment — but the margin for error is thin and the balance sheet has no tangible cushion.

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Transformational deal risk:** Unilever Foods combination targets ≤4.0x net leverage at close; S&P models ~4.2x adjusted for FY26. Integration, regulatory approval, and synergy delivery are all unresolved. MKC's own FY26 guidance excludes the deal.
2. **Goodwill concentration:** $11.3B of goodwill/intangibles (67.6% of assets); **tangible equity ≈ −$3.7B**. Impairment risk is asymmetric.
3. **Liquidity is genuinely tight:** current ratio 0.66x, cash ratio 0.075x, negative working capital −$1.52B, **$1.34B short-term debt vs. $331M cash** — dependence on revolver/commercial paper at a 10Y yield of 5.13% (2Y 4.74%, 2s10s +39bp, no inversion).
4. **Earnings quality:** Q1 FY26 net income of $1,016M includes a ~$900M non-operating gain; Q3 FY26 GAAP net income fell 56.7% YoY and operating margin compressed to 10.7% from 15.65% in FY25 — despite gross margin *expanding* to 39.3%. Watch the ~$141M "other operating charges" line.
5. **Coverage and interest burden:** interest expense run-rate up ~40% YoY; coverage down to 3.26x in Q3.
6. **Dividend/FCF tension:** Q3 FY26 FCF ($112M) < dividends ($129M); FY26 payout likely 80–90% of FCF; 3.8% yield is not well covered if EBITDA falls.
7. **Deleveraging plan underfunded:** $1.5–2.0B over two years vs. ~$100–200M/yr of FCF surplus after dividends.
8. **Valuation not distressed:** EV ~$16.7B = ~13.9x Q3-annualised EBITDA and ~15x adjusted EPS — the market is not paying a distressed multiple, so downside from a leverage scare is not already discounted.
