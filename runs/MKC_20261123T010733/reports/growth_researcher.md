# McCormick & Company (MKC) — Growth Investment Analysis

*Price $47.40 · Market cap $12.8B · EV $16.7B · Sector: Consumer Defensive / Packaged Foods*

---

## 1. Growth Story

McCormick is the world's largest pure-play flavor company — an 137-year-old brand portfolio (McCormick, French's, Frank's RedHot, Cholula, Old Bay, Lawry's, Ducros, Schwartz) sitting on top of a genuinely durable secular theme: **flavor intensity is rising globally** as consumers trade up from commodity cooking to seasoned, ethnic and "swicy" profiles, and as packaged-food and QSR customers outsource flavor formulation. That is a real, growing TAM. **The problem is that McCormick is not currently converting it into growth.** Organic sales are running +2% with volumes *negative* (−0.3% in Q3 FY26), reported growth is being propped up by the McCormick de México consolidation (FY26 guided sales +13–17%), and gross margin has structurally reset from 41% (FY20) to ~38%. This is a high-quality franchise in a cyclical profit trough — a *value-and-income* reset, not a disruptive growth compounder.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

| Market | Size | Growth | MKC position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Global spices & seasonings (retail) | ~$25–30B | ~5–6% | #1 branded, ~20% share |
| Condiments & sauces | ~$45B | ~4–5% | Top-3 in N. America |
| Industrial flavor / flavor solutions | ~$20–25B | ~5–7% | Top-5 globally |

- **Combined SAM ≈ $70–90B**; McCormick's ~$8B revenue implies **~10% penetration** — mathematically meaningful headroom.
- **Where the TAM actually grows:** emerging markets (LatAm/Mexico, China, India, SE Asia), foodservice recovery, clean-label/organic, and Flavor Solutions outsourcing from CPG and QSR customers. McCormick's **Flavor Solutions operating income grew 26% in Q2 FY26** — this is the one genuinely compounding engine and the best argument for the bull case.
- **Where it doesn't:** developed-market retail spices are a low-growth, private-label-threatened category. McCormick's core US consumer business is defending share, not taking it.
- **Differentiation vs. peers (P&G, Unilever, private label):** McCormick's moat is *flavor formulation know-how + customer co-development lock-in* in Flavor Solutions, plus #1 brand equity in a category with genuinely low private-label penetration relative to other staples. That is a real moat — but a moat that protects a 2% grower, not a 15% one.

**Valuation for growth:** 14.8x FY27E EPS · 12.4x EV/EBITDA · 17.3x P/FCF · **4.1% dividend yield**. A 2% organic grower with 15.5% operating margins and ~2.9x net debt/EBITDA (net debt ~$3.9B, interest expense $196M/yr) does not deserve a premium multiple. The market is implying ~3% long-term revenue growth and modest margin recovery — **and that is roughly what the business delivers.** There is no hidden growth option being mispriced here.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Q4/FY2026 earnings — 28 Jan 2027.** Consensus $0.77 EPS / $2.11B revenue. The pivotal event: FY27 guidance, the first clean read on full Mexico consolidation accretion, and whether gross margin can hold above 38.5%. *Highest-impact catalyst.*
2. **Mexico consolidation accretion (H1 FY27).** Full ownership of McCormick de México converts a JV equity line into consolidated sales and should be meaningfully EPS-accretive — but inflates reported growth while masking organic ~2%. Watch organic disclosure.
3. **Restructuring / cost-savings program delivery.** ~$0.36 GAAP vs. ~$0.86 adjusted in Q3 signals heavy special charges. Every quarter of savings realization is a direct margin catalyst toward the 39–40% gross margin target.
4. **Dividend increase (announced Nov 2026, $0.51 +6.3%).** 4.1% yield with ~74% FCF payout — supportive for income buyers, but it caps reinvestment capacity.
5. **Flavor Solutions contract wins / foodservice.** Any disclosed QSR or CPG co-development win validates the only structurally growing segment.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD** *(avoid for growth mandates; own only for income/mean-reversion)*

**The growth thesis fails on every dimension of this framework:** organic growth ~2%, negative volumes, gross margin down 320bps in five years, Rule of 40 of 12.6, EPS growth in the low single digits, and reported growth manufactured by an acquisition. There is no disruptive innovation, no platform dynamic, no data network effect — the Flavor Solutions co-development engine is real but too small to move a $13B-cap company's growth rate.

**However, I would not sell it here.** At 14.8x forward EPS and a 4.1% yield, with margins at cyclical lows, a cost-savings program in flight, Mexico accretion ahead, and 5-of-7 quarters of EPS beats, the downside is largely priced. The bull case is **margin recovery, not revenue acceleration** — and that is a value/income setup, which is explicitly outside this mandate.

**Positioning:** Do not initiate for growth. If held for income, size modestly and use the 28 Jan 2027 print as the decision gate — a credible FY27 organic-growth-acceleration and gross-margin-recovery guide could produce a 15–20% re-rating toward the $55–59 analyst cluster; a fourth consecutive guide-down takes it toward $40.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Structural volume decline.** Three years of negative-to-flat volume means the entire model depends on pricing power — which is eroding as retailers (notably Walmart) push back and private label gains share.
2. **GLP-1 / shifting consumption.** Smaller appetites and health-driven reformulation pressure the at-home cooking occasions that anchor Consumer segment volumes.
3. **Tariff and input-cost exposure.** Spices, pepper, vanilla and packaging are imported and volatile; China (Tianjin) and Mexico supply chains carry tariff risk that flows straight to the depressed gross margin line.
4. **Balance sheet leverage.** ~$3.9B net debt (~2.9x EBITDA) plus $196M annual interest constrains both M&A firepower and buybacks — the two levers that would otherwise mask slow organic growth.
5. **Execution risk on the restructuring.** GAAP EPS of $0.36 vs. $0.86 adjusted shows how wide the gap between "adjusted" narrative and economic reality has become; repeated special charges undermine the earnings base.
6. **Competitive threat.** Private label in spices, plus Unilever/P&G and nimble regional flavor houses in Flavor Solutions bidding on the same co-development contracts.
