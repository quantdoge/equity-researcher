# NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — McCormick & Company (NYSE: MKC / MKC.V)

**Snapshot:** Price $47.40 | Mkt cap $12.75B | Beta 0.67 | 14,100 employees | CEO: Brendan Foley | Data cut-off: ~19 Nov 2026
**Context:** Stock is ~29% below its Feb-2026 level (~$66.84) and near the low end of its 52-week range, amid a **$44.8bn transformative combination with Unilever Foods** (announced 31 Mar 2026) that is the dominant non-financial risk driver for the next 12–18 months.

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (trending weak on control structure)**

**Positives**
- Majority-independent board (proxy: Bramman, Conway, Dierker, Hattersley and others designated independent under NYSE standards); Anne Bramman chairs Audit.
- Active board refreshment: Bramman (Jan 2026), Dierker & Hattersley (1 Feb 2026), Cindy Hoots (1 Jun 2026) — useful deal/integration expertise being added.
- 41st consecutive dividend increase (to $0.51/qtr, 17 Nov 2026) and 103 straight years of payouts — evidence of disciplined capital-return governance.
- Board unanimously approved the transaction; both companies publicly committed to "constructive" CMA engagement.

**Concerns**
- **Combined Chairman/President/CEO** (Brendan Foley) — no independent chair; power concentration is material given the CEO is simultaneously negotiating/executing a merger that will dilute existing owners to a minority.
- **Dual-class structure:** voting Common Stock (MKC) plus **Common Stock Non-Voting (MKC.V)**. Management/family-adjacent holders can retain effective control with less than proportional economic exposure; non-voting holders have no remedy at the ballot box.
- **Control shift without a control premium:** on closing, Unilever holders are expected to own **65.0% (55.1% direct)** and **McCormick shareholders only 35.0%**, yet McCormick's chairman/CEO and management team lead the combined company. This is economically an acquisition of McCormick's shareholders into a Unilever-controlled vehicle while presenting as a McCormick-led merger — a classic related-interest/alignment flashpoint.
- **Retention-award architecture:** 10-Q exhibit (EX-10.16) shows cash retention awards vesting at closing plus 6 months, with full acceleration on termination without cause and **280G "excess parachute" gross-up logic**. Quantum undisclosed publicly → unquantified compensation exposure at a moment of ownership change.
- Board/director churn *during* the largest deal in company history raises continuity risk (five new/retiring directors within 12 months).

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Merger integration execution (highest).** Absorbing Knorr/Hellmann's-scale businesses into McCormick's systems, supply chain, and culture while running a new divisional operating model (announced Sept 2026: EVP/Presidents per division) — a documented, self-acknowledged key-person dependency (Chief Integration Officer; retention awards to "critical" employees). Integration missteps would hit both earnings and credibility simultaneously.
- **Agricultural input & geographic supply concentration.** Spices/herbs (pepper, vanilla, garlic, chili, onion) are single-origin, climate- and FX-exposed commodities; tariffs (~$140M, roughly half still in force) plus the McCormick de Mexico acquisition concentrate emerging-market exposure. Price/volume dynamic is already deteriorating: Q3 organic sales +2% on **+2.2% price / −0.3% volume** — pricing is masking volume softness.
- **Customer & channel concentration with private-label encroachment.** Heavy reliance on a handful of global retailers (Walmart-scale) and a $330bn US private-label market now taking shelf space; combined with a levered post-deal balance sheet (BBB; $1.5–2.0bn debt paydown targeted in 2 years), there is limited slack for a demand or customer-loss shock.
- *Not verified:* cybersecurity incident history and disaster-recovery posture — no disclosure found; this remains a blind spot in the assessment.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Moderate, rising**

- **Brand-integrity allegations are the live issue.** The Cholula suit alleges the "Original" recipe was quietly reformulated with acetic acid and natural flavor after the $800M acquisition — a "shrinkflation/bait-and-switch" narrative that travels well on social media and touches the company's most premium acquired brand. Framed by press as "McCormick changed the recipe after the takeover," it invites follow-on consumer and plaintiff-bar attention.
- **Pattern, not a one-off:** French's "Made in USA" claims, El Guapo "authentic" chile sourcing, and (dismissed) heavy-metals suits. McCormick's marketing-claim risk is structural rather than episodic — four separate challenges since 2022.
- **Narrative risk around the deal:** shareholder pushback ("investors find $65bn food deal hard to swallow", Reuters), analyst PT cuts, and a ~29% drawdown create a story of a company paying up at the wrong moment, while the CEO stays chairman. That is a reputation risk with management, not consumers.
- Offsetting: category-leading brand portfolio, dividend aristocrat status, and no evidence of ESG/labour/environmental controversy. ESG controversy scoring not retrieved — gap.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **CMA outcome unresolved past the 18 Nov 2026 remedies deadline** — a Phase 2 reference would push close beyond mid-2027 and likely require divestiture of UK dry-mix/dry-sauce assets (Schwartz/Knorr overlap). Escalate for scenario analysis of synergy loss; also confirm whether the **Colman's mustard sale** process is a condition of clearance.
2. **Control asymmetry:** McCormick shareholders to 35% of the combined company while McCormick's Chairman/CEO retains both roles. Demand the combined-entity board composition and governance agreement (board seats, dual-class continuation, listing venue) before underwriting the equity.
3. **Combined Chair/CEO + non-voting share class** — limited shareholder recourse if integration or capital allocation disappoints.
4. **Net insider selling ≈$10.8M (former CEO, full liquidation of exercised options at $52.69) against ≈$0.1M of director buying** — no insider support at 52-week lows.
5. **Cholula reformulation class action** — monitor for consolidation with other marketing-claim suits; potential brand-equity damage beyond the $5M claim size.
6. **Unquantified retention awards / 280G acceleration** at closing — request aggregate disclosure.
