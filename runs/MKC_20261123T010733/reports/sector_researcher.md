## SECTOR BRIEF — McCormick & Company (MKC)
**Sector:** Consumer Defensive / Packaged Foods (US-listed branded staples)
**Benchmark set:** MDLZ, GIS, KHC, HSY, CPB, SJM, CAG (true value-added packaged-food peers, not the mega-cap retail/beverage names in the FMP sector screen)
*Data as of latest reported fiscal years (MKC FY-Nov 2025 + Q3 FY26 to Aug-2026; peers FY2025-26).*

### 1. Sector Overview
US branded packaged foods is a mature, low-growth, ~$1tn global category with structurally mid-single-digit nominal growth and roughly flat-to-negative real volumes. The 2022–24 pricing cycle has exhausted itself: after three years of double-digit list-price increases, elasticity has bitten and every major peer is now reporting negative organic volume and, in several cases, negative total revenue. FY2025-26 results show the sector in a **downcycle with an earnings reset** — GIS total revenue −5.5%, CPB −5.0%, KHC −3.5%, CAG −2.9%, and GAAP operating margins collapsing at GIS (17.0%→4.8%), KHC (17.2%→−18.7%), CAG (11.8%→−14.4%), SJM (16.0%→4.0%) and HSY (25.9%→12.3%) as goodwill impairments, cocoa/input costs and negative operating leverage hit. Concentration is moderate-high at the brand level but low at the category level (top-3 share of any single aisle rarely >35%); barriers are brand + shelf-space + distribution, not capital. Porter: **buyer power high** (Walmart/Costco/Amazon and private label), **supplier power moderate-high** (cocoa, dairy, packaging, freight), **rivalry high**, **threat of substitutes/private label rising**, **entry threat low**.

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Defensive demand profile & trade-down-to-home-cooking:** budget-conscious consumers cooking at home lifted MKC to its fastest sales growth since 2021 (+17.4% reported in Q3 FY26, organic +1.9%); Consumer Defensive cash flows are also rate-sensitive beneficiaries as yields fall (MKC beta 0.67).
- **Input-cost relief outside cocoa/dairy:** spice, herb and condiment baskets have seen freight and agricultural inputs normalise, supporting MKC's gross margin (39.3% in Q3 FY26 vs 37.4% a year ago) — a differentiator vs cocoa-exposed HSY and dairy-exposed GIS/CAG.
- **Consolidation & portfolio pruning:** Mars/Kellanova, MdM-style regional buy-ins and divestiture of low-margin non-core brands are shrinking capacity and improving industry discipline; scale players can bolt on ethnic/flavor assets at distressed multiples.

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Private-label share gains:** price/volume mix is now almost entirely price-driven (MKC Q3 FY26: price +2.2%, organic volume −0.3%; US volumes reportedly down ~2.5%), with store brands taking share in staples as food-at-home inflation persists.
- **GLP-1 adoption as a structural volume tax:** FTI estimates ~3bn fewer restaurant-equivalent meals/year by 2030, with double-digit declines in high-calorie snacks/sweets — a headwind for HSY, MDLZ, SJM and confection/snack-heavy portfolios, and a category-mix risk for the whole sector.
- **Impairment/leverage overhang:** the sector is carrying post-2021 M&A debt into a volume decline — interest expense and write-downs are compressing GAAP EPS (MKC interest expense $68.4m in Q3 FY26 vs $50.2m a year earlier), and tariff/packaging cost uncertainty remains unresolved.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (relative), with leverage caveats**
- **Growth:** +1.7% vs peer median −2.9% — MKC is ~460bp ahead and one of only three peers (with HSY, SJM) still growing organically; FY26 reported growth steps to ~+15% from the Jan-2026 McCormick de México consolidation (14.6pp of Q3 growth), though that is purchased, not organic.
- **Margins:** 37.9% gross / 15.7% operating vs 33.4% / 6.8% median — a ~450bp gross and ~890bp operating edge in the current cycle, and the flattest margin trajectory in the group (operating margin has held 13.6–16.1% for six years while peers swung 20+ points).
- **Structural logic:** flavor/seasoning is the most GLP-1-resilient aisle (seasoning makes low-calorie home cooking palatable), has lower private-label penetration than cereal/soup/frozen, and MKC holds #1 global spice share with 14 brands — the best competitive position in this peer set on category dynamics.
- **Caveats:** acquisition is debt-funded — Q3 FY26 net income $97.6m vs $225.5m YoY despite +17.4% sales, with SGA up 24% and interest up 36%; leverage and intangible amortisation dilute GAAP EPS. Equity has de-rated hard: $79.40 (Sep-24) → $43.68 (Oct-26) → $47.40, market cap $12.75bn, ~16x FY25 EPS — the market is pricing sector-wide multiple compression, not MKC-specific failure.

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**
Defensive demand and MKC's category mix are genuine positives, and the impairment-driven margin trough at GIS/KHC/CAG/CPB sets up easy comparisons into FY2027. But the sector is in a **late-cycle volume reset**: negative organic volumes, rising private-label penetration, GLP-1 mix erosion and unresolved input/tariff costs cap pricing power, and sector growth is running below GDP. We would not underweight Consumer Defensive staples as a defensive sleeve, but we see no sector-level tailwind — returns here will come from **share gainers with clean balance sheets and resilient categories**, which is precisely where MKC screens best in this peer set.
