## ESG ANALYSIS — McCORMICK & COMPANY (MKC)

**Sector:** Consumer Defensive / Packaged Foods | **Market cap:** $12.8bn | **Employees:** 14,100
**Critical context:** MKC is mid-transformation — a $44.8bn combination with Unilever Foods (announced 2026) that reshapes the investment case *and* the ESG profile. **All ESG conclusions below must be read through the merger.**

---

### 1. ESG Materiality Map — Packaged Foods / Flavor

| Factor | Materiality | Why it hits cash flow |
|---|---|---|
| **Supply chain ESG (agricultural sourcing / deforestation / human rights)** | **HIGH** | Spices, herbs, palm oil, vanilla, cocoa are smallholder-sourced from high-risk geographies (Indonesia, Madagascar, India, Vietnam). Drives cost of goods, EUDR compliance, retailer delisting risk, brand. |
| **Product safety & contaminant liability** | **HIGH** | Spices are a known vector for heavy metals, EtO, salmonella. Recalls = direct revenue loss + litigation + retail relationship damage. |
| **Governance / shareholder rights (dual-class)** | **HIGH — most material here** | Voting control decoupled from economic ownership post-merger. Affects takeover premium, index inclusion, activist engagement, and minority holders' ability to block value-destructive deals. |
| **Climate / Scope 3 emissions** | **MEDIUM** | Mostly Scope 3 agricultural; no carbon-intensive manufacturing. Risk is regulatory disclosure (CSRD/SB 253) and supplier cost pass-through, not direct carbon tax. |
| **Labour & workforce** | **LOW-MEDIUM** | 14,100 employees, largely US/EU manufacturing. Manageable. |
| **Water / physical climate** | **LOW-MEDIUM** | Agricultural commodity price volatility (crop failures) is the transmission channel. |

The dominant ESG risk for MKC is **not environmental** — it is **governance**, and it is unusually acute right now.

---

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (improving, but a specific regulatory gap)

**Strengths:**
- **SBTi-validated targets**: −42% absolute Scope 1+2 and Scope 3 by 2030 vs. 2021 baseline; **net-zero by 2050 validated by SBTi**. This is best-practice verification, not self-assertion.
- **2025 Purpose-led Performance Report** (June 2026): completed 2025 commitments, including reportedly **100% sustainable sourcing for its top ingredients**; **Scope 3 down ~9% in 2025**, on track for ~40% absolute reduction by 2030.
- Low carbon intensity business model — flavor blending is not energy-intensive; most footprint is upstream agriculture.

**Material weakness:** Per Reuters (May 2026), **McCormick's sustainability report does not contain an explicit company-wide no-deforestation commitment** and provides less traceability detail than peers. This matters because:
- **EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR)** applies to palm oil, cocoa, coffee, soy, rubber, wood, and **cattle** — MKC's European portfolio (Ducros, Schwartz, Kamis) and its Flavor Solutions ingredient sales create direct exposure. Non-compliance = inability to place product on the EU market.
- **Retailer sourcing standards** (Walmart, Tesco, Carrefour) increasingly impose NDPE requirements contractually.
- This is a **self-inflicted gap, not a structural one** — cheap to fix, but currently an open flank.

**Environmental controversies:** no material spills, fines, or remediation liabilities identified. EtO is a product-safety issue, not environmental.

---

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (product safety is the live risk, not labour)

**Product safety — the real social exposure:**
- McCormick **discontinued ethylene oxide (EtO) use across its entire consumer portfolio as of June 2026**, per company FAQ. The phrasing implies prior use — EtO is a known carcinogen and the subject of EU bans and recalls across the spice industry. This is a **completed remediation**, but it was a live regulatory and reputational issue for years.
- **Consumer Reports** testing found **arsenic, cadmium, and lead** in herbs and spices across brands including McCormick (126 products, 38 brands); separate CR testing found high lead in cinnamon products, prompting **FDA public health alerts**. A **proposed class action alleging deceptive advertising** (spices marketed as pure/wholesome) was dropped in April 2024. Financial impact to date: modest (low single-digit $m legal), but the reputational tail is long and the FDA lead-limit rule remains a forward cost risk.
- **Salmonella recalls** (Italian Seasoning; Frank's RedHot Buffalo Ranch) — contained, but demonstrate contaminant-control vulnerability in a low-margin, high-volume spice line.

**Supply chain labour / human rights:**
- MKC publishes a **Modern Slavery Statement (2024–25)** with supplier training on trafficking/slavery identification — standard, not leadership. Spice and vanilla supply chains carry well-documented child-labour and forced-labour risk (Indonesian nutmeg, Madagascar vanilla, Indian seed spices).
- Post-merger, this becomes a **governance flashpoint**: Unilever investors are explicitly demanding the combined entity adopt Unilever's deforestation-free sourcing and traceability standards. Unilever is materially stronger here; MKC is the weaker party inheriting the larger share of the combined sourcing footprint.

**Workforce:** No material safety, union, or turnover controversies identified. CEO transition to Brendan Foley (2023) was orderly.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10** — the weakest pillar, and deteriorating

**Rationale:**

| Item | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Dual-class structure** | **Severe.** Class B common carries ~10 votes/share vs. 1 for Class A. The **McCormick family controls voting outcomes while owning a minority economic stake.** Standard governance red flags: no ability for public holders to effect change; entrenched board. |
| **Post-merger control anomaly** | **The critical issue.** Combined ownership: Unilever shareholders **55.1%**, legacy MKC shareholders **35.0%**, Unilever **9.9%**. Critically, MKC holders are stated to own **35.0% of *each class*** — meaning the dual-class structure **survives the deal**. Result: a company in which **Unilever's shareholders own the majority of the economics but the McCormick family retains voting control.** This is a textbook minority-control arrangement and the single largest governance discount in the equity story. |
| **Board independence** | Guidelines require a **majority-independent board** (per the Feb 2026 proxy), and committees (Audit, Comp, Nom/Gov) are stated to be fully independent. This is adequate on paper — but independence is structurally compromised when the controlling bloc is a family. |
| **CEO/Chair separation** | Appears separated (Foley is CEO); no duality flag. **Positive.** |
| **M&A process & shareholder rights** | A transformational deal that cedes 55% of the combined equity to Unilever holders, with **$15.7bn cash consideration** and a **$75m expense reimbursement** if terminated. The fact that control can be transferred while the family's voting bloc is preserved will attract scrutiny on whether minority holders received adequate process. Deal cleared US antitrust (a key milestone). |
| **Compensation alignment** | Not verifiable from available filings (SEC/DEF 14A fetches blocked). **Gap in our evidence base** — flag as unconfirmed. |
| **Ethics / anti-corruption** | No FCPA or bribery enforcement actions identified. Clean. |
| **Transparency** | 2025 PLP Report discloses progress against completed 2025 goals — but **has not yet set a post-2025 target set**, and omits a company-wide no-deforestation commitment. A sustainability framework reaching its end-date with no visible successor is a **credibility gap**. |

**Score: 4/10.** Environmental management is genuinely good; the governance architecture is shareholder-unfriendly and the merger makes it worse for public holders.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD / CSDDD** — **High.** EU operations (Ducros, Schwartz, Kamis, Drogheria & Alimentari, Vahiné) + the Unilever Foods European portfolio make the combined entity a large EU undertaking subject to CSRD double-materiality reporting. **CSDDD** (supply chain due diligence with civil liability) is directly aimed at exactly MKC's weak point: agricultural supplier human rights and environmental diligence.
- **EUDR (EU Deforestation Regulation)** — **High and imminent.** Palm oil, cocoa, soy exposure plus the absence of a company-wide no-deforestation commitment. Post-merger, Unilever's stronger NDPE framework is the mitigant *if* it is adopted — which is precisely what investors are demanding.
- **US SEC climate disclosure rule** — **Medium, currently stayed.** Scope 1/2 and material Scope 3 disclosure obligations would apply if reinstated; MKC's SBTi-validated inventory means it is already substantially prepared.
- **California SB 253/261** — **Medium.** MKC is a large US entity likely above the revenue threshold; Scope 3 reporting required. Well-positioned given existing disclosure.
- **CBAM** — **Low.** No carbon-intensive imports (aluminium/steel/cement/fertiliser) at scale.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants** — **None identified**, but the $15.7bn cash component of the Unilever deal implies significant new leverage; if refinanced with sustainability-linked instruments, diligence KPIs become covenant-relevant. **Monitor.**
- **US state anti-ESG legislation** — **Low-Medium.** 20+ states restricting ESG considerations in investment/pension decisions; limited direct operating impact for a food company, but affects the shareholder base and index flows.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (environmentally Leader, governance Laggard)

- **Environmental:** Leader-adjacent on climate (SBTi-validated net-zero 2050); **Laggard** on deforestation commitments.
- **Social:** Average. Product-safety issues are being actively remediated; supply chain diligence is thin relative to the merged footprint.
- **Governance:** **Laggard.** Dual-class control preserved through a transaction that transfers 55.1% of economics to Unilever holders. This is the defining ESG characteristic of the security.

**Why not "ESG Risk":** The controversies are remediable, the carbon trajectory is independently validated, and no systemic licence-to-operate threat exists. **Why not "Leader":** The governance structure and the deforestation gap are disqualifying.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE (governance-led), with an environmental option**

**The thesis-critical point:** investors underwrite MKC post-close as a **35%-economic / control-retaining family vehicle** over a **55%-owned Unilever asset base**. Public shareholders bear the capital-allocation risk of the Unilever integration while holding structurally limited ability to influence strategy, board composition, or future capital returns. **This warrants a persistent governance discount to sector multiples and creates risk of exclusion from governance-screened and ESG-mandate funds.**

**Specific transmission to valuation:**
1. **Cost of capital:** Governance-screen exclusion narrows the marginal buyer base → higher required return. Dual-class firms with family control empirically trade at discount in consumer staples where index weight and ESG-mandate flows matter.
2. **Integration ESG risk is real cash risk:** EUDR/CSDDD compliance across a much larger agricultural sourcing base is not a virtue-signalling exercise — it is **market access in Europe**. The absence of a company-wide no-deforestation commitment is the specific, closable gap.
3. **Offsetting positive:** SBTi-validated climate targets + EtO phase-out completion + 100% sustainable sourcing for top ingredients give the combined entity a defensible base — and **Unilever's NDPE framework, if adopted, would upgrade MKC's weakest environmental link.**

**Actionable monitors before/after close:**
- **Adoption of Unilever's deforestation-free sourcing commitment and traceability standards** — the single highest-signal ESG catalyst.
- **Post-close board composition and whether dual-class sunset provisions are introduced.** Absent a sunset, maintain the governance discount.
- **New post-2025 sustainability target set** (the 2025 framework has expired with no visible successor).
- **Compensation alignment disclosure** — could not be verified (SEC/DEF 14A access blocked); resolve prior to committee conviction.

**Evidence gaps to disclose:** SEC filings (10-K risk factors, DEF 14A) were inaccessible via the available tools (HTTP 403); executive compensation alignment and precise board/committee composition are therefore **unverified** and should be confirmed from the EDGAR filings directly before finalising a governance-score-based position. Peer ESG score comparisons were not obtained.
