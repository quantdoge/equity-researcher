## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US tariff-regime volatility and the post-IEEPA vacuum
**Probability: High | Impact: Medium (~$70–140M cost exposure)**
- The **Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on Feb 20, 2026** (*Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump*, No. 24-1287), triggering refunds of up to ~$175B industry-wide.
- McCormick **received $29.2M of IEEPA refunds as of Aug 31, 2026** with a $1.6M receivable remaining; ProPublica tallies **$30.8M** recovered. Management explicitly stated the refund benefit will be **"offset with increased inflationary costs"** rather than drop to profit (Q3 FY2026 release).
- Pre-ruling exposure: **30% tariff on China imports and a 10% baseline tariff**, with the company citing a **~$140M tariff problem** and ~$70M of cost pressure; McCormick spent **$80K lobbying in Q3 2025** on food-import tariffs.
- **Residual risk:** refunds do not resolve the underlying trade policy. Tariffs re-imposed under Sections 122/232/301 remain a live threat, and a refund-enforcement/compliance overhang exists (CBP audit risk on refund claims).

### Risk 2 — Middle East conflict / Strait of Hormuz → freight & ingredient inflation
**Probability: High (active) | Impact: Medium**
- The **Iran war** has raised energy and freight costs and inflated ingredient prices; McCormick **redirected the $28M Q2 FY2026 refund to cover Hormuz-driven freight and ingredient inflation** rather than recognising profit.
- MKC's spice/herb sourcing is concentrated in climate- and conflict-exposed origins (India, Vietnam, Indonesia, Turkey, Madagascar vanilla, West African cocoa/vanilla). Hormuz disruption compounds both inbound freight and sourcing costs.
- Management cut FY2025 profit guidance explicitly citing "rising commodity costs triggered by global trade uncertainty and tariffs" (Reuters, Oct 2025).

### Risk 3 — Antitrust failure/conditionality on the $44.8B Unilever Foods combination
**Probability: Medium (Phase 2 reference ~40–50%) | Impact: High (deal-defining)**
- The transaction (signed **March 31, 2026**; Reverse Morris Trust; **$15.7B cash**; Unilever holders ~55.1%, MKC holders ~35.0%) is expected to close **by mid-2027**.
- **UK CMA Phase 1 decision announced November 11, 2026**: the deal "may harm UK competition," with **dry-mix/seasoning overlaps** identified. Parties had until **November 18, 2026** to offer undertakings-in-lieu (UILs) or face an **in-depth Phase 2 reference**. MKC shares fell ~2.1% on the warning.
- **US cleared:** FTC granted early termination of the HSR waiting period (transaction 20262304, **October 5, 2026**).
- Still required: **McCormick shareholder vote**, EU/other international clearances, and IRS comfort on RMT tax-free treatment. A Phase 2 reference plus mandated divestitures (e.g., UK dry mixes) is the base-case downside.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Merger-related**
- **Halper Sadeh LLC investigation** into the Unilever Foods transaction — probing whether MKC shareholders are receiving **fair value and adequate disclosure**; typical pre-vote M&A plaintiff-bar activity ahead of the proxy. Multiple Form 425 communications filed (e.g., July 23, 2026).
- **Retention award program** (Exhibit 10(xvi) to the Oct 1, 2026 10-Q): cash retention awards vesting 50% at closing / 50% at six months, or at a **June 8, 2027 anniversary date** if closing lags — a tell that closing outside mid-2027 is contemplated. Includes 280G/409A mechanics.
- **Tax structure risk:** RMT treatment requires IRS opinion; loss of tax-free status would be materially value-destructive for Unilever holders and is an underappreciated legal contingency.

**Antitrust**
- UK: CMA Phase 1 (launched 16 Sept 2026, decision 11 Nov 2026) — adverse; UIL deadline 18 Nov 2026.
- US: HSR early termination granted 5 Oct 2026.

**Corporate/other**
- **Delaware Chancery** precedent involving McCormick: claims by minority stockholders against a special committee for breach of fiduciary duty were **dismissed** (A&O Shearman M&A litigation blog) — a favourable precedent, likely connected to the McCormick de México minority buy-in.
- **January 2026:** completed acquisition of an **additional 25% of McCormick de México**, increasing ownership — raises related-party/minority-valuation litigation exposure in Mexico.
- **Product safety / labelling:** historical voluntary recalls (Italian Seasoning / Frank's RedHot Buffalo Ranch for *Salmonella*; FDA-terminated), 2024 cinnamon-lead applesauce industry notice, and **Consumer Reports** heavy-metals findings (arsenic, cadmium, lead) across spice brands including McCormick. FDA has set lead limits in cinnamon; **ASTA guidance levels** for heavy metals exist but no comprehensive FDA spice limits — a live rulemaking/tort risk.

**Regulatory posture:** FDA (food safety, labelling, heavy metals), FTC (advertising/substantiation), UK CMA, EU DG-COMP, and post-close a much larger multi-jurisdiction antitrust/food-law surface.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **MEDIUM** (with a **HIGH** deal-specific legal/regulatory overlay)

**Rationale:**
- **Positives:** ~71% of revenue from the Americas and ~19% from EMEA — overwhelmingly OECD, high rule-of-law jurisdictions. No sanctions, FCPA, or bribery exposure disclosed. US antitrust clearance already secured. Beta of 0.67 reflects the defensive, low-cyclicality nature of the franchise.
- **Negatives:** Three simultaneous, correlated stressors — (1) an unresolved US tariff regime following the IEEPA reversal, with ~$70–140M of cost at stake and only partial refund recovery; (2) Middle East/Hormuz-driven freight and ingredient inflation that is absorbing those refunds; (3) a **transformational but legally contested $44.8B merger** now facing an adverse UK CMA Phase 1 finding, a remedies deadline of Nov 18, 2026, shareholder litigation, and an emerging-market-heavy pro forma footprint.
- **Downgrade triggers to High:** CMA Phase 2 reference or mandated divestitures of core UK brands; re-imposition of broad tariffs under alternative statutory authority; adverse proxy-disclosure injunction ahead of the shareholder vote; or loss of RMT tax-free treatment.
- **Upgrade triggers to Low:** CMA accepts UILs in Q4 2026, closing stays on track for mid-2027, and tariff exposure is replaced by a stable, lower-rate regime.

**Bottom line for the investment memo:** MKC's *current* geopolitical risk is modest and characteristic of a defensive staples name. The risk is **forward-looking and event-driven** — concentrated in the Unilever Foods closing path (UK antitrust + shareholder vote + RMT tax) and in US trade policy, where the company has already booked ~$30M of refunds that it is spending on war-driven cost inflation rather than earning.
