## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

**Lead-sheathed cables (the dominant risk).** AT&T is the largest owner of legacy lead-clad telecom cables in the US. A 2023 *Wall Street Journal* investigation found lead leaching into water and soil. Industry remediation cost estimates range from **$10B–$26B (New Street Research)** to **as much as $60B for the sector** (analyst estimates cited by urgentcomm). AT&T's allocable share is plausibly **$10B–$20B+**, equal to roughly **7–13% of market cap** — a material, under-provisioned tail liability. AT&T disputes health risk findings and says it has mapped impacted locations; litigation and state/federal scrutiny continue. **This alone justifies a High environmental rating.**

**Carbon and energy.** AT&T reports a total footprint of ~**14.2 MtCO2e** (Scope 1, 2 and 3), with roughly a **52% reduction in Scope 1+2 emissions** versus its baseline and a **carbon-neutral-by-2035** target. It has procured substantial renewable energy (wind in Oklahoma/Texas) and offers customer emissions-reduction solutions (a 1-gigaton customer goal by 2035). Weaknesses: the target is not clearly validated by the Science Based Targets initiative (SBTi), Scope 3 disclosure granularity is limited, and network energy demand is rising with 5G/fiber/AI data-center growth. **Transition risk is moderate** because the US has no economy-wide carbon price; however, California's SB 253/261 and customer Scope 3 requirements are raising reporting and procurement costs.

**Physical climate risk.** AT&T's wireline and wireless infrastructure is concentrated in hurricane- and wildfire-prone regions. The company has developed a Climate Change Analysis Tool to map exposure, but hardening costs are real and rising. **Rating: High**, driven by lead cables; carbon and physical risk are medium-high but better managed.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

**Data privacy and cybersecurity (most material).** AT&T has suffered repeated, high-profile breaches:
- **2024 breach** affecting ~73M (some reports say up to 180M records across incidents); a **$177M class-action settlement** was approved in June 2025.
- **FCC $13M settlement** (2024) over a 2023 third-party breach exposing 8.9M customers, with mandated privacy reforms.
- **Salt Typhoon**: the Chinese state-sponsored group breached AT&T's network, including lawful-intercept systems (reported late 2024). This is a **national-security-grade incident** with potential congressional/FCC consequences, elevated IT remediation spending, and severe reputational exposure. AT&T says networks are secure, but the disclosure and notification record has been criticized.

Direct financial impact so far is ~**$190M in settlements/fines**, small versus ~$122B revenue, but the **tail risk** (class actions, regulatory penalties, churn, enterprise-customer loss) is significant. Cybersecurity is both a Social (customer harm) and Governance (board oversight) issue.

**Labour relations.** In 2024, **~17,000 CWA-represented workers struck across nine Southeast states**, accusing AT&T of unfair labour practices and bad-faith bargaining; federal mediation followed. AT&T has also announced **1,600+ CWA layoffs** and continued offshoring/outsourcing. A large share of the workforce is unionized. Strained relations raise service-quality, wage-cost, and strike-risk concerns — a **Medium-High** social risk.

**DEI rollback.** AT&T eliminated all DEI-focused roles and training in December 2025, following pressure from the Trump administration/FCC. New York City pension funds sued to force a shareholder vote on a diversity proposal; AT&T settled in February 2026 and allowed the vote. This creates **reputational risk with ESG-mandated investors and employees**, and signals political vulnerability — a **Medium** social/governance risk.

**Community and product.** Telecom has a social-licence dimension: broadband affordability, digital divide, and 911/network reliability. AT&T offers low-cost plans and participates in public-affairs programs, but the end of federal affordability subsidies (ACP) and network outages are recurring social concerns. **Overall Social: High.**

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**Positives:**
- **Independent Lead Director** (William Kennard, since Feb 2025) provides a counterweight; board has deep telecom, regulatory, and finance experience.
- **Strong shareholder support:** 90.7% say-on-pay at the prior meeting.
- **Responsive to shareholders:** settled the NYC pension-fund lawsuit and permitted a diversity-proposal vote (Feb 2026).
- Pay is majority equity (65%), aligned with TSR/ROIC metrics.

**Negatives:**
- **CEO–Chairman duality:** John Stankey holds both roles. This reduces board independence and is a notable red flag, even with a Lead Director.
- **High pay ratio (215:1)** and CEO compensation of **$29.9M** (up 13% YoY) may draw scrutiny, though say-on-pay support is strong.
- **Capital allocation tension:** high leverage (~$130B+ net debt), a large dividend, and buybacks constrain discretionary ESG/network-resilience investment.
- **Oversight questions:** repeated cyber breaches and the Salt Typhoon intrusion raise doubts about board-level cyber risk oversight; lead-cable disclosure/litigation management is also a governance test.
- **Political vulnerability:** the DEI rollback suggests stakeholder-risk management is reactive to political pressure rather than principled.

**Score rationale:** 6/10 — structurally average. Independent Lead Director and strong shareholder support offset the duality and high pay ratio, but cyber oversight and political reactivity keep it from Leader territory. **Governance is not a laggard, but it is not a strength.**

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

**United States (primary):**
- **FCC:** privacy/data-breach enforcement (recent $13M settlement), national-security/cyber rules (Salt Typhoon response), net-neutrality posture, service-quality oversight. High exposure.
- **SEC:** climate-disclosure rule is stayed; cybersecurity incident disclosure (8-K Item 1.05) applies. AT&T has had to disclose material breaches.
- **State PUCs / attorneys general:** rate cases, service quality, lead-cable remediation, consumer protection. California (CPUC) and Northeast states are most active on lead.
- **EPA / state environmental agencies:** lead-cable investigation and potential remediation orders.
- **California SB 253 (Scope 1–3 reporting) and SB 261 (climate risk):** AT&T is likely in scope given its size and California operations — raising compliance and assurance costs.
- **FTC:** privacy and advertising enforcement.

**International (limited):** AT&T's Mexico/Latin America operations face local telecom and data-privacy regulators; **EU CSRD and CBAM exposure is minimal** because AT&T's EU footprint is small. **Overall regulatory exposure: High** — telecom is among the most heavily regulated sectors, and cyber/lead liabilities add enforcement risk.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE (negative tilt / controversy overhang)**

AT&T is **not a Leader**. It has credible carbon targets, an independent Lead Director, and strong shareholder support — but these are offset by:
- A **potentially enormous lead-cable liability** (High/Critical).
- **Repeated cyber breaches** and a state-sponsored intrusion (High).
- **CEO–Chair duality** and a high pay ratio (governance concern).
- **Labour friction** and a **DEI rollback** that alienates ESG capital.

Relative to telecom peers (Verizon, T-Mobile), AT&T shares the lead-cable and cyber overhangs but carries more leverage and a more contested governance/stakeholder profile. **Rating: Average, with an "ESG Risk" flag on a controversy-adjusted basis.**

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL (negative bias for ESG-mandated capital)**

**For a general/thematic thesis:** ESG is **not the primary driver** of AT&T's valuation. Near-term price action is dominated by competition (Starlink Mobile fears drove a ~6% drop on 21 Oct 2026 despite an earnings beat), fiber growth, dividend sustainability, and leverage. ESG factors are a **modest negative overlay**, not a thesis-breaker.

**Key risks to monitor (material to cash flows/cost of capital):**
1. **Lead-cable remediation/litigation** — a credible multi-billion-dollar tail liability; any adverse regulatory or court outcome could impair capital and force dividend/buyback cuts.
2. **Cyber/Salt Typhoon** — potential fines, enterprise-customer attrition, and elevated IT spend; national-security scrutiny could constrain operations.
3. **Governance duality and DEI rollback** — may reduce demand from ESG-screened funds and increase proxy-contest risk.

**For ESG-mandated/sustainability-focused investors:** **NEGATIVE** — the lead-cable overhang, cyber record, and DEI reversal fail typical controversy and governance screens.

**Bottom line:** AT&T is an **Average ESG performer with outsized controversy tail risk**. The stock is investable on value/dividend grounds, but ESG-integrated investors should demand a discount for the unprovisioned lead-cable liability and cyber remediation costs. **ESG is a risk-management overlay, not a positive catalyst.**
