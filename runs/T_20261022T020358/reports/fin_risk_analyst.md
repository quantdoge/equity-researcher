# AT&T Inc. (NYSE: T) — Financial Risk Assessment

**Sector:** Communication Services / Telecom Services · **Price:** $21.91 · **Market cap:** ~$150.1B · **FY end:** 2025-12-31 (latest reported quarter Q3-2026)

---

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: INVESTMENT GRADE (lower tier) — BBB / Baa2 equivalent, stable bias; improving but leverage-sensitive.**

AT&T is one of the largest non-financial corporate borrowers globally, with **$173.99B gross debt** and **$155.75B net debt** at FY2025. The credit story is a multi-year deleveraging from the post-WarnerMedia peak, funded by the most reliable FCF stream in the sector. Reported net leverage of **2.85x** looks comfortably inside BBB territory, but that figure is flattered by a **~$5.7B one-off gain** booked in Q3-2025 (DirecTV-related, visible as Q3-25 EBITDA of $17.67B vs. a ~$12B quarterly run-rate). On a normalized basis I estimate **adjusted net debt/EBITDA of ~3.1–3.4x** — still investment grade, but with materially less headroom.

Interest coverage is the strongest single credit support: **operating income/interest of 3.67x** and **EBIT/interest of 4.97x** in FY2025, stable at 3.6–3.7x on an operating-income basis across 2023–2025. On an EBITDA basis coverage is ~8x reported / ~7x adjusted. The trend risk is not the level but the direction: quarterly interest expense rose from **$1.610B (Q1-25) to $1.979B (Q3-26), +23% YoY**, as legacy low-coupon debt is refinanced at current market yields. That is a structural, slow-burn credit headwind.

Rating detail: S&P affirmed **BBB** with a stable outlook (most recent confirmation located, April 2022); I could not verify post-2022 rating actions or current Moody's/S&P outlooks from a primary source in this run. Given FY2025 deleveraging and $18B+ FCF guidance, an upgrade bias is plausible, but the $33B YoY increase in gross debt (to $174B) and continued buybacks ($6.58B in 2025) argue against rapid improvement.

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis

**Base (TTM, Q3-26):** Revenue $128.9B; adjusted EBITDA ~$46–49B; FCF $17.8B; dividends $7.9B; interest ~$7.5B run-rate.

**Scenario A — Revenue -10% (mild recession, elasticity ~0.5):**
Revenue $116B; EBITDA margin compresses ~40% → ~34% → **EBITDA ~$39.4B**. Net leverage **4.0x**; EBIT ~$18.5B / interest $7.5B = **2.5x coverage**; FCF ~$11B. Dividend coverage ~1.4x — sustainable only with capex restraint. Rating outlook would turn negative; a downgrade to BBB- becomes a live risk.

**Scenario B — Revenue -20% (severe, sustained downturn):**
Revenue $103B; EBITDA margin ~30% → **EBITDA ~$30.9B**. Net leverage **~5.0x**; EBIT (after ~$20B D&A) ~$10.9B / interest $7.5B = **~1.45x coverage**; FCF ~$4–5B after interest, capex ($16B cut case) and cash taxes. **The ~$8B dividend would exceed FCF** → dividend cut or debt-funded payout. At 5.0x leverage, a sub-investment-grade outcome is credible, and the refinancing channel narrows precisely when ~$10–15B/yr of maturities need rolling. This is the tail scenario that matters: a bond-proxy equity with a 5% yield that cuts its dividend typically de-rates 25–40%.

**Mitigants:** Revenue is contract/subscription-based with low churn; ~$20B+ annual FCF even in stress; $18B cash; demonstrated access to unsecured bond markets ($6.5B multi-tranche deal closed Feb-2026); asset-sale optionality. **Aggravants:** ~$174B gross debt, rising coupon stack, buybacks consuming deleveraging capacity, and a working-capital deficit.

*Covenant note: specific maintenance covenants were not disclosed in available data. IG telecom indentures typically rely on incurrence tests and change-of-control puts rather than maintenance tests; the practical constraint is rating-agency tolerance (~3.5x) rather than a contractual trip-wire.*

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating

# MODERATE (upper end; negative watch items)

**Why not "High":** Investment-grade balance sheet, ~$18B FCF, 2x cash coverage of near-term maturities, 42% FCF dividend payout, and 8/9 Piotroski. The company can service obligations through a normal downturn without market access for 12–18 months.

**Why not "Low":** $174B gross debt, ~3.2x adjusted net leverage (not the reported 2.85x), negative working capital, a ~$7.5B and rising interest bill, Altman Z in the distress zone, and heavy refinancing dependence. The equity's 25% volatility and 29% drawdown confirm the market prices credit risk, not a utility-like stream.

**Triggers to HIGH:** adjusted net leverage >3.8x; interest coverage (EBIT) <2.5x; FCF < dividend payments; loss of IG rating; or a failed/expensive refinancing season.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **One-off-inflated EBITDA.** FY2025 EBITDA of $54.7B includes a ~$5.7B Q3-25 gain; adjusted leverage is ~3.2x, not 2.85x. The headline metric overstates credit quality by roughly a third of a turn.
2. **Rising interest expense.** Quarterly interest rose 23% YoY ($1.61B → $1.98B) as cheap legacy debt rolls into higher coupons — coverage will erode even with flat EBITDA. The yield-curve feed failed, so I could not quantify the current refinancing spread; this is a genuine gap.
3. **Re-levering despite asset sales.** Net debt rose ~$18B YoY while goodwill/intangibles fell ~$127B — proceeds and FCF are being directed to buybacks ($6.6B) rather than debt reduction. Capital allocation is the main swing factor for the rating.
4. **Liquidity optics.** Current ratio 0.91 and working capital -$5.0B; mitigated by $18.2B cash vs. $9.0B short-term debt, but thin if refinancing markets close.
5. **Altman Z-Score 0.87 (distress zone)** — model bias for asset-heavy telecoms (x1 penalizes negative working capital; x4 uses book equity), but even substituting market cap for equity gives only ~1.1, still weak.
6. **Data inconsistencies.** Q3-2026 income statement fields are internally contradictory (EBIT = 0, interest expense negative, net income +$0.855B while cash flow shows -$9.26B net income); Q3-2026 shows a ~$2.6B non-cash impairment per press reports. Quarterly comparisons should be treated with caution. Cost-of-revenue classification also shifts materially between years (42%–57% of revenue), making gross-margin trends unreliable — I used operating margin instead.
