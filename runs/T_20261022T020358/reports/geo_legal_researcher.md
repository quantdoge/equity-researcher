# AT&T Inc. (NYSE: T) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company:** AT&T Inc. | **Sector:** Communication Services / Telecommunications | **HQ:** Dallas, Texas | **Market cap:** ~$150.1B | **FY2025 revenue:** $125.648B (+2.7% YoY) | **Employees:** ~133,030 (≈43% unionized)

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

AT&T is an overwhelmingly **domestic US business**. Per the FY2025 Form 10-K (filed 2026-02-09), the Communications segment supplied **~97% of segment operating revenues**, while the Latin America segment (principally Mexico wireless) contributed **~3% of segment revenues and less than 1% of segment operating income**. AT&T has no material operations in Russia, China, or sanctioned jurisdictions; it exited most international video assets (DIRECTV Latin America/Vrio) years ago.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US regulatory/political discretion over spectrum and M&A approvals
**Probability: High (already materializing) | Impact: Medium**

The FCC and DOJ now exercise discretionary leverage over AT&T's largest capital allocations, and approvals have come with **non-obvious policy conditions**.

- AT&T's **$23B cash purchase of ~50 MHz of EchoStar spectrum** (≈30 MHz of 3.45 GHz mid-band + ≈20 MHz of 600 MHz low-band) sits inside EchoStar's **$40B** spectrum sale to SpaceX and AT&T. The FCC granted **conditional approval on 12 May 2026**, requiring EchoStar to establish a **$2.4B escrow** (Reuters; FCC DOC-421651A1; Fierce Network). Closing was expected mid-2026.
- The FCC approved AT&T's **$1.02B UScellular spectrum purchase only after AT&T agreed to end its DEI programs** (reported via FCC coverage) — a clear signal that approval is conditioned on political/ideological alignment.
- The **$5.75B Lumen Mass Markets fiber acquisition** (≈1M fiber subscribers across 11 states) closed in **February 2026** (thedesk.net; ChannelVision), expanding the regulated footprint and state-PUC exposure.

**Why it matters:** AT&T's growth plan depends on spectrum and fiber M&A that requires federal sign-off. A hostile or conditions-heavy FCC/DOJ posture can delay closings, impose divestitures/behavioral conditions, or extract policy concessions. The Capitol Forum notes FCC/DOJ could even kill part of the spectrum deal without court approval.

### Risk 2 — Mexico policy, regulatory reset and US–Mexico trade friction
**Probability: Medium | Impact: Low**

AT&T Mexico is the country's #2/#3 wireless operator (24.7M subs) and the entirety of the LATAM segment.

- Mexico has **published a law dissolving the IFT**, its telecom regulator, to be replaced by a new agency — creating a multi-year gap in regulatory precedent and enforcement posture (IB-Lenhardt).
- América Móvil's "preponderant agent" asymmetric measures remain contested; Mexico's Supreme Court sided with the regulator on the label (Datafloq). A shifting regulatory framework changes the competitive handicap AT&T Mexico relies on.
- US–Mexico trade tension is live: **tariffs on Mexican imports were floated at 30%** and tariff/trade policy remains volatile (Legal500; Houston Chronicle). A US recession or tariff shock would hit Mexican consumer spending and the peso, which flows straight into USD-reported LATAM revenue (LATAM revenue was up 7.1% YoY in Q3, partly FX-driven — 5G Americas).

**Why it matters:** limited, because LATAM is <1% of segment operating income — AT&T Mexico has historically been loss-making/breakeven. The exposure is to headline sentiment more than to cash flow.

### Risk 3 — US–China tech decoupling, tariffs on network equipment, and state-backed satellite competition
**Probability: Medium–High | Impact: Medium**

- AT&T's **$22–23B annual capex** (guided for 2027–2028) is exposed to tariff-driven cost inflation on steel, aluminum, electronics and fiber/wireless equipment. The FY2025 10-K explicitly flags "inflation, interest rates, **supply chain pressures**" and "evolving telecom regulation" as material risks. *Gap: targeted tariff-exposure searches returned no AT&T-specific quantification.*
- Huawei/ZTE exclusion is already embedded in US policy (rip-and-replace), so incremental decoupling risk is mainly **cost and lead-time**, not market access.
- The competitive-politics overlay: **SpaceX/Starlink** is both a licensed spectrum rival and a politically connected actor. AT&T shares fell ~5–6% on 21 Oct 2026 despite a Q3 beat and raised 2026 EPS outlook (~$2.40) purely on Starlink Mobile competition fears (Invezz; 24/7 Wall St.; Benzinga). Regulatory tilting toward satellite direct-to-device could erode AT&T's rural/mobility economics.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Data breach / privacy litigation and enforcement (active, recurring):**
- **$177M class-action settlement** covering two 2024 breaches — $149M for the initial class action plus ~$28M — with a claims deadline of 18 Nov 2025; *In re AT&T Inc. Customer Data Security Breach Litigation* (N.D. Tex., 3:24-cv-…), with docket activity into 2026 (CourtListener; Yahoo Finance; NewsNation).
- **$13M FCC consent decree** resolving the 2023 vendor cloud breach that exposed ~8.9M customers (Telecompetitor; InfoSecurity Magazine; ComplexDiscovery).
- Historical pattern of FCC privacy enforcement — a **$25M** settlement in 2015 tied to breaches at call centers in **Mexico, Colombia and the Philippines** (Lexology/STB Law) — establishing AT&T as a repeat privacy-enforcement target.

**Legacy lead-clad cable exposure (unquantified tail risk):**
- *In re AT&T Securities Litigation* (3:24-cv-01196) — investor claims that AT&T misled on lead-cable risks; a federal judge **dismissed** the suit (Inside Towers; CaseMine).
- A **shareholder derivative suit** alleging executives/directors knew of lead-cable risks (Law360).
- **Lake Tahoe settlement**: AT&T agreed to remove **8 miles of lead-clad cable**, resolving a California Proposition 65 suit; remediation was later paused pending regulator engagement (Reno Gazette-Journal; MarketWatch). AT&T maintains the cables "pose no public health risk."
- **Residual risk:** state AGs, EPA and plaintiff firms continue to probe; nationwide remediation cost is the single largest unquantified legal contingency.

**Network reliability / 911 enforcement:** FCC has repeatedly fined AT&T for 911 and network-outage rule violations (Gizmodo; National Law Review), a persistent compliance-cost channel.

**Mexico regulatory/competition:** América Móvil faces record fines (~$1B federal competition fine; MXN 90.6M Telcel fine) and "dominant agent" measures; the IFT's dissolution introduces enforcement uncertainty for AT&T Mexico (Telecompaper; Latin Lawyer; Access Partnership).

**Antitrust:** No confirmed active DOJ or SEC investigation into AT&T surfaced in searches. Searches for a current DOJ/SEC probe returned only historical material (Time Warner, 2018) — I treat this as **no known active federal investigation**, with the caveat that absence of evidence is not evidence of absence.

**Labor:** ~43% of the workforce is unionized (CWA/IBEW), with **several key contracts expiring during 2026** — strike/negotiation risk that is domestic-political rather than cross-border.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Country-level geopolitical risk is genuinely **low** — ~96% US revenue, ~3% Mexico, no sanctioned-market presence, no Russia/China asset exposure. The composite rating is driven upward by **regulatory and legal intensity**, not by country risk: a discretionary FCC/DOJ that attaches policy conditions to $23B+ spectrum and fiber approvals, three separate data-breach enforcement episodes totaling ~$190M+, an unquantified nationwide lead-cable remediation tail, ~43% unionized labor with 2026 contract expirations, and a Mexican regulatory regime in transition.

**What would change the rating:**
- **Upgrade to Low** if lead-cable liability is definitively capped/dismissed, breach remediation matures without new incidents, and the EchoStar spectrum deal closes without onerous conditions.
- **Downgrade to High** if a nationwide lead-cable remediation mandate emerges (multi-billion dollar), if the FCC imposes structural conditions on the EchoStar or future spectrum deals, or if US–Mexico tariff/security friction materially disrupts AT&T Mexico.
