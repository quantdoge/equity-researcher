# AT&T Inc. (T) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment

**Company:** AT&T Inc. | Communication Services / Telecommunications Services | US (Dallas, TX)
**Market cap:** ~$150.1B | **Price:** $21.91 | **Employees:** 133,030 | **CEO:** John T. Stankey | **Beta:** 0.43
**Assessment date:** October 2026 (latest data: Q3 2026 results, 10-Q filed 10/21/2026)

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE** (structurally strong, track-record weak)

**Positive evidence:**
- **Independent board with separated Chair/CEO.** The 2026 director Form 4 cohort lists nine non-employee directors (Kennard, Ubinas, Taylor, Rose, Mooney, McCallister, Mayer, Luczo, Grier) plus newly seated Fazal Merchant (Form 3, 9/16/2026) — i.e. 9 of 10 directors independent, with William Kennard as independent Chairman and Stankey as CEO only. No dual-class share structure; one share, one vote.
- **Say-on-Pay passed with 93.05% support** at the 2026 annual meeting (AT&T, "Preliminary Results of 2026 Annual Meeting"). This is well above the ~90% threshold that proxy advisors treat as a warning line, and notably strong in a 2026 season where at least six U.S. issuers failed Say-on-Pay outright (Boardroom Alpha scorecard, 5/21/2026). Board nominees were unanimously recommended and carried.
- **No insider-driven governance alarm:** no open-market insider selling cluster (see §3), no evidence of related-party or control transactions in the data reviewed.
- **Board refreshment is live** (new director seated September 2026), suggesting the board is not static.

**Negative evidence / concerns:**
- **Capital-allocation track record is poor.** The WarnerMedia acquisition and reversal, the DirecTV misadventure, and the ~$180B+ debt load accumulated under prior management destroyed substantial shareholder value. The current "advanced connectivity" simplification (DirecTV exit, AT&T Mexico sale announced 10/13/2026) is a corrective strategy, but the board oversaw the value destruction that necessitated it. This is the single largest governance discount.
- **Governance under regulatory duress.** The FCC conditioned approval of AT&T's ~$1.02B UScellular spectrum purchase on AT&T **ending its DEI programs** (Broadband Breakfast, 12/3/2025; FCC approval reported 12/3/2025). Whatever one's view of DEI, a board accepting policy conditions as the price of a licence transfer is a governance precedent worth monitoring — it signals the regulator now has leverage over corporate social policy, and it exposes AT&T to employee, ESG-investor, and reverse-discrimination litigation risk.
- **Insider alignment is weak in practice.** No director or officer open-market purchase in the last 90 days despite a share price (~$21.91) near the bottom of its two-year range. Compensation is delivered almost entirely as equity awards and options — alignment on paper, absent conviction in behaviour.
- **CEO concentration:** Stankey is CEO and President and the public architect of the strategy. No disclosed heir-apparent or succession plan in the materials retrieved.

**Data gap:** The 10-K and DEF 14A could not be retrieved (tool returned "No 10-K found" and "No DEF 14A found"), so I could not verify CEO pay ratio, pay-vs-performance metrics, board tenure/skills matrix, or the litigation footnote directly. My governance view rests on voting results, director Form 4 rosters, and public reporting.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **HIGH — elevated and recurring**

AT&T is a repeat enforcement target. The pattern, not any single action, is the risk.

| Matter | Status | Quantum |
|---|---|---|
| **FCC privacy/CPNI forfeiture** (data security failures) | Forfeiture order issued April 2024 | **$57,265,625** |
| **2024 data breach class action** (Snowflake cloud warehouse; ~100M+ call/text records + 73M-record leak) | **$177M settlement**, preliminary approval granted; claims deadline 11/18/2025 | **$177M** |
| **911 outage fines** (multiple outages blocking 911 calls) | FCC settled with AT&T and peers; AT&T individually fined $950K (Aug 2024), following an earlier **$5.25M** 911 penalty; FCC total across carriers ~$6M | **~$6M+** |
| **SEC Regulation FD** — selective disclosure to analysts | SEC charged AT&T and three IR executives (2021); subsequently settled | Low-8 figures (scale) |
| **Lead-sheathed cable** (legacy lead-clad network, 2,000+ cables per WSJ investigation) | Congressional/Senate pressure on FCC (2023); securities class actions filed vs. AT&T and Verizon. **No 2025/26 resolution found** | **Unquantified — potentially billions** |
| **EchoStar spectrum ($23B)** | FCC approved (~5/12/2026); AT&T argued to DOJ the deal does not harm competition (11/26/2025) | Antitrust clearance effectively obtained |
| **AT&T Mexico sale** to Grupo Televisa / Fintech Holdings | Announced 10/13/2026; **pending Mexican regulatory approval** | Execution/approval risk |

**Mitigants:** EDGAR full-text search for SEC enforcement actions naming T returned **zero active hits**, and both major spectrum transactions have cleared FCC review. The 2024 breach exposure is now quantified and largely reserved/settled.

**Aggravators:** Three separate FCC enforcement actions in roughly 18 months (privacy, 911 reliability ×2) is regulatory recidivism. Each new violation raises the probability of harsher consent-decree conditions (compliance monitors, mandatory reporting) and increases the cost of the next spectrum or merger approval. The lead-cable liability remains an open, unquantified tail risk that management has historically downplayed.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Cybersecurity and third-party vendor concentration — the highest-severity risk.** AT&T has now suffered repeated, large-scale breaches: ~73M customer records (7.6M current / 65.4M former) exposed in 2024; a separate Snowflake-cloud breach exposing call and text records of "nearly all" wireless customers, disclosed July 2024; a 2021-era leak of 70M+ records. These produced a **$177M class settlement plus a $57.3M FCC forfeiture** — direct evidence that controls have failed more than once. In September 2025, attackers publicly claimed **live access to AT&T infrastructure affecting 24M users**, including the ability to bypass SMS-based 2FA and conduct SIM-swap attacks (Cybernews, 9/1/2025, updated 9/3/2025). AT&T had not confirmed this claim; treat as unverified but high-consequence. A network operator holding call-detail records, location data, and SIM-control infrastructure is a Tier-1 cyber target, and breach fatigue erodes both trust and the credibility of future disclosures.

2. **Network reliability and technology-failure exposure — life-safety grade.** The February 2024 nationwide wireless outage (root cause: a network configuration error during expansion) took ~12 hours to fully restore and drew FCC scrutiny; multiple 911 outages followed, drawing fines. For a carrier, reliability failure is not a service-quality issue but a public-safety and regulatory one. Separately, AT&T issued an urgent iOS 27 software-update warning to iPhone 18 Pro Max customers on its network (Fast Company, 10/2/2026), a reminder that device-interoperability failures sit partly outside AT&T's control. The build programme — >60M fiber locations by 2030 at **$22–23B annual capex** (lowered for 2027–28) — concentrates execution risk in construction labour, permitting, and supplier delivery.

3. **Labour relations and key-person dependency.** AT&T is heavily unionised (~133K employees). The CWA "Orange" Mobility contract was a live strike risk through early 2026: members authorised a walkout before the 3/20/2026 expiry, the contract was extended week-by-week, and a **four-year agreement with wage increases and job-security provisions was subsequently ratified** (CWA District 3). The 2024 Southeast strike lasted 30 days. Risk is currently **resolved but recurring** — the next bargaining cycle is a scheduled disruption. On key-person risk, strategy, capital allocation, and the fiber/5G pivot are personally identified with CEO Stankey, with no disclosed succession plan.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

**Negative drivers:**
- **Data-breach reputational fatigue.** Three breach cycles in five years, ~$234M in combined settlement/forfeiture costs, and a public $177M claims process have made AT&T a byword for telecom data insecurity. Churn and brand-trust damage are the real cost, and they compound with each new claim — including the unverified 24M-user claim.
- **Politically coerced DEI rollback.** Ending DEI programs to win FCC approval (December 2025) is a brand and workforce event with two-sided risk: backlash from ESG-oriented investors and employees on one side, and reverse-discrimination litigation exposure on the other. It also invites further regulatory leverage in future approvals.
- **Legacy lead-cable controversy.** The 2023 WSJ investigation into 2,000+ lead-sheathed cables generated "corporate irresponsibility" framing in Congress and securities litigation. Even without a 2025/26 enforcement action in the public record, this is an un-extinguished environmental/health narrative that reactivates with any state AG action or media follow-up.
- **Management tone on competition.** After the October 2026 Starlink/SpaceX spectrum shock sent T down ~6% on an earnings beat (Invezz, 10/21/2026), CEO Stankey publicly dismissed the threat — "LEO constellations are fantastic for delivering bandwidth to rural areas" (Business Insider, 10/21/2026); Musk's response: "they don't understand." Public dismissiveness of a credible competitive threat is a credibility risk with investors, and the market has already voted.

**Positive offsets — genuinely material:** Q3 2026 was operationally strong — best consolidated service revenue growth in over four years, **>1M Advanced Connectivity net adds for a second consecutive quarter**, ~350K converged relationships added, 2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised to ~$2.40, and capex guidance cut for 2027–28. Execution credibility on the fiber strategy is improving, and the AT&T Mexico exit sharpens the U.S.-focused story. These offset but do not neutralise the trust-related risks.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale.** AT&T carries an unusually dense concentration of non-financial risk for a large-cap: **regulatory recidivism** (three FCC actions in ~18 months, ~$234M in quantified penalties), **repeat cybersecurity failure** at national-infrastructure scale, an **unquantified lead-cable tail liability**, **politically entangled governance** following the DEI-conditioned spectrum approval, and a **credible new competitive threat** that management is publicly minimising. Governance is structurally sound (9/10 independent, independent chair, 93% Say-on-Pay), labour risk is currently settled, and both major spectrum deals have cleared FCC/antitrust review — which is why this is High rather than Critical. There is **no active DOJ or SEC investigation surfaced** in EDGAR full-text search, and the largest known liabilities ($177M settlement, $57.3M FCC forfeiture) are quantified and largely behind the company.

**Watch items that could move this to Critical:** confirmation of the September 2025 "live infrastructure access" claim; a state AG enforcement action on lead cables; or a consent decree imposing a compliance monitor following further 911/network failures.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Unverified September 2025 breach claim (24M users, claimed live access, SIM-swap/2FA bypass).** Not confirmed by AT&T and not corroborated in the 2026 news flow — but if substantiated it is a Critical-severity event. *Action: require IR/security confirmation that no material incident occurred in Q3/Q4 2026, and confirm the $177M settlement covers only the 2024 events.*
2. **Regulatory recidivism.** FCC privacy forfeiture ($57.3M, April 2024) + two 911-outage penalties within ~18 months. Escalating penalty severity and consent-decree risk; also raises the political price of future approvals.
3. **DEI rollback as a licence condition.** A regulator extracting social-policy changes as the price of spectrum approval is a governance precedent and a two-sided reputational/litigation exposure. *Action: monitor shareholder proposals, employee litigation, and state AG responses for the 2027 proxy.*
4. **Zero open-market insider buying at a multi-year-low share price** (~$21.91 vs. ~$25 in August 2026). No fraud signal — but no conviction signal either, at the exact moment management is asking investors to look past the Starlink selloff.
5. **Lead-sheathed cable liability remains unquantified.** No reserve figure or 2025/26 resolution could be located. *Action: obtain the 10-K litigation and environmental footnote — my 10-K retrieval failed ("No 10-K found").*
6. **Management's public dismissal of the SpaceX/Starlink direct-to-cell threat** after a 6% selloff on an earnings beat. Tone risk: if LEO direct-to-cell takes rural/enterprise share, the credibility cost of having dismissed it compounds the fundamental cost.
