# HLN (Haleon plc) — Forensic Short-Seller Analysis

**Price ~$9.27 (ADR, 30-Jun-2026) · Market cap ~$37.7B · Consumer Health (defensive) · UK-domiciled, USD ADR**

*Framing note: My mandate is to build the best possible bear case. On HLN the bear case is real but thin — this is a clean-cash, low-growth compounder, not a fraud candidate. I lay out the strongest red flags, then tell you honestly why they don't clear the bar for a short.*

| FCF / Net income | 1.21x | 1.42x | 1.68x | Excellent |

| Gross margin | 63.4% | 61.9% | 61.6% | **Expanding** — pricing power intact |
| Beneish M-Score | **−2.457** | — | — | "Low risk" (well below −2.22) |
| Piotroski F-Score | **9/9** | — | — | Strong across all nine tests |

Cash earnings exceed reported earnings every year, accruals are negative (not the aggressive-positive signature of manipulation), and margins are rising while revenue falls — the mark of genuine pricing power rather than volume stuffing. **No evidence of revenue-recognition fraud or capitalisation games.**

One data caveat: the composite "earnings quality score" returned **38/100**, which directly contradicts its own inputs (1.40x cash conversion, 1.21x FCF conversion, negative accruals). I treat that composite as unreliable here and lean on the auditable components, which are all high quality.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

1. **Receivables ballooning against falling revenue — the single strongest flag.** DSO has climbed from **44 days (2023) → 62 (2024) → 70 (2025)** — a 26-day extension in two years while revenue went flat-to-down. AR grew **+40.8%** in 2024 and **+11.3%** in 2025 against revenue of −0.6% and −1.8%. Classic channel-stuffing signature. *Mitigant:* operating cash flow stayed robust and working capital was only a modest +£250M tailwind in 2025 — if this were pure stuffing, cash would have visibly cracked. More likely a mix/factoring/timing effect, but it bears watching in the next two prints.
2. **78% of the balance sheet is goodwill and intangibles.** £25.57B of £32.57B total assets — roughly **157% of book equity**. This is spin-off residue (brands capitalised). It is a recurring impairment and write-down risk, and it makes book-value-based metrics (Altman Z = 1.887, "Grey") misleading.
3. **Revenue is shrinking on a reported basis** (−1.8% 2025, −0.6% 2024). FX and portfolio pruning, but the top line offers no operating leverage tailwind.
4. **Leverage**: net debt £7.27B (~1.5x EBITDA). Improving (from £9.76B in 2022) but a persistent overhang from the leveraged spin.
5. **Insider filings unavailable** — no US Form 4 data (foreign private issuer; no 10-K/20-F retrievable via feed). This is a genuine data gap: I could not confirm or refute net insider selling. Stated explicitly rather than assumed clean.

## 3. Beneish & Accruals Analysis

- **M-Score −2.457 → "Low risk."** Every sub-index is at or near neutral: DSRI 1.134 (slightly elevated — echoes the receivables point), GMI 0.976 (margins improving), AQI 1.028, SGI 0.982 (sales dipping), TATA **−0.0201** (negative total accruals — the cleanest possible manipulation signal). No manipulation fingerprint.
- **Accruals ratio −2.0%** and consistent across four years (−2% to −3%). Cash conversion >100% every year. This is the opposite of a red flag.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment — **Moderate, not acute**

- **Private label / store brands** in oral care (Sensodyne) and vitamins (Centrum) is the structural threat — but gross margin *expanding* to 63.4% shows Haleon is holding price, not ceding it.
- **GLP-1 fear is likely mispriced in the bears' favour, not against them.** The consensus worry — weight-loss drugs kill demand for OTC/vitamins — is not supported: GLP-1 users spend *more* on protein, vitamins, supplements and digestive health, and Haleon is already marketing products to counter GLP-1 side-effects (constipation, nutrition) in the US. For HLN, GLP-1 reads as net-neutral-to-tailwind. **This removes a pillar of the naive bear case.**
- Category is mature/low-growth, which caps upside but also protects downside.

## 5. Management & Governance

- **Zantac (ranitidine) litigation overhang** — the one genuine governance/legal risk. Largely ring-fenced: GSK settled ~80,000 US state-court cases (≈93%) for up to $2.2B in 2024, with indemnity arrangements across GSK/Pfizer/Haleon. A Delaware Supreme Court reversal (Jul-2025) reopened some procedural questions. Residual tail risk, not a company-threatening liability.
- No auditor-change, related-party, or promotional-language red flags surfaced in available data.
- Capital returns orderly: dividend ~£600M + buyback ~£640M in 2025, funded from FCF, not debt.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — Is there a credible bear case?

**The best bear case:** a low-single-digit-growth consumer staple trading at ~18–20x earnings / ~12–13x EV/EBITDA, with a shrinking reported top line, a 78%-intangible balance sheet vulnerable to impairment, receivables quality deteriorating (DSO +26 days), and a residual litigation tail. If organic growth decelerates below ~3%, the multiple compresses and the stock drifts back toward the $7–8 floor it visited in 2023 and mid-2026.

**Why it doesn't work as a short:**
- **Cash is real.** 140% cash conversion, negative accruals, F-score 9. This is the antithesis of an accounting short.
- **Valuation is not stretched** — ~12–13x EV/EBITDA and ~18x earnings for a defensive, 63%-gross-margin franchise is fair, not euphoric. There is no multiple air-pocket to exploit.
- **The GLP-1 narrative cuts in HLN's favour**, removing the most fashionable bear argument.
- **Balance sheet is deleveraging**, margins expanding, and the dividend/buyback are FCF-covered.
- **Beta 0.245** — a defensive name with no catalyst for a violent re-rating lower. Shorting low-beta staples is a negative-carry trade that bleeds on borrow and dividend.

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **Reject Short Thesis (No Edge)**

HLN fails every test that makes a forensic short worth the carry. The accounting is clean (Beneish low-risk, accruals negative, F-score 9), cash generation is exemplary, and margins are expanding. The two real flags — **receivables DSO creep** and the **intangible-heavy balance sheet** — are worth monitoring as impairment/quality watch-items, not short triggers. The bear case is a *low-growth de-rating* argument, and at ~12–13x EV/EBITDA that de-rating has largely already happened (the stock sits mid-range of its two-year $7–11 band).

**If forced to act:** the only defensible bear expression is a *mild underweight / relative-value short vs. a higher-growth staples peer*, triggered by a further DSO extension beyond ~75 days or an organic-growth guide cut below 3%. That is a monitor-and-wait setup, not a conviction short today.

**One genuine data gap to flag for the memo:** insider-transaction and full annual-report risk-factor text were not retrievable through the feed (foreign private issuer filings). My governance read is therefore based on secondary sources and should be verified against the latest annual report before any position.
