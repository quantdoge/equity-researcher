# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Haleon plc (NYSE: HLN)

**Company snapshot:** UK-domiciled consumer healthcare pure-play (Sensodyne, Advil, Panadol, Voltaren, Centrum, Theraflu, Otrivin); mkt cap ~$37.7bn; ~24.5k employees; CEO Brian McNamara; spun out of GSK/Pfizer, ADR-listed July 2022. FY2025 organic revenue growth 3.0% to ~£11.0bn.

**Data caveat (material):** HLN's SEC metadata returned empty (foreign private issuer — 20-F/6-K only), no Form 4 records were retrievable, and haleon.com PDFs plus sec.gov archives returned 403/"Challenge Validation" blocks. Findings below are triangulated from press releases, regulatory sites (FDA, Belgian Competition Authority), litigation trackers and news. The specific mechanics of the Zantac indemnity (cap, covered periods, recorded provision) could **not** be verified from primary filings — this is the single largest gap and should be closed before sizing the position.

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate (trending positive)**

- **Roles split:** Independent Chair **Vindi Banga** (ex-Unilever; succeeded Sir Dave Lewis on 1 Jan 2026) with McNamara as CEO — no CEO/Chair duality.
- **Board independence:** Policy requires ≥50% of the Board excluding the Chair to be independent NEDs; **Alan Stewart** is Senior Independent Director (effective 1 Jan 2026). Standard UK-Code-plus architecture, unitary board, KPMG auditor.
- **Shareholder alignment improved:** The **final Pfizer sell-down (March 2025)** removed the last legacy controlling holder — no parent overhang, no related-party drag from GSK/Pfizer.
- **Remuneration:** 2026 AGM — all resolutions passed (turnout ~84.8%); Directors' Remuneration Report 98.0% in favour, but the **new Remuneration Policy drew ~5.4% against** — modest but non-trivial dissent that bears watching if the growth slowdown persists.
- **Weak spots:** Very short standalone track record (4 years); heavy board/exec turnover in 2025–26 (new Chair, new Senior Independent Director, board/committee changes effective 1 Jan 2026, CMO exit + first-ever Chief Growth Officer and Chief Transformation Officer created, six new operating units implemented by mid-2026). Governance is sound on paper; institutional maturity is still thin.

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis: **Neutral — no signal either way**

- **No retrievable Form 4 data** (FPI files 6-K PDMR notices instead; EDGAR tool returned zero filings).
- The only PDMR dealings found for 2026 are **token dividend-reinvestment purchases** by Senior Independent Director Alan Stewart (498 shares on 14 May 2026; **242 shares on 17 Sep 2026 at £3.3786**). These are administratively driven, not conviction signals.
- No evidence of meaningful open-market **buying** by the CEO/CFO during the 2026 share-price weakness — mild negative in itself.
- No evidence of insider **selling** clusters found; Pfizer's exit (completed March 2025) was a legacy overhang clearance, not an insider signal.
- **Verdict:** management is not signalling confidence with personal capital. I would not treat the absence of buying as a red flag, but it removes an expected corroborating signal.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Manufacturing/packaging quality control (highest operational risk).** Repeated contamination-driven recalls (microbial in Robitussin 2024/25; coolant ingress in Gas-X 2026) point to systemic QA and equipment-maintenance weakness across a heavily outsourced manufacturing base. Each event is cheap alone; cumulative FDA scrutiny, consent-decree risk and retailer confidence damage are not.
2. **Execution risk from simultaneous restructuring and demand softness.** New CGO/Chief Transformation Officer, six new operating units, CMO departure — all landing while Q3 2026 organic growth of **2.6% missed expectations**, North America softened on weak cold/flu, and FY guidance was trimmed. Growth is **price-led (+2.5% price, ~flat/negative volume)** per H1 disclosure; Jefferies flagged a 70bp margin beat as reviving doubt over **under-investment behind brands**. That is a "harvest now, pay later" pattern.
3. **Key-person/concentration risk.** CEO McNamara is the architect of the standalone story and the public face of guidance; the CFO seat has been stable but the executive layer beneath is being rebuilt. Customer concentration in US retail (Walmart, Target, drug chains) gives large buyers leverage over the very shelf economics that Belgium showed can be legally hazardous.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Moderate**

- **Mixed-to-negative narrative.** Headlines in 2026: *"Haleon slides as quality of growth questioned"* (Jul 2026); JPMorgan put HLN on **"negative watch"** into Q3; sell-side split (2 sell / 1 hold / 3 buy; consensus "Hold"). Analysts are framing Haleon as a **price-taker reliant on price increases** into a cost-of-living squeeze — a politically exposed posture for an everyday-essentials company.
- **Recall headlines** are frequent enough to create a "quality question mark" brand narrative (Robitussin, Gas-X), though no single event has been severe.
- **Positives:** commercial execution (better Walmart/Target shelf positions, Sep 2026), GLP-1 side-effect portfolio pivot, UBS flagging early flu season as a 2027 tailwind, broad trust in the brand stable. No activist campaign, no ESG scandal, no whistleblower complaint surfaced.
- **Brushed-off risk:** the Belgian cartel fine received little market attention. Consumer/retailer trust damage from a *cartel* finding in OTC pharmacy is a slow-burn reputational item, not a closed one.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Governance is genuinely good (split roles, independent chair, legacy holders gone, pay approved with only modest dissent), the business is diversified and low-beta (0.245), and there is no fraud indicator, no SEC/DOJ action, and no data-privacy fine on record. Offsetting this: a European competition-law conviction, a repeating product-quality pattern, a mid-restructuring leadership layer during a demand slowdown, and — above all — an **unquantified litigation indemnity** whose trigger date is the 11th Circuit's pending ruling.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Zantac indemnity tail (escalate immediately).** Haleon is economically exposed to GSK/Pfizer's OTC Zantac liabilities via indemnities for which **both counterparties have served notice of potential claims**. Two live appellate paths can revive exposure in 2026: the **11th Circuit** decision (expected 2H 2026) on the Daubert exclusion, and the plaintiffs' appeal of the April 2026 Delaware summary judgment. GSK alone has paid **$2.2bn** to resolve ~80,000 state-court claims. The indemnity's cap, period boundaries and any recorded provision could not be verified — **do not model this risk until the 2025 Form 20-F contingent-liability note is read directly.** If the 11th Circuit reverses, escalate the overall rating to **HIGH**.
2. **Belgian cartel fine (€4.81m)** — precedent-setting category-management decision in OTC pharmacy. Any repeat conduct in other EU markets (Hungary/Italy pharmacy-channel scrutiny is ongoing across the sector) would escalate reputational and financial risk disproportionately to the fine size.
3. **Repeat recall pattern** — three US nationwide recalls in ~2.5 years with documented root causes in packaging/process control. Request FDA Form 483/inspection history and the internal QA remediation timeline; consider whether this is a contract-manufacturer governance problem.
4. **"Quality of growth" degradation** — price-led growth with negative volume, a trimmed FY26 outlook, and margin beats partly funded by under-investment. Watch for a share-loss/price-elasticity inflection; this converts from a commercial issue to a governance issue if incentive metrics reward margin over volume.
5. **Absence of insider buying and empty EDGAR/Form 4 coverage** — treat as a disclosure-coverage gap; obtain RNS director-dealing history directly before concluding on insider sentiment.
