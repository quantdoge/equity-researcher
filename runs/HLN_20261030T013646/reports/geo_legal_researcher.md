### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — China regulatory reclassification of Centrum (already crystallised; highest near-term impact)**
- Chinese authorities changed the raw-material classification for Centrum OTC products, forcing Haleon to **suspend imports of those products into China** and make portfolio changes (Reuters, 29 Oct 2026).
- CEO McNamara: "there is not going to be a short-term solution for this. And frankly, we don't see, as we sit here now, necessarily a solution in 2027."
- **Probability of continued drag: >85%** (management-guided). **Impact: High** — this single item, alongside a delayed flu season, drove the FY2026 organic growth guide cut to ~2.5% from 3–5% and the **biggest-ever one-day share fall (~15%)**, worst performer in UK/European benchmarks.
- Read-through: China's tightening of cross-border e-commerce and health-product import rules is a *structural*, not cyclical, regulatory shift for imported VMS brands.

**Risk 2 — Middle East war / Iran conflict: cost shock and consumer-sentiment channel**
- Haleon explicitly warned in April 2026 of **rising costs sparked by the Iran conflict** while holding guidance (Reuters, 29 Apr 2026). By Q3 2026 the CEO attributed worsened consumer sentiment to "the effects of the Iran war."
- Macro backdrop: World Bank sees **commodity prices +23% in 2026** on energy/shipping disruption; Brent hit ~$116/bbl; record VLCC/maritime incidents in the Strait of Hormuz (Oct 2026).
- **Probability of continued cost pressure through 2027: High. Impact: Medium** — energy, freight and packaging inflation on a business with ~42% of revenue in EMEA/LatAm, plus Gulf-region Panadol demand/supply exposure and Houthi risk to Saudi/UAE logistics.

**Risk 3 — US trade policy: Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs**
- A **100% ad valorem Section 232 duty on imported patented pharmaceuticals and associated ingredients** took effect 31 July 2026 (Annex III importers) and 29 September 2026 (all others).
- Haleon's prior stance (CFO Dawn Allen, April 2025): "the impact of tariffs is limited and included in the guidance provided." Haleon manufactures much of its US-sold portfolio domestically and its products are largely non-patented OTC monograph items — so direct exposure is modest.
- **Probability of material direct hit: Low-Medium. Impact: Medium** if scope is widened to consumer-health/API inputs or if EU/UK retaliation targets US exports. This is the key *unpriced* trade-policy tail given 35% US revenue concentration.

*Secondary risk worth flagging:* **Russia**. Third-party research ("Haleon is Doing Business in Russia as Usual") highlights a continuing presence while peers exited. With Treasury expanding secondary-sanctions reach (Oct 2026), residual Russia exposure carries escalating compliance and reputational risk disproportionate to its revenue contribution.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Zantac / ranitidine (the dominant legal contingency)**
- GSK and Pfizer served Haleon with **notices of potential indemnification claims** under the 2022 Stock and Asset Purchase Agreement for OTC Zantac; **Haleon rejected the requests**, arguing the indemnity scope is not triggered and that it was not primarily liable for OTC or Rx Zantac claims (Haleon statement; Reuters; Haleon F-4).
- Industry-wide resolution is progressing: GSK committed **up to $2.2bn to resolve ~80,000 state-court cases (~93%)**; Pfizer ~$250m; Sanofi >$100m. On **13 April 2026 a Delaware state court dismissed 80,000+ cases against Boehringer Ingelheim**; plaintiffs appealed 13 May 2026.
- **Assessment:** the risk to HLN is *indemnity/contribution*, not primary liability, and the direction of travel (mass dismissals + capped industry settlements) is favourable. But the GSK/Pfizer–Haleon indemnity dispute is unresolved and is the largest single legal overhang on the equity.

**Regulatory environment**
- **US:** FDA OTC monograph and labelling; FTC scrutiny of health claims and supplement advertising (the FTC has been issuing warning letters across platforms/consumer health). Haleon also runs periodic voluntary recalls (e.g., four lots of Gas-X Extra Strength) — routine for the category but a recurring headline risk.
- **China:** the Centrum raw-material classification change is a live product-registration/import regulatory action with a multi-year tail (see Risk 1).
- **UK/EU:** MHRA/EMA labelling and advertising rules; GDPR/UK GDPR for consumer health data in apps and loyalty programmes; EU packaging and green-claims regulation raising compliance cost. Haleon's EMTN listing particulars confirm it is subject to "legal proceedings, disputes and regulatory and governmental investigations in various jurisdictions."
- **Governance/listing:** UK-incorporated, LSE primary + NYSE ADR; foreign-private-issuer 20-F reporting. Note that the SEC filing feed for HLN returned no submissions and 20-F text was not retrievable through my tools, and Haleon's own PDF library returned HTTP 403 — **my legal-proceedings detail is therefore sourced from secondary reporting and search snippets of the 20-F rather than the primary document text.** A direct 20-F read is recommended to confirm current provision amounts.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale.** Haleon's revenue base is majority developed-market (35% US, ~40% UK/EU/other developed), which anchors the rating at Medium rather than High. There are no known DOJ/SEC/CMA investigations, no sanctions designation, no expropriation exposure in core markets, and the Zantac tail is an indemnity dispute against a backdrop of industry-wide dismissals and capped settlements.

The offsetting negatives are concrete and already hitting the P&L: a **China regulatory action that management says may not be resolvable before 2028**, a **Middle East war feeding energy/freight inflation and consumer weakness**, and a **Section 232 tariff regime** whose consumer-health scope is not yet fully settled — collectively the proximate cause of the October 2026 guidance cut and the stock's largest one-day fall on record. Add an unresolved Russia presence and the GSK/Pfizer indemnity claim, and the risk profile is best described as **Medium, with a High-risk tail concentrated in China and the Zantac indemnity**.

**Monitoring triggers:** (1) any Chinese re-registration pathway or permanent Centrum China write-down; (2) escalation of GSK/Pfizer indemnity into formal arbitration/litigation; (3) expansion of Section 232 scope to OTC/consumer health or API inputs; (4) new EU/UK sanctions touching residual Russian entities; (5) Q4 2026 flu season outcome given ~70% of cold/flu revenue sits outside the US.
