# Haleon plc (HLN) — Value Investment Analysis

*Reference price $8.57/ADS (ADS = 2 ordinary shares); last data close $9.94 (30-Mar-2026). Financials reported in GBP; market cap ≈ $37.7bn ≈ £29.5bn. FX ≈ 1.28 USD/GBP. Where the data feed mixed USD price with GBP statements I have re-derived multiples in a single currency.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow**

Haleon is the demerged consumer-health arm of GSK/Pfizer: ~£11.0bn revenue, five categories (Oral Health ~30%, Pain, Respiratory, Digestive, Vitamins/Minerals/Supplements) and roughly two-thirds of revenue in #1 or #2 market positions.

**Sources of moat:**
- **Intangible assets (brands) — real and durable.** Sensodyne (global #1 sensitivity toothpaste), parodontax, Polident, Panadol (#1 analgesic across much of EM), Advil, Voltaren (#1 topical pain), Theraflu, Otrivin, Centrum (#1 multivitamin). These are habitual-purchase brands sold through pharmacy channels where the pharmacist's recommendation and shelf position matter.
- **Efficient scale in distribution.** Genuine advantage in emerging-market pharmacy/retail reach — expensive to replicate, unglamorous, durable.
- **Evidence of pricing power:** gross margin expanded ~280bps in four years (60.6% → 63.4%) and organic growth of ~3% in FY25 was essentially all price (Q1-26: price +2.4%, volume **−0.2%**).

**Moat trend: modestly widening on margins, narrowing on volume.** Margin and share data (60% of the business gaining/holding share) are good; but price-only growth with negative volumes in the US and structural private-label pressure in vitamins indicate the brand premium is being tested. Switching costs for a toothpaste or a vitamin are close to zero.

**The honest Buffett test — ROIC:** 9.6% (FY25), up from 6.8% (FY22), versus a WACC I estimate at ~7.5%. That is a *modest* positive spread, not a franchise-level return. The drag is ~£25.6bn of goodwill/intangibles (79% of total assets) inherited at carve-out values. Return on *tangible* capital is very high, so the economic business is better than the accounting one — but a 9–10% ROIC business is not a "wonderful" business; it is a good one.

**Moat rating: Narrow.** Not no-moat (brands are genuinely valuable and margins prove it), but not Wide (low switching costs, 2–3% category growth, no volume leverage).

**Strengths:** excellent cash conversion (FCF consistently >100% of net income — capex is only ~2.9% of sales); 520bps of operating-margin expansion in four years; net debt/EBITDA down to ~2.2x from ~4x at demerger; interest cover ~7x. Shareholder returns are now substantial: £599m dividends + £640m buybacks in FY25 = ~63% of FCF.

**Weaknesses:** reported revenue CAGR of only 2.2% and *declining* for two straight years (FX and portfolio pruning; organic ~3%, guided to ~2.5% for FY26). Growth is price-led, not volume-led. Recurring "one-off" restructuring ("operating model evolution," 2026) and a wide adjusted-vs-IFRS earnings gap are quality caveats. Balance sheet is leveraged by consumer-staples standards, and heavy intangibles leave impairment risk (FY23 net income fell to £1.05bn on charges).

## 4. Management Quality — **Good / adequate (7/10)**

- **Brian McNamara** (CEO since the May 2022 demerger) has executed the spin cleanly: deleveraging ahead of plan, margin expansion, disciplined bolt-on M&A rather than empire-building, and a step-up in buybacks (£121m → £640m). Dividend covered ~3.3x by FCF.
- **Capital allocation is rational but not exceptional.** Buying back stock at 16–18x earnings is acceptable; the better use of cash in a 2.5%-growth category would be either higher buybacks at lower prices or targeted EM/oral-care acquisitions.
- **Concerns:** no founder/owner-operator stake (typical for a demerged multinational); a persistent restructuring habit; and the risk that a management team under growth pressure makes a large, dilutive acquisition. The removal of the GSK and Pfizer sell-down overhang is a genuine positive for the equity story.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

Haleon is a good, cash-generative, brand-owning business with a narrow but real moat, an improving margin structure, a deleveraging balance sheet, and the cheapest cash-flow multiple in consumer health. But it is a **2.5–3% organic grower earning ~9.6% ROIC in a low-switching-cost category with 2.2x net leverage**, and the shares sit within roughly ±10% of my $9.25 intrinsic value estimate. As Buffett would put it: a fine business at a fair price — not a buy.

**Action:** Hold existing positions; add aggressively at **$7.00** (buy) or below. Trim above **$11.00** if the growth algorithm stays at ~2.5% organic.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Volume erosion / private label.** Q1-26 volume was negative; if price-led growth breaks down in oral care or vitamins (the highest-margin categories), the base case degrades toward the $6.85 bear case.
2. **Category and seasonality risk.** Cold/flu weakness alone cost ~130bps of growth in Q1-26; FY26 guidance was already trimmed to ~2.5% organic.
3. **Leverage and refinancing.** £8.6bn gross debt at ~3.9% average cost; a higher-for-longer rate environment raises interest expense (~£330m) directly against FCF.
4. **Intangible impairment.** £25.6bn of goodwill/intangibles (79% of assets) on brands carried at carve-out values — a write-down would not affect cash flow but would reset book value and sentiment (FY23 already saw charges).
5. **Tariffs and supply-chain localization** costs in the US, Haleon's largest profit pool.
6. **Capital-allocation misstep** — a large, expensive acquisition in a slow-growth category would destroy the modest discount that exists today.
7. **FX translation** (~1% headwind guided for 2026) and structural category competition from DTC/online supplement brands.
