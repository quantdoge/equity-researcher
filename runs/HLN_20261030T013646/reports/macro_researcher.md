## Macroeconomic Assessment — Haleon plc (HLN)

**Data as of 28–30 October 2026.** HLN is a GBP-reporting, UK-domiciled consumer healthcare company (North America 34% of sales, Europe 28%, Asia-Pacific 21%, LatAm/RoW 17%; Oral Health 31%, Pain Relief 23%, Respiratory 17%, VMS 15%, Digestive 9%). The 2:1 ADR is priced as *2 ordinary shares × GBP/USD* — verified: 320.6p × 1.3375 × 2 = $8.58 vs. ADR $8.57. **FX passes through ~1:1 to the ADR.**

---

### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Hawkish reflation / bear steepening (risk-off for duration)**

The regime is *not* a soft landing. Growth is above trend, inflation has re-accelerated, and the long end is repricing on real yields. Three facts define it:

- **Inflation re-accelerated.** US CPI YoY: 3.30% (Jul) → 3.35% (Aug) → **3.72% (Sep 2026)**.
- **The Fed is no longer easing.** Effective fed funds rose to **3.75%** in Sep from 3.63% held Jan–Aug. With CPI at 3.72%, the **real policy rate is ~0% — not restrictive.** There is no cut case, so long yields stay high. The 2Y (4.65%) sits ~90bp above fed funds: the market prices *hikes*, not cuts, over two years.
- **The yield rise is real, not inflationary.** 10Y UST went 4.44% (30 Jun) → 5.31% (5 Oct) → **4.99%**. The 10Y TIPS real yield rose **2.20% → 2.67% (+47bp)** while the 10Y breakeven moved only 2.24% → 2.35% (+11bp). **~80% of the shock is real-yield/term-premium — a pure discount-rate shock.** Breakevens anchored at 2.35% says the market trusts the Fed, which is *why* real yields are doing the work.
- Curve: 2Y 4.65 / 5Y 4.80 / 10Y 4.99 / 30Y 5.37, **2s10s +34bp — positively sloped, no recession signal.** This is a *bear steepener* (fiscal/term-premium driven), the worst possible shape for long-duration equities: it raises discount rates without cutting growth.

UK leg matters for HLN's sterling debt and sterling share price: **BoE Bank Rate 3.75%**, UK CPI 3.1% (Aug) and rising, with markets pricing **two hikes to 4.25% in Q4 2026**. UK duration is under pressure too.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**A. Discount rate — NEGATIVE (the dominant channel).** Beta 0.245, ~2% dividend yield, £500m buyback, staple-like cash flows: HLN is a textbook long-duration asset. The +47bp real-yield move mechanically compresses its multiple with no change in fundamentals. The ADR has fallen from $11.20 (23 Feb) to **$8.57**, −23.5% peak-to-trough and −14.7% YTD. Part of that is macro de-rating, part is the guidance cut — the two are now stacked.

**B. Inflation vs. pricing power — NEGATIVE (the most under-appreciated link).** Q3 organic growth of +2.6% decomposed into **price +2.1% and volume +0.5%**. Price is running **~160bp below the 3.72% CPI**. Haleon is no longer pricing at or above inflation — retailer own-brand pressure and consumer resistance are capping increases — while input costs (resins, aluminium tubes, packaging, freight, energy) still inflate. That is the classic late-cycle staples squeeze, and the £200m cost-savings programme is the only offset. **This is a margin story, not a demand story.**

**C. Consumer/volume — NEUTRAL-to-NEGATIVE.** Unemployment 4.2% and GDP +3.4% ann. keep the consumer employed, and HLN's categories are non-discretionary. But 3.7% CPI against ~0% real policy rate means real disposable income is thin. The tell is already visible: **Europe sales −0.6%, with vitamins down high-single-digit.** VMS is 15% of group sales and is the discretionary tail of the portfolio — it behaves like a consumer-discretionary line in a real-income squeeze.

**D. Rates on the balance sheet — NEGATIVE but manageable.** Net debt/adjusted EBITDA ~**2.5x** (~£8.5bn, EBITDA ~£3.4bn). As 2022–23 spin-off-era low-coupon paper rolls into a 4–5% market, interest expense steps up: **each +100bp is ~£85m pre-tax ≈ 4% of adjusted operating profit (~£2.0bn)**. Management matches debt currency to earnings currency, so this is an EPS drag, not a solvency risk. But buyback + ~2% dividend + refinancing now compete for the same cash flow.

**E. Currency — MIXED / net modestly negative for the ADR.** GBP −2.0% vs USD since 25 Aug *lifts* GBP-reported earnings from the 34% North American and 17% LatAm/RoW books (a translation tailwind for the sterling share price) but is a direct **~2% headwind to a USD-based ADR holder** — a mechanical leak with no operating offset. A 2.9% broad-USD rally in seven weeks compounds it. Net: near-neutral to GBP reports, negative to USD holders.

**F. Tariffs / trade — NEUTRAL (watch item).** US Section 232 pharma tariffs are a live sector risk, but Haleon manufactures largely local-for-local, blunting direct duty exposure, and OTC/consumer health has generally been treated separately from branded pharma. I could not verify the current tariff schedule for its HS codes — flagged as a gap, not dismissed.

**G. Important separation — three of the four drags in the Q3 miss were NOT macro.** The North American cold/flu season (epidemiology/weather) and Brazilian supply changes are idiosyncratic; Chinese import-rule changes are regulatory. **Only the pricing-cost squeeze and the discount-rate shock are genuinely macro.** The market took −14.3% in one session (volume 3.7x normal) — much of that was company-specific, and I would not attribute the whole move to the macro regime.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE** (−1 on a −2/+2 scale)

The binding constraint is unambiguous: **a real-yield shock with an anchored breakeven is precisely the combination that hurts a low-beta, 2.5x-levered, sub-inflation-pricing compounder**, and it arrives just as price/mix decelerates below CPI. Offsets that keep this from being −2: firm growth and employment underwrite volumes; 2.5x leverage is investment-grade comfortable; the payout is covered; and the defensive, non-discretionary category mix limits downside. Note the late-October divergence worth watching — **the 10Y has already retraced 32bp from its 5.31% peak while HLN kept falling to new lows.** If real yields keep easing from 2.67%, HLN's de-rating has an unwind path.

### 5. The Surprise Scenario — **Bear: a UK–US duration double-hike**

Bank of England delivers the two priced hikes to 4.25% *and* the Fed, seeing CPI hold above 3.5%, restarts tightening. The 30Y pushes through 5.75% and the 10Y real yield retakes 3.00%. HLN — already de-rated and now dependent on a buyback to support the tape — has no fundamental catalyst until the February results, so it absorbs the full multiple hit; a move toward 12x earnings (roughly 250–270p) becomes plausible, with the £500m buyback insufficient to arrest it. This is not a tail scenario: it requires only that the Fed does nothing while the term premium keeps grinding higher, and 30Y at 5.37% is already close. The mirror-image bull case — a sharp Q4 CPI rollover plus a severe Northern Hemisphere flu season hitting Respiratory (17% of sales) — would do the opposite, driving real yields back to ~2.1% and re-rating HLN toward Morgan Stanley's 460p target.

**Gaps:** PCE series inconsistent with CPI (vintage issue) and therefore excluded from the inflation read; UK CPI series stale (FRED ends Mar-2025, so BoE's 3.1%/3.5% figures are from the BoE, not FRED); HLN's exact debt maturity ladder and weighted average coupon unavailable — the £85m/100bp interest sensitivity is a modelled estimate from the disclosed 2.5x leverage, not a reported figure.
