## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow** (operationally widening, but now levered to a risky deal)

CHRW is the largest North American non‑asset‑based freight broker: ~$18B of gross revenues, ~85,000 contracted carriers, two segments (North American Surface Transportation and Global Forwarding), plus Robinson Fresh. It sits between shippers and a highly fragmented carrier base. I understand this business: it earns a fee (adjusted gross profit, "AGP") per load, holds almost no assets, and funds itself partly with customer receivables and carrier payables.

**Evidence the moat is real and has widened:**
- **14 consecutive quarters** of NAST volume growth outpacing the Cass Freight Shipment Index (Q3'26: +3.5% vs –0.3%). Sustained structural share gain in a commodity service is the single best moat evidence available here.
- **Pricing discipline:** truckload AGP per shipment +17% YoY in Q3'26 even as spot purchase costs spiked — that is pricing power, not just cycle.
- **Returns on capital consistently above cost of capital:** ROIC 15.3% (2023 trough) → 19.3% → **22.9% (2025)**; ROE ~32%. WACC ≈ 9%. The hallmark of a moated business is present.
- **Cost/technology advantage:** "Lean AI" (agentic workflows in pricing, load matching, back‑office) has taken SGA from $640M (2024) to $564M (2025) while volumes grew. Both segments now hit mid‑cycle operating margin targets.

**Why it is not Wide:** brokerage is fundamentally a pass‑through, ~15% net‑revenue‑margin service with real customer churn risk; there are no patents, licences, or brand lock‑in of consequence; large shippers multi‑source and periodically in‑source; competitors (TQL, Uber Freight, Echo, RXO) are numerous; and AI cuts both ways — it is as likely to help shippers/asset carriers disintermediate brokers as it is to help CHRW. The 2023 collapse (net income –65%) shows the earnings stream is cyclical, not annuity‑like.

**2026 step‑change to be aware of:** reported revenue and gross profit jumped mid‑2026 (+29.5% revenue in Q3). Gross revenue is inflated by higher transportation rates and gross‑basis reporting; the economically meaningful metrics are AGP, operating income (+23.1% to $271.8M) and adjusted EPS (+28.6% to $1.80). Treat headline revenue growth as a presentation/rate artifact, not organic growth.
