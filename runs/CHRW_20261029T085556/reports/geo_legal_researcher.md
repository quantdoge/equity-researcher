## C.H. Robinson Worldwide (CHRW) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Current as of latest available filings/news (Q3 2026 reported 28 Oct 2026; FY2025 10-K filed 13 Feb 2026)*

---

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

CHRW is a **US-centric asset-light 3PL**: FY2025 total revenues **$16,232.8M** (−8.4% y/y, reflecting the 1 Feb 2025 divestiture of the Europe Surface Transportation business), gross profit $2,671.2M, operating income $795M, net income $587M (FY2025 10-K; TradingView 10-K summary; Clarifo). Revenue is disclosed by *location of customer*; the FY2025 geographic split is **United States ~88% / other countries ~12%** (10-K geographic disclosure, via Clarifo). The company employed **11,855 people across 37 countries** at 12/31/25 and leases **~180 offices in 36 countries** across North America, Europe, Asia, South America, Oceania and the Middle East (10-K). Cash held outside the US: **$144.9M** (10-K 2025).

| Geography | Rev. exposure (est.) | Basis | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | ~88% | 10-K geographic revenue disclosure | **Medium** (trade-policy transmission, litigation regime, not asset risk) |
| **Asia (incl. China/SE Asia/India)** | ~4–5% (est.) | Global Forwarding ocean/air lanes; 10-K footprint | **Medium-High** (tariffs, Section 301, transpacific volume swings) |

Segment context (Q2 2026 10-Q): **NAST** revenue $3.59B / op. income $189.8M; **Global Forwarding** revenue $896.6M / op. income $61.0M. Sourcing ("Robinson Fresh") is **8.7%** of FY2025 revenue (10-K XBRL via Clarifo) — a food-safety (FDA/FSMA) and perishable-sourcing exposure concentrated in Mexico, Peru, Chile and US domestic grower networks.

**Key structural point:** CHRW owns almost no hard assets abroad and has already exited European surface transport. Its geographic risk is **flow risk, not asset/expropriation risk** — it is short US import volumes, ocean/air pricing and cross-border trade friction.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US trade-policy shock / tariff & de-minimis regime (Probability: High; Impact: Medium-High)**
The Section 321 de minimis exemption has been **fully suspended** (from 29 Aug 2025 across all countries; tightened again June 2026 per trade-compliance trackers: GoBolt, Strix), and layered Section 301/232/IEEPA tariffs remain in flux. For CHRW this is double-edged: customs brokerage and tariff-engineering services are a genuine profit pool and share-gain lever (management cites improved adjusted gross profit per transaction in customs; Q3 2026 "Lean AI for Ocean Shipping, Air Freight and Customs" launch, Business Wire 26 Oct 2026), but tariff-driven volume destruction, lane shifts and trade-lane volatility hit ocean/air forwarding and transpacific demand. CHRW's own trade-policy advisory content flags tariff complexity as a customer pain point. Net: **earnings volatility through Global Forwarding (~18% of revenue) rather than balance-sheet damage.**

**Risk 2 — Systemic shift in US freight-broker liability (Probability: Realised; Impact: High)**
Not "geopolitical" in the classic sense, but it is now the dominant legal-regime risk for the franchise: the Supreme Court's unanimous **Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II (2026)** decision held that the FAAAA safety exception does *not* preempt state-law negligent-hiring claims against brokers (Justia; DLA Piper; Crowell; Adams and Reese). This removes brokers' categorical federal defence and materially raises the cost of carrier vetting — a structural, industry-wide re-pricing of risk that CHRW's 450,000-carrier network cannot avoid. See §3.

**Risk 3 — Concentration in one legal/trade jurisdiction plus a transformative M&A review (Probability: Medium; Impact: Medium)**
88% US revenue means CHRW bears essentially **one** regulatory, judicial and tariff regime, with limited geographic diversification. That regime is simultaneously the most active in tariff policy and the most active in broker-liability litigation. Separately, the **$5.8B RXO acquisition (announced 5 Oct 2026; cash-and-stock, ~$30.25/RXO share; close expected H1 2027)** is CHRW's largest-ever deal and combines the #1 and a top-tier US brokerage, requiring **HSR/antitrust clearance and RXO shareholder approval** (Reuters; Supply Chain Dive; chrobinson.com press release). Expect a second request and potential behavioural conditions/divestitures; the deal also raises counterparty/execution risk (reported ~$185M breakup fee — *unverified, secondary source*). Mexico/Canada cross-border overlaps are the likeliest antitrust friction points. Probability of approval: high given the fragmented market; probability of 6–12 month delay: material.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Lipe matter — $604M jury verdict (the single largest legal contingency).** On **23 July 2026** a Dallas County, Texas jury returned a **~$604M verdict** arising from a fatal 2021 multi-vehicle collision in Mississippi; C.H. Robinson was found negligent for hiring a carrier with known safety alerts, with **23% of damages attributed to CHRW but potential joint-and-several liability for the full amount**. Insurance: **$155M per occurrence (with $5M deductible)**. The company recorded only an **immaterial accrual** and intends to appeal; Q2 2026 10-Q (filed 31 July 2026) states ultimate impact and timing are uncertain (StockTitan 10-Q summary; Husch Blackwell; CCJ; FreightWaves/Arnold & Itkin). Trade press subsequently reported the **trial court upheld the verdict**, "raising the stakes for freight broker carrier vetting" (Logistics Management headline — *article body not retrievable; treat as reported, not confirmed*). **Downside math:** if joint-and-several is enforced, net exposure ≈ **$604M − $155M ≈ $449M pre-tax**, versus FY2025 net income of $587M — i.e. a plausible ~75% of one year's earnings, plus interest and appeal-bond/liquidity implications (Q2 2026 long-term debt already rose to $1.69B from $1.09B at 12/31/25).
- **Montgomery v. Caribe (SCOTUS, 2026)** — unanimous; eliminates broad FAAAA preemption for negligent-hiring/selection claims. This is the *regime* behind Lipe and will drive a multi-year wave of broker-liability suits and higher insurance costs (DLA Piper, Crowell, FMG).
- **RICO / Lanham Act suit (Texas federal court)** — six family-owned trucking companies allege CHRW and TQL used "chameleon carriers," asserting **RICO and Lanham Act claims tied to alleged forced labour and wire fraud**; CHRW has confirmed carriers associated with the named entity are no longer in its network (FreightWaves; Metier Law). Early stage, but reputational and forced-labour-supply-chain (UFLPA/CBP WRO) angle is a live compliance theme.
- **OFAC settlement — C.H. Robinson International Inc., 13 Dec 2024: $257,690** for **82 apparent violations by five non-US subsidiaries** of the **Iranian Transactions and Sanctions Regulations** (and Cuba-related provisions); voluntarily self-disclosed, non-egregious (OFAC recent actions; JD Supra; ABA Banking Journal). This is a **direct, documented sanctions-compliance red flag** for the subsidiary network.
- **Regulatory perimeter:** FMCSA broker authority and carrier-vetting obligations (now litigation-enforced); **CBP** customs-broker compliance and importer-of-record exposure (given the de minimis/tariff churn, misclassification and entry-filing penalties are the key CBP risk); **FMC** NVOCC licensing and OSRA/detention-demurrage rulemaking for ocean; **FDA/FSMA** for Robinson Fresh; **OECD Pillar Two** global minimum tax (referenced in the Q2 2026 10-Q) affecting foreign effective tax rate; state/US privacy and cybersecurity obligations. **No FCPA, DOJ, SEC or FTC enforcement action against CHRW surfaced in this review** — the sole US government settlement identified is the 2024 OFAC matter.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Sanctions screening at non-US subsidiaries — proven weakness (OFAC, 2024).** Five foreign subsidiaries generated 82 apparent Iran/Cuba violations. With ~450,000 contracted carriers and heavy Middle East/Asia forwarding activity, residual sanctions and forced-labour (UFLPA) exposure remains **Medium-High**.
2. **Carrier-vetting/"chameleon carrier" fraud — both criminal-adjacent conduct and civil RICO exposure.** Post-Montgomery, vetting failures convert directly into nine-figure verdicts.
3. **Reserve adequacy / disclosure quality on the Lipe verdict.** An "immaterial" accrual against a $604M joint-and-several verdict invites SEC scrutiny and shareholder-suit risk if the appeal fails — worth flagging to the accounting team as a Q4 2026/Q1 2027 catalyst.
4. **Customs/tariff compliance complexity.** Full de minimis suspension plus rapid tariff changes materially increase mis-entry and penalty risk in the fastest-growing service line.
5. **No evidence found of AML/Bribery Act/FCPA issues** — not a flagged area.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

**Composition:** Geopolitical/geographic risk **Medium** (concentrated but asset-light, US-centric, trade-flow transmission rather than expropriation/currency-control risk; modest FX and repatriation exposure — $144.9M cash abroad, no material currency-control jurisdictions). Legal/regulatory risk **High** (a live $604M joint-and-several verdict with only $155M insurance and an immaterial accrual; a unanimous Supreme Court decision that structurally expands broker liability; a documented OFAC sanctions settlement; a RICO suit; and a $5.8B antitrust-review acquisition that will absorb management attention through H1 2027).
