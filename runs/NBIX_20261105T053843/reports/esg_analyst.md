## ESG ANALYSIS — NEUROCRINE BIOSCIENCES, INC. (NASDAQ: NBIX)

**Sector:** Healthcare / Biotechnology (neuroscience-focused) · **Market cap:** ~$16.8B · **HQ:** San Diego, CA · **Employees:** ~2,500
**Prepared from:** 2026 DEF 14A (filed 15-Apr-2026), FY2025 10-K (11-Feb-2026), company responsibility disclosures, CMS/IRA analyses, ICER health-economics review, litigation records.

> **Evidence gaps, disclosed up front:** SEC EDGAR blocked direct filing retrieval (403) and the 2025 Corporate Responsibility Report was returned as unparsed PDF binary, so I could not extract raw Scope 1/2/3 tonnage or workforce injury rates. Third-party ESG scores (S&P Global, Sustainalytics, MSCI) sit behind paywalls and were not retrievable. Where I infer rather than cite, I say so. Nothing below rests on self-reported ESG marketing.

### 1. ESG Materiality Map

Biotech is one of the lowest-environmental-footprint sectors and one of the highest social/regulatory-footprint sectors. Materiality is therefore inverted versus a typical industrial: **E is largely immaterial, S and G dominate.**

| Rank | Factor | Financially material? | Why it moves cash flow / cost of capital |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Product pricing & patient access (Social)** | **Critical** | Ingrezza is ~85%+ of revenue. US drug pricing reform is a direct revenue-line risk, not a reputational one. |
| **2** | **Product safety & labelling (Social)** | **High** | Boxed warnings, label expansions/withdrawals, and litigation drive addressable market and liability. |
| **3** | **Capital allocation & compensation alignment (Governance)** | **High** | $2.9B all-cash Soleno deal + serial equity-plan expansion = tangible per-share value transfer. |
| **4** | **Board entrenchment / shareholder rights (Governance)** | **Medium** | Classified board, 30+ yr tenures → slower correction of strategic error. |
| **5** | **Clinical/regulatory integrity (Social/Gov)** | **Medium** | FDA interaction quality, trial conduct, expanded-access ethics. |
| **6** | **Environmental (Scope 1/2, own operations)** | **Low** | Asset-light; no owned manufacturing. |
| **7** | **Scope 3 / supply-chain emissions** | **Low–Medium** | Real but concentrated at contract manufacturers the company does not control. |
| **8** | **Anti-corruption / FCPA** | **Low** | Revenue is overwhelmingly US; limited emerging-market exposure. |

**Key structural insight:** NBIX outsources manufacturing to third-party CMOs and operates from leased office/lab space. This means its *own* environmental footprint is de minimis, but its *social* licence depends almost entirely on two things it cannot outsource: the price of Ingrezza and the safety profile of its molecules.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW**

- **Carbon intensity:** Not meaningfully measurable as a risk factor. With no owned production assets, Scope 1 is limited to fleet/backup generation and Scope 2 to leased facilities. Scope 3 (CMO manufacturing, clinical trial logistics, business travel) is the dominant share — typical of the industry — and is Scope 3 *at a company that does not own the plant*. I could not verify absolute tonnage (see gaps).
- **Climate transition risk: Low.** No carbon-tax-exposed heavy processes, no CBAM-covered goods, no stranded-asset exposure. A carbon price at any plausible US level is immaterial to a company with no smokestacks.
- **Physical climate risk: Low–Medium.** San Diego HQ/labs carry moderate wildfire and water-stress exposure; concentrated single-campus footprint means one event could disrupt R&D continuity. Supply chain climate risk sits with CMOs and is unquantified.
- **Environmental controversies: None identified.** No spills, fines, remediation liabilities, or EPA enforcement surfaced in search of recent coverage.
- **Disclosure quality: Weak.** Reporting is SASB-aligned and framed around "minimise our impact on the environment" without disclosed, quantified, time-bound reduction targets. For an investor, this is a *disclosure* gap rather than a *risk* — but it is why NBIX will not score as an ESG "Leader."

**Verdict: Low risk, low materiality.** I would not spend incremental analytical time here, and neither should the investment committee.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM (trending Medium-High on pricing)**

**Labour — Low risk.** ~2,500 employees with **median employee pay of $273,341** (per the 2026 proxy) — an equity-participating, high-wage workforce. No manufacturing means no meaningful OSHA injury exposure; the residual is laboratory safety at R&D sites. No union disputes, strikes, or layoff programmes surfaced. Turnover risk is ordinary biotech scientific-talent competition, not industrial relations.

**Product safety — Medium risk, well-managed but live.**
- **Ingrezza carries a Boxed Warning** for depression and suicidal thoughts/behaviour, with the risk specifically heightened in the Huntington's disease chorea population — the growth indication approved in 2024. This is the single highest-consequence social risk in the portfolio: a post-marketing safety signal in a fragile, high-monitoring patient population would compress the label and invite litigation.
- **Orilissa/Oriahnn (elagolix)** carry bone-mineral-density warnings; this class has already been withdrawn from some ex-US markets by partners. Low revenue materiality today, but a persistent product-safety overhang in women's health.

**Patient access & pricing — High risk. This is the real social issue.**
- Ingrezza list price runs roughly **$7,500–$10,500 per 30-day supply** depending on source; GoodRx cites ~$10,454/month average retail.
- **ICER's evidence review concluded discounts of up to 90% would be required** for tardive dyskinesia treatments to be cost-effective at conventional thresholds. That is an independent health-economics body stating that current pricing is far outside value-based norms. It is exactly the evidence base that payers, PBMs, and CMS negotiators cite.
- Mitigants are genuine: the company runs a patient assistance programme (uninsured/underinsured at no cost), and ~90% of patients reportedly pay ≤$10 out of pocket. But copay support protects *patients*, not the *payer*, and payers are the ones who set gross-to-net.

**Community / trial ethics — Low risk.** An expanded-access programme exists with a formal physician-request policy. No controversy identified.

**Verdict: Medium.** Strong workforce and access programmes offset by a genuinely elevated pricing/health-economics profile and a boxed-warning-dependent core asset.

### 4. Governance Quality Score — **6.5 / 10**

**What is working (supporting the score):**
- **Independent, non-executive Chairman.** Kyle Gano is CEO and a director but does not chair the board — no CEO/chair duality.
- **Shareholder rights are clean.** Single class of common stock; no dual-class supervoting structure, no poison pill identified. Institutional ownership is high quality: BlackRock ~10.5% (two entities), Vanguard ~9.4%, Dodge & Cox 5.5%, State Street 4.3%.
- **Compensation is structurally aligned, not just rhetorically.** CEO pay is **86% equity** ($11.5M of $13.4M in stock and options), with clawbacks, anti-repricing provisions, and ownership guidelines. Prior say-on-pay support was **~99% of votes cast** — among the highest tiers, indicating shareholders are not alienated.
- EY as independent auditor; audit-committee ratification is a routine ballot item; a Code of Business Conduct applies to directors and employees.

**What is dragging the score down (the 3.5 points):**
1. **A classified (staggered) board.** Only three Class III directors stand each year. Combined with the tenures below, this structurally delays shareholder ability to effect board change.
2. **Extreme director tenure.** Nominee **Gary Lyons: 33.8 years** on the board. Nominee **Kevin Gorman: 18.8 years and *not independent*** (company founder and former CEO). A 34-year independent director is, in practice, difficult to call independent in judgment. Boardroom Alpha grades the board **C+**, which is consistent with my read.
3. **Pay-for-performance optics are poor for 2025.** CEO total compensation rose **58% to $13.4M** in a year when **1-year total shareholder return was –0.6%**. The company attributes this to promotion and performance adjustments following the 2024 CEO transition, and PRSUs paid at 125% on 2023 awards. Defensible mechanically, but it is the exact pattern that draws say-on-pay protest votes and proxy-advisor scrutiny.
4. **Serial dilution requests.** The 2026 ballot seeks to add **4,000,000 shares** to the 2025 Equity Incentive Plan — roughly **4% of shares outstanding** on ~101M shares — one year after the prior plan was adopted. A new CMO received a **$9.0M** first-year package (June 2025). Equity burn is running hot.
5. **Capital allocation is concentrated and recent.** The **$2.9B all-cash Soleno Therapeutics acquisition** (closed 18-May-2026) is ~17% of market cap deployed into a single late-stage asset at a rich premium. This is a governance question as much as a strategic one: it consumed the balance sheet and it was not put to a shareholder vote.

**Rationale for 6.5:** Governance is *institutionally sound* (independent chair, one share class, real equity alignment, near-unanimous say-on-pay) but *structurally stale* (staggered board, founder-director, three-decade tenures) with a compensation outcome that fails a simple pay-for-TSR test. Better than the biotech average, well short of best practice. Not a governance-driven short; not a governance-quality premium either.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

| Regime | Applies? | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **IRA Medicare Drug Price Negotiation** | **YES — highest-impact item** | Ingrezza represents roughly **$2.4B of Medicare spend** and is repeatedly flagged in the literature as a near-certain future selection candidate, with eligibility cited as early as **2029 given time-on-market rules**. Neurocrine may be able to claim the **small-biotech exemption** (low Part D share), which would defer selection — this is the single most valuable regulatory nuance in the file. Not yet selected as of the third cycle. **Model a negotiated-price scenario in the late-2020s.** |
| **340B / state price-transparency laws** | YES | Ongoing gross-to-net leakage and compliance cost; standard for the sector. |
| **Hatch-Waxman / ANDA settlements** | YES | Generic entry from 2030 under settlement terms; this is the dominant revenue-cliff driver. |
| **EU CSRD** | Minimal | EU revenue is immaterial; Ingrezza is a US-centric franchise. Low exposure. |
| **SEC climate disclosure rule** | Effectively no | Rule is stayed/contested and would in any case be immaterial given negligible Scope 1/2. |
| **CBAM** | No | No imports of covered goods. |
| **ESG-linked debt covenants** | No evidence | No sustainability-linked bonds or margin ratchets identified. |
| **FCPA / anti-corruption** | Low | US-weighted revenue; limited high-risk-jurisdiction exposure. |
| **Antitrust (HSR)** | Resolved | Soleno cleared. |

**Bottom line on regulation:** The regulatory risk that matters to NBIX is **drug pricing**, and it is already partially priced by the market. The ESG-labelled regimes (CSRD, SEC climate, CBAM) are immaterial. Do not let an ESG framework distract from the pricing file.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

**Not a Leader** — no quantified emissions targets, no net-zero commitment, limited environmental disclosure, a C+ board grade, and a classified/stale board preclude that classification.

**Not a Laggard, and certainly not ESG Risk** — zero environmental or labour controversies, a genuinely clean legal/ethics record, an independent chair, a single share class, 99% say-on-pay support, an 86%-equity CEO package, and a high-wage workforce. The controversies that do exist (ICER pricing critique, boxed warnings) are *industry-structural*, not *company-specific misconduct*.

On an ESG-risk lens, NBIX's material risk is **concentrated, identifiable, and financially quantifiable** — which is the best case for an ESG assessment, because it can be modelled rather than merely monitored.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**ESG factors should not drive a position in NBIX in either direction.** Specifically:

1. **The E is a non-factor.** Asset-light, no manufacturing, no carbon-cost exposure. Any ESG screen penalising NBIX on environmental grounds is applying an industrial template to a company it does not fit.
2. **The S is real but already in the numbers.** Pricing and boxed-warning risk are *the same risks* a fundamental analyst already models as gross-to-net compression and label risk. ESG adds framing, not new information. The genuinely additive insight is the **small-biotech IRA exemption**, which could defer Ingrezza negotiation and is under-appreciated.
3. **The G is the only place ESG changes the view — modestly negative.** A 58% CEO pay increase against a –0.6% one-year TSR, a 4M-share (~4%) equity-plan expansion, 30+ year director tenures, and a $2.9B all-cash acquisition executed without a shareholder vote all point to a board that is comfortable and slow-moving. That argues for a **small discount to governance-quality peers, not a thesis break**.
4. **Watch items that would change the rating:**
   - Any Ingrezza post-marketing safety signal or label restriction (→ downgrade to *ESG Risk*)
   - Ingrezza inclusion in an IRA negotiation cycle without the small-biotech exemption (→ materially negative to the thesis)
   - A failed say-on-pay vote or proxy-advisor "against" recommendation on compensation (→ governance score toward 5.0)
   - Disclosure of quantified Scope 1/2/3 targets and a de-staggered board (→ upgrade toward *Leader*)

**Recommendation to the investment committee: treat NBIX as ESG-neutral. Do not pay for an ESG premium, and do not apply an ESG discount beyond a modest governance-quality haircut. The thesis will be made or broken by Ingrezza durability, the Soleno integration, and IRA timing — none of which is an ESG question.**
