# MACOM Technology Solutions (MTSI) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price $200.20 | Market cap $15.4B | EV ~$15.1B | Tech / Semiconductors (RF, microwave & lightwave analog)**
*Fiscal year ends early October. FY2026 = year ended 10/2/2026.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Narrow (widening, but cyclical)**

MACOM designs high-performance analog/RF/microwave/photonic ICs — laser drivers, TIAs, transimpedance amps, switches, MMICs, diodes — into optical transport, data center interconnect, 5G/carrier infrastructure, radar/EW, medical and T&M.

**Evidence for a moat:**
- **Switching costs / qualification barriers.** Parts are designed into customer systems with 12–36 month qualification cycles; a design win is sticky for the platform's life. This shows up in pricing: gross margin has compounded from **35.2% (FY19) → 44.8% (FY20) → 58.0% (FY26)**. That is not a commodity supplier.
- **Intangible assets / IP.** Decades of GaAs, GaN, SiGe and silicon-photonics process IP, largely fab-lite (outsourced wafer supply) with internal test/assembly — capital intensity stays moderate (capex 4.8% of revenue).
- **Efficient scale in niches.** Sub-$1B TAM markets (e.g. high-speed optical drivers) support only 1–3 credible suppliers; MACOM is #1 or #2 in several.

**Evidence against a wide moat:**
- **The demand base is not durable-compounding.** Revenue was $530M (FY20), $500M (FY19), $649M (FY23), $675M (FY22) — **essentially flat for four years**, then +12.5%, +32.6%, +37.3% in FY24–26. A wide-moat business does not go four years without growth.
- **Customer concentration:** top-10 direct + distribution customers ≈ **60% of revenue**; no single direct customer >10% (FY26 10-K), but the end-customers behind the distributors are few.
- **Geographic/political exposure:** ~38% of revenue from China in the most recent disclosed split; export-control risk on both RF/defense and optical products.
- **ROIC has only just cleared the bar:** 5.2% (FY24), 6.95% (FY25), **14.8% (FY26)**. A moated business earns 15%+ through the cycle, not only at cycle peaks.

**Segment shift (the crux):** Data Center revenue rose **+74% to $509.7M, ~38% of FY26 revenue** (from a small base two years ago). The moat today is being *earned* in AI optical interconnect, which is both the fastest-growing and the most cyclical/least-proven part of the franchise.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **6 / 10**

| Revenue ($M) | 648 | 730 | 967 | 1,328 |
| Gross margin | 55.8% | 50.9% | 54.9% | **58.0%** |
| Operating margin (GAAP) | 18.0% | 11.5% | 13.4% | **23.0%** |
| Net income ($M) | 91.6 | 76.9 | **(54.2)** | 335.3 |
| Diluted EPS | $1.28 | $1.04 | $(0.73) | $4.31 |

| ROIC | 8.1% | 5.2% | 7.0% | **14.8%** |
| SBC ($M) | 38.1 | 45.6 | 86.1 | **88.0** |

**Strengths:** record gross margin (Q4 FY26 59.9%), 31.6% Q4 operating margin, **87% FCF conversion** (excellent), **$792M cash vs $371M total debt** (net cash on a gross basis; ~$259M net debt on the data provider's netting), inventory +20% against revenue +37% (clean), and no debt wall (long-term debt $0).

**Deductions that keep this at a 6, not a 9:**
1. **Stock-based comp of $88M = 6.6% of revenue and ~26% of net income.** Cash flow "adds it back," but it is a real transfer to employees: diluted shares went 73.99M → 78.26M (**+3% in two years**). SBC-adjusted FCF is ~$205M, not $293M.
2. **Earnings quality is uneven.** FY2022's $440M net income contained a large one-time tax valuation-allowance release; FY2025's $(54)M loss came *despite* $129.7M of operating income, implying ~$150M of non-operating charges I could not itemize from the available filings (flagged as a data gap).
3. **Growth is not yet proven durable** (see the FY19–23 flat revenue record).
4. **Working capital is now a use of cash** (−$92.7M in FY26) and capex is ramping ($22M → $64M → likely higher), so FCF margins near 22% are a peak-cycle figure.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **No margin of safety; ~50–60% premium to intrinsic value**

**Current multiples (on FY26 actuals):**
- P/E **46.5x** GAAP; P/B **9.2x**; EV/EBITDA **33.0x**; **P/FCF 53x → FCF yield 1.9%** (1.3% SBC-adjusted)
- Peer median P/E **27.6x** → **68% premium** to semiconductor peers
- Forward: Q1 FY27 guide is $455M revenue / $2.25 adj. EPS; annualized ~**$9.00–9.50 non-GAAP EPS → ~21x forward**. *This is the only lens under which the stock looks merely "expensive" rather than extreme.*

**Intrinsic value work (my own, not the mechanical tool):**
The packaged DCF model returns $20.5B (~$263/share), but it assumes **27.2% FCF growth for ten straight years** — a hockey stick with no terminal fade. I reject it as a valuation anchor.

*Base case:* FY27 FCF ~$400M (20% margin on ~$2.0B revenue), decelerating to ~$660M by FY31 (+20%, +14%, +9%, +6%), 11% WACC, 2.5% terminal growth → **EV ~$6.8B, equity ~$6.5B, ≈ $84/share.**

*Good-execution case:* revenue ~$3.0B by FY29 at 26% operating margin (~$780M EBIT), exit at 20x EBIT, discounted at 11% → **≈ $140/share.**

*Blue-sky case:* AI optical demand compounds through 2031, FCF reaches $1.0B, exit 25x EBIT → **≈ $180–200/share** — i.e. *today's price already discounts the blue-sky case.*

**Implied-expectations check:** at $200 you are paying for revenue to roughly double again to ~$2.6B+ while holding 58% gross margins and 20%+ FCF margins — and for the multiple to stay at 20–25x EBIT. That is a chain of "and then"s, not a margin of safety.

**Intrinsic value estimate: $90–160, midpoint ~$125.** Current price is ~60% above midpoint. A 25–30% margin-of-safety entry would require **< $95**.

**Price context:** 52-week high **$409.68** (May 2026), now $200.20 — **−51%** but still **+80% YTD**. The recent leg down (Nov 20, −12% in a session) was **sector-wide AI-capex de-rating, not company-specific**; the business beat and raised. Analyst targets were cut but remain $220–360 (Evercore $475; Susquehanna $220). Sell-side is anchoring on forward EPS, not on owner earnings.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good (B+)**

- **Stephen G. Daly, CEO since 2012** — a genuine operator-capital allocator. He ran the transform from a lagging GaAs discrete house (35% GM, negative operating margin in FY19) to a 58%-GM analog specialist: divested non-core lines, ran a fab-lite model, and made tuck-in acquisitions (e.g. the FY25 deal flow that generated the non-operating charge) rather than empire-building. Margin and mix discipline is visible in the numbers.
- **Balance sheet discipline:** gross debt down from ~$487M to $371M while cash built to $792M. Delivered in a downturn instead of levering into the up-cycle.
- **Alignment:** founder-adjacent, long-tenured leadership; no dividend (correct — reinvestment returns exceed payout needs right now).
- **Blemishes:** buybacks are token ($43M FY25, **$0** FY26) and do not offset SBC dilution (+3% share count in two years). Capital deployment is now leaning into capacity capex at peak-cycle margins — the classic timing risk. I could **not verify current insider ownership** from the available data (gap).

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (valuation-driven; avoid new capital)

This is a **good business at a bad price**, and the framework is explicit: *a business trading at a fair price is not a buy* — this one trades well above fair.

- Moat: **Narrow**, currently widening, but concentrated in an AI-capex-dependent end market.
- Financial quality: **6/10** — elite margins and FCF conversion, offset by a four-year revenue stall, 6.6% SBC, and ROIC that only just cleared WACC in the peak year.
- Intrinsic value: **~$125** (range $90–160) vs **$200.20** market price. Paying 53x trailing FCF and 33x EV/EBITDA for a cyclical semiconductor with 60% customer concentration is the opposite of a margin of safety.
- **Action:** existing holders should trim/reduce into strength; new money should wait. I would become a genuine buyer at **$95–115** (≈15–17x forward EPS, ~4% SBC-adjusted FCF yield), which is where the "good-execution" case gets a discount rather than a premium.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**To the downside (why the stock can keep falling):**
1. **AI capex digestion.** Data Center is ~38% of revenue and grew 74%. A single hyperscaler pause compresses estimates *and* the multiple simultaneously — the same mechanism produced the −51% drawdown already seen in six months.
2. **Cyclical reversion of record margins.** 58% gross / 23% operating margins are all-time highs; the FY24 trough was 50.9% / 11.5%. A reversion to FY24 margins on FY27 revenue would roughly halve earnings.
3. **China + export controls** (~38% of revenue at last disclosure) and defense/EW program timing.
4. **Dilution:** 3% annualized share growth quietly erodes per-share value.
5. **Multiple compression:** de-rating from 46x to a peer-like 28x P/E removes ~40% of the price with zero change in the business.

**To my Sell call (what would prove me wrong):**
1. Data center revenue keeps compounding >40% through FY28 *with* gross margin ≥58%, and FCF reaches $800M+ by FY29 — that would put intrinsic value near $200 and validate the price.
2. Sustained ROIC >20% through a full cycle, which would justify re-rating the moat from Narrow to Wide.
3. A durable, disclosed multi-year backlog or design-win pipeline in 3.2T optical that converts the current demand surge into contracted visibility.

**Analyst's note on gaps:** I could not itemize the FY2025 non-operating charge, could not verify insider ownership, and could not retrieve the full Q4 press release (403 on the wire service). In my base case I used FY27 FCF of ~$400M, which is a judgment on margin sustainability, not a management guide — the company guides revenue, not free cash flow.

**Bottom line:** MACOM is a genuinely improved, well-run analog franchise. But at 53x trailing free cash flow with an earnings base set at a cyclical peak in the most capex-sensitive part of the AI food chain, the price already assumes everything goes right. Great company, wrong price. **Sell.**
