# MTSI — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
**MACOM Technology Solutions Holdings, Inc. | FY2026 (ended 2 Oct 2026) | Semiconductors — Analog/RF/Photonics**

---

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

FY2026 revenue **$1,327.6M** (+37.3% YoY). Geographic split per 10-K (customer billing address):

| Geography | FY2026 Rev | % of Total | Risk Level | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **China** | ~$501M | **37.7%** (28.4% FY25) | **High** | Largest single exposure and rising ~930bps YoY; direct nexus to US–China export controls and tariff escalation |
| **United States** | ~$506M | **38%** | Low | Home market; defense/Trusted Foundry demand; but exposed to retaliatory tariffs and Section 232 input costs |
| **Asia Pacific ex-China** | ~$154M | **12%** | Low–Medium | Taiwan, Japan, Korea, Malaysia, Singapore — supply chain and assembly partners; Taiwan-strait tail risk |
| **Other foreign** | ~$166M | **13%** | Low–Medium | Primarily Europe (France MESC fab), Middle East, LatAm |

**Critical nuance — the China number overstates true China end-demand.** Management has disclosed **no China manufacturing and only ~85 China-based employees**, and that the **"vast majority" of China shipments are re-exported inside systems ultimately bound for the US** (FactorsToday, 2026-08-22, summarizing Q3 FY26 call). This cuts both ways: today it insulates MACOM from in-country operational/political risk, but it means the China line item is functionally a **US-demand proxy routed through a policy chokepoint** — the worst of both worlds if either end of that pipe is restricted.

**Physical footprint:** HQ + fab, Lowell MA (lease extended to 2043; 10-K EX-10.5); **European Semiconductor Center (MESC), Limeil-Brévannes, France** (from Ommic acquisition; awarded multi-year contract Feb 2025 — BusinessWire, 2025-02-05); RTP wafer fab transfer completed Feb 2025; plus acquired sites via Wolfspeed RF (Dec 2023) and **ENGINIC Inc. (Nov 2024, Korea)**. US **"Trusted Foundry"** status (10-K term) ties it into classified/defense programs.

**End-market mix (relevant because risk concentrates in one):** Industrial & Defense $536.4M (40%), **Data Center $509.7M (38%, +74% YoY)**, Telecom $281.6M (21%). The fastest-growing segment — AI data-center optics/photodetectors at 1.6T — is precisely the product category at the center of US–China tech controls.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — China revenue concentration colliding with US–China trade policy
- **Probability: High (policy friction already live) | Impact: High ($500M+ of revenue)**
- 37.7% of revenue billed to China, up from 28.4%, while the 10-K explicitly flags that **"trade-policy changes could adversely affect sales"** (StockTitan 10-K summary, 2026-11-12).
- Live backdrop (Nov 24, 2026): US preparing a **fresh tariff round on China, EU, Mexico and Japan** targeting excess industrial capacity (WSJ, 2026-11-24); a **Section 301 excess-capacity probe** could produce new duties as Washington/Beijing decide whether to extend the trade truce struck at the Shenzhen summit (Barron's, 2026-11-24). The truce is **time-boxed and reversible**, not structural.
- Because most China-bound product is re-exported to the US, a US tariff on Chinese-origin systems can suppress MACOM demand *without* any action against MACOM directly — non-obvious second-order channel.

### Risk 2 — Export controls (BIS/EAR) and Entity-List contagion into AI optics
- **Probability: Medium-High | Impact: High**
- Semiconductor BIS restrictions are an explicit named risk in the 10-K ("BIS introduced restrictions related to semiconductor manufacturing, prohibit the ability to sell, ship…"; StockTitan/10-K).
- Data Center is now 38% of revenue growing ~74% (guided ~80% in FY27 per Daly) — if AI-optical/DCI components are pulled into controls or if Chinese customers (Huawei-adjacent supply chains flagged by third-party research) are formally restricted, the growth engine is the exposure.
- Precedent for volatility: MACOM fell 33% in July 2026 on semiconductor de-rate tied to "Chinese-competition and AI-capex-durability concerns" with no company-specific news (FactorsToday) — the market already prices this risk reflexively.

### Risk 3 — Tariff and critical-materials dual squeeze on a US/France manufacturer
- **Probability: High (tariff is in force) | Impact: Medium**
- **Section 232: 25% tariff on "semiconductor articles" effective 15 Jan 2026** (Presidential proclamation 14 Jan 2026; Commerce report 22 Dec 2025). Exempt from IEEPA reciprocal but **stacks with IEEPA fentanyl tariffs on China**, and **drawback is unavailable** (Sandler, Travis & Rosenberg). MACOM's France-fab output entering the US is squarely in scope, and it cannot drawback on re-export.
- **Input risk:** China's ban on US-bound **gallium, germanium and antimony** exports is only *suspended* — extended **until January 2027** (Reuters, 2026-11-24). These are direct feedstock for GaAs/GaN/InP compound semiconductor production. A January cliff is a live, dated supply risk for a company guiding ~60% gross margin on fab utilization.
- Also exposed to retaliation against US-origin chips and to EU tariff action (fresh EU duties flagged alongside China).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation — clean.** The FY2026 10-K states: **"We were not involved in any material legal proceedings during the fiscal year ended October 2, 2026."** Prior-quarter 10-Q (period ended 2 Jan 2026) confirms the same. No securities class action, no DOJ/SEC/FTC investigation disclosed.

Legacy matters, effectively resolved:
- **Bell Semiconductor, LLC v. MACOM Technology Solutions** (D. Mass., filed 19 Oct 2022, 2 patents, **closed 2 Feb 2023**) — no ongoing exposure.
- **MACOM v. Infineon / International Rectifier** (C.D. Cal., filed Apr 2016) over Nitronex GaN-on-Si patents — MACOM prevailed; **Infineon was held unable to terminate the patent agreement** (Bloomberg Law). Settled law, no carrying risk.
- Standard boilerplate remains: the 10-K warns of future IP/commercial claims common to the industry.

**Regulatory posture:**
- **Export controls (BIS/EAR)** — primary regulator of consequence. MACOM's defense/radar/EW business and Trusted Foundry status imply **ITAR/DDTC** touchpoints and deemed-export controls on technical data.
- **Section 232 / CBP** — 25% semiconductor tariff compliance, country-of-origin marking on France and Malaysia-assembled goods.
- **Antibribery/FCPA** — indirect sales model (independent reps, resellers, distributors across Asia) is the classic FCPA/diversion-risk channel; **no disclosed enforcement action**.
- **Capital structure legal note:** the **2029 Convertible Notes** stock-price trigger was satisfied in the quarter ended 2 Oct 2026; holders may convert in the quarter ending 1 Jan 2027 (10-K). This is a mechanical dilution/settlement item, not a legal risk, but it is the one dated legal-adjacent event on the calendar.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

No red flags rising to disclosure level. Three amber items worth monitoring:

1. **Channel diversion risk (amber).** China revenue is booked on *billing address*, sold heavily through resellers/distributors, and then re-exported. That structure is exactly the profile BIS scrutinizes for diversion to restricted end-users. Company's own framing ("vast majority re-exported in systems bound for the US") is a mitigating narrative that, if it ever proved inaccurate, converts directly into an enforcement matter.
2. **Trusted Foundry / ITAR deemed-export discipline (amber).** Defense revenue at 40% of the mix plus foreign fabs (France) and foreign design/sales staff raises routine deemed-export licensing burden.
3. **Section 232 origin/tariff exposure (amber, non-compliance-grade).** 25% duty with no drawback and privileged-foreign-status FTZ rules creates real mis-declaration risk on multi-country content.

**Clean:** no OFAC sanctions exposure identified, no Russia/Ukraine operations disclosed, no data-privacy enforcement exposure of consequence, no government investigations pending.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Split the rating honestly, because the components diverge sharply:

- **Legal / litigation risk: LOW.** No material proceedings, no investigations, legacy IP disputes resolved in MACOM's favor.
- **Geopolitical / trade-policy risk: HIGH.** 37.7% China revenue rising fast, growth concentrated in AI-optics (the epicenter of US–China controls), a 25% Section 232 tariff on its core product with no drawback relief, a **January 2027 gallium/germanium cliff**, and a trade truce explicitly characterized as provisional.
- **Mitigants:** zero China manufacturing footprint, ~85 China employees, US Trusted Foundry status (a policy *tailwind* for defense), and a France-based European fab that provides some non-US/non-China sourcing optionality.
- **Net:** **HIGH**, driven entirely by policy rather than legal exposure. The single most important thing to monitor is not a courtroom — it is whether the January 2027 critical-materials suspension lapses and whether AI-optical components enter BIS controls.
