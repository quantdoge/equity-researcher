## MACOM Technology Solutions Holdings (MTSI) — ESG Analysis

**Company snapshot:** Analog/RF/optical semiconductor designer (fab-lite), Lowell MA, ~2,100 employees, FY26 revenue $1,327.6M (+37.3%), net income $335.3M, market cap ~$15.4B. Fiscal year ends early October; FY26 10-K filed 11/12/2026.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW** (with disclosure caveat)

- **Regulatory/permit record is clean.** Violation Tracker (2000–present): total penalties **$19,049** across 2 records — a **$9,048 Massachusetts hazardous-waste penalty (2024)** and a $10,001 ERISA benefit-plan record from predecessor Applied Micro Circuits (2018). Against $1.33B revenue this is de minimis (<0.001%). No spills, no EPA/DOJ actions, no remediation liabilities disclosed.
- **Legacy site issue (monitor, not material):** the leased Lowell, MA premises (100/144 Chelmsford St, lease extended to 2043) are subject to a Massachusetts G.L. c.21E **Activity and Use Limitation** — i.e., historical contamination with institutional controls. Landlord-owned; MACOM is tenant. Low probability of transfer to MACOM but it caps future site use and could complicate exit.
- **Energy/climate:** company discloses a climate policy and an operational programme — Lowell HQ LED/HVAC retrofits, energy-recovery ventilation, and an on-site **CCHP (combined cooling, heat and power) plant** initiated 2020 to cut grid dependence and natural gas use. Directionally positive, but **no quantified Scope 1/2 figures, no net-zero or SBTi-validated target, and no limited assurance** were located in public filings/web properties.
- **Carbon intensity: not computable** — MACOM does not publicly disclose Scope 1/2 totals, so no emissions/revenue ratio is available. This is itself the finding: an investment-grade emitter would report it.
- **Scope 3 is the real exposure and is undisclosed.** As a fab-lite designer, the overwhelming majority of MACOM's footprint sits upstream (foundry wafer production, substrates, assembly/test). Undisclosed Scope 3 means customers with science-based supply-chain targets (hyperscalers, which drive the 74%-growth Data Center segment) will increasingly demand it — a commercial, not just reputational, risk.
- **Physical climate:** direct operations low-risk; supply chain is not — Taiwan/Japan seismic and water-stress concentration plus Gulf Coast/Asia logistics.
- **Stranded-asset risk: nil.** No fossil reserves; energy-efficient products (GaN for RF power, optical for data centre interconnect) are transition *enablers*.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment — **LOW**

- **Workforce:** ~2,100 FTE; high-skill mix with a large R&D contingent. No layoff, union action, strike, or WARN-scale restructuring controversy surfaced in the 12-month news sweep. (One aggregated "250 layoffs" headline traces to an older restructuring, not a current event — not relied upon.)
- **Safety:** semiconductor wet-process and GaAs/GaN handling carry inherent chemical risk; no OSHA citations or fatality records in Violation Tracker. Safety record is clean on available evidence.
- **Talent concentration is the genuine S-factor.** Analog/RF design and GaN process engineering talent is among the scarcest in semiconductors; with $1.33B revenue on 2,100 heads (~$632K revenue/employee, roughly 3–5× typical device-industry productivity), each engineer is highly leveraged. Key-person and option-refresh risk is real, particularly as larger peers (Broadcom, Qorvo, Skyworks, Marvell) bid for the same engineers.
- **Supply-chain labour:** MACOM relies on third-party foundries and OSAT in Asia with no publicly disclosed supplier audit programme, forced-labour (UFLPA) due-diligence disclosure, or **conflict-minerals (SEC Form SD / 3TG)** sourcing transparency located. Standard for the sector, but a gap versus best practice.
- **Product safety / social licence:** medical and aerospace/defence end markets raise the cost of a quality escape; the **Trusted Foundry** designation implies DoD-vetted process security — a positive social/governance signal. Notably, MACOM was **honoured by Massachusetts legislators** (2026), indicating constructive local community relations.
- **Diversity:** no EEO-1-style or board/management gender-and-ethnicity metrics disclosed. Board includes two women (Jihye Whang Rosenband among seven) — 1 of 7 (14%), below the semiconductor-peer norm and below institutional-investor 30% thresholds.

### 4. Governance Quality Score — **6 / 10**

**Positives:** 7-member board with **6 of 7 independent**; **Audit, Compensation and Nominating/Governance committees are all fully independent** (Audit: Ribar, Ritchie, Rosenband; Comp: Chung, Ingram, Ribar, Shanmugaraj; Nom/Gov: Ingram, Ritchie, Rosenband, Shanmugaraj). A designated **Lead Independent Director (Peter Chung)** exists with committee memberships. Single class of common stock — **no dual-class structure, no disclosed poison pill**. Independent directors carry relevant operating/semiconductor pedigree (Bland, Ingram, Shanmugaraj). Convertible notes (2029, conversion window opening Q1 FY27) are conventional, not ESG-linked; no predatory financing features observed.

**Deductions:**
1. **CEO/Chairman duality.** Stephen G. Daly is **Chairman, President and CEO**. He joined the board as an independent director (2015), became CEO in 2019, and retained the chair — a structure that impairs board challenge on exactly the topics that matter here (China exposure, export compliance). A Lead Director mitigates but does not cure this; most major proxies treat it as a withhold-vote trigger.
2. **Small, insider-influenced board** (7 members) with long CEO tenure (7+ years) and a prior abrupt CEO succession (Croteau resignation, 2019) — succession planning quality is unproven.
3. **Compensation:** CEO total comp ~$10.1M against a $15.4B market cap (~0.07%) — not egregious. Structure includes performance equity, and the FY26 P&L (revenue +37%, GM +330bp, net income swing to +$335M) evidences pay-for-performance alignment. However, no explicit ESG/China-compliance or export-control metric in the incentive plan was identified — a gap given that export-control failure is the single largest tail risk to the equity.
4. **ESG disclosure architecture is thin:** no standalone assured sustainability report, no SASB/TCFD-aligned index, no CDP response, no net-zero target. Governance of ESG appears to sit informally rather than with a named board committee (unlike peers who assign it to Nom/Gov).
5. **Reporting quality flag:** the company's own 10-K fiscal 2025 result included a **$54.2M net loss** driven by items not visible in the summary release; combined with a heavy reliance on convertible-note settlement in shares (1,262K shares issued FY26), disclosure complexity is elevated. Not a governance breach, but warrants scrutiny of non-GAAP reconciliation.

**Score rationale:** strong formal independence and clean ethics record offset by CEO/Chair concentration and disclosure deficit → mid-range, not Leader.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

- **E: Low risk, weak disclosure.** Clean compliance record, but no quantified emissions, no target, no assurance — insufficient to be rated a Leader even though underlying risk is genuinely low for a fab-lite designer.
- **S: Low risk.** Stable workforce, no controversies, valuable skilled employment; gaps in supply-chain labour and diversity disclosure.
- **G: Average (6/10).** Genuine independence and clean ethics offset by CEO/Chair duality, a small board, thin ESG oversight structure, and no compliance-linked incentive metric.
- **Dominant factor is neither E, S nor G in the classic sense** — it is **geopolitical/regulatory licence-to-operate**, which most ESG scoring models underweight. A third-party ESG score will likely read better than this assessment because it will miss the 37.7% China concentration.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL on core ESG, NEGATIVE on regulatory/geopolitical overlay**

**Verdict: no ESG-driven exclusion or re-rating argument; a clear risk-management overlay on the thesis.**

1. **This is not an ESG problem stock.** No controversies, no stranded assets, no governance scandal, no litigation tail. ESG screens (exclusions, controversy thresholds, UNGC compliance) should *pass* MTSI. There is no ESG discount to arbitrage and none to fear.
2. **The binding constraint is export-control and China concentration, and it is deteriorating, not improving.** China moved from 28.4% to 37.7% of revenue in a single year — precisely the wrong direction for licence-to-operate risk, even as revenue and margins inflected positively. Investors are being paid for growth that is increasingly dependent on the jurisdiction most likely to be subject to further restriction. This is the single most important non-financial risk in the file and it sits closer to the ESG mandate than to the sell-side model.
3. **Disclosure asymmetry is the actionable insight.** MACOM reports China exposure granularly but discloses almost nothing on Scope 3 — despite selling optical components to hyperscalers whose procurement teams are imposing science-based supply-chain targets. As Data Center grows toward 40%+ of revenue (currently $509.7M / 38.4%), customer-driven Scope 3 data demands create a rising, unpriced compliance and commercial cost.
4. **Governance is adequate but not best-in-class** — the CEO/Chair combination and absence of an export-compliance metric in the incentive plan are the two items an engaged holder should push on. Both are cheap to fix and would likely be welcomed; neither is thesis-breaking.
5. **Watch items:** (a) any BIS rulemaking touching Chinese telecom/optical customers; (b) Chinese gallium/germanium licensing tightening (input-cost shock to GaN); (c) a first Scope 1/2 disclosure or net-zero commitment (would be a modest positive catalyst with ESG-mandated holders); (d) any move to separate Chair and CEO.

**Net:** ESG factors neither support nor undermine the investment case — **but they materially raise the left tail.** Position sizing should reflect a jurisdiction risk that is now ~38% of revenue and rising, and the ESG file offers no offsetting mitigation (no dual-sourcing disclosure, no China-compliance incentive metric, no supplier-diversification commitment).
