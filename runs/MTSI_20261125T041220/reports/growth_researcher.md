## MACOM Technology Solutions (MTSI) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $200.20 (Nov 24, 2026) · Market cap $15.6B · EV $15.1B · Net cash ~$420M · Beta 1.83*

---

### 1. Growth Story

MACOM is the merchant analog front-end of the AI optical interconnect — laser/modulator drivers, TIAs, photodiodes and InP lasers that sit inside every 800G→1.6T→3.2T optical module, plus 200G/lane equalizers for copper scale-up. As lane speeds double, dollar content per connection rises (Goldman models a 13x TAM expansion in optics, with 16x/45x content growth in scale-out/scale-up), and MACOM captures that content without owning a module assembly business — a 60%+ gross margin lever on the fastest-growing TAM in semis. **The setup is unusual: the stock has fallen ~51% from its May 2026 high of $410 to $200 while forward estimates went *up* — a pure multiple compression, not an estimate cut.**

### 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

- **Underlying TAM:** optical transceiver market ~$23.4B in 2025 → **$112.3B by 2031 (~30% CAGR, Yole)**; optical interconnect market $21B in 2025 → $72B by 2035. Goldman's work shows optics TAM expanding ~13x as optical moves from scale-out to **scale-up** (inside the rack, copper's last redoubt).
- **MACOM's serviceable slice** is the analog/photonics content per module (drivers, TIA, photodiode, laser) — plausibly 15–25% of module BOM and rising as 1.6T/3.2T adds lanes and requires faster, lower-power analog. Add NPO (near-packaged optics), 200G/lane active copper equalizers, and 2028 CW laser entry, and the SAM is meaningfully larger than the pluggable-only number.
- **Penetration today is low:** FY26 revenue of $1.33B against a >$100B optics TAM and a ~$20B+ addressable analog/photonics SAM implies **mid-single-digit penetration** — the runway is the point of the thesis, not the current share.
- **Differentiation:** MACOM's edge is a decades-deep III-V/InP and GaAs/GaN-on-SiC process portfolio plus in-house fab — it can co-design the analog front-end with hyperscaler optical engines. Most competitors either sell modules (Coherent, Lumentum, Innolight) or are DSP/ASIC-centric (Broadcom, Marvell). MACOM is the arms dealer.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Q1 FY2027 earnings — Feb 4, 2027.** Consensus $2.25 EPS / $455M revenue. Watch whether data-center revenue beats the "~15% QoQ" guide assumption; a 20%+ sequential print re-rates the stock. Q1 guide: revenue $450–460M, adj. GM 62–63%, adj. EPS $2.21–2.29.
2. **1.6T volume ramp through FY2027** — management's base case is ~80% data-center growth; 3.2T / 448G PAM4 chipsets and TFLN/EML/silicon-photonics drivers extend content into 2027–28.
3. **NPO design-win conversion with multiple hyperscalers** — currently design activity, not production. Any confirmed production award is a step-function TAM event.
4. **200G/lane active copper equalizers** — positive qualification feedback; initial business possible **H2 FY2027**. This is a new, adjacent TAM (currently Credo/Marvell territory).
5. **Industrial & Defense >30% growth in FY27** (GaN-on-SiC, radar, EW, counter-UAS), plus a **~$20M LEO satellite constellation program starting early FY2028**.
6. **InP CW laser entry targeted 2028** — expands SAM into the light-source market for silicon photonics.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY**

*(Conviction: high on the 12-month risk/reward, moderate on the FY2028+ durability.)*

The thesis is simple: a 37%→46% grower with 60%+ gross margins, 38% adjusted operating margins, 22% FCF margins, net cash, a Rule of 40 of 59, and record bookings is trading at **20x forward earnings and 7.8x forward sales** after a 51% drawdown. The market is implying essentially no revenue growth beyond FY2029 — an assumption the optical TAM (30% CAGR to $112B by 2031) makes look too pessimistic. PEG of ~0.46 on a 28% four-year EPS CAGR is the kind of dislocation this framework is built to buy.

I stop short of Strong Buy for two reasons: (1) FY2028 consensus decelerates sharply to +19%, and that deceleration is the real debate — the market is pricing a 1.6T air pocket, and I can't prove it won't happen; (2) this remains a cyclical, high-beta (1.83), concentrated component supplier where one hyperscaler's capex digestion can gap the stock 20% in a day, as demonstrated twice in three weeks.

### 7. Key Risks

- **Order lumpiness / capex digestion.** Q4 data-center revenue grew 37% QoQ; Q1 is guided to only ~15% QoQ and total revenue to +6–8% QoQ despite a 62.7% YoY comp. That deceleration is precisely what broke the stock. A hyperscaler pushing out 1.6T orders would hit hard.
- **Customer and end-market concentration.** Data center is 44% of revenue and flows through a handful of module makers and hyperscalers. Customer concentration is the single largest fundamental risk.
- **Competitive intensity.** Broadcom and Marvell are integrating more analog/DSP content; Credo owns active copper; Coherent/Lumentum and Chinese InP suppliers compete on lasers; the NPO transition could shift value toward switch/ASIC vendors and away from pluggable-optics component suppliers.
- **Margin sustainability.** The 62–63% guided gross margin and 38% adjusted operating margin depend on mix and fab utilization. FY27 capex steps to $100–110M (from $64.4M) and R&D/production-ramp costs rise — FCF margin likely compresses from 22%, and the adjusted-to-GAAP gap is wide (FY26 adj. EPS $5.60 vs GAAP $4.31; SBC is $87.8M, ~6.6% of revenue).
- **Geographic/regulatory.** Significant China and Asia-Pacific revenue exposure carries export-control and tariff risk; telecom (~17% of revenue) remains mixed with broadband/PON softness.
- **Sentiment/beta.** A 1.8 beta and a demonstrated willingness to de-rate 50%+ means position sizing matters as much as the thesis.
- **Estimate quality caveat.** FY2029–FY2030 consensus figures rest on a single analyst — do not anchor terminal value on them.
