## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US–China / Taiwan Strait entanglement (Probability: High | Impact: High)**
SpaceX sits at the intersection of US national-security space and Chinese coercion. Lawmakers have publicly accused the company of withholding Starshield services from US troops in and around Taiwan, a possible **breach of Pentagon contractual obligations** (Bloomberg reported SpaceX denied this, saying it is "in full compliance"). Taiwan's 100% foreign-ownership rule also blocks Starlink. Any Taiwan contingency would simultaneously (a) spike demand for Starshield/Golden Dome, (b) invite Chinese retaliation against Starlink's Asian licensing and against Musk's other ventures (Tesla Shanghai), and (c) trigger ITAR/EAR scrutiny. This is a structural, non-diversifiable exposure.

**Risk 2 — Conflict-of-interest and politicisation of US government contracts (Probability: High | Impact: Medium–High)**
SpaceX's revenue is heavily tied to Washington: ~$5.9–6.45B in FY2026 Space Force awards including a **$4.16B Golden Dome** missile-tracking contract (Golden Dome program total ~$185B), plus NASA business. Musk's DOGE stint, Trump's personal purchase of SpaceX debt before signing a space policy memo, and House/Senate investigations into NASA's ~$525M award create genuine **FCPA-adjacent, procurement-integrity and reputational risk**, plus reversal risk if the administration changes. The mooted Tesla–SpaceX merger (independent committees formed, Oct 2026) would layer additional related-party and disclosure complexity onto a $2T entity. Conversely, this proximity is also a competitive moat — the risk is two-sided, not purely negative.

**Risk 3 — Starlink as a geopolitical instrument: Ukraine service restrictions and sanctions leakage (Probability: High | Impact: Medium)**
Bipartisan US senators demanded an Inspector General investigation after reported Starlink speed restrictions on Ukrainian terminals between 12 and 14 October 2026, impacting drone operations (NYT, BBC, Payload). Separately, Starlink's grey-market terminals in sanctioned or prohibited markets (Russia, Iran, Sudan) create AML/sanctions and export-control exposure. Starlink operates in 160+ markets, several of which are authoritarian or under sanction — each is a potential licensing, human-rights or diversion flashpoint (Namibia, South Africa, Brazil precedents).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active litigation / enforcement**
- **DOJ discrimination suit** — alleges SpaceX wrongly rejected refugees and asylees in hiring; ongoing.
- **NLRB** — multiple complaints (including unlawful severance agreements) and SpaceX's own constitutional challenge to NLRB adjudication; the agency's structure is itself before the Supreme Court in related DOGE/agency-reach litigation.
- **FAA** — $633,009 proposed fine over two 2023 Falcon 9 launches; SpaceX disputes and threatened suit for "regulatory overreach."
- **Environmental litigation** — conservation groups sued the FAA over Starship/Starbase impacts; SpaceX joined the FAA as co-defendant seeking dismissal. A full EIS requirement would slow Starship cadence.
- **Congressional investigations** — Senate IG referral on Ukraine Starlink disruptions; House Oversight on NASA contract awards and the Trump debt purchase; GAO report on DOGE ethics.

**Regulatory posture**
- **FCC** — licensing, spectrum, and Special Temporary Authorizations are the gating item for Starlink capacity; the FCC is also moving to waive NEPA review for space operations (a tailwind).
- **FAA/AST** — launch licensing and mishap investigations remain the binding constraint on Starship cadence.
- **EU** — GDPR, the EU AI Act (relevant now that AI is the largest segment) and national telecom licensing.
- **Export control** — ITAR/EAR govern satellites, Starshield and launch technology; the Taiwan Starshield dispute is the live fault line.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **ITAR/EAR & Starshield** — defense-grade satellite technology plus foreign operations in 160+ markets; any diversion or unauthorized technical disclosure is a criminal-exposure event.
2. **Sanctions/AML leakage** — grey-market Starlink terminals in Russia, Iran and Sudan; distributorship and reseller controls appear weak relative to the footprint.
3. **Government procurement integrity / conflicts** — DOGE overlap, presidential debt purchase, and related-party merger discussions invite FCPA-style and procurement-integrity scrutiny; lobbying-disclosure and ethics issues likely.
4. **Data privacy** — Starlink subscriber data across 160+ jurisdictions, including GDPR and emerging data-localisation regimes (India, Brazil).
5. **Employment/labour** — DOJ and NLRB actions signal weak immigration-verification and labour-relations controls.
6. **Governance concentration** — dual/tri-class structure with founder-controlled Class B and family-transfer provisions (per the restated certificate of formation) limits shareholder remedies and heightens key-person/political risk.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Majority of revenue and nearly all defense revenue are US-generated (medium sovereign risk), but the company's strategic centrality to US national security (Golden Dome, Starshield, NSSL), its 160+ market licensing footprint, its role as a geopolitical instrument in Ukraine and Taiwan, and an unusually dense cluster of active US enforcement actions and congressional probes push the composite to High. It is not "Very High" because SpaceX faces **no entity-level sanctions or export-control designation**, is domiciled in a low-risk jurisdiction, and its core US government demand is currently politically supported — though that support is itself the source of the conflict-of-interest risk.
