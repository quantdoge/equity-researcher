## 2. Consensus Assessment: **HIGH agreement on risk, SPLIT on action — and the split is illusory**

**Consensus (High):**
1. **The balance sheet and dividend are the binding constraint**, not the earnings recovery. Four independent desks (Value, Financial Risk, Short, Quant) converge on the same arithmetic from different methods: FCF ≈ zero, dividend ≈ $980M, shortfall ≈ $900M, funded from the balance sheet. The Altman Z of 1.03 is confirmed by two desks.
2. **Legal overhang is real and un-diversifiable.** Geo/Legal and Non-Fin Risk both rate it the dominant risk; the civil MTD denial is the corroborating fact.
3. **The DS Smith deal destroyed capital** — ~$2.3–4.0bn of impairments (the range itself is a disclosure red flag) — and the spin is the right response, late.
4. **The bear/bull dispersion runs $15 → $40 around a $27 mean.** Nobody disputes the width.

**Contested (Low):**
- **Value 7.0 vs Quant 1.0 momentum.** This is not a disagreement — it is the same fact viewed from two horizons. Cheap *because* estimates fall and quality deteriorates. The Quant desk's own caveat ("value trap") concedes the point.
- **Alt Data's bullish pricing signal vs Sector/Value's volume reality.** I weight pricing as real (+$100/t realised is hard evidence) but **non-recurring**: it is cost-recovery pricing into flat volume. It buys one or two good quarters, not a re-rating — Alt Data says so itself.

**Conflict resolution applied:** Value is bullish-to-neutral on assets; Short flags balance-sheet distress and Legal flags criminal exposure. **Per my framework, fraud/legal risk and solvency risk take precedence over asset value** — a $5.4bn tangible book does not pay a $980M dividend or a treble-damages settlement.

**Where the file is internally inconsistent — and this matters for sizing:**
Three reports value IP at three different multiples (Quant 6.8x, Growth 7.4x, Value 9.1x EV/EBITDA) because they mix **consolidated** and **post-spin RemainCo** denominators — and they treat **$28.98 as if it were the RemainCo price**, while Value subtracts only the 19.9% *retained* DS Smith stake. **The 0.36 DS Smith ADS per share is being distributed to holders.** So the clean entry price for the North American stub is roughly **$25–26, not $28.98**, and no desk verified the post-spin debt allocation (Value calls this "the single largest analytical gap"). **Every multiple in the file is un-auditable until the Form 10/8-K lands.** That alone is a reason not to commit capital this week.

## 3. Position Sizing: **0.0% — Pass**

Edge is negative-to-zero against a blended IV of ~$27 versus a $28.98 price. **Kelly fraction ≤ 0 → size = 0.** You cannot size a position where the payoff distribution is symmetric-to-negative and the left tail is a solvency event.

Beyond 0.5% you are expressing a *view against* eight of eleven desks to capture a 6.8% discount to a contested intrinsic value.

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Correlation (mine, 252d):** IP–SW **0.80**, IP–PKG **0.75**, IP–AMCR 0.63, IP–GPK 0.36, IP–SPY **0.32**.

Three consequences:

1. **This is not a diversifier — it is a high-volatility duplicate.** Anyone running a packaging or materials sleeve already owns this exposure at 0.75–0.80 correlation. IP delivers the *same factor bet* at **44.1% volatility versus PKG's 28.6%** — roughly 1.5x the risk for a 0.75 correlation. If the house view is "containerboard pricing is inflecting," **PKG or SW expresses it more efficiently**; if the house view is "IP is cheap," you must accept that you are buying beta to a factor you already own, with idiosyncratic risk layered on top.

2. **Beta-based risk budgeting will understate IP's risk by ~2.5x.** Beta 0.89 / R² 0.10 means **90% of variance is idiosyncratic** — DOJ headlines, dividend policy, spin mechanics. A risk system sizing IP on beta will assign it roughly market-level risk when its realised vol is ~2.5x the market's. Size on total volatility, never beta.

3. **The 0.32 correlation to SPY is a cost, not a benefit.** It is not diversification — it is uncompensated idiosyncratic risk: Sharpe −0.90, Sortino −1.41, max drawdown −47%. You absorb 44% vol and get no return for it.

**Factor tilt created:** negative momentum, low quality, high volatility, high leverage, small-cap-value/fallen-angel. Adding IP *increases* the portfolio's junk-value and leverage-factor loading at exactly the point in the macro cycle (bear steepener, BAA widening 22bp) where those factors are penalised. **It also correlates with the macro desk's own bear case:** IP is short rates-duration and long the goods cycle, in a portfolio that is likely already long cyclicals.

**The one genuine argument for the position:** IP is **not crowded** — a contrarian, out-of-favour, 16%-shorted name. In a deep-value book hunting *non-consensus* exposure, that is a point in its favour. It is outweighed by the fact that the *downside* is a solvency/settlement event, not a sentiment event.

**Substitution recommendation:** for containerboard exposure, **PKG** (14.0% op margin, no subpoena, 28.6% vol) or **SW at 7.75x EBITDA** — the Value desk's own anchor for IP's fair multiple. SW gives you the same European containerboard exposure IP just spent $4bn writing down, at the multiple IP should trade at.
