### 1. Growth Story

International Paper is not a disruptive innovator — it is a **scale-cost consolidation play masquerading as a growth story**. The 2025 DS Smith acquisition doubled revenue to $24.9B and created two regional corrugated packaging champions; the company is now **splitting them apart on December 7, 2026** (0.36 DS Smith ADS per IP share, London listing, IP retaining 19.9%). The investable thesis post-spin is a **North American containerboard oligopolist with self-help operating leverage**: PS North America segment operating profit doubled sequentially from $204M (Q2'26) to $427M (Q3'26), with Q4 guided to $460–500M.

The honest growth read: **top-line growth is negative organically** (2026E revenue −3% YoY), the +33.7% FY25 growth was 100% acquisition-driven, and the recurring driver is price + cost, not volume. This is a cyclical value/special-situation, not a compounder.

### 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

- **TAM**: global paper/corrugated packaging ~$400–450B; containerboard subset ~$180B, growing **3–4% CAGR** (GDP+). North American containerboard, IP's post-spin core, is ~$60–70B.
- **Penetration**: IP is already the #1/#2 North American containerboard producer with roughly 25–30% of capacity. **There is very little penetration runway left in the core market** — this is the central problem for a growth mandate. Growth must come from *price*, *share*, *mix* and *cost*, not from penetrating an underpenetrated market.
- **The one real secular tailwind**: fiber-for-plastic substitution (EU PPWR, extended producer responsibility) and e-commerce packaging. This expands the TAM modestly and benefits IP incidentally — but IP is a commodity supplier to that shift, not its architect. There is **no proprietary technology, data network effect, or platform dynamic** here. The moat is scale, mill footprint, and fiber self-sufficiency — a cost moat, not an innovation moat.
- **Why IP vs. peer?** Scale and a lower-cost asset base after closing/converting high-cost capacity (Pine Hill suspended, Riverdale converted, NORPAC acquired). That is an execution-based advantage, which is inherently less durable than an innovation moat.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Dec 7, 2026 — DS Smith spin-off completes** (0.36 ADS/share; London listing; IP keeps 19.9%). Removes the $1.38B EMEA impairment overhang, ends consolidated margin drag, and creates two cleaner comparables. Highest-impact event on the calendar.
2. **Q4 2026 results (~Feb 4, 2027)** — first clean RemainCo print. PS NA guide: Adj EBITDA $700–740M, op profit $460–500M. Expect **dividend reset and post-spin capital allocation framework** — the single biggest unresolved question.
3. **Containerboard price realization** — $70/ton increases working through Q4'26 into Q1'27; every $50/ton ≈ meaningful EBITDA leverage given ~$2.3B NA base. Watch published index vs. realized box price lag.
4. **NORPAC integration + Riverdale ramp** — full-quarter contributions compound the cost-out; potential Pine Hill restart as a volume signal.
5. **Balance-sheet actions** — ~$9.6B net debt; management has floated ~$2B of debt reduction and the 19.9% DS Smith stake is a monetizable asset. Deleveraging at 1.0x book would re-rate equity.

### 5. Alternative Data Signals

**Supporting:**
- Q3'26 gross margin jump (27.6% → 31.2%) and PS NA op profit +109% QoQ — hard evidence that cost-out and pricing are landing, not just narrative.
- Guidance *narrowing* to a credible band ($2.27–2.31B NA EBITDA) and three consecutive EPS beats after a period of blowups suggests guidance is now sandbagged.
- Analyst revision score +30% on out-year EPS — estimate momentum has turned up.
- Job postings: ~460 open roles on Indeed, overwhelmingly hourly plant/maintenance labor (Fort Wayne, Amarillo, Chicago) — consistent with disciplined cost-out and mill productivity focus rather than bloated overhead. No white-collar hiring surge.

**Refuting:**
- Management explicitly cites a **"softer demand environment"** with *lower volumes* in both NA and EMEA. Price is doing all the work; without volume, the recovery is fragile.
- Input costs (recovered fiber, energy, **diesel**) forced a **$145M / $110M-at-midpoint guidance cut** in FY26 — the company is a price-taker on a meaningful slice of its cost base.
- The stock fell 38% peak-to-trough ($46.68 Feb → $28.98 Nov) despite improving sequential EBITDA — the market is discounting the durability of the price hikes, not the cost-out.
- Google Trends ("cardboard boxes," US, 5-yr) **failed with a 429 rate-limit** — could not validate e-commerce packaging demand. **Gap acknowledged.**

### 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

**As a growth investment: fails the screen.** Organic revenue growth is negative, Rule of 40 on an organic basis is roughly −4, FCF is negative-to-marginal, ROIC sits below WACC, and there is no innovation moat or underpenetrated TAM to compound into. A Cathie Wood growth mandate should not own this — the 5-year revenue implied by the market (~$27B, ~2% CAGR) is *achievable* but is not the kind of trajectory that justifies premium multiples or delivers 20%+ IRR from growth alone.

**As a special situation: genuinely interesting.** At 1.03x book, 1.0x EV/sales, ~7.4x EBITDA and a 6.4% yield, the market is pricing IP as a broken commodity name while sequential EBITDA is inflecting hard. Post-spin, RemainCo (PS NA, ~$17B revenue, ~$2.3B EBITDA growing toward $3B) plus a 19.9% DS Smith stub arguably sums to more than the current $25B EV.

**Why Hold rather than Buy:** (a) the ~$39.5 consensus target is **pre-spin and will mechanically reset** when DS Smith distributes — headline "36% upside" is overstated; (b) the dividend is 2.4x uncovered and a cut is likely, which caps near-term sentiment; (c) volume demand is still deteriorating. **Upgrade to Buy on:** two consecutive quarters of positive NA volume growth AND PS NA EBITDA run-rate ≥$3.0B annualized AND a clearly articulated, funded post-spin dividend. **Downgrade to Sell** if containerboard price rolls over before Q1'27.

### 7. Key Risks

- **Price-cycle risk (highest):** The entire thesis rests on $70/ton containerboard hikes sticking. Containerboard is cyclical; a rollover would collapse the EBITDA recovery the consensus 2027–29 EPS curve depends on.
- **Volume/demand risk:** Industrial recession or e-commerce normalization keeps volumes negative — price alone cannot offset indefinitely.
- **Spin execution & leverage risk:** Post-spin IP may carry disproportionate debt; dis-synergies, stranded costs, and a 19.9% DS Smith overhang could weigh on both entities. UK regulatory requirements have already curtailed EMEA disclosure.
- **Dividend reset risk:** $980M annual payout vs. ~$400M FCF. A cut would remove the 6.4% yield that anchors the value case and could force income-fund selling.
- **Input cost inflation:** Energy, recovered fiber, diesel — management has limited pass-through and already guided down twice.
- **Competitive capacity:** Smurfit Westrock and Packaging Corp of America capacity additions could pressure pricing in a consolidating but still competitive market.
- **Earnings-quality history:** A −192% and −129% miss within the last five quarters means guidance credibility remains a live issue despite the recent beats.
