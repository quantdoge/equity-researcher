## ESG ANALYSIS — INTERNATIONAL PAPER COMPANY (NYSE: IP)
**Sector:** Basic Materials / Paper, Lumber & Forest Products | **HQ:** Memphis, TN | **Employees:** ~62,600
**Prepared:** November 2026 | Note: ~529.5M shares outstanding at the May 2026 AGM; the data feed's $15.3B market cap appears stale/pre-separation adjusted and should not be relied on.

**Critical structural context:** This is not a stable ESG profile. IP closed its £7.8bn all-share acquisition of DS Smith in January 2025, assumed ~$3.6bn of foreign-currency debt, and then announced (January 2026) a reversal — separation into two companies. The board approved the EMEA/DS Smith spin-off on **23 November 2026, with distribution expected 7 December 2026**, retaining a 20% stake, alongside a $2bn debt-reduction target. Almost every ESG metric below is therefore mid-transition, and the post-spin entity will be a materially different (North America-weighted) risk profile.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (improving post-spin)

**Positives:** SBTi-validated targets — 35% absolute reduction across Scope 1, 2 **and** 3 under Vision 2030, with published sub-targets of −42% Scope 1&2 and −25% Scope 3 and a net-zero ambition by 2050 or sooner. IP reduced Scope 1&2 >20% (2010–2022) and has halved coal and fuel-oil use. Energy base is substantially biomass/CHP, so fossil carbon intensity is structurally lower than cement, steel or chemicals peers despite high gross energy use. Products are largely recyclable, and PPWR (EU packaging rules) is a demand tailwind for fibre-based formats.

**Negatives:** Absolute Scope 1 emissions remain large (integrated kraft mills), leaving exposure to Boiler MACT, CSAPR and regional haze rules and to future US carbon pricing. **EUDR applies to paper/board/wood packaging — large and medium operators must comply from 30 December 2026**, requiring geolocation-level due diligence; IP's EMEA business carries this cost (and it transfers to DS Smith at separation, while IP retains 20% exposure). Physical risk is real: Southern US pine fibre is exposed to Gulf hurricane and drought stress, and several mills sit in water-stressed basins — press coverage has explicitly flagged drought as a margin squeeze. Environmental enforcement is a persistent, low-grade drag (see §5). **Direction of travel is positive:** spinning off EMEA largely exits IP from EU ETS and EU-level disclosure obligations.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

This is the weakest pillar and it is self-inflicted by the restructuring programme:
- **2025:** 20 EMEA facilities closed; ~1,400 EMEA positions eliminated.
- **2026:** at least 7 further EMEA closures and 700+ additional job cuts flagged.
- **US:** four facility shutdowns affecting 330 employees (CA 136, NJ 133, IL 41, KY 20); two Georgia mills (Savannah/Riceboro) with >1,000 employees; ~122 corporate roles in Memphis; ~1,500 IT roles transitioned to an outsourcing partner with the residual 250–300 exiting by July 2026.
- Mill towns are single-employer communities — closures impose concentrated social cost and carry licence-to-operate and local political risk.
- Pay ratio **164:1** on median employee pay of $90,506 — high but not unusual for large-cap industrials; a reputational rather than financial issue.
- Pulp/paper is a structurally high-hazard industry (caustic liquors, rotating equipment, confined space, recovery boilers). **Data gap:** I could not retrieve IP-specific TRIR or fatality statistics — the 10-K was unavailable through the filing tool. Treat safety as a material but unquantified risk.

Mitigant: much of the headcount reduction sits in EMEA and leaves with the spin.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Supporting:**
- Say-on-pay carried with ~**97% support** (May 2025); all 11 director nominees and all 2026 meeting items passed with ~91% of shares represented — no activist or proxy-advisor revolt.
- Compensation is genuinely performance-linked: 2025 CEO pay $14.8M, **down 28%** from $20.7M, with base salary only $1.0M (6.7% of package) — real pay-for-performance sensitivity, not a pay-for-tenure structure.
- Silvernail is a credible external operator hire (ex-KKR/Industrial) brought in to fix cost structure, and is delivering disclosed synergies ($514M target vs. $474M originally guided).

**Detracting:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality.** Silvernail added the chairmanship on top of the CEO role — a clear independence negative at a company where he is also the principal architect of strategy.
- **A 22-month strategic about-face.** IP paid £7.8bn in stock for DS Smith (Jan 2025) and is now distributing it (Dec 2026) while retaining 20%. Whatever the industrial logic, this sequence — largest acquisition in corporate history followed rapidly by reversal — is evidence of weak capital-allocation discipline and elevated strategic risk appetite from a first-two-year CEO.
- **Financial flexibility.** Free cash flow has turned negative with elevated multi-year capex against a 5.5% dividend yield — dividend coverage is a live governance question, and a $2bn debt-reduction target implicitly concedes the balance sheet was stretched by the deal.
- Board size 11, but I could not verify the independence split or lead-independent-director authority (proxy unavailable via the filing tool).

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US:** EPA Clean Air Act / Clean Water Act / RCRA; TSCA (PFAS reporting); **California SB 253/261** climate disclosure (IP exceeds revenue thresholds) — note the SEC climate rule remains stayed, so state law is the binding US disclosure regime. Five state packaging-EPR regimes (ME, OR, CO, CA, MN) raise producer-responsibility costs, partly offset by fibre's recyclability advantage.
- **EU:** **EUDR (30 Dec 2026)** — highest-stakes near-term item; **PPWR** (tailwind); **EU ETS** for EMEA mills (exits IP at spin); **CSRD** — post-separation IP's EU turnover likely falls below the €150m threshold, **materially reducing disclosure burden**, while spun-off DS Smith inherits CSRD/EUDR compliance.
- **CBAM:** not applicable to paper — a genuine relative advantage.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** ~$3.6bn of assumed foreign-currency debt post-DS Smith, but I found **no verified sustainability-linked loan or bond pricing grid** for IP. Flag as unverified rather than assume.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Solid, SBTi-validated environmental framework and an engaged, performance-linked pay structure prevent a Laggard classification. But an aggressive, repeated restructuring programme, a chronic environmental-enforcement record, CEO/Chairman duality and demonstrable capital-allocation volatility block a Leader rating. Environmental and social risk are largely "cost of doing business" for the sector; **governance is the differentiating weakness.**

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (with a governance caveat)

ESG factors do **not** independently alter the investment case — there is no stranded-asset cliff, no CBAM exposure, no exclusion-level controversy, and the environmental trajectory improves as IP sheds its EU-regulated EMEA assets. Two-way balance:

- **Supportive:** the 7 December 2026 spin-off sharply cuts EU regulatory drag (EUDR, EU ETS, CSRD), simplifies the story, and $2bn of debt reduction lowers leverage-led cost of capital. Retaining 20% keeps optionality.
- **Adverse:** the real ESG risk is **governance — capital allocation and balance-sheet discipline.** A CEO who is also Chairman, having bought and then effectively reversed the largest deal in company history within 22 months, with negative free cash flow and a 5.5% dividend yield, is a thesis-quality issue that belongs in the governance risk section of the memo, not the ESG appendix.

**Recommendation:** treat ESG as **neutral to the rating**; monitor (i) dividend coverage and FCF inflection post-spin, (ii) whether the 20% DS Smith stake is monetised opportunistically, (iii) any PFAS naming of IP in municipal water suits, and (iv) whether an independent chair is appointed as the post-spin board is constituted.

**Data gaps disclosed:** 10-K risk factors and DEF 14A were not retrievable through the filing tool (both returned "not found") — board independence split, IP-specific safety metrics, verified assurance-level emissions data, and ESG-linked debt covenants could not be confirmed and are stated as gaps rather than estimated.
