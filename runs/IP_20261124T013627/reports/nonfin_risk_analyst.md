# INTERNATIONAL PAPER COMPANY (IP) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**As of market close 23 Nov 2026 · Price $28.98 · Market cap ~$15.3B · CEO & Chairman: Andrew K. Silvernail**

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (deteriorating)**

**Positives**
- Say-on-pay support of **97%** at the May 2025 AGM — no shareholder revolt on compensation.
- CEO 2025 pay **$14.8M, down 28%** from $20.7M, with **82% in equity** and only 9% in non-equity incentive — pay is genuinely variable and fell with performance (the stock is down ~38% from its Feb 2026 high).
- Board is being refreshed (Ryerkerk, Collins added 2026) and directors are buying stock with their own money.
- Board formally approved the EMEA spin-off (23 Nov 2026) with a stated $2B debt-reduction target — an explicit deleveraging commitment.

**Concerns**
- **CEO/Chairman duality.** Silvernail was elected Chairman on becoming CEO (Sept 2024) and holds both roles through the most complex period in the company's history (integration + separation + antitrust probe). This concentrates power exactly when independent challenge is most needed; there is no disclosed lead independent director with equivalent authority.
- **Capital-allocation credibility.** The company paid ~£5.8B (£7.8B EV) for DS Smith (closed Jan 2025), then took a **$1.38B non-cash goodwill impairment** on that EMEA business by Q3 2026, then announced a spin-off of the same asset (Jan 2026) and is now separating it (expected 7 Dec 2026, retaining a 19.9% stake). Acquire → impair → divest within ~20 months is a strategy reversal that raises questions about M&A diligence and board oversight, not just market conditions.
- **New Executive Severance Plan** adopted in 2026 to replace the 2005 policy, plus a 2026 retention 8-K — standard for a separation, but flag for change-in-control/severance quantum and double-trigger mechanics as the spin-off completes.
- **Weakened incentive differentiation:** removal of the individual performance modifier in the annual incentive plan reduces pay dispersion between strong and weak performers.
- *Data gap:* IP's FY2025 10-K and DEF 14A were not retrievable through the filing tool (proxy details sourced secondhand, filed 27 Mar 2026). Board independence ratios, related-party transactions and legal accruals could not be independently verified.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Separation + integration execution (highest operational risk).** Running a carve-out of EMEA (DS Smith) while simultaneously integrating North American assets and cutting $250–350M of cost and targeting $900M–$1.1B of synergies. Stranded-cost allocation, TSA/IT disentanglement, a retained 19.9% minority stake, and a CEO who publicly called 2027 a "slog." Management bandwidth is the binding constraint.
- **Footprint rationalization and key-person dependency.** Dozens of closures/layoffs — Savannah, Riceboro, Georgetown, Orange and Riegelwood mills, four packaging plants (330 employees), 1,100+ Georgia jobs — create labour-relations, local-political and community risk, plus risk to the $1.3–1.4B FY26 NA operating profit target if curtailment and mill-restart costs run hot. Q3 2026 delivered a **$1.10B loss and $(267)M free cash flow**, so execution has no cushion. The turnaround rests heavily on Silvernail (ex-KKR) personally, with a relatively new CFO.
- **Input-cost and asset concentration.** Q3 showed a **$55M hit from energy, fuel and recovered-fibre inflation**; the system depends on a small number of very large, continuous-process containerboard mills in the US Southeast (fibre basket, hurricane/weather, single-point-of-failure assets). No material cyber incident was disclosed, but OT/ICS exposure in continuous-process manufacturing is inherently high and is *not* publicly evidenced either way — treat as unassessed, not clean.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Antitrust headlines are the brand risk.** Coverage in Bloomberg, Transport Topics and trade press explicitly pairs "International Paper" with "price-fixing investigation." For a B2B supplier whose customers are large consumer-goods and e-commerce accounts, this touches procurement teams and contract negotiations directly.
- **Environmental/community conflict is chronic, not episodic:** persistent odour complaints and a class action at Bogalusa, a $500K federal fine, PFAS litigation, and multiple mill-closure towns. Local press coverage ("terrifying to think about") is unfriendly.
- **Political-optics entanglement:** reporting that a senior EPA nominee previously represented IP in PFAS suits creates a perception channel that can amplify negative coverage regardless of merits.
- **Market-perception damage is measurable:** the stock is down ~38% from its Feb 2026 high, Argus downgraded to Hold on 19 Nov 2026, and coverage now centres on "weak cash-flow coverage" — a narrative that tends to invite activist and short-seller attention.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Weighting: a live **DOJ criminal antitrust investigation** plus a **surviving treble-damages civil class action** (unquantified, unaccrued, overlapping discovery) would alone justify High. Layer on a **$1.38B goodwill impairment**, **negative free cash flow**, CEO/Chairman duality through a spin-off, and a two-front integration/separation programme, and the non-financial risk profile is decisively worse than a typical paper & packaging peer.

Mitigants that keep this out of "Critical": no SEC enforcement action, 97% say-on-pay support, genuine net insider buying led by independent directors, a clear deleveraging target, and a separation that — if it closes cleanly on 7 Dec — removes the impaired EMEA asset and simplifies the story.
