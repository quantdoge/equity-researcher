## International Paper (IP) — Forensic Short-Seller Analysis
*Price $28.98 | Market cap $15.35B | Net debt ~$9.66B | EV ~$25.0B | As of latest data (Q3 FY26, filed 2026‑11‑04)*

---

### 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **LOW**

Not "fraudulent risk" — I found no evidence of fabricated revenue. The problem is **earnings *reality* and capital allocation**. GAAP results have been loss-making for two consecutive years while management narrates "meaningful progress" on an adjusted-EBITDA basis. The gap between the two is the entire story.

| Metric | FY2025 | FY2024 | FY2023 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $24.90B | $18.62B | $18.92B |
| Gross margin | 29.5% | 28.2% | 20.4% |
| Operating income | **–$2.82B** | +$476M | +$1.19B |
| Net income | **–$3.52B** | +$557M | +$302M |
| Operating cash flow | +$1.70B | +$1.68B | +$1.83B |
| Capex | $1.86B | $0.92B | $1.14B |
| **Free cash flow** | **–$159M** | +$757M | +$692M |
| Dividends paid | $977M | $643M | $642M |
| D&A | $2.88B | $1.31B | $1.47B |

Two things jump out. **Capex ($1.86B) ran $1.0B below D&A ($2.88B)** — a capital-intensive converter harvesting its asset base. And the **$977M dividend exceeded free cash flow by ~$1.1B**, with the shortfall funded by debt. Composite earnings-quality score: **0/100**. Cash conversion **–0.48**; FCF conversion **0.045**.

### 2. Key Red Flags

**a) The DS Smith acquisition has been written down twice in under two years.** IP closed the ~$7B+ DS Smith deal in Jan 2025. It has since taken a **$0.96B goodwill impairment** (FY2025, reported Jan 2026) and a **$1.38B non-cash goodwill impairment** against Packaging Solutions EMEA (Q3 FY2026). Cumulative: **~$2.34B written off within 24 months of closing.** This is the single most damning forensic fact in the file — the purchase price was not supportable at the price management paid, and the market for the asset (Europe) has since been judged structurally weaker than underwritten.

**b) IP is now unwinding the deal while keeping the debt.** The EMEA business — the *reason* for the merger — is being spun back out as **DS Smith plc**. IP retains the leverage. Net debt went **$4.68B → $9.66B** on the deal; total debt **$10.8B**.

**c) Dividend is not covered.** ~$1.85/share annualized ≈ **$980M** vs. FY2025 FCF of **–$159M** and Q3 FY26 FCF of **–$267M**. Yield ~6.4% on a payout with no free-cash-flow backing.

**d) Altman Z-Score = 1.031 (Distress zone).** For a $15B large-cap, this is a genuine outlier. The driver is x3 (EBIT/Total Assets) at **–0.0785** — the asset base simply does not earn. Book equity of $14.83B is **63% goodwill and intangibles** ($9.37B); tangible book is only ~$5.46B against a $15.35B market cap.

**e) Revenue decelerating hard.** YoY: +42.9% → +32.8% → +31.1% → +1.2% → **–11.3%** → **–0.4%**. The acquisition-driven growth optics have fully rolled off. Q3 FY26 net sales rose on *price* while volumes fell — a company charging more for less, which is what a declining demand pool looks like.

**f) Serial M&A while free cash flow is negative.** NORPAC mill acquisition (2026) closed while FCF was negative. Two additional months of NORPAC depreciation hit Q3.

**g) "U.K. regulatory requirements" used to stop disclosing EMEA guidance.** Management withdrew EMEA guidance permanently ahead of the spin. Convenient, and it removes the ability to audit the segment being separated.

*Clean screens, for honesty:* Receivables grew **+31.7% vs revenue +33.7%** — no channel-stuffing signal. Inventory days **fell** 48.7 → 41.9 — no demand-weakness-through-inventory signal (likely destocking instead).

### 3. Beneish M-Score and Accruals — **a false negative, and I'll say why**

**M-Score = –2.763 ("Low risk").** Accruals ratio **–0.1373** in FY2025 (negative = apparently high quality). Cash conversion **–0.48**, FCF conversion **0.045**.

**I do not believe the clean M-Score here.** Both models are mechanically broken by the transformational merger and its impairments:
- **SGI = 1.337** — sales growth from *acquisition*, not organic, yet it feeds the score as if it were suspicious growth.
- **AQI = 1.215** — asset quality jump from consolidating DS Smith's PPE.
- **TATA = –0.1373** — total accruals to assets is deeply negative *only because* net income is crushed by ~$2.3B of non-cash impairments. Impairments depress accruals and make the company look conservative. **This is the mechanism by which a serial write-down story produces a "clean" manipulation score.**
- **DEPI = 0.716** and **SGAI = 0.842** — both distorted by the new depreciation base and scale.

Strip out non-cash impairments and accruals move toward zero or positive. The honest read: **the negative accruals are an artefact of value destruction, not evidence of conservative accounting.** An acquirer that overpays and then impairs will *always* screen as high-quality on accrual models. Do not rely on it.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

- **European packaging is structurally impaired.** PS EMEA operating result: **–$73M** in Q3 FY26 (vs –$80M in Q2). Still loss-making after restructuring and cost-out. That is why it is being spun off, and the market will price it accordingly.
- **North America is the only good asset** — PS NA operating profit **$427M** vs $204M in Q2, adjusted EBITDA **$843M** company-wide (+40% sequential). But the company itself cites a "softer demand environment" and lower volumes. Margin recovery is being driven by **cost-out and price, not volume** — a ceiling exists.
- **Containerboard is structurally oversupplied** with new capacity additions across the industry; pricing power is cyclical and reversible. A 6.4% dividend yield on a commodity converter with a distressed balance sheet has limited room if box prices roll over.
- **Moat verdict: weak-to-moderate.** Scale and fibre integration are real, but they did not prevent a $2.34B write-off of the acquired European position. Commodity conversion does not merit a premium multiple in either direction.

### 5. Management Red Flags

- **Capital allocation:** ~$2.34B of impairment within two years of a landmark deal; simultaneous new M&A (NORPAC) with negative FCF; buybacks in FY2022 ($1.28B) at far higher prices; diluted share count up ~53% (347M → 528M).
- **Language vs. delivery:** "meaningful progress on our strategy despite persistent macro headwinds," "core controllables," "conviction." Delivered: two years of GAAP losses, two impairments, a withdrawn segment guidance, and a dividend not covered by cash flow. This is textbook promotional framing over deteriorating results.
- **Insider activity:** 25 Form 4 filings in the trailing period, including **12 on a single day (2026‑05‑14)** — the pattern of equity-compensation vesting and tax withholding, not discretionary conviction. **But note: not one open-market purchase appears in the entire filing list, across a 40% drawdown.** Insiders not buying their own 6.4%-yielding stock after a $2.3B write-off is a soft negative. *(I could not verify transaction direction from filing metadata alone — flagged as a data gap, not asserted as sales.)*

### 6. Short Thesis Summary — and the bull case that worries me more

**The credible bear case:** A serial over-acquirer bought a European packaging business at the wrong price, has written off a third of it, is now spinning the remainder back out to shareholders while keeping the debt. The retained business generates negative free cash flow, pays a ~$980M dividend it does not earn, carries $9.66B of net debt against a 1.03 Altman Z, and reports GAAP losses in both of the last two fiscal years. Capex runs below depreciation. The 6.4% yield is a value trap, and a dividend cut is the natural catalyst.

**Why I am not shorting it here — the other side, honestly:**

- **The stock is already down 40%** ($48.61 → $28.98) and **~15% in the four weeks since Q3**. The thesis is broadly known and largely priced.
- **The impairment is non-cash and forward-looking harmless.** Writing down EMEA before spinning it out is arguably *good housekeeping* — it de-risks the retained balance sheet and removes future drag.
- **Sequential operating momentum is genuinely strong.** PS NA operating profit more than doubled QoQ ($204M → $427M); adjusted EBITDA +40% sequentially. FY26 PS NA guidance of **$2.27–2.31B EBITDA** and **$1.3–1.4B operating profit** is not a collapse narrative.
- **Post-spin, IP becomes a cleaner pure-play NA containerboard producer** — a candidate for re-rating toward Packaging Corp-type multiples, with self-help cost-out and a structurally better end-market than Europe.
- **Valuation is not stretched on normalized earnings:** consensus FY2027 EPS $2.56 (11.3x) and FY2028 $2.96 (9.8x); ~8x EV/EBITDA. Shorting a cyclical at trough earnings on peak multiples is the classic way shorts lose money — operating leverage cuts *against* you on any box-price or volume recovery.
- **Reject the idea that this is a fraud situation.** No receivables divergence, no inventory build, no auditor flag, no related-party signal. This is value destruction and balance-sheet stress, not accounting fraud. Short sellers who cry fraud here will be wrong and squeezed.

### 7. Contrarian Rating: **NO EDGE**

I cannot build a differentiated, risk-controlled short at $28.98. The balance-sheet and earnings-quality findings are real and serious — **Altman Z of 1.03, negative FCF, an uncovered 6.4% dividend, and $2.34B of impairments on a two-year-old deal** — but almost none of it is undiscovered, and the sharp sequential improvement in North American EBITDA plus a pending spin-off give the stock positive catalysts that a short cannot absorb.

**What would flip me to Short:** (1) a dividend cut *denial* paired with another equity or debt raise; (2) PS NA adjusted EBITDA failing to hit the $2.27–2.31B FY26 guide; (3) the spin completed leaving IP with net debt above ~3.5x retained EBITDA; or (4) box price rollover alongside falling volumes — the combination that would prove cost-out was masking structural demand decline rather than fixing the business.

**What would flip me long-biased:** EMEA separated cleanly with debt largely ring-fenced, and PS NA EBITDA run-rate above $3.0B, at which point IP is a self-help cyclical at 8x.
