# Sector Brief: Paper & Packaging (Containerboard) — International Paper (IP)

**Classification note:** FMP tags IP as *Basic Materials / Paper, Lumber & Forest Products*, but that sector's peer list (Linde, BHP, Rio Tinto, Newmont) is economically irrelevant to IP. I have therefore benchmarked against the true comparables — the North American containerboard and paper-based packaging complex (PKG, SW/Smurfit Westrock, GPK, AMCR, SON). FY2025 = calendar 2025 unless noted; AMCR fiscal year ends 30-Jun.

---

## 1. Sector Overview

North American containerboard is a mature, capital-intensive, cyclical oligopoly (~$60bn+ NA revenue pool) currently in an **artificially tight phase**: roughly **3.9–4.0m tons of US containerboard capacity (~10% of the industry) has been permanently removed** through mill closures in 2025, and producers have pushed through **~$100/ton of net price increases in H1 2026** across two rounds, with a third round of **$80–$140/ton announced for September 2026** (PCA leading at an "unprecedented" $140/ton). Critically, this pricing power is **supply-led, not demand-led** — box demand remains subdued, with Rabobank forecasting only **~0.7% annual NA containerboard demand growth through early 2028**, and containerboard production still down YoY in Q3 2026. Input costs are simultaneously inflecting upward (OCC/recovered fibre rising every month of 2026; energy and fuel inflating), so the sector's margin story is a race between realised price and cost-push. Structurally, high capital intensity, fibre self-sufficiency requirements, long permitting lead times and entrenched customer qualification cycles keep barriers high (top-4 NA producers ≈ 55–60% of capacity — *industry estimate, not tool-verified*), but the product is near-commodity with low switching costs, so rivalry is intense and discipline is the swing variable.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Supply discipline is delivering real price.** ~4m tons permanently closed plus a $80–140/ton September round; IP itself expects **$900m–$1.1bn of incremental pricing** benefit. Unlike prior cycles, closures are permanent, not idled — this raises the odds that increases stick.
- **Operating leverage to volume recovery.** Sector cost bases have been cut hard (IP executing a further **$250–350m** of reductions). With demand flat and capacity gone, even 0.7% demand growth translates into tighter operating rates and better fixed-cost absorption from a 2027 recovery base.
- **Raw-material/asset arbitrage for virgin-fibre producers.** Rising OCC costs (up incrementally every month in 2026) penalise recycled-heavy converters (GPK, parts of SW) while integrated virgin-fibre producers with captive wood basket — IP and PKG — gain relative cost advantage; IP separately flagged a **$55m Q3 2026 hit from energy, fuel and recovered fibre**, i.e. contained.
- *Supporting:* e-commerce/resilient food & beverage demand, sustainability-driven substitution from plastics into fibre, and US reshoring of manufacturing supporting medium-term box demand.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Structurally stagnant volume.** NA containerboard demand ~+0.7% p.a. to 2028; Q3 2026 production still down YoY. Price-led growth is a zero-sum game and reverses the moment discipline cracks.
- **Cost-push inflation eroding the price gains.** Recovered fibre, energy and freight are all rising; the September increases are explicitly cost-justified, which caps true margin expansion relative to headline price.
- **Consolidation indigestion / balance-sheet strain.** The sector just absorbed two mega-mergers (Smurfit-Westrock, IP-DS Smith). SW's operating margin has fallen from 11.4% (2023 pro-forma) to 7.1% (2025); IP posts a FY2025 operating loss. Integration, impairments and leverage are suppressing returns industry-wide.
- *Supporting:* tariff/trade uncertainty on cross-border box flows; customer pushback and long-term contract lag passing through increases; DS Smith's EMEA business facing structurally lower European containerboard margins.

## 5. IP's Competitive Position: **AVERAGE (improving from weak)**

**Positives:** Scale — IP is one of the two largest NA containerboard producers, with a low-cost, virgin-fibre-integrated mill system in North America and deep customer relationships. Post-spin (DS Smith distribution scheduled **7 Dec 2026**, IP retaining a **20% stake**), it becomes a cleaner, NA-centric packaging pure-play with a targeted **$2bn debt reduction** and ~$2.3bn FCF. Management guides to outgrowing the packaging industry by **2pp**, i.e. deliberate share gain.

**Negatives:** Underlying profitability of **~3% operating margin versus an 8.8% peer median and 14.0% at PKG** is a structural, not cyclical, gap — the cost base and asset footprint have been under-earning for years. FY2025 was a $3.5bn net loss with a ~$3.5bn non-cash write-down, testifying to value destruction in the DS Smith deal. Management is openly guiding that **2027 will be a "slog"**. Gross margin leadership is at least partly a reporting artefact, not proof of cost advantage.

**Verdict:** Average — genuine scale and improving price capture, but sub-par returns on capital and a multi-year self-help/restructuring story that must still be proven.

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL** (improving, but supply-dependent)

Pricing tailwinds are real and better-supported by permanent capacity removal than in prior cycles — the best set-up for containerboard in roughly a decade. But with demand at ~0.7% growth, cost-push offsetting price, integration overhangs at the two largest players and no evidence of volume recovery, this is a **cost-and-discipline-driven earnings recovery rather than a demand-led upturn**. Attractive for low-cost, disciplined operators (PKG); Neutral for the sector as a whole; and for IP specifically, the rating is contingent on post-spin execution against a 3%-vs-14% margin gap.
