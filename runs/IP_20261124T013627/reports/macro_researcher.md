## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional, tilting risk-off.** The US is in a **reflation-with-a-hawkish-Fed regime**, and that is close to the worst possible combination for a leveraged, cyclical, long-duration industrial like International Paper.

The defining feature is that the **easing cycle has reversed**. Effective fed funds bottomed at 3.63% in July–August 2026 and has since backed up to **3.88%** (Oct), consistent with a hike — yet CPI has simultaneously reaccelerated to **3.82% YoY**. That leaves the real policy rate at roughly **+0.06%** — effectively neutral, not restrictive, while inflation runs almost double target. The bond market has drawn the obvious conclusion: the **10Y has risen ~70bp since late June** (4.44% → 5.31% peak on Oct 5, now ~4.92–5.13%), the **30Y sits at 5.47%**, and the 2s10s has **steepened to +39bp**.

Critically, this is a **bear steepener, not a recovery steepener**. There is no inversion and therefore no classic recession signal, but the steepening is being driven by term premium, fiscal supply and inflation expectations — long-end repricing — rather than by growth optimism. That distinction matters enormously for IP: it raises the discount rate on a capital-intensive, debt-funded business without delivering the stronger goods demand that would justify it.

Growth is deceptively decent in headline terms (Q3 real GDP ~+3.4% SAAR) but the **goods economy is not participating**. Industrial production has stalled (103.85 in Sept → 103.40 in Oct). This is the "cardboard box recession" pattern: services-led GDP growth with soft physical volumes. Credit conditions are still accommodative in absolute terms but **beginning to tighten at the margin** — BAA spreads have widened ~22bp off the late-September low to 1.61%, putting BAA yields near **6.5%**.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Negative — Discount rates / refinancing (the dominant channel).** IP carries roughly **$10.3B gross / $9.2B net debt** on the latest balance sheet, up from ~$5.8B/~$4.8B a year earlier following the DS Smith consolidation, with ~**$918M of current maturities** flagged as of Q1'26 and interest expense running ~$120–125M per quarter. A 6.5% BAA yield versus legacy coupons near 4% means every refinancing permanently raises the interest line, and the 10Y at 5.1–5.3% is a direct hit to the multiple on a business with a ~6% dividend yield competing against a 5% risk-free rate. The board's target of **$2B of debt reduction** around the December 7 DS Smith spin-off is the right response — but it must be executed into the **worst issuance window in a year**.

**Negative — Inflation on the cost side, not the pricing side.** This is the subtle point. IP is currently winning on price — North American producers have pushed **three rounds of containerboard increases in 2026** ($80–140/ton in the third wave; a net +$100/ton in H1), faster than the normal cadence, and a second linerboard increase in four months is underway. But those increases are explicitly **cost-recovery driven** ("mills face costs, supply cuts"), with rising OCC, energy, freight, chemicals and labour. With 3.8% CPI and 5.7% three-month annualized inflation, IP is running to stand still — and FY26 North America adjusted EBITDA guidance was already **cut to $2.27–2.31B** despite the price actions. Cost-push inflation into soft volume is margin-negative.

**Negative — Growth composition.** Q3 net sales were **$6.2B, down 0.4% YoY**, with a $1.1B net loss (including a $1.31B after-tax special charge). Headline GDP strength is services-driven; IP's volumes track goods, e-commerce and durable consumption, where INDPRO shows no momentum. Box demand needs a goods restocking cycle that is not yet visible.

**Negative — Trade policy.** A universal 10% import duty and **50% tariffs on many Canadian imports (July 2026)** cut both ways for IP: cost inflation on cross-border fibre, machinery and chemicals into its Canadian mills, plus demand destruction among tariff-exposed customers, partly offset by import-substitution and reshoring benefits for domestic box plants.

**Mildly negative — USD.** Broad dollar +4.6% in ten weeks hurts translation of EMEA (DS Smith, GBP/EUR-denominated) results in the ~2 weeks before separation, and cheapens European containerboard imports into the US, capping domestic price realisations. **Post-spin, currency exposure largely disappears** — the remaining IP is a North American pure-play, which structurally reduces FX risk.

**Neutral-to-positive — Yield curve.** No inversion means the bond market is not forecasting imminent recession, and a steep curve is historically constructive for bank-like and cyclical credit intermediation. For IP this is a modest offset, not a driver.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **Unfavourable**

The rating is driven less by any single variable than by the **combination**: a hawkish Fed re-tightening into 3.8% inflation, a long end at 5%+, a leveraged balance sheet facing refinancing at 6.5%, and a goods economy that is flat while IP's own cost base inflates. IP's genuine pricing-power offset (three price rounds, capacity closures) is what keeps this from being outright hostile — earnings are being propped up by supply discipline rather than by macro tailwinds.

## 5. Surprise Scenario (Bear, ~30% probability)

**The one that matters: the Fed is forced back to 4.75%+ and the 10Y breaks 5.75%.** With CPI at 3.8% and three-month momentum near 5.7%, a hot Q4 print plus tariff pass-through could convince the FOMC it stopped too early. In that world: BAA spreads widen from 1.61% toward 2.25%, IP's refinancing of ~$900M+ of maturities prices near 7%, the equity's ~6% yield loses its appeal against a 5.75% risk-free rate, and box demand rolls over as the consumer retrenches. Combined with another EBITDA guidance cut, IP trades into the **low-$20s** — a further ~25–30% downside from $28.98.

The bull mirror-image (~20% probability) is cleaner but less likely: the Fed pauses, the 10Y retreats to 4.5% as inflation expectations anchor, the September containerboard increase fully realises into Q4/Q1 with continued capacity closures, and the post-spin North American pure-play re-rates on a leaner, $2B-lower-debt balance sheet. That is worth +25–35%, but it requires the inflation data to cooperate in a way the last three months have not.
