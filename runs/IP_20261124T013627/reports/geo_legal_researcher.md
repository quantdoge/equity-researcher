## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — DOJ Criminal Antitrust Investigation into Containerboard Pricing
**Probability of material adverse development: Medium-High (~55–65%) | Impact: High**

The U.S. DOJ empanelled a **grand jury in the Northern District of Illinois** and issued subpoenas in **late September 2026** to International Paper, Smurfit Westrock, Packaging Corporation of America and Cascades over the pricing and sale of containerboard (Bloomberg, 2026-11-05; The Conveyor, 2026-11-09; Packaging Dive). The criminal probe parallels a **civil class action filed July 2025**; in September 2026 Judge Mary Rowland **denied defendants' motions to dismiss**, finding sufficient allegations of a conspiracy. Smurfit Westrock was dismissed from the civil suit in October 2025, but predecessor units (Smurfit Kappa, WestRock) remain defendants; IP remains a defendant. All four companies say they are cooperating.

Context that raises concern: containerboard prices are ~20% above year-ago levels after **three rounds of price increases**, and the corrugated box PPI rose 8.9% y/y in September 2026 — the fastest five-month run since 2021. Analyst Adam Josephson (Sakonnet) notes no recent DOJ criminal price-fixing prosecutions in U.S. containerboard, which cuts both ways: it implies either a low base rate or a newly aggressive posture. **This is the single largest legal overhang on the equity.** Criminal fines, treble-damages civil exposure, and reputational damage to the North American core are all live.

### Risk 2 — U.S. Tariff and Trade-Policy Volatility
**Probability of material cost/volume disruption: High (~70%) | Impact: Medium**

U.S. tariff policy has been unusually unstable through 2025–26: Section 232 duties on steel, aluminum and copper were restructured effective April 6, 2026 (White & Case; Phillips Lytle), and the **U.S. Supreme Court ruled in late February 2026** in a way that altered the administration's tariff authorities, after which rates dropped and import values rose (Equitable Growth; Yale Budget Lab). A baseline 10% reciprocal tariff regime was imposed in 2025 with higher rates on dozens of countries (ICPA).

Transmission to IP: (i) imported inputs — recovered fiber, starch, energy, and machinery; (ii) **cross-border flows between U.S. and EMEA operations**, which matter acutely while DS Smith is still consolidated; (iii) demand destruction in industrial packaging as tariff costs hit customers; (iv) retaliatory exposure for EMEA-sourced product. IP is a price-taker on diesel, energy and recovered fiber — Q3 2026 results explicitly cited higher recovered fiber, diesel and energy costs as offsets to pricing gains.

### Risk 3 — European Regulatory Compliance Burden (EUDR, CSRD) and EMEA Demand Weakness
**Probability of compliance cost/penalty: High (~80%) | Impact: Medium**

The **EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR)** application date was postponed 12 months to **30 December 2026** for large companies (European Commission Access2Markets; EY Tax News, 2026). DS Smith has publicly stated it is working to comply. From that date, every import of an EUDR-relevant product into the EU must carry proof of due diligence in the customs declaration — a fibre-traceability obligation that reaches IP's pulp and recovered-fibre supply chains. Non-compliance risks shipment detention and penalties in IP's second-largest market.

Compounding this: **EMEA demand is structurally weak.** Q3 2026 Packaging Solutions EMEA sales fell 1.3% y/y to $2.28B with an operating loss of $73M, and IP recorded a **$1.38B non-cash goodwill impairment** on the EMEA business. The EU's new **anti-coercion trade instrument** (European Commission proposal) adds a further layer of transatlantic trade friction risk.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Antitrust / competition**
- DOJ grand jury investigation, N.D. Illinois — criminal, containerboard pricing. Subpoenas served ~Sept 22, 2026. IP cooperating. **No charge or fine to date.**
- Civil class action (filed July 2025), N.D. Illinois, Judge Mary Rowland — motion to dismiss **denied** September 2026. IP is a defendant. Defendants deny wrongdoing. Potential treble damages; class not yet certified per available reporting.

**Environmental**
- IP's estimated **probable environmental liability ≈ $271M** ($270M prior year), per the Commitments and Contingencies note (SEC 10-Q filing, via search index).
- Recent enforcement: Ohio EPA agreement March 14, 2025 ($4,400 penalty plus supplemental environmental project); Georgia wastewater discharge fines ~$28,000 (2024); EPA penalty $587,300 (2022); Connecticut environmental violation $750,000 (2001) — Violation Tracker / Good Jobs First.
- **Legacy asbestos litigation:** IP has been named in numerous asbestos suits arising from facility and product exposure (Mesothelioma.com). Historically self-insured/insured mix; ongoing defence costs.

**Corporate / transactional**
- **DS Smith spin-off:** Board approved **November 23, 2026**; distribution expected to complete **December 7, 2026**, subject to customary conditions and regulatory approvals. Taxable distribution; IP retains **19.9%**, to be sold in ~12–18 months. DS Smith ordinary shares to list on the LSE, with a Level II ADR on the NYSE (ticker SMDS). IP to **delist from London** by end-2026. Expected to reduce debt by ~$2B, exiting with ~$7.5B gross debt. Q3 2026 carried **$52M of spin-off-related charges**.
- **Q3 2026 results:** net loss $1.13B (vs. $1.10B prior year), including the $1.38B EMEA goodwill impairment; adjusted EPS $0.37 vs. $0.30 consensus; net sales $6.20B, down 0.4% y/y and missing consensus by 2.4%; NA box volumes down ~2% y/y.

**Trade / regulatory posture**
- U.S. sanctions and export-control enforcement is explicitly a DOJ priority in 2026 (JD Supra; Global Trade & Sanctions Law). IP's multinational distribution network creates theoretical diversion risk, but materiality is low given no Russia/Iran/North Korea operations.
- No SEC or FCPA enforcement action against IP identified in this review.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **HIGH**

**Rationale.** IP's legal risk is elevated and concentrated rather than diffuse: an **active DOJ criminal antitrust investigation** into the exact product that generates roughly two-thirds of revenue, running in parallel with a civil class action that survived a motion to dismiss. That combination is the dominant risk to the investment case and is not diversifiable by geography.

Geopolitical risk is more moderate and, importantly, **declining by design**. The December 7, 2026 spin-off of DS Smith converts IP from a 61/36 U.S./EMEA split into a North American pure-play, removing exposure to EUDR, weak European demand, the EU anti-coercion instrument, and a 30-country emerging-market footprint. Post-separation, the geographic risk profile would migrate to **Medium**, with the antitrust overhang remaining as the principal concern.

Two offsetting positives: (1) Russia exposure is fully exited (Ilim stake sold for $508M, 2023), leaving no material sanctions perimeter; (2) Asian/Pacific Rim exposure is negligible at 0.1% of sales, so US-China trade friction is not a direct revenue risk — only an indirect input-cost and demand one.

**Watch items for the next 90 days:** (a) any DOJ charging decision or leniency/plea development involving Smurfit Westrock or PCA — a co-defendant plea would materially raise IP's risk; (b) the December 7 spin-off completion and any last-minute regulatory condition; (c) whether the civil class action is certified; (d) EUDR implementation guidance in the December 2026 window; (e) any reversal or reinstatement of tariff authorities following the February 2026 Supreme Court ruling.
