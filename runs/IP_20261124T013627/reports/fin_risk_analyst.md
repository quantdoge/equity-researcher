## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: SUB-INVESTMENT GRADE (crossover, negative momentum)**

The headline GAAP numbers are alarming but need decomposition. FY2025 posted an operating loss of **-$2.98B** and a net loss of **-$3.52B** on revenue of $24.90B; TTM (Q4'25–Q3'26) EBIT is **-$3.43B**. This is *not* an operating collapse — it is **impairment-driven**. IP has booked roughly **$3.8B of cumulative goodwill/asset write-downs** on the DS Smith acquisition (closed Jan-2025): ~$2.4B embedded in FY2025 and a discrete **$1.377B non-cash EMEA goodwill impairment** in Q3'26 (confirmed in the 10-Q and press release). Reported EBITDA of -$97M and the tool's -99.6x net-debt/EBITDA are artifacts of negative EBITDA and are economically meaningless.

Stripping impairments, **normalised TTM EBITDA is ~$2.6B** (Q1'26 $658M, Q2'26 $549M, Q3'26 $805M ex-$1.377B impairment, Q4'25 ~$650M normalised). On that basis:

- **Net debt / adj. EBITDA ≈ 3.5x** — elevated but not distressed for a cyclical packaging name
- **Adj. interest coverage ≈ 6.5x** — adequate; GAAP coverage is -8.4x and unusable

**The credit problem is cash flow, not the P&L.** TTM operating cash flow $2.38B less capex $2.31B leaves **FCF of only ~$75M** against **~$980M of dividends paid**. The dividend is uncovered by roughly **$900M per year** and is being funded off the balance sheet. That is the single most important fact in this assessment. FCF yield is ~0.5% on TTM (tool reports -1.04% on FY2025 FCF of -$159M) — far below any plausible cost of capital, with the 10Y UST at **5.13%** and 30Y at **5.47%**.

Critically, capex ($2.31B) now roughly equals D&A ($2.20B). This is **maintenance-level spending, not discretionary growth capex** — there is little room to cut capex to rescue FCF without eroding the asset base. The leverage problem cannot be solved by capex discipline alone.

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue -20%

Applied to TTM revenue of **$24.18B** (→ $19.34B), using a ~65% variable-cost ratio consistent with containerboard's cost structure (implying ~$1.7B of EBITDA loss on contribution):

| | Base (TTM) | Revenue -10% | Revenue -20% |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $24.18B | $21.76B | $19.34B |
| Adj. EBITDA | ~$2.60B | ~$1.75B | **~$0.91B** |
| Net debt / adj. EBITDA | 3.5x | 5.3x | **~10.2x** |
| Interest coverage | 6.5x | 4.3x | **~2.2x** |
| OCF | $2.38B | ~$1.60B | **~$0.75B** |
| Capex (maintenance) | $2.31B | $2.31B | $2.31B |
| **FCF** | **+$75M** | **-$0.7B** | **~-$1.6B** |
| FCF after $980M dividend | -$0.9B | -$1.7B | **-$2.6B** |

**Conclusion:** a 20% revenue decline puts IP at ~10x net leverage and ~2.2x coverage — squarely in covenant-breach territory for a typical 3.5–4.0x net-leverage maintenance covenant on IG facilities. Even a **10% decline** breaks the dividend and pushes leverage above 5x. In the -20% case the company would need to cut the dividend entirely, slash capex below maintenance, and execute asset sales/spin-offs to avoid a refinancing crisis. Liquidity is the binding constraint: with **$815M cash against $8.59B of current liabilities and $1.96B of short-term debt**, there is no internal buffer to absorb a downturn — IP would be dependent on revolving credit facility access at precisely the moment banks tighten.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical, because normalised EBITDA remains solidly positive (~$2.6B), tangible equity is positive ($5.4B), debt/assets is a manageable 28%, and there is a concrete deleveraging catalyst (the announced DS Smith/EMEA separation, which the market is reportedly viewing as a route to shed DS Smith debt guarantees). But HIGH is warranted because **negative free cash flow after dividends, a collapsed current ratio, a doubling of short-term debt, and a distressed Altman Z-score** combine into a genuine liquidity and refinancing risk over the next 12–24 months.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Dividend is unfunded.** TTM FCF $75M vs. $980M dividends — a ~$900M annual shortfall funded from the balance sheet. Dividend yield of **6.4%** signals the market is already pricing a cut.
2. **Working capital collapse.** $2.21B → $0.12B in nine months (-95%). Current ratio 1.28 → **1.01**. This is the most acute deterioration in the dataset.
3. **Refinancing cliff.** Short-term debt doubled to **$1.96B** (from $992M) against only **$815M cash**. Average effective interest cost is ~4.1% on $10.1B; refinancing into a 5.13% 10Y / 5.47% 30Y curve raises interest expense structurally — a ~$100–140M annual headwind per $1B refinanced.
4. **Capital allocation failure.** ~$3.8B of cumulative impairments on DS Smith within ~18 months of closing. Retained earnings down $6.3B. This is a value-destructive M&A outcome, not a timing issue.
5. **Altman Z-Score 1.03 — deep distress zone** (x3, EBIT/Assets, is -0.079; x5, asset turnover, only 0.66).
6. **Capex ≈ D&A ($2.31B vs $2.20B)** — no discretionary capex left to cut; FCF cannot be fixed without shrinking the business.
7. **Stock at 3-year lows, -47% from peak, negative Sharpe** — and beta 0.92 masks 40.7% idiosyncratic volatility.
