# International Paper Company (IP) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price $28.98 | Market cap ~$15.3B | 528–530M sh | Net debt (pre-spin, 9/30/26) $9.33B | Div $1.85 (6.4% yield)**

> **Critical structural note:** IP completed the spin-off of its EMEA packaging business (DS Smith plc) on **December 7, 2026**, distributing 0.36 DS Smith ADSs per IP share and *retaining a ~19.9% stake*. IP is now essentially a North American containerboard/box pure-play. All pre-spin reported figures below (including the "P/E of −4.3x" and "P/B of 1.03x" from screeners) are stale and misleading. I have re-based the analysis on the post-spin entity; the post-spin capital structure is my estimate and is flagged as such.

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NONE** (weak "narrow" at best)

**What it does:** ~$15B of North American containerboard mills and corrugated box plants. Essentially a converter of wood fiber into boxes at a spread. It sells a near-commodity into a regional oligopoly (IP, Smurfit Westrock, Packaging Corp of America, Georgia-Pacific).

**Evidence the moat is absent or narrow:**

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| Pricing power | Fails. Segment operating profit swung from $1.3–1.4B (FY26) to deeply negative in down-cycles; margins are set by containerboard price indices, not by IP. |
| ROIC > WACC? | **No — through the cycle.** ROIC: 12.2% (2022 peak) → 8.3% (2023) → 3.4% (2024) → −11.0% (2025). Even the 5-yr pre-merger average (~9%) barely touched a ~8–9% WACC. This is the single most damning data point. |
| Cost advantage | Partial — fiber integration, scale, and machine assets. But IP has sold down its timberland over time and is now buying fiber at market. |
| Switching costs | Low. Boxes are specified, qualified and re-qualified easily; converters dual-source as a matter of policy. |
| Efficient scale | Some — new mills cost $2–3B and take 5 years, which does cap new supply. This is the only genuine moat source, and it protects the *industry*, not IP's returns. |

**Moat direction over 5 years: narrowed.** The 2025 DS Smith acquisition (~$7.8B) added a European business with structurally lower margins that IP is now unwinding — after writing off roughly **$4 billion of goodwill** across Q4 2025 (~$2.4B) and Q3 2026 ($1.377B). That is a direct, quantified measure of value destroyed rather than a moat widened.

¹ 11 months of DS Smith. ² Volatile quarter to quarter after purchase accounting. ³ Including goodwill impairments.

**Why the score is low, not zero:**
- **FCF conversion is broken.** Cumulative 2021–2025 FCF of ~$4.0B was less than cumulative dividends of ~$3.7B *plus* $2.4B of buybacks — the business has not self-funded shareholder returns for years. FY2025 FCF was **−$159M** against $977M of dividends; 9M 2026 FCF was **−$180M**. Every dividend dollar is being funded from the balance sheet or asset sales.
- **Leverage is elevated.** Net debt $9.33B at 9/30/26 (~2.9x depressed EBITDA). Even after the spin (my estimate: ~$6.8B retained) leverage remains ~2.5–3.0x EBITDA — high for a cyclical.
- **Working capital is a persistent drag** (−$834M in 2025; −$251M in Q3 2026 alone).
- **Revenue is unpredictable in price, stable in volume** — Q4 2026 PS NA operating profit guided to $460–500M vs $427M in Q3, so the *direction* is improving.
- **Positives:** the business generates real cash at the operating line (9M 2026 OCF $1.48B), assets are tangible and long-lived, and IP has meaningful liquidity. Post-spin, EMEA's cash burn leaves the consolidated numbers.

**Run-rate reality check:** PS NA FY2026 adjusted EBITDA is guided to **$2.27–2.31B** on ~$15B of sales — a ~15–16% margin, versus peak-cycle margins above 20%. That is trough-to-mid-cycle, not normal.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Smurfit Westrock (SW)** — closest structural comp | **7.75x** | 29.7 | 1.13 | 4.6% |

IP should not trade at a PKG multiple — PKG earns structurally higher margins with far less leverage and no integration risk. The right anchor is **SW at ~7.75x**, perhaps 8.0–8.5x for IP's stronger NA position. On that basis IP looks **fairly valued to modestly expensive**, not cheap.

**Scenario / intrinsic value (estimated, post-spin):**

| Scenario | EBITDA | Multiple | Op EV | Equity | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear: cycle stays weak, dividend cut, DOJ overhang | $2.2B | 6.5x | $14.3B | $8.0B | **~$15** |
| Base: trough EBITDA, SW-like multiple | $2.3B | 7.5x | $17.3B | $12.0B | **~$23** |
| Base: mid-cycle normalization | $2.6B | 8.0x | $20.8B | $16.0B | **~$30** |
| Bull: price recovery + self-help synergy | $3.2B | 8.0x | $25.6B | $21.0B | **~$40** |

**Blended intrinsic value: ~$26–29 per share** (probability-weighted: ~$27). At **$28.98 the stock trades at, or slightly above, intrinsic value.** There is **no margin of safety** — I require a 25–30% discount, i.e. a buy price of roughly **$20–21**, which is close to the bear case. A DCF is not usable here: trailing FCF is negative and the tool returns a meaningless negative value, so I have relied on normalized owner earnings and comps.

**The 6.4% dividend yield is a trap, not a return.** Post-spin FCF is likely **$0.7–1.0B** (~$1.35–1.90/share) against a $981M annual dividend cost. Management has indicated a post-separation payout policy of **40–50% of free cash flow**. Arithmetic says the dividend falls to roughly **$0.75–1.00/share — a 45–60% cut.** Anyone buying IP for the 6.4% yield is buying a yield that is about to halve. *(Note: the widely quoted "$2.3B annual free cash flow" is a management-adjusted, pre-integration-capex figure; GAAP FCF is near zero to negative.)*

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Below average on capital allocation; improving on operations**

- **CEO Andy Silvernail** (Chairman & CEO since May 2024; formerly IDEX, Kaman) is a credible operator with a genuine 80/20 cost-discipline playbook. Operating execution in PS NA is visibly improving (segment profit $204M → $427M → guided $460–500M across Q2→Q4 2026).
- **Capital allocation is the problem.** The DS Smith deal closed January 2025 — near the top of the packaging cycle, funded largely with ~$5B of new debt, for a business that IP has written down by ~$4B and is now spinning off less than two years later. Measured *value destruction*: roughly $4B of goodwill, plus a dividend now funded from the balance sheet. That is the opposite of Buffett's test.
- **The spin-off itself is sensible** — it removes a structurally lower-return, cash-consuming European business and, management argues, releases IP from DS Smith debt guarantees. Late, but correct.
- **Shareholder returns:** dividend frozen at $0.4625/quarter ($1.85/yr) since 2022 — no raise in four years. Buybacks were $1.28B in 2022 but trivial since (~$0.1B total 2024–2026). Retained earnings fell from $9.9B (2022) to $3.1B (Q3 2026).
- **Insider ownership** is modest and typical of a large-cap industrial; no owner-operator alignment here.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD — do not initiate; sell if held for income**

**Not a Buy.** This fails the Buffett checklist on the two tests that matter most: (1) **the moat** — ROIC has averaged below WACC for five years and is deeply negative in the current cycle; (2) **the price** — intrinsic value of ~$27 is below the current $28.98, so there is *negative* margin of safety, not a 25–30% discount.

**Reasons for the Hold rather than an outright Sell:** real asset value (15.5B of net PP&E), a genuine self-help operational turnaround underway, containerboard pricing that is directionally improving, and the removal of the European cash sink. Cyclical recovery to $2.8–3.0B EBITDA would plausibly produce $32–36.

**What would make me buy:** price below **~$21** (25%+ discount to intrinsic value); *or* clear evidence that mid-cycle EBITDA power is $3.0B+ (sustained containerboard price increases sticking, PS NA segment profit above $600M/quarter), which would lift intrinsic value to $34–38. A clean, disclosed post-spin capital structure at net debt below $5.5B would also materially change the math.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Dividend cut risk (high, near-certain).** Payout exceeds FCF; the reset to 40–50% of FCF implies $0.75–1.00/share. A 6.4% headline yield becoming ~3% will force income and dividend-ETF selling — this is likely a large part of the 23% YTD-2026 decline.
2. **Post-spin leverage opacity.** I could not source a definitive post-spin debt allocation. If IP retained more than my ~$6.8B estimate, fair value drops toward the low-$20s. **This is the single largest analytical gap in this report** — confirm from the Form 10/8-K before acting.
3. **DOJ price-fixing subpoena (November 2026).** IP and Smurfit Westrock received subpoenas over alleged containerboard price coordination. Even a modest fine plus legal costs and management distraction is material; a structural remedy would be worse. Combined with **~16% short interest**, expect violent volatility both ways.
4. **Cyclical trough extension.** FY26 EBITDA of $2.3B is 20%+ below mid-cycle. A demand recession or new capacity (NORPAC, plus industry restarts) could hold EBITDA at $2.0–2.2B into 2027, producing the $15–18 bear case.
5. **Integration/spin execution.** A taxable spin-off, a residual 19.9% stake in a company IP must now partially support, and UK regulatory requirements all create friction; stranded corporate costs post-spin may be larger than expected.
6. **Obligations not visible in my data:** the defined-benefit pension funded status and environmental remediation liabilities are material for legacy paper companies and were not available in this dataset — treat with caution.

---

**Bottom line:** International Paper is a mediocre business at a fair price being run by a management team whose last major capital-allocation decision destroyed roughly $4 billion of shareholder capital. The operational turnaround is real but is fully reflected at $28.98 against an estimated intrinsic value of ~$27. **Hold, with a buy price of $21 and a strong preference for Smurfit Westrock at 7.75x EBITDA or Packaging Corp if you want quality within this industry.**

*Data gaps acknowledged: post-spin capital structure (estimated), Q4 2026 actuals (report scheduled 2027-02-04), pension/environmental obligations unavailable, and the FMP "Basic Materials" peer set is not a valid packaging comp — packaging-specific comparables (SW, PKG) were substituted.*
