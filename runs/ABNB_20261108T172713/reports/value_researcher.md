### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Wide (with a regulatory cap)**

| Moat source | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Network effects** | Two-sided marketplace with 5M+ hosts and 2B+ guest arrivals cumulative. Neither side can replicate liquidity; direct network effects compound with every booking. |
| **Intangible asset (brand)** | "Airbnb" is a verb. 90%+ of traffic and bookings arrive organically/direct — the single most powerful evidence of brand equity in consumer internet. Near-zero customer acquisition cost. |
| **Efficient scale / capital light** | $14.0B revenue on $257M of PP&E and 8,200 employees. ~$0.9B of D&A against $14B revenue. Incremental capital required to grow is essentially zero. |
| **Switching costs** | Moderate for hosts (accumulated reviews, Superhost status, calendar/pricing tools, listing history — a new host starts at zero reputation). Low for guests, who multi-home with Booking/VRBO. Guest-side lock-in is the weak link. |
| **ROIC > WACC?** | Yes, decisively: ROIC 24.9% (FY25), 23.9% (FY24), 22.8% (FY22) vs ~9–10% WACC. Sustained 2.5x spread for four straight years is the hallmark of a real moat. |

**Has the moat widened over 5 years?** Yes on liquidity, brand and direct traffic; **no on pricing power in supply-constrained cities**, where the moat is legislated rather than earned. That is the honest caveat: the moat is Wide in the aggregate but geographically perforated.

| Metric (TTM Q3-26) | Value | Assessment |

Growth is partly being *bought* through payment/mix costs and lower-margin Services/Experiences. If that continues, operating leverage stalls. Everything else — conversion, net cash, buyback discipline, share count — is textbook excellent.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Multiples at $189.99** (EV ≈ $103.7B, using $10.1B conservative net cash):

| Metric | ABNB | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (TTM) | 35.6x | FY26E EPS ~$5.85 → 32.5x forward |
| EV/EBIT | 32.8x | Full for a 17% grower, but 22% margins + net cash |
| P/FCF | 22.7x | FCF yield **4.4%** (5.0% on EV) |
| P/owner earnings (ex-SBC) | 32x | The Buffett measure — this is the demanding one |
| EV/adj. EBITDA (EBITDA+SBC) | 20.8x | |
| vs. sector peer median P/E | 24.1x (+95% premium) | Peer set is low-growth Consumer Cyclical — a weak comp; I weight this lightly |

**Intrinsic value — two-scenario owner-earnings DCF** (net cash added separately):

| Scenario | Base | Growth path | WACC / terminal | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Conservative** — SBC expensed (real cost) | $3.5B | 15%→11% yrs 1–5, tapering to 3% by yr 10 | 9.5% / 3.0% | **~$175** |
| **B. Market convention** — FCF, SBC not deducted | $5.0B | identical | 9.5% / 3.0% | **~$243** |
| Tool DCF (10.9% growth, 10% WACC) | $4.6B | ~10.9% flat | 10% / 3% | ~$205 |

**My intrinsic value estimate: $185–$210, midpoint ~$195/share.**

**Reverse-DCF check — what does $190 require?** At $190 you are paying a 4.4% unlevered FCF yield that must compound owner earnings ~15% for five years *and* sustain a 25x exit multiple to clear a 10% annual return. My math: 5-yr owner EPS of ~$10.90 exited at 25x = $273 → **7.5%/yr**; at 30x = $327 → **11.5%/yr**. That is an expected return at or below the hurdle rate, not above it.

**Conclusion: the stock trades at approximately intrinsic value.** Buffett's 20–30% margin of safety requires **~$140–155**. ABNB at $128 a year ago was a clear buy; at $190 it is not.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Very Good (with one governance asterisk)**

- **Founder-operator:** Brian Chesky (co-founder, CEO) with Class B super-voting shares — long-term oriented and famously willing to kill products (Airbnb Plus, and the original Experiences) rather than defend sunk costs. That is intellectual honesty, though it also produced a 4-year product detour.
- **Capital allocation — genuinely strong.** No dividend, but $3.95B of buybacks in the last twelve months and ~$11B cumulative since 2022, executed at average prices well below today's $190. Share count is down 4% YoY despite $1.8B of SBC. Buying back stock below intrinsic value is the single best use of cash for a capital-light compounder — and they did it precisely when it was cheapest (2024–25, $110–140).
- **M&A discipline:** Minimal. No empire-building; acquisitions have been small and capability
