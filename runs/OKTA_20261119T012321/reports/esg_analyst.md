### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW**

- **Footprint (FY2025):** Scope 1 = **0 tCO₂e**; Scope 2 market-based = **284 tCO₂e**; **Scope 3 = 99,195 tCO₂e** (8 of 15 categories disclosed).
- **FY2026 total down 13% y/y** (per company GHG inventory, FY ending Jan-2026) — real, verified progress.
- **Carbon intensity:** ~99.5k tCO₂e against ~$2.9B revenue ≈ **34 tCO₂e per $1M revenue** — bottom-decile intensity for any sector. No CBAM, no process emissions, no stranded-asset exposure.
- **Targets:** −67% Scope 1+2 by FY2030 (FY2020 base); −42% Scope 3 business travel + commuting by FY2030; 100% renewable energy.
- **Gaps worth flagging to the analyst:** (a) targets cover only **2 of 15 Scope 3 categories**; (b) **purchased goods & services (Cat. 1) is 49% of the footprint (49,054 t) and sits outside the target boundary** — meaning the target covers roughly a third of actual emissions; (c) **business travel is 38% of footprint and rose 13% y/y** (33,219 → 37,386 t) — the target is working against a growing base; (d) **AI compute energy is the emerging risk** — Okta has built an internal methodology to measure employee AI-tool energy and buys incremental RECs to offset it; this is best-in-class for a mid-cap software firm and offsets the growth concern.
- **Verdict:** Environment is a **non-issue for valuation**, and a mild **positive on disclosure quality**.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment — **HIGH**

Product security is Okta's social factor *and* its core business risk; they are inseparable.

**Positives**
- Disclosure depth above software-peer norms: CDP corporate questionnaire (2025), SBTi-aligned targets, category-level Scope 3, responsible-technology/AI-ethics programme, public "Secure Identity Commitment."
- Human capital: median employee pay **$289,597** (FY2025) — very high; low-wage-labour and union exposure effectively nil.
- Post-2023 remediation was substantive, not cosmetic: dedicated Security Engagement/Trust function, hardened support-system segmentation, third-party (Stroz Friedberg) incident closure attestation.

**Negatives**
- **Serial incident record** (see §5) has damaged the trust premium that is Okta's moat. Okta is a *third-party* identity provider to ~20,000 customers; one customer compromise is a systemic event for all of them.
- **AI-agent identity** (Cross App Access protocol, non-human identity governance) is a new and largely unregulated product-governance frontier — if an AI-agent credential is abused, the liability narrative lands on Okta.
- **Data gaps:** I could not verify current DEI representation data, EEO-1 status, or attrition/engagement metrics in this window; US corporate DEI retrenchment in 2025–26 makes historical Okta DEI targets stale. Treat as unverified, not as clean.

| Date | Incident | Est. financial impact |
|---|---|---|
| Mar 2022 | **LAPSUS$ breach** — support-system access, source code stolen; ~366 customers exposed | Reputational + remediation; underpins the securities suit |

| 2024–26 | **No new material breach identified in the ~400-day controversy window** | — |

**Pattern read:** Four incidents across three years, each exploiting *support/helpdesk human process* rather than core platform crypto. The concerning governance signal is **disclosure latency and scope creep of bad news** (134 customers → all support users), which is precisely the behaviour that generates securities litigation. Mitigant: no material incident in the last ~24 months, and the 2022-era class action is now settled and closed — the litigation overhang is largely cleared.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (mildly negative tilt on governance)

**For a long thesis, nothing here breaks the case.** Environmental is a non-factor; the $60M securities settlement is closed and immaterial to a $32.7B cap (~0.2%); there is no material breach in the last two years; and the S&P 500 inclusion / AI-agent identity narrative is untouched by ESG considerations.

**Three things I would actually underwrite as risk:**

1. **Governance-driven dilution.** ~29% of votes cast against the equity plan, with CEO pay +58% to $36.8M and a 179:1 ratio, tells you SBC discipline is a live shareholder flashpoint. Watch for a repeat revolt and for dilution guidance above ~3%/yr — that is a real, quantifiable cost of capital.
2. **Concentration of ESG risk into one binary event.** For Okta, the entire S-risk profile is a single tail: a material breach of the *core* identity platform (not support systems). That event would plausibly cost multiple points of growth and several turns of multiple. This is uninsurable-by-analysis; it argues for position sizing, not avoidance.
3. **NIS2/DORA contractual drag.** EU customers will increasingly demand audit rights, exit plans and incident-notification SLAs. This raises cost-to-serve in EMEA and favours scale incumbents — modestly negative for Okta's international margin mix.

**Stewardship action if we engage:** (a) push for a de-classified board and a sunset on the Class B 10-vote structure; (b) require the Scope 3 target to be extended to Category 1 purchased goods & services (49% of footprint, currently untargeted); (c) demand that the board's audit/security committee include a director with operational CISO-level experience and that incident-disclosure materiality thresholds be formally documented.

**Bottom line:** ESG is **not a thesis driver for OKTA either way — it is a governance-discount and tail-risk-sizing input.** Rate it Neutral, with governance as the swing factor on whether the name deserves a premium multiple.
