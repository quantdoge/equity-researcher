# Okta, Inc. (OKTA) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price: $182.87** (Nov 13, 2026 close) | Market cap ~$32.8B | EV ~$30.8B | Beta 0.73

---

## 1. Growth Story

Okta is the neutral, vendor-agnostic identity layer for the enterprise, and it is attempting a second act: from securing *human* workforce identities (SSO/MFA/lifecycle, the 2017–2023 growth engine) to securing **non-human and AI-agent identities** — the fastest-growing new product in company history (*Okta for AI Agents* went GA in April 2026). The thesis is that every autonomous agent connecting to enterprise systems needs a governed credential, and whomever owns the credential graph owns the control point. That is a genuinely greenfield TAM layer stacked on a mature one, and it arrives just as Okta's core growth was decaying to ~11%. The problem: the stock has already been paid for this thesis — it tripled from $63 (April 2026) to $232 (October 2026) before a recent 21% sector-wide pullback.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- Global IAM market: **$25.96B (2025) → $42.61B (2030)**, ~10.4% CAGR (MarketsandMarkets). Bull-case estimates run to $48–78B by 2030–34 on broader definitions.
- Okta FY27 revenue of $3.22B against a ~$26B IAM base = **~12% penetration** — real runway on the core.
- The AI-agent layer is the actual option value: non-human identities already outnumber human ones by orders of magnitude in most enterprises, and **zero of them are governed today**. Okta's positioning (Cross App Access protocol extended to all SSO customers, Agent Gateway, partnerships with Google Gemini/Asana, plus the closed **Permiso Security** acquisition for identity threat detection) is a credible land-grab of a standard, not just a product.
- **Differentiation:** neutrality. Microsoft Entra is bundled and cheap but is not neutral across a multi-cloud, multi-SaaS estate; CyberArk (now Palo Alto) skews privileged-access; Okta owns the universal directory and the integration network (7,000+ apps), which is a real data/network moat.

**Honest assessment:** the TAM supports a durable low-to-mid-teens grower with 30%+ FCF margins. It does **not** obviously support a return to the 20%+ growth the multiple implies. Okta's own implied FY31 revenue of $4.47B would be only ~10% of the 2030 IAM TAM — so the market is *not* pricing aggressive share gain, which is mildly reassuring for the downside.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Q3 FY27 earnings — December 1, 2026.** Consensus $0.934 EPS / $816M revenue. Watch: cRPO growth (needs ≥14% to hold the re-rating), first full quarter of Permiso contribution, and any FY27 guidance raise above $3.226B.
2. **FY28 guidance — March 2027.** The decisive test. AI-agent products must bend revenue growth back toward 13–15% or the 9.6x EV/S multiple deflates.
3. **Cross App Access standardization.** If XAA becomes the default agent-authorization standard with hyperscaler adoption, Okta captures a toll on agent transactions — a new revenue channel not in consensus.
4. **Permiso/ITDR cross-sell into the installed base** — a pure attach-rate story on ~19,000 existing customers, high-margin incremental.
5. **Elevated threat environment.** DHS warnings of severe cyber threats plus AI-driven attack surfaces are a demand tailwind; identity is the first line of defense.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

This is a *quality* business that has become a *priced* business. The transformation is real and better than the skeptic case: gross margin up ~790bps in four years, GAAP operating margin from (59%) to a positive double-digit run-rate, FCF margin ~30%, net cash ~$2.3B, 100% beat rate, and cRPO accelerating to +14% against +11% revenue. Okta is no longer a story stock burning cash — it is a cash machine with an AI option attached.

But at $183 the arithmetic has closed:

- **9.6x EV/FY27 sales** for **10–11% guided growth**
- **46.6x FY27 non-GAAP EPS / 42.1x FY28** → **PEG ~4.4x**
- **~33x P/FCF**, on a cash flow number that is 63% SBC add-back
- The multiple already expanded from ~4x to ~9.6x EV/S in seven months; the re-rating is *complete*, and the revenue estimate line has barely moved ($3.19B → $3.22B guide across the year)

The Cathie Wood discipline cuts both ways: I pay premium multiples for *durable high growth*. Eleven percent is not high growth, and the AI-agent revenue is not yet visible in the numbers — it is visible in the narrative and the price target column. I want to see it in cRPO first.

**What would flip me to Buy:** (a) two consecutive quarters of cRPO growth ≥17%, or (b) a re-acceleration in FY28 guidance to ≥13%, or (c) a pullback to ~$140–150 (~7x EV/S, ~35x FY28 EPS). The recent 21% drawdown from $232 to $183 on a sector-wide cybersecurity de-rating is why this is a Hold and not a Sell — the froth is coming out without the fundamentals breaking. **If you own it, hold. If you don't, wait for the December 1 print; do not chase into it.**

## 7. Key Risks

- **Multiple compression (highest probability risk).** At 42x FY28 earnings for 10.5% EPS growth, a routine 20% de-rating needs no fundamental deterioration — as the Oct–Nov drawdown already demonstrated.
- **Secular deceleration.** 55.7% → 42.9% → 21.8% → 15.3% → 11.8%. If cRPO rolls from 14% to 10%, the growth thesis breaks and the stock has no valuation floor nearby.
- **SBC masking profitability.** 18.6% of revenue in stock comp; true FCF margin ~11%. Diluted shares still rising (158M → 179M since FY22). The "Rule of 40" headline is largely manufactured.
- **Microsoft Entra bundling.** Free/cheap identity bundled into E5 is the structural price ceiling on workforce identity; Okta must win on neutrality and agent-era features, which is not guaranteed.
- **Integration/M&A risk.** Permiso makes recent growth partly inorganic, muddying growth-quality optics; identity threat detection is contested by CrowdStrike, Palo Alto/CyberArk, and SentinelOne.
- **Trust concentration.** Okta is a single point of failure for thousands of enterprises. A material breach (cf. the 2023 support-system incident) would be an existential brand event, not a normal earnings miss.
- **Data gaps in this analysis:** net revenue retention and dollar-based expansion were unavailable, and the job-postings proxy failed. Both should be pulled from the 10-Q before acting.
