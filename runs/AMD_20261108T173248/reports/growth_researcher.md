## AMD — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price: $449.04 (close 11/6/26) · Market cap $733B · EV $728.5B · Beta 2.49*

---

### 1. Growth Story

AMD has completed its transition from a CPU share-gainer into the second source of scale for AI infrastructure. Data center revenue hit a record **$8.55B in Q3 FY26 (+97% YoY, +27% QoQ)** and is now **64% of total revenue** (vs. 47% a year ago), driven by EPYC server CPUs riding the agentic-AI wave *and* the MI450/Helios rack-scale ramp with Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle and Anthropic. Management guided **FY27 revenue growth of >80%** with server CPU revenue doubling and "data center AI growth accelerating faster than projected 90 days ago." The stock has de-rated ~31% from its $648 October high on industry-wide AI-capex anxiety and a gross-margin guide of ~55% (vs. 55.3% consensus), creating a rare entry point in a 50%+ compounder.

**Analyst estimates:** FY27 consensus revenue was revised up sharply post-print ($95.4B), and Street 2027 EPS has climbed ~17% in a single quarter ($15.76 → $17.45 over the period). This is a rising-revision regime, not a decelerating one.

### 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

AMD frames the AI hardware stack (CPU + GPU + ASIC + networking) as a **>$1 trillion market by 2030**, with the AI accelerator slice alone ~$500B. That is the single most favorable TAM in semiconductors. Penetration math:

- FY26E data center revenue ≈ $30B (annualizing Q3's $8.55B at accelerating rates) against a ~$400–500B AI accelerator + server CPU pool → **~6–8% share today**.
- AMD needs ~$70B of data center revenue by 2027 per Street models — roughly 10–14% of a $500–700B 2027 TAM. This requires *share gain*, not market creation, which is the realistic bear rebuttal: NVDA's CUDA moat and annual cadence (Rubin) plus hyperscaler custom ASICs (Broadcom/Google TPU, Trainium) cap AMD's ceiling near 15–20% — still ~$100B+ of revenue.
- **Differentiation:** only credible merchant alternative to NVIDIA; open ecosystem (ROCm, UALink) is the anti-CUDA wedge; full-rack Helios is the systems-level land-and-expand; EPYC's x86 incumbency is the underappreciated asset — agentic AI workloads are VM-heavy and CPU-hungry, and AMD's server CPU leadership is genuinely defensible in a way MI450 is not yet.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4 FY26 print (~early Feb 2027):** $17B guide; the key variable is gross margin — a print ≥56% would refute the "structurally dilutive rack mix" bear case.
- **MI450 first 1GW deployment with OpenAI (H2'26 → H1'27):** the 6GW/160M-share warrant partnership is the largest single revenue commitment in AMD's history; first revenue recognition timing is the swing factor for FY27.
- **FY27 full-year formal guidance (Feb 2027):** management has only said ">80%"; quantifying the number (and DC segment mix) is the re-rating event.
- **Meta Helios rack deployments scaling + Anthropic (up to $5B investment) and Oracle ramps** across H1 FY27 — multi-customer proof that AMD is a duopoly participant, not a single-customer story.
- **Server CPU cycle:** agentic workloads extending VM lifespans; continued x86 share gains against Intel (which just guided poorly and fell 29%) is a share-shift tailwind AMD captures by default.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **STRONG BUY**

At **25.7x FY27E EPS and 7.7x FY27E sales** for a business guiding >80% revenue growth with a Rule of 40 score of 54 and 79% EPS CAGR through FY28, the growth is being priced as if it decelerates abruptly. The market is implying roughly $92B of revenue at 8x sales — a bar already reached in FY27 consensus. You are paying for 2027 and getting the FY28–30 data center TAM expansion (~$234B consensus FY30 revenue) as free optionality. A median price target of ~$650 (+45%) alongside a 31% drawdown from highs and 100% beat history makes this the highest-conviction growth entry in large-cap semis.

**Position sizing matters** — beta 2.49 and a 29% peak-to-trough drawdown in five weeks demand a scaled-entry, multi-year horizon approach rather than a single slug.

### 7. Key Risks

1. **Gross margin structure (highest-probability risk).** Rack-scale Helios bundles third-party HBM/networking content, mathematically diluting gross margin %. Q2'26 missed GM (54% vs. 56%) and Q4 guides 55% vs. 55.3% consensus. If GM% stalls near 55% while revenue triples, the "scaling software-like margins" thesis breaks and the multiple compresses regardless of revenue.
2. **Customer concentration + circular financing.** OpenAI (6GW), Meta, Anthropic and Oracle dominate the AI ramp, and AMD is simultaneously *investing* $1B–$5B in those customers' equity. This inflates perceived demand durability; if a partner's funding tightens, orders and equity marks both impair.
3. **NVIDIA's cadence and the ASIC threat.** Rubin's annual rhythm plus custom silicon at Google/Amazon/Microsoft caps AMD's share; a CUDA/ROCm software gap that persists beyond 2027 would strand MI450 at #2 pricing.
4. **PC/client drag.** Client + Gaming fell 8% YoY with gaming −40%, and memory-cost-driven PC shipment declines (−4% industry) cap the non-AI offset to ~$3.7B/quarter.
5. **Supply and input costs.** Wafers, substrates, advanced packaging and especially HBM/DRAM inflation are the binding constraint on both units and margin — Su's "we secured supply" is a cost commitment, not a guarantee.
6. **Valuation reflexivity.** 169x trailing earnings, 100x EV/EBITDA and 109x P/FCF leave no room for a guidance miss; the Nov 5 −17% session after a *beat-and-raise* is the clearest possible evidence of a reflexive multiple.
7. **Macro/AI capex digestion.** The current drawdown is an industry-wide de-rating of AI infrastructure spend; a single hyperscaler capex pause would hit AMD harder than NVDA given its later-stage ramp.
