## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (trending weaker)**

**Positives**
- **Independent oversight exists**: Nora M. Denzel serves as **Lead Independent Director**, chairs Nominating & Corporate Governance, and was added to the Audit & Finance Committee (Aug 19, 2026). Board refresh continues — **Tim Ryan appointed to the board effective Aug 19, 2026**.
- **Clean financial certification trail**: Q3'26 10-Q (filed Nov 3, 2026) carries unqualified SOX 302 certifications from Su and the CFO; no disclosed material weakness, no restatement, no auditor change.
- **Say-on-Pay ≈ 85% support** (2026 proxy) — passing, but **materially below the ~92.3% S&P 500 average** for 2026 and below the ~90% threshold that typically signals broad alignment.

**Concerns**
- **Combined Chair/CEO**: Su has held Chair, President and CEO since **February 2022**. The Lead Independent Director mitigates but does not eliminate power concentration.
- **Extreme key-person dependency**: Su is simultaneously the strategic architect, the public face of the AI narrative, SIA 2026 Chair, a PCAST appointee, and a 2026 National Medal of Science recipient. There is no visible succession bench. This is the single largest governance-adjacent risk at AMD.
- **Compensation appears equity/performance-linked** (multi-year PSU structure implied by plan-based sales), which is directionally aligned — but the 85% support level and the scale of realized insider sales (below) suggest the incentive horizon may not match the multi-year AI ramp management is guiding to.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Single-foundry / single-node dependency on TSMC.** AMD is fabless: leading-edge EPYC "Venice" and Instinct MI450/Helios rack-scale silicon depend on TSMC's most advanced nodes. Any Taiwan geopolitical event, CoWoS/advanced-packaging allocation squeeze (Nvidia has priority claims), or yield problem at N2/A16 translates directly into missed guidance. AMD has **no second source for leading-edge logic** — this is a binary, un-hedgeable exposure.

2. **Extreme customer concentration, now compounded by financial entanglement.** Helios/MI450 launch customers are named as **Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI, Oracle Cloud Infrastructure** — a handful of counterparties. AMD has issued **warrants to customers**: OpenAI (Oct 2025, up to ~160M shares) and **Meta (Feb 2026, up to $60B of chips with warrants for up to ~10% of AMD each)**. This produced a **~$1.097B non-cash charge in Q3'26**, and the 10-Q references "other financing arrangements to support our customer." That is **vendor/circular financing** — the dot-com-era structure that converts a customer's inability to pay into your own equity and revenue. If a hyperscaler's AI capex appetite pauses, AMD's revenue, receivables, *and* warrant marks deteriorate simultaneously.

3. **Working-capital and free-cash-flow quality during the Helios ramp.** Q3'26 was a record ($13.4B revenue, +45% y/y; Data Center $8.5B, **+97%**; GAAP GM 54%), yet **FCF reportedly fell to ~4% of revenue** as **inventory, new debt, and customer financing absorbed the ramp**. Guiding **>80% revenue growth for 2027** on top of that working-capital burden implies continued balance-sheet expansion and dilution risk. Also flag: **Gaming revenue −40% y/y** (semi-custom console cycle rolling off) and two in-flight acquisitions (**World Labs, Taalas**) plus prior ZT Systems integration — classic post-deal integration and impairment risk. Cybersecurity posture appears routine (patchable CVEs, responsible-disclosure program); the 2024 source-code/future-product breach remains the most recent material incident.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **ELEVATED**

- **"Circular financing" is now AMD's defining reputational tag.** AMD is named alongside Nvidia in the Michael Burry / Morningstar / mainstream "AI bubble" critique. Being a *party* to the circularity story is worse than being a *commentator* on it: it invites the accusation that reported growth is financially engineered rather than end-demand-driven.
- **Community/local backlash is now a live force.** Data-center moratoriums passed across dozens of ballots in Nov 2026; "voters rejected AI" framing is mainstream (NYT, Guardian). AMD's demand is one degree removed but the sentiment transmits instantly to its multiple.
- **Guidance credibility risk.** Committing publicly to **>80% revenue growth in 2027** — a doubling of the prior forecast — with FCF at ~4% of revenue and the stock down 25–40% from its peak creates a hair-trigger for reputational damage on the first miss. Management is already publicly framing skeptics as "short-sighted" — a stance that ages badly if the ramp slips.
- **Brand/consumer**: Ryzen client business (+7%) is healthy and carries low controversy. No product-safety, recall, or consumer-backlash issue identified.
- **ESG**: energy/water intensity of AI datacenters is the dominant controversy vector; no company-specific scandal identified.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

The rating is driven by **exogenous policy shock and structural concentration, not by integrity or accounting red flags**. AMD's disclosure record is clean — no restatement, no material weakness, no SEC/DOJ enforcement, no active securities class action — and operational execution through Q3'26 was excellent. But a 22.4% China revenue base is now governed by a hostile-to-volatile policy regime with a 15% government revenue-share toll, the customer base is five hyperscalers who are simultaneously warrant holders and financed buyers, and the CEO has sold >$125M of stock into the narrative while guiding to 80%+ growth. Each of these is individually manageable; jointly they mean a **single political or funding-availability shock can hit revenue, margins, receivables and the equity story at the same time.**

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🚩 CEO distribution of >$125M in 2026 with zero offsetting insider purchases**, concentrated at $503–515 (above current price). All 10b5-1, but directionally one-sided. Escalate as a *conviction signal*, not a legal one.
2. **🚩 Customer warrant + financing structure with OpenAI and Meta** (up to ~10% of AMD each) and the **$1.097B Q3'26 non-cash charge**. This is circular/vendor financing with related-party characteristics. **Request the warrant accounting memo and the customer-financing footnote**; demand disclosure of receivables aging and any non-standard payment terms to warrant-holding customers.
3. **🚩 FCF at ~4% of revenue on record earnings**, driven by inventory build, new debt and customer financing. Verify inventory turns, days-sales-outstanding and channel-inventory commentary before accepting the 2027 guide.
4. **🚩 China policy triple-exposure**: $7.8B (22.4%) revenue base, unresolved Beijing-side import permission for MI325 as of the June 2026 10-Q, and a **15% US government revenue-share** on China AI-chip sales. Model a full-China-loss scenario.
5. **🚩 January 2027 congressional transition.** A Democratic Congress plus bipartisan China-semicap hawks creates **subpoena, hearing and investigation risk specifically targeting AI chipmakers**. Expect forced disclosure of warrant/customer-financing terms under congressional pressure.
6. **⚠️ Combined Chair/CEO with no disclosed succession plan**, at a company whose entire strategy is publicly identified with one individual.
7. **⚠️ Say-on-Pay at ~85%** — below market; a leading indicator of shareholder discontent likely to surface at the 2027 annual meeting.

**Recommended escalation**: items **1, 2, 3 and 4** to the investment committee before any position sizing above benchmark weight. Item **5** to the policy/regulatory desk for ongoing monitoring into Q1 2027.
