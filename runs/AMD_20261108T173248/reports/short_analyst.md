Let me be direct, because it matters for how this report should be used: **I could not find the fraud.** The core accounting is unusually clean for a company at this point in a hype cycle. What I did find is that reported earnings increasingly describe a business whose economics are being *subsidised by shareholders* rather than by customers, and whose cash conversion inflected negative in the most recent quarter.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**Flag 1 — Cash conversion broke in the most recent quarter (most important).**
Q3 FY26: net income $2,428M, operating cash flow **$1,202M**, capex $648M → **free cash flow $554M**. Working capital consumed $3,357M in one quarter. TTM FCF is $7.06B against a $733B market cap — **~104x EV/FCF**. The gap between earnings and cash opened precisely as the AI ramp began.

**Flag 2 — Inventory build of 169 days, into a demand forecast nobody can verify.**
Inventory $7.92B (Dec-25) → $8.05B → $8.47B → **$11.46B (Sep-26), +45% in nine months** while revenue rose ~30%. Inventory days: 106 (FY22) → 130 (FY23) → 160 (FY24) → 165 (FY25) → **169 (Q3 FY26)**. AMD has already taken a ~$800M MI308 write-down in Q2 FY25, the quarter gross margin collapsed to 39.8%. Precedent exists; the balance sheet is again extended.

**Flag 3 — Revenue is being financed with equity, and the true dilution is invisible in the share count.**
OpenAI and Meta each hold warrants for **160M shares at $0.01** — 320M shares total, ~**19.6% of the 1,632M outstanding**. At $449 that is **$143.7B of value transferred for $3.2M of cash**. AMD records revenue from these customers and books a non-cash warrant charge of ~**$1.07B/quarter** (~$4.3B annualised) against a $144B notional. This is vendor financing dressed as demand: the customer cannot pay cash, so AMD pays itself in stock. On a fully diluted basis true market cap is ~**$877B**, not $733B — P/E on TTM earnings is **~115x, not 97x**.

**Flag 4 — Retained earnings do not reconcile with reported net income.**
FY25 RE $6.699B + Q1'26 NI $1.383B = $8.082B ✓. Then Q2'26 RE **$5.909B** despite $2.297B of net income and only $207M of buybacks (−$4.47B unexplained); Q3'26 RE $6.720B despite $2.428B of net income and zero buybacks (−$1.62B). Cumulative unexplained reduction: **~$6.1B**. The warrant charge explains part; the residual is not reconcilable from the data I have and warrants the equity statement in the 10-Q. I flag this as *unresolved*, not as proven manipulation.

**Flag 5 — "Adjusted EBITDA" is heavily fictional.** D&A is running ~$773M/quarter against net PP&E of only $3.9B — implying roughly **$1.8–2.0B/year of acquisition-intangible amortisation** (Xilinx) plus ~$2.0B/year of stock comp. FY26E consensus EBITDA of $16.7B adds all of that back. GAAP net income is the honest number, and it is 7.6% of revenue.

**Flag 6 — Balance sheet is no longer a fortress at the margin.** Debt $4.47B → **$8.92B** in nine months; swung from $1.07B net cash to **$1.51B net debt** while capex run-rate tripled (~$0.97B FY25 → ~$2.6B annualised). Goodwill + intangibles still **$40.6B = 59% of equity**.

## 3. Beneish & Accruals Analysis

M-Score **-2.761** with all eight indices benign: DSRI 0.678 (receivables *shrank* relative to sales), GMI 0.997 (gross margin stable), AQI 0.898, SGI 1.343 (growth, but not paired with receivable bloat), TATA **-0.0439**. There is no evidence of revenue recognition gamesmanship in the reported numbers. Accruals have been negative four years running (-1.2% to -4.4%), which is the signature of *conservative*, not aggressive, accounting.

**Verdict: exclude accounting fraud from the thesis.** The distortion here is structural and disclosed (warrants, SBC, intangible amortisation), not concealed.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

The bear case that AMD's moat erodes is, on current evidence, **wrong**. Gross margin has *expanded*: 46.1% (FY23) → 49.4% (FY24) → 49.5% (FY25) → 50.2%, 52.8%, 53.8%, **53.9%** through Q3 FY26. Pricing power is improving, not deteriorating. Revenue growth is *accelerating*: +34.1%, +37.9%, +50.1%, +44.7% YoY.

The real threat is not erosion — it is **ceiling and dependence**: NVDA's FY26 gross margin is **71.1%** and net margin **55.6%**. AMD's 53.9%/16.8% says AMD remains the price-taker in a market where the incumbent has CUDA, TSMC allocation priority, and 6x AMD's absolute profit ($120.1B vs $7.6B). AMD's growth is dependent on two customers who are simultaneously equity holders and whose own funding is questionable. That is concentration risk of an unusual and poorly-priced kind.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, but it is a multiple-compression thesis, not a fraud thesis.**

The setup: AMD trades at **97x TTM GAAP earnings, 16.1x sales, ~104x EV/FCF, ~44x FY26E EBITDA** — versus **NVDA at ~37x earnings, 20.8x sales, ~31x EBITDA**. AMD is *2.6x more expensive on earnings than the market leader that earns 16x more money at 3.3x the margin.* Consensus requires revenue to go from $34.6B (FY25) to **$234B by 2030** — a 6.8x in five years — with FY27 alone up 83%. The dispersion on FY27 EPS is **$10.83 to $22.81 (2.1x)**: nobody, including the 31 analysts, knows what this company earns in 18 months.

Bear-case arithmetic: at 30x a *low-end* FY27 EPS of $10.83 you get **$325**; at 40x the consensus $17.45 you get $698 but only if the entire 6GW OpenAI/Meta build-out ships on time and collects cash. Meanwhile the operating metrics that lead earnings — inventory days, cash conversion, capex intensity — deteriorated in the latest quarter while earnings hit records. That is the sequence that typically precedes a de-rating, not a collapse.

**The other side, stated fairly:** growth is genuinely accelerating, gross margin is expanding, accounting is clean, the F-score is 8, and AMD holds net cash. If the FY27 number is real, 25.7x forward is not a bubble. A naked short against +45% revenue growth and a 54% gross margin carries serious timing risk, and the warrant expense is *already* flowing through GAAP — meaning some of the dilution cost is being paid, not hidden.

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **SHORT** (valuation- and quality-of-revenue-driven; *not* a fraud short)

- **Not** Strong Short: no accounting manipulation, no moat erosion, no liquidity stress. Beneish, accruals, F-score and Z-score all say the financials are honest and the company is solvent.
- **Not** No Edge: 97x trailing earnings / 115x fully diluted, 104x FCF, ~20% contingent dilution to two customers, sales effectively financed with stock worth $144B, insider net selling of ~$781M, and a Q3 cash-conversion break to 0.50x give a genuine asymmetry.

**Falsification triggers I would monitor:** (1) Q4 FY26 inventory days above 175 or a second write-down; (2) cash conversion below 0.7x for two more quarters; (3) any renegotiation or milestone slip on the OpenAI/Meta warrants; (4) FY27 consensus revenue revisions below $80B; (5) gross margin failing to hold 53%.

**Preferred expression:** not a naked short, but a long-NVDA / short-AMD relative pair, or a 12-month put spread struck 20–35% below spot, sizing for the possibility that the next two quarters are still strong on headline numbers. The thesis pays when the market stops paying 100x for earnings that are 60% funded by its own equity.
