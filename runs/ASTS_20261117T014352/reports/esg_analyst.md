### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Carbon (Low):** Manufacturing is concentrated in Midland, TX (assembly, "touch labor"). Scope 1/2 is immaterial in absolute terms. Scope 3 dominates — launch services (SpaceX Falcon 9, a competitor-supplier), aluminium structures, GaAs solar arrays, semiconductors. Carbon *intensity* per revenue dollar is high only because revenue is still minimal relative to a $11.7B valuation; there is no carbon-tax or stranded-asset exposure of consequence. No CBAM exposure of note beyond minor aluminium/electronics inputs.

**Orbital sustainability (High — the material E factor):**
- **April 2026: BlueBird 7 failed to reach intended orbit and had to be de-orbited** (8-K), with insurance recovery expected. A repeat pattern raises launch/insurance costs and is a live debris-management failure, not a hypothetical.
- BlueBird Block 2 arrays are ~64 m² (BlueWalker 3) scaling toward ~200+ m² — enormous cross-sectional area implies elevated collision/conjunction risk and higher per-satellite casualty-risk-on-reentry analysis.
- **FCC 5-year post-mission disposal rule** applies to the 248-satellite constellation authorised April 2026. Non-compliance risk includes licence conditions and FCC enforcement (the FCC has begun debris enforcement actions).
- **No public orbital-debris-mitigation plan, no TCFD/CDP disclosure, no standalone sustainability report.** Third-party transparency scoring places ASTS at ~2.4/10 on SDG/ESG transparency — i.e., essentially non-disclosing. For a company whose licence to operate is granted by the FCC, this is a real gap.

**Astronomy / light pollution (Medium-High):** BlueWalker 3 reached apparent magnitude 0.4 — brighter than all but ~7 stars in the night sky (*Nature*, 2023), far brighter than Starlink. IAU Centre for the Protection of the Dark and Quiet Sky has published follow-up analyses; the scientific community is actively engaged. This creates a credible pathway to licence conditions, national-science-body opposition in partner jurisdictions (EU, Japan), and reputational friction with the institutional capital base. Mitigations (darkening films, attitude control, dielectric mirrors) reduce antenna performance — a direct economic trade-off.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **LOW–MEDIUM**

**Positives (genuinely material):** The product is digital inclusion — connectivity to unserved/underserved regions, disaster-resilience backup, FirstNet public-safety coordination, maritime and aviation coverage. This is the strongest part of the ESG profile and creates durable political capital (which is what won the FCC SCS authorisation and drives MNO partnerships with Vodafone, Deutsche Telekom, Orange, Telefónica, AT&T, Verizon).

**Concerns:**
- **Human capital scaling risk:** headcount ~1,126 with an announced >1,000-job Midland expansion and 500+ El Paso hires at $20–55/hr. Rapid blue-collar scale-up in satellite manufacturing without disclosed safety metrics (TRIR, fatalities, EHS audit results) is a blind spot. Job postings for "Manager, Safety and Reliability" and EHS compliance indicate the function is being built, not mature.
- **Key-person concentration:** the entire thesis, board control and public narrative rest on Abel Avellan.
- **Product safety / consumer protection:** direct-to-device service carries RF-exposure, handset-interference and emergency-call reliability obligations; the securities complaint alleges overstated competitive position and slow US/Japan adoption — a marketing-claims issue with S-flavour.
- **Dual-use ethics:** prime position on the Missile Defense Agency **SHIELD** programme (Jan 2026) under the Golden Dome umbrella ($185B programme) pulls ASTS into weapons-related end-use, which screens out a portion of the ESG-mandated investor base and adds ITAR/EAR and security-clearance compliance obligations. Low financial risk, moderate exclusion risk.
- Supply chain: launch dependence on a direct competitor (SpaceX Starlink) is an operational and reputational chokepoint.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10** (Weak)

**Rationale:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality + founder control:** Avellan is Chairman and CEO; Class B carries 10 votes vs Class A's 1, giving him ~75.8–80.4% of combined voting power. Board designation rights were structured at the NPA business combination. Minority shareholders cannot effect change.
- **Disclosure/credibility failure (the decisive issue):** Q2 2025 — CFO described the balance sheet as "fully funded" to 45–60 satellites on >$1.5B pro forma liquidity; filings stated cash would cover 12 months of opex/capex. Then: **$850M→$1.0B converts (Oct 2025, stock −9.24%), $1.0B (Feb 2026, −15.17%), $1.0B + $150M option (15 Jul 2026, −17.04%)**. Three raises, ~$3B+, in ten months. The market has repeatedly re-priced the same information asymmetry. Whatever one thinks of the legal merits, the *pattern* is a governance signal — capital-markets credibility is a governance asset and it has been spent.
- **Litigation:** Consolidated federal securities class action (10(b)/20(a)) naming Avellan and CFO/CLO Andrew Johnson; class period 4 Mar 2025 – 15 Jul 2026; lead-plaintiff deadline 13 Nov 2026; multiple firms (Rosen, Kessler, RGRD, SBS) soliciting. A parallel **derivative/fiduciary-duty investigation** targets the board directly.
- **Compensation:** ~$14.2M CEO package tied to aggressive satellite-to-phone milestones, with first-time RSU grants added (250k RSUs). Structure is milestone-linked (arguably long-term aligned), but the combination of mission-critical targets + serial dilution + insider sales by two senior executives during the class period weakens the alignment claim.
- **Mitigants:** independent directors exist; audit/comp/nominating committees in place; a formal severance plan was adopted (standard, mildly shareholder-unfriendly); no evidence of fraud findings, restatements, or corruption/bribery issues; the board did secure a major regulatory win (FCC 248-satellite authorisation).

**Score: 3/10** — structurally entrenched control, weak disclosure discipline, active securities litigation, and no offsetting governance reform catalyst.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD** (trending toward *ESG Risk*)

- **E:** Medium risk — low carbon, but orbital-debris management is materially weak and undisclosed for an operator of a 248-satellite constellation, and the astronomy controversy is unresolved.
- **S:** Low–Medium risk — a legitimately strong social-purpose narrative offset by thin human-capital disclosure and defense dual-use exposure.
- **G:** **3/10 — the binding constraint.** Founder control, CEO/Chair duality, a documented pattern of optimistic liquidity disclosure followed by serial $1B dilutions, active securities and derivative litigation, and zero environmental/sustainability reporting.

Absent the governance issues, this would screen as an *Average* ESG profile with a strong social thesis. With them, it is a Laggard. The company would fail most ESG-mandated screens on the "no active securities litigation / adequate disclosure" and "no sustainability reporting" criteria, and it is not in major ESG indices.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication for the Thesis: **NEGATIVE** (governance-driven, not E/S-driven)

**The ESG verdict does not turn on carbon or on labour. It turns on governance, and it is directionally bearish for the equity thesis for three reasons:**

1. **Governance is the *only* ESG factor here with confirmed, quantifiable value destruction.** Three separate ~$1B convertible raises in ten months, each immediately marked down 9–17%, against prior "fully funded" representations, is a real cost of capital penalty that persists until management rebuilds credibility. Management guidance should be discounted — model dilution as a recurring, not one-off, feature of the capital structure.
2. **Orbital sustainability is an underpriced licence-to-operate risk.** The FCC authorisation is the entire business. A second debris incident, an enforcement action, or darkening conditions imposed following astronomy pressure would hit deployment economics. There is currently no disclosure to assess this — which itself argues for a risk premium.
3. **The social mission is real and durable, and it is the one thing that underwrites political support for spectrum.** Digital inclusion, FirstNet, and rural resilience are why DT/Orange/Telefónica/Vodafone and US regulators are engaging. This is genuine, non-imitable political capital — and it is the strongest argument *against* a sell thesis.

**Net: Neutral-to-Negative on ESG grounds.** We would not use ESG to reject the equity outright — the S narrative is authentic and the E footprint is small — but we would (a) apply a governance discount to any DCF via an elevated cost of equity, (b) treat the convertibles as a structural, recurring dilution channel rather than a financing event, and (c) require, before any upgrade in conviction, (i) resolution or credible provisioning of the securities litigation, (ii) a published orbital-debris mitigation plan and first sustainability report, and (iii) board de-classification or at minimum an independent lead director with real authority. Until then, ASTS is an ESG **laggard** whose principal non-financial risk has already begun converting into financial loss.
