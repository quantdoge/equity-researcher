**Investment Thesis**

ASTS is a genuinely differentiated technology — broadband to unmodified smartphones, FCC-licensed for 248 satellites, 60+ MNO partners with 3B+ subscribers, $1.2B backlog — wrapped in a weakly governed, capital-hungry financing vehicle. The operating business is inflecting (revenue +34% QoQ, gross margin 24.9% → 40.2% over three quarters, $2.8B cash, no debt maturity before 2032, $1.0B non-dilutive Japanese government funding), but the equity is a levered bet on launch cadence, the US yield curve, and management's dilution discipline — three things shareholders do not control. After a −78.5% drawdown, the bear case is substantially priced (our own short desk declines to short here), yet governance and dilution disqualify it from a conviction long. The honest position is: **fairly priced option on execution — hold, don't chase, size like a venture round if you must own it.**

**Bull Case**
- Service activation (US, Canada, Japan, UK, Saudi) lands by year-end 2026 as *recurring* wholesale revenue; Q4 prints ≥$61.5M and holds guidance — the first credibility reset after seven misses.
- Launch cadence delivers 45–60 BlueBirds by mid-2027 and 100+ across 2027–28 (already under contract); at 6.5x FY28 sales, any de-risking of the S-curve re-rates the equity violently (2.85+ beta works both ways).
- $2.8B cash + $1B Japan MIC non-dilutive funding + $1.2B backlog + 2032+ maturities mean no solvency trigger inside the horizon; government/defense revenue (Space Force, SHIELD) becomes a second, defensible TAM.

**Bear Case**
- Reflexive dilution spiral: ~$2B/yr burn against $2.8B cash forces a raise by late 2027; at $28 that's ~+23% dilution, at $15 ~+44% — on top of +66% share growth in the last year alone. Revenue per share has been flat for four years.
- Competitive asymmetry: Starlink (EchoStar spectrum now in hand) can price D2D as a loss-leader with internal launch costs; Amazon adds ~$11.5B of committed capital. ASTS buys launches from its competitor.
- Governance and legal overhang compounds: class action naming the CEO and CFO/CLO, disclosure credibility spent, WRC-27 fragmentation risk, and Blacklift (a $1.3B in-house rocket) threatens to divert capital into a fight SpaceX has already won.

**Key Risks**
1. Q4'26 revenue miss (consensus $75M sits *above* the implied $61.5M guidance path) — an eighth consecutive miss breaks the story.
2. Any equity/convert raise before YE2027 below $30 — confirms the death-spiral dynamic.
3. Term-premium shock (10Y → 6%): mechanically −20–25% on duration alone, and closes the convert window.
4. Launch failure or further slip of the 45-satellite milestone beyond mid-2027.
5. Blacklift funded on-balance-sheet; adverse WRC-27 outcome; class action survives dismissal with a restatement.
