# AST SpaceMobile (NASDAQ: ASTS) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

| Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| CEO/Chairman duality | Avellan is **Founder, Chairman and CEO** simultaneously; board designates only a *Lead Independent Director* as a counterweight. |
| Controlled-company status | Company self-identifies as a **"controlled company"** alongside Avellan, Antares, Vodafone and Rakuten — exempt from majority-independent board and fully independent comp/nominating committee requirements. Dual-class structure with supervoting founder stock concentrates control. |
| Combined CFO + Chief Legal Officer | **Andrew M. Johnson serves as CFO *and* Chief Legal Officer** — finance and legal/control functions under one officer at exactly the moment the company faces a securities-fraud action. A structural control weakness. |
| Disclosure credibility | CFO told analysts post-Q2 2025 that with a >$1.5B pro forma balance sheet the company was **"fully funded" to 45–60 satellites**, and filings asserted 12-month sufficiency. The company then executed **three ~$1B convertible offerings (Oct 2025, Feb 2026, Jul 2026)** in ten months. That gap is the core of the litigation. |
| Insider alignment | Large executive sales during the alleged class period (see §3) while retail holders absorbed dilution. One offsetting director open-market buy (Cisneros). |
| Board refresh | Board expanded; several independent directors (Keith Larson et al.) and a Lead Independent Director exist — modest positive, but insufficient against founder control. |

**Verdict:** Governance is the single weakest dimension. Concentrated founder control + dual-hatted CFO/CLO + a broken "fully funded" narrative is a combination that historically precedes both litigation and restatements.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**No SEC enforcement action or DOJ investigation found** (EDGAR full-text search returned zero enforcement hits). Risk is litigation- and licensing-driven, not enforcement-driven.

**Active securities litigation (material):**
- **Hunter v. AST SpaceMobile, Inc.**, W.D. Tex. (Midland-Odessa Div.), No. **26-cv-00378** — §10(b)/§20(a)/Rule 10b-5 against the Company, **Avellan (CEO)** and **Johnson (CFO/CLO)**. Class period **Mar 4, 2025 – Jul 15, 2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline was **Nov 13, 2026** (multiple firms: Pomerantz, Robbins Geller, Kessler Topaz, Rosen, Kaplan Fox).
- Allegations: (i) capital/liquidity position **overstated** and capital needs under-disclosed; (ii) serial dilution greater and more frequent than represented; (iii) **competitive position in satellite direct-to-cell overstated** post-EchoStar/SpaceX spectrum deal; (iv) **slow user adoption in the US and Japan** not disclosed.
- **Derivative/fiduciary investigations** already launched (e.g., Grabar Law) targeting board oversight of financing, disclosure controls, and insider-trading supervision — a second wave of cost and discovery risk.
- Loss causation markers are clean and documentable: −9.2% (Oct 2025 raise), −15.2% (Feb 2026 raise), **−17.0% to $55.01 on Jul 16, 2026** (July $1B raise + $150M greenshoe). Settlements of this shape are typically material relative to a company burning cash.

**Licensing / regulatory — a relative bright spot, but conditional:**
- **FCC granted commercial SCS authorization (Apr 22, 2026)** for ~**248 satellites**, operating in partner low-band spectrum (700/800/900 MHz) leased from **AT&T, Verizon and FirstNet**. This is the key regulatory unlock.
- **Structural dependency:** AST does **not own** the spectrum. Its entire US business model rests on leased MNO spectrum and partnership economics. Any partner renegotiation, lease lapse, or FCC condition (build-out milestones, bonding, interference remediation) is existential, not incremental.
- **International:** Japan (~$1B subsidy support discussed), Vodafone/EU markets, and government/DoD programs each require separate sovereign approvals; execution risk compounds with each jurisdiction.
- **Export control (ITAR/EAR)** and orbital-debris/ITU coordination obligations apply to a US-designed satellite bus and (prospectively) an in-house launch vehicle.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Launch access is controlled by a direct competitor.** AST depends on third-party rockets (SpaceX Falcon 9, Blue Origin New Glenn — an April 2026 New Glenn mission reportedly underperformed — and ISRO). **SpaceX is simultaneously AST's launch provider and its most dangerous competitor** (Starlink Direct-to-Cell plus the EchoStar spectrum acquisition). The Nov 12, 2026 plan to build a **~$1.3B in-house reusable rocket** (target late-2028) is an admission of that dependency — and a massive new capital/execution risk layered onto a cash-burning business.
- **Manufacturing and deployment slippage.** Q3 2026: revenue $42.3M (+187% YoY) but **missed estimates**; net loss attributable to common holders **$132M**, EPS −$0.44 vs. −$0.41 consensus. The **45-satellite target slipped to mid-2027** and commercial service launch moved to **2027** from late 2026. Slippage is now a *pattern*, not an event — each slip pushes revenue past the next financing need.
- **Key-person and talent concentration.** 1,126 employees supporting a $11.7B enterprise value in a capital-intensive, technically bespoke business; the founder is the strategy, the board influence, and the public narrative. Succession or reputational damage to Avellan is un-hedged.
- *(Additional)* **Liquidity/financing-vehicle risk** — ~$3B+ of converts raised in ten months, convertible at levels ($~79 July 2026) far above the current $28.58 price; conversion/refinancing overhang and likely further dilution. **Cybersecurity** incident history not disclosed in the material reviewed — a **gap in this assessment**, not a clean bill of health.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **ELEVATED**

- **Shareholder-relations damage is acute.** The stock is **~78% below its May 28, 2026 close of $133.09**, at 52-week lows ($28.58; Oct 9, 2026 was a fresh low on 54M shares, Nov 5 fell 15% to $30.20). The retail-heavy holder base has been serially diluted while management described the balance sheet as sufficient — a durable trust breach that raises the cost of every future raise.
- **Litigation-solicitation noise** (a dozen+ law-firm press releases in a single week) is now the dominant media channel around the ticker, crowding out the technology story.
- **Competitive narrative risk:** the market's framing has shifted from "AST vs. nobody" to "AST vs. Starlink + SpaceX spectrum + EchoStar," with UBS (Sep 2025) and Scotiabank (Jan 2026) downgrades citing exactly that. Slow US/Japan adoption allegations convert a technology story into a demand story.
- **No evidence found** of product-safety incidents, recalls, ESG controversies, data-privacy breaches, or consumer backlash of the classic kind. Reputational damage here is **capital-markets-driven, not customer-driven** — which is recoverable but directly raises cost of capital.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale for *High* rather than *Critical*: the FCC commercial authorization (248 satellites, April 2026) is a genuine regulatory asset; a top-tier MNO partner set (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Rakuten, FirstNet) is real; $1.3B of remaining performance obligations and reiterated FY26 revenue guidance of $150–200M show commercial traction; the Q3 10-Q carries an unqualified CEO/CFO §302 certification; and **no SEC or DOJ enforcement action exists**.

Rationale for *High* rather than *Moderate*: an active securities-fraud class action naming the CEO and CFO/CLO, with documented double-digit loss-causation days; governance that is structurally weak (founder control, CEO/Chair duality, combined CFO/CLO, controlled-company exemptions); ~$3B of dilution in ten months after "fully funded" representations; heavy insider selling with no meaningful insider buying into a 78% drawdown; launch dependency on a competitor; and a schedule that has slipped twice. Every one of these risks is self-reinforcing: a delay forces a raise, a raise extends the drawdown and the litigation, and the drawdown raises the cost of the next raise.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Securities fraud class action (No. 26-cv-00378, W.D. Tex.) naming the CEO and the CFO/CLO** — alleged misstatements on liquidity sufficiency, dilution cadence, competitive durability, and US/Japan adoption. **Escalate to GC/compliance review; treat any management liquidity or schedule guidance as unverified.**
2. **"Fully funded" representations contradicted by three ~$1B converts in ten months.** Highest-severity disclosure-control finding in this report. **Request the audit committee's review of disclosure controls and any identified material weakness — not obtained in this assessment.**
3. **Combined CFO and Chief Legal Officer role** at a company under securities litigation — an avoidable control conflict. **Escalate as a governance remediation ask.**
4. **Large insider sales during the alleged class period** (CEO 55,244 sh / >$5.5M; CFO 190,131 sh / >$13M) plus a reported **~2.5M-share (~$183M) structured CEO sale via Citigroup**. **Verify the Form 144 date, price and execution before publication; if it preceded a guidance cut or raise, severity rises materially.**
5. **Multiple near-identical 8-K/Form 4 clusters immediately after the July 15, 2026 raise** — check for any sales within a blackout window or outside a 10b5-1 plan.
6. **Launch dependency on SpaceX while competing with Starlink**, plus a newly announced ~$1.3B in-house rocket program funded during a liquidity squeeze — classic diworsification risk. **Escalate as a capital-allocation override.**
7. **Derivative/fiduciary-duty investigations already in motion** (board oversight of financing and insider trading) — an additional, currently unpriced liability channel beyond the 10b.
