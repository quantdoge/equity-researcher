## AST SpaceMobile (ASTS) — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment
*Research cut-off: 16 Nov 2026. All claims sourced to filings/news retrieved below.*

**Bottom line:** ASTS has low *country* risk (US-centric, allied-market partners) but **High legal/regulatory risk**. Two binary-ish exposures dominate: a securities class action already filed over the launch-schedule miss, and dependence on a direct competitor (SpaceX) for launch, set against the WRC‑27 spectrum rulemaking that will decide whether its D2D model is legal at scale outside the US.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① WRC‑27 spectrum sovereignty — the defining regulatory binary.** Direct-to-device currently runs on an **"experimental" ITU rule**; regulators from Spain, the UK and the FCC want WRC‑27 (2027) to make it permanent, and "international regulators appear far from consensus on how to regulate satellite D2D in geographies where many nations" claim coverage (RCR Wireless, 19 Oct 2026; SpaceIntelReport). Sovereignty — not technology — is the friction: Senate testimony flags "the assumption of a borderless sky" as contested (Commerce Committee PDF). **Probability of adverse/fragmented outcome: Medium-High. Impact: High** — a country-by-country authorization regime caps ASTS's addressable market and its "borderless" economics, and favors incumbents with terrestrial licenses.

**② Launch dependency on a competitor.** BlueBird 8–10 flew on a **SpaceX Falcon 9** (June/Aug 2026) — i.e., ASTS's only scaling path runs through Starlink's owner. Blue Origin's New Glenn is **out of service**; on **15 July 2026** ASTS pushed the 45-satellite milestone from end-2026 to **"early 2027"** with 10 launched. It is now funding a $1B raise partly to pursue **vertical integration in launch** and has announced **BlackLift**, its own ~20-tonne rocket (SatelliteToday 16 Jul 2026; Q3 call). **Probability of further slippage: High (already realized). Impact: High** — and the pivot *into* launch adds ITAR/EAR export-control surface and a capital-intensive new execution risk.

**③ US–China: supply chain and standards competition.** China's 2025–26 export controls on heavy rare earths and permanent magnets remain **"slow, opaque, and highly discretionary,"** with bottlenecks "set to persist in 2026" (CSIS; S&P Global) — direct input cost/schedule risk for satellite manufacturing. Strategically, ASTS competes with Chinese state-backed D2D (Guowang/SatNet) for Global South markets, and its **Taiwan Mobile** tie-up sits directly on the most acute geopolitical fault line. **Probability: Medium. Impact: Medium-High.**

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active securities class action (primary legal overhang).** Filed in 2026; class period **4 Mar 2025 – 15 Jul 2026**; **lead-plaintiff deadline 13 Nov 2026 (now passed)**. Notably, the class period **ends 15 July 2026 — the same day** ASTS disclosed in an SEC filing that the 45-satellite timeline slipped to early 2027 and announced a $1B share sale — a textbook corrective-disclosure alignment. Firms involved: Rosen, Levi & Korsinsky, Kaplan Fox, Pomerantz, Kessler Topaz, KTM, SBS Law. One complaint also cites the **February 2026 $1.0B convertible notes** offering with an **~15.17%** stock decline (Grabar Law). A **prior** 2024 class action (Block 1 BlueBird supplier delays) exists in the background (Capacity, May 2024). Expect years of distraction and D&O cost; outcome range is wide.

**Regulatory — favorable but conditional.** FCC **Space Bureau granted commercial SCS authorization on 22 April 2026**, plus a **permanent license** for the foundational system, enabling an NGSO constellation of **up to 248 satellites** on partner **700/800 MHz** spectrum with Verizon/AT&T/FirstNet. Crucially, the grant is conditioned: SCS is "limited to (a) the spectrum and geography associated with the 700 MHz and 800 MHz licenses identified in the request" (FCC DA‑26‑391A1) — meaning ASTS's rights are **derivative of its MNO partners' licenses**, a structural dependency. The FCC is separately proposing to modernize D2D spectrum-leasing rules.

**Other.** Post-quarter **satellite loss** disclosed (Q3 call coverage) — potential insurance/contract implications. Non-US service launches are each "contingent upon regulatory approval" (stc deal terms).

*No evidence found of any DOJ/SEC/FCPA/AML investigation into ASTS. Note: an SEC subpoena-enforcement litigation release (LR‑26632, 4 Sept 2026) surfaced in search but concerns **Institutional Shareholder Services**, not ASTS — not attributable here.*

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **Export controls (ITAR/EAR) — elevated.** The BlackLift vertical-integration pivot plus the Rakuten **J-LEO technology/constellation JV** implies technology transfer and export licensing. Manageable but now a live compliance function.
- **FCC §310(b) foreign ownership.** ASTS is an FCC licensee with foreign strategic holders (Vodafone, Rakuten) and possible sovereign co-investors (stc). Any equity shift can trigger **foreign-ownership review** — a real constraint on foreign capital raising.
- **Saudi data/localization.** Gateways plus a **Network Operations Center in Riyadh** create data-residency and lawful-intercept exposure; US-person support obligations need jurisdictional firewalling.
- **Sanctions/AML: clean.** No Russia, Iran, or designated-party revenue identified. stc and other partners are not sanctioned.
- **Watch item:** the "borderless sky" vs. national-law enforcement tension (WRC‑27) is itself a compliance risk — service over unlicensed jurisdictions.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Country risk is **Low–Medium** (US, Japan, Canada, UK, Saudi partners). The **High** rating is driven by legal/regulatory factors, not geography: (i) a filed securities class action whose class period terminates precisely on the launch-delay disclosure date, (ii) structural reliance on a **competitor's** rockets following an admitted schedule slip to early 2027, and (iii) **WRC‑27** as a binary international rulemaking that governs whether the business model scales beyond the US. Mitigants: the April 2026 FCC authorization, a $1.3B backlog, $3.2B cash, and a $1B Japanese government-backed J-LEO program.
