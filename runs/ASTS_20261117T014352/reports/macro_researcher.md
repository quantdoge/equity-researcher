### 1. Macro Environment Summary — Risk-Off for Duration, Not for Credit

The US is in an unusual and specifically hostile regime for ASTS: **late-cycle reflation colliding with a term-premium shock.** Three facts define it.

First, growth is *accelerating*, not decelerating. Q3 2026 real GDP rose 3.4% SAAR (advance, released 29-Oct-2026) after 2.2% in Q2 and 2.5% in Q1. Unemployment fell to 4.1% in October from 4.5% a year earlier. There is no recession signal anywhere in the labour data.

Second, inflation has re-accelerated. CPI is 3.82% YoY for October 2026 (index 337.476), up from ~2.4% in mid-2025, and the Fed responded by **hiking 25bp in September 2026 to 3.75–4.00%** and **holding on 28-Oct-2026 with two of twelve voters dissenting** — dissents in the hawkish direction. The effective funds rate has drifted up to 3.88%. Critically, the 2Y at 4.74% sits ~86bp *above* the effective funds rate: the market is pricing further hikes, not cuts.

Third — and this is the decisive variable for ASTS — **the long end has sold off on term premium, not inflation expectations.** The 10Y is 5.13% (5.15% on 13-Nov), up ~68bp from 4.45% on 1-Jun-2026; the 30Y is 5.47%. Yet 10Y breakevens are *contained* at 2.20% and 5y5y forwards at 2.15%. That implies a **10Y real yield near 2.93%** and a 30Y real yield near 3.3% — the highest real cost of long-duration capital in a generation. With gold at $3,852/oz and a bear-steepening curve (2s10s +39bp, no inversion), this is a fiscal-dominance signature: sovereign risk premium and supply, not growth fear.

Where the picture is *not* hostile: credit is open. HY OAS is 3.23% — but note it bottomed at 2.60–2.65% on 28-Aug/1-Sep-2026 and has **widened ~58bp off those tights**, while VIX jumped to 22.91 (+18.7% on 17-Nov). Credit and volatility are the first cracks, not yet a break.

**Stance: transitional / risk-off in duration-sensitive equities, still risk-on in credit and growth.** For a pre-profit, capex-heavy, dilution-dependent equity with a 2.85 beta, this is close to the worst mix available short of outright recession.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

ASTS is the purest long-duration asset in the communication services universe: $11.7bn market cap against FY26 revenue guidance of $150–200M (Q3'26 revenue was $42.3M) — roughly 60–75x sales. Essentially **100% of its intrinsic value sits in post-2028 cash flows**. That single fact determines every macro transmission channel.

**a) Discount rate — the dominant channel (strongly negative).**
With a 2.85 beta and a 5.13% risk-free rate, CAPM cost of equity is roughly **5.13% + 2.85 × 4.5% ≈ 18%**. That is a brutal hurdle for a company whose economics only work at scale. The 68bp rise in the 10Y since June alone raises the cost of equity ~65bp; on a ~15-year-duration cash-flow stream that is mechanically **−10% to −13% of fair value** from that move alone. The 30Y at 5.47% matters more than the 10Y here, because satellite-constellation economics are 30-year assets. This is the single largest macro headwind, and it is not company-specific — it is why ASTS fell from a $133.09 close on 28-May-2026 to $28.58 (−78.5%) even as revenue grew sequentially.

**b) Cost and availability of growth capital (negative, but survivable).**
ASTS is a serial convertible issuer. Known tranches: **$400M 4.25% due 2032** (net $387.9M); **$1.0B 2.00% due Jan-2036** (Oct-2025, conversion price ~$96.30); and **$1.0B 1.625% due Feb-2034** priced 20-Jul-2026 (net $983.6M, +$150M option), with an initial conversion price of **~$79.57** and a **$96.9M capped call**. A further 2.25% issue due 2036 appears in a recent 424B5. Against this, Q3'26 cash and equivalents were **over $3.2bn**, including $1.15bn of 2026 convert proceeds.

Three implications:
- **No near-term refinancing cliff.** Nearest maturities are 2032+, so ASTS does *not* face 2008-style rollover risk from higher rates. This is the important nuance most rate-sensitivity screens miss.
- **The coupons are trivially cheap (1.625–4.25%) precisely because they were priced on volatility, not on credit.** Higher base rates compress the conversion premium investors will accept per dollar raised — the same $1.0bn that bought a $96.30 strike in Oct-2025 bought only $79.57 in Jul-2026. Continued capital raising into a 5%+ rate environment means **more dilution per dollar** even before the stock's decline.
- **Every convert is now deeply out of the money** ($28.58 spot vs $79.57 and $96.30 strikes — 64–70% below). Conversion will not retire this debt. Cash repayment or refinancing is the only path, which converts today's cheap-coupon debt into a 2032–2036 liability that must be met from operating cash flow. Shareholders have effectively sold a long-dated put on the constellation reaching scale.

**c) Credit conditions (the key mitigant — net positive but thinning).**
HY OAS at 3.23% means the convert market is functioning. As long as credit is calm, ASTS can monetise its equity volatility into low-coupon paper regardless of spot price — this is what kept it funded through the drawdown. But the +58bp widening off September tights and the VIX move to 22.91 are early-warning signals. **Credit spreads, not the Fed funds rate, are the variable that decides whether ASTS's funding model survives 2027.** This is the line to watch.

**d) Growth (positive).** 3.4% GDP and 4.1% unemployment support terrestrial carrier budgets and, more importantly, the willingness of MNO partners (Vodafone, AT&T, Verizon, Rakuten, stc, Etisalat, Telstra, Bell Canada) to sign revenue-share agreements rather than defer. A recession would be far worse for ASTS than high rates, because partner commitments are the business model. Also relevant: Q3 revenue leaned on *US government service milestones* — space and defence are among the last protected lines in a federal budget squeezed by ~$1.2trn of interest costs.

**e) Currency (mildly negative).** Broad USD at 122.65, up 4.0% in ten weeks. ASTS contracts in USD, so translation is limited, but a stronger dollar tightens dollar-funding conditions for EM partner carriers (India, Africa, Middle East, SE Asia), compressing their ability to fund gateway build-outs. Dollar strength is also a global liquidity drain that historically hits high-beta, unprofitable growth hardest.

**f) Input costs and trade (modest negative).** Satellite hardware is a semiconductor-, rare-earth- and launch-heavy bill of materials; crude at $85.21 raises launch and logistics costs, and tariff/export-control exposure on components sits in COGS. The announced plan to develop an in-house reusable rocket adds a large, discretionary, multi-year capex programme — the worst kind of spending to commit to at a 5.47% 30-year rate. Rising labour costs at 4.1% unemployment also bite a headcount growing from 1,126.

**Data gaps, stated plainly:** core CPI/PCE and a clean total of convertible principal outstanding were not retrievable within budget; the tranche list above is from 8-K/424B5 disclosures and may not be exhaustive.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE** (not yet hostile)

The rating is driven by a mechanical mismatch: a 2.85-beta, pre-profit, 60–75x-sales, capex-escalating company in a regime of ~18% cost of equity, 2.93% real 10Y yields, and a Fed with a hiking bias. Two things prevent this from being outright *hostile*: **growth is strong** (partner demand intact, no recession) and **credit spreads are tight** (the convert window is open, and $3.2bn of cash plus 2032+ maturities means no near-term solvency trigger). The macro environment is a ~30–40% headwind to valuation and a meaningful dilution tax on future raises — but it is not what determines whether ASTS works. Launch cadence, satellite deployment and competing constellations are. **Macro sets the price of the currency ASTS pays in; it does not set the business outcome.**

### 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise: A Term-Premium Shock

Consensus is comfortable because breakevens are anchored at 2.20% and the Fed is on hold. **The surprise is that anchoring breaks in *real* yields while inflation expectations stay calm.**

Suppose the fiscal trajectory forces the 10Y to 6.00% and the 30Y to 6.5% by mid-2027 — a bear steepener with breakevens *falling* as growth wobbles, the classic "bad steepening." In that world:
1. Cost of equity on ASTS goes from ~18% to ~21%, worth another **−20% to −25%** on a 15-year-duration DCF before any fundamental change.
2. HY OAS widens from 3.23% to 5.5%+. The convert window shuts — the same window that kept ASTS funded through 2026.
3. Equity becomes the only funding source, and it would be raised at $20 or below. A $1.5–2.0bn raise at those levels is 15–25% dilution, on top of the dilution already embedded in capped-call-exceeding issuance.
4. Spot would plausibly trade to the high-teens, i.e. near or below the value of cash plus in-orbit assets — the point where the market stops pricing the constellation at all.

The mirror-image bull surprise: sticky 3.8% CPI rolls over on base effects and oil, the Fed's hiking bias reverses, and the 10Y falls 5.13% → 4.20% by H2-2027. Duration alone is worth **+30–40%**, the convert window reopens at tight coupons, and a high-beta constellation re-rating (in a market where VIX is at 22.9) can move the stock 2x the index. **Both tails are macro-driven and both are large — which is itself the finding: ASTS's equity is currently a levered bet on the shape of the US yield curve, more than on its own launch schedule.**
