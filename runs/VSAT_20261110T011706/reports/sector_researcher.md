# VSAT — Sector Brief: Satellite Communications

**Note on peer set:** The FMP "Technology" classification returns mega-cap semis/software (AAPL, NVDA, MSFT) — not comparable. I have substituted a purpose-built satellite/fixed-wireless peer set: IRDM, GILT, GSAT, GOGO, ECHO (Hughes/EchoStar), CMTL. **Gaps:** SES/Eutelsat and Starlink (private) are the two most important competitors and are unavailable in this data set; peer multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA) were not retrievable, so valuation comparisons below are derived and labelled as estimates.

## 1. Sector Overview

Satellite communications is a ~$100–110bn global market (Grand View: $107bn in 2026) growing roughly **9–11% CAGR**, but the aggregate figure masks a violent mix shift: LEO capacity is compounding at ~12%+ (LEO market ~$75bn by 2026) while GEO FSS — where Viasat's heritage capacity sits — is in structural oversupply and price deflation. The market is consolidating into vertically-integrated scale platforms (Starlink with ~9,800+ satellites in orbit; SES/Intelsat merged; Eutelsat-OneWeb; Amazon Leo), leaving mid-cap GEO operators to defend niches in **government/defense, aviation IFC, maritime and IoT**. Barriers to entry are extreme (capex, spectrum, orbital slots) yet irrelevant to Starlink, whose launch-cost advantage is the single most disruptive force in the sector. The sector is **early-cycle in LEO, late-cycle/declining in consumer GEO broadband** — a barbell, not a single cycle.

Porter's: Rivalry **high/intensifying**; New entrants **high (LEO-native, well-capitalised)**; Buyer power **high** (airlines multi-source, governments concentrated, retail subs churning); Supplier power **moderate** (launch, ASIC foundry); Substitutes **high** (fiber, 5G FWA, terrestrial D2D).

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Defense / government SATCOM modernisation.** Viasat's Q2 FY27 print (5-Nov-26) showed revenue growth led by defence, and it won a NASA NEXUS Phase 1 award (9-Nov-26) alongside legacy tactical/mobile satcom programs — a counter-cyclical, backlog-driven revenue pool with better pricing than consumer.
- **Aviation & mobility IFC adoption.** Free-to-passenger IFC is now table stakes; multi-orbit (GEO + LEO) and electronically-steered terminal architectures favour Viasat's terminal/ASIC and network-integration capabilities. GOGO's revenue +105% and GILT's +48% in their latest years confirm mobility/ground-segment demand is real and growing.
- **Ground-segment and LEO-agnostic opportunity.** Every LEO constellation needs ground terminals, gateways, ASICs and user-terminals — Viasat's Commercial Networks segment sells into that capex whether or not its own GEO capacity wins. Sector capacity demand (broadband, D2D/NTN, IoT) is growing far faster than GEO supply is being absorbed.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Starlink-driven price and share erosion in Viasat's core.** Consumer fixed broadband, maritime and now aviation are being repriced downward; Viasat's FY26 revenue grew just **+2.7%** against a sector growing high-single digits — a clear share-loss signal. Management guided **commercial aviation revenue to decline in fiscal 2028** as customer mix shifts (Q2 FY27), which is the clearest evidence of the LEO threat to its best-margin segment.
- **Leverage and interest burden.** FY26 interest expense of **$360m on $4.64bn revenue (7.8%)** drove a net loss of −$34m despite $1.83bn EBITDA. Refinancing and ViaSat-3 follow-on capex remain the dominant equity risk; the balance sheet, not the P&L, sets the equity value here.
- **GEO capacity price deflation and capital intensity.** Sector-wide oversupply in GEO Ku/Ka, plus 15-year satellite depreciation cycles ($1.35bn D&A in FY26), means returns on legacy capacity compress faster than costs come out. Regulatory/spectrum and budget-appropriation timing (CR risk) add volatility to the government book.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position — **AVERAGE**

*Rationale.* Viasat has genuine, hard-to-replicate assets — global Ka spectrum, government/defence clearances, aviation STCs and a vertically-integrated terminal/ASIC business — and FY26 delivered its **first positive operating income ($108m, 2.3%)** with EBITDA up 48% YoY. That is a real turnaround. But on the two metrics that matter in this sector it screens below median: revenue growth of 2.7% versus a ~7% peer median and 9–11% market growth, and gross margin of 27.4% versus a 49.0% peer median — a structural consequence of owning capital-intensive GEO capacity rather than the higher-return, asset-light models of IRDM (77% GM) or GOGO (59% GM). It is sub-scale against the LEO platforms in every segment except defence. Verdict: **average** — a solid, improving #4–5 in a consolidating industry, not a share gainer, with the balance sheet ($360m annual interest) capping the equity upside. Net margin is the one relative bright spot (−0.7% vs −0.9% median) but that is a low bar.

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Growth (9–11%) and demand visibility are attractive, and the sector re-rating over the last twelve months is earned. But the economics are being redistributed toward launch-cost owners and away from capacity owners: pricing power is negative for GEO-heritage operators, capital intensity is extreme, and competitive intensity is rising, not stabilising. For legacy satellite operators specifically I would rate the *investable sub-segment* **unattractive-to-neutral**; the value case (VSAT at an estimated ~9x FY26 EBITDA versus ~17x for IRDM, on derived enterprise values) is a deleveraging/self-help trade, not a sector tailwind trade.
