## Viasat, Inc. (NASDAQ: VSAT) — ESG Analysis

**Company snapshot:** Satellite communications operator (GEO fleet: ViaSat-1/2/3, KA-SAT, Global Xpress, Anik F2), Carlsbad CA, ~7,000 FTE, market cap ~$10.2B, beta 1.76, FY ends March. Inmarsat acquired April 2023.

**Critical framing:** For a satellite operator, the financially material ESG factors are **not** carbon. They are (a) orbital/space sustainability and physical asset risk, (b) cybersecurity of mission-critical infrastructure, and (c) governance of a heavily levered, founder-controlled balance sheet. Viasat has realized losses in all three, not hypotheticals. This is where the analysis concentrates.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM**

**Climate transition risk: Low.** GEO satellite operations are electricity-and-fuel light relative to industrials. Viasat reports Scope 1+2 market-based intensity of ~0.53–0.59 tCO2e per £1m revenue (FY24 basis), among the lower intensities in the communications-equipment cohort. No CBAM exposure, no carbon-tax-exposed heavy processes. Reported FY25 total absolute emissions of ~2.42 Mt CO2e across all scopes means **>99% is value-chain (Scope 3)** — principally satellite manufacturing, launch services and downstream customer energy use. This is a disclosure/computational risk, not an abatement liability.

**Physical/operational climate risk: Low–Medium.** Ground-segment exposure (earth stations, teleports) carries flood/wind/heat risk; satellites themselves face launch-window and space-weather risk. Not a thesis driver.

**Space sustainability — the real environmental issue (High within this bucket).** Viasat operates large, high-value GEO assets plus a growing LEO/MSS footprint (Equatys shared-infrastructure MoU with Space42/Terrestar, NASA CSP Maxwell spacecraft launched Oct 2026). Key exposures:
- **FCC 5-year deorbit rule** and revamped debris rules (maneuverability, disclosure, disposal metrics) — modest direct bite on GEO, rising exposure as Viasat expands LEO/MSS ambitions.
- **Launch/insurance market capacity.** The 2023 ViaSat-3 F1 reflector anomaly and total loss of Inmarsat I6 F2 ($420m coverage on F1; ~$770m combined claim sought) show that space insurance is repricing. Higher insurance cost is a permanent, recurring cash cost.
- **Litigation posture (governance-adjacent):** Viasat and Balance Group challenged the FCC's approval of SpaceX's lower-orbit constellation on NEPA/environmental grounds; the D.C. Circuit rejected the argument. Viasat has used environmental review as a competitive lever — efficient defensively, but it exposes the company to counter-scrutiny of its own orbital footprint.

**Environmental controversies:** None material in the period (no spills, pollution fines, or remediation liabilities identified). Disclosure quality is genuinely above average: **ISO 14064-1 third-party verified** GHG inventories from FY22 through FY26, published performance tables, and standalone UK/Canada Carbon Reduction Plans (PPN 06/21 requirement for UK public contracts).

### 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM**

**Positives (material and underappreciated):** Viasat's core product is social infrastructure — community internet, disaster-response connectivity, digital inclusion, STEM programmes. This directly supports eligibility for government/defence and public-sector contracts (e.g., the $307m-ceiling USMC enterprise services vehicle awarded Sept 2026) and creates genuine social licence.

**Negatives — workforce restructuring.** Repeated layoffs totalling roughly 15% of headcount over recent years, including a ~320-person (~6%) reduction tied to post-Inmarsat integration. Risks: (i) institutional-knowledge loss in an engineering-intensive business where execution is already the weak link; (ii) severance/restructuring cash costs against a levered balance sheet; (iii) elevated bias/retaliation litigation risk in California, a plaintiff-friendly jurisdiction. Glassdoor commentary corroborates sustained attrition and compressed promotion/comp prospects.

**Safety & supply chain.** Manufacturing/ASIC-MMIC operations imply conventional occupational-safety exposure — no material fines or OSHA-type actions identified. Supplier standards are documented (supplier code, **Modern Slavery Statements** covering UK/Australia requirements), and UK/Ireland **Gender Pay Gap Reports** are published — evidence of a functioning compliance apparatus rather than best-in-class D&I performance (metrics not disclosed at group level).

**Product safety / reliability (social + operational):** The ViaSat-3 F1 antenna reflector failure degraded a flagship asset to a fraction of design capacity and forced service start on a compromised platform rather than replacement. For customers dependent on connectivity for aviation safety services (SwiftBroadband-Safety, Iris) and maritime/energy operations, reliability is a safety-adjacent attribute. This is the single clearest case where a "social" factor is financially material.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10 — Mixed**

**Positives:**
- **Single class of voting stock.** Diligence confirms common stock is the *only* voting class — no dual-class super-voting structure, no structural entrenchment mechanism. This is meaningfully better than the founder-led tech norm and preserves takeover optionality (relevant given 2026 speculation).
- Disclosure discipline: verified GHG data, GRI/SASB-aligned performance tables, whistleblower "Values Line," published certifications and supplier standards.
- Independent directors of substance on the board (e.g., William LaPlante, former senior Pentagon acquisition official) — useful for defence-contract credibility.

**Negatives:**
- **Chairman/CEO duality — combined roles.** Founder Mark Dankberg serves simultaneously as Chairman and CEO, having resumed both roles after a period of separation. For a company with $7.5B of debt, an active acquisition programme and a contested competitive position, the absence of an independent chair is a genuine checks-and-balances deficiency.
- **Capital allocation and leverage.** The Inmarsat combination took debt from ~$2.5B to ~$7.5B; ratings sit at BB-/BB+ (sub-investment-grade), with an Inmarsat revolving-credit-facility **total net leverage and interest-coverage covenant package** and a **2029 maturity wall**. Strategic ambition has repeatedly outrun the balance sheet — a governance-of-risk issue, not merely a financing one.
- **Insider selling.** Director Richard Baldridge sold 223,542 shares; SVP Craig Miller sold 22,500 shares (~$1.58m) in Nov 2026. Routine in isolation, but persistent selling into a takeover-speculation-driven price spike ($29–$93 52-week range; stock more than doubled in 2026) warrants monitoring.
- **M&A optionality vs. shareholder voice.** Barron's reports Viasat is pursuing an independent path despite investor enthusiasm for a deal. With a combined Chair/CEO and no dual-class stock, the board — not shareholders — effectively controls the outcome.

**Net:** Founder-visionary governance with real disclosure strengths, offset by concentrated authority, sub-investment-grade leverage and a thin cushion for execution error. 5/10 is a fair mid-point: better than a controlled company, worse than an independent-chair issuer.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD:** Inmarsat's EU footprint brings Viasat into scope for the non-EU parent regime (EU turnover above the €150m threshold) in the later compliance wave. Double-materiality reporting will require Scope 3 and space-debris-related disclosures Viasat does not currently publish in that format. **Cost: low absolute, moderate effort.**
- **UK:** SECR/TCFD-aligned disclosure; **PPN 06/21 Carbon Reduction Plans** mandated for UK public contracts — already published for Viasat UK Ltd, Inmarsat Global Ltd and Inmarsat Solutions (Canada) Inc. This is a **bid-eligibility requirement**, so compliance is revenue-protective.
- **US SEC climate rule:** stayed/litigated; exposure limited given immaterial Scope 1+2. Defence-customer supply-chain climate and **cybersecurity (DFARS/CMMC)** requirements are the more binding US obligations.
- **FCC:** 5-year deorbit rule, orbital-debris mitigation, space-innovation licensing, spectrum/orbital-slot conditions.
- **CBAM:** negligible.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified — Viasat's debt carries conventional leverage/coverage covenants, **not** sustainability-linked pricing. This insulates the cost of capital from ESG-score slippage, which is a modest positive.

---

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Justification: Best-in-class **disclosure** (third-party-verified GHG data, GRI/SASB tables, modern slavery and gender pay gap reporting) and a **socially valuable product** are offset by **Critical-rated space and cyber risk** that has already produced realized losses, **sub-investment-grade leverage** that constrains the ability to absorb further shocks, and **founder Chair/CEO duality**. On pure E-and-S-scorecard optics Viasat looks like a Leader; on materiality-weighted, cash-flow-relevant ESG it is squarely Average. It is not a Laggard — no systemic governance failure, no dual-class entrenchment, no environmental violations — but it is not an ESG-mandate holding.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (with a governance-tilted negative caveat)

ESG factors are **not** the primary driver of the VSAT thesis — this is an operating-turnaround and deleveraging story, with 2026 price action driven by takeover speculation rather than sustainability fundamentals. Accordingly, ESG is thesis-neutral in direction but contributes identifiable risk weight:

1. **Do not underwrite an ESG-leader multiple.** Verified disclosure quality will not translate into a green premium; Viasat will screen mid-pack in ESG funds and is unlikely to be excluded, but equally unlikely to attract dedicated sustainability capital.
2. **Price the tail risks that are ESG-labelled but financially real.** Orbital-asset failure and cyber breach are the two events that could impair the equity from here, and both are *uninsurable or only partly insurable*. Insurance recoveries are a partial cash offset with multi-year lag.
3. **Governance is the swing factor.** Combined Chair/CEO plus BB-rated leverage plus a 2029 maturity wall plus insider selling into a speculative run-up means shareholder outcomes depend heavily on a small group's capital-allocation judgement. An independent chair, a credible deleveraging path, or a value-accretive strategic transaction would each be a genuine upgrade; a further debt-funded capacity bet would not.
4. **Watch item:** any move toward an **ESG-linked financing** structure, or conversely a covenant reset — both would be signals of deteriorating financial flexibility. Neither is present today, which is itself mildly reassuring.

**Bottom line:** Viasat is an Average ESG performer whose material ESG exposures — space sustainability, cybersecurity, and levered founder-led governance — are already embedded in a 1.76 beta and a sub-investment-grade rating. ESG does not change the investment call; it argues for position sizing discipline and for treating operational/cyber disruption, not carbon, as the relevant downside scenario.
