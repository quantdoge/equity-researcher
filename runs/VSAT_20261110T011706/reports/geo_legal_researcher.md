# Viasat, Inc. (NASDAQ: VSAT) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company snapshot:** Viasat is a global satellite communications operator (legacy Carlsbad GEO/consumer/government business) that roughly doubled in scale via its 2023 acquisition of **Inmarsat** (UK-headquartered maritime/aviation/government MSS operator). It now reports two segments: **Communication Services** ($815M in FQ2'27, −3% y/y) and **Defense & Advanced Technologies / DAT** ($365M, +20% y/y). FY2026 (ended 3/31/26) revenue was **$4.64B**, net loss $(28.4)M; FQ2'27 revenue $1.18B ([MarketBeat, 11/6/26](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-viasat-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-11-06/); [TradingView 10-K summary](https://www.tradingview.com/news/tradingview:e2bd83a8a2ad5:0-viasat-inc-fy-2026-revenue-4-64b-net-income-28-4-m-10-k-summary/)).

**Key disclosed anchors used below (FY2026 10-K, via search excerpt of [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/797721/000119312526248290/vsat-20260331.htm)):**
- **~32% of total revenues were international** (⇒ ~68% U.S.)
- **U.S. Government as a single customer ≈16%** of revenue (FY26; 18% FY25, 17% FY24)
- Five largest contracts ≈19% of revenue

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Gulf state-linked capital enters a U.S. defense/FCC-licensed franchise (Equatys JV, plus strategic-review M&A).**
Viasat and UAE's **Space42** (ADX-listed, Abu Dhabi state-linked) signed a **binding agreement in September 2026** to co-found **Equatys**, a shared direct-to-device/MSS infrastructure company with **up to $1B of combined equity** ($400M each, plus a $200M future Space42 tranche), with a Terrestar (Canada) MoU added in October ([Viasat release](https://www.viasat.com/news/latest-news/corporate/2026/space42-and-viasat-sign-binding-agreement-to-co-found-equatys--c/); [Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/viasat-space42-spending-up-to-1-billion-on-direct-to-device-venture-equatys/)). Management confirms **regulatory approvals are still required**.
- **Probability:** Medium-High that this triggers some form of **FCC/Team Telecom (Committee for the Assessment of Foreign Participation) mitigation agreement** and possibly CFIUS review; Low that it is blocked.
- **Impact:** High. The FCC has just set an enforcement precedent — a **$175K penalty plus compliance obligations against Marlink (Jan 2026)** for breaching Team Telecom commitments ([FCC consent decree](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-417571A1.pdf); [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/fcc-enforces-team-telecom-mitigation-commitments-against-marlink)). Adding a Gulf sovereign-adjacent partner into a holder of U.S. government SATCOM contracts invites conditions on data routing, personnel, and network security — i.e., *operational* constraints, not just delay. Any **takeover bid from a non-U.S. acquirer** during the active strategic review would push this to the top of the risk stack.

**Risk 2 — Sanctions/export-control and maritime-chokepoint exposure in contested theaters.**
Maritime is Viasat/Inmarsat's most internationally distributed business (**1,990 NexusWave vessels in service**, order book >1,500) and it is **already shrinking (−8% y/y)** ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/transcript-viasat-q2-earnings-call-highlights-2026-11-06/)). Shipping connectivity is structurally **sanctions-adjacent**: dark-fleet/AIS-gap vessels, Red Sea/Hormuz re-routing, and Iran/Venezuela/Russia-linked tonnage create both revenue loss and compliance exposure. Viasat also carries direct state-actor history — the **GRU wiper attack on KA-SAT (Feb 2022)** that disabled Ukrainian modems and spilled into European wind-farm and utility SCADA ([CFR](https://www.cfr.org/cyber-operations/targeting-of-viasats-satellite-broadband-service); [The Record](https://therecord.media/viasat-hack-was-two-incidents-and-resulted-in-sanctions)).
- **Probability:** High (continuing) that sanctions screening, end-use monitoring, and cyber remediation remain a recurring cost and headline risk. **Impact:** Medium (single-digit-% revenue; reputational risk disproportionate).

**Risk 3 — LEO competition driven by industrial policy, not just price (Starlink/Kuiper vs. a GEO-heavy incumbent).**
Management explicitly flagged that **customer announcements will affect 15–20% of aviation revenue**, with **commercial aviation revenue declining in fiscal 2028**. That is the market consequence of state-subsidised LEO capacity (U.S. Starlink/Kuiper, EU IRIS²/Eutelsat-OneWeb, China's Guowang/Qianfan) being steered by governments toward "national champion" suppliers. Viasat's own counter-move — the Equatys multi-orbit platform — is therefore strategically necessary but regulatory-laden.
- **Probability:** High. **Impact:** Medium-High (aviation is the growth-margin engine of Communication Services).

*Runner-up:* UK/EU spectrum and industrial policy — Ofcom's **2 GHz MSS band** review and the proposed **award of 1492–1517 MHz for mobile**, adjacent to Inmarsat's L-band, plus EU secure-connectivity procurement preferences. Viasat is actively lobbying both ([Ofcom filings](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/consultations/category-1-10-weeks/call-for-input-future-use-of-the-2-ghz-mss-band/responses/viasat.pdf?v=405366)).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Largely resolved / net positive**
- **Ligado Networks Chapter 11 (filed Jan 2025).** Ligado sued Inmarsat (Viasat subsidiary) in New York County; Ligado's adversary claim alleged Inmarsat breached the spectrum-cooperation agreement by lobbying the FCC against Ligado's L-band deployment — the bankruptcy judge found a "colorable argument" and allowed Ligado to pause a **$100M payment** to Inmarsat ([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/ligado-can-pause-100-million-payment-to-inmarsat-judge-rules/); [SpaceNews](https://spacenews.com/ligado-networks-sues-inmarsat-after-spectrum-partnership-breaks-down/)). **June 2025 term sheet settled this**: Ligado's suit against Inmarsat is **stayed and to be dismissed**, and Viasat expects to **receive ~$568M**, largely to address near-term maturities ([Viasat release](https://investors.viasat.com/news-releases/news-release-details/viasat-announces-comprehensive-agreement-ligado-networks); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/viasat-to-receive-568m-under-ligado-agreement)). *Residual:* collection timing/credit risk on the $568M; Ligado's separate **U.S. Court of Federal Claims** takings suit against the U.S. government continues (not a Viasat liability).
- **ViaSat-3 F1 antenna anomaly (2023).** ~$420M of insurance coverage on the satellite; Viasat filed the claim at end-2023 and began receiving proceeds in 2024 ([Viasat](https://investors.viasat.com/news-releases/news-release-details/viasat-provides-interim-update-vs-3-f1-satellite-status-and); [Via Satellite](https://www.satellitetoday.com/finance/2024/02/07/viasat-to-initiate-service-on-viasat-3-satellite-starts-receiving-insurance-money/)). *Note:* I found **no evidence of pending litigation against insurers** in the searches available — treat that as a watch item, not a known claim. F2 and F3 are now in service, materially reducing this tail.

**Live regulatory dossiers**
- **Merger clearances (2023) now become ongoing obligations:** FCC approval; **UK National Security and Investment Act clearance**; **CMA unconditional clearance** after an initial Phase-1 "airline wi-fi" competition concern ([UK gov](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/satellite-communication-deal-raises-competition-concerns); [Viasat](https://investors.viasat.com/news-releases/news-release-details/viasats-proposed-acquisition-inmarsat-receives-unconditional)). NSIA conditions and FCC/Team Telecom commitments survive as **continuing compliance obligations** — breach is enforceable, per the Marlink precedent.
- **Equatys JV** — multi-jurisdiction approvals pending (U.S., UAE, Canada via Terrestar). This is the single largest *forward* regulatory gating item.
- **Ofcom spectrum:** 2 GHz MSS band consultation; 1.4 GHz mobile award adjacent to L-band; NGSO downlink licence condition. Directly affects Inmarsat's licensed L-band economics.
- **Export controls:** DAT sells ITAR/EAR-controlled tactical networking, encryption, and InfoSec products (tactical networking +31%, InfoSec/cyber +27% in FQ2'27). Requires DDTC State Dept. licenses and Technology Control Plans; **foreign-national staffing and the UAE JV both intersect these controls** — a genuine structural tension.
- **Active strategic review:** a standing board committee is evaluating options. Any non-U.S. suitor would face **CFIUS + Team Telecom + FCC + UK NSIA + EU FSR** — a ~12–18 month gauntlet. That is itself a deal-certainty risk for the equity.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale.** VSAT's center of gravity is U.S. (~68% of revenue, ~16% from the U.S. Government) with strong rule of law, an improving defense-demand tailwind, and its largest legal overhang (Ligado) now settled with a **$568M receivable in Viasat's favor**. That anchors the rating at Medium rather than High.

**What keeps it from Low — three live escalators:**
1. **The Equatys/Space42 JV** injects state-linked Gulf capital into a U.S. defense supplier and FCC licensee, with approvals still pending and a freshly demonstrated FCC willingness to enforce Team Telecom conditions.
2. **Maritime sanctions adjacency + proven Russian state cyber targeting** create recurring compliance and operational risk that cannot be fully mitigated, and maritime revenue is already contracting.
3. **Aviation revenue guided to decline in FY2028** (15–20% of segment revenue at risk) as geopolitically-backed LEO competitors take share — a commercial loss with geopolitical causation.

**Trigger to upgrade to HIGH:** (a) a foreign (especially Gulf or non-U.S.) bid for Viasat or a major segment under the strategic review; (b) imposition of restrictive Team Telecom/CFIUS mitigation conditions on Equatys or on Viasat's FCC licenses; (c) any sanctions enforcement action or maritime dark-fleet customer exposure surfacing publicly; or (d) adverse Ofcom/EU spectrum decisions on the 1.4–2 GHz L-band.

**Data gaps to flag to the desk:** country-level revenue percentages beyond the disclosed U.S./international split; the exact status of the $568M Ligado receivable; the FCC file numbers and condition text for the Inmarsat-era Team Telecom commitments; and whether any ViaSat-3 F1 insurance recovery remains disputed.
