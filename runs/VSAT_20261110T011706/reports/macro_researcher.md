# VSAT — Macroeconomic Environment Assessment
**As of 10 November 2026 | Viasat, Inc. (NASDAQ: VSAT) | Technology / Communication Equipment | $74.37, mkt cap $10.2bn, beta 1.76**

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — "Transitional, tilting risk-off at the margin"

The regime is best described as **re-tightening into a term-premium shock**. The Fed completed its easing cycle in mid-2026, taking the effective funds rate from a 5.33% peak down to **3.63% (May–August 2026)**, then **reversed and began hiking again — 3.75% in September, 3.88% in October 2026**. This is the first genuine re-tightening impulse of the cycle and it is the single most important macro fact for a levered, long-duration equity like Viasat.

Growth is *not* the problem. Real GDP accelerated to **+3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026** (Q2: +2.2%; +2.1% y/y), and unemployment fell to **4.1%** in October from 4.5% a year earlier. But inflation is re-accelerating: CPI rose **+0.65% m/m in September, pushing y/y to 3.72%** from 3.30% in July. Tight labour, 3%+ growth and 3.7% CPI is exactly the mix that forces a Fed to stay restrictive.

The critical diagnostic is the **shape and composition of the curve**. The 2s10s is at **+39bp** (2Y 4.74%, 10Y 5.13%, 30Y 5.47%) — a bear steepener, not an inversion. Yet the **5y5y forward inflation expectation is only 2.19%** (down from 2.36% on 5 Oct). So the ~60bp backup in the 10Y since late June (4.44% → 5.13%, intraday peak 5.31% on 5 Oct) is **almost entirely real rates and term premium, not inflation expectations**. That is a fiscal-supply / duration-premium repricing — the most damaging kind for capital-intensive, sub-investment-grade issuers, because it raises the discount rate without raising the nominal revenue their assets generate.

Credit has begun to notice: **HY OAS widened from 2.65% (1 Sep) to 3.42% (6 Nov), +77bp in nine weeks** — still tight in absolute terms (long-run ~5%), but the momentum has flipped. Meanwhile the **broad USD index rose to 121.88, +3.4% since 9 September and +2.6% since 30 June**.

Note the market echo in VSAT itself: the stock ran from $9.50 (Nov 2024) to a high of **$93.03 on 1 July 2026** — a ~10x move — then fell **24% between 28 October and 5 November** ($78.26 → $59.38) before a violent +25% rebound to $74.37 on 9 November. A beta-1.76, 3.2x-levered name is the market's shock absorber for exactly this rate/credit regime.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

VSAT is a **sub-investment-grade, capital-intensive satellite operator with ~$4.7bn net debt (3.2x trailing adj. EBITDA, improved 0.3x y/y), $850–900m of FY27 capex against >$225m guided FCF**, and roughly $15bn enterprise value. Its macro sensitivity is therefore dominated by the discount rate, not the demand cycle.

| Macro variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Long-term real rates / term premium** | **Strongly negative** | ~$15bn EV, FCF inflection story, beta 1.76. A 5.1% 10Y and 5.5% 30Y compress the multiple on terminal-value-heavy cash flows. Rising term premium also raises the hurdle for ViaSat-3-class constellation spend. This is the dominant variable. |
| **Re-financing / credit spreads** | **Negative** | HY OAS +77bp in nine weeks. On ~$4.7bn of debt, each +100bp of spread on a refinanced tranche costs ~$15–25m pre-tax annually — roughly 7–11% of guided FY27 FCF. Manageable near term, corrosive if the widening continues. |
| **Fed policy path** | **Negative** | Re-tightening removes the "falling-rate tailwind" that powered the 2024–26 re-rating. Every month of hikes delays the deleveraging narrative. |
| **Real GDP growth (+3.4%)** | **Positive** | Supports aviation traffic (4,490 commercial aircraft, +6% y/y; ARPA +3%), maritime and enterprise connectivity demand. |
| **Labour market / wage inflation** | **Negative** | R&D and SBC already cited as drivers of the $129m quarterly net loss; 4.1% unemployment keeps engineering and ground-ops labour costly. Fixed-price government contracts limit pass-through. |
| **Defense / fiscal spending** | **Strongly positive** | DAT revenue +20% to $365m, DAT adj. EBITDA +57%, FY27 DAT awards guided to ~$2.0bn vs $1.6bn, backlog $4.2bn (+7%), NASA NEXUS and CSP wins. The fiscal defence impulse is VSAT's clearest macro tailwind and is largely rate-insensitive. |
| **USD strength (+3.4% since Sep)** | **Negative** | Material non-US revenue (Inmarsat maritime, global aviation, government). A firming dollar is a translation and price-competitiveness headwind at exactly the point reported growth is only +3%. |
| **Commodities / tariffs** | **Neutral to slightly negative** | Limited direct commodity exposure; some semiconductor/RF-component import content and launch costs. Secondary to the rates story. |
| **Consumer fixed broadband** | **Negative (secular)** | Fixed services −23%, 100k subs, still declining post-ViaSat-3 Flight 2 launch. Rate pressure on household budgets reinforces cord-cutting from satellite broadband. |

**The macro core of the VSAT thesis:** the company is executing a genuine operating turnaround — DAT compounding at 20%, FCF +14% to $78m, leverage down to 3.2x — but the *macro* is taxing precisely the two things the equity is priced on: (a) a falling discount rate, and (b) a clean deleveraging path. Meanwhile management has guided **commercial aviation revenue to decline in FY2028**, with customer announcements affecting **15–20% of aviation revenue**. That is an idiosyncratic risk landing squarely in the middle of a rising-real-rate environment, which is the worst sequencing.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Directionally, four of the six macro variables that matter most to VSAT (real rates, credit spreads, Fed path, USD) are moving against it, and the two supports (GDP, defence spending) are ones the market already capitalises at a premium. This is a **rates-driven call, not a demand-driven one** — the operating environment for VSAT's end markets is healthy, but the cost of capital and the valuation multiple applied to its long-dated cash flows are deteriorating simultaneously. A levered, high-beta, long-duration equity in a bear-steepening, re-tightening regime deserves an unfavourable macro overlay regardless of company-specific execution.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario That Would Matter Most — **a long-end capitulation (bear) / a Fed reversal (bull)**

The genuinely under-priced tail is a **disorderly move at the long end**. With 5y5y inflation expectations *falling* to 2.19% while the 30Y sits at 5.47%, the entire back-up is term premium and fiscal duration supply. If the 30Y clears 6.00% and the 10Y breaks above 5.50%, the Fed is effectively read as having lost control of the curve. In that world VSAT does not fall 10% — it falls 30–40%, because a 3.2x-levered name with $850–900m of annual capex and a FY2028 aviation revenue decline is the marginal seller's first exit. HY OAS at 3.42% has room to 5%+ without anybody calling it a crisis, and a 150bp spread widening plus a 75bp long-end move would cut VSAT's equity value roughly in half on a constant-EBITDA basis while raising refinancing cost on $4.7bn of debt.

The mirror-image bull case is equally violent and, on the inflation data, arguably more likely than the market thinks: **September's 3.72% CPI marks the peak**, the Fed's two hikes prove to be a policy error, and the 10Y retraces to 4.20%. With 5y5y already anchored at 2.19%, that is a credible path. VSAT, at 1.76 beta with an improving FCF and DAT-award trajectory, would re-rate toward the July high of $93 and beyond. Investors should size positions for a 2:1 realised-volatility regime, not a 1:1 one.
