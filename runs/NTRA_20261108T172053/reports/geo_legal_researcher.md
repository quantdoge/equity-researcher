### 1. Geographic Risk Map

Natera is a **structurally US-centric business**. The 10-Q XBRL geography tags show exactly four reporting buckets (US, Americas ex-US, EMEA, Asia Pacific & Other), and the FY2025 disclosure showed **~95% of revenue from US direct sales and ~3% from US laboratory partners** (Clarifo summary of FY2025 10-K); revenue from customers outside the US was roughly **2–3%** in the prior-year comparable (Q3 2025 10-Q: $28.1M, ~2% of revenue). Q3 2026 total revenue was $794.0M on a $2.24B nine-month base.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — BGI/UFLPA forced-labor designation contaminating the China partnership (Probability: High that the partnership is disrupted; Impact: Low financially, Medium reputationally)**
On **October 28–29, 2026**, DHS/FLETF added **BGI Group, BGI Genomics and MGI Tech** to the **UFLPA Entity List**, creating an immediate import prohibition on their goods into the US (DHS release 10/28/2026; Federal Register notice 2026-22094, published 10/29/2026; Reuters, 10/28/2026). Natera has a long-standing BGI Genomics partnership (2019 agreement; BGI/Natera Signatera assay launched in China) and the Q3 2026 10-Q still tags a BGI Genomics oncology revenue stream. Even where NTRA's own US supply chain does not touch BGI/MGI instruments or reagents, the relationship now sits inside a forced-labor policy perimeter, inviting customer, payer and investor scrutiny, and requiring a documented supply-chain audit. *Assessment: low dollar materiality (<2% of revenue), but a live compliance and headline risk that did not exist a month ago.*

**Risk 2 — US healthcare reimbursement policy concentration (Probability: Medium; Impact: High)**
The single largest "geopolitical" exposure for NTRA is really **domestic policy risk**: Medicare is a disclosed concentration (10-Q customer-concentration tags), and the franchise depends on CMS MolDX coverage expansions — e.g., the **November 3, 2026 expansion of Signatera coverage into hepatocellular carcinoma** (Business Wire). Coverage giveth and rate-setting taketh away; a CLFS rate revision, MolDX tightening, or a broader push on lab-developed-test reimbursement would hit revenue far harder than anything overseas. Legislative revival of LDT/FDA-style oversight of lab tests is a secondary tail risk.

**Risk 3 — Trade/export-control friction on sequencing inputs (Probability: Low–Medium; Impact: Low)**
NTRA's platforms and reagents are sourced from US/allied suppliers (Illumina ecosystem, plus its own assays), so direct tariff or export-control exposure is modest. The relevant vector is *indirect*: tariffs on imported lab consumables and any tightening of genomic-data or cross-border data rules in the EU/UK would raise COGS or slow ex-US commercial expansion. Ex-US revenue is too small for this to be thesis-changing.

*(Not a risk: currency. Ex-US revenue ~2–3%, negligible translation exposure.)*

**No DOJ, SEC, or OIG investigation into Natera is disclosed or publicly reported** as of this review. Sector context is hostile: DOJ/HHS re-established the FCA Working Group in July 2025 and genetic-testing labs remain a top FCA/kickback enforcement target (Norton Rose Fulbright 2026 mid-year FCA update; DOJ 2026 takedown materials). **Regulatory posture:** CLIA lab framework post-vacatur of the FDA LDT rule; IVDR in Europe; HIPAA/GINA and state genetic-privacy statutes for the reproductive-health franchise; GDPR for the small EU footprint.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **🔴 BGI Genomics relationship vs. UFLPA Entity List (Oct 28, 2026).** A named UFLPA entity is a commercial partner and a recognized revenue counterparty in the current 10-Q. Even if no BGI/MGI product enters a US Natera lab today, this requires (a) a documented supply-chain audit, (b) UFLPA tracing diligence, and (c) disclosure discipline. Highest-priority compliance item.
2. **🟠 Counsel sanctions / litigation-conduct findings.** A federal judge sanctioned Natera's outside counsel for litigating in bad faith, with additional sanctions in 2026. Recurring discovery/advocacy misconduct findings raise disclosure-control and board-oversight questions.
3. **🟠 Genetic-data and cross-border privacy.** Handling of cfDNA/genomic data across US, EU/UK and (via partners) China is the company's most sensitive data category; GDPR transfer mechanisms and any China data-localization requirement deserve diligence.
4. **🟡 No disclosed FCPA/AML/sanctions matters.** Ex-US operations are small and in low-corruption jurisdictions; FCPA exposure is Low.
5. **🟡 Balance-sheet capacity to absorb judgments.** $1.097B cash + restricted cash at 9/30/26, $80.3M drawn on the credit line with only **$20.0M remaining availability**, and continuing net losses ($211.97M 9M26). A $292.5M judgment plus $57M patent award equals roughly a third of liquidity — fundable, but it constrains strategic flexibility and could force a financing.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Geopolitical risk *per se* is **Low** — ~96% US revenue, no Russia/Ukraine exposure, no meaningful China revenue, minimal currency and expropriation risk. The composite rating is lifted to Medium by: (i) **high domestic regulatory dependence** on CMS/MolDX coverage and rate-setting; (ii) **elevated legal risk** — a $292.5M final judgment under Ninth Circuit appeal, a $57M patent judgment under Federal Circuit appeal, and judicially imposed sanctions on counsel; and (iii) the **new BGI/UFLPA compliance overlay** on the China partnership.

**Sub-ratings:** Geopolitical — **Low**. Regulatory — **Medium-High** (US reimbursement concentration). Legal/litigation — **High** (as a share of earnings; moderate as a share of market cap at ~1%). Compliance — **Medium**.

**What would change the rating:** an adverse Ninth Circuit outcome affirming punitives in full → Legal moves toward Very High; a confirmed US lab sourcing BGI/MGI instruments or reagents post-UFLPA → Compliance to High; conversely, a favorable appellate outcome or a CMS national coverage determination for pan-cancer MRD would deflate the two largest overhangs.
