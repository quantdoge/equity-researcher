## GARTNER, INC. (NYSE: IT) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $219.64 (Oct 20, 2026) | Market cap ≈ $14.3B (≈65M sh) | Net debt ≈ $1.8B | EV ≈ $16.1B**

The stock fell from $584 (Feb 2025) to $126 (June 2026) — a 78% peak-to-trough collapse on decelerating contract value (CV) growth and AI-disruption fears — then rallied 75% off the low. My job is to judge whether the franchise is impaired or merely re-priced.

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW (and narrowing)**

Gartner sells subscription research, benchmarks, analyst access and conferences to ~15,000 enterprise clients. The moat rests on **switching costs** (research embedded in CIO budget and vendor-selection workflows), a **proprietary benchmarking dataset** that no single client can replicate, and **brand/intangible** authority in IT decision-making.

Evidence it *has been* a moat: ROIC of 26–33% for four straight years versus ~9% WACC; gross margins 67–71%; ~160% FCF conversion.

Evidence the moat is **narrowing** — this is the crux of the thesis:
- **CV growth collapsed to ~+1% YoY** (≈+3.5% excluding U.S. Federal), down from high-single digits. CV is Gartner's own forward-looking 12-month bookings metric — the single best read on pricing power.
- Revenue growth decayed 15.7% → 7.9% → 6.1% → 3.7% → **guided −1.8% in FY2026** ($6.375B).
- The market has re-priced terminal value: P/FCF from a 10-year average of **30x to ~11x**.

The honest assessment: Gartner's product is *curated, credentialed judgment about what other enterprises are doing*. Generative AI is a direct substitute for the low end of that bundle. Whether Gartner's highest-value assets (peer benchmarks, analyst calls, conferences) remain non-substitutable is a genuinely uncertain 10-year question. **I cannot underwrite a wide moat here, and I will not pretend the AI risk is noise.**

### 3. Valuation

Trailing: **P/E 15.7x** on FY26E adjusted EPS $14.00; **EV/EBITDA 10.4x**; **P/FCF ~11.9x** (8.4% FCF yield); EV/FCF 13.4x. Peer median P/E is 44x — but that comparison is meaningless for a decelerating asset-light subscription business; the 10-year-own-history P/FCF of 30x is the relevant anchor.

**Scenario DCF (9% discount, $1.20B normalized FCF base):**

| Scenario | Assumptions | Value/share |
|---|---|---|
| Bear | CV stays ~1%, revenue −3%/yr, exit 8x FCF | **$129** |
| Base | 4% FCF growth yrs 1–5, 3% yrs 6–10, 2.5% terminal | **$286** |
| Bull | AI fears overblown, CV reaccelerates >5%, 6% growth | **$342** |

Probability-weighted (30/45/25): **≈ $253 intrinsic value.** Current price is a **~13% discount** — real, but short of the 25–30% margin of safety I require, particularly with a bear case 40% below the market price.

*Note: the mechanical DCF tool returns $24.5B EV / ~$370/share, but it extrapolates a 5.76% trailing FCF growth rate that is no longer supported by bookings. I discount that output heavily.*

### 4. Management Quality — **Good operating, mediocre capital allocation**

- Gene Hall (CEO since 2004) built one of the great subscription franchises; margin discipline in 2026 (gross margin +300bp while revenue fell) is evidence of a well-run shop.
- **Share count cut 23% since 2021** (86.2M → ~65M) — genuine per-share value creation.
- **But**: Gartner repurchased **$1.99B of stock in 2025** at an average price likely above $400, then watched the stock fall to $126. That is roughly $1B+ of value transferred. Buying back stock is right; buying it *reflexively regardless of price* is not. The new $500M authorization is being deployed at $219 — far more sensible.
- No dividend, no empire-building M&A (goodwill stable at ~$3.1B). Insider ownership data unavailable — a gap.

### 5. Verdict: **HOLD**

This is a high-quality business with a narrowing moat trading near — not below — fair value. The 78% drawdown created a superb opportunity at $126–150 (11x FCF, ~2x the bear case). That opportunity has been substantially harvested; at $219.64 the stock sits 13% under my $253 intrinsic value with a genuinely bimodal outcome distribution.

**A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy.** I would:
- **Buy** below ~$180 (≈30% discount to intrinsic) or on evidence CV growth reaccelerates sustainably above 4–5%,
- **Trim / avoid** above ~$290 (approaching base-case intrinsic),
- **Reassess the moat** if CV growth turns negative or wallet retention deteriorates.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis
1. **AI substitution (the dominant risk).** If generative AI commoditizes syndicated "best practice" content, CV could turn negative and the terminal value collapses toward the $129 bear case. Structurally, this risk is not observable in one or two quarters — it will show up as slow, grinding renewal-rate erosion.
2. **U.S. Federal exposure.** CV ex-Federal grows 3.5% versus 1% reported — a single, politically exposed client cohort is masking weakness elsewhere.
3. **Revenue now contracting (−1.8% guided FY26).** Margin expansion and buybacks are currently doing all the EPS work; that is a finite lever.
4. **Capital allocation at high prices.** Continued aggressive repurchases above intrinsic value would compound the 2025 error and thin an already small equity base ($320M).
5. **Earnings-quality opacity.** A ~$380M gap between adjusted and reported EBITDA in 2025 without a clean decomposition raises the risk that recurring costs are being labeled one-time.
6. **Sentiment/positioning.** The stock now carries an AI-disruption narrative; multiple compression can recur on any CV miss regardless of cash generation.
