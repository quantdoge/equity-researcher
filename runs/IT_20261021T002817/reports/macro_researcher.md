# Macroeconomic Assessment — Gartner, Inc. (NYSE: IT)
**As of 21 October 2026 | Macro stance: Hawkish transition / late-cycle re-acceleration**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

The US economy is in a **transitional, risk-off-leaning regime**: growth is slowing modestly (~2.2% real), the labour market is intact (4.2% unemployment), but **inflation has re-accelerated at the headline level** and the policy regime has turned hawkish under the new Fed leadership. This is a "higher-for-longer, then higher still" setup rather than a cutting cycle.

**The critical regime change since mid-2026:**
- Headline CPI has risen from **2.39% y/y (Jan-26) to 3.72% y/y (Sep-26)** — driven by food/energy, with core CPI stable but firm at **2.57%** (Jan-26: ~2.4%). The 3-month annualised core run-rate is now **~3.4%**, i.e. core is drifting *up*, not down.
- The Fed **cut** from 5.33% to ~3.63% between Sep-24 and Aug-26, then the effective rate ticked **up to 3.75% in Sep-26**. Markets have decisively flipped: the **2Y sits at 4.65%, ~90bp above EFFR**, implying the market prices multiple hikes. The 10Y has **re-topped 5%** (4.99%) and the 30Y is at **5.37%** — a **bear steepener** with the 2s10s at +34bp.
- The 10Y TIPS real yield has jumped **+38bp in six weeks (2.42% on 2-Sep → 2.80% on 19-Oct)**. This is a *real* rate shock, not just breakevens (10Y breakeven ~2.19%) — the most damaging kind for long-duration equity multiples.
- Credit is still easy but **deteriorating at the margin**: HY OAS 3.29% (19-Oct) vs 2.66% (21-Sep), +63bp in a month while remaining near cycle tights.
- The **dollar has firmed ~3.0% in six weeks** (DTWEXBGS 117.88 on 9-Sep → 121.40 on 16-Oct).

**Relevance to Gartner:** This is a *revenue-realisation and multiple* problem, not a solvency problem. Gartner sells multi-year research subscriptions priced per seat to enterprise IT organisations; its revenue is a direct function of (a) CIO budget growth, (b) client headcount, and (c) the discount rate applied to a long-duration subscription annuity. All three are moving the wrong way simultaneously — though the stock has already de-rated violently, which limits the incremental damage.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Gartner's model: ~80% Research (subscription, recurring), ~10% Conferences, ~10% Consulting. **~40% of revenue is non-US** (Europe ~27%, other international ~12–13%). Cost base is overwhelmingly people (19,285 employees; **SG&A was 48% of revenue** in Q1-26 per the 10-Q), so services wage inflation is the key input cost.

| Macro variable | Impact | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **GDP deceleration to ~2.2%** | **Mildly negative** | Gartner's research wallet tracks enterprise IT budgets, not GDP directly. Growth of 2% is not recessionary; but with CIO budgets guided at **+3.7% for 2027** (Gartner's own survey) and Gartner's Q3-26 contract value growth expected at only **~2.5%** (UBS, 5-Oct-26), the company is outgrowing its addressable budget by very little. |
| **Headline CPI 3.7%, core 2.6%** | **Negative (pricing power gap)** | This is the single most important macro fact for IT. Gartner's **real pricing is negative**: nominal contract value growth of ~2.5% against 3.7% headline CPI = **~1.2pp of real revenue erosion**. Subscriptions renew at contracted escalators (typically 3–4%), so a 3.7% CPI environment does not automatically allow a reset — it squeezes clients' willingness to add seats. |
| **Rising real yields (10Y TIPS 2.80%)** | **Negative (valuation channel)** | IT is a long-duration annuity: ~$1.1–1.3bn FCF against a $13.87bn market cap (~8–9% FCF yield; ~16.6x FY26 adj EPS guidance "at or above $13.25"; EV/EBITDA ~10–11x). The multiple has *already* collapsed from ~30x in 2024 to high-teens; terminal-value sensitivity is therefore far lower than for a 30x software name. Each further 50bp on real yields probably costs 1.5–2.0 turns, not 5. |
| **Fed hike risk / hawkish Warsh Fed** | **Negative (client behaviour)** | Higher hurdle rates on IT projects → vendor consolidation → research and advisory spend is one of the first "nice-to-have" line items scrutinised. This is visible in the deceleration from high-single-digit CV growth in 2023–24 to ~2.5% now. |
| **Credit conditions (HY OAS 3.29%, +63bp)** | **Neutral for IT, negative for clients** | Gartner's own balance sheet is not the issue: gross debt is modest (~$2.5–3.0bn, weighted-average coupon ~4.3–5.0% per 10-Q disclosures), maturities are 2029/2030, and EBITDA of ~$1.5bn implies sub-1x net leverage. **Refinancing risk is negligible.** Tightening credit matters only via the discretionary **Consulting** segment (custom project work) and smaller clients. |
| **USD +3.0% in six weeks** | **Negative** | With ~40% non-US revenue, a sustained 3% stronger dollar is worth roughly **~1.2% of revenue** as a translation headwind in Q4-26/Q1-27. Note the swing: Q2-26 revenue was **-0.6% as reported but -1.6% FX-neutral** — i.e. FX was a ~1.0pp *tailwind* then. That tailwind is now reversing into a headwind, on top of an already negative reported growth rate. |
| **Wage/services inflation** | **Negative** | Consultancy and research delivery is labour-intensive; 2.6% core with wages running higher means ~4%+ comp inflation against 2.5% revenue growth. Margin defence requires headcount restraint (already underway) at the cost of capacity. |
| **AI capex boom / $6.37trn IT spend** | **Mixed — headline tailwind, real effect thin** | Global IT spending is +14.2%, but the increment is concentrated in AI infrastructure (semis, data centres, power) — spending that does **not** flow through CIO research budgets. Gartner's *editorial* franchise (Magic Quadrants, AI readiness, neocloud risk calls) is arguably more valuable in a confusing vendor landscape, which is a genuine structural tailwind for the Research segment's relevance. |
| **Low unemployment 4.2%** | **Neutral/positive** | Client enterprises are not shedding white-collar headcount at the rate feared in early 2026; seat-based revenue is holding, and Conferences demand (travel budgets) remains healthy. |

**Market confirmation of the regime:** IT fell **-20.9% on 3-Feb-26** (opened -27%, worst day since 1999) on the FY26 outlook cut, bottomed at **$124.25 on 22-Jun-26** (-63% from the 2025 peak of ~$337), then rallied **+74%** to **$219.64 (20-Oct-26)** — including **+22.6% on 4-Aug-26** after Q2 revenue of $1.68bn (beat by $27.5m), EPS $4.37 vs $3.76 consensus, accelerating contract value and raised guidance (adj EPS CAGR >12%). YTD the stock is still **-12.9%**. Read: macro/multiple compression did the damage; execution and a de-rated multiple are doing the recovery. Reported beta is 0.978, but realised volatility is dominated by the AI-disruption narrative, not macro.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL** (mildly unfavourable tilt)

**Why not Unfavourable:** Gartner enters this rate/inflation regime already de-rated (~16.6x earnings, ~8–9% FCF yield), with negligible leverage, no refinancing wall, and Q2/Q3-26 contract value growth *accelerating* into the macro headwind. The discount-rate shock has largely been paid.

**Why not Favourable:** Three of four transmission channels are negative at the same time (real pricing vs CPI, USD translation, real yields, client budget scrutiny), core inflation is drifting up, the Fed's next move is priced as a hike, and reported revenue growth is already negative (-0.6% in Q2-26) even *with* an FX tailwind that is now reversing.

**Net:** macro is a **~1–2% revenue headwind and a mild multiple headwind**, not a thesis-breaker. Company-specific execution matters more than macro over the next 12 months.

## 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **Bear (asymmetric)**

**"Warsh hikes twice into a 3.7% headline CPI, and the 10Y clears 5.50%."**
If the energy/food impulse feeding headline CPI is treated as un-anchoring expectations, the Fed delivers 50bp of hikes by Q1-27. Enterprise IT budget growth (+3.7% planned for 2027) gets cut to ~2%, and Gartner's contract value growth stalls at 1–2% — at or below CPI, so real revenue is shrinking. Conferences and Consulting (the ~20% of revenue that is discretionary) see the sharper cut. With the 10Y at 5.5% and a 13x multiple on $13.25 EPS, the stock retests the June low zone (~**$150–165**, -25% to -32%). The tell would be HY OAS pushing through 4.0% (already +63bp in a month) and 10Y TIPS above 3.0%.

**Bull mirror (lower probability near-term, high magnitude):** The energy impulse fades, core CPI settles back to 2.3%, the Fed stays on hold, the 10Y falls to ~4.3%, and AI complexity drives research demand — CV growth returns above 5% and a re-rating to 20x takes the stock to **~$275–290 (+25–32%)**.

**Immediate catalyst, not macro:** Q3-26 results (expected early November 2026) — the stock has moved ±20%+ on two of the last three prints, so the macro view will be overwhelmed by the contract-value number in the near term.
