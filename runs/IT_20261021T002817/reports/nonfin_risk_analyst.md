## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Gartner, Inc. (NYSE: IT)

**Snapshot:** Technology / IT Services, Stamford CT. Market cap ~$13.9B, price $219.63, 19,285 employees. CEO Eugene A. Hall. Three segments: Research (~77% of revenue, subscription), Conferences, Consulting.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate (deteriorating)**

| Evidence | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Combined CEO/Chairman** — Hall added the Chair role in July 2024, succeeding James C. Smith (Chair since 2004), who remained on the board as an independent director; a Lead Independent Director was appointed as counterbalance | Negative |
| **Abrupt CLO exit** — EVP/Chief Legal Officer & Secretary Thomas Kim resigned effective Oct 16, 2026 (8-K Item 5.02 filed Oct 8, 2026), "to pursue a new senior executive opportunity." Company states no disagreement on financials, operations, policies or practices | Strong negative — the chief legal officer departing mid-securities-litigation, eight months after the second corrective disclosure, is precisely when continuity matters most |
| **Sales leadership turnover** — EVP Global Business Sales Dawkins departed Jan 2025, i.e., into the contract-value deceleration | Negative |
| **Board** — Large, predominantly independent board with functioning Audit and Compensation committees (Bousa, Cesan, Serra); director turnover (John Joyce stepping down) | Neutral |
| **Insider/aligned incentives** — Heavy programmatic 10b5-1 selling culture (see §3); buybacks ($547M in Q2 2026 alone, share count −5%) flatter EPS while the core CV metric deteriorated | Watch item |

Director/officer conduct is now the subject of plaintiffs'-side scrutiny (Bernstein Liebhard investigations, July 9 and Sept 22, 2026, into "potential breaches of fiduciary duty by certain directors and officers"). Boilerplate, but it confirms governance is the live attack surface.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary
- **No current SEC or DOJ enforcement action** — EDGAR full-text enforcement search returns nothing for the issuer. This is the single most important mitigant.
- **Securities class action (active, material).** Class period Feb 4, 2025 – Feb 2, 2026; lead-plaintiff deadline May 18, 2026. Allegations: false/misleading statements about **Contract Value and Consulting segment performance** — i.e., that Gartner inflated the consulting outlook. Two corrective disclosures:
  - **Aug 5, 2025:** $336.71 → $243.93 (**−27.6%**)
  - **Feb 3, 2026:** $202.40 → $160.16 (**−20.9%**; intraday low $139.18, **−31%**)
- **Derivative/fiduciary-duty exposure** — follow-on investigations targeting directors and officers; D&O and indemnification cost inflation likely.
- **Prior FCPA history (2023)** — SEC settled charges (May 26, 2023) against Gartner for FCPA violations tied to its **South Africa** operations (anti-bribery, books-and-records and internal accounting controls provisions), with reported ~$2.5M in penalty/disgorgement. A settled, remediated matter — but it establishes a **weak internal-controls precedent** that plaintiffs will reference.
- No pending license/approval risk of consequence (not regulated like a drug or telecom asset).

- **Caveat:** 2026 purchasers/sellers were buying in the $180–$250 range going into a period when CV growth was decelerating — insiders misjudged the trajectory too.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Single-metric fragility (Contract Value).** CV growth at year-end effectively locks in **80–85% of the following year's subscription revenue**, and Gartner itself is the market's only real-time read on it. A 1–2 point CV miss has twice vaporized 20–28% of market value. This is a disclosure/expectations risk as much as an operating one, and it is what the litigation is really about.
2. **AI disintermediation of syndicated research.** The franchise is selling expert opinion and benchmark data at a premium. Generative AI now produces plausible substitutes for the "answer" layer of research; the company's own commentary concedes AI "has not been a major driver of contract value growth yet." Add pricing/packaging pressure on renewals and a Consulting segment that fell ~14.7% in Q1 2026 — the moat is being tested at exactly the moment the sales force (largely account executives doing new-business hunting) is thinning.
3. **Key-person concentration and event concentration.** Hall has been CEO since 2004 and is now also Chair; the CLO and head of global business sales are both gone. Separately, the Conferences segment depends on very large live gatherings (IT Symposium/Xpo drew 7,000+ CIOs in Orlando, Oct 19–22, 2026) — a high-value, low-diversification, physically dependent revenue stream. The client-data estate (CIO technology roadmaps, spending plans) is a high-value cyber target; **no material breach is disclosed in the period reviewed, but I could not verify the control environment from primary filings (see gaps).**

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Moderate (structural, contained)**

- **The Magic Quadrant is both the moat and the liability.** Gartner's brand carries unique soft power — every vendor on earth issues a press release when it is named a Leader. That same position generates a persistent, hard-to-kill critique of pay-to-play/conflict-of-interest between its research ratings, its consulting arm and its conference sponsorships. It is a slow-burn credibility risk rather than an acute one.
- **Litigation-driven headline risk is acute but transient.** "Securities fraud," "60% crash," "inflated outlook" coverage dominated 2026. Notably, the company has **recovered hard** — the stock is up ~75% from the June 2026 low of $125.73 to $219.64, with three-month gains of ~41% on a Q2 beat, CV re-acceleration and a raised outlook. Reputational damage has not impaired the commercial franchise.
- **Forecast risk:** Gartner's public calls (e.g., "80% of neoclouds face extinction by 2030") move third-party stocks and invite backlash when wrong — an unusual source of brand exposure for a research firm.
- No product-safety, environmental, or consumer-backlash exposure of consequence.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not because the business is impaired — it isn't; CV has re-accelerated, cash generation is strong, and buybacks are aggressive — but because **governance and disclosure quality are the dominant risks and they are currently being stress-tested in court**. A validated securities-fraud class action on the company's single most-watched metric, a combined CEO/Chair structure, a chief legal officer walking out mid-litigation, and a settled FCPA controls violation in the prior three years compound into a risk profile that is elevated relative to a $14B-cap, asset-light subscription business with no other obvious tail risk. Absent the litigation, this would rate Moderate.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **CLO resignation effective Oct 16, 2026**, disclosed via 8-K on Oct 8, 2026, while a securities class action alleging inflated contract-value/consulting disclosures is live. "No disagreement" language is standard-form; the timing warrants direct inquiry into whether the departure relates to disclosure-controls disagreements. **Escalate to management access request.**
2. **Two-step corrective disclosure pattern** (Aug 2025, Feb 2026) on the same metric — CV — each triggering 20%+ single-day losses. Suggests either disclosure-controls weakness or guidance-crafting that ran ahead of the underlying business. **Escalate: read the 10-Q Item 4 controls language and any auditor change commentary.**
3. **Combined CEO/Chairman since July 2024**, in place through both corrective disclosures — power concentration with no independent check at the top during the exact period at issue.
4. **FCPA precedent (South Africa, 2023)** — establishes that Gartner's internal accounting controls have failed before; the current allegations are also disclosure/measurement based.
5. **Insufficient primary-source verification** — see gaps below.
