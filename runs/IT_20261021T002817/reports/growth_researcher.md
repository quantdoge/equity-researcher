# Gartner, Inc. (NYSE: IT) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $219.64 (Oct 20, 2026) | Market cap ~$14.6B | EV ~$16.5B | 66.6M diluted shares**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Gartner is the world's dominant syndicated IT research and advisory franchise — a 70%-gross-margin subscription business embedded in CIO decision-making, currently trading 62% below its Feb-2025 peak ($584) after the market repriced it as an AI casualty. The investment debate is no longer "how fast can this compound" but "does agentic AI eat the syndicated research subscription" — and the last two quarters of accelerating contract value (CV +1.7% FX-neutral, $5.30B) are the first hard evidence that it does not, at least not yet. What remains is a **cash-flow compounder with a 70% gross margin, ~9% FCF yield, and a ~13%/yr share-count shrink** — an excellent business, but not a high-growth disruptor by the standards this framework demands.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

Gartner does not publish a crisp, current TAM figure, and I could not retrieve one cleanly — so I frame it from first principles rather than assert a number.

- **Backdrop:** Gartner itself forecasts global AI spending of **$2.7T in 2026, +49.5%** — it is the referee of the largest capital-allocation cycle in enterprise tech.
- **Serviceable market:** research + advisory + conferences across IT and adjacent enterprise functions (HR, finance, supply chain, legal) plausibly exceeds **$100B**, of which Gartner's ~$6.5B revenue implies **mid-single-digit penetration**.
- **The catch:** penetration math looks like upside, but the addressable pool may be *shrinking* as generative tools commoditize the bottom half of syndicated research. Gartner's defensible slice is the top-end: high-stakes, high-ambiguity decisions with accountability attached — where an LLM answer carries no institutional cover. That slice is real, growing, and much smaller than $100B.
- **Differentiation:** proprietary analyst bench (~19,300 employees), benchmark datasets accumulated over 45 years, and the conference network. No peer replicates this. AI startups replicate the *content answer*, not the *accountability relationship*.

**TAM verdict:** penetration upside is theoretical, not the binding constraint. Demand durability is.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Nov 3, 2026 — Q3 2026 results (imminent).** The critical read: a *third consecutive quarter* of CV acceleration. Bar is materially higher after a +76% move off the June low; a flat CV print risks a sharp give-back.
- **~Feb 2027 — FY2026 results + initial 2027 guidance.** The highest-variance event on the calendar: guidance at this same meeting in Feb 2026 took the stock down 21% in a day. Conversely, a clean 2027 outlook is the most likely re-rating trigger toward the $235–260 zone.
- **Feb 2027 — Digital Markets divestiture laps.** A $56M/quarter headwind disappears; reported revenue growth flips mechanically positive, improving the narrative optics.
- **Ongoing — buyback flywheel.** ~$2B/yr against a $14.6B cap = ~13.5% annual share shrink. This alone manufactures low-double-digit EPS growth regardless of top line.
- **Oct 2026 — Gartner IT Symposium/Xpo.** Flagship conference; bookings and 2027 exhibit commitments preview the Conferences segment (+15.5% YoY, 59.5% contribution margin, the fastest-growing piece).
- **Watch item:** any disclosure quantifying **AI-attributed contract value** or monetization of Gartner's own genAI products — a genuine re-rating catalyst if it appears.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

This is a high-quality franchise in a structurally contested category, priced at a fair-to-full multiple *after* a 76% recovery, with the stock now above the median analyst target and a binary earnings event in two weeks.

**Why not Buy:** Rule of 40 of 22 and forward growth of ~3–4% disqualify it under a growth-first mandate. Reported revenue is *contracting* in 2026. CV growth of 1.7% is barely nominal-GDP — that is the market's live evidence that AI substitution is already denting the core. And 13% annual EPS growth here is substantially **share-count math, not franchise math**; at 15x earnings you are paying full price for financial engineering.

**Why not Sell:** the bear case requires CV to go negative, and two quarters of acceleration say otherwise. The balance sheet is sound, FCF is $1.2B+, 70% gross margins are expanding (70.9% in Q2-26), Conferences is compounding at 15%+, the buyback is a genuine ~9%-yield engine, and the AI-panic discount is already ~62% off the high.

**Action:** Hold. Add on either (a) a pullback toward **$170–180** (near the August breakout base), or (b) hard evidence of CV growth >3% at the Nov 3 or Feb 2027 prints. Trim into strength above $240 without a 2027 guide to support it.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **AI substitution of the core product (structural, existential).** LLM/agentic search can answer "what does Gartner think" without paying for a seat. CV growth of 1.7% is the leading indicator already registering this. If CV turns negative, the multiple compresses again and there is no floor.
2. **Guidance-event risk.** Feb 2026's initial guide produced a −21% day and the stock bottomed 22% lower still. The pattern of disappointing forward guidance is not yet broken.
3. **Entry-point / expectations risk.** At $219.64, above the ~$200 median PT and +76% off the low, bad news is now asymmetric. Q3 on Nov 3 is a high-bar event.
4. **Buyback dependence.** ~$1.9B net debt funds a $2B/yr repurchase against a shrinking FCF base ($1.38B → $1.18B in FY2025). A FCF stumble stalls the flywheel that produces the EPS growth.
5. **Discretionary budget sensitivity.** Research seats are cut first in enterprise cost programs; federal/public-sector weakness was already flagged as a drag and only recently stabilizing.
6. **Segment concentration in IT/CIO spend.** All three segments lever to the same buyer — an IT budget downturn hits research renewals, consulting and conference attendance simultaneously.
7. **Data-quality gaps in this analysis:** Google Trends unavailable (429), no fresh company-published TAM, and 2029–30 consensus rests on just 2–3 analysts. Peer multiples were not retrieved (sector peer list returned mega-cap semis, which are not meaningful comparables for a research subscription business — Informa, Forrester, Clarivate, Verisk and CoStar would be the correct set).
