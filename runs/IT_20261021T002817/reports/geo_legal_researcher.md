# Gartner, Inc. (NYSE: IT) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Snapshot:** Global research/advisory firm; FY2025 revenue $6,497M (+4% y/y); clients in ~90 countries; 19,285 employees; market cap $13.9B (price $219.63). Business model is advisory/conference — asset-light, subscription-based, no manufacturing supply chain, no commodities. Geographic and sanctions exposure is therefore structurally *low*, and the dominant risk is **US litigation and US public-sector politics**, not foreign jurisdictions.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US federal / public-sector budget contraction (DOGE-era)
- **What:** Government clients materially slowed spending from 2025 into 2026; management now reports separately on "CV ex-fed" (3.3% growth in Q2 2026) and said government contract value "returned to positive NCVI [net contract value increase]" only in Q2 2026 — i.e., the *deceleration* was government-driven.
- **Evidence:** Q2 2026 call — *"persistent macroeconomic and geopolitical challenges are creating new and shifting budget pressures… executives are scrutinizing expenses, escalating approval processes, and delaying decisions."* Third-party commentary estimated 11 Gartner federal contracts cancelled with ~$58M of contract value at elevated risk (low-confidence, non-filed source).
- **Probability:** High (already realized). **Impact:** Moderate — federal is a minority of total CV (~$5.3B), but it was the marginal swing factor in the 2025 growth collapse.
- **Watch:** OBBBA tax provisions (explicitly cited in 10-K) and any renewed federal procurement squeeze.

### Risk 2 — EMEA regulatory & political volatility (26% of revenue, fastest-growing region)
- **What:** EMEA grew 12% in 2025, making it the growth engine — and it carries the heaviest regulatory load: GDPR enforcement, the EU AI Act's phased obligations, national data-transfer/sovereignty rules, and EU–US trade tension. Gartner is a controller of large volumes of attendee/prospect/benchmark data and increasingly a publisher of AI-related guidance.
- **Political backdrop:** French civil unrest (Oct 2026), EU maintaining Russia sanctions while US policy loosens, Middle East supply/travel risk — all relevant to a conference business (53 events, 83k+ attendees in 2025, conferences revenue +12% y/y in Q2 2026).
- **Probability:** Medium (regulatory friction ongoing; event disruption episodic). **Impact:** Low–Moderate (compliance cost and event-cancellation optionality rather than structural impairment).

### Risk 3 — AI/LLM disintermediation of the research franchise (regulatory/IP dimension)
- **What:** Gartner's own 10-K flags risk from "the exposure of our content to internet search engines and large language models," which "could reduce website traffic and demand for our products," plus liability from inaccurate/biased AI-generated content. This is the thesis underlying the securities class action.
- **Geopolitical overlay:** Divergent AI rules across jurisdictions (EU AI Act vs. US deregulatory posture — White House "AI safety accord," Oct 2026) create conflicting content/liability standards for a firm selling AI guidance globally.
- **Probability:** High (already pressuring growth). **Impact:** High on valuation multiple; Medium on near-term cash flow.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**A. Securities class action — the headline legal contingency (ACTIVE)**
- *Schmidt v. Gartner, Inc. et al.*, filed 17 Mar 2026 (federal court; D. Conn.-linked). Class period **4 Feb 2025 – 2 Feb 2026**.
- Allegations: defendants failed to disclose deterioration in contract value growth — reported as collapsing from **7.8% to ~1% in 2025** — and the impact of third-party AI tools on the core research business.
- Trigger event: shares fell **$92.78 in one session to $243.93** after the Q4 2025 release/2026 outlook miss (Feb 2026).
- **Lead-plaintiff deadline: 18 May 2026.** Multiple firms competing (Pomerantz, Bernstein Liebhard, Levi & Korsinsky, The Gross Law Firm, Smith).
- **Derivative risk:** Bernstein Liebhard issued a 22 Sep 2026 alert investigating "potential breaches of fiduciary duty by certain directors and officers" — a precursor to a derivative suit. D&O exposure is the key unquantified item.
- Sources: [PRNewswire lead-plaintiff notice](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/it-investors-have-opportunity-to-lead-gartner-inc-securities-fraud-lawsuit-302728263.html); [Bernstein Liebhard release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/24/3261386/0/en/shareholder-alert-bernstein-liebhard-llp-announces-a-securities-fraud-class-action-lawsuit-has-been-filed-against-gartner-inc-it.html); [Levi & Korsinsky / CV deterioration](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/04/13/3272814/0/en/it-investor-alert-gartner-inc-securities-fraud-lawsuit-investors-with-losses-may-seek-to-lead-the-class-action-after-allegedly-concealing-cv-growth-deterioration-levi-korsinsky.html); [Sept 2026 fiduciary-duty investigation](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/22/3366708/0/en/gartner-inc-nyse-it-shareholder-investigation-alert-bernstein-liebhard-investigates-potential-breaches-of-fiduciary-duty.html).

**B. Trade-secret / IP litigation — Gartner as plaintiff**
- *Gartner Inc. et al. v. Anmol Virk et al.*, No. 3:26-cv-01450 (D. Conn., 2026). Gartner sues former employees and **IDC**, seeking to enjoin interference with contractual (non-compete/confidentiality) obligations and use of Gartner trade secrets. Plaintiff posture — reputational and competitive-intelligence risk rather than monetary liability. ([Leagle](https://www.leagle.com/decision/infdco20260910c23); [Law360 docket](https://www.law360.com/cases/6aa03cd2072f253b4dfa1be8))

**C. Other litigation**
- *Collection Professionals Inc. v. Gartner*, 0:21-cv-00126 (D. Wyo.) — commercial contract dispute; small. ([Law360](https://www.law360.com/cases/60d3a62f5ea87ceb39b7434a/dockets))
- I found **no evidence of an active DOJ, SEC enforcement, FTC, or EU antitrust/GDPR proceeding against Gartner.** Absence of evidence, not proof of absence — the 10-K legal-proceedings disclosure was not retrievable via the filing tool (it returned "No 10-K/10-Q found"; SEC Archives blocked automated access with HTTP 403).

**D. Restructuring/impairment (accounting-legal adjacency)**
- **$150M goodwill impairment** in the Digital Markets reporting unit; that business was **sold for ~$110M** in 2025. Impairments of this type frequently attract follow-on securities claims — relevant to the *Schmidt* theory.

**E. Regulatory environment**
- Primary rule-sets: GDPR/UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, EU AI Act, SEC disclosure controls, and FTC Act Section 5 (unfair/deceptive practices) for AI-generated content claims. No single dominant sector regulator (Gartner is not a regulated financial or healthcare entity) — a structurally favorable regulatory position.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

| Flag | Severity | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **Disclosure controls / AI-tool use in research production** | **Elevated** | The class action specifically targets non-disclosure of how third-party AI tools affected the core research product. This is a *controls* problem, and a derivative suit would extend it to the board. |
| **Content/IP exposure to LLMs** | Moderate | Gartner is simultaneously IP plaintiff (vs. IDC) and structurally exposed to models trained on its published research. Copyright posture outside the US (EU DSM Directive, Japan's flexible TDM rules) is less favorable. |
| **FCPA / AML / sanctions** | **Low** | Advisory/conference model; no agents, distributors, or government procurement intermediaries of consequence. Residual exposure: client/speaker sanctions screening across ~90 countries and Middle East event operations (Iran/Russia programmes). No disclosed issues. |
| **Data privacy (GDPR class actions)** | Low–Moderate | Large volumes of professional/contact and benchmark data; EU-style collective redress raises tail risk, but no disclosed enforcement action. |
| **FX / repatriation** | Low–Moderate | 10-K states intent to reinvest "substantially all remaining accumulated undistributed foreign earnings," implying trapped cash and repatriation tax friction. No capital controls identified in core markets. |

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **MEDIUM** (low end of Medium)

**Rationale for the rating:**
- **Positives:** ~62% USD/US revenue; no manufacturing or commodity supply chain; no Russia/China concentration disclosed; no sanctions, FCPA, or AML history; no identified active government investigation; asset-light, fee-based model that is difficult to expropriate or tariff directly.
- **Negatives:** (i) **live securities class action plus a probable derivative action** — the single largest identifiable legal liability, with D&O and settlement costs not yet accruable/disclosed; (ii) **direct policy exposure to US federal budget politics** (the specific cause of the 2025 growth break); (iii) **26% EMEA concentration** in the region with the heaviest AI/privacy rule-making and the most active political disruption; (iv) AI disintermediation is both the core equity risk *and* the litigation theory.

**What would move this rating:**
- **To High:** a derivative suit naming directors; an SEC inquiry into AI-related disclosures; a material GDPR fine or EU enforcement action; or federal contract value turning negative again.
- **To Low:** dismissal of *Schmidt* with prejudice; sustained return to mid-single-digit CV growth ex-fed; and continued EMEA growth without event disruption.

**Key gaps in this report (honest limitations):** the 10-K/10-Q legal-proceedings note could not be retrieved directly (filing tool returned no 10-K/10-Q for IT; SEC Archives returned HTTP 403), so litigation provisioning figures and any undisclosed subpoenas could not be verified. Country-level revenue for "Other International" (China, Japan, India, Brazil) is not broken out by the company. The DOGE federal-contract-loss figures ($58M at risk) come from a third-party commentary source, not a filing, and should be treated as indicative only.
