## Sector Brief — Gartner, Inc. (NYSE: IT)
**Sector:** Information Services / IT Research & Advisory (FMP classifies IT within "Technology")
**Price $219.63 · Mkt cap $13.87B · Beta 0.98 · As of ~24-Oct-2026**

> **Methodology note:** FMP's `sector_peers` for IT returns NVIDIA, Apple, Microsoft, TSMC etc. — a $5trn mega-cap semiconductor cohort that shares no demand driver, cost structure or customer with Gartner. I discarded that set and manually constructed the economically correct comp group: **information services / research & data analytics** (Forrester, Verisk, FactSet, Morningstar, Clarivate, CoStar). This is the single most important judgement in this brief.

### 1. Sector Overview

Gartner sits in the IT research & advisory industry — a roughly $15–20B global niche inside the far larger information-services complex, where vendors sell subscription access to proprietary analysis, benchmarks and expert inquiry (Research), plus conferences and bespoke consulting. It is a **structural oligopoly with collapsing barriers to entry**: Gartner holds an estimated >50% share of IT research/advisory and is ~16x Forrester's revenue, but the industry's core product — a written report synthesising expert opinion — is precisely what generative AI now produces at near-zero marginal cost. The defining feature of 2026 is a **sharp divergence between end-market demand and business-model viability**: Gartner's own forecast has worldwide IT spending growing **+14.2% to $6.37trn in 2026** (revised up from +10.8% and then +13.5% during the year) with AI spending **+49.5% to $2.7trn**, yet the information-services equities de-rated violently on AI-disruption fear. Sector cycle position: **late-mature, entering structural disruption** — not a normal cyclical downturn, and therefore not something an IT-budget recovery will fix.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **An unambiguous IT budget boom in the end market.** Gartner's July-2026 forecast puts worldwide IT spend at **$6.37trn, +14.2%** — upgraded three times in 2026 (+10.8% → +13.5% → +14.2%). AI spending at $2.7trn (+49.5%). Rising budget and, critically, rising *architectural complexity* (AI platforms, neoclouds, agentic tooling) is the raw feedstock for advisory demand: buyers cannot evaluate this category unaided.
- **AI is expanding inquiry volume, not just cannibalising content.** Gartner's 1Q26 evidence is that AI-related inquiry is a net demand *driver* — clients need help with AI governance, EU AI Act compliance, vendor selection and cost optimisation. Gartner's own research output (the "80% of neoclouds extinct by 2030" call, which moved CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN) demonstrates the brand still sets industry narrative — a durable pricing asset.
- **Conferences and consulting are counter-cyclical and margin-rich.** The IT Symposium/Xpo franchise is filling on AI themes, and consulting/cost-optimisation work historically *rises* when clients tighten budgets. Meanwhile buybacks cut diluted shares from 86.2M (2021) to 72.1M (2025), ~16% cumulative, mechanically supporting EPS.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Contract value growth has decelerated to stall speed.** Q3-2026 contract value growth of **~+2.5%** (UBS, vs 2.3% consensus) and net new CV of only **~$58M/quarter** — against mid-to-high single digits historically. Reported revenue growth fell from **+15.7% (FY22) → +7.9% (FY23) → +6.1% (FY24) → +3.7% (FY25)**. A subscription business compounding CV at 2.5% cannot support a premium multiple.
- **The AI-disintermediation de-rating is the dominant fact of the sector.** On **3-Feb-2026 IT fell 27.6% in a single session — its worst day since October 1999** — during a global software/data selloff triggered by an Anthropic product release. The shares are ~62% below the Feb-2025 peak of $584. The market has re-priced the entire "sell expert opinion by the seat" model, and it has not restored the multiple despite the operating results holding up.
- **Seat rationalisation and margin erosion.** Enterprise procurement is consolidating read-only seats — the lowest-value tier — while Gartner defends the franchise by reinvesting in AI tooling and restructuring. Operating margin has compressed **-515bp in two years (20.9% FY23 → 15.8% FY25)** and gross margin from 69.5% to 67.7%. The company is buying growth with margin, and the comp set (Verisk 43.7%, Morningstar 28.3%) shows how far the cost structure could still travel.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Strong (within a threatened niche)**

Rationale:
- **Share leadership is not in dispute.** >50% of IT research/advisory and roughly 16x the revenue of its nearest pure-play rival; the Magic Quadrant is a genuine network-effect asset (vendors must participate; buyers use it as a procurement gate).
- **It is the only direct comp still growing and profitably.** +3.7% revenue with a 15.8% operating margin versus Forrester at 0.5% and Clarivate at 2.9% — Gartner is taking the oxygen out of the category even as the category deflates.
- **But the moat is being tested at the point of value capture, not the point of brand.** 2.5% CV growth says the brand still wins renewals while seat counts and price per seat erode. Gartner has the balance sheet and cost base to fund an AI-native re-platforming (a smaller, cheaper, AI-augmented analyst estate), but has not yet demonstrated that it can re-accelerate CV.
- **Valuation is no longer a call option on that fix.** At ~23x trailing FY25 diluted EPS and **+75% off the June-2026 low of $124 to $219.63**, a meaningful part of the recovery is already priced. (Note: FY24 GAAP EPS of $16.00 was inflated by a one-off tax benefit — pretax income was ~$1.03B against $1.25B of net income — so the $16.00 → $9.65 EPS decline overstates the deterioration.)

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

End-market demand is genuinely superb (+14.2% IT spend, +49.5% AI spend) and Gartner is the highest-quality asset in its niche — but "sector" here means the information-services business model, and that model is in the early innings of an AI-driven re-pricing that has already erased ~62% of Gartner's and a similar share of its peers' equity value. The 2.5% CV print is the number to anchor on: if it inflects toward mid-single digits on AI-governance demand, the group re-rates sharply from a de-rated base; if it stalls at 2–3%, the multiple compresses further regardless of how strong the IT budget backdrop is. **Neutral, with the sector cycle best described as a mature oligopoly undergoing structural disruption.**
