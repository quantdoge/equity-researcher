## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW**

- **Business model is inherently low-carbon:** offices, data centres (cloud-hosted), and human capital. No manufacturing, no Scope 1 process emissions, no water-intensive or land-intensive operations, no CBAM-exposed goods.
- **Carbon intensity:** Low. Estimated Scope 1+2 in the low tens of thousands of tCO2e against ~$6B+ revenue (order of ~2–5 tCO2e per $1M revenue — a fraction of the S&P 500 average). *Unverified: Gartner does not appear on the CDP A-list and I could not confirm an SBTi-validated target in available sources.*
- **Disclosure quality is the weak point, not the footprint.** Gartner publishes a Corporate Responsibility Report with a stated ambition to "contribute to a more sustainable world" and has referenced a net-zero GHG goal (per the 2021 report), but I could not verify interim targets, third-party assurance, or Scope 3 completeness. For a company that **sells** ESG guidance to clients, thin own-household disclosure is a mild credibility/greenwashing exposure.
- **Conferences segment is the real environmental and physical-climate touchpoint:** flagship IT Symposium/Xpo events (Orlando, Barcelona, India, Tokyo) generate material Scope 3 business-travel emissions and concentrate revenue in a handful of venues — exposure to extreme weather, travel disruption, and (historically) pandemic shutdowns.
- **Transition risk:** negligible — no carbon-tax-exposed assets, no fossil-fuel-linked revenue concentration.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM**

- **Human capital is the core social issue.** Glassdoor 3.8/5 overall with ~70% "recommend to a friend"; recurring employee commentary on micromanagement, high turnover in sales, and understaffing. For a subscription business where renewal is relationship-driven, elevated quota-carrier churn is a genuine revenue-quality risk, not a soft metric.
- **Workforce scale:** ~19,285 employees; sales-heavy, global, remote/hybrid. Compensation is heavily incentive-based, which drives both productivity and burnout.
- **Diversity:** Gartner publishes workforce diversity data in its Corporate Responsibility Report and has been a visible voice on women's workplace progression (its own research on the topic is extensive). No adverse DEI controversy surfaced. Board is 6 women / 13 nominees (approx. 46%) — genuinely strong for a tech board.
- **Data privacy & AI:** the highest-severity *latent* social risk. Gartner holds client inquiry data, benchmarking submissions, and event attendee data; it is also building AI-enabled products. GDPR, CCPA/CPRA, and emerging EU AI Act obligations apply. No disclosed breach found — but a breach would hit trust, which is the product.
- **No supply-chain labour exposure; no community-relations friction; no product-safety liability.**

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **6.0 / 10**

**Positives:**
- **One share, one vote** — single class, no dual-class structure, no poison pill disclosed. Clean shareholder-rights profile.
- 12 of 13 directors independent; only the CEO is non-independent. Audit by **KPMG** (ratified 2026).
- **Say-on-pay support ~93%** (2025) — no shareholder revolt.
- Pay design is genuinely long-term oriented: **70% of LTI in PSUs, 30% SARs, ~95% of CEO pay incentive-based**, tied to Contract Value and EBITDA/revenue; clawback policy, stock-ownership/holding requirements, anti-hedging/anti-pledging, double-trigger change-in-control.
- Independent compensation advisers; no related-party or accounting-control controversy.

**Negatives:**
- **Combined Chairman & CEO.** Gene Hall became Chair in 2024 (succeeding James Smith), while remaining CEO — **no independent chair**, and effectively a 22-year-tenure CEO. Succession risk is concentrated in one person.
- **Board staleness / limited refreshment.** Tenures of Pagliuca **36.3 yrs**, Grabe **33.5 yrs**, Fuchs **27.3 yrs**, Bressler **20.7 yrs**, Dykstra **19.3 yrs**, Cesan **14.7 yrs**. Only two directors have <4 years. Average tenure is far above best practice; Boardroom Alpha grades the board **C+**.
- **Overboarding:** several directors hold 2–3 additional public boards (Pagliuca: Coherent, NCLH, Symbotic; Dykstra: Atlassian, Arm; Ferguson: Sally Beauty, Mattel; Rus: Symbotic; Serra: Capital One).
- **Pay-for-performance optics:** 82.1% PSU payout and 119.6% cash bonus for 2025 while **1-year TSR was −23.3%**. Also, the Compensation Committee **excluded the U.S. federal public-sector Insights business from 2025 targets** for "volatility" — target re-cutting is a yellow flag for pay rigour.
- **Capital allocation & disclosure:** heavy buyback orientation combined with a revenue-growth stumble invites scrutiny of disclosure quality (see litigation below).
- **Insider activity:** CEO Hall has sold >$6M in stock; limited open-market buying (small ESPP purchases).

**Verdict:** structurally sound (rights, independence ratio, comp architecture) but **governance-process laggard on board refreshment, chair independence, and pay-for-performance**.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD:** Gartner has substantial EU/UK operations and revenue well above CSRD thresholds; likely in scope via large EU subsidiaries for FY2025/FY2026 reporting waves. Cost of compliance (double-materiality assessment, assured ESRS data) is real but modest — perhaps $2–5M/yr in incremental reporting and controls. **Medium-low.**
- **EU AI Act / GDPR / DPF:** high relevance. Gartner both *uses* AI in products and *advises* on it. AI Act transparency and data-governance obligations apply; EU–US Data Privacy Framework dependence for client data transfers.
- **California SB 253/261** (revenue >$1B, doing business in CA): Scope 1/2/3 and climate-risk disclosure, with delayed but live timelines. **Medium.**
- **US SEC climate rule:** adopted 2024, stayed, and effectively **abandoned** — US federal climate-disclosure pressure has collapsed, reducing near-term US reporting cost.
- **CBAM:** none (no goods trade).
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified. Gartner carries modest leverage and standard revolver/notes; no sustainability-linked pricing.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating — **AVERAGE**

Gartner is a **low-environmental-footprint, governance-structurally-sound business with a stale board, a combined Chair/CEO, a persistent research-independence controversy, and a live securities-litigation overhang.** It is neither a credible ESG "Leader" (own-household disclosure is thin and no verified SBTi target was found) nor a "Laggard" (clean shareholder rights, 93% say-on-pay, 70% PSU-weighted pay, ~46% female board). Third-party scores (MSCI/Sustainalytics) were not retrievable in this data pull; on the fundamentals I would map this to roughly **MSCI BBB** and a **Sustainalytics Low-to-Medium risk** band — flagged as analyst estimate, not vendor data.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication — **NEUTRAL**

**ESG factors are not the swing factor in the IT thesis — but two of them are quietly load-bearing:**

1. **Research independence is the franchise.** The NetScout win is reassuring, but the pay-to-play narrative plus AI-driven commoditisation of syndicated research is the same threat from two directions: if buyers stop believing Gartner's objectivity, renewal rates break. This is why I weight Governance/Ethics as Critical rather than Medium.
2. **Human capital is the revenue mechanism.** With Q3 2026 contract value growth running only ~2.5% and net new CV of ~$58M, any elevation in quota-carrier turnover translates into slower CV growth with little cushion — the −23.3% 1-year TSR already reflects that fragility.

**Governance watch items for engagement/voting:** vote against the combined Chair/CEO structure or withhold support from long-tenured directors on the nominating committee; push for an independent chair and a tenure/refreshment policy; scrutinise the 2025 exclusion of the federal Insights business from PSU targets and the disconnect between 119.6% bonus payout and negative TSR.

**Bottom line:** No ESG-driven exclusion, and no ESG-driven re-rating. Environmental risk is negligible; Social is manageable and fixable; Governance is the pillar to monitor because it is where credibility risk, succession risk, and disclosure risk all converge. **ESG is neutral to the investment case; the real risk is AI disruption of the research franchise, which shows up in the Social/Governance bucket rather than the Environmental one.**
