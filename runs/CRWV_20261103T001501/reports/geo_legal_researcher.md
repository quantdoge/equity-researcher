### 1. Geographic Risk Map

CoreWeave is a **US-domiciled, US-revenue-concentrated** business with a fast-growing, capital-intensive European and Asian footprint. Its FY2025 10-K and Q2 2026 10-Q state that it generates "a small portion of our revenue outside of the United States" while conducting "business activities in various foreign countries" ([10-Q, 3/31/26](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000222/crwv-20260331.htm)).

| Country/Region | Revenue exposure | Operating exposure | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | ~95%+ (bulk; exact split not disclosed) | HQ (Livingston, NJ); 1,967 of 2,189 employees at YE25 ([10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000104/crwv-20251231.htm)); majority of 51 data centers, 1.5 GW active / ~4.2 GW contracted | **Medium** — not sovereign risk, but *single-jurisdiction concentration* + state/local permitting, power and tariff risk |
| **United Kingdom** | Small, immaterial today | ~£1bn London/UK build; part of 9 European sites | **Low–Medium** (energy permitting, UK data/AI regulation) |
| **Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Spain** | Small | 9 European data centers across DK, UK, ES, SE, NO; new Copenhagen (atNorth DEN01) 22.5 MW ([release, 10/27/26](https://www.coreweave.com/news/coreweave-expands-global-ai-cloud-platform-with-new-capacity-in-denmark-and-first-ai-network-backbone)) | **Low** (strong rule of law; EU regulatory layer) |
| **India** | Nil today | 240 MW planned Navi Mumbai deployment with AdaniConneX ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/CRWV/core-weave-enters-india-expanding-ai-cloud-platform-with-adani-conne-tgwshdiwbe4w.html)) | **Medium** (execution, FX/capital repatriation, grid reliability) |
| **UAE / Japan** | Nil today | Network points-of-presence on new global AI backbone (extending later in 2026) ([release, 10/27/26](https://investors.coreweave.com/news/news-details/2026/CoreWeave-Expands-Global-AI-Cloud-Platform-With-New-Capacity-in-Denmark-and-First-Global-AI-Network-Backbone/default.aspx)) | **Medium** (UAE: export-licence/end-use controls, diversion risk; Japan: low) |
| **China / Russia** | **Nil** | No operations | **N/A but see supply chain** |

**Critical read-through:** geographic *revenue* risk is low (no EM or sanctioned-market revenue), but the **supply and counterparty chain is concentrated in two places — the US (power, permitting, customers, capital markets) and Taiwan/NVIDIA (silicon)**. That is the real geopolitical exposure.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US political/local backlash against AI data-center build-out — Probability: High / Impact: High**
~4.2 GW contracted power and 1.5 GW active make permitting, power procurement and community consent CoreWeave's binding constraint (risk factor #2 in the FY2025 10-K: power access and cost). WSJ (11/2/26) reports investors are watching **state and local elections for pushback against the AI infrastructure build-out**; Texas has seen moratoria and federal litigation (Hill County repealed a blanket construction pause only after being sued — [K&L Gates, 6/10/26](https://www.klgates.com/thought-leadership/Facing-a-Federal-Lawsuit-Hill-County-Repeals-Blanket-Pause-on-Data-Center-Construction-Adopting-New-Approval-Procedures-Instead-6-10-2026)). There is already an activist campaign against a $1.8bn CoreWeave data center approved without public input. Separately, the 10-Q discloses **393 MW at one site and 355 MW at other sites undelivered at 6/30/26** — permitting/power slippage is a live earnings risk, and it is the same deficiency that triggered the securities suit (below). Compounding: US midterms Nov 3, 2026 (prediction markets ~74–75% Democrats take Senate, CNBC 11/2/26) and **oil above $100** with Hormuz/Iran tensions (Bloomberg/Schwab, 10/31–11/2/26) — both feed power pricing and the AI-credit narrative.

**② Export controls & trade policy on AI hardware — Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium-High**
The 10-K carries a dedicated risk factor: "We are subject to laws and regulations, including governmental export and import controls, sanctions, and anti-corruption laws, that could impair our ability to compete." The filing also flags that **the US announced tariffs on most countries in 2025 and global tariffs in 2026** ([FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1769628/000176962826000104/crwv-20251231.htm)), and the 10-Q repeats that "increasing use of tariffs, economic sanctions and export controls has impacted and may in the future impact the availability" of equipment ([10-Q](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/CRWV/10-q-core-weave-inc-quarterly-earnings-report-d3b596a1f167.html)). Taiwan is tightening AI-chip/server export controls in alignment with US rules (Bloomberg, 10/16/26), tightening the NVIDIA/Taiwan funnel on which 100% of CoreWeave's GPU fleet depends. Effect on CRWV is two-sided: restricted China demand frees GPU supply, but raises input cost and complicates the new **UAE/India/Japan** deployments where end-use and re-export compliance (EAR "taint", data-localisation) becomes an operational requirement.

**③ Energy/credit sovereign-adjacent risk: the AI debt complex — Probability: Medium / Impact: High**
Not classic country risk, but the market is now pricing CoreWeave as a leveraged infrastructure credit: **$35.6bn principal debt**, $985m of H1-26 interest expense, $14.1bn H1 capex, plus an inaugural Eurobond and >$10bn of unsecured/convertible issuance ([10-Q](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/CRWV/10-q-core-weave-inc-quarterly-earnings-report-d3b596a1f167.html)). The stock hit an all-time low of $49.92 on 11/2/26, down 8% on the day and **44% in a month**, explicitly on debt concerns and the AI bond rout ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/trading-ideas/movers/26/11/62795864/coreweave-stock-hits-new-all-time-low); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/coverage/stock-market-today/2026/11/02/stock-market-today-nov-2-coreweave-slides-on-debt-concerns-after-crowdstrike-partnership-expands/)). Financing is dollar-denominated and domestically sourced — no FX/repatriation problem, but refinancing depends on US rates and AI sentiment.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action (principal legal exposure).** Filed 1/12/26 in the **District of New Jersey** against CoreWeave and executives (class period 3/28/25–12/15/25), alleging §§10(b)/20(a) and Rule 10b-5 violations: overstatement of ability to meet demand and material understatement of reliance on a **single third-party data-center developer**; triggered by the 11/10/25 guidance cut for a delayed third-party developer and a 12/15/25 WSJ report ([D&O Diary, 1/14/26](https://www.dandodiary.com/2026/01/articles/artificial-intelligence/ai-infrastructure-company-hit-with-ai-related-securities-suit/)). Lead-plaintiff motions filed 3/13/26; **action ongoing** ([Kessler Topaz](https://www.ktmc.com/new-cases/coreweave-inc/)). Size not quantified; a consolidated-complaint/motion-to-dismiss outcome is the next catalyst.
- **Core Scientific merger — terminated, not litigated to judgment.** The all-stock acquisition (announced 7/7/25) **failed its shareholder vote and was terminated 10/30/25** ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/press-releases/crwv-investor-alert-coreweave-inc-securities-fraud-class-action-focuses)). Residual risk: any disclosure-based claims from that proxy solicitation, and the fact that Core Scientific remains a commercial counterparty/host.
- **No disclosed DOJ/FTC/SEC enforcement action, no antitrust case, and no material pending litigation named in the 10-Q summary reviewed.** I could not retrieve the full "Legal Proceedings" note (SEC EDGAR returned 403 on direct fetch — noted gap).
- **Regulatory regimes flagged by the company:** export/import controls, OFAC sanctions, FCPA/anti-corruption, GDPR (EU/UK data protection), plus AI-specific regulation (EU AI Act obligations phasing in) and US state privacy law — the 10-K's legal/regulatory risk group covers all of these ([gloom.sh FY2025 10-K digest](https://gloom.sh/stocks/crwv/risk-factors/2026)).
- **Counterparty concentration as a quasi-legal risk:** Microsoft ≈**67% of FY2025 revenue** (10-K); H1 2026 shows **Customer A 40%, Customer B 23%** of revenue (10-Q) — improvement, but contract-level exposure to a handful of counterparties (Microsoft, OpenAI, Meta, Jane Street's $6bn commercial commitment). Note the 11/2/26 sector selloff on reports OpenAI cut its annualised revenue figure ($68bn→$50bn) — third-party AI-lab creditworthiness is now a CRWV risk vector.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **GPU end-use/diversion controls on new UAE and India capacity** — the highest-probability compliance failure mode for a neocloud scaling into the Gulf; requires robust Know-Your-Customer and re-export controls (see [Greenberg Traurig, 9/2026](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2026/9/navigating-gpu-export-controls-and-ai-use-restrictions-in-data-center-operations)).
2. **Legacy crypto-mining heritage** (incorporated 2017 as Atlantic Crypto; 10-K notes "previously relied partly on discontinued crypto-mining offerings") — residual AML/sanctions-screening expectations on any retained mining-adjacent customers or wallet-related payments.
3. **Customer-of-customer AI governance risk** — hosting frontier-model training/inference for AI labs exposes CRWV to EU AI Act / data-sovereignty obligations it cannot fully control.
4. **Insider-selling cadence** — 16 consecutive Form 4 filings between 10/1/26 and 10/30/26, with no offsetting 10-Q/8-K of that period in the recent-filing list; worth monitoring but not itself a red flag.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale.** Direct sovereign/EM/sanctions exposure is minimal — essentially all revenue is US-generated, with no China/Russia presence. Offsetting that, the company carries (i) a live securities class action tied to the very permitting/power-delay issue that is now its binding operational constraint, (ii) a concentrated NVIDIA/Taiwan supply funnel exposed to fast-moving US and Taiwanese export controls and 2025–26 tariff regimes, and (iii) an emerging-market and Middle East expansion (India, UAE) that introduces new compliance and repatriation surface without yet contributing revenue. Add high dependence on US state/local permitting for ~4 GW of contracted power and a leveraged capital structure priced as credit, and the risk is **Medium** (rising to High if the securities action survives a motion to dismiss or if a state-level data-center moratorium hits a contracted site).
