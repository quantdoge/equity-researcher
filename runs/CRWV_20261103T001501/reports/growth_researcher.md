## CoreWeave (CRWV) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $50.15 (Nov 2, 2026) · Market cap ~$27.7B · EV ~$54–75B · Down 73% from June 2025 high of $187**

---

### 1. Growth Story

CoreWeave is the purest publicly traded expression of the AI compute buildout — a GPU-specialized cloud that converts NVIDIA allocation into multi-year contracted revenue at 50%+ EBITDA margins. Revenue compounded from **$15.8M (FY22) to $5.13B (FY25)**, and is guided to **$12.4–13.2B in FY26**, backed by a **$104B revenue backlog at 6/30/26 (~$130B including $25B+ of early-Q3 commitments)** — roughly **12.6x annualized revenue**. The disruptive thesis is intact; what has broken is the *financing* narrative: bond yields near 15%, CDS ~1,000bps, and $31–35B of 2026 capex funded overwhelmingly with borrowed money have compressed the equity to ~1.0x 2027E sales. The market is no longer debating whether CoreWeave will grow — it is pricing the probability that the capital structure does not survive the growth.

### 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

- **TAM:** global data-center/AI infrastructure capex is tracking toward **$600B–$1T annually by 2030**; the merchant GPU-cloud (neocloud) slice CoreWeave competes for is a multi-hundred-billion-dollar pool as hyperscalers and AI labs externalize peak compute.
- **Contracted penetration:** $104B backlog + $25B early-Q3 = ~$130B locked. Against a $87B 2030E revenue consensus, backlog already covers ~1.5x the entire 2030 estimate. **This is the single strongest fact in the thesis** — it converts a speculative demand bet into an execution/financing question.
- **Customer concentration is improving fast:** top customer fell from **62% of 2024 revenue (Microsoft) → 67% peak → 36% in Q2'26**, with Meta (~$21B contract) and OpenAI layered in. Diversification is real progress, but each new mega-contract is also a new capex commitment.
- **Why CoreWeave and not a peer:** time-to-power, NVIDIA allocation priority and reference-architecture depth (bare metal, Mission Control, Tensorizer). Nebius, IREN, Crusoe and Lambda compete on the same demand but not the same contracted book. The moat is *operational and relational*, not proprietary IP — the weakest link in the story.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Nov 10, 2026 — Q3 FY26 results (5:00pm ET, ~1 week away).** Consensus ~**$3.54B revenue (+160% YoY)** and EPS **-$1.28 to -$1.53**. The three numbers that matter: backlog refresh (does it clear $130B?), 2027 revenue framework, and whether capex guidance moves off $31–35B.
- **Debt-market thaw.** $2.6B leveraged loan was repriced to ~SOFR+550bps (July 2026); unsecured notes near 15%. **$10.6B of principal matures by end-2027.** Any successful raise at materially tighter spreads — or a Fed easing cycle — is the highest-torque re-rating event available.
- **New mega-contracts / backlog conversion**, particularly incremental OpenAI or enterprise wins that shift the mix away from a single counterparty.
- **First credible path-to-positive-FCF disclosure** (post-2027 capex normalization) — the trigger that lets the market value this on EBITDA rather than on credit.
- **Index/liquidity events & dilution**: further equity issuance would fund growth but reset the per-share math; watch share count (435M → 551M in twelve months).

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** *(speculative; risk-sized position, not a core holding)*

The growth is not the question. A ~$130B contracted backlog, 112% current growth, 57% EBITDA margins, and a first profit year in 2028 do not deserve to trade at **1.0x forward sales and ~7x 2027E EBITDA**. The 73% drawdown has moved this from a momentum name to a **levered call option on AI compute demand**, priced as though refinancing fails. My framework says growth rate over valuation when growth is durable — and here durability is contractually documented in the backlog, which is precisely the condition that justifies looking through a Rule-of-40 failure.

But I will not pretend the balance sheet is a detail. Interest expense is running ~$2.6B annualized against $1.47B of quarterly EBITDA (**2.3x coverage and compressing** as each incremental $10B of debt adds ~$1B of annual interest), GAAP EBIT was only $76M in Q2'26 after $1.39B of quarterly D&A, and capex of $31–35B is only ~30% self-funded (FY25 OCF $3.06B vs. capex $10.3B). **This is a funding-model risk masquerading as a valuation risk.** If credit markets stay shut, equity holders absorb the dilution; if they reopen, the equity is worth multiples of $50.

**What I would do:** establish a starter position ahead of Nov 10, size it so a 50% further drawdown is survivable, and add on evidence of (a) a backlog print above $130B with 2027 revenue framed above $26B, and (b) any debt raise inside of 10%. Confirmation, not prediction, on the financing leg.

### 7. Key Risks

1. **Refinancing / liquidity risk (highest severity).** ~$35.6B borrowed principal at 6/30/26 (total obligations including financing leases cited near $50B), **$10.6B due by end-2027**, and bond yields near 15% with CDS ~1,000bps. A failed or expensive raise is an equity-impairing event, not a multiple-compression event.
2. **Counterparty concentration & AI-demand elasticity.** Top customer still 36% of revenue; OpenAI's downward revenue revision already moved the stock. Backlog is only as good as the credit of the counterparties behind it — and several are pre-profit.
3. **Depreciation policy risk.** GPUs are depreciated over **six years** (extended from four in 2023). If useful life is economically 3–4 years under rapid NVIDIA cadence, reported EBITDA is overstated and the 2028 profit inflection is an accounting artifact. This is the bear case's sharpest weapon.
4. **Gross margin compression** (74.3% → 65.9%) — if pricing power erodes as Nebius, IREN, Crusoe and hyperscaler self-build add capacity, EBITDA margins follow, and the entire levered structure gets thinner.
5. **Dilution and equity-dependence.** Share count +27% in twelve months. Growth funded by equity issuance transfers the upside away from current holders.
6. **Macro/rate sensitivity.** Beta of 7.7 makes this a de facto rates trade; the October drawdown was triggered by rate-hike fear as much as by company specifics.
