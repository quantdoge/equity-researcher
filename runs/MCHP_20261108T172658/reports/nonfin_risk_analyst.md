## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Microchip Technology Inc. (MCHP)

**Snapshot:** $31.7B mkt cap, $58.35/sh, beta 1.85, 17,900 employees. CEO Steve Sanghi is **President, CEO and Chair of the Board** (returned Nov 2024 after Ganesh Moorthy's retirement). FY26 (ended 3/31/26) net sales $4.713B; Q2 FY27 revenue $1.622B (+42.2% YoY) but guided **down ~1% sequentially**.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (low end — one notch above Weak)**

**Positives:** Annual director elections, majority voting, proxy access, no poison pill, separate lead independent director (Matthew Chapman, also Audit Committee chair, appointed Nov 2024). Board of 8 with ~6 independent; 7 nominees at the 2026 annual meeting. Predominantly independent committees.

**Concerns (evidence-based):**
- **CEO/Chair duality** concentrates power in a founder-era executive who was brought back as an emergency CEO. The lead independent director mitigates but does not cure this.
- **FY2025 say-on-pay passed with only 45.65% support** — a majority-scale shareholder rebuke. Management responded by lifting performance-based equity to ~60% of CEO LTI and granting an **interim CEO award of $20.9M** plus a **permanent CEO award of $28.2M** grant-date value. FY2026 CEO pay totaled **~$40.5M, +89% YoY, on a 698:1 CEO-to-median-employee ratio.** 2026 say-on-pay recovered to roughly **83% support** (~17% against) — improved, still meaningful dissent.
- **Pay-metric quality:** CEO LTI vests on 12-quarter **non-GAAP operating margin** targets while FY26 GAAP net income was only **$118.8M** vs. non-GAAP $933.9M (GAAP EPS $0.22 vs. non-GAAP $1.64). A ~7.5x GAAP/non-GAAP gap is exactly the disclosure pattern that previously drew securities litigation here (see §2).
- **Capital return vs. deleveraging:** $984M returned in FY26, ~$12.5B cumulative, in a business still carrying Microsemi-era debt — shareholder-friendly but reduces the buffer for a high-beta cyclical.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **LOW-MODERATE (no live enforcement; elevated sector-level exposure)**
- **EDGAR full-text search for SEC enforcement actions naming MCHP: none found.** No identified BIS/export-control penalty against the company.
- **Resolved — securities class action (D. Ariz.):** arose from the $10B Microsemi acquisition; motion to dismiss denied March 2020 (Judge Tuchi found allegations that defendants "conflate[d] the two" accounting bases and misrepresented Microsemi's "inventory-stuffed channel" cogent); **settled for $9M, final approval June 2022.** This is the single most important reputational scar: a judicial finding that channel-inventory and non-GAAP presentation allegations were plausible.
- **Resolved — *Schuman v. Microchip* (ERISA, Atmel legacy severance):** Ninth Circuit (6/5/25) reversed enforcement of employee severance releases; **$9.53M settlement plus $3.5M fees, final approval Oct 28, 2026**, covering ~220 former employees. Reveals M&A-inherited benefit-plan exposure.
- **Forward-looking:** export-control/licensing volatility (BIS 2026 licensing changes; China's Oct 2026 proposed controls on 40 technology categories), tariff and input-cost pass-through (January 2026 price increases), and government/space program compliance (PIC64-HPSC). **Asia is 49.9% of sales** (Americas 30.2%, Europe 19.9%, FY25), leaving direct China/tariff sensitivity. No material GDPR/CCPA profile (B2B, low consumer data).

### 4. Top Operational Risks
- **Capacity/supply constraint after self-inflicted footprint cuts (highest severity).** Management cut ~2,000 jobs and accelerated the Fab 2 (Tempe) shutdown to May 2025. Now AI data-center buyers have "taken over the whole factory" at trailing-edge foundries and OSATs, lead times are stretching, and the CEO says the fix "could take six months." Result: guided **down sequentially despite +42% YoY demand** — losing revenue in an upcycle, with gross margin already fading from 66.5%.
- **Channel and customer concentration.** Distributors are the dominant sales channel; **Arrow Electronics alone = 12% of FY26 net sales** (10% FY25). Channel inventory/double-ordering risk is structurally tied to the historical litigation, and distributor credit/insolvency risk is unhedged.
- **Geographic and back-end manufacturing concentration + demonstrated cyber fragility.** Asia ~50% of sales; Thailand is separately disclosed as a major long-lived-asset location (assembly/test), exposing the back-end to single-country geopolitical, tariff, and natural-catastrophe risk. The **August 2024 unauthorized-access incident** shut down systems, disrupted multiple manufacturing facilities, and cost **$21.4M in one quarter** — proof of material IT/OT exposure in a fab environment. Add **key-person risk**: turnaround credibility rests almost entirely on Sanghi, with no disclosed successor.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **MODERATE**
B2B design-win model means low consumer-backlash risk, but three live narratives are corrosive: (a) **"pay while cutting"** — CEO compensation up 89% to $40.5M and a 698:1 ratio in the same window as 2,000 layoffs, a fab closure, and severance litigation settled for $9.5M; (b) **channel-stuffing history** from the Microsemi case, which any future inventory correction will revive; (c) **CEO's public "data center is in the bubble" remark** — candid, but it undercuts the 16.7%-of-sales, +55%-growth segment he is selling, and contributed to a 10% single-day drop. ESG posture is disclosure-oriented (human-rights policy, information-security governance oversight) with **no binding climate targets** — a modest ratings/passive-flow irritant rather than a controversy.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**
No active enforcement action, no fraud indicators, no going-concern or whistleblower signals, and high insider ownership argue against High. But the combination of (i) a constrained, newly-shrunk manufacturing footprint in a demand surge, (ii) CEO/Chair concentration with a 45.65% say-on-pay history, (iii) heavy insider selling into strength, and (iv) a demonstrated material cyber loss justifies **Moderate with a negative tilt** — the risks are more likely to compound than cancel.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation
1. **CEO sold ~$64M (738,744 sh) of stock in Jan–Apr 2026 at $79–93 with no offsetting purchases, before a ~37% decline.** Verify 10b5-1 adoption/modification dates; any plan adopted inside 90 days of sale would materially worsen the read.
2. **FY2025 say-on-pay majority rejection (45.65%)**, followed by +89% CEO pay and a $49M combined interim/permanent CEO award stack — test whether the Comp Committee's 12-quarter non-GAAP margin targets are achievable without the margin fade now appearing (66.5% → guided lower).
3. **Supply-side failure to serve demand** — sequential guide-down into a shortage implies lost design-win momentum and share loss to NXP/TI/Renesas; track lead times and distributor inventory quarterly.
4. **Aug-2024 cyber incident recurrence risk** — seek disclosure on remediation spend, insurance recoveries, and whether OT segmentation was completed.
5. **M&A-inherited benefit/release weaknesses** (*Schuman* Ninth Circuit ruling) — check for additional legacy Atmel/Microsemi severance or plan-liability claims.
6. **China/export-control and tariff exposure** embedded in ~50% Asia sales, with no disclosed mitigation beyond pricing.
