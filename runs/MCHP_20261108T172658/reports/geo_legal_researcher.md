## MCHP — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company:** Microchip Technology Inc. (Nasdaq: MCHP) — US-domiciled (Chandler, AZ), semis, $31.7bn mkt cap, 17,900 employees. FY ends March 31. Latest filing: 10-Q for Q2 FY27 (filed 5 Nov 2026); FY2026 10-K filed 21 May 2026.

FY2026: **~75% of net sales to foreign customers, 18% China, 15% Taiwan** (FY2025: 17% China, 16% Taiwan). Q1 FY27 (June 2026 10-Q): **19% China, 15% Taiwan**.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US Section 232 tariff escalation into mature-node/derivative products**
- **Probability: High (60–70%) / Impact: Medium-High (~200–400bp of gross margin at risk)**
- Proclamation 11,002 (14 Jan 2026) invoked Section 232 and imposed a **25% ad valorem tariff effective 15 Jan 2026** on specified advanced computing chips and derivative products (White House proclamation; EY Tax News; Troutman). Critically, the initial tranche is *narrow* — aimed at AI accelerators — and industry commentary (Global Trade Alert, HKTDC, US business groups) flags a **Phase 2 expansion to broader semiconductors and downstream/derivative goods** as in motion.
- MCHP sells overwhelmingly mature-node MCUs/analog, largely *not* in tranche 1. But its products enter the US embedded in customer boards and derivative products, and its back-end sits in Malaysia/Thailand/Philippines — Malaysia is cited as having among the **highest proportional tariff risk** of any country under a broad semiconductor 232 action (CargoTrans). Microchip's own FY26 10-K added a **new risk factor** on supplier/equipment/material restrictions and rewrote its export-control/trade-sanctions risk factor (35% rewritten) and "policies implemented globally by current or future administrations" (42% rewritten).

**Risk 2 — China–Taiwan cross-strait disruption**
- **Probability: Low-Medium (10–20% for a disruptive event within 12–24 months) / Impact: Very High (existential to a 33% revenue block)**
- China + Taiwan = **~33–34% of net sales**. Taiwan is both an end market and a manufacturing/supply node (foundry and OSAT ecosystem). Regional backdrop deteriorated in the current period: China's MOFCOM publicly protested new US sanctions ahead of the Xi–Trump APEC meeting; Beijing has expanded extraterritorial legal reach (FT, Nov 2026) via the **Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law** and **Unreliable Entity List** (MERICS); Taiwan's Taiex posted its largest single-day drop in history during the Nov 2026 AI-driven selloff, prompting emergency market measures. MCHP's Taiwan exposure is customer-concentrated (Taiwanese ODM/EMS and distributor demand), so a blockade or quarantine scenario would hit orders well before it hits MCHP's own fabs.

**Risk 3 — Chinese retaliation via critical-minerals export controls and entity-list action**
- **Probability: Medium (35–45% of further restriction over the next 12 months) / Impact: Medium (supply-side, mitigable with inventory and qualification)**
- FY2026 10-K **new risk factor**: "We may lose sales if critical materials from concentrated sources become restricted or subject to export controls" — explicitly citing **rare earths from China and materials linked to Russia**. China imposed export restrictions on rare-earth/critical-mineral items in April 2025 and has since used minerals as leverage in the Busan trade truce (US pressing China to honor mineral commitments). Separately, a **new FY26 risk factor on Middle East instability** ties conflict to helium (Qatar >⅓ of global supply; Iranian strikes halted major facilities), bromine, fuel and shipping — an indirect but real input-cost channel.

**Watch item (not top-3):** AI data-center demand is now **16.7% of sales and the fastest-growing end market (+55%)** — and is the segment most exposed to chip export-control policy and to the AI capex cycle. Q2 FY27 (5 Nov 2026) revenue $1.622bn, +42.2% y/y, but guidance below consensus on supply constraints as AI buyers crowd out capacity (GlobeNewswire; Zacks; 24/7 Wall St). One analyst explicitly flags the data-center segment's "political and regulatory risks" (Seeking Alpha, 7 Nov 2026).

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Item 3 (Legal Proceedings):** Historically MCHP's disclosure is thin — the company states it is not party to material proceedings beyond ordinary-course matters. The recurring disclosed item is **employment litigation in France** brought by former Atmel employees against Microchip Technology Rousset / Atmel Switzerland Sàrl in a French regional court (disclosed across FY23–FY24 10-Qs).
- **ERISA class action (Schuman v. Microchip)** — Atmel-era severance dispute; **~$13M settlement agreed July 2026**, preliminary approval 3 Aug 2026, **final approval 28 Oct 2026** (~$9.5M+ in unpaid severance to ~220 former employees). Immaterial financially; a recurring governance/human-capital irritant given the 2024–25 restructuring.
- **Prior securities class action** settled for **$9M**, final approval 27 June 2022 (Wolf Popper; Kessler Topaz). No current securities fraud action identified.
- **Patent litigation:** routine, mostly defensive/PTAB. HD Silicon Solutions v. Microchip (N.D. Cal., 3:21-cv-08295, 7 patents, FPGA/processor IP) **closed 14 April 2025**. MCHP appears as an IPR petitioner against Bell Semiconductor patents. No mega-dollar patent exposure identified.
- **Tax disputes:** XBRL in the FY26 10-K references a **Malaysian Inland Revenue Board** matter and long-running unrecognized tax positions dating to 2018 — the most likely source of a future cash settlement. 10-K also newly emphasizes **OECD BEPS / Pillar Two** as a tax-rate risk (60% rewritten).
- **Regulatory regimes in scope:** export controls (EAR/BIS, incl. entity-list and end-use restrictions), OFAC sanctions, EU dual-use, **CMMC / US Department of War** requirements for government and aerospace-defense sales (risk factor rewritten to include *foreign* government customers), PFAS/ RoHS/REACH and EU CSRD-style disclosure, and AI-specific regulation (four AI risk factors, one new for FY26).
- **US entity/regulatory posture:** no FTC, DOJ, SEC or EU antitrust investigation of MCHP identified in this review.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

None **material** identified — no FCPA/AML/sanctions enforcement action, deferred-prosecution agreement, or whistleblower-driven DOJ inquiry surfaced. Items worth monitoring, not red flags:

1. **Dual-use / re-export hygiene** — with 18–19% China revenue, ~65% of net sales from products built at outside foundries, and back-end assembly in China/Malaysia/Philippines, the licensing and deemed-export surface is large. The FY26 10-K *rewrote* (35%) the export-control risk factor rather than leaving it boilerplate — a signal of elevated management attention.
2. **Distributor/reseller channel** — 47% of sales through distributors with Arrow a >10% customer raises third-party-diversion and end-use screening risk (China/HK redistribution).
3. **Data-privacy/AI governance** — four AI risk factors including a new one covering IP, confidential information, privacy and workforce; combined with a standing disclosure that MCHP "continues to be the target of attacks on our IT systems," this is the most likely future regulatory/litigation vector.
4. **France/Atmel employment matters** — repeated litigation by the same group of former employees suggests unresolved labour-relations and restructuring-compliance friction in the EU.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Rationale.** Legal/litigation risk alone is **Low** — no material pending claims, small settled matters ($13M ERISA, $9M securities), routine patent dockets. Regulatory and geopolitical risk is **High**: 75% foreign revenue, one-third of sales in the China–Taiwan theater, ~65% of production from outside foundries, back-end concentrated in tariff-exposed Malaysia/Thailand, and a US administration actively widening semiconductor tariffs and export controls. The offsetting mitigant is product mix — MCHP is a mature-node MCU/analog supplier, not an AI accelerator vendor, so it sits *outside* the current 25% Section 232 tranche and the most restrictive BIS rules. That insulation is the single most important thing to monitor: **any Phase 2 broadening of 232 to derivative/downstream products converts this rating to High.**

**Key falsifiers to track:** (i) BIS/Proclamation Phase 2 scope; (ii) China rare-earth licensing regime and any Unreliable Entity List action touching US semis distributors; (iii) cross-strait military/economic signaling; (iv) the Malaysian tax dispute outcome; (v) any 10-Q Item 1 disclosure expansion beyond the France/Atmel matter.
