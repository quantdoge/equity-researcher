# Sector Brief: Data Storage / NAND Flash — Sandisk (SNDK)

**Classification note:** FMP tags SNDK as "Technology / Computer Hardware," and its listed sector peers are a generic mega-cap tech index (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA) — analytically useless. SNDK is a **pure-play NAND flash** memory manufacturer. The correct comparable set is the memory & storage oligopoly: Micron (MU), SK hynix (SKHY), Western Digital (WDC — SNDK's former parent, spun off Feb 2025) and Seagate (STX).

---

## 1. Sector Overview

NAND flash is a ~$90–110bn global market dominated by five suppliers — Samsung (~30% share), SK hynix/Solidigm (~20%), Kioxia (~13%), Sandisk (~12–13%) and Micron (~11%) — with Chinese entrants (YMTC) marginal at the leading edge. Top-5 share of ~88–90% would ordinarily imply pricing discipline, but NAND remains a **commodity bit business**: all five are adding capacity simultaneously, so rivalry is capacity-led and brutally cyclical. The industry is currently at an **extraordinary cyclical peak** — Sandisk's gross margin went 7.1% (FY23) → 16.1% (FY24) → 30.1% (FY25) → **71.5% (FY26)** → **85.0% in the Oct-2026 quarter**, an all-time high by a wide margin and roughly 2.5x the prior cycle peak. The downturn has already begun: NAND spot prices are down ~20% since October 2026, 512Gb wafers are down 32% QoQ to $14.30, and TrendForce now forecasts contract prices declining from 1Q27 with widening quarterly drops through 2027.

**Sector structure:** High capital intensity (TrendForce pegs 2027 NAND industry capex at **$29.9bn, +26.7% YoY**); low threat of new entry at the leading edge (multi-billion fab economics, IP, and tool access) but rising Chinese state-backed entry; **high buyer power** concentrated in a handful of hyperscalers whose order decisions move price globally.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **AI inference is structurally bit-hungry.** Enterprise SSDs, not consumer, are the industry's growth engine, and Sandisk's data-centre revenue grew **+1,682% YoY** in the latest quarter. TrendForce notes US CSPs *raised* 2027 demand forecasts again on AI-agent and general-server build-outs, and AI workloads (KV-cache and checkpointing) expand NAND's role in inference infrastructure rather than merely displacing HDD.
- **Pricing power still present in reported numbers.** SNDK's Oct-2026 quarter: revenue **$11.03bn (+23% QoQ, +378% YoY)**, gross margin **85.0%**, operating margin **79.6%**, net income **$8.10bn** (73.4% net margin), diluted EPS **$6.58** in a single quarter. QLC penetration and higher layer counts are pulling $/bit down faster than cost, expanding capacity without new wafers.
- **An unusually capital-light model versus memory peers.** Because Sandisk sources wafers through the Kioxia joint ventures (Yokkaichi/Kitakami), quarterly D&A is only **$43m (0.4% of revenue)** and SG&A is **1.9% of revenue** — so incremental revenue converts ~85% to operating income. This is why, at 11,100 employees, SNDK out-earns WDC (40,000 staff) and STX (30,000 staff) combined on an operating basis.

---

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **A supply wave is arriving into a decelerating demand curve.** NAND bit supply is forecast to grow **25–30% YoY in 2027** with capex up 26.7%, while TrendForce has contract prices falling **0–5% QoQ in 1Q27** and "all product categories entering a downward cycle from 2Q27." This is a *price-led* correction — the worst kind for a commodity producer, because volume growth does not offset it.
- **Demand concentration has already cracked.** Chinese CSPs cut 2027 enterprise-SSD orders by an amount that **exceeds** the upward revision from US CSPs (TrendForce), citing GPU shortages, elevated inventory and token-price competition. On the consumer side, depletion of low-cost memory inventory forces OEMs to cut storage capacity per device — i.e. bit-demand destruction, not just price softness.
- **No DRAM/HBM hedge, maximum cyclical beta.** Micron, SK hynix and Samsung all cross-subsidise NAND with DRAM/HBM, which remains far tighter. Sandisk is 100% NAND. Its **beta of 5.65** and a **-75% drawdown from the 25 June 2026 close of $291.88 to $73.61** (25 Nov 2026) — despite *+148% YTD* — show the equity is pricing the turn well ahead of the P&L. New tariff threats are adding cost noise on top.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Average**

**Positives:** Best-in-class *reported* margins in the peer set; a genuinely differentiated cost structure via the Kioxia JV (asset-light relative to Samsung/Micron, no fab capex burden on the P&L); real, rapid traction in enterprise SSD (+1,682% data-centre growth); strong brand in removable/embedded flash; and a valuation with essentially no cycle premium embedded.

**Negatives:** Structural cost/scale disadvantage — at ~12–13% NAND bit share it is the smallest of the five leading-edge suppliers and a price-taker, whereas Samsung and SK hynix set price direction. Single-product exposure with **no DRAM or HBM to smooth the cycle**, which is precisely why beta is 5.65 versus 2.2–2.34 for WDC/STX. Wafer supply dependence on the Kioxia JV is a strategic lever held partly by another party. And critically, its 85% gross margin is not a moat — it is a cyclical artefact that will compress hard once 25–30% bit supply growth meets cut CSP orders.

Net: a well-run, high-quality *operator* in a structurally commoditised position. The franchise is average-to-good; the earnings are peak-cycle and should not be capitalised as normal.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **Neutral (bifurcated) — NAND sub-segment: Unattractive**

The data-storage sector is not one trade. The **HDD/nearline** sub-segment (WDC, STX) is genuinely **Attractive**: an oligopoly with no capacity wave, structural AI data-lake demand, and 45–49% gross margins being re-rated (7.9–8.9x sales). The **NAND** sub-segment — where SNDK sits — is **Unattractive on a 12-month view**: 2027 brings +25–30% bit supply against cut Chinese CSP orders, contract prices turning negative from 1Q27 with widening declines, and margins exiting an unprecedented 85% peak with nowhere to go but down.

**Implication for the memo:** SNDK's 4.7x P/E is not a bargain multiple on normalised earnings — it is the market correctly applying a trough multiple to peak EPS. The bull case rests entirely on AI inference demand outrunning the supply wave; the trend in spot pricing (−20% since October) and Chinese order cuts says it is not. **Sector cycle position: late-cycle/peak, post-inflection, in the first leg of a downcycle.**
