# Macro Assessment — SanDisk Corp. (SNDK)

*Data as of 25–26 Nov 2026. Fed funds target 3.75–4.00%; effective rate 3.88% (Oct). SNDK last close $73.61 (25 Nov), −59% from its $178.06 high on 21 Oct.*

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary: **Transitional → risk-off (policy-tightening regime)**

The US has flipped from the 2025 easing cycle into a **re-tightening, inflation-reacceleration regime**. The Fed cut from 4.33% to a 3.63% trough by mid-2026, then under Chair Kevin Warsh **hiked 25bp on 16 Sep 2026 to 3.75–4.00%**, with the September dot plot pointing to another hike and the December meeting pricing ~90–95% odds of a move (futures also discount a further hike in 2027). The September SEP raised the median end-2026 core PCE forecast to 3.4%.

Inflation is the binding constraint: **CPI +3.8% y/y in Oct 2026** (0.35% m/m, ~4.2% annualised) versus ~2.4% in late 2025, and **PPI +5.4% y/y through August**. Growth is *not* the problem — real GDP rose 4.3% q/q SAAR in Q3 2026 (+2.3% y/y), unemployment is 4.1%. This is a late-cycle "too-hot" macro: nominal demand is fine, but the discount rate is rising hard.

The bond market is the transmission channel that matters for SNDK. The 10Y sits at **5.13%** and the 30Y at **5.47%** — a **bear-steepening** curve (2s10s +39bp, 10Y +125bp over the effective funds rate), i.e. a term-premium/inflation-risk selloff, not a growth-scare inversion. Credit has begun to notice: **HY OAS widened from 2.63% (31 Aug) to 3.72% (24 Nov)**, though still tight in absolute terms and far from systemic stress. The equity event is an **AI-capex de-rating plus a NAND down-cycle**, not a macro credit event — but the macro backdrop gives it no cushion.

Dollar: the broad USD index is **123.6 (20 Nov), +4.7% since 10 Sep** and +3.0% since early June. Stronger dollar, higher long yields, and a hiking Fed is the classic tight-financial-conditions mix for a high-beta, USD-reporting exporter.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

| Macro variable | Impact | Mechanism for SNDK |
|---|---|---|
| Fed hiking / 10Y at 5.13% | **Negative** | SNDK is a long-duration asset: a pure-play cyclical whose valuation rests on *peak-cycle* earnings (FQ1'27: $11.03bn revenue, +378% y/y; 85.1% non-GAAP GM; $49.51 non-GAAP EPS). Rising discount rates compress the multiple applied to exactly this kind of earnings stream. Any easing expectation that supported the Sep–Oct melt-up (+209% YTD) has been withdrawn. |
| Inflation at 3.8%, PPI 5.4% | **Negative** | A memory maker has *procyclical* pricing power, not defensive pricing power: NAND contract prices were rising 15–20% earlier in 2026 (now cut to 8–13% by TrendForce) while fab input costs (power, substrates, logistics, tariffs) rise. Gross margin at 85% is a spread between two volatile prices — it mean-reverts violently in either direction. |
| Real GDP +2.3% y/y, AI capex | **Positive, decelerating** | Demand is AI-inference-driven (data-centre SSD/QLC), plus $88.9bn of remaining performance obligations and multi-year LTAs. But the marginal buyer, Chinese cloud, is cutting 2027 orders (TrendForce, 25 Nov) — an air pocket in the fastest-growing demand pool. |
| Stronger USD (+4.7% since Sep) | **Negative** | Revenue is USD-translated from Asian/European customers; a firmer dollar raises local-currency NAND prices for Chinese and European buyers, amplifying the demand slowdown. Partially offset: yen-denominated Kioxia JV fab costs become cheaper in USD. |
| Section 232 semiconductor tariffs (25% implemented via Proclamation 11,002, Jan-2026) + fresh memory-chip tariff threats (25 Nov 2026) | **Negative** | SNDK manufactures in Japan (Kioxia JVs: Yokkaichi/Kitakami), Malaysia and China and ships into the US. Tariffs raise landed cost, complicate customer allocation, and invite retaliation against a group already politically targeted by the House China committee for more export/trade protection. |
| Tight credit spreads but widening | **Neutral** | No refinancing stress in evidence: FQ1'27 generated $5.7bn adjusted FCF, so the macro risk here is **margin mean-reversion, not solvency**. But the +109bp HY widening is the early-warning signal for anyone underwriting the peak-cycle earnings base. |

**Net:** roughly 70% of the drawdown is industry/micro (NAND price inflection, Chinese order cuts), but macro is doing the damage *multiplier* work — a hawkish Fed at 5%+ long yields removes the valuation bid precisely when the earnings base becomes questionable.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE** (6–12 month horizon)

Justification: (i) policy rate rising into a demand-driven inflation problem with no growth offset; (ii) long-end yields at 5%+ steepening into term-premium territory; (iii) dollar at the top of its range; (iv) an active tariff/export-control regime aimed squarely at semiconductors and increasingly at memory; (v) a buyer base (Chinese cloud) that is macro-constrained by US export controls. The offsetting positive — a Fed that may only need one or two more hikes if goods inflation cools — is not sufficient to upgrade the rating near term.

## 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **Bear (stagflation pincer)**

**Scenario:** Q4-26/Q1-27 CPI prints stay above 3.5% because tariff pass-through into goods is stickier than modelled; the Fed hikes in December *and* again in March 2027 to 4.25–4.50%, and the 10Y breaks 5.75%. Simultaneously the NAND contract-price decline (currently guided at up to −5% for Q1 CY27) compounds to −20/30% as hyperscalers digest 2026 AI capex and Chinese cloud stays out. 85% gross margins revert toward the high-30s/low-40s — SNDK's $49.51 quarterly EPS is a peak, not a run-rate — and the "cheap" post-selloff multiple re-rates again on collapsing earnings.

**Asymmetry:** the bull surprise (Fed pauses in March, NAND demand for AI-inference storage re-accelerates, LTAs hold) is real but requires the long end to fall *first*. Rate direction is the gating variable for SNDK in a way it is not for most cyclicals.
