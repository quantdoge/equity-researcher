**Market cap:** ~$83.8B | **Employees:** ~11,100 | **HQ:** Milpitas, CA | **CEO & Chair:** David V. Goeckeler

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

Reported FY2025 (inaugural Sustainability Report):

| Metric | FY2025 |
|---|---|
| Scope 1 | 1,265 tCO2e |
| Scope 2 (market-based) | 82,058 tCO2e |
| **Scope 3** | **2,309,126 tCO2e** |
| Scope 1+2 intensity | ~8.8 tCO2e per $M revenue |
| Scope 3 share of footprint | **96.5%** |
| Target | Net zero Scope 1+2 by **2040** |

**Interpretation and risks:**
- **Scope 3 dominance is structural, not incidental.** As a JV/outsourced-wafer model, most carbon sits in purchased NAND wafers (Kioxia Yokkaichi/Kitakami) and capital equipment. SNDK controls neither directly — decarbonisation depends on JV partners and suppliers. This is a *disclosure and engagement* liability more than an operating cost today, but hyperscale customers (the source of its data-centre revenue surge) increasingly impose supplier Scope 3 and renewable-energy requirements in procurement. That is the channel through which this becomes a revenue risk.
- **Carbon intensity is flattering by arithmetic, not by decarbonisation.** FY2026 revenue is running at roughly a $44B annualised pace (Q1 FY26 revenue $11.0B) versus ~$9.5B in FY2025. On that denominator, Scope 1+2 intensity falls to ~5 tCO2e/$M and total footprint intensity to ~54 tCO2e/$M — while **absolute** emissions rise with volume. Any "intensity improvement" here is AI-cycle revenue inflation, not climate performance. Analysts should score absolute Scope 3, not intensity.
- **Water/chemical exposure is indirect** through the Japan JV fabs from which it sources wafers (Japan is comparatively water-secure; assembly/test in Malaysia and China carries more water-stress exposure). **Data gap:** I could not retrieve water withdrawal, renewable-electricity %, or waste-diversion figures — the FY2025 fact-sheet PDF was not machine-readable and the corporate-responsibility pages returned only navigation chrome. Treat environmental metrics as *self-reported, first-year, limited-assurance*.
- **No evidence of environmental enforcement, spills, or remediation liabilities** found in searches. No carbon-tax-covered installations of consequence (no EU ETS exposure); CBAM does not cover semiconductor HS codes directly.

**Rating: MEDIUM** — low absolute/owned footprint, but high Scope 3 concentration, an immature disclosure regime, and rising customer-driven supply-chain carbon requirements.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives:** 11,100 employees; engineered/technical workforce with high retention value in a NAND upcycle; responsible-minerals programme inherited from Western Digital (RMI/CMRT smelter due diligence, Form SD filer); published FY2025 Statement Against Modern Slavery and Human Trafficking.

**Negatives / watch items:**
- **Product safety & quality — the single most material S factor.** The 2023–24 SanDisk Extreme / Extreme Pro portable SSD failures (drives ejecting, becoming unreadable, permanent data loss) generated multiple class actions alleging the products were "worthless"; remediation came via firmware. This is a *consumer-trust* liability for a brand whose entire pricing power rests on data reliability — and it now matters more, not less, as the company sells into enterprise/data-centre accounts where a field failure is a qualification event. Financial impact of the litigation itself is small (likely low-eight figures, immaterial against $44B revenue run-rate); the tail risk is a repeat defect in a high-capacity enterprise SSD.
- **Restructuring (24 Aug 2026):** a significant workforce reduction was announced during a period of record demand — a classic social/reputational mismatch (cutting headcount while guiding to record revenue) that raises execution and employer-brand risk, and invites scrutiny of whether the cut targeted legacy consumer/retail functions.
- **Supply-chain labour:** tantalum, tin, tungsten, gold and cobalt inputs; China-based assembly implies **UFLPA** exposure and customer forced-labour due-diligence audits.
- **Data gaps:** no lost-time injury rate, turnover, gender/ethnic pay-gap, or engagement data disclosed at the level needed for scoring. Human-capital disclosure is thin.

**Rating: MEDIUM** (product-quality legacy + restructuring outweighed by a non-hazardous manufacturing profile and no evidence of systemic labour violations).

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (trending *improving* on disclosure, *stable* on substance)

SNDK is a **newly listed, single-year discloser with a genuinely strong board** but **immature ESG infrastructure** and a **product-quality scar**. It is not a Laggard on governance or emissions; it is not a Leader because (i) targets are long-dated and unlinked to pay, (ii) human-capital and water data are not disclosed to an investable standard, and (iii) the Scope 3-heavy footprint is out of its direct control. A plausible trajectory to "Average-to-Leader" exists by FY2027 if the second sustainability report adds assurance, water/injury metrics, and a Scope 3 supplier-engagement plan with hyperscaler alignment.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (no ESG-driven rating or cost-of-capital penalty; two monitoring triggers)

**For the thesis:**
1. **ESG is not the swing factor.** At ~$84B market cap on an AI/NAND-cycle re-rating, the valuation debate is pricing, supply (2027 oversupply risk), and China. ESG screens will not exclude SNDK: single-class shares, 87.5% independent board, 96% say-on-pay, no environmental enforcement, and a net-zero 2040 commitment keep it inside most mainstream ESG indices. Expected cost-of-capital impact: **nil to +10bp**.
2. **Two ESG factors are worth real monitoring weight** because they can become cash-flow events:
 - **Product integrity in enterprise/high-capacity SSD.** A repeat of the Extreme-series defect pattern in a data-centre SKU would hit qualification cycles and pricing, not just legal reserves. Evidence of tightening quality systems (and a clean field-reliability record through FY2027) would be a positive signal.
 - **Governance of the upcycle.** Chair/CEO duality plus a 200%-of-target short-term payout plus 200% PSU caps, in a memory cycle, is a recipe for peak-cycle capacity and inventory commitments that historically destroy value at the turn. Watch capex guidance and any capacity prepayments/floor-price contracts for signs the incentive structure is being gamed by the cycle; the ~$89–94B of remaining performance obligations with price floors is a genuine mitigant reducing this risk.
3. **Under-appreciated regulatory cost, not yet material:** EU ESPR/Ecodesign for consumer memory products and California SB 253/261 reporting. Combined, likely <$10M/yr — immaterial to earnings, but a design and packaging constraint on the consumer segment.

**Bottom line:** ESG neither supports nor undermines the investment case. Underwrite SNDK on cyclical and contract-durability grounds; use the governance and product-quality factors above as risk monitors rather than as valuation inputs.
