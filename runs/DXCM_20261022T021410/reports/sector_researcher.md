# Sector Brief: Continuous Glucose Monitoring (CGM) / Diabetes Technology

**Target:** DexCom, Inc. (DXCM) — Healthcare / Medical Devices
**Market cap:** $30.3B | **Price:** $80.26 | **Beta:** 1.48 | **Employees:** ~11,050

---

## 1. Sector Overview

CGM is a high-growth sub-segment of diabetes care within Medical Devices, sitting at the intersection of biosensors, consumables and digital health. The global CGM market is growing roughly **13–15% annually** (2023 volume +13.8%, value +13.0%), with the US market at ~$3.7B in 2024 projected to reach ~$8.4B by 2033 (~9.5% CAGR), and OTC/wellness glucose monitors adding a further ~7.6% CAGR layer ($12.4B → $19.4B by 2030). The market is **highly concentrated** — effectively a two-horse race between DexCom and Abbott (Libre), with Medtronic a distant third and Insulet/Tandem as ecosystem partners rather than sensor competitors — implying an HHI well above 3,000. Barriers to entry are substantial: sensor IP and manufacturing yield, iCGM regulatory classification, clinical-outcome evidence, payer/PBM contracting and insulin-pump integration. Porter's Five Forces skew unfavourable on rivalry and buyer power: **high rivalry** (Abbott prices Libre at roughly half DexCom's monthly cost), **high buyer power** (consolidated payers/CMS), moderate supplier power, low-moderate new-entry threat (capital and FDA barriers, though Apple/Google non-invasive R&D is a long-dated wildcard) and **complementary rather than substitutive GLP-1s**.

**Cycle position: mid-to-late growth.** The insulin-dependent US core is maturing (DexCom's growth decelerated from 27.1% in 2021 to 11.3% in 2024 before re-accelerating to 15.6% in 2025), while the non-insulin Type 2 and OTC/wellness vectors are early-stage. Input costs are improving (15-day sensor manufacturing yields lifted Q2 2026 gross margin ~400bps to 64.1%), but pricing power is shifting toward payers and low-cost competitor Abbott.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Non-insulin Type 2 expansion — the largest untapped TAM.** The G7 CONNECT study (published in NEJM) showed improved A1C and glucose control in non-insulin T2D users, corroborated by Reuters coverage that CGM benefits patients regardless of insulin dependence. With only a low-double-digit percentage of the ~38M US diabetes population using CGM and reimbursement broadening beyond insulin users, this is the sector's principal multi-year growth engine.
- **GLP-1 adoption is complementary, not cannibalistic.** The GLP-1 market exceeded $62.8B in 2025 and is projected toward $325B by 2035; users need glucose feedback to titrate therapy and measure response. DexCom reports clinical leadership specifically among GLP-1 users, converting the GLP-1 wave into a glucose-aware patient funnel rather than a demand destroyer.
- **Cost-curve reset and channel expansion.** The G7 15-Day sensor cut cost per patient-day, enabling **~70% incremental operating margins**; the non-prescription Stelo biosensor opens a cash-pay wellness channel, and international penetration remains well below US levels — a structural volume runway at improving unit economics.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Abbott's price-led share assault and ecosystem lock-in.** Libre 3/3 Plus retails at roughly half DexCom's monthly cost, and Abbott is embedding itself in automated insulin delivery via a global partnership with Medtronic and pump integrations — threatening DexCom's historical AID sensor franchise (Insulet remains a key ally, but the pump channel is contestable).
- **Payer/PBM reimbursement and ASP compression.** Consolidated buyer power, CMS contracting and OTC channel mix-shift pressure realised pricing; DexCom's gross margin already fell from **68.6% (2021) to 60.1% (2025)** before the 2026 recovery. Tariff and policy risk adds cost uncertainty.
- **Growth-deceleration and perception risk.** US insulin-dependent penetration is high, growth has historically been volatile (2023 +24.5%, 2024 +11.3%), and shares are range-bound ($54.11–$92.59 52-week) — including a 6.4% drop on 21 Oct 2026 — reflecting sensitivity to any reimbursement or GLP-1 headline. Medtronic (Guardian 4/Simplera) and long-dated non-invasive sensing from big tech cap the multiple.

## 5. DexCom's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

**Supporting evidence:** DexCom is one of two scaled CGM franchises globally, with the deepest clinical-evidence base in the category (NEJM-published CONNECT data), best-in-class sensor accuracy, and entrenched AID integration (Insulet Omnipod 5). It converts that position into **19.6% operating margins — above every listed peer** — and **19.3% five-year revenue CAGR, roughly double the peer median**, on a $4.66B base still compounding at 15.6%. The 15-day sensor transition demonstrates pricing-led cost discipline rather than volume-led erosion.

**Caveats:** Gross margin at 60.1% is only at peer median and materially below Abbott/Insulet, and DexCom carries a structural cost disadvantage against Libre's low-price model. The Abbott–Medtronic AID partnership and Tandem's Libre integration contest DexCom's pump channel. Verdict remains **Strong**, but the moat is narrowing at the low-price end even as it widens in clinical evidence and non-insulin T2D.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

Mid-teens structural growth, a concentrated two-player structure, high regulatory/manufacturing barriers and a long adoption runway in non-insulin Type 2 and OTC wellness all support an Attractive rating. The tempering factors are real — payer-driven ASP compression, Abbott's price leadership, AID-channel contestability and GLP-1 headline risk — which argue for selectivity within the sector rather than blanket exposure. **DexCom is a quality-growth way to own the theme**, best suited to investors comfortable with reimbursement-policy volatility and a gross-margin recovery story that depends on manufacturing yield and mix rather than pricing power.
