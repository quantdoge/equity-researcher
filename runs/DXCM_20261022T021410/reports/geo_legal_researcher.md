## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1: US–China Trade Tensions & Medical Device Tariffs
- **Probability:** High
- **Impact:** Medium–High
- **Analysis:** The US has raised tariffs on medical devices and components, sending shockwaves through global MedTech supply chains. While DexCom’s primary manufacturing is in the US (Mesa, AZ; San Diego, CA) and Malaysia (Penang), the broader medical device ecosystem relies on global component sourcing. Escalating trade tensions could increase input costs, disrupt supply chains, and invite retaliatory measures affecting international revenue. [Source: Medical Buyer, MassDevice, Congress.gov]

### Risk 2: Regulatory & Reimbursement Pressure in Core Markets (US & EU)
- **Probability:** High
- **Impact:** High
- **Analysis:** The FDA issued a Warning Letter to DexCom in March 2025 following inspections of facilities in Arizona and California. The letter cites failure to keep records for inspection (including complaint documentation) and alleges unauthorized changes to glucose sensors. While DexCom disputes the claims and expects no material financial impact, this signals heightened regulatory scrutiny. Simultaneously, European markets (UK, Spain, etc.) operate under strict MDR rules and national pricing controls that could cap growth. [Source: Investors.com, Yahoo Finance, LinkedIn]

### Risk 3: Supply Chain Concentration in Malaysia
- **Probability:** Medium
- **Impact:** High
- **Analysis:** DexCom’s first manufacturing facility outside the US is located in Batu Kawan Industrial Park, Penang, Malaysia. This facility is critical for international expansion (serving the UK, Europe, and Asia). Malaysia is generally stable, but its position in the global electronics/medical device supply chain makes it vulnerable to US-China decoupling, semiconductor shortages, and potential tariffs on Malaysian goods. Any disruption here would directly hit international revenue growth. [Source: MyNext, Bernama, AmCham Malaysia]

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

### A. Securities Class Action Litigation (High Severity)
- **Status:** Active. Multiple law firms (Gross Law Firm, Kessler Topaz, BFA Law) have filed class actions.
- **Class Period:** July 26, 2024 to September 17, 2025 (with another tranche covering January 8, 2024 to July 25, 2024).
- **Allegations:** Defendants issued materially false/misleading statements and concealed that DexCom had made **material design changes to its G6 and G7 glucose monitoring products that were unauthorized by the FDA**. The lawsuits allege misrepresentation of the safety and accuracy of the G7 device. Stock price plummeted over 40% (per ZLK) following disappointing financial results and disclosure of these issues.
- **Lead Plaintiff Deadline:** December 26/29, 2025.
- **Impact:** Significant potential liability; reputational damage; management distraction. [Source: GlobeNewswire, PRNewswire, ZLK, 11th.com]

### B. FDA Warning Letter (March 2025) (High Severity)
- **Details:** Issued following inspections of US facilities (Arizona and California). Cites failure to maintain adequate records for inspection, including documentation of user complaints, and alleges unauthorized device changes. DexCom publicly pushed back on the claims.
- **Impact:** While the company states no material impact on 2025 guidance, a Warning Letter is a serious regulatory escalation. It can lead to stricter oversight, require costly remediation, and potentially restrict product approvals or imports until resolved. [Source: Investors.com, Yahoo Finance]

### C. Product Liability & Consumer Class Actions (Medium–High Severity)
- **Details:** Lawsuits allege G7 sensor failures, signal loss, and app malfunctions (including lack of audible alarms for hypoglycemia). The FDA has issued a correction for G7 and ONE+ apps that fail to alert users when sensors fail, potentially causing delayed treatment.
- **Impact:** Direct patient harm allegations create significant liability exposure. Motley Rice and other firms are investigating. [Source: Yahoo News, Motley Rice, FDA.gov]

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **Quality System Regulation (QSR) / Design Control:** The FDA Warning Letter’s focus on "unauthorized design changes" and record-keeping failures is a major red flag. It suggests potential systemic weaknesses in design control and quality management, which could trigger further inspections or enforcement.
- **Data Privacy (HIPAA / GDPR):** As a digital health company managing continuous glucose data and cloud-connected apps, DexCom is a high-value target for data privacy regulators. No active investigation was found in this search, but this remains a latent high-risk area given the sensitivity of health data.
- **FCPA / AML:** No active investigations or red flags identified in public sources.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating

# **HIGH**

**Rationale:** DexCom faces a **High** legal and regulatory risk profile. The combination of an active FDA Warning Letter for manufacturing/design control failures and multiple securities class actions alleging unauthorized design changes and safety misrepresentations creates material contingent liability. Product liability suits related to G7 sensor failures add further risk. While the geographic revenue base is concentrated in the relatively stable US and European markets (limiting pure geopolitical risk), the supply chain concentration in Malaysia and exposure to US-China trade tensions introduce moderate operational and geopolitical risk. The overall risk is elevated due to the severity and active nature of the regulatory and legal proceedings.
