# Dexcom (DXCM) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $80.26 | Market cap ~$32.5B | EV ~$31.9B | Sector: Healthcare / Medical Devices**
*(Data as of the Oct 21, 2026 close; next catalyst Oct 29, 2026)*

## 1. Growth Story
Dexcom is the technology leader in continuous glucose monitoring (CGM), a category that is converting the world's ~600M+ diabetes population from episodic fingersticks to always-on, real-time glucose data. The bull case is no longer just insulin-dependent diabetes share gains — it is a **platform expansion**: OTC wellness (Stelo, >$100M revenue in its first 12 months), non-insulin Type 2 diabetes, hospital/inpatient monitoring, and CGM as a companion to GLP-1 therapy. The 2026 story is a **margin-inflection plus new-TAM** story: gross margin bottomed at 60.1% in FY2025 and has recovered to ~63% in 1H26 while operating margin expanded to 24% — evidence that the Dexcom ONE/Stelo mix drag is being absorbed by manufacturing leverage. The debate is whether decelerating growth (Q3'26E +9.9% YoY) reflects temporary competitive noise or structural share loss to Abbott.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity
**Core CGM market:** ~$6.3B in 2023, projected ~$13.1B by 2032 (~8.4% CAGR) — but estimates vary widely and several are stale. The **broader diabetes-devices pool is ~$38.6B in 2026 → ~$87B by 2036**, and CGM is taking share of that wallet.

**Penetration math is the real upside:** Only ~10–15% of insulin-dependent people with diabetes in developed markets use CGM, and **less than 2% of the ~30M+ US non-insulin Type 2 diabetes population** is penetrated. Add ~96M US adults with prediabetes (a pure cash-pay/wellness funnel now addressable via Stelo) and inpatient hospital glucose monitoring. On this basis the serviceable market is **$20B+ by 2030**, roughly 4x Dexcom's current revenue base — which is why FY2030 consensus of only $7.9B looks conservative rather than aggressive.

**Share position:** Dexcom holds an estimated ~35–40% of global CGM revenue against Abbott's Libre franchise (~40%+), with Medtronic and Senseonics trailing. Dexcom's moat is **sensor accuracy (MARD leadership), the G7 15-day wear extension, and the Real-Time API ecosystem** that embeds its data into insulin pumps (Tandem, Insulet), digital health apps, and now GLP-1 companion use cases — a genuine data/network effect that a cheaper sensor alone cannot replicate. **Stelo is the key call option**: an OTC, cash-pay product that creates a consumer-health channel outside the reimbursement system entirely.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts
1. **Q3 2026 earnings — Oct 29, 2026 (~8 days).** Consensus $1.329B revenue (+9.9% YoY) and $0.675 EPS. Given four straight beats and a July guidance raise to $5.18–5.25B, a beat-and-narrow is the base case. This is a binary, near-dated event.
2. **G7 15-day wear US FDA clearance.** Already approved in Health Canada; US submission pending. Longer wear cuts cost-of-goods per patient-day and neutralizes Abbott's 15-day Libre 3 advantage — directly additive to gross margin.
3. **Stelo reimbursement and payer expansion** (Medicare Advantage / commercial coverage for non-insulin T2D) plus international OTC launches — the mechanism by which the wellness TAM converts to recurring revenue.
4. **GLP-1 companion positioning.** As GLP-1 adoption scales, CGM becomes the monitoring layer; any formal partnership or payer bundling would re-rate the growth narrative.
5. **Hospital/inpatient CGM commercialization** — a high-ASP, high-barrier channel where Dexcom has early positioning.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (moderate-to-high conviction)
**Why not Strong Buy:** the growth rate has fallen to ~12% and is guided to ~10% in 2H26; Rule of 40 sits just under 40; and at **30x FY2026E EPS / 20x EV/EBITDA / 6.2x sales**, the multiple still discounts a mid-teens grower. PEG of ~2.5 is not cheap. Abbott's scale and pricing power are a real, persistent threat, and the FY2030 revenue path ($7.9B, ~9% CAGR) is underwhelming for a "disruptive innovation" premium.

**Why Buy anyway:** (a) the margin inflection is confirmed and under-appreciated — gross margin back above 63% and operating margin at 24% mean EPS growth (~25–28% in 2026–27) runs well ahead of revenue growth; (b) the beat-and-raise cadence is intact and estimate revisions are positive; (c) Stelo and non-insulin T2D are genuine new-TAM options not in the base case; (d) the stock is 13% off its high and 8–25% below recent analyst targets ($87–$115, latest $100). Asymmetric setup into the Oct 29 print: a beat likely retests $90–95; a miss likely retests $70.

**Fair-value frame:** 26x FY2027E EPS of $3.13 = ~$81 (current price); 30x = ~$94; 22x EV/EBITDA on FY2027E = ~$100. So the stock is fairly valued on a growth-adjusted basis with ~15–20% upside if the margin story holds. Not a bargain — a quality compounder with optionality.

## 7. Key Risks
1. **Abbott competition (highest risk).** Libre 3/Lingo undercuts on price with massive manufacturing scale and a 15-day wear sensor; Lingo directly targets the OTC wellness channel Stelo pioneered. US share loss is the bear case behind the Q3'26 deceleration to ~10%.
2. **Pricing and reimbursement pressure.** Gross margin fell 850bps in four years from geographic/product mix; DME competitive bidding and any CMS cut would compress further. The 63% GM recovery is not yet proven durable.
3. **Growth deceleration / multiple compression.** At 30x forward earnings, a cut to FY2027 guidance or a sub-10% growth year would likely de-rate the stock to the low-20s multiple (~$65–70).
4. **Execution and quality.** $53M inventory write-downs, replacement costs, and commercial leadership turnover (CCO departure) are evidence of operational fragility in a device business where reliability is the brand.
5. **GLP-1 cannibalization.** If GLP-1 drugs delay insulin initiation and reduce severe diabetes progression, the high-ASP insulin-dependent CGM pool grows more slowly — the offsetting wellness/non-insulin demand must materialize to compensate.
6. **Small-sample estimates.** FY2029–2030 consensus rests on only 2–4 analysts; the long-dated revenue and EPS figures carry wide error bars and should not be treated as reliable.
