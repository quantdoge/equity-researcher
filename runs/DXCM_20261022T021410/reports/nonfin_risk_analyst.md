## 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (with caveats)

**Evidence supporting adequacy:**
- **Activist-driven refresh is real and recent.** Following engagement with Elliott Investment Management, DexCom committed to adding two independent directors and restructuring a board committee. The board was expanded to **13 directors** on September 10, 2026, with **Glenn S. Boehnlein** appointed as an independent director (term expiring at the 2027 annual meeting, two committee seats, ~$500k equity grant). Board refresh under external pressure is a net positive versus entrenchment.
- **Planned CEO succession executed.** Jake Leach (former President/COO) succeeded Kevin Sayer on January 1, 2026, after a publicly announced transition — an orderly handoff, not a forced exit. Leach is an internal promote with operating credibility.
- **No evidence of related-party or accounting-related governance failure** in available filings; EDGAR full-text search returned no SEC/DOJ enforcement actions against DXCM.

**Caveats / weaknesses:**
- **Executive Chair is not independent.** Sayer moved from CEO to **Executive Chair**, meaning the board is chaired by the prior CEO while a new CEO runs the company. This is a "duality-adjacent" structure that dilutes board independence and can create a shadow-CEO dynamic during the first 12–18 months of a transition. It also means shareholders have not yet seen a genuinely independent chair.
- **13-member board is large** and was expanded partly under activist pressure — watch for committee fragmentation and the risk that the Elliott settlement is cosmetic (seats without strategy change). Boardroom Alpha commentary has flagged director discontent and committee chair concentration in post-settlement boards generally.
- **Compensation appears heavily variable and under-disclosed.** Reported total compensation for Leach is ~$7.99M, of which **~90% is bonus/incentive-based** (per third-party aggregator; not verified against the proxy). A structure this levered to short-term incentive metrics raises pay-for-performance and earnings-management alignment questions, particularly given the FY2024 guidance cut and the securities class action.
- **Ongoing activist presence.** Elliott's stake means governance is now partly externally directed; further demands (buybacks, cost cuts, portfolio action) are a live possibility.

**Verdict:** Better than the pre-2025 status quo, but not yet "Strong." Independent chair and clean proxy disclosure are the two things that would upgrade this to Strong.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: HIGH

**a) Open FDA Warning Letter — the single largest regulatory overhang.**
- FDA issued a **Warning Letter to DexCom (~March 2025)** following inspections at **two manufacturing sites** (mid-2024 and late-2024), citing non-conformities in manufacturing processes and quality management systems, and deficiencies in the company's responses to prior observations. Public reporting indicates FDA was **not satisfied with corrective actions** at the time of the letter.
- **No close-out letter has been confirmed** in available sources. Until FDA verifies remediation (typically a re-inspection and close-out), this remains an open enforcement matter that can escalate to injunctive relief, consent decree, or facility certification consequences. Management has publicly expressed confidence in corrective actions; that is a management assertion, not a regulatory fact.

**b) Class 1 / high-severity device recalls — recurring.**
- **G7 receiver Class 1 recall** (speaker malfunction that could prevent alerts — a patient-safety-critical failure mode).
- **G6 receiver recalls across 19 models** for the same speaker defect.
- **Multiple 2026 recall events** appear in FDA's recall database (e.g., Z-2574-2026, Z-0318-2026), and MAUDE adverse-event reports reference G7 failures. Recall frequency at this cadence is a quality-system signal, not just an isolated event.

**c) Securities class action (investor litigation).**
- Class action filed on behalf of investors for the period **July 26, 2024 – September 17, 2025**, alleging misrepresentation of **G7 safety/accuracy and undisclosed manufacturing/quality issues**. Lead-plaintiff deadline was **December 26, 2025**; status as of October 2026 is **not confirmed** (likely in motion-to-dismiss or discovery phase). This is a legal/reputational risk that keeps the quality narrative in the public record.

**d) Patent/IP litigation history (Abbott).**
- DexCom and Abbott have litigated CGM patents across US and UK courts for years; a **settlement of outstanding patent disputes was reported** (Bloomberg Law), though a US docket (Abbott Diabetes Care v. DexCom, 1:23-cv-00239) shows activity into 2026. IP risk is structural in this duopoly and should be treated as ongoing, not resolved.

**e) EDGAR full-text enforcement search: zero results** for DXCM. No disclosed SEC civil actions, DOJ investigations, or FCPA matters. Absence of evidence here is mildly reassuring but not proof of absence — warning letters and recalls do not appear in EDGAR.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Manufacturing quality and regulatory remediation execution.** The open FDA Warning Letter covering two sites is the dominant operational risk. Remediation is capital- and management-intensive, and failure to close out invites escalated FDA action (consent decree, injunctive relief), which would constrain supply and reimbursement. The G7 15-day sensor transition lowered manufacturing costs (Q2 2026 gross margin +400bps to 64.1%) — but a cost-driven ramp across newly configured lines is precisely when quality escapes happen. **Quality and margin expansion are pulling in opposite directions right now.**

2. **Product reliability at the patient-safety level.** Class 1 recalls on G7/G6 receivers for speaker malfunctions — which can silence hypoglycemia alerts — plus reported sensor failures, accuracy complaints, and connectivity problems. In CGM, an alert failure is not a warranty issue; it is an adverse-event and litigation issue. The MAUDE record and the securities class action both center on this.

3. **Concentrated duopoly competition and reimbursement/pricing pressure.** Abbott (FreeStyle Libre) is a lower-cost, well-capitalized rival; Medtronic is re-entering with integrated pump/CGM systems; GLP-1 adoption is reshaping diabetes treatment economics. U.S. revenue growth (+11%) trails international, and coverage expansion for non-insulin Type 2 depends on payer decisions. A single large payer or Medicare/Medicaid coverage change would hit revenue concentration directly. **Supplier concentration** (single-source components for sensors/electronics) is also unquantified in available data — a gap.

*Not assessed due to data gaps:* cybersecurity incident history and business-continuity posture. DexCom operates a connected cloud platform (Dexcom Share/CLARITY, Real-Time API) and is subject to HIPAA and GDPR; this should be flagged as an unassessed exposure, not a clean bill of health.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: MODERATE-HIGH

**Negative drivers:**
- **Patient-safety narrative.** Recall + warning letter + class action is a compound story: "the market leader in CGM has a quality-control problem." Social/community channels (diabetes forums, Facebook groups) show active user campaigns urging patients to report G7 failures to FDA — organic, grassroots pressure that precedes formal recall expansion. That is an early-warning indicator of further recalls.
- **Securities fraud allegation** keeps a "management credibility" discount in place. The class period overlaps the FY2024 guidance cut and the November 2025 gap-down (Oct 30 close $68.20 → Oct 31 close $58.22, −14.6% on 26.5M shares), a 5–6x normal volume event that indicates a genuine credibility shock among institutional holders.
- **Activist involvement itself** signals that governance/performance was perceived as sub-par; Elliott's presence is a reputational marker even as it is a corrective.

**Offsetting positives:**
- **Strong clinical evidence:** G7 CONNECT study results in non-insulin Type 2 diabetes, published in *NEJM* and covered by Reuters, materially strengthen the franchise narrative. GLP-1-user data is a differentiated asset.
- **Sell-side support:** consensus "Moderate Buy" (21 buy / 2 hold / 1 sell of 26 covering analysts) and institutional accumulation in Q2–Q3 2026 (HWG Holdings new 96k-share stake ~$8.3M; American Capital 235k shares ~$15.8M; Aurora $2.34M) — institutional buying has continued *through* the recall/warning-letter news flow. That is a meaningful counter-signal to reputational damage becoming valuation damage.
- **No consumer boycott, ESG controversy, or executive misconduct scandal** identified. Reputational risk here is clinical/regulatory, not social-license.

**Verdict:** The brand is not damaged in the eyes of institutional capital, but it is exposed to a clinical-quality narrative that could turn quickly if a serious adverse event or a second warning letter occurs. Watch diabetes-community sentiment as the leading indicator.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Three independent, simultaneously live risk vectors — (i) an **open, unresolved FDA Warning Letter** across two manufacturing sites, (ii) **recurring Class 1 recalls** in a patient-safety-critical product, and (iii) a **pending securities fraud class action** — combined with a governance structure that is still mid-transition (new CEO, non-independent Executive Chair, activist-driven board expansion) and **one-sided insider selling with zero purchases over 12 months**.

Offsetting these to a degree: zero SEC/DOJ enforcement hits in EDGAR full-text search; documented corrective-action progress (margin expansion, G7 15-day ramp); orderly CEO succession; genuine board refresh; and continued institutional accumulation. The trajectory is **improving**, but the risk is not yet contained. If FDA issues a close-out letter and the securities action is dismissed, this rating should migrate to **Moderate**.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **FDA Warning Letter without confirmed close-out (highest priority).** Two-site QMS non-conformities with FDA dissatisfaction over corrective actions. Escalate to the investment committee as an **existential-supply-chain risk**: a consent decree would cap growth and could trigger reimbursement exclusion. *Action: request the FY2025 10-K "Regulatory" MD&A and any 8-K disclosure on inspection status; verify whether a close-out letter has been received.*
2. **Class 1 recalls on a safety-alert device, recurring across G6 and G7.** Speaker failure on a hypoglycemia alarm is a foreseeable-harm defect. Any fatality or severe hypoglycemia event tied to a missed alert converts this from a product-quality issue to a product-liability and DOJ matter.
3. **Securities class action alleging G7 safety/accuracy misrepresentation (class period Jul 2024–Sep 2025).** Credibility overhang; status unconfirmed. Escalate for legal reserve/d&o insurance review.
4. **Zero insider purchases across 12 months despite a 40% drawdown and a subsequent 70% rebound.** Net sales ~$17.1M/90 days. Executive Chair (ex-CEO) selling under 10b5-1 while chairing the board is a governance signal to monitor.
5. **Non-independent Executive Chair alongside a first-year CEO.** Verify the board's lead-independent-director designation and whether the Elliott committee restructuring produced genuine oversight or simply added seats. Verify compensation metrics and whether FY2026 incentive targets were reset after the FY2024 guidance cut.
6. **Abbott patent litigation not fully resolved** (docket active into 2026 despite reported settlement). IP injunctive risk in the core CGM franchise.
7. **Data gaps to close before publication:** 10-K/10-Q/DEF 14A text unavailable via tool; cybersecurity incident history, supplier concentration, and payer-concentration disclosures unverified. These are **unassessed**, not clean.
