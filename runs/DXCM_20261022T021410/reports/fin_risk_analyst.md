**Sector:** Healthcare / Medical–Devices (Continuous Glucose Monitoring) · **Market cap:** ~$30.3B (profile, at $80.26) / ~$28–29B at 2026-02-20 close of $73.08 · **Employees:** 11,050 · **FY end:** Dec-31

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE (implied)**

DXCM is, on every balance-sheet measure available, a net-cash, high-coverage, self-funding borrower. FY2025 total debt of $1,389.7M against $1,998.7M of cash and short-term investments produces a **net cash position of −$609M** (leverage tool), i.e. **net debt/EBITDA of −0.45x**. Debt/equity is 0.51x and debt/assets 0.22x. Interest coverage on operating income is **49.8x** ($911.8M / $18.3M); on EBIT including other income it is ~60x.

Notably, DXCM **delevered hard in FY2025**: total debt fell from $2,587.4M to $1,389.7M and short-term debt from $1,204.4M to **zero**. That ~$1.2B reduction is consistent with retirement of the convertible notes that had been reclassified current — i.e. the refinancing cliff that existed at FY2024 year-end has already been resolved in cash. The remaining ~$1.24B is long-term. The very low interest expense ($18.3M on ~$1.39B, ~1.3% implied average cost) indicates the debt is largely low-coupon convertible paper rather than straight senior notes.

*Data gap:* No public issuer credit rating (Moody's/S&P) could be confirmed from available sources. The "Investment Grade" designation here is **inferred** from balance-sheet metrics, not from a published rating. Similarly, CDS spreads and the size of any revolving credit facility are unavailable — the revolver is an unquantified liquidity backstop.

**The beta conflict matters.** The computed 365-day beta of 0.63 sits alongside a profile beta of 1.48. The resolution is that DXCM's largest drawdowns were **idiosyncratic, not market-driven**: **−40.7% in a single session** on 2024-07-26 ($107.85 → $64.00) on a guidance reset, and **−14.6% on 2025-10-31** ($68.20 → $58.22). **Valuation risk:** at $73.08 / FY2025 diluted EPS of $2.09, the multiple is ~35x. A 35x multiple on decelerating growth and compressing gross margin leaves little room for error; the July 2024 episode demonstrated how fast the multiple can compress.

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue −20%

**Base case FY2025:** Revenue $4,662M · gross margin 60.1% · operating income $911.8M (19.6%) · EBITDA $1,358.5M · FCF $1,077M.

**Stress case (−20% revenue = $3,730M), assuming gross margin compresses to ~57.5% on fixed-cost under-absorption and opex cut only 8–10% (R&D is not easily reducible in med-tech):**

| Line item | Base | Stress | Delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 4,662 | 3,730 | −932 |
| Gross profit @ 57.5% | 2,802 | 2,145 | −657 |
| R&D + SG&A (cut ~10%/8%) | 1,890 | 1,727 | −163 |
| **Operating income** | 912 | **~418** | **−494 (−54%)** |
| Operating margin | 19.6% | **~11.2%** | −840bp |
| Interest expense | 18.3 | 18.3 | flat |
| **Interest coverage** | 49.8x | **~22.8x** | Still IG-comfortable |
| EBITDA (ex-other income) | ~1,107 | ~670 | −40% |
| Net debt/EBITDA | −0.45x | ~+0.7x | Still <1x |
| FCF (est., ~12–14% margin) | 1,077 | **~450–520** | −52% |

**Conclusion:** a 20% revenue decline is **absorbable without covenant breach, liquidity strain, or credit deterioration**. Coverage stays above 20x, leverage stays under 1x, FCF stays positive and comfortably funds ~$360M of capex. Liquidity remains ~$2.0B cash plus ~$1.9B working capital. The real damage in this scenario is **equity-side, not credit-side**: operating margin halves and the ~35x multiple would almost certainly compress toward the low-20s, implying a further 40–50% drawdown on top of the 48% already suffered — consistent with the July 2024 tape.

**More severe variant (−20% revenue AND gross margin to 55%):** operating income ~$325M, coverage ~17.8x, EBITDA ~$580M, net debt/EBITDA ~0.8x. Still no credit event. DXCM would have to lose roughly **half its revenue** before net leverage approached 3x — the credit is genuinely resilient.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

The **balance sheet and cash-generative profile are Low risk** — net cash, 49.8x coverage, F-Score 8, Z-score ~7.3 on market equity, no maturity wall, $2.0B liquidity, quadrupling FCF. What pushes the composite to Moderate is the **combination of (a) 39% volatility with repeated double-digit single-day gap events, (b) a two-year 60% drawdown, and (c) a deteriorating gross-margin trajectory that threatens the operating leverage the model depends on**. This is not a solvency story; it is a franchise-durability and expectations-risk story. Credit risk: **Low**. Liquidity risk: **Low**. Market/equity risk: **High**. Business-model/competitive risk: **Moderate-to-High**.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Gross margin erosion — 460bp in three years** (64.7% → 60.1%). The Piotroski F-Score's only failure (F8) is precisely this. Mix shift toward lower-priced channels (Stelo OTC, Dexcom ONE, G7 international) plus competitive pricing from Abbott Libre and Medtronic is structurally dilutive. If this continues at ~150bp/yr, the FY2025 operating-margin record (19.6%) is the peak, not a base.
2. **Growth deceleration with a reset history.** +24.5% → +11.3% → +15.6%. The FY2024 trough coincided with the July 2024 guidance shock and −41% one-day move. Revenue trajectory is no longer the reliable compounding story the multiple assumes.
3. **Earnings quality dependence on non-operating income.** EBIT ($1,106.7M) exceeds operating income ($911.8M) by ~$195M — 17.6% of EBIT comes from interest income on the cash pile and other non-operating items. Falling short-term rates erode this automatically, and it is not repeatable operating profit.
4. **Aggressive capital returns vs. shrinking cash cushion.** $1,939M of buybacks over 2022–25 (~87% of cumulative FCF) plus $1.2B of debt retirement cut cash from $2,724M to $1,999M. If FCF disappoints while buybacks continue, the net-cash cushion thins quickly.
5. **Extreme event/gap risk.** −40.7% in one session (2024-07-26) and −14.6% (2025-10-31). A low 0.63 computed beta disguises this; the 1.48 long-run beta in the company profile is arguably the more honest risk number for drawdown planning. 39.4% annualised vol and a −60.8% three-year peak-to-trough drawdown are not consistent with a "defensive healthcare" risk budget.
6. **Valuation fragility.** ~35x FY2025 diluted EPS on decelerating growth and compressing margins — the multiple itself is the largest single risk factor in a downside scenario.
7. **Minor disclosures/data gaps:** total debt exceeds reported long-term debt by ~$149M (likely leases) with no breakdown; no confirmed public credit rating; no revolver size disclosed; Sortino unavailable; balance-sheet `net_debt` ($472M) and the leverage tool's `net_debt` (−$609M) use different cash definitions — I have used the total-debt-less-cash figure throughout. Company-profile price ($80.26) is stale versus the 2026-02-20 close ($73.08); the FCF-yield tool's market-cap input ($32.5B) also differs from the profile ($30.3B), so the quoted 3.31% yield is modestly understated.

*Gap acknowledged:* the US Treasury yield-curve feed failed twice (tool error), so no macro rate-environment overlay is included; the peer set returned by the tool is large-cap pharmaceutical rather than CGM/device comparables (Abbott, Medtronic, Tandem), so no relative-multiple benchmarking is offered.

---

### Bottom line
DXCM's **credit is not the risk** — a net-cash balance sheet, ~50x interest coverage, no near-term maturities and $1.08B of FCF make a covenant breach or refinancing failure remote even under a severe revenue shock. The **risk sits in the equity**: a 35x multiple on decelerating growth and a 460bp gross-margin slide, punctuated by repeated 15–40% single-day gaps. Underwrite this as a **high-volatility, event-risk equity with an investment-grade balance sheet** — position sizing and drawdown tolerance, not solvency analysis, are the binding constraints.
