## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH**
- **Carbon intensity:** Extremely high in absolute and per-revenue terms; Scope 3 (purchased livestock) is the overwhelming majority. Company's own net-zero-by-2050 ambition lacked a credible Scope 3 pathway — 70% of Scope 3 traced to livestock.
- **Greenwashing liability crystallised:** EWG/ALDF/Earthjustice suit (D.C. Superior Court, Sept 2024) **settled Nov 17, 2025** — Tyson agreed to **stop making "net-zero" and "climate-smart beef" claims and is barred from new related claims for five years** absent expert substantiation. This removes a marketing/margin lever on value-added beef and invites follow-on state AG action (cf. NY AG v. JBS USA).
- **Water:** UCS (Apr 2024) documented **371M lbs of pollutants discharged** to US waterways over five years, with Nebraska (Dakota City) most affected. Violation Tracker: **~$35.8M in water-pollution penalties since 2000**, including prior criminal CWA pleas.
- **Transition risk is deferred, not absent:** US agriculture faces no carbon price; the near-term E risk is *litigation and customer specification*, not tax. EUDR/CSDDD bite only on EU-destined volumes.
**Verdict: High** (tail-risk driven, not run-rate cost driven).

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**
- **Worker safety:** Meatpacking remains among the most hazardous US industries. Industry injury rate has fallen to ~4.0 (BLS all-time low) and Tyson reports ~22% YoY reductions, **but** EPI analysis of OSHA severe-injury data found Tyson reported **~6x the severe injuries of Ford despite only 33% more workers**. Investigative reporting alleges in-house clinics under-record injuries — i.e., reported metrics may understate risk.
- **Child labour (most acute):** 2023 DOL investigations into Tyson and Perdue; sanitation contractor PSSI paid **$1.5M** for minors as young as 13 in plants; **May 2025 — Senator Hawley opened a Senate investigation** and urged DOL action following a whistleblower allegation at a Tyson plant. Elevated headline/reputational risk; criminal-liability tail.
- **Immigration/worksite enforcement:** Industry-wide 2025–26 enforcement actions create operational disruption risk in a 124k-person, high-turnover, immigrant-heavy workforce.
- **Wage & hour:** Violation Tracker shows **~$73.9M** in wage-and-hour penalties since 2000 — the largest single penalty category, indicating a persistent compliance pattern rather than isolated error.
- Mitigants: 20+ year UFCW safety partnership; unionised footprint improves grievance channels.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10**
- **Dual-class control:** Class B carries **10 votes/share**; the Tyson family holds **~22% economic / ~72% voting power** (up from 71.7% via buybacks that shrink Class A float). A shareholder proposal to eliminate the structure won **>88% support** — the board has not implemented it. This is the defining governance defect: minority holders cannot effect change.
- **Board independence:** Chairman **John Tyson** (founder's grandson, former CEO) is not independent; combined with family voting control, the chair is effectively unaccountable to public holders.
- **Succession process:** **Jeff Schomburger** — a director since 2016 — became President & CEO effective **Oct 4, 2026**, succeeding Donnie King (43-year veteran, remains on board). An insider-to-insider handoff with no external search is a classic entrenchment signal, though it preserves strategic continuity.
- **Compensation:** FY25 CEO pay **$34.5M, +51% YoY** from $22.8M — in a year preceding a **$654M Beef operating loss**. Pay-for-performance optics are poor; say-on-pay nonetheless passed with **~95–98% support**, suggesting institutional investors are reluctant to publicly oppose the family. Compensation metrics are largely EPS/EBIT/TSR — **no ESG or safety modifier disclosed**.
- **Positives:** Board refresh announced Nov 19, 2026; 2026 proxy includes proposals on class-by-class voting disclosure; no poison pill evident; say-on-pay frequency annual.
- **Score rationale:** 3/10 reflects structural minority disenfranchisement + insider succession + adverse compensation optics, partly offset by a functioning (if captured) proxy process.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure
- **US (dominant, ~85–90% of sales):** Low *carbon-cost* exposure (ag excluded from pricing schemes). Real exposure is **enforcement**: DOJ Antitrust (criminal probe), DOL/OSHA, EPA CWA, state AG consumer-protection (the D.C. CPPA vehicle used successfully by EWG is replicable).
- **California SB 253/261:** Applies (>$1B revenue, doing business in CA) — requires Scope 1/2/3 assurance and climate-risk reporting. Tyson must publish Scope 3 it has limited ability to control. **Medium** cost, **high** disclosure-risk given the greenwashing history.
- **SEC climate rule:** stayed/effectively withdrawn — relief, but no shield from state AGs.
- **EU — EUDR:** Cattle products in scope; Tyson's **own SEC exempt solicitation flagged fines up to 4% of EU turnover** for non-compliance. Traceability to plot level for Brazilian-origin beef is operationally hard. **EU CSDDD/CSRD** apply via EU turnover thresholds; EU is a small revenue share, so exposure is **medium**, concentrated in export/compliance cost not revenue loss.
- **CBAM:** Indirect — fertiliser/feed input cost pass-through only. **Low.**
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** None verified in this review — **gap**.
- **Immigration/IRCA worksite enforcement:** Elevated 2025–26 policy risk.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD**
Tyson combines (a) an intrinsically high-footprint, hard-to-decarbonise Scope 3 model, (b) a documented pattern of antitrust, wage-and-hour, water and child-labour violations, (c) a settled greenwashing finding that has legally gagged its core climate marketing, and (d) a 72%-voting-control family structure that neutralises minority ESG pressure despite 88% shareholder support for reform. Peer context: the closest true comparables — **JBS, Pilgrim's Pride (PPC), Hormel (HRL), Smithfield (SFD)** — are not meat specialists in this sector feed (returned WMT/COST/PG are not comparable); directionally, JBS carries similar or worse controversy load, while Hormel and Pilgrim's screen better on governance. *Peer multiples/scores unavailable — a genuine gap in this analysis.*

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL, with a NEGATIVE governance tilt**
- **Not a thesis-breaker:** No near-term carbon price, no binding ESG-linked debt, EU exposure small. The FY27 recovery narrative (adj. operating income $1.70–2.15B vs. FY26 Beef loss of $654M; Prepared Foods at 12.6% margins) is driven by the cattle cycle, not ESG.
- **Where ESG does bite the thesis:**
 1. **DOJ criminal antitrust probe is the live tail risk** — unquantifiable, uncapped, and the third leg of a >$267M settlement pattern. Assign a discount for litigation overhang.
 2. **Governance caps any re-rating.** With 72% family control and a chairman who is a former CEO, public holders cannot force capital discipline; the counter-cyclical buyback that raised family voting power from 71.7% is value-destructive for minorities in a beef downcycle.
 3. **Child-labour and safety exposure is systematically underpriced** by sell-side models — it lands as customer specification (WMT/COST audits), Senate headlines, and potential criminal referral, not as a line item.
 4. **ESG-labelled fund exclusion** is a persistent, low-grade demand headwind; TSN is screened out of most sustainability indices, narrowing the marginal buyer base.
- **Actionable:** No ESG-driven position change, but **haircut the terminal multiple / raise the litigation provision** for the DOJ probe, and treat the 5-year climate-marketing ban as evidence that management's forward guidance on "value-added beef" carries promotional risk. Escalate governance engagement (dual-class, independent chair, safety/ESG modifier in the incentive plan) as the highest-return ESG lever — 88% support already exists.
