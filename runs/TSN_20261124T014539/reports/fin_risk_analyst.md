**F-Score detail (5/9):** Positives — positive ROA, positive operating cash flow, accruals quality (OCF $2.43B > net income $453M, i.e. earnings are cash-backed), lower leverage, asset turnover improvement. **Liquidity detail:** Current ratio 1.50x looks adequate until you strip inventory. ## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue −20%

**The critical structural fact: with a 1.95% operating margin and a ~25% contribution margin on lost revenue, an approximately 8% revenue decline eliminates all EBIT.** Tyson has no earnings buffer.

| Scenario | Revenue | EBITDA | EBIT | EBIT/Int. | Net debt/EBITDA | FCF | Dividend covered? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base FY26A | 56,419 | 2,473 | 1,075 | 2.65x | 2.80x | 1,663 | 2.36x |
| −5% rev | 53,598 | 1,768 | 370 | 0.91x | 3.92x | 958 | 1.36x |
| −10% rev | 50,777 | 1,062 | −336 | **negative** | 6.52x | 252 | **0.36x** |
| **−20% rev** | **45,135** | **651** | **−1,746** | **negative** | **10.64x** | **−651** | **No** |

*(Assumes 25% contribution margin on lost revenue, interest held at $406M, capex held at $770M. Working-capital release is ignored — conservatively, since a volume decline would actually free cash.)*

**At −20% revenue:**
- EBITDA falls to ~$651M; net leverage explodes to **~10.6x** — deep sub-investment-grade territory
- EBIT of −$1,746M means **interest coverage is negative**; the company cannot service debt from operations
- FCF turns negative ~$650M; cash of $824M is consumed in ~15 months even after suspending the $704M dividend and $414M buyback
- **A covenant breach is highly likely.** Typical IG food-company maintenance covenants include interest coverage floors of 2.5–3.0x; TSN would breach at roughly a 5% revenue decline. **Data gap: the actual credit-agreement covenant package is not in my toolset — this is the key unverified assumption in the scenario.**
- Rating outcome: multi-notch downgrade to BB territory. Fitch's BBB is not survivable at 10x leverage.
- The $14.9B goodwill balance would almost certainly be impaired, cutting book equity by up to 84% and further damaging the rating.

**More probable downside (and the one I weight most heavily) — the Q4 run-rate persisting.** FY2026 quarterly progression shows a violent deterioration:

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Q4 gross margin collapsed 290bp sequentially to 3.7%; EBIT of $24M covers interest of $107M **0.22 times**; the quarter produced a net loss. Annualising Q4 EBITDA ($367M × 4 = $1,468M) against $6,926M net debt gives **4.7x leverage** — a two-notch deterioration from the reported 2.80x, on *flat* revenue. This is not a stress case; it is the exit run-rate.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE** *(deteriorating — one notch from High)*

The balance sheet is not the problem: $7.75B of debt against $35.1B of assets, 0.44x debt/equity, $2.0B repaid in two years, 50.5% equity/assets, positive and improving FCF, and affirmed investment-grade ratings. Solvency risk over a 12-month horizon is low.

The problem is the **income statement**, and it is compounding into a balance-sheet problem. A 1.95% operating margin, a 4.38% EBITDA margin, and a Q4 exit rate at 0.22x interest coverage leave a company that is one modest volume shock away from covenant pressure. Tyson's credit quality is now a function of the beef cycle, not of its capital structure — and the capital structure has already been optimised.

**I would assign High, not Moderate, if the Q4 FY2026 gross margin of 3.7% is confirmed as a new run-rate rather than a seasonal trough.** Beef seasonality is real, but a 290bp sequential compression is beyond normal seasonal pattern.
