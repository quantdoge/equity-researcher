### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US labor supply / immigration enforcement disruption — Probability: High | Impact: Medium-High**
2026 saw an escalation of ICE operations across Kansas, Texas and Oklahoma beef plants, with reports of workers staying home and cattle shipment delays (Reuters, 9/25/2026; AP; Insurance Journal 9/28/2026). A Haitian worker's arrest at Tyson's Temperanceville, VA poultry plant reportedly spread fear through the immigrant workforce. Meatpacking is structurally dependent on foreign-born labor; Tyson's ~124–133k team members are concentrated in these roles. This is Tyson's most acute *operational-geopolitical* exposure — it hits throughput and overtime cost directly, and is unhedgeable.
- Add-on: **child-labor scrutiny** — DOL investigation opened 2023, Senate Finance (Cassidy) document requests 2024 and a renewed 2025 Senate probe (Reuters, 5/6/2025). Tail risk of consent decrees, fines, and reputational damage with retail customers.

**② Trade policy whiplash — beef import liberalization, retaliation and the USMCA review — Probability: High | Impact: Medium**
- The administration removed/increased quota for **tariff-free lean beef imports (300,000 MT, incl. +80,000 MT Argentina, plus Brazil and Paraguay)** in Aug–Sep 2026 (White House proclamation; Forbes 9/5/2026; The Hill). This directly compresses Tyson's beef spread — it lowers Tyson's input cost on trimmings but also imports competing boxed beef and undercuts domestic cattle values, while Tyson's own beef segment is already at a **–$654M adjusted loss**.
- **USMCA six-year joint review began July 1, 2026** — Mexico and Canada are Tyson's largest foreign protein markets. Any erosion of ag market access, or Mexican retaliation, hits Pork/Beef volumes.
- **Mexico's Jan 1, 2026 tariff regime** on non-FTA countries (5–50%) raises costs/risk for Tyson's Asian supply chains and its Mexico operations (White & Case; Alvarez & Marsal).
- China retains retaliatory tariffs on most US agricultural goods (CRS R45903), capping Tyson's highest-value beef export channel and adding political risk to its China chicken/import business (FY26 included a "China plant relocation remuneration" item).

**③ Antitrust and consumer-protection litigation overhang — Probability: High (incremental settlements) | Impact: Medium**
Tyson has recognized **legal contingency accruals as reductions to Sales of $358M in FY26 and $698M in FY25** — i.e., litigation is now a recurring, revenue-line item, not a one-off. Details in §3. The structural driver (protein price-fixing plaintiffs' bar + DOJ interest in information sharing, cf. *US v. Agri Stats* proposed final judgment, June 2026) is not going away.

*Watch item:* disease/animal-health trade restrictions (HPAI, ASF, and New World screwworm-related cattle import constraints) are a live swing factor on US cattle cost — I did not verify 2026 status within budget; flag for follow-up.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Antitrust (multi-front, largely settling):**
- **Broiler chicken:** ~$99M consumer class settlement; total poultry price-fixing payouts reported at ~$221.5M; final court approval obtained.
- **Beef:** $82.5M direct-purchaser settlement (final approval **Nov 12, 2026**) plus **$55M** consumer indirect settlement (with Cargill's $32.5M = $87.5M); separate commercial/indirect beef settlement of $47M.
- **Pork:** **$85M** — described as the largest pork price-fixing settlement (Reuters, 10/1/2025); final approval hearing **Dec 11, 2026**; ~$117M total across eight producers; a further ~$64M foodservice/direct-pork settlement with two other producers.
- **Wage-fixing:** Tyson is among meatpackers covered by ~$208M in court-approved wage-fixing settlements (Meat+Poultry).
- **Ongoing exposure:** remaining classes/opt-outs and non-settling co-defendants (JBS, Cargill, National Beef, Sanderson, Perdue) keep discovery and copycat risk alive.

**Consumer / ESG / securities:**
- **EWG lawsuit** alleging false environmental and sustainability marketing claims (greenwashing) — a growing category for branded protein.
- **Securities plaintiffs' firms** publicly probing Tyson disclosures around its FY2026 outlook (WATT/Poultry International, 2026) — a stock-drop suit risk if beef guidance slips again.

**Regulatory:**
- USDA/FSIS inspection and labeling; FDA on additives; state-level mandates (e.g., California Proposition 12) raising pork/chicken cost of compliance.
- Environmental/water permits and Clean Water Act exposure at processing sites; EU-facing sustainability and deforestation-linked disclosure obligations for European operations.
- **Legacy FCPA:** 2011 SEC action (bribes to Mexican veterinary officials; ~$4.5M disgorgement + $1.2M penalty) and a 2017 SEC subpoena that was closed without action. No current FCPA/OFAC matter disclosed — but the 2011 precedent means Mexico/Asia agent and customs-intermediary payments are a permanent compliance focus.
- Collective bargaining: a portion of US team members are under CBAs — strike risk at individual plants.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Immigration/I-9 and child-labor compliance** — the single highest-probability enforcement vector (DOL, Senate, ICE). Recommend tracking E-Verify/ICE audit disclosures in 10-Qs.
2. **Recurring litigation accruals booked against *Sales*** ($358M FY26 / $698M FY25) — unusual presentation; check whether FY27 guidance assumes further accruals.
3. **Antitrust information-sharing hygiene** — post-*Agri Stats*, any Agri Stats participation or benchmarking of margins/capacity with peers is a live DOJ/FTC theory.
4. **Greenwashing/marketing claims** — ESG claims in brand marketing are now litigable; low dollar risk, high reputational/retail risk.
5. **China/Asia subsidiary governance** — plant relocation and government interactions in China warrant FCPA/books-and-records scrutiny; no OFAC/sanctions exposure identified (no Russia/Iran/Burma operations disclosed).
6. **Customer concentration (Walmart 19%)** — not a compliance item, but a de facto regulatory lever via retailer standards (animal welfare, antibiotics, labor audits).

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** ~86% of revenue is US-generated under strong rule of law with negligible expropriation or currency-control risk, and the foreign footprint (~13.5%) is diversified across ~140 countries with no Russia/sanctions-belt concentration and no disclosed OFAC exposure. That anchors the rating at Medium rather than High.

**What keeps it from Low:** (a) an entrenched, still-settling antitrust docket that has converted into a recurring P&L charge; (b) acute US labor-enforcement exposure that is operational, not just financial; (c) trade policy that now cuts *against* Tyson's beef economics (tariff-free lean beef import expansion) while the beef segment runs a –$654M adjusted loss; and (d) a 2026 USMCA review that puts Mexico/Canada market access on the table.

**Upgrade triggers:** resolution of pork/beef settlements with no new classes; easing of ICE activity; cattle cycle turn in FY27 (guide: beef –$300M to +$100M).
**Downgrade triggers:** a DOL consent decree or ICE-driven plant closure; USMCA review producing ag retaliation; a securities class action tied to the FY26 outlook reset; new DOJ antitrust action against Tyson directly.
