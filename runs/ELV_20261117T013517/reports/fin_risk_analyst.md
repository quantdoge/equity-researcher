# Elevance Health Inc. (ELV) — Financial Risk Assessment

**Data as of:** FY2025 (period end 2025-12-31, filed 2026-02-06); quarterly data through Q3 2026 (filed 2026-10-21). Price $446.28, market cap ~$96.4–99.0B.

---

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: INVESTMENT GRADE (lower tier) — but with deteriorating trajectory.**

- **S&P: 'A-', downgraded from 'A' on 30-Oct-2025** — explicitly attributed to *"a lower capital adequacy assessment"* at the regulated insurance subsidiaries, not to holding-company leverage. Outlook **Stable**.
- **Fitch: 'BBB+', affirmed 11-Dec-2025** (senior unsecured).
- FY2025 10-K confirms insurance subsidiary claims-paying/financial strength ratings of **"AA"** as of 31-Dec-2025 — a wide (5–6 notch) gap between operating-company strength and holding-company debt, i.e., structural subordination is a live risk.
- AM Best: financial leverage at Elevance rose to **~42%**, expected to decline slightly.

The downgrade trigger was **regulatory capital adequacy, not solvency**. That distinction matters: ELV can be IG-clean on leverage ratios while still being downgraded again if subsidiary capital doesn't rebuild — and the parent's access to subsidiary dividends is the binding constraint on both debt service and buybacks.

**Gross leverage is not an outlier vs. plan peers (FY2025):**

| | Net debt/EBITDA | Interest coverage | Debt/assets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ELV** | **2.46x** | **5.79x** | **0.27** |
| UNH | 2.18x | 4.74x | 0.25 |
| CI | 2.02x | 6.61x | 0.20 |
| HUM | 3.00x | 2.30x | 0.27 |

ELV sits mid-pack. Its coverage is second-best in the group and *better than UNH's*. This is the strongest argument against a distress call. **The problem is direction of travel, not the level.**

## 3. Market Risk Profile

| Metric | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Annualised volatility (3Y) | **35.5%** | Extreme for a large-cap managed-care name |
| Beta vs. SPY (3Y regression) | **0.194** | Profile beta (5Y) = 0.711 — see note |
| Max drawdown (3Y) | **−47.9%** | Peak-to-trough |
| Sharpe ratio | **−0.005** | Effectively zero risk-adjusted return over 3 years |

**Interpretation — this is the critical market-risk finding:** with β ≈ 0.19 and σ ≈ 35.5%, implied R² is roughly **1%**. Essentially *none* of ELV's volatility is explained by the market. The stock is a **pure single-name/idiosyncratic risk**, currently driven by policy and medical-cost headlines (Medicaid redeterminations, ACA subsidy expiry, MLR trends). An investor cannot hedge this with index shorts, and a low headline beta here is dangerously misleading — it reflects a negative-alpha stock drifting on its own news flow, not defensiveness. The 0.194 vs. 0.711 discrepancy between the 3Y regression beta and the vendor's long-run beta is itself a red flag of structural regime change.

A −47.9% drawdown with a ~zero Sharpe over three years, in a business with 4% operating margins, is the market pricing **permanent impairment risk to the earnings base**, not a cyclical wobble.

## 4. Downside Scenario: −20% Revenue

Applied to FY2025 base (revenue $199,125M; COGS/claims $148,223M; EBIT $8,112M; D&A $1,546M; interest $1,402M). Two calibrations:

**A. "Medical costs flex, overhead does not" (realistic managed shock).** Claims scale at the 74.4% variable rate; 60% of admin variable, 40% fixed.
- Revenue **$159,300M**; EBIT **~$2,880M** (−64%)
- Interest coverage **~2.05x** (from 5.79x)
- EBITDA ~$4,430M → **net debt/EBITDA ~5.4x**
- Net income ~$1,150M, EPS ~$5.2; FCF ~$1,580M, FCF yield ~1.6%
- **Capital returns must be fully suspended** to avoid net debt rising

**B. "Fully variable cost base" (naive accounting stress).** EBIT falls to **−$2.1B**. Interest coverage **0.0x**; covenant breach, dividend suspension, and equity issuance become live questions. With tangible equity of just **$4.5B** against $33.2B of debt, a stress of this magnitude would likely force a **goodwill impairment** (a 12% write-down of the $39.5B balance erases reported equity), triggering a multi-notch downgrade toward BBB−/BB+.

**Conclusion:** a 20% top-line shock takes a lower-tier IG credit to **leveraged, sub-IG** territory (5x+) even under the *benign* calibration. The 4.07% operating margin provides almost no shock absorption — a 1-point margin decline is a 25% EBIT hit.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Justified by: (a) leverage (2.5–2.7x) and coverage (5.1–5.8x) that are at or better than plan-peer medians; (b) no refinancing cliff — $9.5B cash against $1.4B of short-term debt, current ratio 1.24x, positive OCF; (c) an A−/BBB+ IG rating with a Stable outlook. Tempered by three-year FCF deterioration of 56%, negative margin momentum through 2026, and a downgrade already recorded.

I would move this to **HIGH** if any of the following appear: net debt/EBITDA > 3.0x, interest coverage < 4.5x, FCF < $2.5B, or a second downgrade.

**Positives the bear case must contend with:** FY2026 is described by management as the **trough year** (Medicaid margin −1.75% vs. −0.5% in 2025), with a **12% EPS growth target for 2027**; 9M-2026 EBIT of $6,806M was only −9.6% YoY, and Q3-2026 EBIT actually rose **+22.5% YoY** ($2,152M vs. $1,757M) — corroborating a turn. Balance sheet debt is flat ($32.7B → $33.2B) and net debt *fell* $0.7B in FY2025.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **FCF down 56% since FY2022** ($7.25B → $3.17B); conversion 0.56x of earnings; $3.3B working-capital outflow — the clearest sign of claims/reserve stress.
2. **Buybacks + dividends = 130% of FCF** while earnings fall — balance-sheet-funded, not self-funded.
3. **S&P downgrade to A− (Oct-2025) on *capital adequacy***, a regulatory-capital constraint at the subsidiaries that the leverage ratios do not capture. Risk of a second downgrade if capital doesn't rebuild.
4. **Interest expense +65% since FY2022 while EBIT fell 4%** — coverage decayed from 9.9x to ~5.1x TTM.
5. **Margin collapse in Q4-2025**: EBIT $581M on $49.7B revenue = **1.17% operating margin**, with a 93.5% medical loss ratio. Medicaid margin to −1.75% in 2026.
6. **Goodwill of $39.5B = 90% of equity**; tangible equity only $4.5B (debt/tangible equity 7.4x). Impairment risk is the transmission channel from an earnings shock to an equity/ratings shock.
7. **Idiosyncratic risk concentration**: β 0.19 with 35.5% vol and −47.9% max drawdown — no index hedge available, no risk-adjusted return over 3 years.
