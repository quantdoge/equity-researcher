# Sector Brief — Managed Care / Health Benefits (US)
**Target: Elevance Health (ELV)** | Sector: Healthcare → Medical – Healthcare Plans | Data as of FY2025 (filed Feb-2026), market data current

---

## 1. Sector Overview

US managed care is a ~$1.7T-revenue oligopoly (our seven-name peer set) sitting inside a ~$5T national health expenditure base growing ~5% annually on demographics alone. The industry converts premium dollars into medical claims under statutory MLR floors (80% commercial / 85% Medicaid-MA), which structurally caps underwriting returns and makes *scale in administration* and *non-regulated diversification* (PBM, care delivery, analytics) the only durable sources of excess margin. The sector is currently in a pronounced **margin trough within a structurally growing industry**: FY2025 revenue growth was double-digit across the peer set (median +11.8%), yet operating margins collapsed everywhere as utilisation normalised above pricing. Critically, ELV is the *least damaged* of the large-cap names — its operating margin fell 238bp since FY2021 versus 409bp at UNH and 297bp at HUM.

Concentration is high: the top-3 names (UNH, CVS, CI) hold **66% of peer revenue** and 74% of peer market cap; revenue-HHI across the seven is ~1,860 (DOJ "concentrated"). Barriers to entry are very high — state licensure, reserve/capital requirements, provider-network contracting, and regulatory approval — but they protect *incumbency*, not *pricing power*, since buyers are sophisticated (CMS, state Medicaid agencies, Fortune 500 benefits teams).

**Porter's Five Forces:** Rivalry **high** (oligopoly but bid-market pricing); Buyer power **high** (CMS/states/large employers); Supplier power **high and rising** (hospital and physician consolidation, specialty drug pricing, GLP-1s); New entrants **low**; Substitutes **moderate** (ASO/self-insurance, ICHRA, direct-to-employer).

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Reimbursement catch-up is now contractual, not hoped-for.** CMS raised CY2026 MA payments **+5.06% (>$25B)** and the CY2027 Rate Announcement carries a further **~+$26B** increase. State Medicaid rate increases are flowing with the typical 12–18 month lag. ELV has explicitly guided to **2027 margin recovery** and **≥12% adjusted EPS growth** off a raised 2026 baseline of **≥$26.75**.
- **Structural demand growth is non-cyclical.** Medicare Advantage now covers >50% of eligible beneficiaries (~34M), Medicaid managed care covers ~70M+ lives, and exchange enrolment sits near records. Insurers compound premium revenue regardless of the underwriting cycle — the peer set has grown revenue at an **11.4% median 5-year CAGR**.
- **Cost trend is peaking, which resets the pricing spread.** PwC projects commercial medical cost trend at **9% for 2027** — the highest in nearly two decades — but that is being fully priced into 2027 rate filings *and* the 2026–27 utilisation base is a peak, not a plateau. Any deceleration in trend drops straight to margin given fixed-rate contracts.
- **Vertical integration converts regulated premium into fee-based earnings.** ELV's Carelon (PBM/care delivery), UNH's Optum, and CI's Evernorth all earn non-MLR-capped income — a structural escape valve from the MLR ceiling.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Medical loss ratio shock is sector-wide and still unresolved.** Operating margins FY2021→FY2025: UNH 8.33%→4.24%, CVS 4.56%→1.16%, HUM 4.09%→1.12%, MOH 3.67%→1.72%, **CNC 1.42%→−3.91%**. ELV 6.45%→4.07%. Utilisation, acuity mix and provider rate increases are outrunning premium yields.
- **Regulatory and rate-setting risk is asymmetric to the downside.** CMS risk-adjustment model changes (V28), a stated CMS posture of *holding MA rates flat* in real terms, PBM-reform legislation, prior-authorisation rules, and Medicaid directed-payment scrutiny. MLR rebate floors (~$759M industry-wide in 2026) mean outperformance is partly refunded.
- **ACA exchange instability.** Expiry of enhanced subsidies drives premium spikes and adverse risk-pool selection; 2027 rate filings are running well above trend to compensate, which suppresses enrolment.
- **State Medicaid budget pressure.** States face budget gaps, are re-basing capitation rates and scrutinising supplemental payments — the segment where CNC and MOH have been most damaged.
- **Supplier power.** Hospital/physician consolidation and specialty drug inflation (including GLP-1s) structurally shift value from payer to provider.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

**Rationale:**
1. **Best-in-class underwriting discipline through a sector-wide margin shock.** ELV's −238bp operating-margin decline over four years compares with −409bp at UNH, −340bp at CVS and −297bp at HUM. Management protected margin where peers did not.
2. **Scale plus franchise moat.** ~$199B revenue, ~46M members, and the largest Blue Cross Blue Shield-affiliated portfolio (14 states) — a brand and provider-contracting asset that cannot be replicated. It is #4 by peer revenue but #2–3 by covered lives in commercial and MA, because CVS/CI revenue is inflated by low-margin pharmacy pass-through.
3. **Diversified book reduces single-policy exposure.** Balanced Commercial/ASO (~40%), Medicaid (~30%) and Medicare Advantage (~20%), plus Carelon providing non-MLR-capped, fee-based growth. This is precisely the mix that let ELV sidestep the MA-only carnage at HUM and the Medicaid-only carnage at CNC.
4. **Earnings recovery is already contracted, not speculative.** Medicaid rate increases plus the CY2026/27 MA rate increases underpin guided ≥12% 2027 adjusted EPS growth — and ELV enters that recovery at 16.7x forward, versus peers whose multiples already discount normalisation.

**Where it is weaker:** no Optum-equivalent scale services engine to subsidise the insurance segment the way UNH can; Medicaid exposure leaves it hostage to state budget cycles; and 5-year revenue CAGR (10.3%) trails the peer median (11.4%), reflecting slower MA share gains than UNH or MOH.

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL** (with a positive inflection bias into FY2027)

**Justification:** Demand is structurally growing and non-cyclical, entry barriers are formidable, and the sector is at a **cycle trough in margins with visible, policy-driven recovery** — MA rates +5.06% (2026) and ~+$26B (2027), plus Medicaid rate catch-up. That argues against "Unattractive."

But it is not yet "Attractive": returns are MLR-capped by statute, buyer power is concentrated in CMS and state legislatures, medical cost trend is running at a two-decade high (9% commercial for 2027), and the 2027 recovery is *priced by regulators, not earned by competitive advantage*. Sector ROE is being reset lower structurally.

**Sector cycle position:** Late-cycle trough / early recovery. Revenue growth remains robust while margins bottom — the classic setup where **relative margin durability, not growth, is the differentiator.** On that axis ELV screens as the highest-quality large-cap exposure in the group, trading at the low end of its own historical multiple.

**What would change the rating:** Upgrade to Attractive on evidence of MLR stabilisation for two consecutive quarters plus a favourable final CY2027 MA rate; downgrade to Unattractive if ACA subsidy lapse drives adverse selection and commercial trend breaks above 10%.
