# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Elevance Health, Inc. (NYSE: ELV)

**Snapshot:** Healthcare / Managed Care · Market cap ≈$96.4B · Price $446.28 · CEO Gail Koziara Boudreaux · ~96,600 employees · ~118M members served. Data as of mid-November 2026 (latest filings: 10-Q 10/21/26, 8-K 11/10/26).

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate** (trending positive on structure, strained on disclosure/conduct)

**Evidence supporting:**
- **Chair/CEO separation maintained** — Ramiro G. Peru serves as Independent Chair; the 2026 proxy explicitly states there is no Lead Director *because* an independent chair exists. This removes the single most common concentration-of-power flag.
- **Active board refreshment** — Amy Schulman (healthcare executive/investor) added as independent director effective 1/12/2026; Steve Collis (global healthcare) effective 8/1/2026; Deanna Strable appointed to Audit and Governance committees. Fresh Audit/Governance talent is a genuine positive.
- **No SEC enforcement actions** returned against ELV in EDGAR full-text search — a clean civil-enforcement record.
- **AM Best affirmed** indicative "bbb+/bbb/bbb-" on the new shelf registration, outlook stable — no counterparty-confidence signal of governance distress.
- 89.2% institutional ownership; standard shelf registration filed (capacity, not a red flag in itself).

**Evidence weighing against:**
- **Conduct risk at the top of the Medicare segment.** DOJ alleges Elevance announced the departure of Peter Haytaian — the executive who oversaw Medicare Advantage — *immediately after* DOJ first sought to depose him, and is now refusing to produce him (STAT, 4/30/26). Whether or not the timing was coincidental, the optics and the litigation posture are a board-oversight question, not merely a legal one.
- **Compensation/guidance framing** leans heavily on "adjusted" metrics while GAAP and operating results diverge (see §7). Aggressive non-GAAP framing is a soft governance flag.
- Two senior transitions inside 12 months (MA leadership exit; new Carelon President Jeff Park effective 12/1/26).

**Gap:** The 10-K and DEF 14A could not be retrieved through the filing tool (only 10-Q/8-K/Form 4 metadata available); board-independence ratios, related-party transactions, and exact comp metrics were **not independently verified** and are inferred from the proxy excerpt and press releases.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — **Elevated / the dominant risk in this name**

**Active and material:**

| Matter | Status | Exposure |
|---|---|---|
| **DOJ False Claims Act suit (MA risk-adjustment)** — filed 2020; alleges fraudulent submission of inaccurate diagnoses for five years | Live; discovery dispute over deposing outgoing MA executive (Apr 2026) | Uncapped FCA damages (treble + per-claim penalties); reputational |
| **CMS intermediate sanctions (2/27/26)** — "substantial and persistent noncompliance" with MA risk-adjustment data validation; also failure to report/return identified overpayments within 60 days via correct systems | Marketing + **enrollment suspension** set for 3/31/26, pushed to 7/1/26; CMS granted a reprieve (5/29/26). **Current resolution status unconfirmed — treat as unresolved** | Direct hit to MA growth (~$40B+ revenue segment) plus treble-damages overpayment recovery |
| **2026 Star Ratings litigation** — 5 contracts, ~$115M of 2027 quality bonus payments | Preliminary injunction **denied** 8/27/26 (Judge Wood found likely success on merits but no irreparable harm); now in summary-judgment briefing | $115M |
| **2027 Star Ratings litigation** — filed 11/6/26, D.D.C.; 11 measures across 12 contracts | New; Elevance is the 9th of 11 plans suing | **~$516M of 2028 bonus/rebate retention** (company estimate, untested) |
| **Medicaid rate adequacy** — state rate actions lagging elevated medical cost trend | Ongoing; management guides to 2027 margin recovery | Structural margin risk |

**Read-through:** ELV is simultaneously a defendant in the government's flagship MA fraud case *and* is arguing in court that the quality program determining a large share of its MA economics was unlawfully constructed. Both are binary, headline-generating, and outside management control. Industry context is hostile: DOJ settled three MA False Claims Act matters in Aug–Sep 2026 alone ($14.1M, $22.5M), and CMS is appealing the Clover ruling — so favorable Star rulings may not stick.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Risk-adjustment data infrastructure failure — now documented by the regulator.** CMS's finding that Elevance used incorrect systems to submit RADV data and failed to return overpayments within 60 days is a *systems and controls* failure, not a modeling error. It sits at the heart of the DOJ case too. This is the single highest-severity operational exposure: it is auditable, repeatable, and quantified by the government.
2. **Earnings dependence on Star Ratings mechanics.** Star ratings gate quality bonus payments and rebate retention. Two consecutive years of contested ratings ($115M + $516M) show the company's margin recovery thesis rests on litigation outcomes and CMS methodology, not on operating execution alone.
3. **Government-program concentration + Carelon transition.** Revenue is concentrated in Medicaid, Medicare Advantage and Individual/ACA — the three lines most exposed to federal and state rate action, and all three currently under margin pressure. Simultaneously, Carelon (the growth engine, cited for 15% reductions in avoidable admissions in its cancer program) gets a new President on 12/1/26 — a key-person/execution handoff at the moment it must carry the earnings story.

*Not assessed for lack of data: 2026 cybersecurity incident history, supplier/vendor concentration, business-continuity posture. Elevance's 2015 Anthem breach (78.8M records) remains the historical benchmark; no new material breach surfaced in this review.*

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Moderate-to-High**

- **Utilization-management flashpoints are live and repeatable.** Anthem BCBS's anesthesia time-cap policy (CT/NY/MO) triggered a national backlash, attorney-general attention, and a **full reversal on 12/5/2024** — management retreated only after political and media escalation, demonstrating that the brand is one clinical-coverage decision away from a viral controversy. The reversal also cedes pricing leverage.
- **DOJ fraud allegations are the reputational core.** "Fraudulently submitted inaccurate diagnoses of Medicare Advantage enrollees for five years," combined with alleged witness-access obstruction, is the kind of story that attaches to a brand permanently — and Elevance spends real money arguing publicly that Star Ratings are unfairly built, which keeps the topic in trade press.
- **Offsetting positives:** Q3 beat, guidance raise, 52-week high, generally favorable analyst tone, and a visible public-policy voice. Consumer sentiment is currently neutral-to-negative rather than crisis-level; the anesthesia episode shows the tail is fat but recoverable.
- ESRG/social-sentiment scores were not available — assessed qualitatively from press coverage.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: a live DOJ fraud case with alleged obstruction, an unresolved CMS enrollment-suspension order over the company's most profitable growth segment, ~$631M of Star Ratings economics in litigation, structural Medicaid margin fragility, and a meaningful adjusted-to-GAAP earnings gap. These are offset — but not neutralized — by genuinely improved board structure (independent chair, three new directors), a clean SEC enforcement record, stable AM Best ratings, and the CEO's own $2.1M open-market purchase at $366.

This is **not** Critical: there is no going-concern, liquidity, or accounting-integrity question, solvency signals are stable, and the CEO is buying.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **DOJ witness-access dispute (highest priority).** Government alleges ELV announced a key Medicare executive's departure right after DOJ sought his deposition and is now refusing to produce him. Escalate to legal/governance: this creates obstruction-adjacent headline risk and could influence damages/settlement posture. Request the litigation reserve disclosure and the audit committee's handling.
2. **CMS enrollment suspension — status genuinely unresolved.** Media trail stops at the May 29 reprieve and a July 1 effective date. Confirm in writing whether marketing/enrollment restrictions were lifted; a reinstated freeze would hit 2027 MA membership and EPS. **This is the largest single unverified item in this report.**
3. **Adjusted-vs-GAAP divergence.** Q3 adjusted EPS of **$6.99 vs. $4.66 consensus** (+50%) alongside a **25.7% nine-month decline in operating gain** and GAAP net income of $1.34B. A ~50% beat driven by items outside operating gain is an earnings-quality question that belongs in the fraud-indicator file, especially adjacent to an FCA case about revenue recognition in MA.
4. **CFO sale of ~$4.66M at a 52-week high with no reported 10b5-1 plan**, following a prior discretionary 10b5-1 termination in 2024. Request the raw Form 4s and any current trading plan.
5. **Star-ratings litigation dependency** — $115M (2027) and $516M (2028) at stake, with the 2026 injunction already denied. Model these as contingent, not resolved.
6. **Medicaid rate lag** — the 2027 "margin recovery" thesis depends on state rate increases that have not yet been granted.
