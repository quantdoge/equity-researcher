## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW (and narrowing)**

Genmab is not a drug company in the traditional sense; it is a **royalty machine** built on antibody engineering (DuoBody, HexaBody platforms). That makes the economics extraordinary and the concentration alarming in equal measure:

- **Gross margin 91.7%** (FY2025), 91–99% for eight straight years. This is the profile of a licensor, not a manufacturer.
- **But the franchise is one molecule.** DARZALEX royalties are guided at **~$2.83B of 2026 revenue (~64% of total, and 80% of all royalty income)**. Genmab does not commercialise DARZALEX — J&J does. Genmab has no pricing power, no sales force relationship with the prescriber, and no control over lifecycle strategy. It owns a contract and a patent estate.
- **The moat is contractual, not durable.** Company disclosure is explicit: *"we expect that our royalties from sales of DARZALEX will begin to decline materially in 2029."* Genmab's own US/EU/JP patents run off in the late 2020s and early 2030s; analysts model royalties to 2035. Either way, the largest cash stream has a **visible expiry date inside this decade**.
- **Erosion is already visible in the moat's quality:** a royalty-rate dispute with J&J over subcutaneous DARZALEX (Faspro) went to arbitration, on the theory that the delivery change shouldn't reset the royalty base. Whenever your economic moat requires litigating against your own licensee, the moat is a legal construct rather than a commercial one.
- **The genuine asset is the platform + pipeline:** Kesimpta (ofatumumab royalty to Novartis, growing fast), Epkinly (epcoritamab, wholly owned), Tivdak, TEPEZZA, plus the two acquisitions — **ProfoundBio (Rina-S, FRα ADC, ~$1.8B, 2024)** and **Merus (petosemtamab, EGFR×LGR5 bispecific, ~$8B, 2025)**.

**Direction over 5 years: narrowing.** In 2020 Genmab was a net-cash royalty collector with a broad partnered pipeline. Today it is a levered, integrated commercial organisation whose profit pool still rests on one J&J molecule with a 2029–2035 fuse. ROIC has fallen from **22.5% (2022) → 16.4% (2023) → 17.8% (2024) → 11.1% (2025)** — a direct consequence of $10B of M&A capital landing on the balance sheet as goodwill rather than earning assets. ROIC now sits barely above any reasonable cost of capital. That is the single most important number in this report.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — Intrinsic value ~$41/share; **price is ~9% above fair value**

Trailing metrics at $44.86: **P/E 29x; EV/EBITDA 23.8x; P/FCF 24.5x; FCF yield 4.1%** — versus a peer-median P/E of 31.7x (an 8% discount to large-cap pharma peers who do *not* face a 2029 concentration cliff). The discount is warranted, not an opportunity.

I built a two-part sum-of-the-parts because a single growth-rate DCF is meaningless for a decaying royalty annuity:

**(A) DARZALEX royalty stream** — gross royalties $2.83B (2026) → ~$3.0–3.1B peak (2027–28), then the company's own guided material decline from 2029, tapering to a residual tail by 2035. After-tax at ~78%, discounted at 9%: **≈ $9.4B**.

**(B) Everything else** — Kesimpta royalties + Epkinly + Tivdak + TEPEZZA + petosemtamab + Rina-S. 2026E revenue ~$1.57B, FCF ~$0.47B, growing 20% for six years, 8% for four, 3% terminal, at 9%: **≈ $17.6B**.

**Enterprise value ≈ $27.0B → less $3.6B net debt → equity ≈ $23.4B ≈ $38/share.**

| Scenario | Assumptions | Value/share |
|---|---|---|
| **Bear** (25%) | Royalties fall off a 2029–32 cliff; petosemtamab/Rina-S disappoint; R&D stays elevated | **$28–30** |
| **Base** (50%) | As above; pipeline grows into a real second franchise | **$38** |
| **Bull** (25%) | DARZALEX royalties persist to 2035 (sell-side assumption); petosemtamab becomes a multi-billion head-and-neck/colorectal asset; 8% WACC | **$54–58** |
| **Probability-weighted** | | **~$41** |

Every input above is defensible; the honest range is **$35–50**, centred at ~$41. At $44.86 the market is pricing the base-to-bull case with no discount. **Applying my required 25–30% margin of safety, the entry point is $29–31 per share.**

**What the price implies:** to justify $44.86 you must believe DARZALEX royalties run to 2035 *and* that the $8B Merus bet converts into a durable second franchise *and* accept an 8% discount rate on a single-franchise biotech. That is a bull case, not a base case.

**On the October move:** the stock rose from $34.75 (2-Oct) to $44.86 (26-Oct) — +29% in three weeks — on **positive petosemtamab colorectal and head-and-neck data** (two ~10% single-day gaps) and an Incyte collaboration. This is exactly the kind of clinical binary that a fundamentals-driven investor should not chase. Analyst targets straddle the price: Morgan Stanley $42 (Equalweight, 20-Oct), Guggenheim $58, Truist $40, Jefferies $41.50, H.C. Wainwright $38, Wolfe $32.

## 4. Management Quality — Good scientifically, questioned on capital allocation

**Positives:** CEO Jan van de Winkel has led since 2010 and built Genmab from a platform licensor into a ~$28B enterprise with a genuinely differentiated antibody toolkit. The ProfoundBio deal (Rina-S) looks astute at $1.8B and has delivered clinical validation. Disclosure quality is high — management states the 2029 royalty decline plainly rather than obfuscating. Buybacks are disciplined in size; no empire-building in headcount.

**Concerns:**
1. **The $8B Merus acquisition is a concentrated, pre-approval bet funded with debt** — executed immediately before the royalty cliff, at a valuation that requires petosemtamab to succeed commercially. That is the opposite of a margin-of-safety capital allocation. It converted a fortress balance sheet into 2.8x levered in a single decision, and it dropped ROIC from 17.8% to 11.1%.
2. **Insider ownership ~1.2%;** top-25 institutions hold ~53% (Orbis ~4.7%). There is no owner-operator. Alignment rests on equity comp, which is dilutive.
3. **A royalty dispute with J&J went to arbitration** — a licensee relationship where the counterparty has every incentive to narrow the royalty base.
4. Applying GAAP: adjusted operating profit runs ~$185M below IFRS due to acquisition charges, and intangible amortisation will suppress reported EPS for years. Management communicates on adjusted numbers, which requires skepticism.

Verdict: **competent, credible, not owner-operator.**

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (Do not initiate; not a buy at $44.86)

This is a genuinely high-quality business — 92% gross margins, ~20% revenue growth, FCF conversion above 100%, a validated platform — trading **above** my probability-weighted intrinsic value after a 29% three-week rally driven entirely by clinical data.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Understandable? | Partially — the royalty economics are; the pipeline value is not |
| Durable moat? | No — Narrow, contractually time-limited |
| ROIC > WACC consistently? | Marginal now (11.1%), previously yes |
| Margin of safety? | **None — negative** |
| Verdict | **HOLD** (would be Sell on a strict valuation-only mandate) |

**Buy zone: $30–32** (25–30% below intrinsic value). **Trim zone: $52+.** I would not own this for a 5–10 year holding period at today's price precisely because the DARZALEX annuity — the thing that funds everything — has a defined end date inside the holding period, and the replacement has not yet been proven.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Downside risks**
1. **The 2029 royalty cliff.** ~64% of revenue and effectively 100% of the free cash flow that funds R&D decays from 2029. If royalty rates step down faster (Faspro arbitration, biosimilar timing, J&J lifecycle switches to next-gen assets), the bear case is $28.
2. **Execution concentration on petosemtamab.** $8B of purchase price and $5.4B of debt rest on one Phase 3 asset. A safety signal or a failed readout that wipes a third off this bet would be catastrophic to equity value given the leverage.
3. **Leverage.** Net debt/EBITDA ~2.8x with $174M of semi-annual interest expense in a business whose cash flows are about to plateau. It cannot buy its way out of a second failure.
