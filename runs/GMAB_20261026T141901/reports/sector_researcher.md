# SECTOR BRIEF — GENMAB A/S (GMAB)
### Sector: Healthcare / Biotechnology (antibody therapeutics, oncology)
*Prepared by Sector Economy Research | Data as of 26 October 2026; FY2025 = last reported fiscal year*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

Genmab sits in the **biologics / antibody-therapeutics sub-sector of biotechnology**, with a hybrid model: royalty and profit-share income from big-pharma partners (J&J's DARZALEX, Novartis' Kesimpta, AbbVie's epcoritamab) plus an increasingly own-commercialised product portfolio (TEPEZZA, Tivdak, Epkinly). This is a structurally high-margin, IP-defended industry: gross margins of 70–92% are normal, and value accrues to whoever owns the validated target and the manufacturing know-how. Global oncology spend is roughly $250–280bn and compounding mid-to-high single digits, but the growth pockets Genmab competes in — **bispecific antibodies (~$10–12bn, >25% growth) and ADCs (~$15bn, ~20% growth)** — are growing 3–4× the sector rate. Concentration is high at the top (top-10 pharma capture a majority of industry profit) but the biotech tier is fragmented; barriers to entry are severe (10–12 year development cycles, $1–2bn per approval, manufacturing complexity). **Porter's Five Forces: rivalry HIGH** (crowded BCMA/CD3 and CD20xCD3 niches), **buyer power HIGH** (concentrated US payers, IRA negotiation), **supplier power MODERATE** (CDMOs, tariffs), **threat of new entrants LOW**, **threat of substitutes HIGH** (CAR-T, ADCs, biosimilars post-LOE).

**Cycle position: mid-cycle recovery / early expansion.** After a three-year funding winter, 2026 is a distinctly better year: biopharma deal value exceeded $65bn in Q1 2026 alone (strongest quarter since 2020), R&D licensing value reached $221bn through Q3 2026, and policy visibility has improved (Janus Henderson, PwC, IQVIA all frame 2026 as a catalyst-rich, constructive year). The recovery is **bifurcated**: profitable, platform-owning large caps are re-rating while cash-constrained single-asset biotechs remain starved. Capital intensity is rising as companies onshore manufacturing.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Late-stage catalyst density is unusually high.** Rina-S (FRα ADC) hit both PFS and OS endpoints in Phase 3 RAINFOL-02 in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer (23 Oct 2026, stock +9.5%); petosemtamab posted 28.2-month median OS / 63% ORR in 1L HNSCC with Phase 3 topline due this year. Each is a potential multi-billion-dollar franchise and the market is now pricing optionality rather than just royalty annuity.
- **The strongest M&A and licensing market since the pandemic.** Big pharma faces 2028–2030 patent cliffs and is bidding aggressively for validated bispecific/ADC platforms; JPMorgan puts announced R&D deal value at $221bn YTD 2026 with upfront cash at only 6% of total — a seller's market for de-risked Phase 3 assets.
- **Policy backdrop improved.** Clearer IRA implementation and trade-deal frameworks have reduced the discount rate applied to long-duration pharma cash flows; US onshoring commitments (Genmab has US manufacturing capacity) mitigate Section-232 tariff exposure relative to EU-only manufacturers.

---

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **DARZALEX royalty concentration + Medicare price negotiation.** DARZALEX was selected in the IPAY-2027 negotiation cycle, with maximum fair prices taking effect **1 January 2027**. Genmab's royalty is levied on J&J's *net sales*, so a negotiated Medicare price cut flows straight through to Genmab revenue with no offsetting cost reduction — a structural, permanent step-down in the highest-margin line. I could not independently confirm the exact MFP percentage from the sources retrieved; treat the magnitude as an open item for the analyst.
- **Royalty/LOE sunset and mix-driven margin dilution.** DARZALEX US composition-of-matter protection runs to roughly the early 2030s, and Genmab's tiered royalty rate steps down as sales thresholds are crossed. Simultaneously, gross margin has already fallen from 100% (2022) → 98.6% (2023) → 95.4% (2024) → **91.7% (2025)** as the mix shifts from royalty income to lower-margin own-product sales and COGS is booked — a trend that mechanically continues.
- **Crowded competitive niches and inflated asset prices.** Every major haematology target now has 3–5 competing bispecifics (teclistamab, elranatamab, epcoritamab, glofitamab), and China-origin licensing has raised the cost of replenishing pipelines. Rivalry caps pricing power precisely where Genmab's growth is concentrated.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

Rationale:
- **Best-in-class unit economics.** 91.7% gross margin and 33.6% operating margin on a $3.7bn base place it in the top decile of commercial-stage biotech — only Vertex is comparable on operating margin, and Vertex is 3× larger.
- **Diversified, de-risked revenue.** Royalty streams from three separate big-pharma partners plus four commercialised products means no single binary readout can impair the equity, unlike single-asset peers.
- **Self-funding optionality.** ~$964m of net income and a net-cash balance sheet mean pipeline funding does not depend on the capital markets — a decisive advantage in a still-bifurcated biotech funding environment.
- **Validated platform with multiple shots on goal.** Two positive late-stage datasets within four days (Oct 2026) confirm the DuoBody/ADC platform is productive, not a one-target story.

Caveats that keep this from being unambiguously best-in-class: **high revenue concentration in DARZALEX royalties** (outside management's control, exposed to IRA pricing from 2027 and royalty step-downs), **decelerating growth** (+11% vs +31% in 2024), and a **structurally eroding gross margin** as the model shifts to owned products. The stock now trades at roughly **7.4× sales and ~29× earnings** — no longer a deep-value royalty vehicle, and it has already absorbed a good deal of the good news.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

The biologics/biotech sector enters late 2026 in the most constructive phase of its cycle in four years: M&A at post-pandemic highs, improving policy visibility, and a clear shift in investor preference toward **profitable, platform-owning companies with late-stage catalysts** — precisely Genmab's profile. Oncology bispecifics and ADCs are growing 3–4× the underlying healthcare market, and the top-22 healthcare aggregate still earns only a 12.4% net margin versus Genmab's 25.9%, underscoring how much of the sector's economics sit with differentiated IP rather than scale.

The rating carries two conditions: (i) it applies to **large-cap, cash-generative biotech**, not the small-cap cohort still dependent on dilutive financing; and (ii) the **US drug-pricing reset from January 2027 is the sector's dominant idiosyncratic risk**, and it bites Genmab specifically through the DARZALEX royalty. Position sizing should reflect that single-name policy exposure.
