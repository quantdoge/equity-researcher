*As of 26 October 2026 | Data: FRED, company filings, policy releases*

### 1. Macro Environment Summary: **TRANSITIONAL / LATE-CYCLE HAWKISH**

The regime has changed materially since 2025. The Fed **cut** from 4.33% to ~3.63% between Sept 2025 and Jan 2026 — but has now **reversed**. On **16 September 2026 the FOMC raised the target range 25bp to 3.75–4.00% in a 12–0 vote, the first hike since 2023**, and the minutes signal most participants expect another increase this year (next meeting: **27–28 October 2026**). Street projections point to ~4.25% in 2027.

The driver is an **energy supply shock**, not demand overheating: WTI has gone from **$69.60 (1 Jul 2026) to $107.89 (20 Oct 2026)**, +55% in under four months. Headline CPI has re-accelerated to **+3.72% y/y (Sept 2026)** from 2.4% in January, while **core CPI is only +2.57% y/y** and 10-year breakevens are contained at **2.34%**. That is the important nuance: the market believes this is a transitory supply shock, but the Fed is not willing to look through it.

Growth and labour remain solid — real GDP **+2.2% y/y in Q2 2026**, unemployment **4.2%** — so this is not a recessionary backdrop. Credit is wide open: **US high-yield OAS at 3.38%** (23 Oct), near cycle tights. Long-end yields are the pressure point: the curve has **bear-steepened** to 10Y 4.99% / 30Y 5.37% (2s10s +34bp), implying rising term premium and fiscal supply concerns rather than growth fear.

Globally, tightening has resumed in tandem: the **ECB hiked 25bp in June and again on 10 September 2026 to a 2.50% deposit rate**, and **Danmarks Nationalbank followed, lifting its current-account rate to 2.10% (effective 11 Sept)**. The krone peg means Danish monetary conditions are imported from Frankfurt — GMAB's domestic rate environment is ~150–190bp *below* US rates, which is the mechanical reason USD/DKK has risen to 6.6554 from 6.3436 in May.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**GMAB profile:** Copenhagen-based antibody/ADC biotech; **$27.6bn market cap**, beta **0.645** (low market sensitivity). Revenue is essentially **USD-denominated** — DARZALEX royalties from J&J ($1,708m royalty revenue in H1 2026, +24% y/y), plus AbbVie, Pfizer and BioNTech collaborations and US product sales — while the cost base is largely DKK/EUR/NL and US. It carries **net cash**, so there is no refinancing or credit-spread exposure whatsoever.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Not unfavourable, despite a 5% 10-year and a hawkish Fed, for four reasons:

1. **Low macro beta (0.645) and catalyst-driven tape.** GMAB is +41% y/y and +88% off its June 2026 low ($23.85 → $44.86), driven by the Rina-S RAINFOL-02 Phase 3 win (23 Oct) and petosemtamab data — not by rates. Macro is currently the *second* derivative for this stock.
2. **Net cash, not net debt.** The one thing high rates punish — refinancing — does not apply. GMAB is a rate *recipient*.
3. **USD revenue with a weakening DKK** is a live translation tailwind into FY2026 and H1 2027 numbers.
4. **Core inflation at 2.57% and breakevens at 2.34%** imply the hiking cycle is closer to its end than its beginning; one more 25bp and the Fed is likely on hold.

The offsetting negatives — 5%+ long yields capping the multiple and the **Section 232 / MFN policy complex** — are real but are policy risks layered on top of an otherwise benign macro cycle. Note the imminent catalyst: **FOMC 27–28 October** and **GMAB Q3 results 5 November 2026**.

### 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise: **BEAR — "Good News, Lower Stock"**

**Oil does not normalise.** WTI holds above $110 through winter; headline CPI pushes to 4.5%+, and the Fed is forced to 4.75–5.00% by mid-2027 with the 10Y breaking 5.75% and the 30Y above 6%.

In that world, GMAB's clinical execution becomes irrelevant to the share price. Long-duration biotech de-rates 20–30% on *multiple*, not on fundamentals — precisely the mechanism that took GMAB from ~$34 to ~$24 between January and June 2026 even as the pipeline improved. The tell to watch: **10Y breakevens**. At 2.34% the market still believes the shock is transitory; if breakevens break above 2.60% while core CPI also accelerates, the "supply shock" narrative dies — that is the trigger for a genuine duration de-rating across the sector.

Secondary (bull) surprise: a negotiated **MFN agreement granting tariff relief** for Genmab's US-manufactured products would remove the single largest policy overhang and, combined with a Fed pause, could re-rate the stock independent of the macro cycle.
