## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Genmab A/S (Nasdaq: GMAB)

**Company:** Danish foreign private issuer, biotech (antibody therapeutics); ~$27.6B market cap; 3,119 FTE; CEO Jan van de Winkel. ADR-listed, home regulator Danish FSA, SEC reporting via Form 6-K/20-F.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate** (structure strong, conduct mixed)

Evidence for:
- **No CEO/Chairman duality.** Deirdre P. Connelly is Independent Chairman; Pernille Erenbjerg is Independent Deputy Chairman; Rolf Hoffmann and Elizabeth O'Farrell are independent directors. Board is composed of independent industry experts — a genuinely clean structure, not a founder-controlled board.
- 2025 Compensation Report was put to and approved at the AGM — say-on-pay discipline is in place.

Evidence against:
- **Adverse arbitration outcome vs. Janssen/J&J** on daratumumab — Genmab's appeal was *denied* and dismissal of its claims in the second arbitration is now "concluded." Management litigated a core economic right and lost; that is a capital-allocation and judgment issue, not merely legal bad luck.
- **Insider-filing correction.** Genmab had to correct the pricing on the 2 March 2026 insider share transactions (CEO van de Winkel, CFO Pagano). Benign in size, but it is a disclosure-controls blemish at the exact issuer where insider filings matter most.
- **Structural conflict:** AbbVie is simultaneously Genmab's epcoritamab commercialisation partner and its trade-secret litigation adversary. Board-level oversight of that dual relationship warrants scrutiny.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **No SEC enforcement actions.** EDGAR full-text search returns zero actions naming GMAB.
- **No Genmab-specific FDA warning letter or cGMP enforcement found.**
- **ITC Section 337 investigation (337-TA-1466) — RESOLVED.** Instituted Dec 2025 on AbbVie/ImmunoGen complaint alleging unlawful importation of ADCs; **terminated in its entirety on 7 July 2026** following an unopposed motion (8 June 2026). This removes the worst tail risk — a US import ban on the ADC franchise.
- **Live litigation:** *AbbVie Inc. v. Genmab A/S et al.*, W.D. Wash. 2:25-cv-00510 (Judge Tana Lin), DTSA claim over disaccharide/"sugar scaffold" ADC linkers, tied to the $1.8B **ProfoundBio** acquisition (May 2024). AbbVie seeks **damages and broad injunctive relief**. Genmab/ProfoundBio moved to dismiss (Aug 2026), arguing claims were "concocted." **No ruling yet.**
- **Pending approvals (execution risk, not enforcement):** Rina-S in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer (Ph3 RAINFOL-02 positive), epcoritamab + R-CHOP in 1L DLBCL (EPCORE DLBCL-2 positive), petosemtamab in 1L HNSCC (Ph3 topline due).

### 3. Insider Activity Analysis

**Net: no meaningful open-market insider buying over the trailing 12 months; selling de minimis.**

- The Form 4 feed is **stale — latest filing 30 Dec 2025**, so SEC-sourced coverage does not reach the 2026 window. I am relying on company announcements and third-party aggregators for 2026.
- 2026 activity is overwhelmingly **mechanical, not discretionary**: the 2 March 2026 "acquisitions" by CEO van de Winkel and CFO Pagano were **RSU vesting and net settlement**, not conviction purchases — and were subsequently repriced by correction.
- One director (**Michael Kavanagh**) sold **526 shares** on 29 May 2026 on Nasdaq Copenhagen — economically trivial.
- A third-party screener reports a "320,000 SEK purchase by Chairman Ulf Gejhammar" — **this is inconsistent with Genmab's actual board and is denominated in SEK; I treat it as a data artifact and do not credit it.**
- **Interpretation:** absence of insider buying into a period of strongly positive clinical data is mildly notable, but the Danish/European norm is RSU-based compensation with little open-market activity. Not a red flag on its own.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Extreme single-asset, single-counterparty revenue concentration.** 2025 revenue $3,720M, of which royalty revenue was the dominant component. J&J's DARZALEX net sales were **$14,351M in 2025** (vs $11,670M in 2024) — Genmab's royalty on that one molecule is the majority of the P&L. Genmab neither controls the asset nor the commercialization, and it has already **lost its royalty arbitration against Janssen**. Kesimpta (Novartis) adds further royalty dependence.
- **IP/trade-secret overhang on the growth engine.** The ProfoundBio-derived ADC platform — the entire basis of the Rina-S story and the $1.8B price paid — is the subject of AbbVie's damages-and-injunction claim. An adverse outcome impairs the asset the market is now pricing. Due-diligence quality on the acquisition is directly in question.
- **Key-person dependency.** Founder-era CEO Jan van de Winkel (CEO since 2010) is the scientific and strategic anchor; his contract carries a **two-year notice/severance versus one year for other executives**, evidencing his outsized importance. Depreciation risk is high and unhedged.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Low, but high-variance**

Current press is overwhelmingly favorable — WSJ coverage of "shares jump" on RAINFOL-02, a stream of positive Ph2/Ph3 readouts, and a new Incyte collaboration. Genmab sells no consumer products, so consumer-backlash and ESG-controversy exposure is structurally minimal. The reputational asymmetry runs almost entirely through **clinical failure** and through the **AbbVie litigation narrative** ("willfully blind to trade secret theft") — a framing that, if sustained in a public ruling, would attach a rare integrity discount to a company whose brand rests on scientific credibility. Low today; non-linear.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

The governance architecture is genuinely good (independent chair, no duality, contested pay), the ITC import-ban threat is extinguished, there is no SEC or FDA enforcement history, and insider activity shows no distribution. These are meaningful offsets. What holds the rating at Moderate rather than Low is the **stacking of concentrated, correlated exposures**: a royalty stream dependent on a counterparty Genmab has already lost an arbitration to, a growth asset whose IP title is being actively challenged with injunctive relief sought, and a CEO without a credible succession hedge.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **AbbVie's injunctive-relief request over the ProfoundBio ADC linker technology** — the single largest non-financial risk. A ruling restricting Rina-S would impair the primary value driver. *Watch for the motion-to-dismiss ruling in W.D. Wash. 2:25-cv-00510.*
2. **DARZALEX royalty concentration plus a lost Janssen arbitration** — the royalty base is contractually contested and counterparty-controlled. Escalate for a quantified sensitivity.
3. **ProfoundBio due-diligence adequacy** — AbbVie is quoting Genmab's own executives' diligence commentary to allege willful blindness. This is a governance question about the M&A process, not just a legal one.
4. **Insider-disclosure pricing correction (Mar 2026)** — minor, but a disclosure-controls signal to be tracked, especially combined with the stale Form 4 data.
5. **CEO key-person risk** — two-year severance confirms structural dependence; no publicly evidenced succession plan.
