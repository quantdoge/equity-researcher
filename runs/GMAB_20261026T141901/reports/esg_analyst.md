### 1. ESG Materiality Map

Biotech is one of the lowest-environmental-intensity, highest-social-intensity sectors. Materiality is sharply skewed toward **product safety, clinical/ethical conduct, access-to-medicines, human capital and capital-allocation governance**.

| Factor | Materiality | Why it hits cash flow / cost of capital |
|---|---|---|

| **Access to medicines / pricing (S)** | **High** | IRA Medicare negotiation, EU Pharma Package access conditions — flows straight into the DARZALEX royalty base. |
| **Governance of capital allocation (G)** | **High** | ~$1.8bn ProfoundBio ADC deal; royalty concentration in J&J/AbbVie; M&A integration is the primary value-destruction risk. |

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- **Footprint:** No large-scale commercial manufacturing; sites are R&D/office (Copenhagen, Utrecht, Brooklyn Park MN, Plainsboro NJ). Scope 1+2 emissions are immaterial to the P&L; Scope 3 (purchased goods, CDMO manufacturing, clinical trial supply, business travel) dominates the inventory — a common and structurally benign profile for commercial-stage biotech.
- **Carbon intensity:** Materially below pharma-major peers (LLY, JNJ, ABBV, MRK) which carry manufacturing and cold-chain burdens. Estimated Scope 1+2 intensity in the single-digit tCO₂e per $m revenue — I could not verify the exact figure from primary filings.
- **Physical climate risk:** Minimal — no coastal manufacturing, no agricultural inputs, water stress not material to lab operations.
- **Controversies:** **No spills, pollution fines or remediation liabilities found** in the 2024–2026 news sweep.
- **Gap versus peers:** Third-party trackers list **no SBTi-committed target and no disclosed net-zero year** for Genmab, while Genmab itself reports CSRD-compliant climate content in its annual report. This is a *disclosure/validation* gap rather than a performance gap, but it costs points with climate-focused index screens and is cheap to fix.
- **Verdict:** Low risk, low opportunity. Not a valuation driver.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**The single most financially material ESG factor for Genmab.**

- **Product safety (elevated):**
  - **TIVDAK (tisotumab vedotin):** FDA label carries a boxed-warning-adjacent profile — **fatal adverse reactions in 4% of patients** (septic shock, pneumonitis, sudden death, multi-system organ failure) and **ocular adverse reactions in 55%** of cervical cancer patients. Manageable in a relapsed/refractory oncology population, but it constrains label expansion into earlier lines and invites class scrutiny of the MMAE payload.
  - **EPKINLY (epcoritamab):** Boxed warning for **cytokine release syndrome, including serious or fatal reactions**. Standard for CD3-bispecifics but a standing liability as the franchise scales.
  - **Rina-S / petosemtamab (2026 readouts):** Both are advancing on strong efficacy (RAINFOL-02 met PFS and OS; petosemtamab 1L HNSCC median OS 28.2 months). Safety differentiation on ocular/CRS/EGFR-toxicity will determine commercial ceiling. This is a *feature*, not a defect, of the model: Genmab's upside and its tail risk are the same variable.
- **Access to medicines:** DARZALEX is a standard of care in multiple myeloma with **$14.35bn of 2025 J&J net sales** — precisely the profile targeted by US IRA Medicare negotiation and European access/pricing pressure. Genmab does not set the price (J&J does) but **bears the royalty consequence**, making pricing policy a direct revenue risk with no operational lever.
- **Human capital:** ~3,119 FTE, growing through the ProfoundBio acquisition. **No layoffs, strikes, or labour disputes found** in 2026 coverage — a positive differentiator versus much of the biotech sector's 2025–26 restructuring cycle. Talent concentration in ADC/bispecific engineering remains the key retention risk.
- **Supply chain labour:** CDMO-dependent; oversight obligations rising under EU due-diligence rules.
- **Verdict:** Medium. No evidence of negligence or unsafe-conduct controversy, but inherent product-safety and pricing-policy exposure is unavoidable and material.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Strengths (genuinely above sector median):**
- **One share class, one share one vote.** No dual-class structure, no poison pill, no controlling foundation — a meaningful advantage versus Danish and US biotech peers where control is entrenched.
- **CEO/Chair separation**; the CEO is not a member of the Board, and the Board is majority independent with separate Audit & Finance, Compensation, Nominating & Corporate Governance, and Scientific Committees.
- **Disclosure quality:** Second consecutive **CSRD-compliant Sustainability Statement** inside the Annual Report, plus a statutory Danish corporate governance report with a comply-or-explain deviations section. This is top-decile transparency for a company of this size.
- **No ESG-linked debt covenants and effectively net cash** — zero refinancing or covenant risk tied to ESG metrics.
- **Board responsiveness:** When shareholders rejected the 2026 board remuneration proposal, the Board re-solicited rather than imposed.

**Weaknesses (why not 8–9):**
- **Remuneration governance is the live issue.** The 2026 board remuneration proposal **failed at the AGM on 19 March 2026**, forcing an **Extraordinary General Meeting on 7 October 2026** to approve board fees plus **share-based remuneration in the form of restricted stock units**. Genmab has repeatedly deviated from the Danish Corporate Governance Recommendations' position that non-executive board pay should not include share-based instruments. MedWatch framed it as an "unusual board pay package." RSUs for non-executives are defensible for alignment but **blur the oversight/monitoring distinction** and are a standing source of proxy-adviser opposition. The Board argued pay sat at the **31st percentile** of a recalibrated peer group — a legitimate competitiveness argument that nonetheless did not persuade shareholders on first vote.
- **Key-person and tenure risk:** a founder-CEO in post since 2010. Stability is a strength; succession opacity is not.
- **Counterparty/litigation record:** Genmab **lost its DARZALEX arbitration against Janssen** (tribunal ruled for J&J in 2022; the ~**$405m** claim was dismissed and the dismissal upheld on appeal), and separately must share royalties with Halozyme on the subcutaneous formulation. Repeated adverse outcomes against its most important partner point to weak contractual leverage — a governance-of-relationships issue as much as a legal one.
- **Capital allocation concentration:** the ~$1.8bn ProfoundBio/Rina-S bet and heavy reliance on royalties from partners it does not control.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (high-quality governance architecture, moderate social risk)

- Third-party quantitative anchor: **medium ESG risk, score ~20.4** (Sustainalytics-style), broadly in line with large-cap pharma rather than the high-risk tail of biotech.
- Not a **Leader**: the remuneration-governance friction, absence of SBTi-validated climate targets, and unresolved product-safety exposure on newly launched assets block that classification.
- Decisively not a **Laggard** or **ESG Risk** name: single share class, independent board, CSRD-grade disclosure, no environmental liabilities, no corruption or labour controversies.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL for the thesis**

ESG factors are **not** a primary driver of the GMAB investment case and do not materially move estimated cash flows or cost of capital — but they are **not irrelevant**, and the risks are asymmetric in one direction:

- **Nothing here impairs the franchise.** Low environmental intensity, clean labour record, one-share-one-vote governance, and CSRD-leading disclosure mean Genmab should pass most institutional ESG screens and faces no green- or governance-driven de-rating.
- **The two genuine watch items are social and governance, not environmental.** (i) *Product safety on the newly commercialised franchises* — TIVDAK's 4% fatal-AR rate and EPKINLY's CRS boxed warning mean a single class-wide safety signal or a restrictive label outcome on Rina-S/petosemtamab would damage the highest-multiple part of the pipeline. (ii) *Remuneration governance* — a repeat AGM defeat or escalation by proxy advisers would be a governance irritant, not a valuation event.
- **The channel that actually matters is pricing policy, dressed as "S."** IRA Medicare negotiation and European access pressure transmit directly into the DARZALEX royalty stream ($14.35bn of 2025 partner sales) that underwrites Genmab's earnings base. This is the single ESG-adjacent factor with real cash-flow consequence.
- **Positioning:** treat ESG as a **tail-risk monitor, not a thesis input**. Escalation trigger for a Negative view would be a TIVDAK/Rina-S safety signal, an FDA/EMA label restriction, or a second consecutive shareholder defeat on board pay.
