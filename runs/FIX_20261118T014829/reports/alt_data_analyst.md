### 2. Business Activity Signals

**Backlog (the best alt proxy available) — strongly positive.** Q3 2026 backlog hit a record **$17.81B, +90% YoY** (+$3.8B added in the quarter alone), with duration extended to **18–24 months**. Against ~$15B annualized revenue that is >1.1x forward coverage — i.e., **roughly 1.5–2 years of revenue already under signed contract**. Modular/off-site reached **17% of revenue**.

**Hiring — stable, not accelerating.** ~121 open roles on Indeed (~20 sheet metal mechanics, 14 HVAC techs, 11 pipefitters), plus programmatic roles (VDC Project Manager, Traveling Project Controls Specialist) consistent with modular build-out staffing. 121 roles / 22,700 employees ≈ 0.5% — consistent with project-driven labor absorption, **no evidence of either a hiring surge or retrenchment**. Direction: flat. Low confidence (qualitative web proxy, no historical series).

**Patents — gap.** USPTO tool returned a parse error; retry abandoned. Structurally low relevance for a trade-secret-driven mechanical contractor.

**Peer tape — idiosyncratic de-rate.** Nov 3 → Nov 5 crash (FIX –25%, PWR –22%, EME –16%, MTZ –20%) was clearly sector-wide AI-infrastructure de-rating. But FIX's *post-earnings* bleed (Oct 22 –9%, Oct 23 –12%) and its –21% aggregate vs PWR's flat performance is **company-specific**: drivers were CFO retirement, **no formal guidance**, and Electrical segment margin deterioration partially offset by Mechanical (+43% revenue growth, expanding margins).

### 7. Confidence Level: **MEDIUM**

**Why not High:** (a) SimilarWeb web-traffic and patent tools failed — two of five business-activity channels are dark; (b) job postings are a qualitative web proxy with no historical baseline, so no trend can be computed; (c) the sector-peer tool returned mega-cap Industrials (GE, CAT, RTX) rather than true MEP/E&C comps, forcing a manual PWR/EME/MTZ substitution; (d) Google Trends on a ticker measures **attention, not demand** — it tells you about flows, not HVAC bookings.

**Data lag and risk:** Backlog is a signed-contract figure and the highest-quality signal here, but it lags bookings momentum and can mask **permitting/start-date slippage** — precisely what "data center backlash" headlines describe. Google Trends' ~70% decline in "data center construction" interest is a 1–2 quarter leading caution on *new* awards even as existing backlog converts. Beta is 1.64, so a continued AI-capex de-rate can overwhelm a fundamentally strong print. **Alt data here argues the Q4 (Feb 18) print beats; it does not argue the multiple re-rates until the AI-infrastructure narrative stabilizes.**
