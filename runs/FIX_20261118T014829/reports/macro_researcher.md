# Macroeconomic Assessment: Comfort Systems USA (FIX)
**As of 18 November 2026** | Sector: Industrials / Engineering & Construction | Market cap ~$38.5B | Beta 1.64

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — "Nominal Boom, Restrictive Squeeze" (Transitional, risk-off at the long end)

The US is in an unusual and internally contradictory regime. **Real activity is accelerating** (Q3 2026 real GDP +3.4% annualised, unemployment down to 4.1%), but **inflation has re-accelerated to a three-year high of 3.82%**, forcing the Fed into a *second* tightening cycle. The FOMC **hiked 25bp on 16 September 2026** to 3.75–4.00% — the first increase since 2023 — then held 10–2 on 28 October, with markets pricing one more hike.

The critical fact: with effective Fed funds at 3.88% and CPI at 3.82%, the **real policy rate is approximately +0.06%** — essentially zero. Policy is *not* restrictive in real terms, and sell-side commentary (Yahoo Finance, 29 Sep) explicitly argues the Fed is "falling behind the curve." **Rate risk is therefore skewed to the upside, not the downside.**

Meanwhile the long end has sold off hard: the **10Y has risen 68bp since 30 June** (4.44% → 5.12%), touching 5.31% on 5 October, with the 30Y at 5.47% and the 2s10s at **+39bp (bear steepening, term-premium driven, not growth driven)**.

Yet credit is *not* stressed: **HY OAS is 334bp**, versus a long-run median near 500bp. It has widened 69bp from 265bp on 1 September, but remains historically tight. This is the defining divergence: **equity duration and Treasury term premium are repricing violently while corporate credit remains complacent.** The canaries are in AI project finance specifically — CoreWeave paper at ~15% — not in the broad market.

**Stance: risk-off for long-duration equities, risk-on for nominal revenue. Transitional and fragile.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

FIX is a **pure-domestic US MEP contractor** (mechanical + electrical) whose revenue is now overwhelmingly driven by data-centre and industrial mega-projects. Q3 2026 results were extraordinary: **revenue $3.77B (+53.7% YoY), net income $489M (+67.7%), backlog $17.81B — up 90% from $9.38B a year ago.** Gross margin held at 24.8%. This is a company with *maximum operational leverage to the AI buildout*.

### Negative

**1. Discount-rate shock — the dominant driver of the share price.** FIX trades at $1,099, **down 47% from its 6 May 2026 peak of $2,070** — roughly **$34B of market value erased while earnings rose 68%**. This is pure multiple compression: an estimated ~43x forward P/E at the peak compressing toward the low-20s. With beta of 1.64, FIX is priced as a **long-duration growth asset**, and a 10Y at 5.13% is precisely the environment that de-rates such assets. FIX's own balance sheet carries minimal leverage, so **refinancing risk is negligible — the rate sensitivity is entirely via the discount rate and via its customers' cost of capital.**

**2. Customer financing costs.** Data-centre projects are funded with long-dated debt and lease structures. A 30Y at 5.47% with term premium rising directly raises hurdle rates for hyperscalers, neoclouds and their lenders. The **5 November AI-infrastructure selloff** (FIX −15.1% on 4 Nov, −11.6% on 5 Nov) was triggered by exactly this: panic over data-centre capex durability. FIX's equity is now a **levered proxy on AI capex financing conditions**, not on GDP.

**3. Input-cost inflation — copper and tariffs.** Copper at $14,473/mt is **+45% YoY and +61% since Jan 2025**, compounded by **50% Section 232 tariffs on imported steel, aluminium and copper products** (plus 25% on derivative goods). Copper pipe, wire and cable are core FIX inputs. Gross margin has so far been defended at 24.8% via pass-through and cost-plus contracts — evidence of genuine pricing power — but this is the key margin risk if backlog is executed on fixed-price terms.

**4. Labour costs.** Unemployment at 4.1% and falling, with 22,700 employees in a skilled-trades shortage, means wage inflation above CPI. Labour is FIX's largest cost line.

### Positive

**5. Nominal GDP and nominal spending boom.** Nominal GDP is growing ~6%, and nominal PCE is **+6.3% YoY** ($22,453B vs $21,127B). For a contractor, *nominal* revenue and *nominal* contract values are what matter — and inflation is inflating the top line and backlog dollars directly. The $17.81B backlog is partly a price effect, but the 90% growth is overwhelmingly volume.

**6. Pricing power in an inflationary regime.** Flat 24.8% gross margins *despite* 45% copper inflation is the single best macro evidence in this dataset: FIX can pass through costs. Inflation is a headwind to input costs but **not currently a margin destroyer**.

**7. Aggregate construction is shrinking — FIX is taking share.** Total construction spending is **−2.7% nominal / ~−6% real YoY**. The non-data-centre commercial/institutional market is in a real contraction. FIX's +54% growth against that backdrop is a concentration bet: the company is winning the one vertical that is booming while the rest of its end market deflates. **This is a macro tailwind that is also a macro concentration risk.**

### Neutral

**8. Currency.** ~100% US revenue → **no translation exposure**. Dollar strength (+3.9% in two months) modestly cheapens imported equipment but is offset by tariffs. Effectively a non-issue.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL** (barbell: fundamentals Favourable / valuation environment Unfavourable)

The split is unusually wide and should be stated plainly rather than averaged away:

- **Operating environment: FAVOURABLE.** Nominal growth ~6%, tight labour (but pricing power), record backlog, a Fed hike that is itself a symptom of strong nominal demand, and credit markets still open (334bp HY OAS).
- **Valuation/rate environment: UNFAVOURABLE.** 10Y at 5.13%, 30Y at 5.47%, real policy rate at zero with inflation at a 3-year high, and a Fed that markets believe must hike again. The path of least resistance for long yields is up.
- **Cost environment: UNFAVOURABLE.** Copper +45%, 50% metals tariffs, wage inflation above CPI.

Net: **Neutral, with negative skew.** I would move to outright **Unfavourable** on a sustained 10Y break above ~5.50%, which would both compress FIX's multiple further and start to bite into data-centre project financing.

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**Bear surprise (higher probability of the two, ~35%): a disorderly long-end repricing into a policy error.**
Because the real fed funds rate is ~0% with CPI at 3.82%, the Fed has little room to look through inflation. If the December/January FOMC hikes again and long-end term premium keeps widening, the 10Y breaks 5.50–6.00%. The transmission to FIX is brutal and non-linear: (a) the multiple compresses from the low-20s toward the mid-teens as the market reclassifies FIX from "AI growth" to "cyclical contractor," and (b) more importantly, **the marginal data-centre project stops clearing its hurdle rate**, backlog conversion slows, and the $17.81B backlog gets questioned rather than capitalised. HY OAS at 334bp has 170bp+ of room to widen before it even reaches median — the credit market has not yet voted. **A FIX re-test of the October low (~$1,160) or below would be the first sign.**

**Bull surprise (~25%): the Fed's September hike was the peak, and inflation rolls over in H1 2027.**
Tariff base effects lap, copper mean-reverts from $14,473, and the 10Y falls back to ~4.50%. FIX re-rates on a $17.8B backlog with earnings up 68% — a violent snap-back from ~22x toward the low-30s. This is a *pure multiple* trade; no fundamental improvement is needed. Note that FIX's own buyback expansion (announced 17 Nov, authorising up to 1,000,000 shares) provides modest support.

**The asymmetry to watch:** FIX's earnings are near-certainly fine for the next four quarters — the backlog is contracted. The *entire* investment debate is macro: **the discount rate and the durability of AI capex financing.** Those are the same variable.
