### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**#1 — UAE sovereign concentration as an export-control chokepoint. Probability: High. Impact: High.**
MBZUAI (62%) and G42 (24%) were 86% of FY2025 revenue. This is the same relationship that triggered a **CFIUS review that delayed the IPO in 2024** (Reuters, 8 Oct 2024: "Cerebras likely to postpone IPO due to CFIUS review delay over G42 deal"). G42 holds a 1% stake ($40m) plus a convertible purchase agreement for 22.9m shares — customer, shareholder and JV partner in one sovereign-backed entity. Any re-tightening of US–UAE AI policy directly hits the P&L. Tailwind: UAE was reclassified to **Country Group A:5 effective 10 July 2026** (BIS/STR Trade) and G42 received US approval for advanced chip exports — but export policy has reversed twice in three years.

**#2 — Third-country diversion / end-use compliance on the MBZUAI contract. Probability: Medium. Impact: Very High.**
The 10-Q filed 12 Nov 2026 attaches **Amendment No. 1 to the MBZUAI purchase order (Ex-10.1, signed 18 Sep 2026)**, which states the Training+Inference Cluster is:
- "**not intended to be deployed, operated or otherwise used by MBZUAI in the United States**";
- "**not intended to be supplied... shipped through, imported into, or made available for use or enjoyment in, the United Arab Emirates**";
- delivered to a **redacted storage facility `[***]`**, with title and risk transferring on delivery;

and that "**Cerebras shall assist... by serving as exporter of record**." A US exporter shipping advanced AI compute for a UAE sovereign-endowed university to a jurisdiction that is explicitly neither the US nor the UAE is a textbook diversion/end-use-monitoring exposure, carrying potential BIS administrative penalties, Entity List designation risk, and reputational contagion. This is the single most important item in the file and warrants direct management questioning on the identity of the end-user and destination.

**#3 — Gulf regional conflict spillover. Probability: Medium. Impact: Medium–High.**
The UAE **suspended all trade, commercial and financial transactions with Iran on 19 Aug 2026** after renewed Iranian fire (AP, Aug 2026); reporting also indicates the $30bn **Stargate UAE initiative is being decentralized "following regional security concerns."** Cerebras' stated UAE/Stargate deployment and its Core42 (G42) partnership sit directly in the blast radius, as does the credit quality of a UAE counterparty whose budget is oil- and sovereign-wealth-dependent.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Copyright class action — *James v. Cerebras Systems*, No. 3:25-cv-09361 (N.D. Cal., Judge Martínez-Olguín), filed 30 Oct 2025.** Active. Alleges Cerebras downloaded the **Books3 dataset via the private tracker Bibliotik** to pre-train Cerebras-GPT, pleaded as **willful** infringement under 17 U.S.C. §501, on behalf of a class of US copyright holders. Cerebras **answered the First Amended Complaint on 9 July 2026**; named plaintiffs Farnsworth and Morgenstein filed a **voluntary dismissal of their claims on 5 Nov 2026 (ordered 6 Nov 2026)** — a partial, not global, resolution. Willful statutory damages (up to $150k/work) make the tail large even at low probability.
- **Securities litigation risk — escalating.** Post-IPO investigations opened by **Kaplan Fox** (26 Jun 2026, after the CFO's Q1 FY2026 capex commentary), **Block & Leviton**, and **Levi & Korsinsky** (5 Nov 2026, explicitly targeting "**processor durability risk**"). No filed complaint was confirmed in the sources reviewed, but the stock is ~41% below its $185 IPO price and down ~33% in a 30-day window — a filing is the base case.
- **CFIUS / related-party governance.** Prior CFIUS scrutiny of G42; G42 equity and convertible overhang creates ongoing foreign-investment-review sensitivity.
- **Regulatory perimeter.** BIS export licensing is the operative regulator (not the FTC/SEC) for revenue recognition. Newly public: no 10-K yet, 107+ filings since 2024, heavy Form 4 activity including an officer's $24.8m proposed sale (Form 144, Sep 2026).

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **🚩 Redacted delivery location + dual negative covenants (not-US, not-UAE)** in the MBZUAI amendment, with Cerebras as exporter of record. Unresolved diversion question.
2. **🚩 Related-party sovereign concentration** — G42 is simultaneously equityholder, JV partner (Condor Galaxy/Core42) and customer. FCPA/AML-adjacent; heightened due-diligence expectation for state-linked counterparties.
3. **🚩 UAE as a sanctions-evasion jurisdiction of concern** — multi-year US/allied pressure over Russian dual-use re-exports, gold smuggling from conflict zones, and the August 2026 Iran trade freeze. Counterparty and freight-forwarder screening is the weak link.
4. **🚩 Training-data provenance** (Books3/Bibliotik) — a data-lawfulness problem, not merely a damages problem; also raises EU AI Act / privacy exposure as sales shift to Europe.
5. **⚠️ Disclosure immaturity** — no audited 10-K, one quarter of clean public reporting, and customer identities partly redacted in material contracts.

**Mitigants:** mix is genuinely improving — US revenue 29% → 62% YoY, OpenAI at 34% of Q3'26 revenue ($72.4m) on a >$20bn / 750MW master agreement, plus Microsoft, Oracle Cloud and AWS wins, and a $26.1bn RPO. Concentration risk is being diluted, not eliminated.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Justified by (i) 45% current / 86% FY2025 revenue from UAE sovereign-linked entities, (ii) an unresolved third-country delivery arrangement on the flagship UAE contract with the company acting as exporter of record, (iii) an active willful-copyright class action plus three announced securities investigations, and (iv) a Gulf conflict backdrop that has already interrupted UAE–Iran trade. Trajectory is *improving* on geography but *deteriorating* on litigation. The rating would move to Medium if UAE-linked revenue fell below ~25% with clean disclosed delivery destinations, and no securities complaint survived a motion to dismiss.

**Key gaps:** No 10-K yet; no itemized non-US geographic revenue split beyond US vs. total; delivery destination redacted; no confirmed securities complaint on file; Taiwan/TSMC sourcing inferred rather than documented in the filings reviewed.
