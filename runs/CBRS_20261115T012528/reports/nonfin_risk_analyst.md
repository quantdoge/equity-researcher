## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Cerebras Systems Inc. (NASDAQ: CBRS)

**Snapshot:** IPO 2026-05-14 (opened $350, first-day close $311.07); last close $108.72 on 2026-11-13 — **~65% below the first-day close and ~69% below the opening print**. Market cap $24.6B, 708 employees, beta 1.85. Q3 2026 GAAP revenue $210.8M (+55%), core revenue $230.9M (+70%), GAAP operating margin **(106.3%)**. The gap between the operating loss and "core profitability" is $155.1M of stock comp plus $36.4M of customer warrant amortization — a disclosure-quality issue in its own right.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

Evidence:

- **Extreme voting concentration.** Three-class structure: Class A (1 vote), Class B (**20 votes**), Class N (non-voting). Class B held ~89.6–99.2% of voting power post-IPO. Founder-CEO Andrew Feldman personally holds ~14.04M Class B shares (~9.2% economic). A ~9% economic holder controls the company. Minority shareholders have effectively no franchise.
- **Classified (staggered) board** established in the Restated Certificate — three classes, only a third up for election annually. Combined with the dual class, this is a near-impregnable entrenchment structure and materially delays any shareholder remediation of the problems below.
- **First post-IPO quarter produced a 19.6% single-day collapse** ($44.46 decline to $182.26 on 2026-06-24) on a $14M revenue miss and guidance that core gross margin would fall to 36–38%. Selling shareholders eight weeks earlier were not told margins would compress to that level — this is the substance of the litigation wave.
- **Capital structure / promotional optics.** Culper Research alleges private-round investors paid a weighted-average ~$23/share — roughly 79% below where public investors bought. Directed share program was in the S-1.
- **Board refresh:** new directors and a CFO (Bob Komin, appointed Mar 2024, i.e. pre-IPO) were added — reasonable, but independence is diluted by founder control and 20:1 votes. Chair/CEO separation could not be confirmed (gap).

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **HIGH and rising**

- **No SEC/DOJ enforcement action** appears in EDGAR full-text search — the formal enforcement docket is currently clean. This is the one genuinely reassuring data point.
- **Securities litigation wave (7+ firms):** Pomerantz, Block & Leviton, Glancy Prongay & Wolke, Levi & Korsinsky, Kaplan Fox, Howard G. Smith, Frank R. Cruz, and SBS Law/Schall Brown & Schwartz are all investigating or soliciting claimants. Two distinct triggers: (i) the 2026-06-24 Q2 disclosure/margin collapse, and (ii) the 2026-10-28 Culper Research short report. A consolidated class action and likely SEC informal inquiry are the base case; formal subpoena risk is meaningful.
- **Export controls / Middle East exposure — the most underappreciated item.** Exhibit 10.1 to the 11/12/2026 10-Q is Amendment No. 1 to the MBZUAI purchase order (signed 2026-09-18). It restructures the order so the training+inference cluster is delivered to a **redacted third-country storage facility**, is explicitly *not* for use in the US *or the UAE*, and has **Cerebras acting as exporter of record**. This is a transaction deliberately engineered around deemed-export and end-use rules. Any diversion finding, or a BIS rule change on advanced AI compute to Gulf/third-country routing, is a direct revenue and criminal/civil liability event. Note G42's prior Entity List history (2022 listing, later removed) and that G42 has also been a Cerebras investor — a customer-shareholder overlap.
- **Pending approvals:** none identified (no FDA/FCC-type gates), but export licence dependency is a de facto approval regime.
- **Data privacy/IP:** no material GDPR/CCPA action surfaced; not a leading risk here.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Product physics / warranty exposure (the Culper thesis).** Culper alleges the wafer-scale engine has a functional field life of only **1.5–2.5 years**, versus company statements, and notes the disclosed **1–2 year warranty** overlaps that failure window. If true, the entire customer ROI model — and warranty reserves, and the $26.1B RPO — are mis-stated. This is the single highest-severity allegation in the file because it attacks the product, not the accounting.
2. **Customer concentration of ~79% in three counterparties, two of them Abu Dhabi-linked.** Q3 2026: **MBZUAI 38%, OpenAI 34%, G42 7%**. (Q3 2025 was MBZUAI 70% / G42 14%.) US revenue improved to 62% from 29%, but ~38% of revenue sits in a single Gulf academic entity and its ecosystem, under export-control scrutiny. Losing or repricing MBZUAI removes over a third of revenue overnight.
3. **Execution and key-person risk.** The CEO publicly conceded on 2026-11-13 that "we are behind schedule" on data-center builds, with portions of the OpenAI contract delayed. The model requires 700MW of contracted capacity by end-2027 — a permitting/power/construction task far outside this company's core competence. Hardware revenue actually **fell 28.8% YoY** while cloud carried growth. With only 708 employees, dependency on Feldman and CTO Lie (both selling) is acute. Sole-source wafer supply from TSMC at 5nm with advanced packaging is an unhedged single point of failure.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- Active short-seller campaign (Culper Research, 2026-10-28) framing the core product as degrading faster than disclosed — a technically specific, hard-to-dismiss thesis that has already driven coverage in DataCenterDynamics and trade press.
- A 69% drawdown from the IPO open plus blanket plaintiff-firm advertising ("Did you lose money on CBRS?") has made the ticker a retail-loss symbol. Search results are now dominated by law-firm notices rather than product news.
- Countervailing positives: Q3 beat with Microsoft and Oracle signed as customers, guidance raised, stock +11–13% on 2026-11-13, and a $26.1B RPO. Reputation with *customers* looks intact; reputation with *public-market investors* is damaged.
- ESG/social controversy: low. The Gulf sovereign-capital association carries governance and human-rights-adjacent screening overhang for some institutional mandates.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical — there is no confirmed SEC enforcement action, no restatement, no auditor change, no going-concern language, and Q3 delivered a genuine commercial inflection. But this is a *high-risk* High: governance is structurally unaccountable, the fraud allegations go to product physics rather than mere guidance, insiders have monetized >$400M while retail holds the drawdown, and nearly 40% of revenue runs through an export-controlled Gulf counterparty under a contract that was explicitly re-papered to route hardware to an undisclosed third country.

**What would tip this to Critical:** an SEC subpoena or formal order, a 10-Q/10-K restatement or warranty-reserve charge, loss/repricing of MBZUAI, a BIS action or rule change touching the delivery structure, or CFO/CTO departure.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Exhibit 10.1 export-control structuring** — Cerebras as exporter of record, delivery to a redacted storage facility, explicit exclusion of US and UAE end-use. Escalate to counsel: potential deemed-export/diversion exposure and a reputational event if reported.
2. **Culper's 1.5–2.5 year chip-lifespan claim vs. the 1–2 year warranty** — requires independent technical diligence and a check of warranty reserves in the 10-Q. If reserves are immaterial, the disclosure is either right or the reserve is understated; one of those is a problem.
3. **CEO sold $55.2M and CTO sold ~$7M into the decline; zero insider buys** — with the lock-up fully open since 2026-11-09, expect continued supply and interpret any further executive sales as a negative signal on 2027 guidance credibility.
4. **79% revenue concentration (MBZUAI 38% + OpenAI 34% + G42 7%)** with MBZUAI also sitting inside a related-party/sovereign orbit (G42 has been both customer and investor).
5. **$26.1B RPO where only 33% is due within 24 months** — a backlog metric being used to support a $24.6B market cap, against 700MW of capacity that management admits is behind schedule. Delivery slippage converts backlog into litigation.
6. **GAAP-to-"core" bridge of $191.5M** in a single quarter (SBC + customer warrant amortization booked against revenue). Warrant amortization netted against revenue flatters the gross margin line specifically — verify the presentation.

**Gaps:** board independence ratios, chair/CEO separation, audit-committee composition, and any 8-K Item 4.01/4.02 history were not directly verified; the 10-K has not yet been filed (first annual report pending), so risk-factor language is only available at 10-Q depth.
