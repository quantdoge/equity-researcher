# Cerebras Systems Inc. (CBRS) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price $108.72 (Nov 13, 2026) | Market cap ~$24.6B | EV ~$18.7B | IPO'd May 14, 2026 at $185**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: Narrow — and narrowing**

Cerebras builds the Wafer-Scale Engine (WSE), a single chip spanning an entire silicon wafer, sold as rack-scale systems plus a fast-inference cloud. The engineering is genuinely differentiated: massive on-chip SRAM and memory bandwidth give real latency advantages for inference versus GPU clusters. That is a *technical* advantage. A moat requires more.

The evidence against durable pricing power is stark:

| Metric | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | Q1'26 | Q2'26 | Q3'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 11.7% | 33.5% | 42.3% | 39.0% | 44.6% | 14.2% | **20.0%** |
| Operating margin | −726% | −170% | −35% | −28.5% | −7.8% | −265% | −106% |

A business with a true moat does not watch gross margin fall from 42% to 20% in eighteen months. The cause is mix: cloud/inference services revenue hit $141.9M in Q3 (+265% YoY) and now dominates, and that revenue carries data-center depreciation and power — neocloud economics, not semiconductor economics. Q3 gross profit was just $42.1M on $210.8M of revenue.

**Customer concentration is the single largest quality defect.** Q3'26 revenue: MBZUAI 38%, OpenAI 34%, G42 7% — roughly 79% from three counterparties. FY2025 was worse (two UAE-affiliated entities ≈86%). Concentration has *rotated*, not disappeared. And the counterparties are not blue-chip credits: MBZUAI is a sovereign-funded UAE university, and OpenAI is simultaneously a $20B customer, a ~$1B investor, and a potential competitor — circular revenue of exactly the kind that should make a conservative analyst uncomfortable.

**Moat verdict:** Narrow, driven by one architectural bet against Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem — the strongest network-effect moat in technology. Switching costs for customers are low (they multi-source), and Cerebras must spend billions on its own data centers to win inference workloads. That is a capital-consuming advantage, not a capital-light one.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **2 / 10**

- **Revenue:** $510M FY25 (+75.7%); FY26 guidance ~$860M core; FY27 consensus ~$2.81B. Growth is real and exceptional — this is the one unambiguously good number.
- **Profitability is an accounting artifact.** FY25 "net income" of +$237.8M coexists with an operating *loss* of −$145.3M. EBIT of +$244.9M vs. operating income of −$145.3M implies ~$390M of non-operating income (fair-value marks on preferred/warrant liabilities, interest income). The 265x P/E is therefore meaningless.
- **Free cash flow is deeply negative and worsening:** FY25 −$392.8M; 9M'26 −$1,435M (capex $1,046M). Q3 alone burned $839M of FCF.
- **Owner earnings:** −$110M in FY25, and that flatters reality (it nets a non-cash gain against capex).
- **Balance sheet:** Post-IPO, this is the saving grace. Cash and investments $7.36B, debt $1.45B → net cash ~$5.9B. Equity swung from −$579M to +$9.16B. No goodwill. But at Q3's burn rate, that cash is roughly two years of runway — before funding 700 gross megawatts of contracted data-center capacity. Another raise is likely.
- **Dilution:** Stock-based comp was $155M in Q3 alone ($587M across 9M'26). Share count went 215M → 239M diluted in six months.

**Score: 2/10.** Excellent top-line growth, but negative FCF conversion, collapsing gross margin, negative owner earnings, and heavy dilution. Only the cash pile earns points.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

The DCF tool returns a negative intrinsic value (it extrapolates trailing negative FCF), which is the correct signal: **there is no defensible intrinsic value from current cash flows.** Valuation must be scenario-based on future economics.

Current: EV $18.7B = **21.7x FY26E sales ($860M)**, 6.7x FY27 consensus ($2.81B), and — most tellingly — **~111x annualized Q3 gross profit ($168M)**. Peer median P/E is 33x versus Cerebras's 265x.

| Scenario (2030 exit) | Revenue | Gross margin | 2030 EV | Equity value | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** — backlog converts, margin recovers, 700MW monetized | $10B | 35% | ~$36B | ~$38B | **~$160** |
| **Base** — solid execution, structural 25–28% GM | $5B | 28% | ~$20B | ~$22B | **~$90** |
| **Bear** — OpenAI renegotiates, UAE exposure constrained, GM stays ~20%, further dilution | $2.5B | 20% | ~$6B | ~$8B | **~$35** |

Probability-weighted (25/50/25): **~$91 per share**, versus $108.72 today. The stock is roughly 15–20% *above* my estimate of intrinsic value. Put differently: **the current price already embeds something close to the bull case.** The $25–26B RPO is impressive, but backlog is not cash flow — it is a promise to deliver capacity that must be built with capital Cerebras does not yet have, at margins it has not yet demonstrated.

At a 25–30% margin-of-safety discount to ~$91, I would need a price near **$65–68** to be interested.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

- **Founder-led:** Andrew Feldman (CEO) has built and sold chip companies before; technically credible and mission-driven. Founder-led is a plus in my book.
- **Capital allocation is the problem.** Committing to 700MW of data-center capacity by end-2027 converts a fabless-style chip story into a capital-intensive infrastructure story, and the $417M share repurchase in Q2'26 — while burning cash and with a negative book value — is indefensible.
- **Related-party complexity:** G42/AI71 investment, CFIUS history, and MBZUAI revenue create governance and geopolitical overhang.
- **Data gap:** I could not verify insider ownership percentages or dual-class structure within budget. Insiders are almost certainly well-aligned post-IPO, but I treat this as unverified.

**Assessment: technically strong, capital-allocation discipline unproven.** The repurchase and the debt-funded capacity build are yellow-to-red flags.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

This fails my test on three independent counts:

1. **Outside the circle of competence** — the terminal economics of AI inference silicon in 2032 are not predictable. Nvidia's ecosystem, custom ASICs from hyperscalers, and pricing wars make 5–10 year cash flows unknowable.
2. **No economic moat with pricing power** — a 20% gross margin in a demand boom is proof that Cerebras is a price-taker, not a price-setter.
3. **No margin of safety** — at $108.72 the market already pays for the bull case. My probability-weighted intrinsic value is ~$91, and the base case is ~$90.

The business is impressive and the revenue growth is real. But great growth is not the same as a great investment, and "a business that trades at a fair price is not a buy" — this one trades above a *generous* fair price. I would revisit only on a substantial decline toward the mid-$60s, or after two to three years of demonstrated positive gross-margin expansion and FCF inflection.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (i.e., to the Sell call)

- **Backlog conversion shock:** $25–26B of RPO could produce revenue far above my $5B base case; OpenAI's 750MW commitment is the swing factor.
- **Gross margin inflection:** If cloud scale drives gross margin back to 35–40% rather than my 25–28%, my base case value roughly doubles.
- **New logos:** Microsoft reportedly signed on; broader hyperscaler adoption would reduce concentration and re-rate the equity.
- **Short squeeze dynamics:** Beta 1.85, 65% off the IPO close, and a heavily-shorted, headline-driven float. The stock can move violently against a fundamentally sound Sell thesis.
- **Takeout optionality:** A strategic buyer (or sovereign fund) could pay for the technology irrespective of near-term FCF.
