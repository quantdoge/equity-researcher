# Cerebras Systems (CBRS) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $108.72 | Mkt cap $23.4B | EV $22.6B | Nov 13, 2026**

## 1. Growth Story

Cerebras is the only credible architectural challenger to NVIDIA in AI compute: its Wafer-Scale Engine puts an entire silicon wafer on one die, eliminating the networking bottleneck that throttles GPU clusters on memory-bound inference. The company has converted that engineering edge into a revenue ramp (FY25 $510M → FY27E ~$2.85B) and, critically, a **$26.1B RPO backlog** — 28x current annual revenue — anchored by OpenAI, AWS, Oracle, G42/sovereign AI, and a new **Microsoft hyperscaler deal signed in October with revenue starting Q1'27**. The stock is 41% below its $185 IPO price and 58% off its August peak, so the growth is now priced at **3.0x 2028E sales** — a valuation normally reserved for 25%-growth hardware.

## 3. TAM and Penetration

NVIDIA alone did $215.9B of revenue in FY26 (+65%) — that is the floor for the AI compute TAM, not the ceiling. Inference is the fastest-growing slice as frontier-model usage scales, and Cerebras' differentiation (tokens/sec, no HBM dependency, 44GB on-chip SRAM) targets exactly that workload plus sovereign AI. We size a serviceable inference/systems SAM of **$100–150B by 2028**; $7.6B of consensus 2028 revenue implies only **5–7% SAM penetration**. Current penetration is ~0.4% of NVIDIA's revenue base. The constraint is not market size — it is capacity, capital, and customer concentration.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4'26 results (~Feb 2027):** first test of the 47–49% gross-margin guide at $310–330M scale. Delivering >47% GM alongside +90% growth would re-rate the multiple hard.
- **Microsoft revenue starting Q1'27** (deal signed Oct 2026) — first proof that the backlog diversifies beyond OpenAI/UAE.
- **Q1'27 quarterly step-up toward the $2.85B FY27 plan** (~$600M+ quarters). This is the single biggest execution hurdle.
- **Financing announcement:** CEO has said the company is "evaluating financing options to meet ramping demand." A clean strategic/sovereign capital raise removes the overhang; a large equity print at $109 is dilutive.
- **Post-lockup supply clearing:** 180-day lockup expired Nov 9–10 (~19.4M shares), with CEO/COO insider sales already reported. Overhang absorption plus potential index/coverage expansion is a mechanical tailwind into 2027.

## 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **RPO $26.1B (+28x TTM revenue):** the strongest forward indicator available — contracted demand that vastly exceeds anything in the model.
- **Google Trends ("Cerebras"):** 100 in IPO week (May 10, 2026) → 4–9 range from September onward, ticking to 9 into the print. Interpretation: speculative froth has fully washed out while baseline brand interest holds. No organic demand collapse, but no consumer-led re-acceleration either (it's an enterprise/sovereign sale).
- **Headcount/hiring:** Indeed shows only ~29–79 US openings against 708 FTEs, with revenue/employee ~$1.3M. Capacity is being added through capex and datacenters, not labor — positive for operating leverage, but it also means the P&L is increasingly a depreciation story.
- **Tape:** Nov 5 capitulation low of $81.71 on 16.4M shares, then Nov 13 reversal on **36.9M shares (~7x average)** after the beat — a classic washout-plus-accumulation signature off a –58% drawdown.
- **Negative alt-signal:** October reports that OpenAI chose NVIDIA GPUs for the "Ultrafast" mode of GPT-6.1 Sol. With OpenAI at **34% of revenue**, that is the clearest evidence yet of displacement risk in the core account.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY**

This is a genuine frontier-innovation franchise with a defensible architecture, a $26B contracted backlog, three hyperscalers signed in twelve months, and gross margins inflecting from 39% to 47–49% — now trading at **24x FY26E sales, 7.9x FY27E and 3.0x FY28E**, against AMD at ~25x *trailing* sales on 34% growth and a 10% operating margin. On a growth-adjusted basis Cerebras is the cheapest high-growth asset in AI compute. At 12.4x consensus 2028 EPS, the market is implying roughly a 40–50% haircut to the plan; even a half-sized outcome supports a materially higher price. We are not at Strong Buy because core growth decelerated to +70% in Q3, the 2027 guide merely matched consensus, and the largest customer just gave a workload to NVIDIA. **Accumulate on weakness; size for 30–40% drawdown tolerance.**

## 7. Key Risks

- **Customer concentration (highest):** OpenAI ~34% of revenue with demonstrated willingness to route flagship workloads to NVIDIA; G42/sovereign AI has historically dominated the book. A single account loss breaks the 2027 model.
- **Capital intensity and dilution:** FY25 FCF –$393M on $383M capex, negative book value, and management openly shopping financing. Growth capex converts to margin only if utilization holds; equity issued at $109 versus a $185 IPO is expensive capital.
- **Margin structure:** 39% FY25 gross margin vs NVIDIA's 71% reflects a hardware-heavy mix. If cloud/owned-datacenter revenue dominates, depreciation could cap margins in the high-40s rather than the 60s the 2028 model may require.
- **Competitive moat erosion:** CUDA's software lock-in, NVIDIA's inference-specific roadmap, hyperscaler in-house ASICs (TPU, Trainium, MTIA), and AMD. A leadership vacuum at NVIDIA (interim CEO) cuts both ways — opportunity for share shift, but also potential aggressive pricing.
- **Execution/scale risk:** ramping $940M → $7.6B in two years is an 8x, requiring TSMC wafer-scale supply, packaging, and datacenter buildout to land on schedule. Any slip compresses a 24x sales multiple violently.
- **IPO-class volatility:** 1.85 beta, no price history beyond six months, negative tangible book, and heavy post-lockup insider supply. Position sizing matters more here than the thesis.
