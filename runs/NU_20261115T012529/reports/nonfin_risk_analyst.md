# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Nu Holdings Ltd. (NYSE: NU)

**Snapshot:** Digital bank, Brazil-domiciled (Cayman Islands incorporated), $63.9B market cap, 140M+ customers, 8,037 FTEs, ~90%+ of revenue from Brazil. Stock $13.22, down from ~$16.17 in October 2026.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (with concentrated-control weakness)

**Evidence supporting:**
- Board of 9 directors, majority independent; Audit & Risk Committee has three members (Rogério Paulo Calderón Peres — Chair, Anita Sands, Thuan Quang Pham), all with relevant financial/technology depth. Annual director elections; Aug 6, 2026 AGM re-elected the full slate and ratified the 2025 20-F with 99.88% support.

**Evidence against (material):**
- **Control is not contestable.** Dual-class structure: Class A = 1 vote, Class B = 20 votes. Founder **David Vélez holds ~88% of Class B and ~74% of voting power**; co-founder Cristina Junqueira holds ~9.9%. The AGM's 99.88% approval is arithmetically meaningless as an accountability signal.
- **CEO/Chairman duality** confirmed: Vélez is "Founder, Chairman of our Board of Directors and our Chief Executive Officer." No independent chair, no lead-independent-director counterweight disclosed.
- **Disclosure architecture is thinner than US peers.** Foreign private issuer and Cayman incorporation: files 20-F/6-K, not 10-K/10-Q; **no DEF 14A proxy exists** (confirmed — the filing tool returns none). Quarterly granularity, proxy-level compensation detail, and say-on-pay are all absent. The company is also exempt from several NYSE corporate-governance standards.
- **Compensation scale watch item:** October 2026 Form 4 activities report a Class A award of ~13.6M shares (~$200M notional) to Vélez — a very large founder equity package that deserves dilution and alignment scrutiny.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**US/SEC: clean.** EDGAR full-text enforcement search for NU returns **zero enforcement actions**; no DOJ/class-action securities litigation surfaced in searches. (Caveat: as an FPI, US enforcement tail risk is structurally lower — the real regulator is Brazilian.)

**Brazil — structural re-regulation underway:**
- The BCB ended the **"asymmetric regulation"** that allowed non-bank fintechs light prudential treatment; Nubank is now **required to hold a full banking license**. Resolved via the acquisition of **Banco Porto Real** (6-K, July 20, 2026), with management stating the license adds **no incremental capital or liquidity requirements**. This removes a tail risk but raises supervisory intensity, resolution-planning and governance expectations.
- **Credit-card revolving interest cap** is the single largest regulatory P&L threat. Revolving APRs run ~450% p.a.; Lula has pressured on household debt (82% of households indebted). BCB Governor Galípolo **publicly opposed caps** (Reuters, Mar 26, 2026) but is exploring alternatives. A legislative cap would directly hit NU's core card franchise.
- **Pix:** BCB tightening Pix participant and security/liability rules (effective March 5, 2026). Pix fraud losses reached **R$2.7B, +43% y/y, ~28M cases** — liability allocation is shifting toward institutions.
- **AML:** the Nov 6, 2026 crypto deposit/withdrawal suspension was triggered by an **external AML-provider incident** (Mercado Pago affected simultaneously) — a live supervisory flashpoint on vendor/AML controls.
- Data privacy exposure under Brazil's LGPD, Mexico's LFPDPPP, Colombia's regime; US operations now add CCPA.

**Licensing/approvals (executed, not pending):** Mexico's CNBV granted Nu Mexico a **full banking license, effective July 10, 2026** (upgrade from SOFIPO; US$4.2B investment commitment). Colombia operating as a financing company since 2024.

**Political risk — new and under-priced:** **Lula won the October 2026 election**; NU fell **~17–21%** on the shock. Consensus now sees end-2027 policy rate at 12.50% (from 11.75%) and end-2027 inflation at 4.76%. Fiscal expansion, tax-rise fears and populist credit-relief pressure are direct negatives for a consumer lender concentrated in Brazil.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Key-person and technology-leadership vacuum (highest severity).** CTO **Eric Young resigned Oct 23, 2026** (effective Nov 12). Rather than appointing a successor, **CEO/Chairman Vélez personally took over Global Platforms on an interim basis** — concentrating founder, board, and now CTO authority in one person at a 140M-customer, 8,000-person technology company. This follows the May 2025 COO departure and 2026 Mexico leadership turnover. Bench depth is unproven precisely where NU's entire moat (proprietary cloud-native stack) sits.
2. **Third-party vendor concentration on regulated rails.** The Nov 6, 2026 halt of crypto deposits/withdrawals was caused by an external provider — a single vendor disabling a product line instantly. With hundreds of millions of Pix transactions monthly and ~8,000 employees, supplier concentration in payments, KYC/AML and cloud is an un-hedged single point of failure carrying direct AML supervisory consequences.
3. **Change-control and operational-resilience failure.** On **June 12, 2026, Nubank sent Brazilian customers a false message stating the bank had been liquidated by the central bank**, which it attributed to a "one-time operational error" (Reuters, Bloomberg). In a deposit-funded institution, a mass false insolvency notice is a bank-run trigger and unequivocally a BCB supervisory event. That this passed release controls indicates weak DevOps/communications governance. Compounding: Pix fraud growing ~43% y/y requires continuous loss-absorption.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: ELEVATED

- **Worst-in-class event:** the fake liquidation notice is the single most brand-damaging incident in the company's history, amplified by national and international coverage and public speculation about whether it was error or cyberattack.
- **Politically exposed franchise.** Reuters' Sept 18, 2026 feature ("Some Brazilian voters are feeling burned by fintech revolution") positions neobanks as the cause of household over-indebtedness, citing **neobank credit-card delinquency rising to ~20.3% in 2025 from ~7%**. Nubank, as the category leader and most recognised consumer fintech brand in Brazil, is the natural target for politicians and consumer-defence bodies (Procon) — a scapegoating channel that is now live post-election.
- **Product-trust erosion:** crypto withdrawal suspension compounds a pattern of customers being unable to access their own money.
- **Positives:** strong financial-inclusion narrative, published 2026 ESG report (bilingual, detailed security disclosures), no ESG/labour controversy or discrimination litigation identified, no short-seller accounting/brand report found. Reputation is currently a political asset *and* liability — it cuts both ways.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** four of five sub-domains are elevated — concentrated founder control with 74% voting power and no independent chair; an active Brazilian re-regulation cycle plus a newly hostile political regime; a CTO vacuum filled by a double-hatted CEO; two customer-visible operational failures in six months; and a business model that is the political lightning rod for Brazilian household debt. The amplifier is **structural concentration**: ~90%+ of earnings from one country, one regulatory regime, and one founder.

**Offsetting factors preventing "Critical":** no fraud indicators, no restatement, no whistleblower complaint, no SEC/DOJ action, majority-independent board with a functioning Audit & Risk Committee, successfully executed banking licenses in both Brazil and Mexico, record Q3 2026 net income of $1.1B, and no open-market insider selling.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **CTO resignation with no named successor** and CEO/Chairman assuming Global Platforms — escalate to management; request the permanent search timeline and technology-org succession plan. *Highest priority.*
2. **False "liquidation" customer communication (June 2026)** — request root-cause writeup, remediation evidence, and disclosure of any BCB inquiry, sanction, or remedial supervisory plan. Undisclosed supervisory action here would be material.
3. **Revolving credit cap legislation** — model the downside; this is the largest identifiable non-financial threat to earnings and is currently a political priority for the re-elected government.
4. **74% voting control + Cayman FPI status + no proxy statement** — minority shareholders cannot discipline management; no say-on-pay. Size positions accordingly.
5. **Third-party/AML vendor concentration** (Nov 2026 crypto halt) — request the critical-vendor register, concentration limits, and exit/BCP testing results.
6. **Zero insider open-market buying in a drawdown** alongside a ~$200M founder equity award and a prior 33M-share founder sale — monitor for 10b5-1 adoption or further founder liquidity.
7. **M&A headline risk** — NU moved ~3% on merely ruling out a Monzo deal (Oct 1, 2026). A founder-controlled acquirer with 74% voting power can transact without shareholder approval; cross-border integration would layer new regulatory regimes onto an already stressed ops function.
