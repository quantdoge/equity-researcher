# Nu Holdings (NYSE: NU) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Price $13.22 | Market cap ~$64.9B | 52-wk high $18.98 (Jan-26), -30% from high | 4.91B diluted shares**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Nubank is the largest digital bank in Latin America — 143 million customers across Brazil, Mexico and Colombia — and it is proving that a branchless, 19%-efficiency-ratio operating model can compound revenue at ~35–40% while generating 32% ROE and record profitability ($1.09B net income in Q3'26). The thesis is no longer "customer acquisition" but **ARPAC monetization**: with only ~$18/month revenue per active customer across a 320M-adult footprint it has barely begun cross-selling credit, payroll loans, insurance, investments and marketplace. The 2026 launch of **Nu US** (all 50 states, 3.5% APY) and **Nu Global** (stablecoin multi-currency account, 37 countries) plus Mexico's full banking license extends the TAM beyond LatAm for the first time. The stock has de-rated ~30% from its January high purely on Brazil political/country risk — not on fundamentals, which hit records in the most recent quarter.

## 3. TAM & Penetration

- **Brazil:** ~120M customers = **65% of adults**, activity rate 86.3% (7th consecutive quarterly record). Land-grab is largely over; growth here must come from ARPAC (payroll/consignado loans, Croma ~1M users, NuCoin 6.7M sign-ups, insurance, marketplace).
- **Mexico:** 16.6M customers = **17% of adults**, +27% YoY, 870k added in first quarter as a licensed bank; deposit insurance raised 16x to ~$195k enabled payroll accounts. This is the 2027–2029 growth engine.
- **Colombia:** 6.1M = **15% of adults**, +62% YoY; already the largest card issuer by cardholders (3M) and #4 in individual deposits.
- **New:** Nu US (50 states), Nu Global (stablecoin account, 37 countries), OCC conditional bank charter (Jan-26) — pure optionality, costed at ≤100bps of efficiency ratio in 2026 and 2027.
- **Monetization runway:** ARPAC ~$18/mo. Mexico/Colombia ARPAC is a fraction of Brazil's. Getting group ARPAC to $30 on the existing base adds ~65% revenue with near-zero incremental acquisition cost — this is the core of the TAM argument.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Q4'26 + FY2026 results — Feb 24, 2027.** Watch: FY26 efficiency ratio landing at 19–19.5%, first full quarter of Nu US/Nu Global drag, NPL inflection, and any formal FY27 framework.
2. **Mexico bank ramp (H1'27).** Payroll accounts and the expanded product set in a 100M-adult market; Mexico reaching profitability would re-rate the multiple as the "second Brazil" proof point.
3. **Nu US / Nu Global scaling (through 2027).** US national bank charter conversion and stablecoin-based remittance corridor (US→LatAm) — a genuinely new TAM and the swing factor in any re-rating above $18.
4. **Brazil payroll lending.** Unsecured originations hit a record R$22.1B (+28% YoY); secured origination at a record. Consignado growth offsets FGTS runoff and supports NIM.
5. **Brazil policy window.** Lula's victory triggered the Oct-26 country-risk selloff (NU -17% in a day) and revived fears of credit-card revolving-rate caps. Clarity on the regulatory agenda is a binary catalyst in either direction.

## 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Google Trends (Brazil, 5-yr):** "Nubank" interest is **flat-to-rising at 48–77** with no post-election decay; the only spike to 100 was Jan-2026. Brand demand is not deteriorating despite a 30% drawdown — consistent with a macro/political, not company-specific, de-rating.
- **Engagement:** activity rate up to 83.6% globally and 86.3% in Brazil (7 straight records) — the highest-quality growth signal the company publishes.
- **Hiring:** job postings include *Senior Software Engineer (Institutional, USDC)* and US-focused engineering roles — confirms the stablecoin/US build-out is staffed, not slideware.
- **Volume/tape:** capitulation volumes (339M shares on Oct-26; 145–165M on other gap-downs) followed by recovery to $13.22 suggests sellers were event-driven, not fundamental.
- **Estimate revisions:** 2029 EPS consensus up **+27.9%** (revision tool flags "upgrade"); UBS raised its PT to $20.50 in Oct-26. Offset by June-26 downgrades (Scotiabank, Susquehanna to Neutral, $13) and BofA's Underperform/$10 after the CFO transition.
- **Earnings surprises:** beat rate only **42.9%** (avg +0.74%); 3 of the last 4 quarters were slight EPS misses despite record profit — guidance is now set tightly/optimistically rather than conservatively. A genuine negative change in guidance behavior.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (high conviction; Strong Buy on Q4 confirmation)

Fundamentals and price have fully decoupled. Nubank is compounding ~37% FXN revenue at a 19.3% efficiency ratio with 32% ROE, and is available at **15x 2026E earnings, 12x 2027E, 2.8x sales, PEG ~0.55**. Consensus PT is ~$18 (+36%); my 12-month fair value is **$18–20** (20x FY27E EPS of $1.10 = $22; 3.0x FY27 sales ≈ $17.7). The discount is a Brazil political-risk premium, not an execution discount — a rational entry for a 3–5 year growth holder, sized for macro volatility.

## 7. Key Risks

- **Brazil policy/regulatory (highest):** Lula's win reintroduced country-risk and the credit-card revolving-interest-cap threat — the same fear that drove the -19% Feb-25 gap and the Oct-26 crash. Direct hit to the highest-yield Brazilian card book.
- **Credit normalization:** 90+ NPL rising (6.9% → 7.3%), risk-adjusted NIM compressing; rapid payroll/consignado expansion into public-sector borrowers is unseasoned. Coverage of 234% is a mitigant, not a guarantee.
- **CFO transition:** Guilherme Lago's departure triggered BofA's downgrade to Underperform ($10 PT). Nu has never managed a CFO handoff in public markets.
- **Brazil saturation / deceleration:** 65% of adults and 86.3% activity means Brazilian ARPAC expansion (not customer adds) must carry growth; sequential IFRS revenue growth slowed to ~+1.5% in Q3'26.
- **New-market drag & dilution of model:** Nu US/Nu Global cost up to 100bps of efficiency in each of 2026–27; stablecoin regulation is unsettled.
- **FX & concentration:** USD-reported, BRL-earned; ~80% of revenue still Brazil. A renewed BRL selloff mechanically hits EPS and the multiple simultaneously.
- **Competition:** Mercado Pago, Inter, PicPay, Itaú's digital push in Brazil; Revolut/Stori in Mexico.
- *Data caveats:* consensus FY2028 ($34.5B) and FY2029 ($30.9B) revenue appear internally inconsistent (2029 below 2028) — I have relied on FY26/27 estimates; the revision tool compares the FY29 estimate to the FY28 prior estimate, so treat its "+28%" as directional only.
