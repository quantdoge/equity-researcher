## SECTOR BRIEF — Nu Holdings (NU) | Brazilian Digital Banking & Consumer Credit

**Data note:** NU reports in USD; Itaú/Bradesco/PagSeguro report in BRL. Margin ratios are currency-neutral and directly comparable; growth rates are affected by FX (BRL ≈ 5.34/USD) and by Brazilian nominal inflation (~5%). FX used for P/E cross-comparison. FMP tags PAGS under "Industrials" — it is a Brazilian payments/digital bank and is retained as a peer.

---

### 1. Sector Overview

NU competes in Brazilian (and increasingly Mexican/Colombian) retail banking and consumer credit — a large, highly concentrated market where the credit stock is growing ~8.3% nominal (Febraban, Sep-2026), i.e. only ~3% real, and the top-5 incumbents (Itaú, Bradesco, BB, Caixa, Santander Brasil) still hold the large majority of deposits and credit. The binding macro variable is the Selic, held at **13.75%** on 4-Nov-2026 after Copom ended its easing run post-election, with end-2026 consensus revised up to ~13.50% and a weaker real (5.34/USD). That is a restrictive, late-cycle policy stance: it keeps funding costs and provisioning high while suppressing new lending (−3.2% expected in new originations). Structurally, however, the sector is mid-displacement — digital channels are taking share from branch-heavy incumbents, with digital financial institutions estimated to have added a ~R$1.6trn differential to GDP. NU is the share-gainer inside a slow-growing pool.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Digital share shift is still running.** NU grew net revenue 34% YoY in Q3-2026 (US$5.60bn) against sector credit growth of 8.3% — a ~4x share-gain multiple. Incumbent revenue growth (Itaú +18%, Bradesco +25% in BRL) is now mostly nominal/FX/inflation, not volume.
- **Operating leverage from a branchless cost base.** NU carries 8,037 employees versus Itaú's 99,600 and Bradesco's 82,095, producing a 24.4% operating margin and **18.1% net margin vs a peer median of ~10.4%**. Management raised its full-year efficiency outlook on the Q3 call.
- **Credit-mix expansion into higher-yield products.** Credit book +33% YoY with lending margins improving; incremental products (Pix financing, payroll/secured loans, SME accounts, insurance, NuCel mobile) raise revenue per active customer without new customer acquisition cost.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Selic at 13.75% is a direct tax on a deposit-funded lender.** NU's cost of revenue has climbed to **57.8% of revenue in Q3-2026 from 53.4% a year earlier**, compressing gross margin to 42.2% from 46.6% (−448bp YoY). Operating margin fell 260bp over the same span. This is the single clearest pressure point in the model.
- **A renewed credit-quality cycle.** With rates held high and Brazil's fiscal path fragile, unsecured consumer credit — NU's core product — is the most rate-sensitive segment. A slower nominal credit pool (8.3%) means incumbents defend share more aggressively, and personal-loan/credit-card delinquencies are the sector's classic late-cycle failure mode.
- **Concentration, FX and political risk.** NU's earnings are BRL-denominated and reported in USD, so a real drifting to 5.40/USD is a mechanical earnings headwind. Post-election repricing has already hit the stock: NU fell ~17% in a single session (26-Oct-2026) on ~340m shares. Regulatory pressure on interchange fees and Pix-adjacent economics remains a persistent tail risk.

**Two observations that matter most.** First, NU beats the peer median on every profitability line and grows ~2x faster, yet trades at ~16x versus SOFI at ~42x — the "Brazil discount" is roughly 60% versus the closest US analogue. ### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **Growth:** +43.0% FY25, +34.2% YoY Q3'26 — vs 21.9% peer median. CAGR 2022–25 of ~52% off a US$4.5bn base with no acquisition-driven distortion.
- **Profitability:** 18.1% net margin, the highest in the group and ~7.7pp above median; 24.4% operating margin vs 12.1% median. NU has converted from a −6.8% operating margin in FY2022 to a mid-20s margin business in three years — proof the model scales.
- **Scale/moat:** largest digital bank in the region by customers, which on a branchless cost base converts to a structural price advantage incumbents cannot match without cannibalising their own P&L. Switching costs are rising as salary accounts, credit and insurance stack onto the app.
- **Caveat (the honest weak spot):** margin *direction* is negative. Gross margin −448bp YoY and operating margin −260bp YoY says NU is paying up for growth in a 13.75% Selic world. The franchise is strong; the near-term earnings trajectory is decelerating (34% vs 43%) and rate-dependent.
- **Valuation implication:** 16.3x TTM for 34% growth and 18% net margin is undemanding. The market is pricing NU as a Brazil macro bet rather than a compounder — the report set's own framing ("a compounder priced like a Brazil bet") is, on these numbers, fair.

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL** (with a favourable tilt toward digital share-gainers)

The aggregate Brazilian banking sector is late-cycle and rate-constrained: nominal credit growth of 8.3% is barely above inflation, the Selic is stuck at 13.75% after Copom halted easing, and funding costs are rising faster than asset yields for everyone. That is not an "Attractive" backdrop for the sector as a whole — hence Neutral.

But the dispersion inside the sector is enormous, and that is where the return is. A branchless operator growing 34% with an 18% net margin at 16x earnings is a materially better risk/reward than a 6–10x incumbent with single-digit margin and no share momentum, or a 42x US neobank with a 10% margin. The rating is Neutral on Brazil's credit cycle, not on NU's competitive position.

**Key monitorables:** (1) quarterly gross margin — does 42% stabilise or keep sliding; (2) Copom's path beyond 13.50% and any BRL move through 5.40; (3) unsecured NPL formation as the 2025–26 vintage seasons; (4) Mexico/Colombia contribution, the only lever that can re-rate NU off the Brazil multiple.
