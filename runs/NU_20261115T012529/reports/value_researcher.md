# Nu Holdings Ltd. (NYSE: NU) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $13.22 (close 13-Nov-2026) | Market cap ≈ $64.5B | Diluted shares 4,881M**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW — but widening**

Nu is the largest digital bank outside Asia: ~143M customers in Q3'26 (from 54M in 2021), $47.5B deposits, $41.6B credit portfolio, ~8,000 employees serving all of it. The moat evidence is real:

- **Cost advantage (the strongest leg).** A branchless, single-app model gives Nu a structurally lower cost to serve than Incumbents Itaú/Bradesco/Santander Brasil, which carry branch networks and legacy IT. This is a genuine, durable, hard-to-replicate advantage — incumbents cannot shed their cost base without destroying their franchise.
- **Switching costs.** Nu has become the *primary* account for a large cohort: Pix, salary portability, Money Boxes savings, insurance, and increasingly credit. ARPU has more than tripled since 2021 while the customer base grew ~2.6x — that is the signature of deepening relationships, not just user acquisition.
- **Network effects (weak form).** P2P within the app is stickier as penetration rises, but Pix is an open central-bank rail — Nu does not own the network.
- **Intangibles.** Strong brand with younger, underbanked Brazilians; a hard-won Mexican banking licence; a Colombian licence.

**Where the moat is thin:** ~70% of gross revenue is net interest income on unsecured consumer credit — credit cards, personal loans, Pix credit. **Credit is a commodity, and it is where Nu's profit actually comes from.** Deposits and payments are the moat; credit is the monetization and the cyclical exposure. Nu has never run a mature loan book through a full Brazilian credit down-cycle.

**Verdict on moat trend:** widening in customers, deposits and cost position; *narrowing* on the asset-quality dimension (see below). Net: a genuinely good business, not yet a bulletproof one.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **8 / 10**

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | TTM (Q3'26) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 7.67 | 11.10 | 15.88 | 20.81 |
| Revenue growth | +70% | +45% | +43% | +34% |
| Operating margin | 20.1% | 25.2% | 24.4% | ~24% |
| Net income ($B) | 1.03 | 1.97 | 2.87 | 3.92 |
| ROE | 16.1% | 25.8% | 25.3% | **30.5%** |
| ROA | 2.38% | 3.95% | 3.83% | **4.82%** |

**Strengths**
- **Exceptional profitability:** 4.8% ROA and ~30% ROE is elite for any bank, let alone one still compounding revenue at 34%. Return on capital clears any reasonable cost of capital by a wide margin — the hallmark of a moated business.
- **Balance sheet strength:** equity/assets of **16.4%** ($14.3B equity on $87.4B assets) is roughly double the capitalization of a typical Brazilian bank. Net cash position; debt is minimal ($3.0B total).
- **Earnings quality:** no acquisition-driven growth; goodwill is only $1.2B (8% of equity); D&A and capex are trivial. Accounting is clean and conservative on provisioning relative to peers.
- **Capital retention:** 100% of earnings retained and redeployed into a 30% ROE loan book — the single best use of capital available to it. SBC is modest at ~6.6% of net income, though it does dilute ~1%/yr.

**Deductions (why not 9–10)**
- **Asset quality is deteriorating:** 90+ day NPL rose to **7.3%** in Q3'26 (from 6.9% in Q2'26) — the highest in 13 quarters. 15–90 day delinquency at 4.9%, and delinquency measured against interest-earning balances is **12.1%**. Brazilian neobank credit-card delinquency ran ~20% industry-wide in 2025. Cost of risk is the whole ballgame here.
- **NIM of 22.5% is a function of a distressed-rate environment**, not a permanent franchise attribute. Brazilian credit-card rates near 440% are a political target; a revolving-rate cap is a live legislative risk (Fitch flagged it as credit-negative for Brazilian FIs).
- **FCF is not a meaningful metric here.** Reported FCF of $3.49B (122% of net income) is an artifact of deposit and loan-flow working capital. The DCF tool's $112B / ~$23-per-share output is built on that base and should be **discarded**; for a bank, owner earnings ≈ net income, net of whatever equity must be retained to support asset growth. I have valued NU on earnings, not cash flow.
- Growth is decelerating cleanly: 70% → 45% → 43% → 34%.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples:** P/E (TTM) **16.5x** · P/E (FY26E ~$0.85) **15.6x** · P/B **4.5x** (BVPS $2.94) · ROE 30.5% · no dividend.

Note that the headline 22.8x P/E in most screens is stale — it uses FY2025 EPS of $0.58, not the $0.80 TTM run-rate.

**Intrinsic value — three-year earnings-power model** (for a bank, ROE × book and EPS × exit multiple are the right tools):

| Scenario | 2029E EPS | Exit P/E | Value (PV @ 14%) | Prob. |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bull — Mexico/Colombia inflect, ROE holds 30% | $1.65 | 18x | $20.0 | 25% |
| Base — ROE 25–27%, growth decays to mid-teens | $1.46 | 14x | $13.8 | 50% |
| Bear — credit cycle bites, ROE → 17%, growth → 10% | $1.10 | 10x | $7.4 | 25% |

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $13.8–14.0 per share.**

Cross-checks: a justified P/B of 3.2x on ROE 26% / growth 10% / cost of equity 15% yields ~$9.4 on today's book but ~$12–13 on 2028 book — consistent. Forward P/E of 13x on FY2027E EPS of ~$1.04 is not demanding for a 30%-ROE compounder, but neither is it a gift once you price in Brazilian political, currency and credit-cycle risk.

**Current price vs. intrinsic value: $13.22 vs. ~$14.00 = roughly a 5% discount.**

That is *fairly valued to modestly cheap* — emphatically **not** the 20–30% margin of safety my discipline requires. My buy trigger is **~$10.50 or below** (a 25% discount to base-case value), which sits right where BofA's $10 target lands.

**Analyst context:** targets range $10 (BofA, Underperform) to $22 (Susquehanna); the median of the last twelve sits near $18–19, but the *most recent* actions are lower ($20, $20.50, $19) after the October de-rating. Sell-side is polarized, which is what you'd expect at a genuine inflection point.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Mixed — strong operator, two real governance flags.**

**The good:**
- **Founder-led and owner-operator.** David Vélez (CEO/Chairman) retains a large personal stake — his interests are aligned with long-term compounding, and the capital-allocation record is exemplary: zero dividends, zero buybacks, every dollar of the ~$9.7B of accumulated retained earnings plowed back into a 30% ROE loan book. That is exactly the right decision and most managements would not make it.
- **Disciplined M&A.** Growth has been organic. The Mexico and Colombia entries were built, not bought.
- **Execution.** Scaling from 54M to 143M customers while *expanding* operating margin from negative to 24% and ROE to 30% is a rare operational achievement.

**The flags:**
1. **Surprise CFO departure (2026).** Guilherme Lago's exit triggered a BofA downgrade to Underperform with a $10 target. At a high-growth lender where provisioning judgement and funding strategy are the whole game, an unplanned CFO exit is a genuine information event, and management's handling of it was not reassuring.
2. **Founder selling.** Vélez sold 33M shares (~3.5% of his holding) in August 2025, following earlier trims. Defensible as diversification, but it is not a buy signal.
3. **US expansion ambitions.** Nu has signalled interest in entering the US, where it has no cost advantage and competition is fierce. This is the one place where I see empire-building risk rather than rational capital deployment. The stock fell 15.6% in February 2026 largely on this.
4. **Dual-class structure** (Class B super-voting) limits minority influence — standard for founder-led companies, but it means shareholders cannot constrain capital allocation if it drifts.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

**This is a high-quality business trading at a fair price — which, by my standards, is not a buy.**

The bull case is legitimate and I take it seriously: 143M customers, a structurally lower cost base than incumbents it is steadily taking share from, 30% ROE, a fortress balance sheet at 16% equity/assets, 34% revenue growth, and a 16.5x trailing / 15.6x forward multiple. Fifteen years from now, Nu could plausibly be the dominant retail financial institution in Latin America. There is no accounting fraud here, no leverage masquerading as return, no empire-building acquisition spree.

But three things keep me at Hold:
1. **No margin of safety.** ~$14 of value for a $13.22 stock is a 5% discount. I need 25%+.
2. **The credit cycle has not been tested.** 90+ NPLs at a 13-quarter high, 12.1% delinquency on interest-earning balances, and a loan book grown 33% in a year. Fast loan growth plus rising delinquency is the oldest warning sign in banking. My bear case ($7–8) is a live 25% probability, and it is asymmetric to the downside right now.
3. **The risks are exogenous and unpriced.** The 17% one-day fall on 26-Oct-2026 was a Brazilian election/BRL reaction, not a Nu-specific event. Nu's dollar-reported earnings are hostage to Brazilian fiscal policy, the Selic path, and a legislative threat to credit-card revolving rates. I cannot underwrite those, and Buffett's rule is to only play games where I can see the whole table.

**What would make me a buyer:**
- Price **below ~$10.50** (25% discount to base-case value) with the business intact, or
- Evidence of a genuine credit-cycle peak-and-recovery: two to three quarters of *falling* 15–90 day delinquency alongside sustained NIM, which would let me raise the base case and shrink the bear-case probability, or
- Clarity and stabilization in the CFO seat and a credible, costed US strategy.

**What would make me a seller (avoid):**
- 90+ NPLs through 8.5% without a matching NIM offset,
- a legislated credit-card rate cap in Brazil,
- US expansion funded by meaningful equity issuance or acquisition.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Brazilian consumer credit cycle (highest severity).** Unsecured credit to a newly-banked population with no prior credit history through a downturn. Provisions are the swing factor between a 30% ROE and a 17% ROE — and between $14 and $7 per share.
2. **Political / regulatory.** Brazilian elections, fiscal slippage, BRL depreciation (mechanically shrinks USD-reported revenue — Nu reports in dollars), and the persistent risk of a revolving-credit interest-rate cap.
3. **Competition.** Incumbents are digitizing; Mercado Pago, PicPay and Pix itself compress payments economics. Nu's deposit advantage does not protect its credit spreads.
4. **Currency translation.** A pure accounting risk that nonetheless drives the reported EPS and the multiple.
5. **Management continuity.** An unresolved CFO transition at the exact moment credit risk is rising.
6. **Valuation multiple compression.** Even in a base case, a de-rating from 16x to 12x on unchanged earnings costs ~25%. The stock has already fallen from $18.76 (Jan 2026) to $13.22 — that is what de-rating looks like, and it may not be finished.

---

**Bottom line:** A genuinely good franchise at a fair price. I want to own it — at $10.50, or once the credit book has proven itself through a full cycle. Not today. **HOLD.**
