### 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: RISK-OFF, EM-led.** Two independent shocks have hit NU simultaneously in the last three weeks.

**(a) Brazil political/fiscal regime shock.** Lula won a fourth term in the 25 October runoff, an outcome markets had not priced (a Bolsonaro win was the base case). Brazilian assets convulsed on Monday 26 October: the real fell ~8% in one day — its largest single-day decline since 1999 — with intraday weakness to 5.60, and the Ibovespa sold off hard. USD/BRL went from **4.979 (9 Oct) to 5.4895 (6 Nov), a 9.3% BRL depreciation in four weeks**, and Lula's first post-election fiscal plan (12 Nov) was read as too timid, leaving the real at its weakest level since mid-2025. This is a **fiscal risk-premium repricing**, not a growth story.

**(b) US re-tightening.** US CPI re-accelerated to **3.82% YoY (Oct 2026)**, with the index up 0.35% m/m (~4.3% annualized). The Fed has stopped cutting and resumed hiking: effective fed funds **3.63% (May–Aug) → 3.75% (Sep) → 3.88% (Oct)**, with the 10-year at **5.13%** and hawkish Fed commentary favouring a "gradual hike path" (+50bp priced by early 2027). Growth is not the problem — Q3 real GDP ran **3.4% SAAR** — which means this is an inflation/term-premium problem. The 30Y at 5.47% and 2s10s at +39bp is a **bear steepening**: rising term premium, not recession.

**The binding combination for NU:** a Brazilian central bank that has just been forced to **abandon its easing cycle** (Selic held at 13.75% on 4 Nov after five consecutive cuts from 15.00%), a currency in disorderly decline, and a global discount rate at 5%+. That is the worst quadrant for a long-duration BRL-earning, USD-valued growth financial.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**① BRL translation — the single largest, fastest channel (NEGATIVE).**
NU reports in USD but earns in BRL. A 9.3% BRL depreciation mechanically removes ~8–9% from USD revenue and EPS with zero operational change. Q4'26 consensus struck at USD/BRL 5.00–5.20 needs a **6–8% translation haircut**. NU is arguably a *pure* play on this: the equity's 2x beta to the BRL is confirmed by 26 October — **BRL -8%, NU -17.0%** ($15.16 → $12.59) on 339.7M shares, ~5x normal volume. NU has fallen from $16.74 (31 Dec'25) to $13.22, **-21% YTD and -30% from the 28 Jan high of $18.76**, despite record Q3 results ($1.1B net income, $6.3B gross revenue).

**② Selic at 13.75% and the end of easing (NEGATIVE, with an offset).**
As a short-duration consumer lender, NU is hurt through the **credit channel, not the duration channel**. Cost of funds tracks CDI at ~13.75–14%, while asset yields are contractually fixed for short periods — the repricing lag squeezes spreads. This is already visible: **NIM 22.5% but risk-adjusted NIM fell to 12.1% in Q3'26 from 12.4% in Q2'26** — the 30bp compression is cost of risk, not pricing. With 33% credit growth into a 9% real rate environment, the loan book is being seasoned into the most restrictive Brazilian conditions since 2016. 90-day delinquency in Brazil (~6.8% in the comparable quarter) is the metric to watch; every 50bp of Selic held higher for a year is roughly 20–30bp of additional cost of risk on an unsecured consumer book. **Offset:** high rates keep incumbent banks' spreads wide, sustain the zero-fee share-gain thesis, and generate real yield on NU's own equity/deposit float; a 33% ROE business earning CDI on its float is not a rate victim in the way a REIT or utility is.

**③ US 10Y at 5.13% / Fed hiking (NEGATIVE for the multiple).**
NU is a long-duration equity — most of its value sits in terminal years growing 25–35%. A 69bp rise in the long end since June, plus loss of Fed cut expectations, compresses growth multiples directly. **This is where the damage has landed:** annualizing Q3 EPS of $0.22 (~$0.88) puts NU at roughly **14–15x earnings** — a deep discount for a compounder, i.e., the "Brazil discount" is already substantially embedded. That is the main mitigant to the negative macro read.

**④ Brazil fiscal credibility (NEGATIVE, dominant).**
The market's problem is not Lula per se but the **neutral-rate and risk-premium implication** of a fourth-term PT government with a weak first fiscal plan. A higher Brazilian neutral rate permanently lowers the present value of Brazilian earnings streams and keeps the Selic floor elevated — capping NU's multiple even if credit stays clean.

**⑤ US 25% Section 301 tariff (MILDLY NEGATIVE).**
Effective 22 July 2026 on most Brazilian goods (with exclusions for beef, coffee, metals, energy). NU does not export, but its mass-market, low-income customer base sits in exactly the industrial/agri supply chains hit. Second-order: employment, income, and SME credit quality.

**⑥ Mexico/Colombia expansion (PARTIAL HEDGE / POSITIVE).**
Non-Brazil growth diversifies the FX and rate exposure away from a single fiscal regime. It is the main reason the macro rating is not worse.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Three of the four variables that drive NU — BRL, Selic trajectory, and the US long rate — are moving against it at once, and the fourth (Brazilian fiscal credibility) has deteriorated structurally. The credit cycle has not yet broken (losses are normalising, not spiking), and Q3 delivered record profitability, so this is a **de-rating and translation problem rather than an earnings-solvency problem**. On a 12-month view the rating improves toward **Neutral**: the Selic is near its cyclical peak, much of the FX shock is priced, and 14x earnings for 30%+ growth leaves limited downside if the BRL merely stabilises.

### 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — BEAR: "Fiscal Dominance Spiral"

**~30% probability.** Lula's fiscal plan continues to disappoint through Q4; the real breaks 6.00/USD; IPCA (already 4.63% and with food inflation tripling since February) breaches 5.5%, forcing Copom to **reverse and hike the Selic back toward 15–16%** in H1 2027. Transmission to NU, in order:
1. FX: 6.00/USD is a further **9% translation hit** to USD EPS on top of the Q4 hit.
2. Credit: 90-day delinquency pushes back above 7.5% from 6.8%; risk-adjusted NIM compresses a further 80–120bp from 12.1%.
3. Multiple: with the 10Y at 5.5%+ and EM outflows, NU holds 14x on a *lower* earnings base.

Combined, that is a ~25–30% EPS cut — a **$9–10 stock**, another 25–30% downside, and ROE back into the low-20s. The tell would be a September/October Selic *hike* and a further BRL break past 5.60.

**Mirror-image bull (~20%): "Disinflation double."** US CPI rolls over as tariff effects lap and the Fed pauses at 4.0%, pulling the 10Y back to 4.5%; simultaneously Lula anchors fiscal policy to satisfy Congress, the real retraces to 4.80, and Copom resumes cuts toward 12%. That is roughly a **17% FX tailwind and a ~15% multiple re-rating** — a $18–19 stock. The asymmetry is unusually wide, and it is driven almost entirely by two variables NU does not control: USD/BRL and the US 10-year.
