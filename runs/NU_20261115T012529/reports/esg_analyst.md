# ESG ANALYSIS — Nu Holdings Ltd. (NYSE: NU)
**Sector:** Financial Services / Digital Banking (LatAm) | **Domicile:** Cayman Islands | **HQ:** São Paulo, Brazil | **Market cap:** ~US$63.9bn | **Customers:** 142.6m (Q3 2026)

---

## 1. ESG Materiality Map

For a consumer-credit digital bank, most ESG "factors" are noise. Four are financially material to NU:

| Rank | Factor | Why it moves cash flows / cost of capital | Pillar |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Product & consumer protection (responsible lending, over-indebtedness)** | Directly feeds credit losses. NPL 90+ at 7.3%, Stage 3 loans 8.4% of book vs 6.2% at Dec-25; ECL +70.7% y/y to $1.67bn in Q3-26 | S |
| 2 | **Governance: founder control, dual-class, CEO/Chair duality** | Minority shareholders cannot constrain capital allocation, M&A, or related-party terms; supports a governance discount in the multiple | G |
| 3 | **Data privacy / operational resilience (LGPD)** | Business is 100% digital, 142.6m data-rich customers; a breach or trust event compresses customer acquisition and triggers ANPD exposure | S |
| 4 | **Regulatory trajectory (BCB consumer-credit rules, interest caps, Pix)** | A statutory cap on revolving card interest or new instalment rules hits NIM directly; NIM 22.5% is the core earnings driver | G/Reg |

**Immaterial for NU:** carbon intensity, stranded assets, CBAM, water stress, biodiversity, supply-chain labour. This is a branchless bank whose operational footprint is offices and cloud.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**
- **Operational emissions are de minimis.** Branchless model; company-reported Scope 1+2 in the low hundreds of tCO2e. Nubank has declared itself **carbon neutral since August/September 2020**, offsetting emissions through projects in Brazil and Mexico, with a stated "carbon neutral forever" commitment.
- **Financed emissions:** $41.6bn loan book is overwhelmingly consumer (cards $27.2bn, unsecured $11.0bn, secured $3.4bn) — no project finance, no thermal coal, no upstream oil & gas. Climate transition risk is second-order.
- **Physical climate risk is indirect:** Brazil flood/drought events (e.g. Rio Grande do Sul) hit household incomes and therefore delinquency — an S/credit issue disguised as E. No owned asset concentration at risk.
- **Environmental controversies:** none identified. No spills, no remediation liabilities, no pollution fines.
- **Governance of E:** Social & Environmental Responsibility Policy (PRSA) mandated by CMN Resolution 4.327/2014, with a social/environmental risk unit reporting into risk management, segregated from business lines, and losses booked alongside operational risk. Genuine regulatory-grade framework, low materiality.
- **Weakness:** no TCFD/PCAF-grade financed-emissions disclosure; offsets (rather than abatement) do most of the work.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**
Two-sided — a real positive, and the company's single largest ESG-driven financial exposure.

**Positive (financial inclusion):** ~120m Brazilian customers, roughly 65% of the adult population; 16.6m Mexico, 6.1m Colombia; fee-light model that historically saved customers billions in tariffs. This is a genuine social licence asset, and it *is* the growth story (ARPAC $17.7, +21% FX-neutral).

**Negative — responsible lending and over-indebtedness (the material risk):**
- **Aug-2025:** the **Public Prosecutor's Office of Bahia (MP-BA) filed a civil lawsuit** alleging abusive credit practices: loans offered without customer request, collection of already-paid debts, unauthorised instalment plans, charging of disputed purchases, no early-repayment/amortisation alternatives, and abusive interest rates. Relief sought includes clear pre-contractual disclosure, mandatory affordability assessment, and preservation of an "existential minimum." Nubank **declined to sign a Conduct Adjustment Agreement (TAC)** — signalling a contested, potentially precedent-setting posture under Brazil's Over-Indebtedness Law.
- A second consumer action seeks **R$10m (~US$1.99m) in collective moral damages** plus an injunction, in a case where a **US$664 card debt was alleged to have ballooned** (card revolving rates in Brazil have run into the hundreds of percent).
- **Macro context amplifies this:** 82% of Brazilian households are in debt, roughly one-third delinquent; unsecured personal credit costs ~61% p.a.; large incumbents (Itaú, Bradesco) are retreating to secured lending and higher-income borrowers — i.e. NU is increasingly the lender of last resort to the most stressed cohort in a high-Selic economy.
- **Direct financial translation:** Stage 3 migration from 6.2% to 8.4% of gross loans in nine months, coverage 234%, and risk-adjusted NIM slipping to 12.1%. Responsible-lending is not a reputational issue here; it is the provisioning line.

**Other S factors:**
- **Data privacy/trust:** on **12 June 2026** Nubank sent customers an erroneous notice of **extrajudicial liquidation by the Central Bank**; founder Cristina Junqueira publicly apologised, attributing it to a developer inadvertently triggering a workflow. Low direct cost, high trust signal for a 100%-digital deposit franchise ($47.5bn deposits).
- **Labour:** ~8,000 FTEs against 142.6m customers (extreme revenue-per-head efficiency). June-2026 dismissal of two employees over an alleged internal sabotage plot indicates elevated internal-security and employee-relations tension. No widespread union campaign found; no material safety issues (office-based).
- **Governance gap in disclosure:** diversity/turnover metrics sit in the ESG Report PDF, which was not machine-readable in this workflow — a data-availability limitation of the analysis, and itself a minor transparency critique.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10**

**Positives:** Audit & Risk Committee is 100% NYSE-independent (Rogério Calderón Peres — audit committee financial expert, ex-PwC/Itaú/HSBC — as chair, plus Anita Sands and Thuan Pham); a **Lead Independent Director** (Anita Sands, since Oct-2022); separate Compensation & People and Nominating & Corporate Governance committees; KPMG audited IFRS accounts; $1bn buyback with $500.4m executed by 30-Sep-26.

**Negatives that dominate the score:**
1. **CEO = Chairman.** David Vélez is Founder, **Chairman and CEO** simultaneously. Textbook duality conflict, no separation.
2. **20:1 dual-class control.** Class B carries 20 votes; Vélez holds **88.3% of Class B and 74.4% of total voting power**; co-founder Junqueira ~9.6%. Minority holders of the NYSE-listed Class A cannot block anything — capital allocation, compensation, related-party transactions, or a value-destructive acquisition.
3. **Foreign private issuer exemptions.** As a Cayman-incorporated FPI, NU explicitly relies on exemptions from NYSE corporate-governance standards applicable to US issuers (including majority-independent-board expectations). Board independence is therefore lighter than the NYSE brand implies.
4. **Management instability.** Long-serving CFO Guilherme Lago departed (announced ~June 2026, after five years as CFO) and was replaced by ex-Visa executive Rob Livingston in July 2026; shares fell and **BofA downgraded to Underperform specifically citing the CFO exit and leadership turnover**. A founder-controlled board replacing its CFO amid a credit cycle inflection is precisely where independent oversight matters.
5. **Dilution/comp alignment.** Share count has risen ~1% p.a.; the $1bn buyback largely offsets equity-comp dilution rather than returning capital. Buyback size is <2% of shares.

**Verdict:** structurally entrenched founder control with competent committee hygiene. Not a fraud-risk governance profile; a *minority-shareholder-rights* profile.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (leaning "Average with a Governance discount")

**Rationale:** Environmental is a non-issue (low risk, credible net-zero/offset posture). Social is where the substance sits — a genuine financial-inclusion benefit set against hard evidence of consumer-protection litigation, rising delinquency among first-time borrowers, and an operating trust failure. Governance is the weakest pillar: founder control at 74.4% of votes, a 20:1 dual class, CEO-Chair duality, FPI exemptions, and executive churn. Third-party ESG scores for NU tend to be middling-to-weak on governance and neutral on environment — consistent with the primary evidence gathered here.

**Why not "Leader":** no amount of inclusion narrative survives a Stage 3 ratio moving 6.2% → 8.4% in three quarters alongside a prosecutor alleging loans issued without request.
**Why not "Laggard"/"ESG Risk":** no environmental liabilities, no corruption record, an independent and financially literate audit committee, lead independent director, and a market-leading inclusion footprint.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL (mildly negative at the margin)**

- **Not thesis-breaking.** At 16.6x TTM earnings and 4.5x book, the market is already pricing NU as a Brazil-credit-risk story rather than an ESG premium name. There is no exclusion risk (no coal, no weapons, no tobacco), no CBAM exposure, and no decarbonisation capex requirement. ESG screens will not force selling.
- **The S factor is the swing variable for the equity.** Responsible-lending enforcement is the transmission mechanism into the one number that matters — cost of risk. Watch: (i) outcome of the MP-BA action and whether other state prosecutors follow; (ii) any legislative cap on revolving-card interest; (iii) Stage 3 and 15–90 day NPL trajectory (currently 4.9%); (iv) whether secured lending ($3.4bn, +23%) can be scaled faster than unsecured ($11.0bn, +39%).
- **Governance discount is structural, not fixable near-term.** The dual-class structure and CEO/Chair duality will keep NU out of governance-tilted indices and cap the multiple relative to an equivalent-quality US financial. The CFO transition (Lago → Livingston, July 2026) should be tracked for disclosure quality — BofA's downgrade on leadership turnover is a reasonable caution, though an ex-Visa North America CFO is arguably a net upgrade for the US expansion.
- **Positioning conclusion:** ESG is a **risk-monitoring item, not an alpha source**. It modestly raises the required cost of equity (call it +50–100bp) and argues for sizing discipline rather than exclusion. If the MP-BA case broadens into a national consumer-credit conduct framework, reclassify to Negative.
