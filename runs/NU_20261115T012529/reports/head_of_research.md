### Investment Memo: NU — 14 November 2026

**Verdict**: BUY (Initiate — small starter position only) | Conviction: Medium for a starter; Low for full size
**Price Target**: $14.50 (~+10% from $13.22); bull path $18–20 conditional on credit inflection; bear $8.5–9.5
**Time Horizon**: 12–24 months

**Investment Thesis**
Nu is the highest-quality franchise in Latin American financial services — 143M customers, ~32% ROE, 19.3% efficiency ratio, 34% FX-neutral revenue growth — trading at ~15x forward earnings because of a Brazilian political/fiscal de-rating (−17% on the October election, −30% from the January high), not because of operational deterioration. Forensics are clean, the Q4 revenue bar is set below the run-rate just delivered, and the most credible bearish voice on the team declines to short it. But the credit book is genuinely deteriorating into an untested cycle and the stock carries a real, unpriceable political/sanctions left tail — so this is a 1% starter with a 2.5% ceiling, not a conviction bet. We earn the right to add through credit inflection, not through hope.

**Key Findings by Domain**
- **Macro & Sector**: Macro rated *Unfavourable* — Selic frozen at 13.75% after Copom abandoned easing, BRL −9.3% in four weeks, US 10Y at 5.13%: the worst quadrant for a long-duration BRL earner. Mitigant: much is priced, and the rating improves toward Neutral on a 12-month view. Sector is late-cycle Neutral, but NU is the share-gainer — 34% growth vs 8.3% system credit growth, best-in-class margins, ~60% valuation discount to SOFI.
- **Geopolitical & Legal**: *High risk*. ~90% of revenue in one BB-rated country; Pix — NU's core rail — is explicitly cited in the US Section 301 action, and the June 2026 FTO/SDGT designations create an OFAC "material-support" overhang on the entire Brazilian payment system. Low probability, existential-style impact. Card revolving-cap legislation is live in the Senate.
- **Value**: Fairly valued — probability-weighted IV ~$13.8–14.0 vs $13.22 (~5% discount). No margin of safety; buy trigger ≤$10.50; bear case $7.4 at a live 25% probability.

The bear case is cyclical, not forensic — and after a 30% drawdown the short asymmetry is poor. Short triggers: 90+ NPL >8% for two quarters, or an enacted card-rate cap.

**Agent disagreement flagged**: Growth ($18–20) vs Value ($13.8–14.0) is not a factual dispute — it is a 14x vs 18–20x exit-multiple dispute that hinges entirely on whether NPLs peak in the next two quarters. The memo's PT uses the Value base; the Growth case is treated as the conditional bull.

**Bull Case**
- A 32%-ROE compounder priced like a Brazil macro bet: 15x forward earnings against 34% FXN growth and a 19.3% efficiency ratio, with the Q4 bar set conservatively and alt data showing zero demand decay.
- Monetization runway: ARPAC ~$18/month across 143M customers; Mexico (full bank licence, Jul-26) and Colombia inflecting; US charter and stablecoin accounts are free optionality at this price.
- Reflexive recovery: Selic near peak, BRL shock largely priced, no forensic short case — two quarters of stabilizing NPLs re-rates the stock toward $18–20 (+36–50%).

**Bear Case**
- Credit cycle turning on a 92% unsecured book: 90+ NPL 6.5%→7.3% in two quarters, provisions growing 2x revenue, gross margin −440bp YoY — and NU has never run a book through a full Brazilian down-cycle.
- Fiscal-dominance spiral (~30% probability per Macro): BRL through 6.00 and Selic re-hiked toward 15–16% implies a ~25–30% EPS cut and a $9–10 stock.
- Political left tail: an enacted revolving-credit cap hits the highest-yield $27.2B card book directly; the OFAC/Pix overhang is unpriceable and potentially existential.

**Key Risks**
1. 90+ NPL through 8.5% / Stage 3 through 10% — converts cyclical concern into structural (exit trigger).
2. Enacted card-rate cap or mandated affordability rules (not merely proposed).
3. USD/BRL >6.00 or Selic >15% — ~9% incremental translation hit plus cost-of-risk step-up.
4. OFAC action touching Pix participants, correspondent-banking loss, or capital controls.
5. Governance/ops: CFO and CTO seats both unfilled past mid-2027, or a second customer-visible operational failure.
6. Disclosure hygiene: the ~$1.8B SG&A-to-"other opex" reclassification must be verified against the next 6-K; Q2'26 balance-sheet feed was internally inconsistent.
