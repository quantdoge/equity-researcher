## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US–Brazil escalation reaching the payments rails (Section 301 + FTO/SDGT designations). Probability: High (already partly realized). Impact: Very High.**
The USTR's June 1, 2026 Section 301 determination explicitly cited **Pix** — the central-bank-owned instant payment system that Nu depends on and that processes most Brazilian digital payments — as a discriminatory trade practice, with a **25% additional tariff on most Brazilian goods effective July 22, 2026** (exclusions: beef, coffee, metals, energy) (PIIE, Jun 9 2026; Legal500/US Chamber). More seriously, the State Department's **FTO/SDGT designation of PCC and Comando Vermelho (effective June 5, 2026)** creates a "material support" theory (EO 13224; INA §219) that reaches *payment processing itself*, and with Pix owned/regulated by the BCB the threat "hangs over the entire Brazilian financial system," including **OFAC secondary sanctions and loss of correspondent-banking access** (PIIE). Steptoe's 2026 outlook flags "the risk of increased US sanctions on Brazil is high," with OFAC expanding its Brazil transnational-crime programme. A designated-counterparty finding against a Pix participant would be an existential-style tail risk for Brazilian fintechs' dollar funding and for Nu's US bank charter viability.

**Risk 2 — Brazil macro/fiscal regime after Lula's re-election. Probability: High. Impact: High.**
Lula won the Oct 25, 2026 runoff decisively; the real sank and local assets sold off on spending/fiscal concerns (Reuters, Al Jazeera, WSJ Oct 26–27). Fitch affirmed Brazil at **BB/Stable** (Jun 16, 2026) but flagged large deficits; Fitch's post-election note is titled "Lula's Brazil Election Win Raises Fiscal Uncertainties." Selic expected held at **13.75%**, with 33 of 48 economists in a Reuters poll seeing no cut (Reuters, Nov 3, 2026). Transmission to Nu: (a) higher-for-longer rates compress NIM expansion and raise funding costs; (b) FX depreciation hits USD-reported earnings and the ~$41.6bn credit book; (c) politically-driven pressure for rate caps and credit subsidies. FXN revenue growth (+37%) already flatters reported USD growth (+39.5% as reported vs +31% FXN on net income) — the gap is the FX risk.

**Risk 3 — Politicised credit pricing and tax/regulatory intervention in Brazil. Probability: Medium-High. Impact: Medium-High.**
Card repricing is now a live legislative target: Brazil's Senate approved legislation limiting the growth of credit-card debt to 100% of principal (i.e., capping total revolving debt near 2x), with average card APRs reported near 450% (Bloomberg). Card receivables are $27.2bn of NU's $41.6bn book (+32% YoY), so a binding revolving cap hits the highest-yield asset. Separately, the **IOF decrees (12.466/12.467 of May 2025)** — partially overturned by Congress but with rate increases phased in (some FX measures from Sep 1, 2026) — raise transaction-tax costs on credit, FX and insurance, and remain a recurring legislative battleground (BDO, Mayer Brown, EY). Add Brazil's fast-tracked **Digital Markets Bill** (ex-ante conduct rules modelled on the EU DMA) which, if applied to large digital platforms, could constrain Nu's bundling/marketplace tactics.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights
- **US charter:** OCC granted **preliminary conditional approval** for Nubank, N.A. (Corporate Decision #1390, Sept 2026; application filed Sep 30, 2025). Conditional status = ongoing OCC supervisory conditions; a de novo bank brings BSA/AML, CRA and capital obligations and a heightened examination posture.
- **Mexico:** CNBV approved conversion to a full-service bank (Apr 2025; final authorisation Jul 2026), lifting deposit insurance ~16x to ~$195k/customer and enabling payroll accounts — a genuine regulatory upgrade, but it imports CNBV capital/liquidity rules and Mexican anti-money-laundering scrutiny.
- **Colombia:** Usury ceiling remains binding — statutory 1.5x the average consumer rate (Resolution 1472 of 2026 put it at ~28.59%, then ~28.34%). Founder-CEO David Vélez has publicly campaigned to **scrap the usury cap**, even as he was named Colombia's unpaid AI adviser (Rio Times, 2026) — a founder publicly lobbying a regulator that supervises his entity is a headline/governance risk. Nubank plans COP 5trn (~$1.58bn) of Colombian investment to 2030, which raises expropriation-adjacent sunk-cost exposure in a country with a 6.4%-of-GDP central government deficit and a new administration (Abelardo de la Espriella, elected Jun 2026 — a sharp policy turn).
- **Litigation/disclosure:** No 20-F or 10-K text could be retrieved through the filing tools (tool returned "No 20-F found"; NU files 6-K/20-F on EDGAR under CIK 1691493). I could not verify current contingency disclosures or Brazilian public civil actions (ações civis públicas) against Nubank, nor any ANPD (LGPD) enforcement. **This is a data gap I could not close within budget** — I found no evidence of a material active securities class action or DOJ/SEC investigation in 2026 coverage, but absence of evidence here is not evidence of absence.
- **Data privacy:** Brazil's ANPD has moved from "educational mode" to active investigation and fining; Nu's ~120m-customer dataset, open-finance participation and credit-scoring models are natural targets. Also note Pix-related data-sharing and the "bets"/gambling-credit nexus that Brazilian regulators have been probing.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags
1. **OFAC / sanctions over Pix (highest severity).** The FTO/SDGT designation of PCC and Comando Vermelho, combined with Brazil's own 2025 money-laundering investigation that "uncovered money laundering activities in fintech companies," means Nu's transaction-monitoring and designated-party screening on Pix and NuPay flows is now a US-sanctions question, not just a local AML question. Secondary-sanctions exposure exists even as a non-US institution through correspondent-banking dependency.
2. **Stablecoins/"Nu Global."** A stablecoin-based multi-currency account live in **37 countries** (Oct 15, 2026) is a multi-jurisdiction AML/CTF, travel-rule and sanctions-screening buildout at speed, under BCB Instruction 701/2026 (virtual-asset framework) plus the US GENIUS Act regime. Cross-border, multi-currency, stablecoin rails are the classic enforcement vector.
3. **FCPA/UK Bribery Act:** Cayman-incorporated, NYSE-listed, Brazilian-operated group = FCPA jurisdiction attaches via the issuer and US listing; no evidence of issues, but third-party/payroll-lending and government-adjacent payroll partnerships (public-sector payroll loans are explicitly growing) warrant monitoring.
4. **Consumer-credit conduct risk:** 90+ NPL at 7.3% (12.1% on interest-earning balances) with unsecured originations at a record R$22.1bn — rising delinquencies plus aggressive unsecured growth is the combination that invites regulator-imposed provisioning or collections-conduct rules.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Nu is a high-quality compounder with a genuinely improved regulatory footprint (US OCC charter, Mexican bank licence, Colombian scale), but ~90%+ of revenue sits in one country whose sovereign is BB-rated, whose currency just sold off on a fiscally expansionary election result, whose central-bank-owned payment rail is now the explicit target of a US Section 301 action, and whose financial system faces an unprecedented **OFAC "material support" overhang** from the June 2026 FTO designations. That is an unusual and underappreciated combination: a well-run company with a single-jurisdiction concentration of both earnings *and* sanctions-legal risk.

**What would change the rating (to Medium):** a US–Brazil framework agreement removing Pix from the Section 301 action and clarifying that Pix participants face no OFAC exposure; a credible Brazilian fiscal anchor that pulls Selic off 13.75%; failure of the card-revolving cap to become law in binding form.

**What would push it to Very High:** any OFAC designation or enforcement action touching Pix participants, loss of correspondent-banking access for Brazilian banks, or capital controls/repatriation restrictions on FX out of Brazil (the group's USD reporting and its Cayman holding structure both depend on unimpeded FX conversion).
