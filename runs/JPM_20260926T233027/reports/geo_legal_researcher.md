## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1. US–China escalation over Taiwan → sanctions war / capital controls on the financial system**
- *Probability:* **Low-Medium (~15–25% over 2–3 yrs)** of armed conflict; *higher* probability of tit-for-tat sanctions and capital-flow restrictions.
- *Impact:* **High.** JPM is explicitly one of the lenders running Taiwan-contingency plans; a sanctions regime would force its $bn China/HK franchise into wind-down mode (per the Russia playbook), and China's first-ever invocation of its **2021 Blocking Rule (May 2026)** ordering domestic firms to disregard US sanctions (Steptoe sanctions update) creates contradictory-compliance exposure for US banks clearing RMB/China payments.
- *Source:* Bloomberg/Yahoo, "Wall Street Banks Prep for Grim China Scenarios Over Taiwan"; Steptoe Weekly Sanctions Update (May 2026); JPM SEC filing language citing "sharp oil price declines, a potential slowdown in the macroeconomic prospects in China."

**2. US "maximum pressure" sanctions expansion (Russia, Iran) and secondary-sanctions risk to a top USD clearer**
- *Probability:* **Medium-High** (trend, not event) — sanctions/export controls/tariffs were the dominant US statecraft tool in 2025 (ABA National Security Year in Review).
- *Impact:* **Medium.** JPM is a critical US clearing intermediary; risk is operational (KYC/OFAC screening costs, frozen-asset disputes) and reputational (Senate AML findings, see §3). Prior Russia episode (blocked sovereign-debt principal, OFAC waiver lapsed 2023) shows the tail: litigation with asset holders (e.g., Noble Capital RSD v. JPMorgan).
- *Source:* ABA National Security Legal Developments 2025; U.S. Treasury/OFAC actions 2025–26; SEC filing risk factors.

**3. De-dollarization / payments fragmentation (CIPS, mBridge, BRICS+ trade in local currency)**
- *Probability:* **Low-Medium over 3–5 years**; slow erosion, not sudden.
- *Impact:* **Medium (structural).** JPM monetizes the dollar clearing franchise (payments/cash management is a core, high-margin business); incremental share loss to alternative rails would compress that economics without threatening solvency.
- *Source:* Trade/geopolitics coverage of China-Russia de-dollarization pushes (Atlantic Council, foreign-policy commentary 2025–26).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Zelle / consumer-fraud front: two-sides story.** CFPB's Dec 2024 suit against JPMorgan, BofA, Wells and Zelle operator Early Warning Services (EWS) was **dismissed by the new administration (2025)** — direct relief. But **NY AG Letitia James sued EWS (Aug 2025)** alleging ~**$1B in consumer fraud**, and the case **survived a motion-to-dismiss (July 2026)**. JPMorgan is an owner bank and a named party in related reporting; reimbursement and reputational exposure remains live.
- **Senate AML/Epstein scrutiny:** Senator Wyden's reports (2024 Epstein report; **Aug 2026 follow-up**) fault JPMorgan and peers for failure to timely file suspicious-activity reports (Epstein network, money laundering). Reputational tail; potential BSA/FinCEN follow-on.
- **Markets manipulation:** JPM has paid comfortably **>$1B** in DOJ/CFTC spoofing-related penalties (2020–22); a 2024 House committee referred **silver/eurodollar manipulation** allegations to DOJ, and DOJ has acknowledged an ongoing probe. LIBOR antitrust MDL **terminated Oct 2025** (legacy chapter closed).
- **Consumer class actions:** data-breach litigation (2023 incident affecting tens of millions of customers), a $100M minimum-payment settlement, $8.75M FCRA settlement, flood-insurance $22.1M settlement — chronic, but sized within the firm's litigation budget.
- **Regulatory environment direction (2025–26):** benign. JPMorgan **left the Net-Zero Banking Alliance (Jan 7, 2025)**, and Texas AG subsequently **cleared the exiting Wall Street banks** (Jan 2025) — the anti-ESG political threat has largely been neutralized by exit. The Basel III Endgame rule trajectory under the new Fed leadership points to materially diluted capital increases vs. the 2023 proposal (search on the final rule timed out — treat as reported trajectory, verify). CFPB's activist-era agenda has been rolled back.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **AML/BSA execution risk** — Senate findings on SAR failures (Epstein; 2024 cash-smuggling report) keep JPM in the enforcement crosshairs despite heavy compliance spend (the largest in the sector).
2. **Sanctions contradiction risk** — continuing to hold/process any residual Russia-linked assets (NCSP-related dividends received as recently as Jan 2026; blocked-principal dispute) plus China's Blocking Rule creates competing-jurisdiction compliance tension.
3. **Consumer-protection exposure** — Zelle reimbursement disputes (NY AG $1B case) and recurring scam-fraud litigation.
4. **Data privacy/cyber** — repeated breach class actions; Chase's 2026 Data Security Center launch is a remediation signal, but multi-billion-claim headroom remains.
5. **Foreign Corrupt Practices Act** — no major disclosed FCPA matter; monitored, not flagged.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Revenue is concentrated in the US (lowest-risk jurisdiction with a deregulatory posture and massive capital firepower), and headline litigation headwinds (CFPB Zelle suit, LIBOR MDL) are resolving favorably. Offsetting to **High** are: (i) structural US–China/Taiwan tail risk touching an irreplaceable Greater-China franchise, (ii) chronic US enforcement/AML scrutiny as the world's top USD clearer, and (iii) sanctions-fragmentation pressure on the clearing model. Not **Low** because the China exposure and AML pedigree are real, even under a friendly regulatory regime.
