# Micron Technology, Inc. (MU) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**As of ~Nov 22, 2026 | Price ~$595 (mkt cap ~$673B) | FY2026 revenue ~$133B | CEO: Sanjay Mehrotra**

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (with material concerns)**

**Positives**
- Board of ~10 directors, ~7 independent (~70%) per third-party tracking as of 30 Jun 2026; no dual-class control structure, no controlling family/Founder bloc.
- Genuine refresh: four long-tenured directors retired at the 15 Jan 2026 annual meeting (incl. Lead Independent Director Lynn Dugle, Steven Gomo, Linnie Haynesworth, T. Mark Liu), replaced by credible names (Robert Swan, ex-Intel CFO; Christine Simons; MaryAnn Wright; Alexis Black Björlin).
- Executive bench is stable and deep: CFO Mark Murphy in seat since 2022 (orderly succession from Zinsner), no CFO/CLO churn.

**Concerns**
- **Chairman/CEO duality**: Mehrotra is both Chairman and CEO. Governance guidelines require a Lead Independent Director when the chair is not independent — the prior LID (Dugle) departed in Jan 2026, and the *identity* of the current LID could not be verified from available filings (data gap; verify in FY2026 proxy).
- **Four seats turned over in a single cycle** — meaningful loss of institutional memory precisely as the company commits $100B+ of capital to New York/Idaho.
- **Compensation opacity**: equity-heavy PRSU design is directionally aligned, but the FY2026 DEF 14A was not retrievable through available tools; no say-on-pay vote percentages or pay-ratio data verified. Treat as unconfirmed rather than clean.
- Insider behavior (below) is the strongest governance signal in the file, and it is not reassuring.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — **HIGH**

No SEC enforcement actions or DOJ matters returned against MU (EDGAR full-text search: clean). However:

- **China exposure is the single largest regulatory tail risk.** China + Hong Kong customers generated ~$14.6B (~11%) of FY2026 revenue. The May 2023 Cyberspace Administration of China finding of "serious network security risks" — barring Micron products from Critical Information Infrastructure operators — **remains in force** and has never been rescinded. Micron is the only major memory maker with a state-level Chinese prohibition on its book.
- **US Section 232 tariffs**: Proclamation 11,002 (14 Jan 2026) imposed a **25% tariff on specified semiconductor articles**, plus new AI-chip export licensing rules — raising cost/compliance friction on cross-border flows and inviting retaliation against US memory suppliers.
- **China's gallium/germanium/rare-earth export controls were suspended only through roughly Nov 2026** (some tranches to 27 Nov 2026). That suspension is now at or past expiry — a live, imminent input-cost and supply-continuity risk for DRAM/NAND production.
- **Antitrust — the escalation to watch.** A DRAM price-fixing class action (filed 25 Jun 2026, *Garciaguirre*) against Micron, Samsung and SK hynix **survived a motion to dismiss**; the court found collusion "plausible" after an alleged ~500–700% cumulative DRAM price increase, describing the defendants as acting "like hawks flying in formation." Treble-damages exposure, plus realistic follow-on risk from state AGs, the DOJ, or the EC/KFTC. This is the highest-velocity legal risk in the file.
- **IP litigation stack (quantified, not contingent):**
  - **Netlist**: settled 6 Oct 2026 — **$600M**, paid $30M/quarter from Q4 2026 through Q3 2031, five-year license only.
  - **YMTC**: multiple patent infringement complaints filed 6 Oct 2025, unresolved.
  - **Mimir IP**: claim of up to ~$480M (patents acquired from SK hynix).
  - Prior federal securities class action **dismissed 3 Feb 2026** (D. Idaho) — favorable, and a useful rebuttal to fraud claims.
- **Permitting/environmental**: *Neighbors for a Better Micron v. Onondaga County IDA* challenges the SEQRA/climate review of the Clay, NY megafab; agencies' approvals are being contested. Litigation risk overlays an already **2–3 year construction delay** (second NY fab possibly late 2033).

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Single-point AI demand concentration.** HBM is sold out for 2026 and **>75% of FY2027 output is already committed** — the backlog is a strength, but it means revenue is levered to a handful of AI accelerator/hyperscaler programs (one customer represented ~17% of revenue in the FY2025 disclosure; FY2026 language indicates no customer ≥10%). Cancellation or a design-loss at the lead customer would be violent, not gradual.
- **Cyclical oversupply tipping point.** Sell-side (GF Securities, Nov 2026) is already cutting NAND price forecasts and warning of a 2027 glut; the stock's 13–14% single-day declines and ~50% drawdown show the market repricing this. Micron is simultaneously adding the industry's largest greenfield capacity ($100B Clay, NY; two Idaho fabs; $6.4B CHIPS funding) — capital committed at cycle peak, delivered at cycle risk.
- **Geographic manufacturing concentration + key-person dependency.** Fabs/assembly in Taiwan, Japan, Singapore, Malaysia and China (Xi'an) carry earthquake/typhoon/geopolitical and logistical exposure with limited substitutability for leading-edge DRAM. Separately, strategy, HBM roadmap and the customer relationships are tightly bound to **Mehrotra (Chairman & CEO, age ~68)**; no public succession plan or heir apparent has been disclosed. Executive turnover at CTO/COO-adjacent levels (DeBoer, Bhatia monetizing) warrants monitoring.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **ELEVATED**

- **"RAM price gouging" narrative is now mainstream.** The antitrust complaint's framing (~500–700% price increases, supply deliberately restricted) is being amplified in consumer tech press and among PC/gaming communities; MU ranks among the most-discussed tickers on retail forums (#7 across finance subreddits, top of r/WallStreetBets). Memory has become the visible villain of the AI cost story — a reputational position that invites political attention and consumer class actions.
- **Retail shareholder damage.** A ~51% drawdown from the June record, with heavy retail dip-buying on the way down, creates a litigious, hostile shareholder base and a ready plaintiff bar — even though the prior securities case was dismissed.
- **Community/environmental opposition** to the Clay, NY megafab (toxicity, water, rushed SEQRA review) is organized, funded and in court; a construction injunction or further delay would be a visible national story given the CHIPS Act subsidy.
- **Political-financial optics**: STOCK Act disclosures show members of Congress (Ro Khanna, John McGuire, John Boozman) trading MU during the run-up — not a Micron violation, but it feeds the "AI-memory complex" narrative.
- Offsetting: strong product-execution reputation (HBM4 share gains, sold-out capacity), credible board additions, and no product-safety/recall or cybersecurity-breach incidents identified.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: no fraud indicators, no SEC/DOJ enforcement, a dismissed securities class action, and a functional board keep this out of "Critical." But three independent High-severity vectors now overlap: (i) a surviving DRAM antitrust conspiracy case with treble-damages and follow-on-government potential, (ii) ~11% China revenue sitting under an unrepealed CII ban plus expiring Chinese raw-material controls and new US tariffs/licensing, and (iii) management-wide insider distribution into the peak with zero insider buying. In a name with 2.4 beta and a 50% drawdown, these risks are correlated, not diversifying.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **DRAM price-fixing class action survived dismissal** — court found collusion "plausible" on ~500%+ pricing. Escalate for legal-provision adequacy and disclosure review; ask management directly about DOJ/state-AG contacts.
2. **CEO sold ~$182M in five tranches bracketing the all-time high; zero insider purchases company-wide through a 50% decline.** Escalate as a valuation/conviction signal, notwithstanding 10b5-1 plans.
3. **China: $14.6B revenue with an unrepealed CII ban, expiring gallium/rare-earth suspension, and new 25% Section 232 tariffs + AI-chip licensing.** Request a quantified sensitivity of China revenue and input-cost pass-through.
4. **Stacked IP liabilities**: $600M Netlist settlement (cash, 5-year license only — renegotiation risk from 2031), unresolved YMTC complaints, Mimir's ~$480M claim.
5. **Chairman/CEO duality coinciding with the Lead Independent Director's departure** — verify the current LID and the independence of the post-2026 board in the FY2026 proxy before relying on board-oversight strength.
6. **Clay, NY megafab: 2–3 year delay plus active SEQRA litigation** — test capex schedule, CHIPS-funding milestone conditions and depreciation assumptions.
