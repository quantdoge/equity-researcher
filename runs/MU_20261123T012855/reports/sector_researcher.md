## SECTOR BRIEF — MICRON TECHNOLOGY (MU)
**Sector:** Technology / Semiconductors — Memory (DRAM, NAND, HBM)
**Data as of:** 20–22 Nov 2026 (MU close $594.51; market cap ~$673B)

---

### 1. Sector Overview

Memory has become the tightest node in the AI supply chain and is in the most extreme upcycle in its history. WSTS has raised 2026 global semiconductor revenue to **~$1.66T, with Memory growing ~305% YoY** (vs. Logic +45%), and forecasts a further **+27% to ~$1.9T in 2027** — a sharp deceleration in growth rate even as absolute dollars rise. DRAM is a three-player oligopoly (Samsung, SK hynix, Micron) with structural barriers that are effectively prohibitive, and the HBM build-out is physically cannibalising conventional DRAM supply: TrendForce estimates HBM consumes **~23% of DRAM wafers in 2026 and ~30% in 2027**, on top of conventional DRAM contract prices rising **58–63% q/q in Q2 2026**. Capacity is sold: industry reports suggest **all of 2027 memory capacity is booked**, and Micron states **>75% of FY27 shipments are already committed**. The equity market, however, has stopped paying for it — MU is **-51% from its 25 June record close of $1,213.56**, and the S&P 500 is in correction on AI-capex concerns.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **HBM is a supply tax on the whole DRAM market.** Micron guides HBM TAM from **$35B (2025) to $100B (2028E)** — larger than the entire 2025 DRAM market. Because HBM4 die are ~3x the wafer area per bit, every HBM ramp removes conventional DRAM bit supply. This is the mechanism behind 80%+ gross margins.
- **Multi-year revenue visibility, not spot exposure.** With 2027 capacity booked and >75% of Micron's FY27 shipments committed (largely under long-term agreements with pre-agreed pricing), the traditional "spot price collapse" risk is deferred into FY28 rather than FY27.
- **Extreme barriers to entry + repaired balance sheets.** A greenfield DRAM fab is $15–20B+ with a 3–4 year lead time and 2+ year HBM customer qualification; no new entrant exists. Micron carries effectively no interest burden (interest expense ~$0 in Q4 FY26), so incremental pricing flows almost entirely to equity.

---

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **The supply response is dated and visible.** Sell-side and trade coverage points to industry-wide production ramps **by late 2028**, with NAND supply already loosening first (NAND is 5–6 players vs. DRAM's 3, and is the weak flank). WSTS's 2027 growth of +27% versus ~305% memory growth in 2026 is the mathematical definition of a peaking cycle.
- **Demand is concentrated in one capex cycle.** The marginal buyer is a handful of hyperscalers plus NVIDIA's HBM stack. The current drawdown is a live demonstration of how fast the sector re-rates when AI-spend conviction wobbles — the sector's beta to that single variable is close to 1.
- **Cost and policy escalation.** HBM4 transition lifts capex intensity and qualification risk at exactly the point when cash generation is highest (the classic cyclical trap); add export controls, China commodity DRAM/NAND capacity, and scarce leading-edge tool/packaging (CoWoS) supply.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **Scale within a three-player game.** MU's Sep-26 quarter revenue of $54.2B compares with SK hynix's KRW 104.6T (~$75B) and Samsung's larger memory business — Micron is the smallest of the DRAM trio but participates fully in the oligopoly pricing. Estimated 2026 memory industry revenue approaching $930B–$1T puts MU at roughly **14–17% share**, with SK hynix ~25% and Samsung the balance.
- **Best-in-class margin execution, and it has been that way through the cycle.** MU's FY23 trough was a **-9.1% gross margin / -$5.8B net loss** while it kept capex running — then delivered +48.8%, +61.6% and +256% revenue growth in the three following years. Peer-relative, its FY26 gross margin of 80.7% is **+20pts above the peer median**, and its operating margin of 74.6% is **+26pts above**.
- **Asymmetry of the risk:** MU is a US-domiciled, CHIPS-supported supplier to US hyperscalers with strategic scarcity value, which supports both LTA pricing and policy support. Its weakness is scale and balance-sheet depth versus Samsung — in a 2028 downcycle, Samsung can absorb losses MU cannot, and NAND (Sandisk, Kioxia, Samsung, SK) is the more competitive half of its business.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL** *(fundamentals attractive, risk/reward deteriorating — late-cycle)*

**Cycle position: late-cycle / blow-off phase, not early growth.** The evidence is unambiguous on both sides. Operating fundamentals are still improving sequentially — MU's quarterly revenue went $13.6B → $23.9B → $41.5B → $54.2B through FY26, with Q4 gross margin of **86.8%**, which is roughly double any prior cycle peak in the company's history. But three markers say the peak is in sight: (i) growth-rate deceleration is already embedded in WSTS's 2027 forecast; (ii) the equity market has de-rated the group by ~50% *while earnings estimates rose*, i.e. multiple compression is leading the earnings peak; and (iii) supply additions are scheduled for late 2028, which is precisely when the committed 2027 book rolls off.

**Practical implication for the memo:** the sector is a **"show-me" hold, not a fresh buy at the index level**. The durable case rests on whether the AI-driven HBM allocation model structurally dampens the 2028–29 downcycle relative to 2018–19 and 2022–23. Micron's own contracted position (>75% of FY27 committed) means FY27 numbers are largely locked and low-risk; the entire investment debate is the FY28 normalised earnings power, and the current price already assumes a severe cut to it. **Recommendation bias: Neutral sector / stock-level Constructive, contingent on a credible view that FY28 EPS holds above ~$40.**
