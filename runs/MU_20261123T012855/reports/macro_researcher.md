# Macroeconomic Assessment — Micron Technology (MU)

**As of 20 November 2026 (last close $594.51; mkt cap ~$673B; beta 2.37)**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — "Late-Cycle Reflation with a Hawkish Fed Pivot"

**Stance: RISK-OFF transition inside an inflationary, no-landing regime.**

The US economy is running *above* trend while inflation is re-accelerating, and the Fed has abandoned its easing cycle and turned back to hiking. Q3-2026 real GDP came in at **+3.4%** (advance, 29 Oct), the strongest print since Q2-2025, and unemployment has *fallen* to **4.1%** from 4.5% a year ago. Yet CPI inflation has climbed back to **3.82% y/y** (Oct 2026) from a ~2.6% trough in mid-2025. The Fed cut from 4.33% to 3.63% between Sept-2025 and May-2026, held through September, then **hiked to 3.75% (Sept) and 3.88% effective (Oct)**; Fed funds futures assign ~85% odds to a further 25bp hike in December, taking the target range to 4.00–4.25%.

The bond market has repriced violently, but *not* because of inflation expectations. The 10-year breakeven implied by TIPS (5.04% nominal vs **2.88% real**, 19 Nov) is only **~2.16%** — the market believes inflation converges. Essentially the entire ~70bp rise in long yields since June is a **real-rate/term-premium move**: the 10Y real yield has gone from **2.19% (25 Jun) to 2.88–2.92% (mid-Nov)**, +~70bp in five months. The curve is a **bear steepener** (2Y 4.74%, 10Y 5.13%, 30Y 5.47%; 2s10s +39bp, not inverted) — the classic signature of a market that thinks policy is *behind* the curve on inflation, not that growth is collapsing.

Crucially, **credit is not confirming the equity selloff**: high-yield OAS is **3.52%**, up 87bp from its 2.65% September low but still near the tightest decile in history. This is a **discount-rate event, not a credit or recession event** — a vital distinction for the MU thesis. The S&P 500 has entered correction on AI-spending concerns, and MU has led it down.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Rate sensitivity — STRONGLY NEGATIVE (dominant variable).** MU is among the most rate-sensitive large caps in the market: beta 2.37, a long-duration growth asset whose value is the PV of cash flows that peak in 2028–2030, plus a ~$20B+/yr capex program (Idaho, New York, Japan, Singapore fabs). The arithmetic is unusually clean: the stock's record close of **$1,213.56 (25 Jun)** coincided almost exactly with the **local low in the 10Y real yield (2.19%)**; the **$594.51 (20 Nov)** trough coincides with the **local high (~2.92%)**. A ~70bp real-yield shock has been translated into a **−51% drawdown**. Note the earnings were *rising* throughout. This is pure multiple compression: the discount rate on 2029 terminal value moved, and the equity de-rated by half. Every further 25bp of December tightening is a direct valuation headwind of roughly the same character.

**Growth — NEUTRAL to MILDLY POSITIVE.** 3.4% GDP and 4.1% unemployment support unit demand for servers, PCs, smartphones and autos. But the marginal demand driver is AI datacenter capex, which is *equity-funded and cost-of-capital-sensitive*; the November rout was explicitly "AI spending concerns." Higher real yields lower the NPV of hyperscaler AI infrastructure, which ultimately feeds back into memory order books.

**Inflation / pricing power — POSITIVE on earnings, NEGATIVE on the multiple.** MU is a genuine *price setter* in a shortage: 16Gb DDR5 contract prices went from $8.70 (Oct-25) to $48 (Sep-26), and DRAM contract prices rose ~90%+ cumulatively in 2026. That is a nominal windfall. But the policy response — Fed hikes → higher real yields — has cost the stock more in multiple than it gained in earnings. Meanwhile cost inflation is real: fabs are energy-intensive, electricity and fuel costs are elevated, and labor costs are rising. Micron's cost base is dominated by depreciation (fixed) and energy, so inflation is a second-order margin headwind against a first-order price tailwind.

**Currency — NEGATIVE.** Roughly 80%+ of Micron's revenue is booked outside the US (China, Taiwan, Japan, Korea, Europe). The broad dollar index has risen **~4% in ten weeks to 122.65**, its strongest level in at least two years. On a ~0.8 translation sensitivity, that is a **~3% headwind to reported revenue and ~3–4% to EPS** versus September levels. There is a second, subtler channel: memory is invoiced in USD, so a stronger dollar raises the *local-currency* price of DRAM for Asian OEMs and smartphone/PC makers — amplifying demand destruction and hastening the mid-2027 price rollover that TrendForce and Korean brokers now forecast. Partially offsetting: the **won has appreciated 14% vs USD since June**, eroding Samsung's and SK Hynix's USD cost advantage — a rare relative positive for Micron.

**Memory "commodity" cycle — NEGATIVE and turning.** This is MU's true macro variable, and the leading indicators have rolled: DRAM contract momentum decelerated from +93–98% q/q (Q1-26) → +58–63% (Q2) → +10–15% (Q3) → **+0–5% (Q4)**; TrendForce (19 Nov) forecasts **NAND contract prices declining from Q1-2027** on Chinese CSP order cuts, with NAND spot already −20% and BofA forecasting a further −5–10% in Q1-27. DRAM (HBM-led) stays tight through 2027, but NAND — ~20% of MU revenue — is the first crack.

**Trade/tariff — NEGATIVE (unquantified).** Section 232 semiconductor tariffs and China export-control exposure remain live risks that I could not size with the data available; treat as a tail risk, not a base case.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The macro configuration is close to the worst possible mix for this specific stock: **policy tightening + rising real yields + a strengthening dollar + decelerating memory prices**, aimed at a beta-2.37, capex-heavy, long-duration cyclical whose price momentum is peaking. Three of the four primary transmission channels (discount rate, currency, commodity cycle) are all pointing the wrong way simultaneously — the first time that has been true since this upcycle began.

The rating is *not* "bearish on the company." Offsets are material: GDP at 3.4%, credit spreads at 3.52% (no recession or funding stress), unemployment falling, breakevens anchored at 2.16%, and management's disclosure that **>75% of FY2027 shipments are already contracted at high prices** — near-term earnings are substantially hedged against a spot-price rollover. Micron's problem is the *multiple*, not the *cash flow*, and that is a macro problem, not an operational one.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — BULL (multiple re-rating)

**Consensus is now "hawkish Fed forever + AI capex bust." The non-consensus surprise is a violent, rate-driven re-rating higher in H1-2027.**

**Mechanism:** CPI at 3.82% is being held up by the *level* of energy/fuel costs and tariffs, not by demand — and the market already prices inflation back to 2.16%. If Q1-2027 CPI prints roll toward ~2.8–3.0% on base effects and energy relief, the December hike becomes the last one, the term premium deflates, and the **10Y real yield retraces from 2.90% to ~2.30%**. On the observed ~70bp-to-−51% relationship, a 60bp real-yield *rally* is worth a 40–60% multiple expansion *on its own*. Layer on MU's locked-in FY27 pricing (75% committed) delivering earnings that refuse to fall as fast as feared, a stabilising won, and a still-tight DRAM/HBM market, and the stock could retrace a large part of its drawdown without any improvement in sentiment toward AI. **Trigger to watch: the December FOMC statement plus the January CPI print.**

**Mirror risk (the bear tail):** If the Fed is forced to 4.50–5.00% because inflation sticks above 4%, pushing the 10Y through 5.75% and real yields through 3.25% *while* TrendForce's Q1-2027 NAND decline broadens into DRAM, MU faces the classic double hit — falling estimates *and* a falling multiple. History is unkind here: after three of MU's five prior 40%+ drawdowns, the stock was another 42–68% lower a year later. The tell will be HY spreads — at 3.52% they are calm; a move through ~4.50% would signal the credit channel has finally broken and the bear case has taken over.
