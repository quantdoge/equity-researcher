# Micron Technology (MU) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $594.51 | Market Cap $672.6B | EV ~$641B (net cash ~$31B) | Beta 2.37**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Micron has been converted from a commodity memory cyclical into the scarcity layer of the AI compute stack. HBM is now the binding constraint on GPU throughput (the "memory wall"), and with DRAM/HBM sold out through 2027 under fixed-price contracts — including 2027 HBM supply agreements signed at significant price increases — Micron's revenue went from $37.4B (FY25) to **$133.2B (FY26), +256%**, at an **80.7% gross margin**. The disruptive thesis is genuine: a three-player oligopoly (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron) has replaced a spot-market knife fight with contracted, prepaid capacity. The problem is that this is *price*, not volume — and price is the one variable in memory that always mean-reverts.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

- **HBM TAM:** ~$35B (2025) → **$100B+ by 2028E**. HBM4 36GB stack pricing $600 (2026) → **$1,300 (2027), +117%**.
- **Implied total DRAM TAM** has repriced from ~$90B (2024) to a ~$400B+ run-rate on current contracts. Critical distinction: **this is scarcity repricing, not structural TAM expansion.** Roughly 60–70% of the TAM increase is price, not bits.
- **Micron penetration:** ~20–22% of DRAM, ~10–13% of NAND, and gaining HBM share (targeting low-to-mid 20s%) on HBM3E/HBM4 execution. It remains the #3 DRAM player — a price-taker with a good seat, not the architect.
- **Differentiation:** real but narrow. Micron's edge is 1-beta/1-gamma DRAM process density and packaging capacity, plus US-based fab footprint (CHIPS Act tailwind, supply-chain-sovereignty premium). It does not have TSMC's or ASML's moat; it has an oligopoly seat and a cost curve.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY (medium conviction, cycle-contingent — not a compounder)**

The setup is the most extreme I cover: **3.4x FY27 EPS, 2.8x FY28 EPS, net cash, $59B of FCF in a single year, capacity sold out through 2027 with signed price increases, and a 100% beat rate.** Meanwhile the stock sits 51% below its high. The market is pricing a severe downcycle that the contract data does not yet corroborate. If the Dec 23 print delivers another beat (base case at ~70% probability), the multiple has room to re-rate meaningfully.

Why not higher conviction: this is **price-driven, not volume-driven growth**. Revenue up 256% in a year with sequential growth decelerating 75% → 74% → 31% → 13% is the definition of a cycle, not a durable compounder. An 86.8% gross margin is not a moat — TSMC, a demonstrably better business, runs ~58%. Memory has never once compounded through a full cycle, and the sell-side's own FY2029 estimate models a plateau at $333B before the FY2031 number falls to $156B. A P/B of 4.9x is also not "cheap" on a cyclical basis.

**Position discipline:** size this as a cyclical trade, not a core growth holding. Take profits into the December print. Exit on the first confirmed negative QoQ DRAM contract price print.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Gross margin mean reversion (highest probability, highest magnitude).** 86.8% → a 45–50% mid-cycle margin cuts EPS by 60–70% on modest revenue decline. At $594, a bear case of $70B revenue / 35% GM implies ~$12 EPS = **50x**. This is the dominant risk.
2. **Oversupply 2028–2029.** Capex is rising (FY26 $30.7B = 23% of revenue, FY27 raised). Every player is adding capacity at 80% margins. CNBC flagged oversupply as early as 2029; TrendForce models "dramatic" price drops by mid-2027.
3. **China / CXMT.** Chinese DRAM capacity is the structural break in the oligopoly; it already triggered the October sector drawdown and is outside Micron's control.
4. **AI capex concentration.** Micron's demand is a derivative of four hyperscalers' spend. Any AI spending pause — the Nov 20 selloff was exactly this, with Anthropic's IPO failing to rescue sentiment — hits memory first and hardest.
5. **Estimate quality risk.** 23–25 analysts for FY27/28 is credible, but the FY29–31 dispersion ($241B–$404B) shows the Street has no real visibility past 2028. Anyone modeling $500B of FY2030 revenue for a memory company is guessing.
6. **Extreme volatility.** Beta 2.37; ±14% days are routine. Position sizing must reflect a 50%+ drawdown tolerance.

---

**Bottom line:** Micron's growth is real, its pricing power is temporarily extraordinary, and its multiple is priced for a bust that hasn't arrived. That asymmetry justifies a Buy — but held as a **tactical cyclical position with a defined exit at the first negative contract-price print**, not as a Cathie Wood-style multi-year compounder. The innovation is authentic; the earnings durability is not yet.
