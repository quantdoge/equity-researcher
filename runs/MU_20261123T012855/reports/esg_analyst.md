**Sector:** Technology / Semiconductors (Memory) · **Market cap:** ~$673B · **Employees:** ~61,000 · **HQ:** Boise, ID · **Price:** $594.51 (down ~51% from the $1,213 June 2026 peak)

**Key insight:** For Micron, ESG is *not* an environmental story. It is a **labour-and-governance story with an environmental cost line attached.** The single highest-conviction ESG risk is that record AI profits are being distributed in a way that has unionised two-thirds of the Taiwan workforce.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

**Strengths**
- **70% reduction in Scope 1+2 emissions per unit of production** vs. 2018 baseline (FY2025 reporting) — genuine intensity improvement.
- Net-zero by 2050 commitment; ~$1B environmental investment programme through 2028; targets of 75% water conservation and 95% waste diversion.
- 100% renewable electricity achieved in **Malaysia** (Green Electricity Tariff); Terra-Gen PPA supporting the US goal.
- New Taichung A3 facility built as company's first green building.

**Risks**
- **Intensity is not absolute.** Micron is in a historic capacity build-out ($100B NY megafab, Taiwan expansion, HBM lines). Absolute Scope 1+2 will rise even with falling intensity — the metric that matters to regulators and to customers' Scope 3 accounting.
- **The US "100% renewable energy by end-2025" target** appears at risk of being missed or quietly re-based; I found no confirmation of achievement. *Unverified — treat as a reporting-integrity flag.*
- **PFC abatement**: process GHG + electricity + heat transfer fluids = **91% of total Scope 1+2**. Abatement is capital-intensive and technically hard.
- **Taiwan resource constraint (material):** Greenpeace (Nov-2026) projects Micron/TSMC/UMC at **49.8bn kWh by 2030 — 15% of Taiwan's total power demand** — and 192m m³ of tap water, 8% of residential supply. Taiwan's Mar-2026 rationing curtailed agriculture while industry kept supply: this is a **live political flashpoint** that converts into permit friction and community opposition.
- **Fence-line controversy:** noise measured at ~70 dB near Micron's Taichung/Houli facility; resident complaints of sleep disturbance from 24-hour operations. Localised, but it feeds the social-licence problem below.

**Physical climate risk: High.** Taiwan is typhoon- and drought-exposed, and Micron's DRAM concentration there is a single-geography physical risk that insurance and lenders increasingly price.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH** — *the core issue*

**What happened (2026):**
- **Sept 2026:** Unions representing **nearly two-thirds of Micron's Taiwan staff** threatened strike over the bonus structure.
- **Oct 2026:** *First strike in the history of Taiwan's semiconductor industry* — ~12 workers at Taoyuan walked out for one day. Largely symbolic; enormously significant as precedent (TSMC workers have since discussed organising).
- **Union membership across Micron Taiwan surged from ~440 to over 13,000.**
- **22 Oct 2026:** Micron announced a Taichung deal (higher salaries, quarterly bonuses, larger pension contributions). **Taoyuan union members rejected it**, alleging it was negotiated in secret; majority of Taoyuan members dissatisfied. The 2,300-member Taoyuan union retains the right to strike.
- **Nov 2026:** Dispute escalated to **Premier Cho and Taiwan's Ministry of Labor**; Idaho Governor Brad Little's investment mission drawn into it. Digitimes frames the standoff as a threat to **foreign-investment confidence**.

**The substance:** Unions demand **15% of company profits** to workers. Management offered a **one-time bonus equal to 4–5% of 2026 profits**, with no commitment to reform the Incentive Pay Plan (IPP), which unions call opaque. CHIPS Communities United alleges **anti-union activity and unfair labour practices** — a serious charge given Micron is a CHIPS Act subsidy recipient.

**Quantified financial exposure**
- **The $4.5B Taoyuan DRAM/HBM fab is reported suspended** amid the dispute (Bloomberg, 5-Nov-2026). This is ESG risk that has *already* become capex slippage and HBM capacity-timing risk — the most valuable product line in the portfolio.
- With **>75% of FY2027 shipments already committed**, delivery-reliability doubt is a contracted-revenue risk. Each full week of Taiwan DRAM disruption ≈ ~1.2% of annual output — small in isolation, but memory pricing is set at the margin, so a disruption during a tight HBM market moves price and share.
- **Customer concern is the real transmission mechanism**, per TrendForce: hyperscalers qualifying HBM suppliers weight supply assurance heavily.

**Other social factors**
- **CEO pay ratio 529:1** (CEO $30.94M FY2025, incl. $25.36M stock; median employee $58,461) vs. S&P 500 median ~185:1. This is the *fuel* for the Taiwan dispute and is precisely the "unequal distribution between executives and rank-and-file" the Taoyuan union president cited to press.
- **NY megafab social licence:** ~$20B in public subsidies, $500M Green CHIPS Community Fund, $35.5M new community investment — yet a **25-group coalition** is pressing for a binding community-benefits agreement. Unresolved community demands ahead of a 20-year build.
- **Supply chain labour:** Transparentem (Oct-2026) found excessive recruitment fees creating debt bondage risk among **migrant workers at seven Taiwan electronics manufacturers**. Micron not named, but it is exposed via the same labour pool.
- Safety record: no major fatality/OSHA event surfaced in this review — a relative strength versus historical sector norms.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **5.5 / 10**

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Board independence | **Adequate.** 10 directors, 7 independent (~70%). Lynn Dugle serves as Lead Independent Director. |
| **CEO/Chair duality** | **Negative.** Sanjay Mehrotra was appointed **Chairman in Jan 2025** while retaining President & CEO. Mitigated by the Lead Independent Director, but this is a check-the-box governance deduction and a live proxy-advisor concern. |
| Shareholder rights | **Positive.** Single class of common stock — no dual-class structure, no poison pill identified. Equal voting rights. |
| **Compensation alignment** | **Negative.** 529:1 ratio; heavily equity-weighted ($25.36M of $30.94M in stock — long-term *alignment* is fine, but the *level* is politically and operationally toxic given the labour dispute). |
| **Labour governance** | **Weakest link.** Management negotiated a Taichung deal that its own Taoyuan members rejected as secretive. This signals a genuine capability gap in industrial-relations management — a board-level oversight failure, not a comms failure. |
| Litigation history | 2021 shareholder derivative suit alleging board breach of fiduciary duty over diversity commitments. Recurring derivative-suit exposure is a modest distraction cost. |
| Capital allocation / disclosure | Sustainability report is GRI-referenced; no confirmed CSRD-grade assurance. Transparency adequate, not leading. |

**Verdict:** Structurally sound (independent majority, single share class, lead independent director) but **behaviourally weak on the two things that matter most right now: pay legitimacy and labour relations.** The duality decision in Jan-2025 was a gratuitous own-goal in a year when the pay ratio became a strike trigger.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE, trending toward ESG RISK**

**Justification:** Micron has credible environmental ambition and a structurally acceptable board (single share class, independent majority, lead independent director). But three factors prevent a "Leader" or even a comfortable "Average":

1. **A governance decision (CEO/Chair duality, Jan-2025)** combined with **a 529:1 pay ratio** produced **the first strike in Taiwanese semiconductor history** and the **suspension of a $4.5B fab**. That is ESG risk converting into financial loss in real time — the exact thing this framework exists to detect.
2. **Union density went from ~440 to 13,000+** in months. This is not a transient dispute; it is a structural change in Micron's labour cost base and operating flexibility in its most critical geography.
3. **Absolute emissions and Taiwan water/power demand are rising** through the largest capacity build-out in company history, in a jurisdiction where resource allocation is becoming politically contested.

I would place Micron in the **bottom half of large-cap semiconductor peers** on S and G, and **mid-pack on E**. It is not an ESG pariah — nothing here is fraud or environmental catastrophe — but it is an ESG *laggard on labour governance* with unhedged operational consequence.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE (modifier, not a veto)**

**This is not a reason to avoid MU. It is a reason to size and time the position differently.**

**Where ESG changes the thesis:**
- **It adds a non-market risk to a stock already down ~51% from June.** Micron's bull case rests on >75% of FY2027 shipments being committed and HBM pricing power. Both assumptions require *supply reliability*. A Taoyuan strike, or a prolonged renegotiation, directly attacks the one variable the thesis cannot tolerate: delivery certainty.
- **It caps the re-rating.** ESG-aware capital and proxy advisors will press the duality and the pay ratio. Expect a meaningful "against" vote on say-on-pay and a governance-committee campaign at the 2027 AGM. That's a persistent, low-grade discount-rate irritant.
- **It converts a soft target into a hard cost.** The 15% profit-share demand, if partially conceded (say toward 8–10%), is a permanent step-up in Taiwan labour cost — thin in gross-margin terms for DRAM, but permanent, and it will spread to TSMC and peers. Model it as a structural opex headwind, not a one-off.
- **Environmental capex is non-discretionary.** PFC abatement, reclaimed water, and renewable procurement in Taiwan are now licence-to-operate spending. Upside: this is a *relative* advantage against smaller memory players who cannot fund it.

**What would move this to NEUTRAL or POSITIVE:**
1. A **binding, transparent profit-sharing agreement** ratified by the Taoyuan union — resolves the strike overhang and restores the $4.5B fab timeline.
2. **Separation of the Chair and CEO roles**, or a credible Lead Independent Director with an explicit labour-oversight mandate.
3. **Published absolute (not just intensity) Scope 1+2 targets** with third-party assurance, and confirmation or honest re-basing of the missed US 100%-renewable goal.
4. A **signed community-benefits agreement in Central New York** ahead of megafab ramp.

**Monitoring triggers (next 2 quarters):** Taoyuan union strike ballot; Taoyuan fab construction restart; FY2026 say-on-pay support level; Taiwan water-allocation notices; any US labour-board complaint over alleged anti-union conduct.

**Bottom line for the memo:** Treat MU's ESG profile as an **operational-risk overlay on a cyclical AI-memory thesis** — specifically, a ~$4.5B already-realised capex timing hit and an unquantified but non-trivial delivery-assurance discount. Do not let the post-drawdown valuation screen mask that the governance and labour failures are *self-inflicted and recurring*, and that they sit precisely on top of the company's most critical manufacturing geography.
