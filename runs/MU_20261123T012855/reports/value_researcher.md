## MICRON TECHNOLOGY (MU) — VALUE INVESTMENT ANALYSIS
**Price $594.51 | Market cap $679.5B | Net cash ~$33B | EV ~$646B | FY2026 ended 3-Sep-26**

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: NARROW (improving)

Micron is one of only three scaled DRAM makers (with Samsung and SK Hynix) and a top-tier NAND producer. The moat rests on **cost advantage from scale** (FY26 capex $30.7B, PP&E $63.3B) and **intangible/technology assets** (1-gamma DRAM, HBM4, G9 NAND). The past five years show the moat's limits and its direction:

| FY | Revenue | Gross margin | Operating margin | ROIC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2022 | $30.8B | 45.2% | 31.5% | — |
| 2023 | $15.5B | **-9.1%** | -37.0% | **-9.9%** |
| 2024 | $25.1B | 22.4% | 5.2% | 2.2% |
| 2025 | $37.4B | 39.8% | 26.1% | 14.1% |
| 2026 | $133.2B | **80.7%** | 74.6% | **69.2%** |

A genuine wide-moat business does not post a negative gross margin and negative ROIC in a downturn. That is the defining evidence against a Wide rating. **However, the moat has clearly widened**: 75% of 2027 output is already sold under fixed-price contracts, HBM is sold out into 2028, and the industry has consolidated to three rational suppliers. That is real structural improvement — but it is *untested* through a full down-cycle. Narrow moat, widening trend, not yet proven durable.

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Strengths:** FY26 revenue $133.2B (+256%), operating income $99.3B, net income $85.0B, diluted EPS $74.33. FCF $59.0B (OCF $89.7B less capex $30.7B) — FCF/net income conversion of 69%, solid for a capital-intensive fab business. Fortress balance sheet: cash $43.4B vs total debt $5.2B, net cash ~$33B, equity $138.4B. Interest expense a trivial $106M.

**Deductions:** Volatility is extreme — revenue fell 49% in FY23 and gross margin swung 900bp negative. Owner earnings are wildly cyclical: -$5.75B (FY23), $0.17B (FY24), $1.03B (FY25), $63.8B (FY26); the **4-year average is only ~$14.8B**. Working capital absorbed $6.1B in FY26. Diluted shares rose from 1.118B (FY24) to 1.143B (FY26) — net dilution despite $1.8B of buybacks. Dividend is token ($0.60/yr, 0.1% yield).

### 3. Valuation Analysis

| Metric | MU | Peer median (8 tech/semis) |
|---|---|---|
| P/E | 8.0x | 27.0x (-70%) |
| P/B | 4.91x | — |
| EV/EBITDA | 5.89x | — |
| P/FCF | 11.5x | — |
| FCF yield | 8.68% | — |

The peer set (NVDA, TSM, AVGO, AAPL) is not the right benchmark — the true memory comparables are SK Hynix ($735B) and Samsung, which also trade at low peak-cycle multiples. **The 8x P/E is not a bargain signal; it is the market's standard discount for peak-cycle earnings.**

**DCF (peak FCF $59.0B base, 5% growth, 12% WACC, 3% terminal):** intrinsic value **$732.6B ≈ $641/share** — only ~8% above the current price. And that is a *generous* DCF because it capitalizes peak-cycle cash flow.

**Normalized (mid-cycle) valuation — my base case:** revenue ~$140B, ~45% gross margin, ~$45B operating income, ~$40B net income, at 12x = **$480B ≈ $420/share**. Bear case (glut, $90B revenue, 25% GM) ≈ $105–150/share. Bull case (oligopoly holds, $280B revenue at 60% GM) ≈ $1,300+/share.

**Base-case intrinsic value ≈ $420–500/share vs $594.51 today → the stock trades ~20–25% ABOVE my base case.** No margin of safety. The market is already capitalizing near-permanent super-cycle earnings.

### 4. Management Quality — Good, with one yellow flag

CEO Sanjay Mehrotra (since 2017, SanDisk founder) has executed superbly: HBM qualification, technology leadership, net-cash balance sheet, and Q1 FY27 guidance of $61.5B revenue at ~86% gross margin. Capital allocation is **conservative to a fault** — only $1.8B buybacks and $0.61B dividends against $59B of FCF (4% total return of capital), with the remainder funding capex. Prudent at a cycle peak, but share count is still rising.

**Yellow flag:** consistent insider selling into strength — Mehrotra sold ~$38M at ~$969 and ~$46M at ~$1,158 under 10b5-1 plans. Defensible as diversification, but it is not the behavior of an owner-operator who thinks the stock is cheap.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (Trim / Do Not Add)

This is a **good business at a bad price** — the mirror image of the value investor's ideal. The structural story is real (HBM contracted, sold out through 2027–28, oligopoly discipline, net cash), but the shares discount a cycle that has never persisted. At 4.9x book and 8x peak earnings, with a base-case intrinsic value 20–25% below the current quote, there is no margin of safety.

- **Buy zone:** below ~$400/share (≈12x normalized earnings, ~3.3x book)
- **Trim zone:** above ~$650/share
- **Current stance:** Hold existing positions for contracted FY27 revenue; do not commit new capital.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Cycle inversion is already visible** — NAND spot prices began slipping in late 2026 even as contract prices rose; the stock fell ~45% from its ~$1,211 June high and ~39% from $1,097 on 1-Oct. Spot weakness leads contract renegotiation by 2–4 quarters.
2. **Capex arms race** — FY27 capex guided above FY26's $30.7B, with industry-wide capacity additions. Supply typically arrives 18–24 months later, into 2028 — exactly when today's sold-out contracts roll off.
3. **Customer concentration / AI capex digestion** — hyperscaler spending cuts would hit HBM pricing and utilization simultaneously.
4. **Fixed-price contract risk** — if spot falls below contract, customers inventory-correct or seek renegotiation; "sold out" is not "locked in."
5. **Asset support is thin** — 4.9x book vs a 1.2–2.5x historical range; a reversion to 2.5x book on $121 BVPS implies ~$300/share.
6. **Geopolitics** — China's CXMT capacity ramp, export controls, and Korea/Taiwan competition.
7. **Insider selling** at $969–1,158 while retail bought the dip.

**Bottom line:** Micron's fundamentals have genuinely improved, but the price already pays for the improvement and more. Discipline says wait for the cycle — and the price — to come to you.
