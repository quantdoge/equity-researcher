**China + Hong Kong = $14.63B, ~11.0% of revenue** (247wallst.com, citing FY2026 10-K, Nov 16 2026). COO Manish Bhatia guided on the Sept 30, 2026 call that China exposure "will be in the single-digit range in fiscal '27."

**Two critical nuances:**
- **Revenue-by-HQ understates real exposure.** US revenue reflects hyperscaler/OEM headquarters, not end destination; a large share of "US" and "Taiwan" revenue physically ships into Asian assembly and ultimately to Chinese end-demand. Micron's China footprint is therefore meaningfully larger than 11% on a ship-to/destination basis.
- **Hong Kong is rising while mainland falls** ($1.07B→$6.29B over two years). That pattern is consistent with routing/transshipment around the mainland CII ban and warrants monitoring as an export-control enforcement red flag in its own right.

**Manufacturing footprint — the bigger geopolitical fact than revenue.** ~15 sites: DRAM fabs in **Hiroshima, Japan** ($9.3B expansion, groundbreaking July 2026, production 2028) and **Taiwan** (Tainan/Taoyuan, plus the $1.8B PSMC Tongluo P5 fab acquired March 2026); NAND in **Singapore**; back-end/ATMP in **Sanand, Gujarat, India** ($2.75B, opened Feb 28, 2026, ~70% government-subsidised) and **Muhamma/Hyderabad**; US front-end at Boise, ID and Manassas, VA. Leading-edge DRAM and HBM capacity is effectively concentrated in **Taiwan and Japan** — a single-corridor concentration that no amount of revenue diversification fixes.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Escalating US export controls on AI compute compressing the AI memory cycle (Probability: High, ~60–70% of further tightening in FY27; Impact: High)**
On **November 16, 2026**, Commerce/BIS issued an interim final rule requiring export licences for advanced AI chips and servers destined for data centres in **Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil**, targeting remote-access workarounds by China-headquartered entities; it also rescinds the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule and carries a 90-day transition (Crypto Briefing; 24/7 Wall St., Nov 16 2026). MU fell 5–8% on the news. This matters disproportionately because **Core Data Center ($18.0B) and Cloud Memory ($16.3B) were over 60% of Q4 FY26 revenue** — Micron's growth engine *is* the policy target. Related: since Dec 2024 HBM exports to China require licences; in January 2026 BIS shifted advanced-computing licence review for China/Macau from presumption-of-denial to case-by-case with conditions (Federal Register 2026-00789) — a modest easing that is politically reversible.

**Risk 2 — Cross-strait / Taiwan production concentration (Probability: Low, ~3–5% p.a. of a material disruption; Impact: Severe/catastrophic)**
A majority of Micron's leading-edge DRAM and its HBM advanced-packaging chain sits in Taiwan. A blockade, quarantine, or kinetic event would remove capacity that cannot be re-sourced from Hiroshima or Singapore inside a year. Taiwan is also tightening its own controls — reportedly extending advanced-chip export curbs to *all* China customers rather than designated entities (Global Taiwan Institute, Sept 2026) — which cuts both ways for a US-IDM operating on the island.

**Risk 3 — Chinese retaliation targeted at Micron specifically (Probability: Medium, ~30–40%; Impact: Medium)**
The **CAC's May 2023 finding** that Micron products pose "serious network security risks," barring critical-information-infrastructure operators from purchasing them, remains the template and has never been lifted (Reuters, CSIS, DigiChina). China has since built out blocking rules and, per Baker McKenzie (Nov 2026), expanded export-control investigation powers. With the Nov 2026 Trump–Xi summit producing a truce that Eurasia Group's Ian Bremmer expects to be extended, near-term retaliation risk is contained — but he explicitly flags that *tighter chip restrictions could prompt measured Chinese retaliation*, and Micron is the highest-profile, most-sanctionable US memory name.

**Also live:** the **Section 232 semiconductor tariff** — a 25% ad valorem duty on certain advanced computing chips and derivative "covered products," effective January 15, 2026 (White & Case; EY; CBP guidance). Standalone DRAM/NAND appear not to be directly covered per at least one secondary analysis, and management said in March 2026 it saw no remaining operational impact and negligible cost impact (Marvin Labs). Residual exposure is via derivative products, Section 232 metals duties on inputs, and retaliation risk.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Hong Kong revenue 6× mainland growth divergence** — a 4.7% HK share alongside collapsing mainland share is the classic signature of re-routing. Any of that volume reaching banned CII end-users would be a BIS/OFAC problem, not just a commercial one.
2. **Export-control classification risk on HBM** — Micron is one of three global HBM suppliers; performance-density thresholds make each new product generation a fresh licensing question. Peer benchmark: BIS fined Applied Materials $252M (Feb 2026), second-largest penalty ever, and Seagate $300M (2023) — enforcement is real and large.
3. **AI-diffusion rule exposes third-country distributors** — new licences for Singapore/Malaysia/Thailand/Indonesia/Brazil data centres create distributor-diligence obligations Micron must now evidence.
4. **Subsidy-condition compliance** (India ~70% funding, Japan, US CHIPS) — audit and clawback exposure.
5. **No FCPA/AML matter identified** — but a 61,000-employee operation with mainland China sales and HK intermediaries is inherently FCPA-exposed; unverified absent Item 3.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

- **Pure legal/litigation risk: MEDIUM-LOW.** The Netlist settlement ($600M) retires the single largest known contingency, and no DOJ/SEC/antitrust action surfaced. This is the least problematic pillar.
- **Regulatory/policy risk: HIGH.** Micron is a named instrument of US–China chip policy in both directions: it is simultaneously a beneficiary of US controls on Chinese memory rivals and a casualty of controls on the AI demand that now drives >60% of revenue. That asymmetry — record FY26 ($133.2B revenue, $37.7B net income, 87% non-GAAP gross margin) against a stock down ~51% from its June 2026 $1,213 high — is the market pricing exactly this.
- **Geographic/geopolitical risk: HIGH.** 11% direct China+HK revenue (guided to single digits), ~83% of revenue in Asia-HQ'd customers or Asian-destination supply chains, and leading-edge DRAM/HBM capacity concentrated in Taiwan and Japan.

**Net:** legal risk is de-risking (Netlist resolved); policy risk is escalating (Nov 2026 BIS rule, Section 232, Taiwan's own China curbs). I would weight the rating **High**, driven by export-control optionality on the AI memory cycle and the Taiwan capacity concentration rather than by any pending lawsuit. Legal/regulatory disclosure quality is the one gap — Item 3 should be read directly from the FY2026 10-K before publication.
