## 4. DOWNSIDE SCENARIO ANALYSIS

**Scenario A — Mild recession, revenue −20% ($133.2B → $106.5B)**
Memory cost of goods is largely fixed (fab depreciation). Holding COGS near $27B and opex at ~$8.2B:
- Gross profit ≈ $79.5B (GM 74.6%), Operating income ≈ **$71.3B** (down ~28%)
- EBITDA ≈ $81B; interest coverage still >750x; net cash position unchanged
- **Verdict: financially untouchable.** Solvency, liquidity, and covenants are unaffected.

**Scenario B — Full memory downcycle, revenue −60% ($133.2B → $53B, toward prior trough levels of $25–37B)**
With COGS sticky near $34B and ASP compression:
- Gross margin compresses to ~35%, Operating income ≈ **$11B**, net income ≈ $9B
- Net cash still intact (debt $5.2B vs. cash $43B)
- **Verdict: still profitable and solvent — but earnings collapse ~90% from peak.** This is the scenario that matters for equity: a 90% EPS decline on a $673B market cap is catastrophic for the share price even though the company survives comfortably. This is precisely what happened in FY2023–24 (net income fell to $778M).

The key insight: **Micron's balance sheet is built to survive a downcycle; its share price is not priced to survive one.**

## 5. OVERALL FINANCIAL RISK RATING

### **CREDIT RISK: LOW | EQUITY / INVESTMENT RISK: HIGH → Composite: MODERATE-HIGH**

The two ratings diverge sharply and must not be conflated. As a *going-concern credit*, Micron is essentially risk-free. As an *equity investment*, it carries elevated downside because (a) it trades on peak-cycle earnings, (b) volatility/beta are extreme, and (c) the current earnings power is not structurally defensible in a commodity memory market.

## 6. KEY RED FLAGS

1. **Peak-cycle earnings distortion.** 74.6% operating margin and 256% revenue growth are cyclically unsustainable; a reversion toward mid-cycle margins is the dominant financial risk. Trailing P/E of ~7.9x is illusory — it is calculated on $75.38 peak EPS. On normalized EPS ($10–15), the multiple is **40–60x**.
2. **Realized 51% drawdown + 73% vol + 2.54 beta.** The stock has already demonstrated catastrophic downside; further market- or cycle-driven declines are highly probable.
3. **FCF is wildly cyclical, not a stable yield.** FCF swung from **$121M (FY24) → $1.67B (FY25) → $58.96B (FY26)**. The 8.68% FCF yield is a peak artifact, not a durable return.
4. **Severe end-market concentration.** Revenue is levered to AI/data-center/HBM capex by hyperscalers — a pause in AI spend would hit both volumes and pricing simultaneously.
5. **Minor dilution.** Piotroski F7 (no new shares) scored 0 — shares outstanding rose (1.105B → 1.127B), a modest negative.
