# Tempus AI, Inc. (NASDAQ: TEM) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price (Nov 2, 2026 close): $78.48 | Market cap: ~$13.9B (177M sh) | Net debt: $665M | EV: ~$14.6B**
*Note: TEM is a June-2024 IPO with ~2.3 years of public history. Data through Q3 2026 (filed 11/02/2026). Q3 cash-flow detail shows sign/bookkeeping inconsistencies in the feed — I flag rather than rely on those line items.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Narrow (unproven)**

**What it is:** ~78% of revenue is a molecular diagnostics laboratory (oncology NGS panels xT/xR, hereditary testing via the Ambry acquisition, PCR, pathology) — a high-volume, low-margin lab business. ~22% is "Data & Applications": licensing de-identified multimodal clinical/genomic/imaging data and AI models to pharma (AstraZeneca, BMS — up to $137.5M over five years, GSK, Merck, Recursion, Pathos). That second piece is the actual investment thesis; it is also the small piece.

**Genuine moat candidates:**
- **Data scale/network effect**: one of the largest linked clinical+molecular+imaging repositories in oncology. Real and hard to replicate quickly; a plausible flywheel (more tests → more data → better models → better tests).
- **Switching costs**: the "Hub" EHR-embedded ordering platform across a large hospital footprint. Modest — oncologists routinely order from multiple labs.
- **Intangibles**: Medicare coverage determinations, FDA-approved companion diagnostics.
- **Cost advantage**: large DNA+RNA panels run at scale.

**Evidence the moat is NOT wide:**
- **Gross margin is 62.7% (FY25) / 72.3% (Q3'26)** — lab economics, not software. A wide-moat data asset would show up here; it partially has, but only recently, and revenue mix (data deals) explains most of the swing.
- **ROIC has been negative every year as a public company** (FY25: −13.9%; FY24: −131.8%). Buffett's test — ROIC > WACC consistently — is simply failed. There is not one year of evidence.
- **Diagnostics is brutally competitive**: Foundation Medicine/Roche, Guardant, Natera, Caris, NeoGenomics, Exact Sciences. Pricing is set by CMS/payers, not by Tempus.
- **Data revenue is concentrated and contractual**: a handful of pharma counterparties, some of them related parties (Pathos AI). A moat that rests on ~10 contracts that must be renewed is a *deal pipeline*, not a moat.

**Verdict:** A legitimate, fast-growing, category-leading company — with a *narrow, as-yet-unproven* moat. The bull case requires you to underwrite the data flywheel 3–5 years before the income statement confirms it. That is venture capital, not value investing.

**Negatives that matter:**

**Score 2/10** — high growth and improving margins earn the two points; negative owner earnings, no ROIC history, and 13% SBC-to-revenue cost it the rest.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples (FY26E revenue $1.60B, adj. EBITDA ~$65M):**
- EV/Revenue: **9.1x** (vs. Zacks sub-industry ~5.6x — a ~60% premium; the stock has re-rated from ~5.5x when it traded in the high-$40s)
- EV/Adjusted EBITDA: **224x**
- EV/EBITDA (GAAP, SBC expensed): **negative**
- P/FCF: **meaningless** (FCF ≈ $0)
- P/B: **27.8x** (book equity $500M, of which $782M is goodwill/intangibles — tangible book is **negative**)

**Intrinsic value — three independent methods:**

*(a) Owner-earnings DCF (base case):* $1.60B revenue today; growth fading 22% → 13% through 2031; FCF margin ramping 0% → 15% by 2035; 9% WACC, 3% terminal. → **EV ~$8.4B, equity ~$7.7B ≈ $43/share.**

*(b) Sum of the parts:* Diagnostics (~$1.25B revenue, lab economics) at 4x sales = $5.0B; Data & Applications (~$350M, 90%+ incremental margin) at 10x sales = $3.5B. → **EV ~$8.5B ≈ $44/share.** Two different methods converge — that's a useful signal.

*(c) Scenario weighting:*
| Scenario | Assumptions | Value/sh | Prob. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | Growth fades to 15%, margins stall at 8%, reimbursement pressure | $25 | 20% |
| Base | 20% CAGR to ~$3.6B, 15% FCF margin at scale | $45 | 55% |
| Bull | 30% CAGR to ~$6B, 22% operating margin, 28x exit | $97 | 25% |
| **Expected value** | | **~$54** | |

**Current price $78.48 is ~45% above my base case and ~45% above probability-weighted value.** The market is discounting the bull case as if it were the base case. Sell-side targets cluster at $66–$92 (median ~$75–80) — i.e., the street itself sees fair value *at today's price*, which is not a reason to own something.

**What the price implies:** to earn a 10% return, TEM must compound revenue at ~25–26% for the next decade *and* reach a 15% FCF margin — i.e., ~$12–14B of revenue by 2035. That's not impossible for a category leader in genomic data, but it is the outcome you must be *certain* of, and I'm not. There is zero margin of safety; there is a negative one.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment: **4 / 10**

- **Founder-led and operationally formidable.** Eric Lefkofsky built and scaled Catamaran (sold to UNH), Echo Global Logistics, and MediaBank; Tempus went from zero to $1.6B revenue in ten years. Execution credibility is high, and the Ambry Genetics deal (hereditary/germline testing, added meaningful revenue and cross-sell) looks strategically sound.
- **But the shareholder record is mixed at best.** Groupon — Lefkofsky's highest-profile public company — destroyed enormous shareholder value post-IPO. Investors should not ignore the pattern of promotional communication and aggressive non-GAAP framing.
- **Alignment is weak.** Dual/triple-class super-voting structure concentrates control with the founder; the board is not independent in spirit. **Related-party dealings are a live concern**: Tempus licenses data to and invests alongside Pathos AI, a company Lefkofsky is personally involved with — precisely the kind of circular revenue that demands a skeptical haircut to the "Data & Applications" growth line.
- **Capital allocation:** no dividend, no meaningful buyback ($3M in 2025 — derisory against $125M of SBC), a history of stock-funded M&A, and $1.26B of converts. Share count rises ~6% a year. Management is *funding growth with shareholders' ownership*, which is rational for them and costly for you.
- Insiders sell under 10b5-1 plans; there is no evidence of meaningful open-market insider buying at these levels.

Owner-operator *instinct*, yes. Owner-operator *economics for outside shareholders*, not demonstrated.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

This is a real company with a real product, real growth, and a plausible long-term data moat. It is also, at $78.48, a **pre-profit, cash-burning, 9x-sales lab business with 13% SBC dilution, a negative tangible book value, a 3.6 beta, and no demonstrated year of ROIC above its cost of capital** — priced for a bull case I can't underwrite with confidence.

Applying the two rules that matter: (1) *never pay full price* — this is 45% above my base-case intrinsic value; (2) *circle of competence* — I can value the lab; I cannot underwrite the AI data flywheel with the precision required to pay 9x sales for it.

**What would change my mind:**
- Price below **$35–40** (base case less a 20–25% margin of safety), *and*
- Evidence of the flywheel in the P&L: FCF margin >8% on a trailing basis, SBC <8% of revenue, ROIC positive and rising, and Data & Applications revenue diversified beyond a handful of related-party pharma contracts.

The business is on a credible path toward that. It is simply priced as if it has already arrived.

## 6. Key Risks to My (Bearish) Thesis

**Risks to being too negative (the stock could keep winning):**
1. **Data licensing is hyper-scalable.** If even two or three more BMS/AstraZeneca-scale deals land, Data & Applications could double and add 1,500bps of blended gross margin with almost no cost — the P&L would inflect violently and 2028 FCF could beat my base case by 2x.
2. **Adjusted EBITDA turned positive a year earlier than the market expected**, and Q3 free cash flow was positive. If the 2027 guide implies >$150M of adjusted EBITDA, my "no earnings power" premise weakens materially.
3. **Strategic scarcity value.** Roche, AstraZeneca, or a large diagnostics player could pay a control premium for the data asset. The stock has a takeover floor that my DCF ignores.
4. **Clinical/regulatory wins** (expanded Medicare coverage, new FDA companion diagnostic approvals) would raise realized price per test without adding cost.

**Risks that support my caution (and that the market is under-pricing):**
1. **Reimbursement compression.** ~78% of revenue is lab testing exposed to CMS/MolDx rate setting and payer utilization management. A single unfavorable coverage decision moves the entire model.
2. **Customer concentration and related-party optics.** If Pathos-type data deals are re-examined or fail to renew, the highest-margin revenue line unwinds first.
3. **Financing risk.** Negative tangible equity, $1.26B of converts, and a business that only just reached breakeven FCF. Another downturn-plus-acquisition combination means another equity raise at whatever price the market offers.
4. **Extreme volatility** (beta 3.6; a $42 → $103 → $42 → $85 round trip in 18 months). A 50% drawdown is a routine event here, not a tail risk — and position sizing at this volatility is nearly impossible for a fundamental holder.

**Bottom line:** Avoid. Quality: improving but unproven. Price: egregious. Margin of safety: negative. Revisit at $35–40 or on two years of GAAP-profitable, SBC-adjusted free cash flow — whichever comes first.
