# Macroeconomic Assessment: Tempus AI, Inc. (NASDAQ: TEM)
**As of 3 November 2026 | Price $78.48 | Market cap $13.9B | Beta 3.61**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Risk-off in duration, risk-on in credit — a "reflation-with-a-hawkish-Fed" regime.**

This is not the 2024–25 easing cycle. The Fed **raised** the target range 25bp to **3.75%–4.00% on 16 September 2026** — the first hike in three years — and **held on 28 October**, with 16 of 18 officials signalling further tightening. Effective fed funds sits at **3.88% (Oct)** having bottomed at 3.63% mid-year.

The critical feature for a long-duration equity is *what* is driving the long end. The 10-year has moved from ~4.44% (30 June) to **5.13%** — a level not seen in roughly two decades — while **10-year breakevens have fallen** from 2.49% (19 May) to **2.29% (2 Nov)**. In other words, **the entire ~60bp back-up in yields is real yields**, not inflation expectations. The 10-year real yield is now ~2.60%. This is the most hostile possible configuration for assets whose value sits in a distant terminal cash flow: the discount rate is rising precisely because the market believes policy will stay tight.

Yet credit is calm: **high-yield OAS at 3.44%** remains near cycle tights, though it has widened ~79bp from 2.65% (1 Sept) — an early divergence worth watching. Growth is solid (Q3 real GDP **+3.4% annualized**, +2.1% YoY), unemployment is **4.2%**, and CPI has **re-accelerated to 3.72% YoY (Sept)** from 3.30% in July. This is a late-cycle, above-trend-growth, sticky-inflation, hawkish-policy mix.

For TEM specifically, this is a headwind-dominant environment: a 3.6-beta, GAAP-unprofitable, ~200x EV/EBITDA asset financed partly by convertibles, walking into a rising real-yield regime.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

TEM's business model has three macro transmission channels: **(a)** the discount rate on a long-duration, largely unprofitable equity; **(b)** U.S. diagnostic reimbursement volumes and rates; **(c)** biopharma R&D budgets that fund the high-margin Data & Applications segment (+33% YoY, >71% gross margin).

| Macro Variable | Impact on TEM | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Real yields / discount rate** | **STRONGLY NEGATIVE** | This is the dominant channel. At Rf 5.13% and beta 3.61, CAPM implies a ~21% required return (4.5% ERP) — an extraordinary hurdle for a company guiding to only ~$65M FY26 adj. EBITDA against a $13.9B market cap (~8.5x EV/Sales, ~200x EV/EBITDA). Every 50bp of real-yield increase mechanically compresses the terminal value. TEM's 3.6 beta acts as a lever on this. |
| **Fed policy path (hikes, not cuts)** | **NEGATIVE** | The forward curve has *removed* the cut option. There is no policy easing to rescue duration in 2027 unless growth breaks. Two FOMC dissents in October show the committee leaning hawkish, not accommodative. |

Offset: mix shift into D&A (71%+ GM) is a structural margin tailwind that has so far outrun the cost pressure. |
| **GDP growth / employment** | **POSITIVE** | Solid +3.4% GDP and 4.2% unemployment underpin oncology test volumes, hospital lab budgets and clinical trial starts. No recession signal in the data. TEM's Q3 +23% revenue growth (+32% oncology unit growth) is a cyclical as well as secular story. |

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

TEM is the archetypal long-duration asset in a rising-real-yield, hawkish-policy regime. The company is executing exceptionally well — Q3 revenue of **$411.5M (+23%)**, adjusted EPS of **+$0.07** versus a **−$0.11** loss a year ago, positive adjusted EBITDA and a raised full-year outlook — and yet the stock is **~24% below its October 2025 level of ~$103** and still trades at ~200x EV/EBITDA. When a company beats and raises and the multiple still compresses, macro is doing the damage.

The empirical duration is visible: TEM fell **−25%** from $85.08 (28 Sept) to $63.81 (22 Oct) across the Fed's hawkish pivot and the long-end selloff, before the Q3 beat drove +11% on 2 Nov to $78.48. Note honestly, however, that short-window moves are dominated by idiosyncratic factors (Personalis deal anxiety, the CEO's 250,000-share sale on 27 Oct, AI-sector de-rating). **Macro is the amplifier, not the trigger** — but with a 3.6 beta, the amplifier is set very high.

**What is not a risk:** recession. Growth is too strong, spreads too tight, and unemployment too low for a near-term hard landing. The risk is the opposite — that policy must stay restrictive for longer.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**Bear surprise: disinflation fails and the Fed is forced to 4.75–5.00% by mid-2027.**

Markets are positioned for the opposite. Breakevens at **2.29%** say inflation normalises; HY OAS at **3.44%** says credit is pristine; the 2Y at 4.74% prices a modest, terminal hike cycle. If CPI (already 3.72% and *rising*) breaks above 4% on services and shelter, all three assumptions fail together. The Fed would extend to 4.75–5.00%, the 10Y would break 6.00% and the 30Y toward 6.25%, and HY spreads would widen from 3.44% toward 5%+.

In that world TEM is the worst-possible holding: required return under CAPM would exceed **22–23%**, the $1.5B Personalis close becomes expensive to finance, and the equity — which has already demonstrated a 60% drawdown from Oct 2025 ($103) to Jul 2026 ($40.77) in a *less* hostile rate environment — could de-rate 40–50% toward the high-$30s/high-$40s even with revenue compounding at 20%+. The tell to watch is the convergence of **10Y breakevens rising back above 2.60%** while **HY OAS widens past 4.00%** — those two moving together would be the regime-change signal, and neither is priced.

*The offsetting bull tail (worth flagging for balance): if the September hike proves to be the terminal one and breakevens are right, a 75–100bp decline in real yields into 2027 would re-rate every long-duration growth asset violently, and TEM's 3.6 beta would deliver that in reverse — a $78 stock could plausibly retest $110+ on a policy pivot combined with the Personalis close and 20%+ organic growth. The asymmetry is real in both directions, which is why position sizing, not direction, is the macro call here.*
