## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: SUB-INVESTMENT GRADE (speculative / crossover)**

TEM is a high-growth, loss-making diagnostics and data platform that has scaled revenue rapidly (FY25 $1,271.8M, +83% YoY) but has never generated positive EBITDA, EBIT, net income, or free cash flow. On a *pure credit* view the profile is speculative: **EBITDA is negative (−$118.5M), EBIT is negative (−$220.8M), and interest coverage is −3.1x**. Leverage has risen in absolute terms — total debt grew from $468.1M to **$1,327.1M** (+184%), largely to fund the acquisitions that drove goodwill/intangibles to **$825.5M** (37% of assets).

Two factors keep this out of "Distress" on a liquidity basis:
- **Liquidity is genuinely strong**: cash $755.0M, current ratio 3.13x, cash ratio 2.03x, working capital $794.8M.
- **The debt is cheap and long-dated**: TEM issued **0.75% Convertible Senior Notes due 2030** (upsized to ~$750M), so the near-term refinancing cliff is *not* the issue — 2030 is the wall. This is a materially better structure than a floating-rate term loan.

However, the credit is structurally weak: **equity cushion is thin ($491.3M, 21.6% of assets)** and **tangible book equity is deeply negative (−$334.2M)** once $825.5M of goodwill/intangibles are stripped out. The $2,395.9M accumulated deficit means the balance sheet is propped up by paid-in capital, not retained earnings.

> ⚠️ **Data tension flagged:** Reported interest expense of **$70.3M** is *inconsistent* with a 0.75% coupon on ~$750M of converts (~$5.6M cash interest). The $70.3M almost certainly blends non-cash issuance-cost amortization and/or higher-cost acquisition debt. This overstates the cash interest burden but makes the *economic* debt cost opaque. I could not obtain a **formal credit rating** — no Moody's/S&P public rating was found; TEM appears unrated.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** TEM combines negative EBITDA, negative FCF, a distress-zone Altman score, negative tangible equity, and 75% volatility with a beta of 2.5–3.6 — a genuinely fragile combination for a levered, unprofitable company. It is **not** "Critical" because liquidity is real ($755M cash, 3.1x current ratio), the debt is cheap (0.75% converts) and long-dated (2030), gross margins are strong and improving (55%→63%), and revenue is compounding at 83%. The risk is that growth is the *only* thing holding the capital structure together.

**Credit → Sub-Investment Grade. Overall financial risk → HIGH.**

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Never generated positive operating cash flow in any year shown** (2023–2025: −$214M, −$189M, −$218M). FCF has been **flat at ~−$245M for three years despite revenue growing 2.4x** — there is **no operating leverage in cash conversion** yet. This is the single most important negative.
2. **Interest coverage is negative (−3.1x).** Debt service depends entirely on the cash balance, not operations.
3. **Dilution is ongoing** — shares +13% since 2023, SBC 9.8% of revenue, and Piotroski flags dilution (F7 fail). Equity raises are the likely funding path.
4. **Debt nearly tripled in one year** ($468M → $1,327M) to fund M&A; **goodwill/intangibles now $825.5M (37% of assets)** and **tangible equity is negative (−$334M)**. Impairment risk is real if acquired assets underperform.
5. **Working capital is a large cash drag** — FY25 change in WC was **−$178.5M**, driven by receivables (+$165M). Growth is consuming cash faster than it earns it; receivables growth outpacing revenue warrants a DSO check.
6. **Altman Z = −0.65 (distress zone)** — caveated by book-equity methodology, but the negative-EBIT and accumulated-deficit components are real.
7. **Extreme market risk**: 75% vol, beta 2.5–3.6, −59.8% max drawdown, ~10.9x sales on negative earnings — no valuation floor.
