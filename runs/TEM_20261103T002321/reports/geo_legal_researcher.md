# Tempus AI, Inc. (NASDAQ: TEM) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

Tempus is a **predominantly U.S. domestic business**. Its diagnostics laboratories are CLIA-certified U.S. facilities, and the overwhelming majority of revenue is generated in the United States. International activity is emerging but small, centered on data-licensing partnerships with European pharma (AstraZeneca, GSK — UK; Servier — France) and a new London hub.

|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | ~90–95% (est.) | Medium | Core labs, Diagnostics + Data & Applications; heaviest regulatory/legal exposure (FDA, CMS, state genetic-privacy statutes) |

Treat the international percentages as directional, not precise.

**Key insight:** Geopolitical risk here is *not* foreign-jurisdiction risk (no meaningful China, Russia, or EM exposure). It is **domestic U.S. regulatory and legal risk** — an important distinction from a typical "country risk" profile.

## 2. Top Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**Risk #1 — CMS reimbursement policy for AI/algorithmic tests (HIGH probability, HIGH impact)**
Tempus's Diagnostics segment (~70% of revenue) depends on Medicare/CLFS reimbursement. CMS has proposed reclassifying "algorithm-only" tests out of the Clinical Laboratory Fee Schedule (Sept 2026 proposed rule), and the 2026 PAMA reporting cycle will set CLFS rates for 2027–2029. Tempus's xT CDx holds **ADLT status** (a favorable reimbursement pathway), but any adverse reclassification or rate cut directly hits the largest revenue line. *Probability: medium-high over the next 12–24 months. Impact: high.*

**Risk #2 — Genetic-data privacy litigation & expanding state privacy statutes (HIGH probability, MEDIUM-HIGH impact)**
The consolidated **Farrier v. Tempus AI** class action (N.D. Ill., Apr 2026; amended Aug 2026) is a 21-count suit alleging Tempus compelled Ambry Genetics to hand over genetic data and then disclosed it to 70+ pharma partners (~$1.1bn in deals), violating the **Illinois Genetic Information Privacy Act (GIPA)** and the consumer/data-protection and deceptive-trade laws of **California, Florida, Georgia, Michigan, New York, and West Virginia**. GIPA carries statutory damages, making this a systemic business-model risk, not a one-off. *Probability of continued/escalating litigation: high. Impact: medium-high (damages, injunction risk against the data-licensing model that underpins Data & Applications).*

**Risk #3 — FDA/LDT regulatory regime uncertainty (MEDIUM probability, MEDIUM impact)**
Following *ACLA v. FDA*, the FDA's LDT rule was vacated/its final rule reverted (Sept 19, 2025), and Congress is now weighing the **"Enhancing CLIA Act of 2026"** to reaffirm CMS authority over LDTs. Tempus's lab-developed tests face a shifting oversight regime — a framework-change risk rather than an existing enforcement action. Tempus has FDA clearances for device products (e.g., ECG-PH 510(k)), which is a positive offset. *Probability: medium. Impact: medium.*

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action ("AI-washing")** — filed June 12, 2025, N.D. Ill., on behalf of purchasers Aug 6, 2024–May 27, 2025; alleges Tempus branded itself as an AI company with misleading claims. Lead-plaintiff deadline passed Aug 2025. Standard disclosure/valuation suit; monitor settlement accruals.
- **Farrier genetic-data class action** (above) — the material legal contingency; consolidated and amended, active.
- **CMS/ADLT** — xT CDx ADLT reimbursement status secured (favorable); watch proposed algorithm-test rule and PAMA rate-setting.
- **IVDR certification** (Oct 2026) — enables EU clinical expansion; also means Tempus now holds EU-regulated device obligations (GDPR + EU MDR/IVDR).
- **Ambry Genetics acquisition** — $600m deal (Feb 2025) closed; no disclosed antitrust challenge, but the data-transfer condition of the deal is the direct genesis of the privacy suits.
- **No disclosed DOJ/FTC/SEC formal investigation** in the sources reviewed — a mitigating finding (this is litigation risk, not enforcement risk).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Genetic-privacy / GIPA exposure (RED)** — whether genetic data can truly be "de-identified" is the core legal question; the entire Data & Applications model (71%+ gross margins) is built on licensing de-identified data. An adverse ruling could force consent-based restructuring.
2. **Cross-border health-data transfer (AMBER)** — EU/UK expansion into genomics raises GDPR "special category" data and data-sovereignty risk; the new London hub must navigate EU–UK adequacy and patient-consent regimes more stringent than U.S. law.
3. **Healthcare AI regulatory perimeter (AMBER)** — FDA device oversight (510(k)/LDT) and potential FTC AI/health-claims scrutiny of "AI-washing" marketing, which is also the theory of the securities suit.
4. **No meaningful sanctions, FCPA, AML, or export-control exposure** — no Russia/China/EM operations, no disclosed CFIUS issues. This is a genuine mitigant versus typical multinationals.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Tempus carries **minimal foreign geopolitical and sanctions exposure** — it is a U.S.-centric business with no China, Russia, or emerging-market footprint, which keeps country risk low. The rating is lifted to **Medium** by concentrated **U.S. regulatory dependence** (CMS reimbursement, FDA/LDT regime) and a **material, active genetic-data privacy class action** that directly threatens its highest-margin data-licensing business model, plus a pending AI-washing securities suit. These are domestic legal/policy risks rather than classic geopolitical ones, but they are the risks most likely to impair value.

**What would move the rating:** *Up to High* — an adverse GIPA ruling or injunction against data licensing, or an unfavorable CMS algorithm-test reimbursement final rule. *Down to Low-Medium* — resolution/dismissal of the privacy suits and clarity on LDT/CLFS rules.
