### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① North American trade architecture — probability: High (already materialising) | impact: High**
- US 25% auto tariffs cost Stellantis **~€300m** in 2025 alone (BBC); Trump has threatened to raise EU auto tariffs to **25%**, and a third round of **USMCA bilateral talks** opened in 2026 (Baker Botts tariff tracker, 21 Jul 2026).
- Canada retaliated with **25% surtax on US-built vehicles** (effective Apr 2025) and has **cut Stellantis' and GM's Canadian import quotas** — direct volume damage in a market where US-built imports previously dominated.
- Stellantis idled **Windsor (Canada) and Toluca (Mexico)** within hours of the April 2025 tariff announcement — evidence of extreme sensitivity of its cross-border production model.
- **Impact estimate:** each 500bp of incremental tariff cost ≈ €400–700m of EBIT on a ~€150bn revenue base with thin margins. Moody's put industry-wide 2025 tariff profit hit at >$30bn.

**② Canadian sovereign/contractual dispute over Brampton — probability: High (~70% of formal claim or clawback) | impact: Medium-High**
- Stellantis signed an asset purchase agreement to sell Brampton Assembly to Canadian defence firm **Roshel** (Reuters/WSJ, 9 Nov 2026), ending car manufacturing there.
- PM **Carney called it a "betrayal" and "unacceptable"**; the Industry Ministry states the government will use "**every tool at our disposal and the full force of the law**" (CBC, 11 Nov 2026). A **notice of default** was already served in Dec 2025 by Minister Joly.
- At least **C$220m** of federal funding is at stake, plus separate NextStar battery JV agreements (Stellantis exited its stake in 2026). Unifor is publicly demanding repayment of incentives.
- Contract carve-outs for "commercially unviable" circumstances give Stellantis a genuine defence, but this is now a **politically-adjudicated** dispute — the worst kind of legal risk, because outcome depends on election dynamics, not contract text.

**③ EU decarbonisation regime + Italian political conditionality — probability: Medium-High | impact: High**
- EU CO2 fines of **€95/g per vehicle**; compliance is via **2025–2027 averaging** and a **Tesla CO2 pool** (Autonews; Reuters 1 Jul 2025 — Stellantis warned it "may close factories" over fines).
- Stellantis has taken to purchasing credits rather than earning them — a recurring cash cost and a structural admission its BEV mix lags. The €22bn reset was explicitly attributed to "an **initial overestimation of pace of adoption of electrification**."
- Conversely, Rome has committed **€1.6bn** in support (FT: €2bn Italian investment pledge to "thaw" relations with Meloni's government) — **subsidy dependence creates political leverage**, including Golden Power-style conditions on plant closures and a 2026 Italian output forecast cut ~10% (Fim-Cisl/Bloomberg).

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Emissions-compliance recidivism** (US settlement 2022; live French/Italian criminal probes; UK civil exposure). Repeat-offender status is an enforcement aggravator and a governance red flag.
2. **Politically-exposed contract performance** — Canada is now openly alleging breach. Any finding of default could theoretically touch **export credit / government-backed financing** eligibility in other jurisdictions.
3. **China technology transfer & data** — Leapmotor JV now includes **B10 assembly at Zaragoza, Spain** (production began Oct 2026). This couples Stellantis to EU anti-subsidy/tariff treatment of Chinese EVs and to EU FSR/foreign-subsidy scrutiny; data-governance obligations run to both GDPR and Chinese rules.
4. **FX/transfer-pricing controls in Argentina and Türkiye** — 26% Argentine share with currency controls creates pricing, dividend-repatriation and transfer-pricing compliance exposure.
5. **No FCPA/OFAC/Sanctions red flag identified.** Stellantis exited Russian manufacturing and shows no disclosed Russia/Iran operations of scale; sanctions exposure is assessed **Low**. (Search returned only generic EU 22nd-package/OFAC commentary, no company-specific hits.)

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** STLA is simultaneously (a) on the wrong side of the dominant geopolitical trade vector — a cross-border North American production model now taxed at 25%, with USMCA renegotiation live; (b) in an **active contractual confrontation with a G7 government** that has explicitly invoked "the full force of the law"; and (c) carrying a fresh **US securities class action** tied to a €22bn write-down and a 58% YTD share decline. Mitigants: the UK diesel litigation largely broke in its favour, US fuel-economy rules are loosening, South America is a genuine franchise (25.6% Brazil / 26% Argentina share), and no sanctions or FCPA exposure of note. On balance, **elevated geopolitical and legal risk is a first-order driver of the equity story here, not a footnote.**
