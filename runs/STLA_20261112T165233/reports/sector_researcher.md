## Sector Brief — Stellantis N.V. (STLA)
**Sector:** Consumer Cyclical / Auto Manufacturers | **HQ:** Netherlands | **Mkt cap:** ~$11.4bn | **Price:** ~$3.92

---

### 1. Sector Overview

Global light-vehicle (LV) sales reached ~91.7m units in 2025 (+3.4%) and are forecast essentially flat at ~91.8m in 2026, with production edging *down* to ~92.6m against a 2.7% global GDP backdrop. The sector is structurally fragmented — the top three OEMs (Toyota ~10.8m, VW ~9m, Hyundai-Kia ~7m) hold only ~29% of global units, implying an HHI well below 1,000 — so pricing power is weak and rivalry is the dominant force. Growth is regionally divergent: North America falls ~590k units to 19.3m, China slips ~267k on incentive pull-forward, Western/Central Europe adds ~260k to 15.4m, and South Asia grows 5.3% to 10.4m. Electrification is advancing but decelerating: BEVs hit 14.6m units (16.1% share) in 2025 and are guided to ~17.4m (~19% share) in 2026 as hybrids absorb the mix shift and governments trim incentives.

**Cycle position: late-cycle / mature, in a volume downturn with structural margin compression.** Capital intensity is extreme, capacity utilisation is poor (China ~70%), and global overcapacity is exporting deflation — BYD's average discounts hit a record ~10% in March 2026. This is a self-help and cost story, not a growth story.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **European volume and mix recovery:** W/C Europe LV sales +260k to 15.4m on improved real incomes, lower inflation and a resilient labour market — STLA's home turf, where it still holds ~19.1% share (19.4% incl. Leapmotor).
- **Regulatory recalibration lowers capex risk:** Softening EU CO2/2035 mandates and the US rollback of BEV incentives let OEMs re-phase multi-billion electrification spend toward hybrids, where consumers actually are and margins are better.
- **Tariff-driven localisation favours incumbents with US footprints:** STLA builds Jeep/Ram in North America, giving it a structural cost advantage versus import-dependent peers and Chinese entrants facing punitive duties — a relative, not absolute, win.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **US tariff and affordability shock:** NA LV sales falling ~3% to 19.3m as buyers pull forward purchases ahead of price hikes; tariffs simultaneously raise landed costs and suppress unit demand — the worst of both.
- **Chinese export/price pressure in Europe and LatAm:** ~22m EVs produced globally in 2025 with China overcapacity at ~70% utilisation; Chinese OEMs are localising (bypassing tariffs) and compressing the mid-market where STLA's Peugeot/Citroën/Fiat brands sit.
- **Margin-dilutive electrification and fixed-cost deleverage:** BEV adoption stalling at ~19% share forces OEMs to carry dual powertrain cost bases; with volumes ~19% below 2023 levels, STLA's €150bn+ revenue base is badly under-absorbing fixed costs.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **WEAK**

- **Scale without pricing power:** ~5.5m units and 14 brands (Abarth to Maserati) spread across a sub-scale portfolio — brand proliferation fragments R&D and marketing spend that Toyota and VW amortise over twice the volume.
- **Margin structurally below the cost of capital:** FY25 operating margin of -14.5% and 9M-26 of 0.8% versus a peer median of 1.5% — STLA has not earned its cost of capital since 2023 (12.1% OM).
- **Self-inflicted execution damage:** the 2024-25 inventory and pricing missteps in North America (its profit engine) destroyed ~€36bn of revenue between 2023 and 2025 (-19% peak-to-trough) and precipitated the CEO change to Antonio Filosa.
- **Credible but unproven turnaround:** the €60bn/$70bn plan (60 new models by 2030, NA share recapture) is directionally right, and 2026 guidance has been reaffirmed — but management itself says pay-off lands in **2027**, and the equity has priced scepticism: -67% since Jan-2025 ($11.74 → $3.92), -60% YTD, at all-time lows.
- **One genuine asset:** European LV leadership at ~19.1% share and a defensible Ram/Jeep franchise in NA light trucks — the two pillars any recovery must be built on.

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL** (with a negative bias for mass-market volume OEMs)

Flat unit volumes (~91.8m), weak pricing power, ~70% China utilisation and decelerating BEV adoption make this a zero-sum share fight in which only the lowest-cost (Toyota, BYD) or highest-price (Ferrari, 29% OM) operators compound. The sector is not structurally broken — European volumes are recovering, tariffs protect localised capacity, and self-help cost programmes are real — but a peer-median operating margin of 1.5% is a poor return on a capital-intensive, cyclical, regulated asset base. **Sector: Neutral. STLA within it: underweight — a levered recovery bet whose valuation case rests entirely on 2027 execution that the market has stopped paying for.**
