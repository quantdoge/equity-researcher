### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

**The single largest ESG-related P&L event in the sector's recent history has already occurred.** On 6 Feb 2026 Stellantis took a **€22.2bn (~$26bn) charge** — driving a **FY2025 net loss of €22.2bn on €153.5bn revenues** — to cancel EV programmes (incl. the all-electric Ram 1500 REV), accelerate ICE/hybrid spending, and exit its 49% stake in Ontario cell-maker **NextStar Energy** (sold to LGES). This is stranded-asset risk realised, and it *raises* rather than lowers forward carbon risk: the fleet mix now leans further to ICE.

- **EU CO₂ compliance (Critical):** Management has publicly quantified **~€2.5bn of potential EU excess-emissions penalties over 2–3 years** and warned of plant closures on that basis (Reuters, Jul-2025). Mitigants since: Brussels moved to **3-year averaging (2025-27)** and the Commission's **Dec-2025 proposal softened the 2035 ban to a 90% CO₂ cut** (allowing e-fuels/plug-in residual). STLA bought Tesla-pool credits in 2025 but has **exited the Tesla pool for 2026** (EU filing, Feb-2026), implying in-house compliance via PHEV/BEV mix — a margin-positive signal if it holds, but a hard fail-risk if EU demand for ZEVs stays weak (industry was ~1 g/km from target per ICCT/ACEA).
- **US (High):** ~**$191m of CAFE civil penalties paid**; the US GHG-credit trading market was effectively eliminated in 2025, removing a cheap compliance valve while CAFE/EPA standards tighten again.
- **Transition opportunity:** Net-zero **2038** target retained and Paris-aligned in framing, but **no SBTi commitment and no CDP climate disclosure** — weak third-party validation versus European peers.
- **Physical risk:** Material but second-order — water stress in Mexico/Brazil/India plants and Southern-European heat exposure on utilisation.

**Verdict:** Environmental is where ESG *becomes* financial for STLA. Treat CO₂ premium/credit spend as a recurring, under-modelled opex line rather than a tail risk.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Labour (High but improving):** ~258,700 employees. 2023 UAW contract created ~$19bn of commitments; 2024-25 saw nationwide grievance filings and strike threats over Belvidere re-opening and the Dodge Durango. **These grievances have since been resolved** ("resolved our grievances concerning the Dodge Durango and the reopening of Belvidere" — UAW Stellantis dept.), materially de-risking a 2026 strike. Italian/ French overcapacity remains the live flashpoint: the EU-fine-driven plant closures flagged by management are a social-cost decision, and Italian political pressure is high.
- **Product safety (High):** Repeated large campaigns — **1.07m US vehicles (power-steering defect, Jun-2026)** and **>1m Jeep Wrangler/Gladiator (fire risk, park-outside order)**. Warranty/recall accruals and NHTSA consent-order risk (cf. Ford's $165m penalty precedent) are the exposure; no STLA consent order identified.
- **Supply chain (Medium-High):** Battery ESG due-diligence policy and human-rights programme in place; cobalt/child-labour exposure via EU CSDDD and EU Battery Regulation is a compliance cost and a NextStar-exit-relevant diligence issue. No live adverse finding located.
- **Diversity/community:** No material financial exposure identified; disclosure adequate.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Positives (recent, real):**
- **Say-on-pay has normalised:** the 2026 AGM (14 Apr 2026) saw the Remuneration Report carry with **93.17% support** — a decisive break from a toxic record (52% opposed in 2022, 48% in 2023, ~30% in 2024; AllianzGI and Phitrust publicly opposed Tavares's packages).
- Board refresh executed: Elkann re-elected executive director; Peugeot Vice-Chair; **Henri de Castries Senior Independent Director**; Juergen Esser added as NED qualifying as independent under both **NYSE standards and the Dutch Corporate Governance Code**; Audit, Remuneration and ESG committees reconstituted.

**Negatives:**
- **Control asymmetry:** Exor (Agnelli) is the reference shareholder and the group uses a **loyalty/special voting-right structure**, so economic ownership ≠ voting control (Exor's own cap structure shows the pattern: ~53% of ordinary shares → >85% of votes). Minority shareholders have limited ability to force change.
- **Chairman–CEO separation but concentrated influence:** Elkann is *executive* Chairman alongside a new CEO (Filosa, from Jun-2025) — an unusual, influence-heavy arrangement for a €153bn-revenue group.
- **Pay quantum still contested:** Filosa's package runs to **~$10.2m potential**, and the €23.1m final payout to Tavares was approved despite prior rejections; proxy advisors remain sceptical. Pay-for-performance is defensible post-write-down, but the optics of large awards in a €22.2bn loss year are poor.
- **Compliance culture:** the FCA US diesel-defeat guilty plea is the sector's clearest governance failure of the last decade.
- No evidence of a poison pill; capital structure is single-class ordinary + special voting shares.

**Score rationale:** governance *mechanics* (committees, independence, disclosure, say-on-pay outcome) are now average-to-good; **control and pay legitimacy** remain weak. 5/10 = investable, not a governance discount trigger, but no margin of safety for minority holders.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD** (trajectory: stabilising)

STLA screens as a laggard on outcomes, not on disclosure. It has the weakest quantified environmental outcome record of the large European OEMs (diesel-defeat criminal plea + ~€2.5bn forward fine exposure + a €22.2bn transition write-down), a controlling-shareholder governance model with a recent history of 30-52% pay opposition, and no SBTi target or CDP disclosure. It is **not** an "ESG Risk" name in the Sustainalytics sense of unmanaged tail risk — coverage exists across MSCI, Sustainalytics and LSEG, processes are being rebuilt, and the 2026 AGM/UAW resolutions show governance and social risk actively de-escalating. Net-zero **2038** remains a genuine, earlier-than-sector commitment.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL (slight negative tilt, but not the thesis driver)**

**Why neutral, not negative:** the market already knows STLA is an ESG laggard and a carbon laggard — that is priced. The bull case's ESG dependency is narrow but real: (i) **EU CO₂ costs must stay capped** (2025-27 averaging and the 90%-by-2035 softening are the two live supports); (ii) **UAW peace must hold** through the 2028 bargaining round; (iii) management must stop destroying capital (two consecutive years of ~€20bn+ of largely transition-driven charges).

**The underappreciated risk to underwrite:** STLA has just **shifted its product mix toward ICE/hybrid while the EU 2030-35 step-downs approach**. If EU ZEV demand does not recover, the €2.5bn penalty estimate becomes a *recurring* mid-decade opex item and management faces a second, forced-EV re-investment cycle — i.e. today's write-down is not the last one. Conversely, exiting the Tesla CO₂ pool for 2026 is management signalling in-house compliance confidence; if delivered, that is a genuine positive catalyst and a credibility rebuild.

**Positioning view:** ESG is a **monitor, not a rating action**. For an ESG-constrained mandate, STLA is uninvestable at Laggard with a controlling shareholder and no SBTi target. For a value/turnaround mandate, ESG is not the swing factor — **but CO₂ premium spend should be modelled explicitly as a recurring line through 2030**, and the 2028 UAW round is the key event risk on the social side.

**Key ESG monitoring triggers:** EU 2035 co-decision outcome; any 2026 EU excess-emissions charge disclosed at H1-26; Filosa's first full say-on-pay cycle (2027 AGM); UAW 2028 talks opening posture; NHTSA consent-order escalation; SBTi validation.
