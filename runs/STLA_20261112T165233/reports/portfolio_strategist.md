Book value is a melting, not a fixed, floor.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation

### **0.0% of the core portfolio — PASS.**
### **Maximum 0.50% in a designated distressed / special-situations sleeve, and only for mandates explicitly permitted to own sub-investment-grade-adjacent cyclicals.**

**Rationale:**

1. **Kelly is unusable here, and that is itself the answer.** Running Kelly on the Value analyst's own scenario set (35% bear €3.6 / 40% base €15.8 / 15% bull €32 / 10% severe €0.8) returns an expected value of roughly +2.7× and a *large* optimal fraction (mid-teens percent). That is a red flag, not a green light: raw Kelly is hypersensitive to `p`, and `p` here carries a **45% probability of value loss and a 10% probability of near-total loss** driven by an event (equity issuance at 0.19× book) that is management-controlled, not market-controlled. Applying a **1/5 fractional Kelly** and then haircutting for the solvency tail lands at ~1.0–1.5% — and the tail haircut (you cannot stop-loss a dilution event; it gaps through) takes it to **≤0.5%**.
2. **The risk is unhedgeable, so it must be sized, not managed.** Beta 1.02 with **R² = 0.074** means 93% of variance is idiosyncratic and *market hedging does not work*. There is no overlay that truncates the downside. A 54%-vol position with an uncapped dilution tail does not belong in a core book at any weight above noise.
3. **Multiple independent risk flags breach my sizing matrix.** My framework caps "multiple risk flags" at 0.5–1.0% **or pass**. STLA exceeds the flag threshold: solvency-adjacent leverage *and* a disclosure-integrity cluster *and* a sovereign counterparty dispute *and* a broken cash-conversion engine *and* an all-time-low price with negative revisions. It sits in the "pass" bucket, not the "0.5–1%" bucket.
4. **Risk-budget check.** At 0.5%, portfolio-vol contribution is ~11bp (using 0.4 marginal correlation to an equity book). At 2.0% it would be ~43bp — still small in vol terms, but a −40% dilution gap would cost 80bp of portfolio and arrive *correlated* with a risk-off backdrop. At 0.5% the same event costs 20bp: survivable, and the option value is retained.
5. **Sizing convention for the sleeve:** if held, cap it at **0.5% and treat the maximum loss as 100% of the position** (not the stop-loss level) when computing the sleeve's aggregate risk budget. Do not let it contribute more than 1/4 of the sleeve's total drawdown tolerance.

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector concentration — negative.**
Auto Manufacturers is the sector team's explicit underweight within a Neutral Consumer Cyclical. STLA is the sector's *weakest* operator (worst on all three margin metrics). Adding it deepens an already-unattractive cyclical exposure at the worst point in the volume cycle (SAAR 16.0M, EU recovery deferred, China at 70% utilisation).

**Factor exposure — the decisive objection.**
STLA is a concentrated short-quality / short-momentum / long-static-value / long-leverage bet. Every one of those loads is the *wrong* side for the prevailing macro regime (rates up, real yields +59bp, late-cycle):

| Factor | STLA Loading | Portfolio Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Value (static) | Q1 — P/B 0.19 | Only supportive load; but P/FCF −1.01 nullifies it |
| Momentum | Q5 — 12-1 of **−22.7%** | Adds to any existing anti-momentum drag |
| Quality | Q5 — ROIC 0.8%, F-Score 5/9 | Concentrates a factor the market is penalising |
| Leverage | Q5 — net debt/EBITDA 4.11× | Adds credit-beta to an equity book |
| Volatility | Q5 — 54% vs SPY ~15% | 3.5× market vol in a single name |
| Size | Mid ($11.4bn) | Not a diversifier |

**Correlation — genuinely low, but illusory diversification.**
0.27 to SPY and 0.17–0.28 to TM/TSLA is the lowest in the peer set and would *look* attractive on a covariance optimizer. Three reasons to discount it:
- **Within-sector it is not a diversifier:** 0.44 to GM and **0.49 to Ford**. If the book already owns GM or F, STLA is largely the same cyclical bet with worse fundamentals — you would be doubling exposure to the same driver (NA light-truck demand, tariffs, auto credit) at a higher volatility.
- **Correlations converge in stress.** Distressed equities trade on credit/solvency, not on their own factor. The observed path — **−84% while broad equity markets rose** — is the definition of a diversifier that fails precisely when you need it.
- **Currency:** EUR-functional, USD-listed. A weaker euro is a genuine translation tailwind (Macro), so there is a small, accidental EUR-short overlay. Not a reason to own it; worth noting it is not a clean USD auto exposure.

**Liquidity — the one clean positive.** ~25–60M shares/day ($100–230M notional) on an $11.4bn cap. A 0.5% position in any plausible portfolio is <1 day of ADV. No execution constraint.

**Verdict on fit:** STLA does not earn a place on diversification grounds. Its low correlation is offensively useless (it falls on its own news), and its factor loads duplicate and worsen exposures a quality/momentum-aware book already carries. It belongs, if anywhere, in a hedged-by-construction or explicitly distressed sleeve.

## 6. Risk Management Rules

**Stop-loss (price):** **$2.75 (−30% from $3.93).** Deliberately wide — a tighter stop is meaningless against 54% annualised vol and ~7% average daily range, and would be triggered by noise. $2.75 is not arbitrary: it is simultaneously (a) <0.5× tangible book, below which the "asset floor" ceases to be a floor and the thesis converts from deep value to incipient capital impairment, and (b) the Value analyst's own line between cheap and uninvestable. **Exit in full on the close below it, no re-entry without a new proof trigger.**

**Thesis invalidation — exit on ANY of these, regardless of price:**
1. **Any equity issuance / rights issue / convertible at these levels.** Immediate full exit. At 0.19× book a raise is a permanent per-share transfer to creditors, and it is the #1 flagged risk across Value, Growth and Fin Risk.
2. **Rating migration to sub-investment grade** (S&P below BBB− / Moody's below Baa3). Fallen-angel status triggers index-driven and IG-mandate forced selling — the exit door narrows sharply.
3. **Second consecutive year of industrial FCF worse than −€3bn**, or the "positive industrial FCF in 2027" guidance being withdrawn or re-based.
4. **Net debt above €30bn**, or a disclosed covenant breach/waiver request.
5. **AOI margin below 2.0% through FY2027** — evidence the €6bn cost programme is slideware.
6. **A disclosed SEC/DOJ formal investigation** into the Feb-2026 disclosures, or any restatement. This is the Nonfin analyst's explicit "downgrade to Critical" trigger.
7. **Adverse Canadian adjudication** — a court/regulatory block on the Brampton sale combined with clawback of the C$220m plus retaliatory policy action against Windsor.
8. **Dividend/buyback reinstatement before FCF turns positive** — a repeat of the 2024 capital-allocation error (€7.65bn returned in a −€7.1bn FCF year).

**Trim triggers:**
- **Trim 1/3 at $6.50** (~0.65× tangible book; validates that the market's implied impairment was too severe).

- **Time stop:** a full reassessment at **H1-2027 results**. If the FY2027 FCF proof point has not been delivered by then, exit the residual regardless of price — do not let a 2027-guidance story roll into a 2028-guidance story.

**Monitoring cadence (highest signal first):** (1) Jeep/Ram **days' supply** monthly — the cleanest leading indicator, target <130 by the December reading; (2) weekly **10Y TIPS real yield** and **WTI**; (3) monthly **US SAAR** — is 16.0M a floor or a waypoint; (4) EU30 market share (18.6% Q3 vs 19.1% Q2); (5) **FOMC 9–10 Dec 2026** and the USMCA joint-review outcome; (6) AFM MAR insider filings — any Filosa/Elkann open-market purchase would be a material positive tell and is currently absent.
