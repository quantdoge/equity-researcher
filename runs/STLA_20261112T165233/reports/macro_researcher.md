## Macro Assessment: STLA (Stellantis N.V.)

**Data as of 12 November 2026. STLA last close $3.93; market cap $11.4bn (~€9.9bn); 52-week range collapse from $25.28 (Mar-2024 high) — down ~84%.**

---

### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-Off for Rate-Sensitive Cyclicals**

The US economy is in a **late-cycle re-acceleration with re-accelerating inflation** — the worst possible regime for a highly-levered, capital-intensive, thin-margin automaker.

The defining macro event is a **policy turn**: the FOMC *raised* rates 25bp on 16 September 2026 to 3.75%–4.00% — the first hike since 2023 — and held 10–2 on 28 October, with markets pricing one further 25bp. The ECB moved in lockstep, lifting the deposit rate to 2.50% on 16 September 2026. The long end has repriced violently: the 10Y UST sits at **5.13%** and the 30Y at **5.47%**, a bear-steepened curve (+39bp 2s10s). Critically, the **10Y TIPS real yield is 2.66%**, up ~59bp since end-May 2026. This is a *real* rate shock, not just inflation compensation (5Y breakeven is contained at 2.25%).

Inflation re-accelerated from a July trough of ~3.3% to **3.8% y/y in October**, tracking a **42% WTI rally from ~$70 (1 Jul) to $99.64 (3 Nov)**. Growth is *not* the problem — Q3 2026 real GDP grew **~3.4% annualized (+2.1% y/y)** with unemployment at **4.1%**. That combination is why the Fed is hiking into a consumer that is employed but whose financing costs are rising again.

For autos specifically, this is a pincer. US light-vehicle SAAR has rolled over from **17.2M (Jun-2026) to 16.0M (Oct-2026)**, the lowest since January, despite 4.1% unemployment — the signature of an affordability-constrained, rate-sensitive category. The 48-month new-car loan rate has only eased to **~7.5%** (Aug-2026) from the 8.63% 2024 peak; with 5.13% 10Y yields it has bottomed and is turning back up.

**Company context that makes macro bite harder:** FY2025 net loss of **€22.3bn** on **€25.4bn** of write-downs (peer-wide EV retreat), adjusted operating loss of €842m (vs +€8.65bn in 2024), dividend suspended, **up to €5bn of hybrid bonds issued**, and industrial FCF not positive until **2027**. Equity is pricing a turnaround with no earnings cushion and no self-funding.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

STLA is simultaneously exposed to every leg of the current regime: it is **rate-sensitive** (consumer financing + €5bn hybrid issuance + €60bn capex), **inflation-exposed without pricing power**, **tariff-exposed** (€1.6bn direct), **oil-exposed** through its truck/SUV mix, and **cyclically exposed** into a decelerating SAAR. Beta of ~1.0 understates this — the stock's 84% drawdown is ~90% idiosyncratic (the €25.4bn write-down alone is ~2.5x current market capitalisation), but the macro backdrop determines whether the turnaround can be *financed and executed* before the cycle turns. On that test, the environment is actively hostile: financing is getting more expensive, not less, precisely as STLA needs to rebuild credibility.

At **0.064x FY2025 revenue (€154bn)** the equity is discounting a near-permanent impairment of earning power. That is a valuation observation, not a macro one — but it means macro upside is highly convex if the regime turns.

---

### 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise — **A Violent Bull Case**

**A demand-driven oil reversal forces the Fed to abort the hiking cycle.** If WTI falls back from ~$100 to $70 (OPEC+ supply discipline breaking, or a growth scare), headline CPI retraces toward 2.5% within two quarters, the Fed's priced-in hike is removed and a cut is reinstated, and the **10Y falls from 5.13% to ~4.2% with real yields back near 2.0%**. The transmission to STLA is unusually direct and mechanical:

- 48-month auto loan rate falls from ~7.5% to ~6.5% → affordability improves for exactly the high-ticket trucks/SUVs that are STLA's profit pool
- SAAR recovers from 16.0M toward 17M+, restoring North American mix and pricing
- The **€5bn hybrid** and the €60bn FaSTLAne programme get refinanced/funded at materially lower cost, and the 2027 FCF inflection becomes credible rather than aspirational
- Valuation re-rates off a 0.064x sales base

**A 50-100% re-rating from $3.93 is entirely plausible in that regime without any change to the strategic plan.** The asymmetry is the point: consensus is positioned for the bear case, and the bear case is largely *already in the price*. The mirror-image risk — the Fed delivers the additional hike, 10Y reaches 6%, SAAR breaks 15M, and STLA must raise equity at these prices — is the reason this is a scenario and not a base case.

**What to watch:** (1) FOMC 9-10 December 2026 and whether the second hike is delivered; (2) the USMCA joint-review outcome — the €1.6bn tariff number is a *guidance assumption*, not a ceiling; (3) weekly WTI and the 10Y TIPS yield as the two fastest-moving inputs to STLA's re-rating; (4) monthly SAAR for confirmation of whether 16M is a floor or a waypoint.
