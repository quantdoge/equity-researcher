# Crown Castle Inc. (CCI) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price $88.17 | Market cap ~$37.5B | Net debt ~$22.3B | EV ~$60B | Dividend $4.25 (4.8%)**

*Data-integrity note up front: CCI's reported 2025 figures (revenue −35%, negative PP&E swings, a $3.9B 2024 net loss) are artifacts of the fiber/small-cell divestiture and related impairments. Reported 5-yr revenue CAGR (−6.1%) and trailing P/E (86.6x) are therefore not meaningful. I base this analysis on the post-divestiture tower-only business and on AFFO, the correct REIT cash-earnings metric. The standard DCF tool is unusable here (its FCF base is inflated by held-for-sale accounting), so I built the valuation from an AFFO bridge instead.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Wide on the assets, Narrow at the enterprise level**

**What CCI is now:** ~40,000 U.S. macro towers, 100% domestic, ~2.3 tenants per tower, ~$4.3B revenue, ~$3.0B EBITDA (≈70% margin). The fiber and small-cell segment — a 10-year, ~$15B capital-allocation detour — was sold to Zayo/EQT for $8.5B in 2026 after ~$3.5B of write-downs.

**Moat evidence (durable):**
- **Efficient scale / irreplaceable assets.** The U.S. is a three-tower-company market (AMT, CCI, SBA). A new tower in a good location requires 2–5 years of zoning, permitting and NIMBY battles. Nobody rationally builds a fourth tower next to three existing ones. Replacement cost per site is far above CCI's depreciated book.
- **Switching costs are near-prohibitive.** Carrier radios are physically mounted, backhauled, and engineered into RF plans. Tenant churn runs ~1–2% annually on 5–10 year leases.
- **Contractual pricing power.** ~3% annual escalators embedded in leases, largely inflation-insensitive.
- **Incremental economics are exceptional.** A second or third tenant on an existing tower drops through at ~85% EBITDA margin. This is the closest thing in REITs to a toll booth.

**Where the moat has narrowed:** not in the towers — in the *returns on capital*. Management spent a decade converting a superb tower business into a mediocre telecom-infrastructure conglomerate. The proof is in the numbers: ROIC of 7.4% (2025), 6.7–6.9% (2022–23) — roughly *at* cost of capital, not above it. A genuinely wide-moat compounder earns 12–15% ROIC. CCI has never cleared its own hurdle rate on the incremental capital deployed since 2015. The moat protected the *assets*; management squandered the *franchise*.

**Structural watch item:** direct-to-cell satellite (Starlink/DISH spectrum) is a genuine long-dated threat to rural tower economics, though likely a complement rather than substitute in dense markets over a 5–10 year horizon.

| Net debt/EBITDA | ~7.0x | ~8.5x | ~7.8x | **~7.5x** |

*\*Post-divestiture perimeter.*

**Strengths:** ~70% EBITDA margins, highly predictable contracted revenue, now-light capex ($182M in 2025 vs. $1.2–1.4B pre-sale), and AFFO that converts to cash almost 1:1. Q3 2026 AFFO of $1.24/share was +11% YoY; Q2 was +10%. The business is genuinely cash-generative and the fiber capex drain is gone.

**Weaknesses:** (a) **Leverage of ~7.5x net debt/EBITDA** with ~$2.8B of short-term maturities — roughly double AMT's leverage; (b) **negative book equity (−$1.6B) and −$20.2B retained earnings** — no accounting cushion; (c) **~91% AFFO payout ratio**, which means the dividend consumes nearly all discretionary cash and leaves little for deleveraging or new builds; (d) refinancing at ~5% vs. a ~4% book cost is a structural AFFO headwind.

I deduct heavily for the balance sheet. This is a good business wrapped in a leveraged capital structure.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**2026E AFFO ≈ $2.0B ($4.65/share).** At $88.17 that is **18.8x AFFO** with a **4.8% dividend yield** and a ~5.3% AFFO yield.

Three independent methods:

| Method | Assumption | Value/share |
|---|---|---|
| AFFO capitalization | 16.5–18.0x (3–4% grower, 7.5x leverage; AMT ~17–18x) | $77–84 |
| Dividend discount | D₁ $4.38, r = 8.0–8.5%, g = 3% | $80–88 |
| Private-market / sum-of-parts | 21–23x EBITDA ($63–69B EV) − $22.3B net debt | $95–109 |

**Intrinsic value: ~$85–92, midpoint ≈ $88.**

The current price sits *at* intrinsic value. The private-market case (tower assets routinely clear at 20–25x EBITDA) is the bull argument and the reason I would not sell, but it requires a buyer or an activist to crystallize it, and today's private clearing multiples are well below 2021's.

**What the price implies:** ~3% perpetual AFFO growth and a 8.3% required return — i.e., the market is already paying full freight for the escalator. There is **no margin of safety**; a 20–30% discount requires **$62–70/share**.

**Relative value caveat:** the peer-median P/E of 48x returned by the screen is meaningless for REITs. On the metrics that matter, CCI at ~19x AFFO and ~21x EV/EBITDA is trading *at or above* American Tower despite weaker growth, higher leverage, single-country concentration and a worse capital-allocation record. That is not a value setup — it's a re-rating that has already happened (the stock is up from ~$73 in August 2026 to $88).

## 4. Management Quality: **Improving, unproven (6/10)**

- **Governance reset was real.** The board terminated CEO Steven Moskowitz in early 2025 after the strategic failures, and installed **Christian Hillabrant (Sept. 2025)** — former CEO of Vantage Towers, a genuine tower operator rather than an empire-builder. That is the single most encouraging development in five years.
- **Capital-allocation decisions are now correct, if painful:** exiting fiber ($8.5B, ~$7B to debt paydown), cutting the dividend 32% to $4.25 to stop overdistributing, and repurchasing $1.0B of stock at ~$80 (below my intrinsic value estimate — value-accretive).
- **The scar tissue is real:** ~$10B+ of shareholder capital incinerated in fiber/small cells, a 52% drawdown from the 2021 peak, and a dividend that is *lower today than in 2019*. Insider ownership is minimal (typical for a large-cap REIT), so management's alignment is salary-and-options rather than owner-operator.
- **Verdict:** a credible operator inheriting a repaired but still over-levered balance sheet. I want to see two more years of ROIC above cost of capital before I pay up for the "new CCI."

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

Crown Castle is, finally, a clean, high-margin, wide-moat U.S. tower business with embedded 3% escalators and light capex. That is a business I would happily own for a decade.

But at $88 I would be buying it at fair value with **7.5x leverage, a 91% AFFO payout ratio, negative book equity, ~3–4% growth, and refinancing headwinds** — and at a premium to the better-capitalized, faster-growing operator in the same industry. My rules are unambiguous: *a business that trades at a fair price is not a buy.*

- **Buy trigger:** **≤$72** (a 20% discount to the ~$88 midpoint). At $65–70 the 6.5%+ dividend yield, 15x AFFO, and private-market asset value provide a real margin of safety.
- **Trim/sell trigger:** **> $100** (a premium to even the private-market case, unless accompanied by a credible strategic review).
- **Existing holders:** hold, collect the 4.8% yield, and let the deleveraging and buyback do the work.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Tenant concentration.** Three carriers plus EchoStar account for the large majority of revenue. A DISH/EchoStar bankruptcy would remove ~3–5% of revenue and create pricing pressure.
2. **Leverage + rates.** ~7.5x net debt/EBITDA with meaningful near-term maturities. Refinancing $20B+ of debt at 5% vs. ~4% book cost permanently impairs AFFO; a credit-spread widening would be severe for equity holders given negative book equity.
3. **Payout ratio leaves no oxygen.** At ~91% of AFFO, a revenue disappointments forces either another dividend cut or deferred investment — the very trap that precedes REIT de-ratings.
4. **Carrier capex cycle.** U.S. wireless capital intensity is declining post-5G build; tower amendments and new colocations are the growth engine and they are slowing.
5. **Satellite disintermediation.** Starlink direct-to-cell could compress rural tower demand beyond 2030.
6. **Execution risk on the "new CCI" narrative.** The bull case rests on a first-year CEO; the prior decade is a cautionary record of the same board's judgment.
7. **Data gap (disclosed honestly):** current-year peer AFFO multiples and live post-sale net debt were not directly available from the data feeds; I estimated net debt (~$22.3B) from the disclosed $7B repayment and used approximate peer multiples. My conclusion is robust to a ±$2B net-debt error (it moves IV by ~$5/share, not the verdict).

---

**Bottom line:** A wide-moat asset, a repaired strategy, and a fair price — but no discount. **Hold; buy the leverage-driven selloff, not the current re-rating.**
