## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

Post-divestiture the footprint collapses to a genuinely small operational base (2025 continuing operations):

- **Electricity: 55,208 MWh** (towers 44,151; offices 11,057) vs 154,738 MWh consolidated pre-deal — a ~64% reduction in energy use from the fiber/small-cell exit alone.
- **Fuel: 69,988 MMBtu** (fleet 40,959; back-up generators 17,977).
- **100% of 2025 electricity matched with renewable purchases**; 12,000 carbon offsets retired; **Scope 1 & 2 carbon-neutral target for 2025 achieved.**
- Abatement actions are real but modest: LED retrofits on lit towers, fleet efficiency, office footprint reduction.

**Where the risk actually sits:**
1. **Target substance, not ambition.** Carbon neutrality is delivered via RECs + offsets against a very small absolute base — the cheapest form of decarbonisation. Management explicitly targets only *maintaining coverage through 2027*, then "will reassess" — i.e., **no post-2027 target, no net-zero commitment, and no Scope 3 target**. Against AMT (net-zero 2040) and EQIX (2030), CCI screens as a target-setter laggard.
2. **Physical climate risk is the largest E item** — a geographically dispersed 40,000+ tower portfolio has concentrated Gulf/Atlantic coastal exposure. Mitigants: distributed risk, insurance, contractual restoration/force-majeure pass-through, and mission-critical tenant demand (carriers restore service first).
3. **Legacy environmental liabilities largely transferred** with the fiber business (SPA allocates "Fiber Business Liabilities" to EQT/Zayo purchasers, subject to customary indemnities) — a meaningful de-risking, including reduced exposure to the industry's lead-clad-cable issue. **Indemnity scope could not be verified from the SPA excerpt — flag for diligence.**
4. No spills, fines, or remediation actions identified in the period.

**Transition risk: Low.** Towers are enabling infrastructure for connectivity, not a carbon-taxed activity; no CBAM or stranded-asset exposure.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **LOW**

**Positives:**
- **TRIR of 0.27 in 2025**, down 46% YoY and ~90% below the US BLS average — best-in-class for a portfolio with field operations.
- Small, white-collar-skewed workforce (~1,500) with low union density → low strike/labour-dispute risk.
- Shared-infrastructure model is a genuine social positive: multi-tenant colocation reduces land use and speeds coverage; supports public safety, E911, and rural connectivity — strong **social licence** foundation.
- 93% year-end performance-conversation completion; AI literacy training at ~100% (governance-of-technology signal).

**Residual risks:**
- **Contractor safety.** Tower climbing remains one of the most dangerous US trades; industry fatalities recur and OSHA fines are trivial ($4,500 in a 2025 Texas fatality settlement). CCI is largely legally insulated as asset owner, but a high-profile contractor fatality on a CCI site is a live reputational event.
- **Municipal siting friction / NIMBY** — a recurring cost of growth (local zoning litigation, "effective denial" suits), not a systemic threat.
- **Customer concentration** (Verizon, T-Mobile, AT&T, DISH) is a commercial, not social, risk — but the DISH default dispute (>$3.5bn claimed by CCI; a $32m Colorado appellate ruling affirmed against CCI) is a reminder that tenant credit, not ESG, is the dominant risk to this equity.
- No diversity metrics disclosed with numeric targets — a disclosure gap vs peers, not a controversy.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10**

**Deductions — this is CCI's defining ESG weakness:**
- **Serial CEO turnover.** Jay Brown out (Dec 2023, after Elliott pressure) → Steven Moskowitz hired Apr 2024, **terminated Mar 2025 after 11 months** → Dan Schlanger interim → Christian Hillabrant permanent from 15 Sep 2025. Three CEOs in under two years at a business whose sole strategic question (own fiber or not) was answered by reversal.
- **Pay for failure.** Moskowitz received **~$18.2m total FY2025 compensation** and departed with a severance of **2× base salary + target bonus** despite a sub-one-year tenure and a strategic U-turn. Board simultaneously told investors the termination was "not due to any disagreements on policies or financial performance" — an unsatisfying explanation that undermines board credibility.
- **Contested board legitimacy.** Elliott's Dec 2023 cooperation agreement installed **Jason Genrich** as director and **Sunit Patel** as CFO without a shareholder vote; co-founder Ted Miller's Boots Capital sued in Delaware Chancery calling the arrangement "coercive and disenfranchising." Miller then lost his 2024 proxy contest decisively (all four nominees rejected, all 12 company nominees elected) — which cuts *for* the board, but the litigation and the two-year activist war are a real distraction cost.
- **Capital-allocation record.** Multi-year fiber/small-cell buildout, subsequent impairments, then an **$8.5bn sale (closed 1 May 2026)** — value destruction that governance should have prevented.
- **Disclosure sloppiness.** The current sustainability page still labels Hillabrant "Interim President & CEO" months after his permanent appointment.

**Credits:**
- **89% independent board with an independent chair**; CEO/Chair roles separated.
- **Single share class**, no dual-class structure, no poison pill, proxy access available.
- **~93% historical say-on-pay support**; board has committed to reviewing the comp programme.
- High leverage (~6.3× net debt/EBITDA) with an IG-rated, almost entirely fixed-rate book — no ESG-linked debt covenants to trip, but also limited green-finance signalling.

**Verdict:** structurally sound framework, but **demonstrably poor execution and accountability at the top**. A 4/10 reflects process quality discounted for outcomes.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **Jurisdiction: effectively US-only** post-divestiture. **EU CSRD and CBAM: not applicable** — a structural advantage versus AMT (global) and EQIX/DLR (EU data-centre exposure).
- **California SB 253/261**: CCI exceeds the $1bn revenue threshold and does business in CA → Scope 1&2 GHG reporting from 2026, Scope 3 potentially narrowed following the 9th Circuit proceedings. Low cost given the small footprint; manageable.
- **SEC climate disclosure rule**: stayed/substantially withdrawn — no near-term federal obligation.
- **Sector-specific**: FCC siting and NEPA review, FAA obstruction lighting, Migratory Bird Treaty Act / bald-eagle permitting, state wireless facility statutes. These are permitting-friction items, not disclosure regime risk.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants: none identified.** Debt is IG, predominantly fixed-rate — relevant because a 6.3× levered REIT is acutely sensitive to cost of capital, and governance is the ESG factor most likely to widen its spread.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

**Environmental: Leader (on footprint), Laggard (on target-setting)** — genuinely low carbon intensity, but a REC/offset-dependent, 2027-expiring commitment with no Scope 3 or net-zero target.
**Social: Average-to-Leader** — excellent safety metrics and a defensible social-purpose business model; thin disclosure on workforce diversity.
**Governance: Laggard** — three CEOs in two years, pay-for-failure, activist-installed leadership, litigation over shareholder rights.

The result is a company whose *operations* are ESG-clean and whose *boardroom* is not. Environmental and social strength cannot offset a governance score in the bottom quartile of large-cap REITs.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (mildly negative tilt from Governance)

- **The divestiture is an ESG positive that the market is under-crediting.** CCI now runs a ~55 GWh electricity business with carbon-neutral Scope 1&2, no CBAM/CSRD exposure, no EU regulatory perimeter, and legacy fiber/lead-cable liabilities largely transferred to purchasers. That is a materially cleaner risk profile than the pre-2025 conglomerate.
- **But governance is a live discount-rate input, not a footnote.** A board that pays a fired 11-month CEO $18.2m and has cycled through three CEOs while reversing an $8.5bn strategy warrants a governance discount. Until Hillabrant establishes a track record across a full cycle and comp is re-based on AFFO/share and leverage reduction, expect intermittent activist and proxy noise.
- **ESG is not the thesis driver here — leverage and AFFO inflection are.** At 6.3× net debt/EBITDA, the binding constraint is the cost and availability of capital; the SpaceX/80,000-site narrative and Q3 2026 guidance raise are the real catalysts. ESG neither adds nor subtracts meaningfully from the equity case, but it does argue against paying a premium multiple versus AMT.
- **Watch items:** (1) post-2027 climate target — or absence of one — in the next sustainability report; (2) any Scope 3 disclosure under CA SB 253; (3) compensation redesign at the 2027 proxy; (4) indemnity scope for retained fiber environmental liabilities; (5) a contractor fatality on a CCI site.
