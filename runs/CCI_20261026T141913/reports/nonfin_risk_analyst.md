# CROWN CASTLE INC. (NYSE: CCI) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
*As of late October 2026. Company is now a pure-play U.S. tower REIT following the May 1, 2026 completion of the ~$8.5B fiber/small-cell sale to EQT and Zayo.*

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (improving, but still contested)

**Positives**
- **Independent Board Chair** — P. Robert Bartolo (chair since May 2022; independent; ex-T. Rowe Price telecom PM). No CEO/chairman duality. Independent directors hold executive sessions without management.
- **Board accountability demonstrated** — at the May 20, 2026 annual meeting, shareholders **rejected all four Boots Capital (co-founder Ted Miller) nominees and elected all 12 company nominees**, a clear ratification of the board's strategy.
- **CEO change forced by activists** — Jay Brown's Dec-2023 retirement followed Elliott's "Reclaiming the Crown" campaign; Chris Hillabrant (ex-Vantage Towers CEO, ex-Tillman COO, ex-T-Mobile/Ericsson/Samsung) took over Sept 15, 2025. The board has shown willingness to override incumbency.
- **Pay alignment** — Hillabrant's FY total compensation ~$14.15M, ~1.9% salary, overwhelmingly equity-based; refreshed 2026 Executive Management Team plan.

**Negatives**
- **Governance under legal attack.** Miller's Delaware Chancery action seeks to invalidate the Elliott cooperation agreement (calling it "coercive and disenfranchising") and challenges the board's 2026 expansion to add Steven Moskowitz; the judge permitted an expedited case and said he would examine whether directors "put a thumb on the scale." Even a win leaves reputational residue around board-process integrity.
- **Serial strategy resets** — three years of activist-driven reversals (fiber build-out → fiber sale; dividend cut; CEO and now CFO/COO turnover) point to weak historical capital-allocation discipline and reactive rather than proactive strategy-setting.
- **Deep management churn** (see §4): CEO replaced Sept 2025, and the CFO and COO are now both leaving within months of each other.
- **Board tenure/skills mix** — several long-tenured directors (Bartolo since 2014, Goldsmith since 2018) alongside newer activist-era additions; refresh is underway but not complete.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: LOW–MODERATE
- **No SEC enforcement actions, DOJ investigations, or EDGAR full-text enforcement hits** found for CCI. No restatement, no material-weakness disclosure; the Q2-2026 10-Q carries clean CEO/CFO certifications (Hillabrant signed Aug 5, 2026).
- **Litigation is the real exposure, and it is counterparty/commercial, not regulatory:**
  - **DISH Wireless MSA termination** — CCI declared default, terminated the agreement and is claiming **>$3.5B** in remaining payments; DISH answered in 2026 denying breach. **DISH DBS emerged from a prepackaged Chapter 11 on Oct 1, 2026** ($4.75B deleveraging); the DISH Wireless cases continue, and CCI has objected to debtor-in-possession/escrow arrangements that could drain recovery. Claims bar date was Aug 7, 2026. Recovery is now a **bankruptcy-claims exercise, not a contract dispute.**
  - **Delaware Chancery** — Miller/Boots Capital vs. the Board and Elliott (above). Non-monetary but governance-relevant.
  - **Legacy/easement and zoning matters** — e.g., *RJI v. Crown Castle South* (11th Cir. 2024) and recurring municipal siting disputes; individually immaterial, collectively a friction cost.
- **Pending federal rulemaking** — FCC proposals to fast-track/shorten state and local siting review are a **two-sided** regulatory item: they lower CCI's permitting cost and speed but invite municipal litigation and political backlash. No pending license, spectrum, or merger approvals are material to CCI.
- **Legacy data-privacy exposure** — an older payroll-information breach notification to U.S. employees; no current material GDPR/CCPA action identified.

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis (trailing ~12 months): NET SELLING, NO CONVICTION BUYING
- SEC Form 4 flow is dominated by **RSU vesting, share withholding for taxes, and programmatic sales**, not discretionary conviction trades. Aggregator data shows **net insider selling of roughly $0.7M over the trailing six months.**
- **CEO Hillabrant (Sept 15–17, 2026):** converted 31,806 time-based RSUs; **12,160 shares withheld at $74.17** to cover taxes — mechanically a disposition, not an open-market sale signal. He retains ~$2.5M of stock.
- **Feb 2026 cluster** (~20 Form 4s across 27–28 Feb and 23 Feb) is a **single annual equity-award/vesting event**, not broad-based selling.
- Most recent open-market print is a **1,500-share sale by a VP/Controller** — immaterial.
- **Critically: no open-market insider purchases by any director or officer were identified** over the past year, including after a ~12% drawdown from the 52-week high of $100.50. Given the shares trade near $88, that absence of buying is a soft negative read on management's own confidence in near-term re-rating.

## 4. Top Operational Risks
- **Extreme customer concentration plus a bankrupt counterparty.** As of Q3-2026 annualized site rental revenue: **T-Mobile ~42%, AT&T ~28%, Verizon ~23%, all others ~7%.** Three carriers = ~93% of rent roll. The fourth customer (DISH) is in Chapter 11 with CCI asserting a $3.5B claim that is likely to be recovered only in partial, discounted form. A single network-decision or merger among the Big Three (further Sprint-style consolidation/decommissioning) is an existential-level revenue event, and CCI's pricing power is structurally inferior to a diversified tower operator.
- **Leadership and key-person risk at the worst possible moment.** The CEO seat changed hands in Sept 2025; on Sept 14, 2026 CCI disclosed **CFO Sunit Patel's retirement effective March 31, 2027 and COO Cathy Piche's departure Feb 22, 2027**, with Kris Hinson named incoming CFO. That is the **entire C-suite turning over within ~18 months** while the company simultaneously re-bases itself as a pure-play tower operator, cuts operating costs, and executes an $8.4B integration/separation. Institutional knowledge loss and execution slippage risk are elevated; ~1,500–4,000 employees post-divestiture means thin bench depth.
- **Post-divestiture operating and continuity risk.** The fiber/small-cell separation depends on transitional services arrangements with EQT/Zayo; a pure-play tower model concentrates all execution risk on field operations, ground-lease renewals, structural/regulatory compliance across ~40,000+ sites, and IT/cyber resilience — at 6.3x net debt/EBITDA (≈$17.1B debt) there is little cushion to absorb an operational shock. Note also the emerging, unconfirmed **SpaceX 80,000-site lease negotiation** — a large potential mitigant to concentration, but one that would create a new single-customer dependency if signed.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: MODERATE
- **Contested-control narrative is public and persistent**: a co-founder suing his own company in Chancery, a proxy fight the company won but that aired internal dysfunction, and an activist cooperation agreement characterized in the press as disenfranchising. This is governance-theatre risk that keeps CCI on ESG/proxy-advisor watchlists.
- **Leadership churn will be read by the Street as instability**, especially CFO+COO exits disclosed weeks before Q3 results — sell-side framing already includes "Be Aware Of Lingering Risks."
- **Dividend and strategy credibility**: the 2024 dividend reset and repeated portfolio reversals have damaged management's forward-guidance credibility with income investors (yield ~5.4% on a reduced base).
- **Local/political friction**: cell-tower siting and zoning opposition is chronic but low-severity for a REIT that does not sell to consumers; **no consumer brand, product-recall, or ESG-controversy score issue** of the kind that hits retail-facing issuers.
- Offsets: an $8.5B clean exit from fiber, a raised 2026 outlook (site rental revenue, EBITDA, AFFO) and the SpaceX headline have improved the narrative in the last quarter.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE (upper end)** — with a credible path to HIGH
Regulatory and fraud risk are genuinely low; governance is structurally sound (independent chair, contested election won, equity-heavy pay) but operationally immature and under litigation. The rating is held at Moderate rather than High because the two largest exposures — DISH and the C-suite transition — are *identified, disclosed, and being actively managed*. It would migrate to **High** on: an adverse Chancery ruling on the board-expansion/Elliott agreement, a worse-than-expected DISH recovery, or any gap in the CFO/COO succession (e.g., a second CFO departure before the 2026 10-K is filed).

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation
1. **Simultaneous CFO and COO departures (Sept 14, 2026)** effective Feb–Mar 2027, on top of a CEO change 13 months earlier — combined with an incoming CFO who must sign the 2026 10-K. **Escalate: audit-committee and disclosure-control continuity.** Watch for any 8-K disclosing a restatement, material weakness, or a second finance departure.
2. **$3.5B DISH claim against a Chapter 11 estate**, with CCI objecting to escrow/DIP mechanics. Quantify the recovery assumption embedded in consensus and probe for any reserve/contingency language; revenue already guided down on DISH.
3. **~93% revenue concentration in three carriers (T-Mobile 42%)** — no structural hedge exists; confirm whether the SpaceX discussions are contractual or press speculation before crediting them.
4. **Active Delaware Chancery litigation over board process** (Miller/Elliott cooperation agreement and 2026 board expansion). An adverse ruling on director conduct would be a governance and proxy-advisor event.
5. **Absence of any insider open-market buying into a 12% drawdown**, with net insider selling on file — weak internal signal.
6. **Leverage of 6.3x net debt/EBITDA (~$17.1B)** is financial, but it is the transmission mechanism that converts any of the above operational shocks into an equity event; treat as a risk multiplier, not a standalone item.
