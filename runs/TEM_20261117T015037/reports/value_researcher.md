# TEM — Tempus AI, Inc.
**Value Investment Analysis | Price $64.76 (Nov 16, 2026) | Market cap $11.7B | EV ≈ $12.6B**

---

## 0. Data Basis & Caveats

I use trailing-twelve-month (TTM) figures through **Q3 2026** (reported Nov 2, 2026) plus FY2022–FY2025 annuals. Current price $64.76 × 181.3M diluted shares = $11.74B equity; plus $841M net debt (Q3'26) = **$12.58B enterprise value**.

Two data-integrity notes: (a) the quarterly feed reports operating income and EBITDA that do not reconcile ($56.9M operating loss vs. +$20.5M "EBITDA" in Q3'26 — the latter is adjusted and adds back ~$48M SBC); (b) cash fell $179M in Q3'26 while reported FCF was only −$5M, implying an unexplained ~$170M outflow (small acquisitions/intangibles explain only ~$40M). I could not resolve this. Treat near-term cash figures with caution.

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow (and unproven)**

Tempus is two businesses bolted together:

- **Diagnostics (~85%+ of revenue)**: next-gen sequencing (xT/xR), PCR, and — via the 2025 **Ambry Genetics** acquisition — hereditary/genetic testing. This is a **clinical laboratory business**. It has real scale in ordering/interpretation and a genuinely differentiated multimodal database, but labs are reimbursement-driven, price-taker economics. CMS PAMA cuts and payer pressure are structural, not hypothetical.
- **Data & Services (Lens, Insights, Trials, Next, Algos)**: licensing de-identified clinical + molecular + imaging data to pharma (AstraZeneca, GSK, Merck, Recursion). Higher margin, higher strategic value.

**Evidence the moat is real:** 62.7%→64.8% gross margin expansion; a data asset that compounds with every test ordered (a genuine flywheel); Hub/EHR integration creates modest switching costs; long-term pharma data partnerships.

**Evidence against:**
- **ROIC is deeply negative** (−13.9% in 2025), and the two "positive ROIC/ROE" years in the dataset (2022–23) are artifacts of *negative* equity — meaningless.
- Foundational Medicine/Roche, Caris, Guardant, Natera, Flatiron, and the payers' own data arms all compete for the same dollars. No demonstrated pricing power.
- Revenue growth is heavily acquisition-distorted: FY2025's +83% includes Ambry. **Q3'26 organic YoY growth was ~23%** — good, but decelerating and nowhere near the headline.
- The company is 11 years old. A durable moat should show up in returns on capital by now. It does not.

Verdict: the data flywheel is a real asset; the *business model around it* has not yet demonstrated moat economics. **Narrow moat, low conviction.**

**What's working:** FCF has improved for four straight quarters: −$128M → −$43M → −$83M → −$19M → **−$5.5M**. Gross margin is up 24 points in four years. Revenue is nearly 5x 2022 levels. There is a real operating-leverage story here.

**What isn't:** The company has never produced a dollar of free cash flow, and once you charge SBC as the compensation expense it is, the economic burn is ~$350M/year. SBC at 13% of revenue plus 12% annual dilution means shareholders have been funding ~$200M/yr of payroll through the printing press. Goodwill and intangibles ($823M) exceed total equity ($500M). Retained earnings are −$2.53B.

This is a **3/10**: the trajectory is right, the destination is unproven, and the shareholder-cost structure is poor.

## 3. Valuation — Intrinsic value ≈ **$34/share** (range $12–$78)

Discounted cash flow is the only honest method here, and it requires explicit scenario work because the base year FCF is negative.

**Reverse-engineering the current price.** At $12.58B EV, a 10% required return with 3% terminal growth implies ~$880M of steady-state free cash flow (7% implied FCF yield). At a generous 20% FCF margin that requires **~$4.4B of revenue with 22% margins** — roughly 2.9x today's run-rate, executed flawlessly, with no capital shortfall along the way.

**Forward scenarios (2031 exit, 10% discount rate, 185M diluted shares):**

| Scenario | 2031 Revenue | FCF margin | Exit multiple | PV/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear (30%)** — reimbursement pressure, growth fades to high-single digits, continued dilution | $2.3B | 8% | 12x | **$12** |
| **Base (45%)** — 15% revenue CAGR, mid-teens EBIT margins, lab-like multiple | $3.2B | 14% | 18x | **$22–25** |
| **Bull (25%)** — the "AI data platform" winner, 29% CAGR, best-in-class margins | $4.5B | 22% | 25x | **$78** |

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $34.**

Current price $64.76 is **~90% above base-case intrinsic value** and roughly equal to my *bull* case. To earn a 25% margin of safety on the base case, I would need to buy at **$17–19**; even against the bull case, the entry would need to be ~$58.

Critically, the **asymmetry is upside-down**: another 20% of upside to the blue-sky scenario versus 60–80% downside to the bear. That is the opposite of what a value investor is paid to accept. Notably, the sell-side targets cluster at $66–100 (BTIG $100, H.C. Wainwright $85, Needham $89) with Goldman, UBS, Jefferies and Redburn all initiating **Neutral** at $67–75 — a Street that is already fully priced with the price.

## 4. Management Quality — **Mixed / Watch Item**

**Positives:** Founder-led and founder-controlled (Eric Lefkofsky, also co-founder of Groupon), substantial personal stake and obvious long-term conviction. Built a genuine strategic asset from scratch; pharma partnerships with AstraZeneca, GSK, Merck and Recursion validate the data platform to sophisticated buyers.

**Concerns:**
- **Capital allocation is acquisition-led**: Ambry Genetics (hundreds of millions in cash and stock), Deep 6 AI, miRecule. Goodwill now exceeds book equity. Organic R&D is not the growth engine — buying revenue is. This is exactly the empire-building pattern I look for early.
- **The SBC/dilution policy is the single largest destroyer of per-share value**: ~12% annual share growth with $200M/yr of stock comp. Management is paying itself in the currency shareholders are diluted by, which makes adjusted profitability metrics close to meaningless.
- **No dividend, no meaningful buyback** ($3M/year, tax-withholding only). Entirely rational for a cash-burning company — but it means shareholders bear all the dilution.
- **Insider ownership percentage: data gap.** I could not retrieve a reliable figure; this should be verified from the DEF 14A before initiating any position.
- Debt is $1.43B of convertibles against $637M cash — a refinancing/conversion overhang that will keep diluting.

I would not call management dishonest. I would call them aggressive growth allocators, which for a Buffett-style owner is a materially different (and worse) proposition.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / DO NOT INITIATE**

TEM is a fast-growing, strategically interesting, well-run-in-a-growth-sense company. It is **not a value investment**, and at $64.76 it is not even a reasonably priced growth investment.

- **Moat:** Narrow and unproven; ROIC −14% after 11 years.
- **Financial quality:** 3/10; never generated FCF; $200M/yr SBC; 12% dilution; negative tangible equity.
- **Valuation:** ~$34 probability-weighted intrinsic value vs. $64.76 — a **negative margin of safety of roughly 90%**. The market has already capitalized the bull case.
- **Circle of competence test:** I can understand this business, but I cannot underwrite its terminal economics with the confidence the price requires. That alone argues for passing.

**A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy. This one trades at the price of a business that has already succeeded.** If the FCF inflection now visible in Q3'26 (FCF −$5.5M) is sustained and the stock re-rates to $19–25, I would revisit with real interest — that is the level at which the narrative stops being priced in for free.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to my Sell call (i.e., where I could be wrong):**
1. **Genuine operating leverage surprise.** FCF has improved $122M in four quarters. If Q4'26 prints FCF-positive and 2027 guides to $500M+ incremental revenue at 65%+ gross margin, the DCF base case moves up fast.
2. **Data-licensing step-change.** If a pharma partner converts an Insight/Lens relationship into a nine-figure, multi-year, high-margin deal, the 22% FCF margin bull case becomes base case.
3. **Ambry cross-sell.** If hereditary testing funnels into oncology data at rising attach rates, the flywheel is more real than I credit.

**Risks to owning it (why I won't):**
1. **Reimbursement policy** — CMS rate action on molecular diagnostics could compress the 65% gross margin directly.
2. **Competitive intensity** from Foundation Medicine/Roche, Guardant, Natera, Caris — a lab price war destroys the margin story.
3. **Dilution and convertibles** — 12%/yr share growth plus $1.43B of converts structurally transfers value from owners to option-holders.
4. **SBC as hidden payroll** — reported "adjusted EBITDA" flatters by ~$200M/year; the true breakeven is much further away than headlines imply.
5. **Beta 3.6** — this is one of the most volatile names in the healthcare complex; a 40% drawdown would be unremarkable and is inconsistent with a 5–10 year patient holding period.

**Bottom line:** I want to own the data flywheel at a price. That price is roughly a quarter of today's.
