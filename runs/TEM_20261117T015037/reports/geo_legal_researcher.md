## Tempus AI, Inc. (NASDAQ: TEM) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Prepared for the investment memo. Data as of the most recent filings (FY2025 10-K, Q3 2026 10-Q/8-K, 2 Nov 2026) and news through mid-Nov 2026.*

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

Tempus is a **single-jurisdiction business by revenue** with a global customer base by *counterparty*. Employees are ~97.5% North America (Revelio Labs), and the FY2025 10-K Exhibit 21.1 subsidiary list is short: Ambry Genetics Corporation (Delaware), Genome Smart Inc., **Europe Limited (Ireland)**, Paige.AI, Inc. — i.e., one Irish/EU vehicle from the Paige acquisition. Exact revenue-by-geography is disclosed with minimal granularity (the company reports by product line — Diagnostics vs. Data & Applications — not by region), so percentages below are estimates and should be treated as such.

| Country / region | Revenue exposure (est.) | Nature of exposure | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | ~95%+ (est.) | Core CLIA lab (Chicago), Ambry Genetics, all payer/reimbursement, all litigation venues | **High** (legal/regulatory, not political) |
| **Ireland / EU (Paige Europe)** | <2% (est.) | IVDR-certified digital pathology (Paige Prostate); EU AI Act exposure | Medium |
| **United Kingdom** | <1% (est.) | Paige UK / academic pathology sites | Low–Medium |
| **Japan, South Korea, Singapore** | <1% (est.) | Scientific presence only (e.g., CTOS 2026 in Seoul); no lab revenue disclosed | Low |
| **Pharma counterparties domiciled ex-US (AstraZeneca, GSK — UK; Servier — France)** | Embedded in Data & Applications (~25% of revenue) | Cross-border data licensing = DOJ bulk-data rule exposure | Medium–High |
| **China / Russia / Iran / DPRK / Cuba / Venezuela** | **~0%** | No disclosed operations; *inbound* restriction under DOJ rule, not sanctions on TEM | Low (but see Risk #2) |

**Key point for the memo:** this is not a sanctions, expropriation, or EM-currency story. It is a **domestic U.S. legal and data-governance story** with one genuine cross-border dimension (genomic data flows to ex-U.S. pharma partners).

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**Risk #1 — U.S. genetic-privacy litigation escalation (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
The consolidated class action *Farrier et al. v. Tempus AI, Inc.* (N.D. Ill., filed 15 Apr 2026; **amended consolidated complaint 24 Aug 2026**) alleges that Tempus compelled Ambry Genetics to hand over >1 million genetic test records post-acquisition, used them to train AI models, and licensed them to 70+ partners in deals exceeding **$1.1 billion** without written authorization. The 21-count complaint runs on Illinois GIPA plus California CMIA and consumer-protection statutes in Florida, Georgia, Michigan, New York, and West Virginia. **GIPA carries statutory damages of $15,000 per intentional/reckless violation ($2,500 negligent)** — with a class plausibly in the hundreds of thousands, theoretical exposure is in the billions, materially above the ~$11.5bn market cap tolerance for a single contingency. Note the *legal theory* is systemic: plaintiffs attack de-identification itself ("genetic information cannot be deidentified"), which strikes at the foundation of the entire Insights/Data licensing model. Peers and follow-on suits against Tempus's pharma *customers* are a realistic second wave (Fisher Phillips).

**Risk #2 — DOJ Bulk Sensitive Data Rule constraining cross-border data licensing (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium-High)**
The DOJ Data Security Program (28 CFR Part 202, implementing EO 14117) prohibits/restricts "bulk" human genomic data transactions with countries of concern and covered persons; **enforcement actions begin 6 Oct 2026** (UCSF compliance guidance; Morgan Lewis, Egan Nelson). Tempus's Data & Applications revenue depends on licensing de-identified molecular data to AstraZeneca, GSK, Merck, BMS, Pfizer, Moderna, Recursion and others — several with non-U.S. parents and non-U.S. research staff. Even where the parent is UK/French (not a country of concern), "covered person" and restricted-transaction analysis, data-download caps, and due-diligence/audit clauses now apply. Practical effects: slower deal cycles, contractual restructuring (note the Sept 2026 Recursion amendment adding an aggregate record cap on downloaded data — Exhibit 10.1 to the Q3 10-Q), and potential loss of certain ex-U.S. customers.

**Risk #3 — EU/UK regulatory divergence as a growth constraint (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium)**
The Paige acquisition (announced Oct 2025) is the vehicle for any international expansion, and it brings Tempus into **EU IVDR** and **EU AI Act** scope. AI diagnostic software used in medical devices is Annex I high-risk under the AI Act, with conformity-assessment obligations phasing in ahead of the August 2027 deadline; the FY2025 10-K explicitly flags the AI Act's "transparency, conformity and risk" obligations as a cost driver (SEC 10-K, tem-20251231.htm). Any EU expansion also multiplies GIPA-style exposure under GDPR Art. 9 special-category rules and emerging national genetic-data statutes. Impact is medium because EU revenue is currently immaterial — this is a *ceiling* on the international growth story, not a threat to the base case.

*Watch item (not top-3):* Section 232 pharma tariffs and drug-pricing pressure compress pharma R&D/data budgets — Tempus's highest-margin revenue line (Data & Applications, +33% y/y in Q3 2026) is levered to pharma discretionary data spend.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Ambry/Farrier class action** — the dominant contingency; consolidated, amended, proceeding in N.D. Ill.; plaintiffs' executive committee appointed May 2026. No reserve disclosed; no ruling on class certification yet. This is the single largest binary risk in the equity story.
- **Securities "AI-washing" class action (filed June 2025, post short-seller report)** — the **lead plaintiff voluntarily dismissed**, citing inability to satisfy PSLRA pleading standards (Bloomberg Law, "Tempus Investor Drops Lawsuit Levying Short Seller Accusations"). Risk has decayed but copycat firms (Rosen, Robbins, Kessler Topaz) continue soliciting; any restatement or SEC inquiry reopens it.
- **M&A/antitrust** — the **Tempus–Personalis HSR waiting period expired 2 Oct 2026** (MLex). Competitively benign, but note that absorbing another genomic dataset *increases* the privacy-litigation surface — diligence on consent scope is now the gating regulatory issue for Tempus's roll-up strategy (Crowell & Moring).
- **FDA device regulation** — positive momentum: four FDA-cleared ECG-AI products (ECG-Low EF, ECG-AF, ECG-PH, ECG-MR) and an FDA approval for xT with ~$85m of annual pricing upside (Yahoo Finance). But the majority of the menu remains **LDTs under CLIA**, a regime that has whipsawed politically (FDA's LDT rule was vacated in 2025); renewed federal rulemaking or Congressional action is a live, two-sided regulatory catalyst.
- **Reimbursement (CMS)** — Tempus is actively lobbying CMS (comment on CMS-1850-P; xT CDx carries ~$4,500 Medicare ADLT pricing). CMS payment policy for AI-enabled and multi-cancer assays is the main non-litigation earnings risk.
- **State legislative wave** — Utah and South Dakota enacted genetic-privacy statutes in 2026, with more advancing; Illinois GIPA, California, New Hampshire, Nevada, New Mexico, Alaska, Oregon statutes are all being tested against Tempus. Compliance cost rises structurally, and each new statute creates a new damages theory.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **De-identification as a control is legally contested.** Plaintiffs argue genomic data is *inherently* identifiable; if a court agrees, the "de-identified" basis for Insights licensing — and the company's public statements about it — become both a GIPA exposure and a disclosure/securities exposure. **This is the highest-severity flag in the file.**
2. **Consent-scope inheritance through M&A.** Legacy Ambry consents were obtained for diagnostic testing, not AI training or third-party licensing. Applies equally to any future acquisition (Personalis, Deep 6, Paige).
3. **Cross-border data transfer governance.** Needs documented DSP compliance (data-download caps, audit rights, country-of-concern screening of customer personnel) — evidenced only partially by the Recursion amendment.
4. **FCPA / AML / sanctions: low.** No operations or disclosed revenue in sanctioned jurisdictions; no DOJ/SEC enforcement disclosed. Counterparty risk in pharma licensing is negligible.
5. **No disclosed government investigation** (DOJ, FTC, SEC, state AG) as of the Q3 2026 10-Q. State AG interest in genetic data is the most likely next escalation.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

The rating is driven almost entirely by **domestic U.S. legal and privacy-regulatory exposure**, not by classic geopolitical risk. Strip out the Ambry litigation and the profile is **Low-Medium**: ~95% U.S. revenue, no China/Russia dependency, no sanctions nexus, no currency-repatriation or expropriation risk, and a U.S.-domiciled supply chain (Illumina-based sequencing). The reason to hold the rating at High rather than Medium is asymmetry: a single adverse class-certification or de-identification ruling could plausibly impair the Data & Applications segment (>$400m run-rate, highest-margin) and force a consent-remediation process across the entire 45-million-record library — an outcome the market is not currently pricing into a 3.6-beta, ~$11.5bn-cap name.
