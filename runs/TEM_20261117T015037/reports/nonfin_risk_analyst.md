## NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — TEMPUS AI, INC. (NASDAQ: TEM)

**Snapshot:** Healthcare IT / precision medicine. IPO June 14, 2024. Market cap ~$11.5B. Share price $71.60 (Nov 13, 2026) vs. $89.09 peak (Sep 29) — ~20% off high. Beta 3.61 (top-decile volatility). CEO/Chairman Eric Lefkofsky, founder, ~24% economic owner. 3,800 FTE. FY25 10-K filed (CIK 0001717115, `tem-20251231.htm`). Q3'26: revenue $411.5M (+23%), net loss $14.9M, first positive adjusted EBITDA quarter.

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (strong board, weak control architecture)

**Positives — genuinely above-average board caliber:**
- Eight-member independent roster: Peter Barris (ex-NEA), Eric Belcher, Jennifer Doudna (Nobel laureate, CRISPR), David Epstein (ex-Novartis CEO), Wayne Frederick MD, **Scott Gottlieb MD (former FDA Commissioner)**, Theodore Leonsis, Nadja West MD (former U.S. Army Surgeon General). Regulatory/clinical depth is exceptional and directly relevant to the FDA/MolDX reimbursement risk this business runs.
- Standard audit/comp/nominating committee structure; Lefkofsky sits on no committees.

**Structural negatives:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality** — Lefkofsky is simultaneously Founder, CEO, Chairman and a 10%+ owner (Form 4s list "CEO and Chairman, 10% Owner"). No independent chair, no lead-independent-director disclosure evident.
- **Dual-class control:** Class A = 1 vote, **Class B = 30 votes** per share (per FY2026 proxy). Lefkofsky beneficially holds ~42.8M shares as-converted = 24.0% of combined common (13G/A). A 30:1 Class B plus a 24% economic block implies **de facto voting control of the shareholder franchise** — a minority holder cannot outvote management on any matter. *(Exact voting-power percentage not verified — flagged as gap.)*
- **Reincorporation completed** (approved in the 2025 proxy). The registrant is tracked in some data sources as "Tempus AI Inc. (Pre-Reincorporation)," and my filing tools return "No 10-K / No DEF 14A found" for TEM — an entity-structure change that complicates filing access and is worth confirming with IR.
- **Related-party transactions are the single most serious governance issue.** The April 2025 **Pathos AI** deal (~$200M of contract value) is with a company **founded, managed, and funded by Tempus leadership**; the 2024 **SoftBank JV** had each side contribute ¥15.0B (~$95M) with revenue-recognition questions attached. These were the core of Spruce Point's allegations.
- **Board has not visibly exonerated the related-party concern.** No public disclosure of an independent audit-committee review of the Pathos/SoftBank arrangements was found — an absence that matters given the short report.

**Verdict:** Board quality would rate Strong; control architecture rates Weak. Blended: **Adequate**, with a negative tilt because the board's independence is formally compromised by founder super-voting stock.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — MODERATE (no enforcement, but live litigation)

**No SEC enforcement actions, no DOJ/qui tam, no fines.** EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned zero hits. This is the strongest single data point in the report.

**Cleared / positive regulatory milestones:**
- **Personalis (PSNL) $1.5B acquisition:** HSR premerger form pulled-and-refiled Aug 31, 2026; **U.S. antitrust waiting period expired Oct 2, 2026 with no second request.** Antitrust risk is now largely resolved. Remaining gating items: Personalis shareholder vote, S-4 effectiveness, non-U.S. clearances. Close targeted late 2026 / early 2027.
- **EU IVDR certification** for xR RNA sequencing (Class C IVD), Oct 2026 — a meaningful de-risking for European expansion.
- **xT CDx FDA approval** secured, with reimbursement-driven pricing uplift expected from 2027.

**Active litigation (two separate class actions):**
1. **Farrier et al. v. Tempus AI** (N.D. Ill., 1:26-cv-04246, Judge Andrea R. Wood, filed/consolidated Apr 15, 2026; related 1:26-cv-01525 "Tempus AI Genetic Privacy Litigation," plus Barone et al. filed Mar 30, 2026). Alleges **Illinois Genetic Information Privacy Act (GIPA)** and other state-statute violations: that Tempus compelled Ambry Genetics (acquired Feb 2025, $600M) to hand over a genetic database covering hundreds of thousands of individuals, used it to **train its AI models**, and passed data to **70+ pharma clients** (AstraZeneca, BMS, Pfizer, GSK, Incyte, Servier, and others) without written consent. Defendants' motion to dismiss filed ~June 22, 2026 — **pending**. *This is the existential one:* GIPA carries statutory damages of $2,500 (negligent) / $15,000 (intentional or reckless) per violation. At 100k class members that is $250M–$1.5B of theoretical exposure, and the theory directly attacks the consent basis of the Data & Applications licensing model.
2. **AI-washing securities class action** (N.D. Ill., filed June 12, 2025, Judge Matthew F. Kennelly, ongoing). Alleges Tempus branded itself an AI company while **AI-attributable revenue was only $12.4M in 2024 (<2% of total)**, with growth actually driven by acquisitions, genomic testing and data licensing; challenges statements on AstraZeneca contract durability, the AstraZeneca/Pathos JV, the SoftBank Japan JV, and Ambry's growth. Filed in the wake of the Spruce Point report. Amended-complaint MTD briefing was noticed for late 2025; **no dismissal ruling confirmed** — treat as live.

**Reimbursement/policy dependency:** revenue is levered to Medicare MolDX coverage decisions and the LDT oversight regime. A single adverse coverage determination would hit the Diagnostics segment ($303.5M of Q3 revenue).

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **The data-consent model is the asset and the liability.** The competitive moat is a de-identified multimodal clinical/genomic library monetized across 70+ pharma partners. The Farrier/GIPA suit asserts that the consent chain inherited via Ambry does not support that monetization. An adverse ruling or a settlement imposing prospective consent requirements would impair, not merely fine, the Data & Applications segment (33% growth, >71% gross margin — the highest-margin, most valuation-critical line). **Watch state-law proliferation** (Illinois GIPA, Washington My Health My Data, Texas).
2. **Key-person concentration in Lefkofsky.** Founder/CEO/Chairman/controlling holder with no disclosed succession plan or independent chair. He is a serial entrepreneur (Groupon, InnerWorkings, Echo Global, MediaBank) whose public-market track record is mixed — Groupon being a cautionary governance precedent. Simultaneously the principal architect of the Pathos and SoftBank related-party structures. Removal or distraction would be a step-change event.
3. **Serial-M&A integration and revenue-quality risk.** Ambry ($600M, 2025) plus Personalis ($1.5B, closing late 2026/early 2027) plus the Pathos/SoftBank JVs create goodwill-heavy, hard-to-audit growth. Related-party-sourced contract value (~$200M Pathos TCV) and JV structures are exactly where revenue-quality disputes originate. Persistent GAAP losses ($14.9M net loss in Q3 despite positive adj. EBITDA) mean adj. EBITDA remains the metric of record — a measure that excludes SBC and acquisition costs and is therefore the softest place in the P&L.

**Additional watch items:** (a) no cybersecurity incident or breach disclosure was found — but a repository of hundreds of thousands of individuals' genomic records is a high-value target and a single breach would be catastrophic for both reputation and the consent litigation; absence of *disclosure* is not evidence of *absence of exposure*. (b) Customer concentration in AstraZeneca for flagship data deals. (c) Compute-cost inflation on AI workloads, already cited as a margin drag.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — ELEVATED

- **Short-seller overhang is unresolved.** Spruce Point Capital's May 28, 2025 "Strong Sell" report — alleging aggressive accounting, related-party revenue inflation, and AI-revenue overstatement — was publicly rebutted by management and largely downplayed by sell-side, with the stock recovering ~15% within days. But the claims were never adjudicated, the securities class action is built on them, and a Feb 2026 revisit kept the related-party question in circulation. The allegations resurface with each new JV or acquisition.
- **Patient-privacy narrative is the highest-velocity brand risk.** "AI company trained its models on your DNA without consent and sold it to 70 drugmakers" is a durable, media-friendly storyline already covered by HIPAA Journal, GenomeWeb, Fisher Phillips, and local broadcast. In a consumer-facing diagnostics business, this can damage physician referral and research-partner trust.
- **Positives partially offsetting:** consistent scientific credibility (CTOS abstracts, ARPA-H ADVOCATE award up to $9.5M), marquee partnerships (Moderna/Merck individualized neoantigen therapy, BMS), IVDR certification. Q3 beat and guidance raise has shifted analyst tone constructive. ESG controversy profile is narrow (no environmental or labor controversies) but deep on data ethics — the one dimension that matters most here.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical — there are no SEC/DOJ enforcement actions, no fines, no accounting restatement, no auditor departure, antitrust clearance is in hand, and the board's regulatory expertise is best-in-class. But four independent risk vectors converge: (i) a live class action that tests the legality of the core data-monetization model; (ii) an unresolved short-seller/related-party accounting controversy now formalized as a securities fraud suit; (iii) founder voting control secured by 30:1 super-voting stock with combined CEO/Chairman roles; and (iv) >$100M of insider selling with not a single open-market purchase across the entire executive team. The business is compounding well operationally — but the non-financial risk stack is dense and largely uncured.
