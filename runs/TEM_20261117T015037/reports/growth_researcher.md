### 1. Growth Story

Tempus has built the closest thing to a **proprietary, closed-loop data flywheel in oncology**: every clinical NGS test (xT/xR, now ~31% oncology volume growth) feeds a de-identified multimodal library (clinical + molecular + imaging) that it monetizes to pharma through multi-year, high-margin licensing deals — BMS through 2031, plus Merck, Gilead, AZ and GSK — with a newly disclosed >$1.1B AI drug-development contract in neuroscience. The disruptable thesis is that diagnostics is merely the *data acquisition cost* while the real asset is a longitudinal oncology dataset that no competitor can replicate retroactively, and gross margins expanding from 32% (2021) to ~65% (Q3'26) prove the scale leverage is real. The debate is no longer "does the model work" but "how fast can it grow organically once Ambry and now Personalis stop flattering the top line."

### 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

- **US precision oncology diagnostics TAM ~$40B** is the figure most sell-side models anchor to; broader "data-driven healthcare" framings run to **~$200B+** (company/bull-case pitch). I weight the $40B figure as the credible near-term SAM for the diagnostics franchise.
- **Pharma R&D data/AI is the underappreciated second TAM.** Real-world data + AI modeling for target discovery/clinical trial design is a tens-of-billions market currently served by Flatiron (Roche), TriNetX, Aetion, Owkin. Tempus's differentiation is that its data is *generated prospectively inside the clinical workflow* — competitors must buy or scrape it.
- **Penetration: ~4% of the $40B SAM** ($1.6B FY26 revenue) and <1% of the broader $200B framing. Even doubling to $3.1B by 2030 (consensus) implies ~8% penetration — the runway argument is intact and does not require heroic share gains.
- **Why Tempus and not a peer:** the only player with (a) ordering + EHR integration at the point of care, (b) a full transcriptome (RNA) large-panel assay, and (c) an owned data library that compounds with every test. Guardant and Natera own specific assays; Foundation Medicine owns a competing dataset but sits inside Roche and lacks the neutral, multi-partner licensing posture.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4'26 results / first FY2027 guide (~Feb 23, 2027):** the key re-rate event. Watch for FY27 revenue above the current $1.99B consensus and the first explicit GAAP-profitability timeline.
- **Personalis ($1.7B) close, late 2026/early 2027:** brings the NeXT Personal **MRD** platform — the fastest-growing, highest-value liquid biopsy category, and the one hole in Tempus's menu. Market reaction was negative (−4.5%), so a clean close plus accretion commentary is upside.
- **xT CDx tumor-only FDA approval (Q2'26):** expands eligible patient population and de-risks reimbursement; watch for Medicare/private payer coverage decisions through 2027.
- **Pharma data contract cadence:** BMS through 2031, nine-figure Merck and Gilead RWD/AI deals in Q1'26, and a newly disclosed **>$1.1B** Alzheimer's/neurology AI drug-development contract. Each new nine-figure deal is a discrete re-rating event and validates pricing power.
- **Adjusted EBITDA inflection:** guided to ~$65M for FY26; crossing to positive GAAP operating income (Q2'26 net income was briefly positive at +$5.6M) would force a reclassification from "cash-burning story stock" to "self-funding platform."

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (not Strong Buy)

The moat is genuine and rare — a prospectively generated, multimodal oncology dataset with pharma willing to pay nine figures and commit through 2031. Gross margin +32pp in four years, 100% beat rate, and +94% FY30 EPS revisions are exactly the profile I want in a frontier compounder, and at **~7–10x FY26E EV/revenue and ~6–8x FY27E**, TEM screens *below* where high-growth MRD peers trade despite comparable growth and better margin trajectory.

What keeps it from Strong Buy: **the growth rate is decelerating faster than the narrative** (36% → 23%), reported growth is increasingly M&A-manufactured (Ambry, then Personalis), and the Rule of 40 collapses from 64 to ~29 on forward numbers. Free cash flow was **negative $245M** in FY25 and hasn't inflected in three years, with $125M of SBC and rising interest expense sitting between "Adjusted EBITDA positive" and real cash generation. Beta of 3.6 and a >50% peak-to-trough drawdown mean this is a position to size at 1–2%, add on weakness, and hold through 2027.

**What would make it a Strong Buy:** two consecutive quarters of organic growth ≥25% with Data & Applications ≥35%, plus positive FCF.

### 7. Key Risks

- **Organic growth decay (highest risk).** Strip out Ambry and the organic rate looks mid-to-high-20s and falling. If FY27 guides below ~20%, the multiple compresses toward 4–5x sales.
- **Persistent cash burn / dilution.** FY25 FCF −$245M including a −$179M working-capital swing; shares 174M → 181M+ and a $1.7B stock-and-cash Personalis deal likely adds more. "Adj. EBITDA +$65M" coexisting with negative FCF is a credibility gap the market will eventually price.
- **M&A integration and multiple-creep.** Ambry (2025) plus Personalis (2026) in 18 months is a lot of lab infrastructure and culture to absorb; history of roll-ups in diagnostics is poor.
- **Reimbursement/regulatory.** Molecular diagnostic rate pressure (CLFS/PAMA), coverage decisions on tumor-only xT CDx, and possible data-privacy constraints on de-identified data licensing.
- **Pharma customer concentration and lumpiness.** Data revenue is milestone- and contract-timed; a single large deal slipping a quarter creates violent revenue variance — a real problem with a beta of 3.6.
- **Competition.** Guardant and Natera in MRD (Natera's Signatera is the entrenched leader), Foundation Medicine/Roche in profiling, and TriNetX/Flatiron/Aetion in RWD — Tempus must win on breadth, not any single assay.
- **Thin long-dated estimates.** FY29/FY30 consensus rests on only 4–6 analysts; the $3.1B 2030 revenue figure should be treated as directional, not a forecast.
