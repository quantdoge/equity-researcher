### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-Off (Inflation / Term-Premium Shock)**

The US is in a **re-acceleration regime, not a slowdown**. Real GDP grew ~3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026 (real GDP $24,611B, +2.1% YoY), unemployment has *fallen* to 4.1% from 4.5% a year ago, and yet inflation is moving the wrong way: CPI 3.82% YoY in October, with **PPI +6.5% YoY — the hottest print since November 2022** and core PPI +0.5% m/m.

The Fed cut to 3.63% through mid-2026, then **reversed**: 3.75% in September, 3.88% in October. Markets price **100% odds of a hike at the 9 December FOMC**, with 89% odds of 25bp. The 10Y has backed up from 4.44% (30 June) to **5.13%** — a +69bp move in 4½ months — driven by a **bear steepener** (30Y 5.47%, 2s10s +39bp). This is a term-premium/fiscal-supply shock layered on sticky goods inflation, *not* a growth scare. That distinction matters enormously for TEM: no recession signal, but a savage discount-rate signal.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Not because of recession — the economy is too hot for TEM's benefit. Because the Fed is tightening into 3.8% CPI with PPI at 6.5%, the long end is repricing term premium to 5%+, and TEM is the highest-beta, longest-duration expression of that trade. The stock is a leveraged short on the bond market right now.

**Mitigants that keep this from being "Severely Unfavourable":** no debt wall, $590M cash, sharply improving burn, and a business whose revenue is counter-cyclical to rate pressure (diagnostics volumes are non-discretionary once a patient has cancer). TEM will *survive* this regime comfortably; it will simply be *valued* far lower within it.

---

### 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise (Bull)

**"The 6.5% PPI is a tariff pass-through spike, not persistence."**

Consensus (and the CME curve) has fully capitulated to a hiking Fed — 100% odds of a December hike, with the 10Y at 5.13% and TEM at $64.76. But PPI is a *goods* series, and the y/y jump to 6.5% is largely tariff-driven base effects in traded goods. If Q4/Q1 core PCE decelerates while unemployment ticks up from 4.1%, the December hike becomes the **last** one, the term premium deflates, and the 10Y retraces to ~4.3% by mid-2027.

For a 20+ year duration asset with beta 3.6, **a −80bp move in the 10Y is worth roughly +50% to +70% on TEM** — before any operating leverage from the improving burn rate. That is the asymmetry: the market is priced for a persistent hiking cycle with zero cushion, and TEM's downside to the $42–45 July 2026 lows (−30%) is narrower than its upside to $85+ (+31%) on a simple rate retracement.

**Bear tail (14% priced):** a 50bp hike on 9 December, or the 10Y breaking 5.50% on fiscal-supply concerns, de-rates TEM a further 25–35% toward the low-$40s. Given the stock's 3.6 beta and demonstrated −0.9% per basis-point sensitivity in stress weeks, position sizing — not thesis — is the risk control here.
