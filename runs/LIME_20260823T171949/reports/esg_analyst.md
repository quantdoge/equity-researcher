# LIME (Neutron Holdings, Inc.) — ESG & Sustainability Report
**Scope:** Lime micromobility, NASDAQ "LIME," San Francisco, ~$2.63B market cap, 1,148 employees (FMP `company/profile-symbol`). Recent IPO 1 Jul 2026.
**Vendor rating note:** FMP's `ESG` tool is gold-tier and **plan-denied on this account**, so no MSCI/Sustainalytics vendor ESG score is cited. This report is built from primary evidence: SEC filings (via FMP `secFilings`), insider filings (`insiderTrades`), Lime's own sustainability disclosure (li.me), and third-party reporting. **No vendor ESG rating was available** — treat all directional ratings below as analyst judgment.

## (1) Environmental Assessment — **Positive-to-Strong, with greenwashing flag on "avoided emissions"**

The core product is genuinely decarbonization-oriented, and Lime's operational footprint reduction is verified — but the *environmental mandate is also the bull-case's second leg*, so the green criteria below are investment-critical, not boilerplate.

**Materiality Map (E):** Fleet lifecycle (vehicle manufacturing = **56% of 2025 emissions**) is the largest lever; batteries, aluminum, e-waste, and rebalancing logistics dominate. Carbon intensity reduction is the headline metric.

**Verified progress (self-reported, but third-party-checked):**
- Carbon intensity cut **81.4% since 2019** (Lime sustainability page; Zag Daily, July 2026).
- **SBTi-validated net-zero target by 2030:** Scope 1+2 **-90% absolute**; Scope 3 **-97% per rider-km** from 2019 baseline (li.me/why/sustainability/net-zero). Lime claims to be the first micromobility firm with an SBTi-validated target.
- **100% renewable energy** for charging, warehouses, offices (li.me/why/sustainability).
- **68% of operations vehicles electric** (vans/e-trikes); 140+ added; swappable batteries extension (li.me net-zero page).
- **Gen4 vehicles designed in-house to last 5+ years**; modular/swappable batteries rated 5–7 yrs / ~500 cycles (Lime + Levy Electric).
- **Circularity:** exclusive **Redwood Materials** battery-recycling deal (Apr 2025) for end-of-life US batteries; >97% components recyclable (li.me net-zero page).
- Two **independent ISO-compliant LCAs** — incl. Fraunhofer ISI study showing Gen4 scooters ~84% lower hardware emissions (li.me blog).

**Net climate impact / material risks**
- **Greenwashing risk (HIGHESARY for the thesis):** Lime's headline "avoided-emissions" metrics ("~40,000 tCO2 avoided," "75% less carbon than a comparable car trip," "120M car trips replaced") are **self-reported** and rely on *substitution assumptions* (that riders would otherwise have driven). Avoided emissions have **no standardized GHG-Protocol category** and are not part of the SBTi footprint. COMPUTE REQUEST 1 — recalculate avoided-emissions under a conservative substitution rate and test sensitivity.
- **Fleet lifecycle is the material cost:** vehicle production = 56% of emissions; ~40% of parts are custom aluminum (energy+carbon intensive). Mitigations (5-yr lifespans, low-carbon aluminum, recycled plastics) are real but depend on fleet durability being achieved at scale.
- **E-waste / battery:** batteries now route to Redwood (US); EU coverage and non-EU markets need confirmation.

**Environmental Risk: Medium** (operational footprint well-managed and verified; primary residual risk is accounting/credibility of avoided-emissions claims and fleet-lifecycle durability).

## (2) Social Assessment — **Moderate-to-Elevated risk**

**Material risk factors for a consumer-rental, city-regulated business: rider safety; labor classification; service equity; community license to operate.**

- **Rider safety:** Industry-wide e-scooter injury pattern is well documented — roughly **20 injuries per 100,000 rides, >90% riding without helmets** (Batta Fulkerson, 2018); Consumer Reports documented **1,500+ scooter injuries ladder-wide** (Feb 2019). Media reporting notes injuries trending up while helmet use declines ("Hell on Wheels," 2026). Lime mitigations: **~250,000 free helmets distributed**, "Lime Hero" insurance, "Lime Assist" (li.me). **Materiality:** rider-injury litigation is a known sector cost; cities have imposed speed limits, geofencing, and parking rules that directly cap revenue.
- **Labor / gig workforce ("Juicers"):** Lime settled a worker-**misclassification** suit for **$8.5M** (court-approved July 2021; RBGG; Staffing Industry). Prior CBS/NELP reporting documented classification/wage-fairness grievances. Lime has since shifted much charging to in-house operations + logistics partners, reducing gig reliance — but contract workforce remains across markets. **Contingent-liability risk is live.**
- **Equity/access:** **product is strong**: "Lime Access" reduced-fare program (up to 95%/50–70% discounts), cash payments, text-to-unlock; ~**3M rides by ~40,000 riders** (Mobilizing Justice; City of Vancouver; Better Bike Share Partnership equity scan). This is a genuine positive relative to peers and helps the city-permit case. Residual risk: vehicle availability/densification anyhow under-serves low-income neighborhoods.
- **Community relations:** dockless clutter/parking complaints and vandalism are structural; city permits and community sanctions are Lime's actual **license to operate**. Lime's in-house government-relations team is a stated competitive asset (S-1 via Mostly Metrics).

**Social Risk: High-to-Moderate** — strengths in equity pricing and safety programs; persistent exposure to rider-injury liability and labor-classification litigation.

## (3) Governance — **Above-average for a recent IPO, but with a material Uber concentration**

**Evidence**
- **Single-class stock, one vote per share, no dual-class** — notably shareholder-friendly for a 2026 IPO (Mostly Metrics S-1 breakdown). CEO **Wayne Ting owns ~1.9%** post-offering — he "serves at the board's pleasure," not founder-controlled. **Positive.**
- **CEO/Chair split:** Ting is CEO & Director; **James Rowan is an independent Chair** (appointed March 2026; on board since Sept 2025 — SEC S-1 snippet). **Positive; no CEO duality.**
- **Executive comp:** CEO ~**$1.01M**, CFO ~**$883k** (FMP `company/company-executives`); RSU-heavy post-IPO; CEO flagged Form-4 tax-withholding sales (Stock Titan). Reasonable, aligned to equity.
- **Related-party concentration — MATERIAL:** **Uber Technologies is a 10% owner** (~14.86M shares post-IPO; converted Convertible Notes + Series C Preferred into common; bought 800k shares at $25 — FMP `insiderTrades/search-insider-trades`, Form 4 July 2026). Uber is simultaneously Lime's largest investor and a rideshare partner/competitor (Uber was also the COVID emergency lender). **Oversight of the Uber relationship is a governance watch-item.**

## (4) Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU Battery Regulation (mandatory, in-scope):** Applies to batteries placed on the EU market, including e-transport batteries. Requires **supply-chain due diligence for cobalt/lithium/nickel**, carbon-footprint declaration, and the **EU Battery Passport** (phased from ~2027). Lime operates across ~29 countries incl. EU markets — **directly in scope**. Its Redwood recycling and battery-provenance work partially addresses this, but battery-passport data infrastructure is a compliance build-out.
- **EU CSRD / CSDDD (low direct exposure):** Thresholds (CSRD non-EU parent ~€450M+ **EU-member** turnover; CSDDD scale thresholds) are well above Lime's EU revenue. **Not directly applicable now** — but Lime is a value-chain partner of many in-scope companies and reports voluntarily.
- **California SB 261 (CLIMATE-related financial risk) — LIKELY in scope:** appliesto companies with **>$500M revenue** doing business in California. Lime FY2025 revenue **~$886.7M** (>$500M) and is HQ'd in SF. SB 261 therefore **applies** if enforced (California has been delaying/pausing implementation). COMPUTE REQUEST 3 — confirm Lime crosses SB 261 revenue test and monitor CA implementation status.
- **California SB 253 (carbon accounting, >$1B revenue):** Lime at $886.7M is **below** the $1B threshold — near boundary in 1–2 years. Monitor.
- **US SEC climate-disclosure rule:** final rule stayed and effectively eliminated by the SEC (2025); **not applicable.** 
- **Conflict minerals (SEC Rule 13p-1 / Form SD):** Lime is a **public filer that manufacturers vehicles with electronics in-house** → likely **in scope** for 3TG conflict-minerals diligence/Form SD. Confirm filing posture. COMPUTE REQUEST 4.
- **Operational licensing/transport policy:** micromobility regulation (permits, speed caps, parking) is the dominant de-facto "ESG"/operating regulation across cities.

## Overall ESG Rating: **Average** (leaning positive on E; offset by S/G)
Vendor rating unavailable; on analyst assessment Lime is an **above-average environmental performer** with authenticated SBTi-aligned decarbonization — arguably a sector "Leader" on E — but **moderate-to-elevated** social risk (safety, labor) and a governance profile that is structurally good (single class, independent chair) yet carries a material **Uber 10%-owner concentration** and unverified board diversity.

## Investment Implication: **Neutral (with an E tailwind and defined S/G downside)**
ESG is genuinely double-edged here. The environmental mandate is built into revenue (it IS the substitution product), so a green-credibility failure would be a brand/valuation event, while safety litigation, labor reclassification, and EU/California battery and climate-disclosure compliance are the practical cost drivers. This does **not** veto the thesis, but ESG screening value surfaces most in monitoring avoided-emissions accounting, rider-injury and misclassification case flow, Uber-related-party governance, and the California/EU compliance build-out.
