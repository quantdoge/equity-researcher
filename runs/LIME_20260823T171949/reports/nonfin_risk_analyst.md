## 1) Governance & Control

**Rating: ADEQUATE, with a disclosure-control flag** (no dual-class, standard lock-ups, but Uber sits on the board as both the largest holder and a ~14% revenue channel).

**Ownership & control structure**
- **Single class of common stock — no dual-class. Current class converts automatically at IPO.** StockTitan explicitly reports "no dual-class voting structure" (stocktitan.net URL via SEC/IPO coverage, Jun 22 2026). Preferred-held shares (~4.65B) and convertible notes convert to common at the IPO (micromobility.io S-1 analysis, Jun 4 2026).
- **Uber = largest stockholder.** Pre-IPO 24.4% → ~21.9% post-IPO (IPOScoop, Jun 30 2026). Uber reports **sole voting power** over 14,859,661 common shares (StockTitan Form 13G headline, Aug 13 2026). This exceeds the 20%+ threshold that normally triggers governance interlocks — worth monitoring.
- **Uber board seat:** Andrew Macdonald, Uber's President & COO, sits on the Lime board (micromobility.io). Also co-founder **Zhoujia Bao** holds a director seat (FMP company/company-executives).

**Lock-up mechanics & insider selling**
- **Officers/directors standard 180-day lock-up** from the prospectus date, noted in the SEC file (sec.gov/Archives/..., "#filename1.htm" — "securities... for a period of 180 days").
- **Uber has a bespoke lock-up side letter** (signed Oct 4 2023, amended Jun 21 2024): **no sales in year 1; up to 25% months 12–18; up to 50% months 18–24**; and **if the Uber Integration Agreement ends, the lock-up terminates** (micromobility.io). This creates a two-way gating risk — an integration break-up releases selling pressure.
- **No founder/CEO share selling observed** at or after IPO (see Section 2).

**Executive compensation**
- CEO (Ting) $1,005,567; CFO (Gugino) $883,367; co-founder Bao $115,815 (FMP company/company-executives). Details of equity/incentive grants not pulled — **not available** on Starter plan (executive-compensation endpoint denied).
- **Key-man succession:** the President since 2018, Joseph Kraus, **retired May 22, 2026**, days before the expected IPO pricing (micromobility.io). CEO/Chairman duality not confirmed — **not available**.

**Disclosure / internal control flag (key issue)**
- The S-1 **discloses a "material weakness" in internal controls** — the auditor flagged that certain processes for tracking/reporting financial figures were not where they should be; Lime acknowledges remediation (micromibility.io S-1 breakdown). Post-IPO accounting-wash mitigates the going-concern note, but the internal-control gap is a governance wart.
- **Auditor "going concern" paragraph:** with ~$846M of obligations maturing within 12 months vs $261M cash (senior loan $115M due Sep 2026; 2021 converts ~$536M; 2020 notes $170M), the auditor stated the company could not continue without the IPO — resolved at pricing (IPOScoop/micromobility). Business risk more than strict fraud indicator, but worth noting visibility/controls.

**Board independence:** FMP only surfaced 10 executives; the full director slate (independent director count) was **not available**. Validation flag below.

## 3. Operational Risk

**High inherent; rated Moderate given controls, but structurally exposure-heavy.**

- **Permit dependency (highest operational risk)** — revenue is a city permission business across ~230 cities / 29 countries. Revocation history: Paris 2023 referendum expelled **all** scooter operators (Lime exited); **Madrid revoked all e-scooter permits in 2024**; Prague banned/ell parking 2022, Lime lost the scooter line (micromobility.io). 116% fleet retention rate in 2025 = still net-winner, but single municipal decisions are an inherent destruction risk.
- **Fleet theft/vandalism & asset churn** — hardware-intensive: 325,137 vehicles; fully-landed cost ~$1.3k, 12-month payback (S-1). Vandalism/theft is a structural fleet-cost line; modern Gen4 IoT + swappable batteries reduce but do not eliminate. Cite: micromobility.io / S-1.
- **Tariff/supply-chain concentration** — vehicle components sourced chiefly from **China, Cambodia, Vietnam**; S-1 flags tariff escalation as a meaningful input-cost / supply risk (micromophy.io). GEOPOLITICAL exposure to cost of the entire replacement fleet.
- **Weather/seasonality** — Q1/Q Q4 fleet capex outflows and winter losses; concentrated market (UK 23% of Q1 2026 revenue — S-1 watch item) raises single-market exposure.
- **Labor model** — operations largely transitioned away from the traditional gig "juicer/charger" harvest model to company-run van/field operations (S-1; shiftTracker/Lime). Still exposed to independent-contractor misclassification litigation in the US/EU-GDP; scale modest vs. Uber/Lyft but relevant.

## 4. Reputational & Data Risk

- **Location-data privacy (RECURRING)**: Lime's privacy policy states it collects start/end locations and riding trajectory of vehicles (li.me/privacy). ACLU of Northern California has publicly criticized scooter operators' location-data practices ("Electric Scooters Are Racing to Collect Your Data"). EU GDPR exposure is real given UK/France revenue share (~32% combined) — this is the most persistent reputational/regulatory pool.
- **Sidewalk clutter/blockage backlash:** Dockless fleets historically drew sidewalk-blockage complaints; a San–Diego class action cites "blocking access on sidewalks" (Reddit/scoot litigation cited). Risk of city friction again in permit renewals.
- **Safety/injury litigation (persistent):** 2018–2019 battery-fire and brake **recall** history (CNet, Oct 31 2018; Ledger Law Mar 2019); 2020 San Francisco **mass action over scooter injuries** (aswtlawyers.com Aug 2020); many statewide/city PI suits filed. Case data shows operators won some rider-liability defenses, but claim inventory is chronic.
- **Post-IPO narrative/quiet-period** matters: first earnings (Q2) hello ("Lime Stock's Post-IPO Rally", investors.com Aug 5 2026) and Goldman Communacopia Sep 9 2026 (Lime press release, Aug 19 2026) — no brand scandals located; reputation is more "permit/footpath friction" than scandal.
