## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT** — narrow in facts, extremely wide in valuation

This is the most instructive split I've reviewed. **Eleven agents unanimously agree on four facts:**

1. **There is no fraud.** The Short Analyst — whose incentive is to find one — explicitly cleared it: negative accruals (TATA −2.4%), OCF/NI of 105%, Piotroski 8/9, net cash. Three independent agents reached the same conclusion. The forensic tail is *removed*, not merely unproven.
2. **There is no solvency risk.** $5.2B debt against $43.4B cash, no maturity wall, investment-grade at all three agencies.
3. **The near-term book is genuinely sold out** through 2027 across all three suppliers, with 60%+ cumulative contract price lifts.
4. **80.7% gross margin will mean-revert** — not one agent argues it is sustainable.

**The entire disagreement reduces to one variable: normalised EPS × the multiple it deserves.** And here the dispersion is enormous:

- **Bear cluster (three independently derived estimates converge at $390–480):** Value's DCF $425 · Short's mid-cycle $390 · Fin Risk's normalised-EBITDA math implying −64%. That three-methodology convergence is the highest-quality analytical finding in the file. It is ~45% below spot.
- **Bull case:** Growth's $1,170–1,250, which requires accepting that SCAs structurally alter a 48-year commodity cycle. Note the SCA floor covers only ~26% of revenue with price bands — the other ~74% remains spot-priced.

**How I resolve it:** both sides are right on their own timeline. The bear case is a **2028 event**; the bull case is a **2026–27 earnings event**. That is why the stock can fall 28% *while* beating and raising. The market is de-rating ahead of the earnings turn, exactly as memory equities historically do (6–9 months before ASP peak). My own probability weighting: **Bull 25% / Base 40% / Bear 35%**, giving roughly **+30% expected return** — genuinely positive, but carried on 88% volatility and a −70% tail.

**Contested but adjudicated in favour of the bears:** the "cheap at 4.5x FY27E" argument. FY27 consensus of $175.61 EPS carries a $138–$341 analyst range (±48%) and rests on +109% revenue growth. It is not an anchor; it is noise. Cheapness on peak memory EPS has historically *preceded* drawdowns rather than protected against them.

## 3. Position Sizing: **1.0% of portfolio** (range 0.75–1.25%)

**Kelly logic:** μ ≈ 30%, σ = 88% → full Kelly is absurd for a single name (μ/σ² ≈ 0.39). Applying half-Kelly with a fat left tail, a 35% probability of a −70% outcome, and a portfolio concentration penalty, the appropriate fraction is **~1%**.

**Risk-budget check:** a 1% weight at 88% vol contributes ~0.88% of portfolio volatility — roughly **7–8% of a 10–12% vol portfolio's total risk budget** for a single name. That is the maximum defensible. A 3% position would consume ~25% of risk budget in one ticker; a 5% position would be a beta bet on the AI complex, not a stock position.

**Why 1% and not 0.5% or pass:** expected value is positive, the balance sheet removes the solvency tail, near-term revenue is contractually locked, and the de-rating has already delivered 28–35%. **Why 1% and not 3%:** beta 3.31, 88% vol, 0.66 correlation to AMD, a live antitrust action, and the single largest insider-selling cluster in the peer group.

**Hard ceiling of 2.0%** only if the 23-Dec print shows gross margin ≥78% *and* the stock is still below $850. **Floor of 0%** if forward GM guidance prints below 75%.
