## Equity Research — Micron Technology (MU)
**Price $787.28 | Market cap ~$900B | EV ~$867B | Net cash $33.2B | 1.143B diluted shares**
*FY2026 ended September 3, 2026. Analysis dated ~November 12, 2026.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW** (and narrower than the current income statement implies)

Micron is one of three scaled DRAM producers globally (with Samsung and SK Hynix) and one of roughly five in NAND. That oligopoly, plus $60B+ of installed PP&E, deep process IP, and ~$31B/yr of capex, is a genuine barrier to entry. The moat has *modestly widened* over five years on two counts: HBM attaches to customer designs with long qualification cycles (capacity is reportedly sold out through 2027), and the DRAM industry has consolidated to three rational players.

But the moat fails the decisive Buffett test — **consistent ROIC above the cost of capital**:

| FY | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROIC | **-9.9%** | **2.2%** | 14.2% | 69.2% |
| Gross margin | **-9.1%** | 22.4% | 39.8% | **80.7%** |
| Net income | **-$5.8B** | $0.8B | $8.5B | $85.0B |

A business whose gross margin ranges from **-9% to +81%** in four years does not have pricing power. It has operating leverage on a commodity. Three years ago Micron lost $5.8B; today it earns $85B. That is not a moat — that is a price cycle. The customer "lock-in" for HBM buys volume visibility, not margin protection; memory has no switching costs at the device architecture level, and every incumbent is now adding capacity simultaneously — the textbook setup for the next glut.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **4 / 10**

Balance sheet and cash generation are genuinely excellent *right now*; the volatility is the disqualifier.

| Metric | FY2026 | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $133.2B (+256% YoY) | Q1→Q4: $13.6B → $23.9B → $41.5B → $54.2B |
| Gross margin | 80.7% (Q4: 86.8%) | Unprecedented; prior 8-yr peak ~58% |
| Operating margin | 74.6% | Q4: 80.7% |
| Net income / EPS | $85.0B / $74.33 | Q4 alone: $32.87 |
| FCF | $59.0B | FCF/NI = **69%** (below the 80% bar) |
| Owner earnings | $63.8B | Capex $30.7B, still ramping |
| Net cash | +$33.2B | Debt only $5.2B — fortress |
| Equity / BVPS | $138.4B / $121.07 | 61% ROE |

**Positives:** net cash at the cycle peak is precisely the right place to be — a stark contrast to 2024's $7.0B net debt. Dividend + buyback of only $2.4B (2.8% of FCF) shows restraint.
**Negatives:** FCF conversion is only 69% because capex is being expanded aggressively into peak pricing; revenue growth is 100% price, not volume; receivables have quadrupled to $36.2B (99 days DSO), a classic sign of price-driven billing inflation.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

The optical multiples scream "cheap"; the cyclical math says otherwise.

| Metric | Current | Historical cyclical context |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (trailing) | **10.6x** | Cheapest in years — because *E* is peak |
| EV/EBITDA | **8.0x** | A peak-cycle cyclical usually trades **3–5x peak EBITDA** |
| P/FCF | 15.3x | FCF yield 6.6% |
| P/B | 6.5x | On peak-cycle book; P/B at prior peaks ~2x |
| P/Sales | 6.8x | Prior peak-cycle P/S: ~2.0–2.3x |
| Fwd P/E (Dec-Q cons. $38.20) | ~4.5x | 2018 analog: 4x fwd at the top, then -40% |

**What the price implies.** At $900B market cap, the market is paying 6.8x *trailing peak* sales and 8x *peak* EBITDA. To justify that at a normal 15x multiple, Micron must earn **~$60B of net income as a permanent run-rate** — 7x its best pre-AI year ($8.7B in FY2022).

**Intrinsic value (modeled manually; the built-in DCF mis-parsed decimal WACC inputs and returned garbage).**
- *Normalized earnings power:* if DRAM ASPs normalize while bit volume keeps the larger installed base, mid-cycle revenue is ~$90–110B at 45–50% gross / ~30% net margin → **normalized EPS ~$25–30**. At 15–18x, plus $33B net cash: **$400–450/share**.
- *Cross-check, EBITDA:* mid-cycle EBITDA ~$55–60B at 8x = $425–475/share.
- *Cross-check, sales:* 4.5x normalized revenue of $100B ≈ $400/share.
- *Cross-check, replacement cost:* ~$200–300B of replacement fab value plus oligopoly franchise, ≈ $220–310/share.

**Central intrinsic value: ~$400–450/share — roughly 45% below the current price.** With a required 25–30% margin of safety, my buy price is **~$300 or below.**
- Bear ($150): AI capex digests, 2027–28 glut, EPS back to $10.
- Base ($425): margins normalize to the industry's long-run 30–40% gross.
- Bull ($1,400+): HBM structurally re-rates memory; capacity sold out for years.

Probability-weighted (~20/50/30): **~$700** — still below spot, and that bull case requires underwriting "this time is different" for a commodity, which is the single most expensive sentence in cyclical investing. The sell-side doesn't help: D.A. Davidson tripling its target to **$3,000** is a classic late-cycle sentiment marker, not analysis.

## 4. Management Quality: **7 / 10 (Good)**

Sanjay Mehrotra (co-founder of SanDisk) is a credible technologist-operator who has executed the HBM roadmap superbly and cut capital at the FY2023 trough. Balance sheet is now net cash; no empire-building M&A; the dividend is being kept nominal. Deductions: insider ownership is modest; the buyback at the FY2024 lows was trivial ($0.3B) — a missed opportunity to repurchase a generational cheap asset; and $30.7B of capex is being committed at peak pricing, which is pro-cyclical by definition. Competent, but not a management that allocates capital counter-cyclically with conviction.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** *(avoid / do not initiate; trim or exit if held)*

Micron is a well-run, financially strongest-ever version of a commodity business trading at a valuation that assumes the cycle has been abolished. Three facts drive the verdict:

1. **The earnings are peak.** 86.8% gross margin in a DRAM business cannot persist; every prior peak normalized within 12–24 months.
2. **The multiple is not cheap on the right denominator.** 8x peak EBITDA is *expensive* for a cyclical — 5x peak EBITDA would be $505/share.
3. **No margin of safety.** Even generous normalized earnings leave the stock ~2x intrinsic value.

If you already own it, the capital gain is real — harvest it. If you don't, there is no price at which the current risk/reward is a value investment; the December 23 print (consensus $38.20 EPS) may well be another blowout, and the stock can trade higher, but that is momentum, not value.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (i.e., where I could be wrong)

- **HBM structurally changes the industry.** If AI memory demand compounds at 25%+ for five years and the three-player oligopoly prices rationally, mid-cycle EPS could be $80–120 and $787 is cheap. My bear case is that "rationally" has never survived a supply rush.
- **Capacity is sold out through 2027** and Apple calls memory pricing "unprecedented" — near-term earnings have real runway.
- **Melt-up risk for shorts/underweights:** beta is 2.37; the stock moved $656→$970 in five days this month. Being early on a cyclical is indistinguishable from being wrong.
- **Earnings quality:** $36.2B of receivables and inventory of only $10.4B suggest extreme pricing rather than unit growth — when price reverses, the revenue line can halve in two quarters (I have not verified channel inventory or HBM contract duration in detail).
- **Data-limitation note:** peer-median P/E (39.8x) and the "73% discount" screen are artifacts of peak-cycle earnings and are not a valid valuation argument; I did not have access to Samsung/SK Hynix margin data to test the industry-wide margin structure directly.

---
**Bottom line:** An excellent operator at the top of an extraordinary cycle. The business is better-run and better-financed than ever; the *stock* prices a permanent state of grace. My discipline is to buy great businesses at fair prices — this is a cyclical business, near peak earnings, at a rich peak multiple. **Sell.** Fair value ~$425; act below $300.
