## Micron Technology (MU) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $787.28 (Nov 12, 2026) · Market cap ~$892B · Net cash ~$38B · EV ~$862B*

---

### 1. Growth Story

Micron has been transformed from a commodity DRAM price-taker into the scarcest strategic input in the AI build-out. FY2026 revenue hit **$133.2B (+256% YoY)** with gross margins of **80.7%** — a figure with no precedent in memory history — as HBM and server DRAM supply stayed structurally short of AI data-center demand. The genuinely disruptive element is not the pricing spike but the **business-model shift**: 26 strategic customer agreements (SCAs) running through at least 2030 now cover **>35% of revenue**, three-quarters of that under price-band frameworks, backed by **$32B of customer financial commitments** and **$12.9B of cash deposits** on the balance sheet. That converts a portion of a boom/bust spot business into a contracted, visibility-rich franchise — which is precisely the re-rating argument the market is currently refusing to pay for.

Cost of revenue is almost **flat in absolute dollars** while revenue quadrupled. That is pure pricing leverage — the clearest signature of an extreme supply-demand dislocation, and simultaneously the single most fragile line in the model.

- **Rule of 40: 300.6** (256.3% growth + 44.3% FCF margin). Even against a high-growth tech bar this is an outlier.
- **FCF FY2026: $58.96B** on $89.7B operating cash flow, against $30.7B capex (23% of revenue). Capex is heavy but well-covered.
- **5-yr revenue CAGR: 35.6%** — and note the shape: a −49% collapse in FY2023 followed by a 4x in three years. That amplitude *is* the risk.
- **Earnings beats: 7 of 7, average surprise +16.1%.** But the beat magnitude is decaying fast: +20.7% → +32.8% → +19.7% → **+5.2%** in Q4 FY26. Guidance is finally catching up to reality. That is a subtle negative for forward revisions.
- **Valuation:** P/E 10.6x trailing, **4.5x FY2027E**, **3.7x FY2028E**, EV/EBITDA 7.9x, P/FCF 15.3x. For a franchise growing triple digits with net cash and 80% gross margins, these are liquidation-style multiples — the market is unambiguously pricing a cyclical peak.

### 3. TAM and Penetration

Micron's FY2026 revenue of $133B against a historical ~20–23% DRAM share implies an industry DRAM+HBM pool now well north of $500B — a roughly 3x expansion of the pre-AI memory market in under two years. The forward TAM driver is straightforward: every AI accelerator generation carries more HBM stacks at higher die content (HBM3E → HBM4 → HBM4E), and each rack-scale system multiplies DRAM per system. Micron has stated it has **no line of sight to supply-demand normalization**, expects 2027–2028 to be *tighter* than 2026, and has sold out capacity through 2026.

Penetration logic runs the other way, though: at $278B of FY2027E revenue Micron would be capturing a share of global semiconductor spend that is historically unprecedented for a memory maker. **The penetration question is no longer "how much can they capture" but "how much of this revenue is transitory shortage rent versus durable contracted volume."** That is the entire valuation debate.

**TAM verdict: enormous and genuinely structural on the AI/HBM axis, but the current revenue base is inflated by acute scarcity pricing that no penetration model should extrapolate.**

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Dec 23, 2026 — FQ1 2027 earnings.** Consensus $38.20 EPS / $61.7B revenue. This is the immediate swing factor; a fourth consecutive deceleration in beat magnitude would confirm peak-cycle momentum.
- **HBM4 qualification and ramp** at NVIDIA's next platform and at custom-ASIC customers — share gains here would validate Micron as a co-designer rather than a spot supplier. Timeline: next two quarters.
- **SCA count progression (16 → 26 in one quarter).** Management is adding roughly 10 agreements per quarter; each incremental SCA raises the contracted revenue floor. Watch for the >35% figure to rise toward 40–50%.
- **Customer deposit build ($12.9B).** Continued growth is a hard, cash-based proof point that customers believe the shortage is durable — arguably the best single tell in the model.
- **DRAM contract price prints for CY2027.** Bernstein models **+43% in 2027 then −53% in 2028**; any 2028 contract data point that breaks that pattern is a major re-rating catalyst.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** *(staged entry, not a full position)*

The setup is a rare collision: a structurally improved business trading at a cyclical-peak multiple. At **4.5x FY2027E EPS and 7.9x EV/EBITDA with $38B net cash**, the market is discounting a violent 2028 downcycle *and then some*.

| Scenario | FY2027–28 EPS | Multiple | Implied price | Return |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** — shortage extends through 2028, SCAs scale to 50% of revenue | $210 | 10x | ~$2,100 | +167% |
| **Base** — SCAs cushion a 2028 price rollover; EPS normalizes to ~$130 | $160 | 8x | ~$1,280 | +63% |
| **Bear** — Bernstein's −53% DRAM 2028 plays out; EPS resets to $55 | $55 | 10x | ~$550 | −30% |

Probability-weighted roughly 25/45/30, that implies ~$1,170–1,250 fair value — **~50–60% upside with approximately 3:1 reward-to-risk.** Not a Strong Buy only because the base rate for memory cycles is brutal and the FY2028 consensus of $211 is, in my view, materially too high and will be revised down.

The variant perception: the market is treating the SCAs as marketing. I treat them as the single most important structural change in Micron's 48-year history — **contracted price bands through 2030 on over a third of revenue genuinely truncate the left tail of the historical cycle.** That deserves a higher multiple than 4.5x, not a lower one.

### 7. Key Risks

1. **Cyclical peak risk (the dominant risk).** Bernstein's model — DRAM +43% in 2027, then **−53% in 2028** — is the base case for most buy-siders. Low multiples on peak EPS are a classic value trap; the stock at 4.5x forward is cheap for a reason.
2. **Capex-driven oversupply.** $30.7B of FY2026 capex, plus Samsung and SK hynix expansions, all lands as 2028 supply. Industry logic is unchanged: high margins fund the capacity that kills the next cycle.
3. **Estimate risk is asymmetric and large.** FY2027E $278B revenue and $175.61 EPS rest on 30–43 analysts with a $214B–$316B revenue range. Decelerating beats (+5.2% last quarter) suggest the upward revision cycle is maturing.
4. **Customer concentration / AI capex sensitivity.** HBM demand is a derivative of hyperscaler capex, now guided to ~$725–844B in 2026. A single round of capex discipline at two or three customers transmits directly into Micron's pricing.
5. **SCA fine print.** Only 75% of SCA revenue has price bands, and the remaining quarter is renegotiated at prevailing market prices — so the floor is real but partial. Price bands also **cap upside** in the event the shortage extends.
6. **Sentiment and volatility.** Beta 2.37; a −17.5% single-day move and a −35% drawdown from peak in under five months. Position sizing matters as much as the thesis here.

**Bottom line:** own it, size it carefully, and add on confirmation from the December 23 print and the next SCA update. The growth is real and the franchise has structurally improved — but this remains a high-beta cyclical wearing a growth stock's clothes, and the 2028 supply cliff is not a hypothetical.
