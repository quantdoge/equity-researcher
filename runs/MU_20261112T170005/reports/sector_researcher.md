# SECTOR BRIEF — Semiconductors / Memory (target: Micron Technology, MU)

**Prepared:** 13 Nov 2026 | **Sector:** Technology → Semiconductors (Memory & Storage) | **Data as at last close $788.54, mkt cap $892.0B**

---

## 1. Sector Overview

Memory is a ~$200B+ revenue oligopoly (DRAM + NAND + HBM) currently in the most extreme upcycle in its history. DRAM is a three-player market — Samsung (43.9% Q3 2026 revenue share), SK hynix (27.6%) and Micron (~24%) — with China's CXMT the only material new entrant at ~10% (Counterpoint, Q2 2026). Unlike prior cycles, this upturn is **supply-rationing-led, not demand-broadening-led**: hyperscaler AI build-outs have pulled wafer capacity into HBM/high-capacity DDR5 and away from PC/mobile DRAM, producing a genuine shortage rather than a restock. DRAM contract prices rose 58–63% across 2026 (with 40–50% in Q1 alone), while IDC estimates 2026 bit supply growth of only **16% DRAM / 17% NAND** — structurally below demand. The result is peak-of-cycle profitability (MU at 80.7% gross margin, SK hynix 60.4%) and, simultaneously, demand destruction in consumer end-markets.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **AI/HBM capacity absorption with pricing power, not just volume.** HBM and high-capacity DDR5 command structurally higher ASPs and are consuming cleanroom capacity that would otherwise serve commodity DRAM — a zero-sum reallocation that supports pricing across *all* product lines. SK hynix's HBM share slipped to ~50% in Q2 2026 from 58%, i.e. Micron and Samsung are **gaining HBM share** into a sold-out market.
- **Unprecedented operating leverage on a fixed asset base.** MU's FY2026 revenue rose $95.8B YoY while D&A rose only $1.15B (+14%) — incremental gross margin on new revenue was ~97%. Memory is a fixed-cost business; in a shortage, essentially all price flows to EBIT. MU operating margin went from 26.4% to **74.6%** in one year.
- **A consolidated, disciplined supply side.** DRAM HHI ≈ 2,700–3,300 (well above the 2,500 "highly concentrated" threshold). Three players with matched capex discipline have repeatedly chosen margin over share this cycle — a markedly better industry structure than the 2018 or 2001 downturns.

---

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Peak-margin mean reversion is the defining risk.** MU's 80.7% gross margin has *no historical precedent* in memory (prior cycle peak: 45–59%). Memory has never sustained margins of this level; the question is not *if* but *when* and *how far* they normalise. Every prior cycle peak was followed by a loss-making trough (MU FY2023: −9.1% GM, −$5.8B net loss).
- **2027–28 supply cliff meets demand destruction.** Greenfield capex committed at today's prices delivers bit supply in 2027–28, and CXMT's capacity share is forecast to rise from ~11% (2025) to ~13.9% (2027). Simultaneously, price elasticity is biting: IDC flags 15–20% smartphone unit declines and a PC shipment contraction as memory inflates BOM by 15–20% on mid-range devices — the customer base is being priced out.
- **Acute customer/AI-capex concentration.** A handful of hyperscalers plus Nvidia drive the marginal demand. The November 2026 resignation of Nvidia's CEO roiled the entire AI supply chain and took MU from $1,097 (1 Oct) to $656 (5 Nov), **−40% in five weeks** — evidence that the sector's beta to AI sentiment (MU beta 2.37) overwhelms fundamental news flow.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **Share gains, not just price.** Micron holds ~24% DRAM share and is described by Counterpoint as *catching up*; it is taking HBM share while SK hynix cedes it, and its fivefold revenue growth from Q2 2025 is the fastest of the big three.
- **Cost/technology standing:** #3 in DRAM but a credible HBM4 challenger with vertically integrated US manufacturing — strategically advantaged as customers seek supply-chain diversification away from Korea and China. 61,000 employees, $9.5B D&A base = scale player.
- **Financial position:** $85.0B net income in FY26 against a $892B market cap leaves enormous capacity to self-fund capex; leverage no longer a constraint (interest expense $106M vs $108.8B EBITDA).
- **The caveat that keeps this from being "dominant":** Micron is the *price taker* in a three-player oligopoly — Samsung's 43.9% share gives it the swing capacity that will ultimately break the pricing cycle. Micron's margin leadership is a function of cycle timing and product mix, not a durable cost moat.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL** *(cyclically late-stage)*

The *structure* is the best memory has ever had — concentrated, disciplined, AI-demand-backed — and that justifies a premium to history. But the *position in the cycle* argues for restraint:

- Gross margins at 80.7% are ~2x any prior peak; the risk/reward on forward estimates is asymmetric to the downside.
- Supply additions (CXMT + committed greenfield capex) land in 2027–28 exactly as consumer demand contracts 15–20% in handsets.
- The sector is now a leveraged proxy on AI capex sentiment (single-news-event drawdowns of 40%), which caps the multiple the market will pay regardless of earnings.

**Implication for MU:** excellent company, best-in-class relative momentum and a genuine 10.5x multiple — but this is a *trade* in a late-cycle upswing, not a compounder. Position sizing should reflect 2.37 beta and the 35% distance already travelled from the $1,254 peak.
