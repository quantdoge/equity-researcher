# Micron Technology (MU) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*As of 12 November 2026 | FY2026 ended ~3 September 2026*

---

## 0. Scale Check — Why This Matters
MU is no longer a mid-cap cyclical: market cap ~$892bn, price ~$788, FY2026 revenue **$133bn** (Q4 FY26 revenue $54.2bn alone; FY26 revenue +256% and net income +895% YoY). At this size, a policy shock that would once have been immaterial is now a multiple-percent earnings event. Beta 2.37; 61,000 employees.

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

|---|---|---|---|---|

| **Taiwan** | $16.95bn | **12.7%** | **High** | Cross-strait risk; HBM/DRAM fabs (Taichung/Tongluo) *plus* ODM customers → double exposure |
| **China (mainland)** | $8.34bn | **6.3%** | **Very High** | CAC 2023 ban in force; CXMT competition; retaliatory-target risk |
| **Hong Kong** | $6.29bn | 4.7% | High | Distributor channel largely re-exporting into mainland; sanction/export-control nexus |
| **Other Asia Pacific** | $5.78bn | 4.3% | Medium | Singapore (NAND + new wafer fab), Malaysia assembly, Korea customers |

| **Other foreign** | $0.73bn | 0.5% | Low–Medium | EM/other |

**Trend:** China mainland has fallen from 12.1% (FY24) → 7.1% (FY25) → **6.3% (FY26)** — partly the CAC ban, partly demand mix. This is a de-risking positive.

**Analyst caveat (important):** Micron reports by *ship-to*, not end-demand. Taiwan's 12.7% and Hong Kong's 4.7% are largely ODM/distributor shipments whose end products are sold globally or into China. **True China-linked end-demand is materially higher than 6.3%** — likely low-to-mid teens as a share of consumption, even if the legal revenue line is small. Do not underwrite China risk off the 6.3% headline.

**Manufacturing footprint (asset-level geopolitical exposure, arguably more important than revenue):**
- **US:** Boise ID, Manassas VA, Clay NY (up to 4 fabs; ~$100bn; largest private investment in NY state history; first concrete poured 2026)
- **Japan:** Hiroshima — **$9.3bn / ¥1.5tn expansion** (1-gamma, 1-delta, HBM4E), supported by up to ¥536bn Japanese government funding
- **Taiwan:** leading-edge DRAM/HBM — the single most concentrated strategic chokepoint
- **Singapore:** NAND + new advanced wafer fab
- **India:** Sanand, Gujarat ATMP ($2.75bn) — India's first memory assembly/test facility, opened 28 Feb 2026, commercial output ramping to hundreds of millions of chips in 2027
- **Malaysia / China:** SSD assembly, legacy test

FY2027 capex guided **above $50bn** (raised from >$40bn) — i.e., the company is placing an enormous, largely irreversible bet on jurisdictions with elevated political risk.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**#1 — Cross-strait disruption / Taiwan concentration**
*Probability (5-yr, disruptive event):* Low-to-medium (~10–15% for blockade/significant disruption; higher for harassment/quarantine incidents). *Impact:* **Severe** (potentially >30–40% of DRAM output and a double-digit share of revenue at risk simultaneously).
Taiwan is simultaneously a top-2 revenue geography (12.7%) *and* the location of leading-edge HBM/DRAM capacity — an unusually bad correlation. Beijing continues to threaten trade sanctions over Taiwan; the US House passed the **PROTECT Taiwan Act 395–2**, raising the sanctions-escalation ladder. Micron's mitigation (Japan, Singapore, US, India) is real but 3–5 year lead-time; it does not hedge the near term.

**#2 — US–China export-control escalation cutting both ways**
*Probability:* **High (>60%)** of further tightening within 12 months. *Impact:* Medium (indirect).
Congress is actively negotiating China chip limits into the must-pass defense bill: the **MATCH Act (H.R. 8170 / S. 4281)** would force allied toolmakers to align with US controls on semiconductor manufacturing equipment. Speaker Johnson said on 12 Nov 2026 there is "growing consensus" on limiting chip exports to China; the House passed its $1.15tn NDAA version on 22 July 2026. **Micron itself is lobbying for MATCH** (Reuters, 22 Apr 2026) — it wants Chinese rivals (CXMT) hobbled at the tool level. That is a rational but two-sided trade: tighter controls raise Micron's own equipment/service costs and invite Chinese retaliation. BIS already updated controls on advanced wafer-fab tools/services to certain China customers in April 2026; the "Affiliates Rule" suspension was extended only to **January 2027** — a hard cliff to watch.

**#3 — Chinese state-backed memory supply (CXMT) as an instrument of policy**
*Probability:* **High** (already occurring). *Impact:* Medium–High on pricing/mix.
CXMT reached **10% of global DRAM revenue in Q2 2026** (Counterpoint), up ~150% YoY, versus Samsung 38% / SK hynix 25% / Micron 24%. CXMT's new server-memory architecture triggered an ~11% five-day drop in MU on 15 Oct 2026. This is not a normal competitor: it is a subsidised national champion used to compress Western memory margins. Micron's 26 strategic contracts covering >35% of revenue through 2030 and 3–5 year LTAs with price collars provide partial insulation, but commodity DRAM remains exposed.

**Also live — but secondary:**
- **Tariffs (Section 232):** 25% tariff on certain semiconductor imports and derivatives effective **15 January 2026**; **15%** on polysilicon and minimum import prices from 6 August 2026. USTR Greer indicated (May 2026) no *imminent* new chip tariffs, but the mechanism is live and scope can widen. Micron is a net beneficiary over time (US fabs) but a near-term payer on imported output.
- **AI-chain concentration:** data-centre revenue concentration rose to **~60% of revenue** (up from ~50%), per the FY2026 10-K. The November 2026 resignation of Nvidia's CEO amid a China espionage scandal is a live reminder that MU's demand curve is politically entangled.
- **China's countermeasures:** Beijing extended its suspension of US trade countermeasures by two months (Nov 2026) and extended its rare-earth export-control suspension on 9 Nov 2026; the US extended tariff exclusions for 178 items. A **fragile, transactional détente** — the tail risk is snap-back.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation**
- **Netlist:** **$600m global settlement reached 6 October 2026**, ending multi-year, multi-front patent litigation (after a $445m jury verdict against Micron in May 2024 and mixed PTAB/Federal Circuit outcomes, incl. CAFC No. 24-1707, 2 Sept 2026). *Resolved — removes a recurring overhang.*
- **Mimir IP:** South Korean NPE (patents acquired from SK hynix) seeking **up to $480m**. *Open.*
- **Katana:** state-law bad-faith patent claim in W.D. Tex. (expired patents). *Open, small.*
- 10-K Item 1A flags "legal, regulatory, and administrative investigations, inquiries, proceedings, and claims" and IP infringement/licensing as standing risks. **No DOJ, SEC, FTC or EU antitrust action against Micron surfaced in this review** — a clean finding relative to peers.

**Regulatory / policy**
- **FCPA/AML/sanctions:** No disclosed FCPA or sanctions enforcement. Risk is structural, not incident-driven — a global distributor network, a Hong Kong channel equal to 4.7% of revenue, and Chinese customers create entity-list/sanctions screening burden. This is the most under-appreciated compliance exposure.
- **China CAC ban (May 2023):** Micron products barred from Chinese critical-infrastructure operators after a failed cybersecurity review; still in force as of Nov 2026. Micron remains a named retaliatory target.
- **CHIPS Act incentives:** up to **$6.4bn** direct funding (2 Idaho + 2 New York fabs) within a ~$200bn US investment programme. 10-K carries an explicit risk factor that incentives are "conditioned upon achieving or maintaining certain outcomes… subject to reduction, termination, or clawback." Note the political shift toward equity/performance-linked structures in Washington — expect more strings, not fewer.
- **Data privacy / AI:** new FY2026 risk factor on **labour disputes/union activity** at concentrated fabs (first time disclosed); plus reliance on third-party AI providers. Neither is a compliance red flag, but both are new.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **HIGH** (lower end of High)

| Component | Rating |
|---|---|
| Geographic / political risk | **High** — Taiwan concentration (revenue + fabs), China retaliation history |
| Trade & export-control risk | **High** — MATCH Act, Section 232 tariffs, BIS churn, Jan 2027 cliff |
| Legal / litigation risk | **Medium** — Netlist closed; Mimir and Katana modest |
| Regulatory / compliance risk | **Medium** — no active enforcement; structural sanctions-screening burden |
| Sanctions (Russia/OFAC) exposure | **Low** |

**Why High and not Medium:** the risk is *correlation*, not magnitude of any single item. Taiwan supplies both a top-2 revenue line and leading-edge capacity; US and Chinese policy are simultaneously pointed at Micron's end market (AI accelerators) and its competitive position (CXMT). A single cross-strait event or MATCH-plus-retaliation sequence hits revenue, cost, and supply at once.

**Why not Very High:** 68.6% US revenue, China mainland down to 6.3%, no active government investigation, the largest IP overhang ($600m Netlist) is settled, and the manufacturing footprint is genuinely diversifying (Japan, Singapore, India, upstate New York). Micron is also a *policy beneficiary* — CHIPS funding, Japanese subsidies, and its own MATCH Act advocacy mean Washington's industrial policy is currently a tailwind, not a headwind.

**What would change the rating:** (i) BIS Affiliates Rule lapse/expansion in Jan 2027 triggering Chinese retaliation against Micron; (ii) MATCH Act enactment with aggressive extraterritorial scope; (iii) Section 232 tariff scope extended to memory modules/derivatives; (iv) any indication of an export-control diversion investigation touching the Hong Kong channel. Any of (i)–(iv) → **Very High**.
