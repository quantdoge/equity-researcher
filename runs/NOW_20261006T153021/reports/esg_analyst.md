## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **Low**

- **Carbon intensity:** Scope 1+2 ≈ **75.7K tCO2e (2024)** — *above* peer intensity (5.7 vs 4.0 tCO2e/$M on Scopes 1–2; direct energy intensity 17.3 vs 4.2 MWh/$M, ~4× benchmark, likely hosting/offices). But on **total footprint incl. Scope 3**, NOW is *better* than benchmark (27.2 vs 41.8 tCO2e/$M). So: efficient value chain, heavyset direct footprint for a software firm.
- **Transition risk:** Low. Net-zero target **by 2030**, near-term **SBTi 1.5°C-aligned** targets, published **Climate Transition Plan**, 94% renewable energy (rank **3/436** in sector per illuminem/GIST). CBAM: immaterial (no goods manufacturing). Carbon pricing pass-through negligible.
- **Physical risk:** Minimal — office/cloud asset base; no water stress, no coastal assets, no supply-chain commodity exposure.
- **Controversies:** None environmental.

**E verdict: Low, with one watch-item** — absolute Scope 1–2 and direct energy intensity are peer-lagging for a company marketing itself as climate-credible ("runs on 100% renewables" claims need to stay true as hosted AI compute scales).

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **Medium**

- **Workforce:** Reported cuts of **up to ~1,000 roles (~3% of headcount)** as part of a global "rightsizing," timed with an AI-efficiency narrative and a ~$7B M&A push. Standard enterprise-SaaS AI transition, but the optics are amplified by a −26% to −38% stock decline — cost discipline is landing on employees while the CEO's pay package has repeatedly drawn shareholder suits. This is the single most material social factor; it is a **Medium**, not Critical — no strikes, no safety catastrophe, no supply-chain labour scandal.
- **D&I:** Female workforce **38%** (peer-"aligned" per aggregator data; sector median ~30–40%). Public representation data on US racial/ethnic composition **not retrievable in this run** — flagged as a disclosure gap; NOW voluntarily publishes an annual ESG report with EEO-style data.
- **Product safety / privacy:** No product-safety or data-breach incidents surfaced. Positive: NOW sells GRC, ESG-management and "safe workplace" modules — its social licence depends on *staying clean* here, and its AI Control Tower productises exactly this governance risk for customers.
- **Community:** Low exposure.

**S verdict: Medium** — restructuring and responsible-AI deployment are the live risks; no crisis indicators.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Strengths:** single-class common stock (no dual-class/poison-pill); standard Silicon Valley independent-majority board; no founder entrenchment; disciplined R&D (22% intensity) and capital allocation; ESG disclosure apparatus in place (SBTi, TCFD-style reporting, GRC product suite); **Weaknesses:** (1) **2023 SEC $5M settlement** for inadequate disclosure of stock-based compensation accounting — a formal accounting-integrity ding, rare for a mega-cap software firm; (2) **periodic class actions on CEO compensation** (McDermott; plaintiff claims include SAP-era crossover allegations) — comp is heavily stock-based and repeatedly litigated; (3) **M&A risk:** a ~$7B acquisition announced into a −38% market-cap year tests governance discipline.

**G verdict: 7/10** — above-average for the sector on structure and disclosure quality, marked down on the SEC settlement and recurring comp controversy. Not a clean bill of health, but no governance crisis.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD:** NOW's EU operations and its role as *supplier* to CSRD-reporting customers generate disclosure obligations and customer data requests — compliance cost plus **bid-eligibility channel** (its own ESG Management software monetises this for clients: regulation is a mild demand tailwind).
- **EU CSDDD:** supply-chain due-diligence duties (scope softened by 2025 omnibus) — moderate.
- **EU AI Act:** **the key regulatory vector.** NOW embeds GenAI into HR, recruitment, healthcare, and government workflows → obligations when customers deploy in high-risk use cases; general-purpose-AI code-of-practice duties; compliance pass-through to customers. Cost and liability exposure moderate but rising.
- **GDPR / CCPA:** appliable to its HR, CSM, and data modules — no incidents retrieved.
- **US SEC climate rule:** vacated/held in abeyance — effectively off in the US as of 2025; California SB 253/261 remains a live residual. US regulatory burden Low.
- **CBAM / ESG-linked debt covenants:** immaterial (no goods trade; low debt).

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **Average**

Rationale: **Leader** is precluded by the SEC accounting settlement, recurring CEO-comp litigation, an above-benchmark direct carbon footprint, and opaque (to this analysis) US diversity disclosures. **Laggard/ESG-Risk** is clearly wrong: 94% renewables, SBTi-aligned net-zero-2030 plan, strong GRC/ethics product positioning, low controversy baseline, no dual-class entrenchment. So: **Average, with a positive E-execution tail.**

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **Neutral**

ESG is **not the driver** of NOW's 2025–26 repricing — that is the AI-era re-rating of enterprise SaaS growth (revenue still +17%; the December 2025 single-day −11.6% / 148M-share event came on guidance/AI-concern news, not an ESG event). ESG factors neither block nor catalyse the equity thesis, but two are **valuation-sensitive watch-items**:

1. **Negative tilt (monitor):** governance optics (SEC comp settlement + CEO pay litigation) are a recurring discount factor precisely while shareholders are underwater; workforce morale through the ~1,000-job rightsizing is the attrition risk as NOW fights for AI talent.
2. **Positive tilt (mild):** EU CSRD/AI-Act-driven demand for climate-governance and responsible-AI tooling is a **tailwind for NOW's product suite** — its GRC/ESG/AI-Governance modules sell into the compliance budgets that this regulation creates.

Net: **Neutral** for the long-term thesis, conditional on clean execution of the restructuring and the Armis integration.
