## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **None to Narrow (unproven)**

QXO is no longer the company its stale profile describes. In 18 months Brad Jacobs converted a shell into the second-largest publicly traded building-products distributor in North America:

| Deal | Closed | Consideration |
|---|---|---|
| Beacon Roofing (roofing/waterproofing) | Apr 29, 2025 | ~$11bn |
| Kodiak Building Partners (lumber/LBM) | Apr 1, 2026 | undisclosed |
| TopBuild (insulation distribution & install) | Jul 1, 2026 | **$17bn** |

Pro forma scale: ~$16–17bn revenue, ~$2.0bn adjusted EBITDA, 194,000 customers, ~28,000 employees.

**What is genuinely good here:** local route density and purchasing scale are real cost advantages in distribution; QXO is #1 in insulation, #1 in waterproofing, #2 in roofing. The private label (TRI-BUILT at a record 10.6% of roofing sales) and mechanical-insulation exposure to data centers (+16% revenue) are the two places where pricing power could actually be built. TopBuild is a genuinely superior asset (~18% EBITDA margins vs Beacon's ~9–10%).

**Why the moat is not yet investable:**
- **The underlying business has no pricing power.** Companywide pricing was +2.2% in a soft market; gross margin is 26–28%; customers multi-source, switching costs are low, and Home Depot/ABC Supply compete aggressively. This is a working-capital-intensive, cyclical, commoditized industry.
- **The moat test fails at the consolidated level.** Owner earnings ≈ $2.0bn adj. EBITDA − ~$300m maintenance capex/capex-equivalent − ~$640m cash interest − ~$150m cash taxes ≈ **$0.9bn**. Against ~$30bn of invested capital (total assets less current liabilities and cash), that is a **ROIC of roughly 4%**, versus a WACC of ~8–9%. The operating subsidiaries earn attractive returns *on tangible capital*; the acquisition premiums (~$25.3bn of goodwill and intangibles, 73% of total assets) transfer those returns to the sellers. Reported ROIC is negative (−1.8% in 2025).
- **Moat direction: narrowing in the base business.** Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $557m included ~$270m (est.) from TopBuild and ~$55–60m from Kodiak, implying legacy Beacon earned roughly **$230–280m versus $301m in Q3 2025** — i.e., the core roofing franchise is flat to down ~10–25% year over year on soft construction volumes. That is an estimate from segment arithmetic, not a disclosed figure, but the direction is unambiguous.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **2 / 10**

| Metric | FY2025 | Q3 2026 / Latest |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $6.84bn (partial yr) | $4.90bn in the quarter |
| Gross margin | 23.0% | 28.2% |
| Adjusted EBITDA | ~$647m | $557m (11.4% margin) |
| GAAP net income | **−$279m** | **−$123m** |
| Free cash flow | +$183m | **9M 2026: ≈ −$260m** |
| Total debt / net debt | $3.9bn / $1.55bn | **$10.3bn / $9.75bn** |
| Goodwill + intangibles | $8.9bn | **$25.3bn (73% of assets)** |
| Tangible book value | — | **≈ −$6/share** |

- **Cash conversion is poor and worsening.** Nine-month 2026 operating cash flow was **−$145m** after a $285m working-capital build in Q3 alone; FCF was about −$260m. Adjusted EBITDA is not converting to cash.
- **"Adjusted" is doing heavy lifting.** Q3 adjustments exclude ~$40m transaction costs, ~$28m transformation costs, $79m of stock comp, and $371m of quarterly D&A (much of it purchase-accounting amortization that represents real cost of the acquired customer relationships). Adjusted EPS of $0.13/quarter (~$0.52 annualized) is a generous framing.
- **Leverage:** ~9.9x LTM adjusted EBITDA; ~4.8–5.0x pro forma for a full year of all three businesses. S&P expects sub-5x by 2027 — that is a thesis dependent on synergy delivery, not a cushion.
- **Dilution:** diluted shares went from 613m (FY2025 average) to **1,081m** in Q3 2026 — +76% in nine months. Equity was the currency for TopBuild.
- **Dividend + buyback while FCF-negative:** ~$264m/yr dividend (~2.2% yield) and a new $500m repurchase authorization, funded from the balance sheet at ~5x leverage. Sentiment-positive, balance-sheet-negative.

Predictability, earnings quality, and balance-sheet strength all score poorly. This is the opposite of a Buffett "certainty" business today.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**What the market is paying:** EV of $22.8bn = **11.4x** pro forma adjusted EBITDA of ~$2.0bn, **~23x** annualized adjusted EPS, and ~25x owner earnings (including stock comp). Book value of $17.08/share is 70% of price — but 73% of that book is goodwill, so P/B is not a valuation anchor.

**Scenario-based intrinsic value (2030 horizon, discounted at 9%):**

| Scenario | 2030 adj. EBITDA | Exit multiple | Equity value | PV per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull (25%)** — most synergies realized, private label + tech lift margins | $3.6bn | 9.5x | ~$26.7bn | **$16.4** |
| **Base (45%)** — solid integration, margins ~17–18%, no cyclical help | $2.7bn | 8.5x | ~$14.5bn | **$8.9** |
| **Bear (30%)** — construction downturn, synergy slippage, multiple compression | $2.1bn | 7.0x | ~$4.7bn | **$2.9** |

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $9/share** (range $6–$14). A simpler cross-check: 9x pro forma EBITDA (a normal distributor multiple) less net debt gives ~$7.6/share; even at today's 11.4x the equity is worth ~$11.2/share — essentially the current price.

**Conclusion: the stock trades at or modestly above intrinsic value. There is no margin of safety; the market is already capitalizing the full execution of the plan.**

**On the $4bn-by-2030 target:** management says it will reach $4bn of adjusted EBITDA "from the businesses we already own," off ~$2bn today. On ~$17bn of existing revenue that implies a **~24% EBITDA margin** — a level no building-products distributor has ever earned (Fastenal and Pool are different business models). I do not underwrite it. Note the sell side has already capitulated: Truist cut $26→$15, RBC $27→$18, Wells Fargo $18→$16→$15 between August and October 2026, even while retaining Buy ratings.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Mixed: excellent operator, undisciplined price-payer**

**Positives:**
- Jacobs has built United Rentals, XPO, GXO and RXO; the operating playbook (scale purchasing, densify routes, add technology, decentralize P&L) is real and has worked before.
- Large insider/founder alignment; he is a meaningful owner, not a hired hand. The board authorized a $500m buyback and initiated a dividend — signals of confidence.
- Disclosure is candid about organic trends and transaction costs.

**Serious concerns:**
1. **Price paid.** $17bn for TopBuild is ~18x EBITDA — while QXO's own equity trades at ~11x. Issuing stock at 11x to buy assets at 18x is value-destructive arbitrage unless synergies are extraordinary. The same pattern applies to Beacon at ~$11bn.
2. **Empire-building cadence.** Three multi-billion deals in 18 months, $10.3bn of new debt, 76% dilution. Integration of any *one* of these would be a full-time job.
3. **Attention and conflicts.** In November 2026 Jacobs announced a **second** acquisition platform (Jacobs Private Equity II investing $1bn into CKX Lands) and will be chairman/CEO of both. Two public companies, one CEO, undisclosed target industry, potential deal-flow conflicts. For QXO holders this is an unambiguous negative.
4. **Capital allocation sequencing.** Paying a dividend and authorizing buybacks while free cash flow is negative and leverage is ~5x is not disciplined allocation; it is optics management.

Verdict on management: I would trust Jacobs to improve the operations. I would not trust him to stop buying things at full prices — and in a roll-up, the price paid *is* the return.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID** (not a value investment at this price)

This is a well-run-by-intent, poorly-priced-by-evidence serial acquirer. The operating businesses are decent; the consolidated entity earns ~4% ROIC on ~$30bn of invested capital, carries ~5x leverage, is burning cash, and is priced at 11.4x pro forma EBITDA for a 2030 plan that requires a margin structure the industry has never achieved. My intrinsic value estimate of ~$9/share (range $6–$14) sits *below* the $11.97 price.

Applying the discipline that a fair price is not a buy: I would need ~$6.50–$7.00 (a 25–30% discount to my ~$9 central estimate, or ~7x pro forma EBITDA) before the asymmetry compensated for the leverage and integration risk. **If you already own it, the honest framing is that you are making a bet on Brad Jacobs personally, not on a moated business — size it accordingly.**

*What would change my mind:* two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow after interest and dividends; net debt/EBITDA below 3.5x; evidence that legacy Beacon EBITDA is growing again; and a halt to new multi-billion acquisitions. Until then, this is a levered equity option on execution, not a value stock.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to owning it (downside):**
1. **Leverage and refinancing.** $10.3bn debt, ~$640m annualized interest, negative FCF. A construction downturn or credit-market closure hits equity with brutal leverage — a 1x turn of EBITDA multiple compression plus flat EBITDA is a ~40% equity loss.
2. **Integration failure.** Three simultaneous integrations; the technology/AI promise is unquantified. Every dollar of unrealized synergy is worth ~$8bn of EV at 8x.
3. **Goodwill impairment.** $25.3bn of goodwill/intangibles against $18.6bn of equity. A 25% write-down erases a third of book and could trigger covenant and sentiment stress.
4. **Cyclicality.** Roofing and LBM volumes are tied to residential repair/remodel and housing turnover, both soft. Only data-center-driven mechanical insulation is growing strongly — and that is one segment.
5. **Estimate resets.** The 2026 price-target cuts from $26–27 to $15–18 show how quickly the narrative can deflate. A miss on 2027 synergy targets likely compresses the multiple toward 7–8x.
6. **Governance/dilution.** A distracted CEO and a demonstrated willingness to issue equity at 11x to buy at 18x.

**Risk to my cautious view (what I could be wrong about):**
- Jacobs has done exactly this before and created enormous value at United Rentals and XPO. If QXO lifts EBITDA margins from ~11% to 16–17% on $20bn+ of revenue, 2030 EBITDA of $3.5bn+ is achievable, and the stock is worth $18–25. My probability weight on that outcome is 25%, not zero.
- At 0.70x reported book with a $500m buyback authorized, a "sum-of-parts at 9x" re-rating is plausible if cash flow turns — which would make today's price roughly fair rather than expensive.

**Data gaps disclosed:** live peer multiples unavailable; Q3 2026 segment-level EBITDA (Beacon/Kodiak/TopBuild) is not separately disclosed, so the legacy-Beacon decline is my arithmetic inference; total debt may exclude preferred or minority interests I could not verify.
