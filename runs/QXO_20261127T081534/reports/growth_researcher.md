# QXO, Inc. (NYSE: QXO) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Price $11.97 | Market cap ≈$12.4B | 52-wk range ≈$10.48–$27.60 | Consensus PT ≈$17–18**

*Data note: QXO is a serial acquirer whose fiscal 2025–26 results are non-comparable year-over-year (Beacon closed 4/29/25, Kodiak 4/1/26, TopBuild 7/1/26). Growth rates below are stated on organic, pro-forma-adjusted, and reported bases separately — conflating them is the single biggest analytical trap in this name.*

---

## 1. Growth Story

QXO is Brad Jacobs' third industrial roll-up (after United Rentals and XPO), executing a "buy the fragmented $800B North American building-products channel, then tech-enable it" thesis. In 15 months it has assembled ~$20B of run-rate revenue — Beacon ($10.6B, roofing), Kodiak ($2.25B, lumber/LBM), and TopBuild ($17B, insulation distribution **and installation**) — converting itself from a pure low-margin distributor into a distribution-plus-services platform. The underappreciated pillar is TopBuild: it carries structurally higher margins (~20% EBITDA vs. Beacon's ~11%), which is why blended gross margin jumped from 23.0% (FY25) to **28.2% (Q3'26)** — that mix shift, not the deal math, is the durable value driver. This is consolidation-as-innovation (shared purchasing scale, private-label penetration, AI-driven share-of-wallet selling), not frontier technology — the moat is scale economics and data across 194,000 customers, not a patent.

## 3. TAM and Penetration

- **TAM: ~$800B** (company-defined North American building-products distribution/installation). QXO at ~$20B run-rate = **~2.5% penetration**; the $50B-by-2030s target = ~6%.
- The market is thousands of sub-scale regional distributors — the consolidation runway is genuinely decades-long, and QXO is now the #1 insulation distributor/installer, #2 roofing distributor, #2 public LBM distributor, #1 waterproofing.
- **Differentiation:** purchasing scale (TopBuild/Beacon combined buy), private label (TRI-BUILT at a record 10.6% of Roofing sales), and the ability to sell *product + installation* on one ticket — the "fewer suppliers" pitch. Whitespace is quantified: QXO typically supplies only a *portion* of what each of its 194,000 customers needs per project.
- **Structural tailwind:** mechanical insulation distribution +16%, explicitly tied to data-center demand — the one genuinely high-growth, secular end-market in the portfolio.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Q4/FY26 results — Feb 24, 2027.** First full-year guide with TopBuild consolidated. The number to watch: FY27 Adj. EBITDA (a >$2.2B guide validates; <$2.0B breaks the model). *Near-term, highest-impact.*
2. **TopBuild + Kodiak synergy disclosure (2027).** Jacobs' playbooks historically deliver 200–300bps of margin expansion; first hard synergy quantification would re-rate the multiple.
3. **$500M buyback execution (announced Nov 2026).** First capital-return signal in company history — modest (~4% of cap) but management-confidence-revealing.
4. **Next acquisition announcement.** $2B+ liquidity and a stated $50B revenue ambition mean another deal is likely within 6–12 months; each deal is a binary re-rating event (scale accretion vs. dilution/leverage fear).
5. **Deleveraging / refinancing (2027–28).** Movement toward target leverage and any rating upgrade would compress the cost of capital that currently eats adjusted EPS.

## 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Google Trends ("QXO"):** structurally higher — weekly interest averaged ~25–35 in late 2025 vs. **~45–60 through 2026**, with a spike to 100 the week of Apr 19, 2026 (TopBuild announcement). Awareness is compounding, but the series is event-driven (deal headlines) rather than demand-driven — weak signal on fundamentals.
- **Job postings (Indeed proxy):** ~25–28 open QXO reqs per metro (Dallas 28, Garland 25, Brandon FL 25), skewed to drivers, service technicians, and installers. Consistent with capacity build-out for volume — **not** a differentiating growth signal.
- **Stronger proprietary alt-data (company-disclosed):** pricing **+2.2%**, complementary product sales **+4.2%**, TRI-BUILT private label at record **10.6%** of sales, mechanical insulation **+16%**, and organic daily revenue improving two consecutive quarters *into a soft construction market*. Private-label penetration and complement attach are the two cleanest read-throughs on whether the "share of wallet" thesis is actually working — both are moving the right way.
- **Contrarian: analyst PT direction is negative.** Truist $26→$15, RBC $27→$18, Wells Fargo $18→$16→$15, D.A. Davidson $26→$20 — aggressive cuts through H2'26 as leverage and organic softness dominated. Only Raymond James ($21, Nov 16) and Melius ($17 init.) sit above the pack. Estimates were cut, not raised; do not mistake the tool's "+36% revision upgrade" for a signal (it is comparing FY30 to FY29, an artifact).

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY (speculative / position-sized)**

The de-rating from $27.60 to ~$12 has converted an expensive story stock into an asymmetric, but genuinely levered, execution bet. Supporting the Buy:

- **Multiple compression with intact earnings power.** Consensus of $0.82 in FY28 and $1.77 in FY30 prices QXO at 14.6x and 6.8x — against peers at ~25x P/E (IBP 24.4x, WSO 25.3x, SITE 34.5x, BLDR 15.7x). On EV/EBITDA, QXO screens ~16x FY26E adj. EBITDA but only ~9–10x FY27E vs. a peer median of ~14x (BLDR 8.9x, IBP 13.4x, WSO 14.7x, SITE 15.9x).
- **The 2030 math is real if management is directionally right.** $4B Adj. EBITDA from *already-owned* businesses at a peer-median 12x = ~$48B EV, ~$40B equity vs. $12.4B today — a ~26% IRR, before any further acquisitions.
- **Margin trajectory is the proof point.** 23.0% → 28.2% gross margin in four quarters is exactly what the thesis requires; Q3'26 Adj. EBITDA +85% is compounding faster than revenue.
- **Valuation floor support:** ~1.2–1.3x book (vs. BLDR 1.57x), $500M buyback live, and a PT average ~45% above spot.

I stop short of Strong Buy: free cash flow is only ~2.7% of sales, adjusted-to-GAAP gap is wide, organic growth is still negative, and the entire thesis rests on one 61-year-old operator's execution bandwidth — which he just divided by launching a *second* $1B acquisition platform. This is a buy-the-operator, not buy-the-business, decision.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Leverage and interest burden (highest severity).** FY25 interest expense was $173.5M on only partial-year debt; fully annualized post-TopBuild it plausibly exceeds $700M — against ~$1.3B of EBITDA and only $183M of FY25 FCF. Rising rates or a covenant/refinancing stumble is the fastest path to permanent capital loss.
2. **Integration execution — three mega-deals in 15 months** ($10.6B Beacon, $2.25B Kodiak, $17B TopBuild). Prior Jacobs platforms integrated more slowly; here, systems, sales forces, and ERP consolidation across 28,000+ employees are simultaneous. Synergy slippage is the base-case bear risk, not a tail.
3. **Organic growth is not there yet.** Management describes *improving trends*, not growth, in a soft construction market. If US housing/repair-remodel stays weak through 2027, Adj. EBITDA growth becomes purely acquisition-driven and the multiple will not re-rate.
4. **Dilution / capital-markets dependence.** Share count has roughly doubled in a year; a $50B revenue ambition cannot be funded internally. Each equity-funded deal dilutes per-share economics and caps upside.
5. **Key-man and governance concentration.** Brad Jacobs' new parallel $1B vehicle creates real conflict-of-attention risk and likely contributed to the H2'26 de-rating.
6. **Accounting quality.** Adjusted EBITDA excludes $144.5M of SBC and heavy deal/integration costs; GAAP losses persist. Adjusted metrics should be haircut when modeling.
7. **Cyclicality and customer concentration in new segments** — TopBuild's installer network and Kodiak's lumber exposure both carry housing-cycle beta above Beacon's re-roofing base (which is comparatively non-discretionary).

**What would change the call:** FY27 Adj. EBITDA guidance below ~$2.0B on Feb 24, 2027 → downgrade to Hold. Evidence of *positive* organic volume plus a leverage ratio below ~3x → upgrade toward the higher end of the PT range.
