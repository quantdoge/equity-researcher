## ⚠️ Critical Context: QXO Is No Longer the Company in Its Own Historical Statements

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **Sub-Investment Grade (BB tier)**

S&P assigns **BB+ issue-level rating with a '1' recovery rating** on QXO Building Products' secured term loans (affirmed on TopBuild acquisition financing). This implies an issuer credit rating in the **BB / BB- crossover** range — solidly sub-investment grade, **two to three notches below investment grade**, with meaningful headroom to downgrade.

The company **cannot yet self-fund**: GAAP net loss of **-$495M TTM**, GAAP EBIT of **-$327M TTM**, and **negative trailing FCF of -$98M**. Solvency rests entirely on adjusted EBITDA (a non-GAAP construct excluding SBC, transaction, transformation and restructuring costs) and continued capital-market access.

## 4. Downside Scenario: 20% Revenue Decline

Applied to pro-forma revenue of **$19.6B** (Q3'26 annualized), assuming a 28.2% gross margin and **~20% decremental contribution margin** (distributors carry substantial fixed SG&A/warehouse costs):

| Line Item | Base | -20% Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $19.6B | $15.7B |
| Adjusted EBITDA | $2.23B | **~$1.44B** (-35%) |
| **Net debt / adj. EBITDA** | 4.4x | **~6.8x** |
| **Adj. EBITDA / interest** | 3.4x | **~2.2x** |
| Net debt / GAAP EBITDA | 17.3x | **>25x** |
| FCF | Negative | **Deeply negative** |

**Severe case (25% decremental margin):** EBITDA ~$1.25B → **net leverage ~7.8x, coverage ~1.9x.** At that level QXO would almost certainly **breach a typical 5.0-5.5x maintenance covenant** (EBITDA-addback dependent) and face a **multi-notch downgrade to B**. With **73% of assets in goodwill/intangibles**, a construction-led downturn would likely trigger a **multi-billion-dollar impairment**, eroding the $18.6B book equity that currently supports the BB rating.

**Mitigants:** capex is very low ($139M TTM, <1% of sales — an asset-light distribution model), so cash burn is driven by working capital rather than investment. QXO also has a **$500M buyback authorization** — which I view as **poorly timed capital allocation** given negative FCF and $10.3B of debt.

## 5. Liquidity & Refinancing Deep-Dive

- **Cash runway is thin:** $528M cash against a Q3 FCF of -$56M and Q2 FCF of -$248M. Roughly **2-4 quarters of standalone burn**, offset by revolver access and rapid working-capital release potential. **Cash ratio of 0.16x is the single weakest liquidity metric.**
- **Working capital is the swing factor:** -$285M in Q3'26 alone, -$418M in Q2'26 — a $700M two-quarter drag from inventory and receivables build. This is acquisition-integration related and should partially reverse.
- **Maturity profile (partial data):** identified debt includes a **$3.0B 2026 Incremental Term B loan**, a **$1.5B senior notes issue due 2031**, and secured first-lien term facilities. **No 2027-2028 refinancing cliff was identified** — the structure appears laddered to 2030-2031, which is a genuine strength. ⚠️ *Data gap: I could not retrieve the full contractual maturity schedule (S&P page returned 403); the above is assembled from SEC filings and press releases and should be verified against the 10-Q debt footnote.*
- **Dependence on capital markets:** the three acquisitions were equity- and debt-funded. Leverage is now at the ceiling of the BB category; further debt-funded M&A is constrained.

## 6. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical — QXO has genuine scale ($19.6B run-rate revenue), positive adjusted EBITDA ($2.23B annualized), a long-dated maturity ladder, and low capex intensity. But the combination of **17x GAAP net leverage, 1.1x Altman Z, negative FCF, 73% goodwill concentration, 76% share dilution, 68% volatility, and $528M of cash against $10.3B of debt** places this firmly in the High bucket. The margin of safety is thin and entirely dependent on the adjusted-EBITDA bridge being real.

## 7. Key Red Flags

1. **Goodwill/intangibles at 73.4% of total assets ($25.3B)** vs. a $12.4B market cap — the market is already capitalizing ~$13B of that as impaired. Impairment risk is severe.
2. **Cash down 83% in two quarters** ($3.05B → $528M) while debt tripled — no longer a fortress balance sheet.
3. **GAAP interest coverage is negative (-1.11x)**; even adjusted EBITDA covers interest only 3.4x. A 20% revenue decline takes this to 2.2x.
4. **Adjusted-EBITDA dependence:** $2.23B adjusted vs. $564M GAAP-derived TTM EBITDA — a **4x gap** driven by SBC ($175M TTM), transaction, transformation and restructuring addbacks. Investors are underwriting management's definition of earnings, not audited earnings.
5. **76% share count inflation** (613M → 1,081M) with a **$500M buyback launched into negative FCF** — capital allocation is aggressive.
6. **Negative FCF (-$98M TTM) and -$495M TTM net loss** despite record revenue — the roll-up is not yet generating cash.
7. **Activist-style serial-M&A model with no track record of integration** — QXO's stated $50B revenue / $4B EBITDA-by-2030 targets require further deals, implying more leverage and more dilution.
8. **2.44 beta and 62% max drawdown** — position sizing risk is extreme for a $12B cap.
