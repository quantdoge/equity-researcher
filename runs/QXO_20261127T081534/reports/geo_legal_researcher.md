## QXO, Inc. (NYSE: QXO) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Prepared from SEC filing metadata, QXO investor materials, and press coverage through mid-November 2026.*

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

QXO is now a **North-America-only business**: the Q1 2026 10-Q XBRL tags long-lived assets by only two countries (`country:US`, `country:CA`), and the company operates ~**1,150 branches/locations across the U.S. and Canada** with ~28,000 employees (Wikipedia/QXO materials; QXO account summary). Beacon was described at announcement as having "nearly 600 branches across the U.S. and Canada" (QXO press release, 2025).

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Regulatory Risks

**Risk 1 — Cumulative antitrust exposure from serial consolidation (highest probability, medium-high impact)**
- QXO's stated strategy is **$50B of annual revenue within a decade via accretive acquisitions** (QXO investor release). Each deal is HSR-reportable; the TopBuild deal was filed with FTC/DOJ on April 24, 2026 and the waiting period **expired May 26, 2026**, with a Canadian "no action" letter (SEC 424B3; MLex; CTFN).
- **Probability of future Second Request / remedy condition: High** (>60%) on the next material roofing/insulation distribution deal, because QXO is now a top-3 player in several local distribution markets. Impact: deal delay/divestiture conditions, not deal failure.
- Note also the FTC's Early Termination Notice for "QXO, Inc. / Court Square Capital Partners III" (March 26, 2026) — evidence of continuing bolt-on volume.

**Risk 2 — Tariffs, trade policy and input-cost pass-through (medium probability, medium impact)**
- Management's position: **limited direct tariff exposure** because the "vast majority of its products [are] made and sold in the U.S." (Roofing Contractor, on the Beacon deal) — QXO is a relative *net* beneficiary vs. import-dependent peers.
- But QXO raised prices **~6–10%** (commercial) and up to **9–12%** on shingles/siding (QXO price-alert pages, June 2026), signaling real input inflation. The residual risk is **demand destruction** in a housing/repair-remodel downturn plus **Canada/USMCA friction** hitting the Canadian branch network and cross-border sourcing (softwood lumber duties). Offsetting: semicustom/domestic sourcing supports share gains.

**Risk 3 — Balance-sheet-driven legal/contractual risk from the M&A funding stack (high salience, medium probability of adverse event)**
- QXO carries **6.75% senior notes**, a **$2.25B term loan facility**, and an **ABL revolver** (Q1 2026 10-Q summary; June 2026 offering memorandum), plus **$498.6M convertible preferred and $558.1M mandatory convertible preferred** and warrants. Q1 2026 net loss was **$227.1M** vs. $8.8M net income a year earlier, with $116.9M of quarterly amortization.
- Legal/contractual consequence: **covenant compliance, change-of-control consents, and refinancing risk** are the most likely sources of a "legal" event at QXO — more probable than litigation. Interest burden plus equity-linked dilution is what drove adjusted EPS down 7% in Q3 despite record sales.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **No material pending litigation or government investigation was identified** in the sources I could access. The FY2025 10-K risk-factor text and the 10-Q "Legal Proceedings/Commitments & Contingencies" note could not be retrieved with available tools — **treat this as an evidence gap, not a clean bill of health.**
- **M&A strike suits:** a shareholder-litigation firm publicly announced it is "investigating the merger" for QXO/BLD (PR Newswire, ~2026). These are typically **cost-of-doing-business disclosure suits with negligible expected damages**; the TopBuild structure (45% cash / 55% stock per the deal release) reduces appraisal-risk exposure.
- **A legacy beacon of acquisition-related liability:** the merger agreements expressly flag "unknown liabilities," "potential litigation and/or regulatory action relating to the acquisition," and "periodic litigation, regulatory proceedings and enforcement actions" (QXO/Beacon 8-K exhibits, March–April 2025). Post-close, QXO inherited Beacon's and Allied Building Products' **product-liability, construction-defect and asbestos-adjacent** exposure — an inherent feature of buying 600+ legacy distribution branches. No quantified reserve was retrievable.
- **Regulatory/environmental compliance:** Violation Tracker records OSHA "workplace safety or health" citations against **Beacon Roofing Supply ($21,876, 2023)** and **Allied Building Products** — i.e., routine OSHA/DOT/EPA-type matters typical of a branch-heavy distributor (forklift, fall-protection, hazmat/roofing-chemical handling). Low materiality, but **repeat-citation risk rises with 1,150 sites** and a cost-cutting integration program ($17.2M of restructuring/severance and facility closures in Q1 2026 alone).
- **Governance/key-person:** Brad Jacobs is simultaneously launching another company while remaining QXO CEO (Motley Fool, Nov 9 2026); the CFO is changing (**Dev Ahuja in, Ihsan Essaid to Vice Chairman, effective Jan 2027**) and a new COO (Ken West) starts Sept 2026. High executive churn during peak integration is a governance flag.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

- **Geopolitical exposure: LOW.** Effectively a pure U.S.–Canada business with domestic manufacturing/sourcing. There is **no China revenue exposure, no Russia/EM exposure, no sanctions nexus, and no expropriation or currency-control risk** (CAD is freely convertible; no repatriation restrictions). The trade-war transmission channel is **indirect (input costs and housing demand)**, and management reports minimal direct tariff exposure.
- **Regulatory/legal exposure: MEDIUM, trending up.** The rating is driven not by political geography but by (a) **cumulative antitrust scrutiny of a serial-acquirer roll-up strategy targeting $50B revenue**, (b) **leverage and covenant complexity** from ~$30B of acquisition funding, (c) **inherited long-tail product/legacy branch liabilities**, and (d) **executive turnover** during integration.
- **What would move this to HIGH:** a Second Request or divestiture order on the next major distribution deal; a quantified adverse asbestos/construction-defect reserve at Beacon/Allied; a covenant breach or distressed refinancing; or a U.S.–Canada trade rupture that materially impairs the Canadian branch network.
- **What would move this to LOW:** disclosure of a clean legal-proceedings note with no material reserves, evidence of sustained deleveraging post-TopBuild, and continued unconditional antitrust clearance.
