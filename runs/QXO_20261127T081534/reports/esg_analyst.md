## ESG ANALYSIS: QXO, Inc. (NYSE: QXO)
**Sector:** Industrials / Industrial Distribution (building products distribution & installation) | **Market cap:** ~$12.4B | **Employees:** ~28,000 (pre-TopBuild consolidation) | **Reporting date:** post-Q3 2026 (quarter ended 30-Sep-2026)

**Critical context for this analysis:** QXO is not a normal operating company with an ESG history. It is the former SGH (SMART Global Holdings) shell relaunched by Bradley Jacobs in 2024, which has since completed three transformational acquisitions — **Beacon Roofing Supply** (closed 2025 after a hostile tender offer and proxy fight), **Kodiak Building Partners** (~$2.25B, closed 1-Apr-2026), and **TopBuild** (~$17B, closed ~1-Jul-2026). Q3 2026 net sales were **$4.90B** (TopBuild $1.64B, Kodiak $589M, Beacon balance) — roughly a **$19–20B annualised run-rate**. ESG data, targets and disclosure are therefore inherited from predecessor companies and **not yet integrated at the group level**. That gap is itself the single most material ESG finding.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW–MEDIUM**

- **Direct emissions are modest in absolute terms.** QXO does not manufacture. Beacon's own 10-K states: *"Our greatest direct impact on the environment is through fleet emissions."* Scope 1+2 is dominated by ~2,000+ delivery vehicles and ~500 branch facilities.
- **Inherited target:** Beacon committed to a **50% reduction in GHG intensity (Scope 1+2) by 2030**, with three years of disclosed progress, EV fleet pilots, and the "OTC Network" routing strategy to cut fuel use. This is a credible, operationally-grounded target — but it was **not reaffirmed at QXO group level** post-acquisition, and TopBuild/Kodiak emissions have not been folded in.
- **No Scope 3 inventory.** For a distributor, purchased goods (roofing shingles, insulation, lumber) dwarf Scope 1+2. Absence of a Scope 3 estimate is the main disclosure gap and a growing problem given customer EPD/embodied-carbon requests.
- **Physical climate risk: MEDIUM but rising.** Roofing demand is *positively* correlated with storm activity (a revenue tailwind), but QXO's branch network and Gulf/Southeast concentration carry genuine physical risk — hurricane/ hail damage to facilities, business interruption, and insurance cost inflation. This is a two-sided exposure the market currently reads only as upside.
- **Positive environmental optionality:** TopBuild makes QXO one of North America's largest insulation installers — a direct beneficiary of building-efficiency codes and IRA incentives. This is the strongest E-linked value driver in the portfolio.

---

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Safety is the #1 social issue.** Beacon began disclosing **OSHA indicators/TRIR** and safety performance in its CSR reports — a genuine transparency positive and evidence of management systems ("Making Every Day Safer"). TopBuild adds installer safety exposure, historically a higher-hazard activity than branch warehousing.
- **Workforce disclosure:** Beacon published **EEO-1 data** and workforce diversity metrics annually — better than many industrial peers. QXO has **not** published group-level workforce data.
- **Labour relations:** Largely non-unionised branch/driver workforce; driver retention and CDL shortage are the practical constraint. No material strikes or NLRB controversies surfaced in the review window.
- **Community/social licence:** Low controversy. Beacon had community partnerships (NWIR, Rebuilding Together). Roofing distribution has minimal "social licence" friction versus extractives or chemicals.
- **Post-merger integration risk is the real S issue:** three large acquisitions in ~18 months, ~40,000 combined employees, and a stated intent to become "tech-enabled." Culture and retention risk during integration is underappreciated and flows directly into service quality and revenue retention.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Negatives (material):**
1. **CEO/Chairman duality + dual-CEO role.** Jacobs is Founder, Chairman and CEO of QXO *and* became Chairman and CEO of **CKX**, his ninth company (backed by $900M from Jacobs Private Equity in a $1B venture). Running two public companies concurrently is a **key-person and attention-split risk** with no peer precedent in the industrial distribution universe.
2. **M&A disclosure litigation.** A QXO stockholder filed a **proposed class action in Delaware Chancery Court seeking to block the shareholder vote on the TopBuild merger**, alleging inadequate deal disclosures (vote held 29-Jun-2026; ~99% of votes cast in favour; deal closed ~1-Jul-2026). Related **Section 220 books-and-records demands** have also been litigated. The deal closed, so financial impact is likely confined to legal fees and disclosure-remediation — but it signals a pattern of aggressive transaction process.
3. **Capital-structure complexity.** ~$3B convertible perpetual preferred (Apollo-led; **Series C at 4.75%**, with separate-class protective provisions and registration rights) plus mandatory convertible preferred and depositary shares. Preferred claims rank ahead of common and the Kodiak consideration shares carry a **$40/share company repurchase right** — unusual features that reduce minority-holder transparency on the true economic share count.
4. **Acquirer conduct.** QXO ran a hostile tender offer and board-replacement proxy fight against Beacon, which adopted a poison pill in response. Legal and ultimately successful, but indicative of a board willing to use maximum pressure.

**Positives (real, and why this is not a 2 or 3):**
- **Lead Independent Director** in place since June 2024 (Allison Landry) — a meaningful check on duality.
- **Independent committee structure**, including a **Nominating, Corporate Governance and Sustainability Committee** whose charter explicitly covers oversight of "purpose-driven sustainability strategies, performance and external disclosures… material ESG matters." Board-level ESG accountability exists.
- **Single class of common stock** — no dual-class super-voting structure. One share, one vote.
- **Compensation is genuinely long-dated and TSR-linked.** Executive PSUs vest **31-Dec-2030** against **relative TSR vs. the S&P 500**, with 0–225% payout, and settled shares carry **one-year post-vest lock-ups**. Senior hires also receive multi-year RSUs with lock-ups. This is among the better long-term alignment structures in Industrials — it directly mitigates the "short-term roll-up" critique.
- **Insider ownership is large and aligned** (Jacobs' personal capital is materially committed), which substitutes imperfectly for board independence.

**Net:** structurally sound machinery, but concentrated in one person and complicated by concurrent control of a second company. Score **5/10**.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD / CBAM: not applicable.** QXO's operations are US and Canada only. This is a genuine advantage — the most expensive disclosure regimes do not bite.
- **SEC climate disclosure rule:** currently stayed/under legal challenge; no near-term obligation.
- **California SB 253 / SB 261 — APPLIES and is the binding near-term driver.** QXO exceeds the $1B revenue threshold and has substantial California operations (Beacon, TopBuild). Scope 1+2 reporting obligations phase in from 2026, **Scope 3 from 2027**. QXO currently has **no group-level Scope 3 inventory** — this is a concrete, dated compliance gap requiring spend on data systems.
- **Canada:** CSSB/IFRS S1–S2 adoption pathway; voluntary now, likely mandatory for large issuers. QXO Canada operations are in scope.
- **Green building codes & customer specs (IECC, CALGreen, LEED, EPD requirements):** the most commercially relevant "regulation" — favours TopBuild's insulation installation and pressures QXO to supply embodied-carbon data to contractors.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified in the Apollo-led $1.8B financing or preferred terms. **Leverage covenants, not ESG covenants, are the real constraint.**
- **Disclosure frameworks:** No SASB-aligned reporting, no TCFD, no CDP response identified. S&P Global publishes a CSA ESG Score for QXO, but the underlying company disclosure base is thin.

---

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD** (disclosure-led, not risk-led)

QXO rates as a **Laggard on ESG transparency and integration**: no consolidated QXO sustainability report, no group-level targets, no Scope 3, no SASB/TCFD/CDP alignment, and the most recent publicly hosted CSR report is **Beacon's 2022 report** still sitting on qxo.com. Three acquisitions have been absorbed with no restated ESG baseline. It rates **Average on underlying operational ESG risk** — distribution is a low-carbon, low-controversy business, and inherited Beacon safety and diversity programmes are real.

Two mitigating considerations: (a) the company is functionally ~24 months old and prioritising integration; (b) the **governance architecture (lead independent director, single-class shares, 2030-vesting relative-TSR PSUs) is better than the "founder roll-up" stereotype implies.** If QXO publishes a consolidated, Scope 3-inclusive report with reaffirmed 2030 targets within the next 12–18 months, the rating moves to Average quickly.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (with a governance discount)

**ESG is not the thesis driver for QXO — capital allocation, M&A accretion, and leverage are. ESG does not argue against the position, but it should not be ignored in the discount rate.**

- **Environmental — mild positive skew.** Fleet emissions are a cost line, not a liability. TopBuild's insulation installation is levered to energy-efficiency codes and IRA incentives, making the E factor a **demand tailwind** rather than a risk. No stranded-asset exposure, no EU/CBAM exposure.
- **Social — neutral.** Safety is a managed operating risk with disclosed metrics. Integration/retention across three deals is the live issue, and it will show up in service levels before it shows up in ESG scores.
- **Governance — the material negative, and it is priced by sophisticated holders, not by ESG raters.** The correct way to weight this is **not** an ESG exclusion but a **cost-of-capital adjustment** for: (i) Jacobs splitting CEO duties with CKX; (ii) preferred stock and repurchase rights that complicate the economic share count; (iii) a demonstrated willingness to litigate/disclose aggressively on deals. Monitor **CKX time commitment, preferred conversion dilution, and any repeat of merger-disclosure litigation** as leading indicators.
- **Actionable monitoring triggers:** (1) publication of a group-level sustainability report with Scope 3 and a reaffirmed 2030 intensity target; (2) any Delaware settlement or injunction precedent from the TopBuild disclosure suit; (3) TRIR trend at the combined entity post-integration; (4) any change to the 2030 PSU vesting horizon, which would weaken the strongest governance mitigant.

**Bottom line: Positive on the E/S fundamentals of the underlying business, Negative on governance concentration, Net Neutral for the thesis.** ESG disclosure weakness is a reputational and compliance cost, not an impairment to intrinsic value — but the governance profile justifies a modest discount rate premium that the current multiple likely does not fully embed.
