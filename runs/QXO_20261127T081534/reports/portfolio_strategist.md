### 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT — with a bearish risk-weighted tilt (6 negative / 4 neutral / 1 positive)**

**Where agents agree (highest-confidence, multi-sourced):**
- **Leverage is the swing variable.** Fin Risk, Macro, Value, Short, Non-Fin and Geo/Legal independently identify the ~$650m interest bill and ~4.4x adj. leverage (≈5.7x incl. preferred) as the dominant driver of equity outcomes. Five agents derive the same number from independent data. **This is the single most reliable conclusion in the book.**
- **Organic growth has not inflected.** Sector, Alt Data, Non-Fin and Short all note legacy Beacon −2.5% / roofing −4.3% YoY; the +79% headline is pure M&A. The +5.4% October daily-sales datapoint is real but one month.
- **Accounting/complexity is unusually opaque.** Adjusted EBITDA ~$2.2bn vs GAAP-derived ~$564m (4x gap); 73% goodwill; interim CAOs. Three agents flag this independently. Nothing suggests fraud — the Short Analyst explicitly cleared it.

**Where agents disagree:**
- **Growth vs. Value on the same facts.** Growth sizes on FY28–30 consensus EPS ($0.82/$1.77); Value shows those estimates require a ~24% EBITDA margin no distributor has ever earned and note consensus 2030 EBITDA of $1.63bn is **59% below management's $4bn**. Resolution: **Value wins.** Growth's case depends on guidance that management itself has not defended and that the sell side has already abandoned. The target dispersion proves it — Oppenheimer $32 (Apr) → Truist $15 (Oct) → recent median ~$17 with a range of $15–21.
- **Sector (+organic share gains) vs. Short (core is shrinking).** Partly reconcilable: QXO is taking share in a shrinking market. That's a real but modest positive that does not offset leverage.

**Conflict resolution applied:** where the Growth Researcher's bullishness collides with the Fin Risk / Non-Fin Risk / Value evidence on cash conversion and governance, **the risk flags take precedence** under the stated framework. A thesis that requires positive FCF to work, where FCF is −$98m and the CFO and two interim CAOs have all turned over mid-integration, is not financeable by an improving gross-margin line alone.

### 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **0.0% of a diversified portfolio — PASS**

**Kelly criterion: size = 0.** Probability-weighted IV of ~$9 sits *below* the $11.97 price. Edge is negative; a negative-edge bet gets zero allocation regardless of convexity. Even on the most generous reading — 25% weight to the bull case at $16.4 — the expected value is below spot.

If the mandate explicitly permits speculative, catalyst-driven, high-beta exposure, the ceiling is **0.5% of portfolio** — not 1–3%. Rationale: 67.7% annualized vol and 2.44 beta mean a 0.5% position carries ~1.2% of portfolio volatility contribution and a beta-adjusted exposure equivalent to a **~1.2% position in the S&P**. Sizing must be done on volatility, not notional. A 0.5% position at a 25% stop costs ~12bp of portfolio — acceptable as tuition for a genuine option on a proven operator.

**Explicitly rejected:** 1–3% on the "below-book" argument. The 0.76x P/B is an artifact — tangible book is ~$1.27/share, so the "book value floor" that both retail and the Alt Data Analyst cite does not exist as a valuation support.

### 7. Final Portfolio Action: **PASS**

**Do not initiate. If already held, HOLD at ≤0.5% with the $9.90 stop — do not average down.**

**Rationale in one paragraph:** eleven specialists have converged, from independent data, on a single fact pattern — QXO is a genuinely well-assembled collection of distribution assets run by a proven operator, financed at ~5x leverage into the worst possible macro regime, priced at or above a probability-weighted intrinsic value of ~$9, converting ~$2.2bn of "adjusted EBITDA" into *negative* free cash flow, while its accounting function turns over and not one insider buys a share through a 43% collapse. The bull case is not irrational — the gross margin moving 23.0% → 28.2% in four quarters is real, and the 2027–28 disinflation scenario would plausibly re-rate the stock to $16–18 — but it requires a macro turn, a synergy delivery, and a capital-markets reopening that no agent can date, and the price does not compensate for that conjunction. **The correct portfolio decision is to decline the bet at $11.97, hold a live watchlist at $8.50–9.50, and let the 24 Feb 2027 print — either the FY27 EBITDA guide or the first positive-FCF quarter — be the gate that earns an allocation.** There is no urgency here: at 2.44 beta and 68% volatility, entry will be available; the risk of missing it is far smaller than the risk of sizing it before the cash-conversion question is answered.
