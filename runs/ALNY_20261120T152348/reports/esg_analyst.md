### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW** (relative to sector)

Positives (management-stated, partially third-party verified):
- Reports **Scope 1, 2 and material Scope 3 GHG with third-party assurance** — above median for a biotech of this size and a genuine plus for data quality.
- **2030 Scope 1+2 reduction framework** (renewables, fleet electrification, facility optimisation, on-site solar at Norton, MA), backed by an independent third-party assessment.
- Reporting aligned to **SASB, GRI, IFRS, CDP and ESRS** — a credible indicator of CSRD readiness.
- Process innovation as decarbonisation: **siRELIS** enzymatic ligation platform claimed to use fewer materials/plant resources per unit — a rare case of sustainability logic embedded in COGS.

Negatives / watch items:
- **No net-zero or SBTi-validated target identified** in the materials reviewed — the 2030 framework is Scope 1+2 only, with Scope 3 (which dominates a pharma footprint: CMOs, oligo API, cold-chain air freight) addressed qualitatively via "supplier visibility."
- **No absolute emissions figures verified.** As revenue compounds >50% YoY, an intensity-only or unquantified framework risks being a growth-excused increase in absolute emissions — a CSRD/ESRS E1 disclosure vulnerability.
- Hazardous waste, solvent and water intensity inherent to oligonucleotide synthesis; Norton site expansion increases exposure.

**Verdict:** not a valuation issue. The E dimension is a **disclosure-readiness** issue with modest compliance cost, not a stranded-asset or carbon-tax issue. Alnylam faces no CBAM-relevant goods and no material EU ETS exposure.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

This is where the real ESG risk sits, and it is almost entirely **price and access**.

- **US list price:** Amvuttra carries an annual list price of roughly **$463,500** (ATTR-PN) and was launched into ATTR-CM at essentially the same price — reported at **~$476,000/year**, a premium to incumbent tafamidis. Canadian review filings show a submitted price of **CAD ~$572,000/patient-year**.
- **Health-economics pressure:** ICER's ATTR-CM assessment explicitly benchmarked vutrisiran against a health-benefit price threshold, implying the list price sits well above cost-effectiveness. This is the single most likely trigger for payer utilisation management in the highest-value indication.
- **Political/regulatory exposure:** At this price point ALNY is squarely in the crosshairs of IRA Medicare drug-price negotiation, state price-transparency boards, and EU joint procurement/HTA (JCA). Because RNAi oligos are chemically synthesised, the **7-year (not 11-year) IRA clock** likely applies — a structural pricing risk that is *underpriced* in most ESG screens.
- **Mitigants (real but softer):** ~99% coverage in ATTR-PN with most patients at **$0 out-of-pocket**; active patient-support/financial-assistance programmes; rare-disease patient-advocacy alignment; transparency in grants-and-giving reporting. Company maintains an explicit "Patient Access Philosophy" page.
- **Product safety:** no major new safety controversy surfaced in the review window; the HELIOS-B five-year open-label data (AHA, Nov 2026) presented sustained benefit — a social positive. Residual risk is GMP/inspection at the expanding Norton site.
- **Workforce:** no significant labour controversy identified. Risk is key-scientific-talent retention, which is being addressed with heavy equity (see Governance).

**Verdict: HIGH** — not from misconduct, but from the structural collision between a ~$476k price point and payers, ICER, and governments. Any single payer action or IRA selection on Amvuttra/patisiran transmits directly to a franchise that is ~87.5% of product revenue.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Positives:**
- Chair is **Amy Schulman**, a non-executive/independent chair — **no CEO/Chairman duality** (CEO: Yvonne Greenstreet, M.D.).
- Board of 11 (expanded from 10 in June 2026 with the addition of Benjamin Cravatt, Ph.D.) with a majority of independent directors; only Greenstreet is a management director on the ballot.
- Compensation architecture is genuinely at-risk-weighted: PSUs + RSUs + options, annual incentive tied to corporate goals, plus **clawback, anti-hedging/pledging, and stock-ownership guidelines**.
- Strong institutional ownership base with engaged long-only holders — **Capital Group ~18.5% combined, Fidelity ~6.8%, Vanguard ~9.5%, BlackRock ~5.8%** — a cohort that historically disciplines boards.
- Clean auditor continuity (**PwC**, ratified 2026); dedicated **People, Culture and Compensation** committee.
- No dual-class share structure identified; one share, one vote.

**Negatives:**
- **Classified (staggered) board** — three classes, Class I directors elected in 2026 to serve until 2029. Combined with a **–60.4% one-year TSR**, this materially blunts shareholder ability to effect board change. Third-party governance grading is **C-**, which is consistent with my read.
- **Pay-vs-performance optics deteriorated sharply.** The proxy discloses **special CEO PSU awards and special RSUs** justified as retention "during a critical growth phase" — granted into a year in which the stock fell ~60% and 2026 guidance was cut twice. Boardroom Alpha explicitly flags "the size and discretion in special awards and change-in-control protections" as the key voting concern. Expect shareholder dissent on the 2026 say-on-pay; the vote result was not verified.
- **Disclosure credibility is the biggest problem.** Shares fell **28.35% on 30 July 2026** on a ~$200M FY26 guidance cut attributed to "normalized" Amvuttra patient demand, then **~25% again on 29 October 2026**. Multiple firms (**Pomerantz, Glancy Prongay Wolke & Rotter, Howard G. Smith, Brodsky**) are investigating securities-fraud claims referencing both dates. A pattern of guidance set ahead of demand reality, followed by legal action, is a governance and controls signal, not merely a stock-price event.
- **Capital allocation tension:** a **$1B buyback** authorised in November 2026 — days after a second guidance cut and alongside a change in commercial leadership. Buybacks at a post-drawdown price can be defensible, but the sequencing invites questions about board oversight of capital allocation and whether cash is better held against pipeline/launch risk.
- **Revenue concentration (87.5% one product)** is a strategy/risk-oversight critique — the board did not diversify fast enough against the single-asset dependence it accepted.

**Score rationale:** the *machinery* of governance is above average (independent chair, majority-independent, PSU-heavy comp, strong holders, no dual class) — that alone earns 6–7. The *behaviour* — staggered board, large discretionary retention awards amid severe underperformance, and a serial-disclosure problem that has drawn securities-fraud probes — deducts to **5/10**.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Alnylam is a **two-sided** case:

- **Environmental: Leader-for-scale** (assured Scope 1–3, ESRS/SASB/GRI alignment, 2030 framework, process innovation), albeit with no net-zero/SBTi target and no verified absolute tonnage.
- **Social: Laggard on affordability optics**, driven by a ~$476k price point that ICER and payers consider above cost-effectiveness, layered on 87.5% single-product concentration.
- **Governance: Average machinery, deteriorating behaviour** — independent chair and PSU-heavy comp undercut by a staggered board, discretionary retention awards into a –60% TSR year, and a disclosure record now attracting securities-fraud investigations.

No environmental, safety, labour or corruption scandal. The binding constraints are **pricing legitimacy** and **disclosure credibility**.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE (modest) — i.e., a drag, not a veto**

**For the thesis:** ESG is *not* the reason to own or avoid ALNY — commercial execution on Amvuttra is. But three ESG-derived factors should be embedded in the model and the position sizing:

1. **Pricing/access is a live earnings risk, not an ESG abstraction.** The US list price sits above ICER's cost-effectiveness benchmark while the US is the profit pool. IRA selection plus payer management is a **bear-case accelerant**; the July–October demand misses may be the first visible symptom of it. Discount terminal ATTR-CM penetration and attach a higher probability to price concessions in EU/Japan.
2. **Governance/disclosure is a real overhang on the multiple.** Securities-fraud investigations following two guidance cuts in one year justify a **discount-rate penalty of ~50–100bps** and cap re-rating until management re-establishes guidance credibility. The staggered board means shareholders cannot force change quickly — which argues for **engagement over activism**.
3. **The buyback is the tell.** A $1B repurchase announced after a second cut and a commercial-leadership change should be scrutinised for whether the board is signalling confidence or managing the narrative. Watch for insider buying (a genuine signal) versus programme execution.

**Action:** Neutral-to-slightly-negative on the ESG overlay. **Engage** the board on (a) absolute Scope 1–2 targets and Scope 3 quantification ahead of CSRD, (b) removing the classified board and re-basing executive pay to post-2026 performance, (c) disclosure controls around demand guidance. **Escalate to Negative** if a formal securities class action is filed with a broadened class period, if Amvuttra is selected for IRA negotiation earlier than modelled, or if say-on-pay support falls below ~70%. **Upgrade to Neutral/Positive** if the company publishes quantified absolute emissions with SBTi validation and restores two consecutive quarters of guidance-consistent Amvuttra demand.
