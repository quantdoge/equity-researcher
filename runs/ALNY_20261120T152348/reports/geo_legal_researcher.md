### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — U.S. drug-pricing policy stack (Probability: Very High | Impact: High)**
Four overlapping mechanisms now bear on a franchise that is ~89% of product revenue:
- **Section 232 pharma tariffs in force in 2026** — 100% headline rate, reduced to 15% for EU/Japan/Korea and 20% under onshoring commitments; staged effective dates of 31-Jul-26 and 29-Sep-26 (White House proclamation, Apr-26; WilmerHale 15-Oct-26; EY 2026-0786; PwC 10-Apr-26). Alnylam's U.S. plant limits direct hit, but imported API/CMO inputs and any ex-U.S. finished goods remain exposed.
- **MFN / TrumpRx deals (Dec-2025 onward)** — nine drugmakers agreed MFN pricing in exchange for tariff relief (White House fact sheet 19-Dec-25; Reuters). Alnylam's participation status is **not confirmed in the sources retrieved** — an unresolved binary: signing compresses U.S. net price; not signing invites tariff and political friction.
- **IRA Medicare negotiation** — AMVUTTRA's ATTR-CM label makes it a structurally obvious future selectee once the Part D/B timing gates are met; management has already publicly attributed a Phase III cut to IRA incentives (Endpoints).
- **340B rebate pilot (HRSA, Aug-2026)** — new rebate model aimed at duplicate 340B/MFP discounts; sell-side flagged **rising 340B risk** in the Q3'26 print (Seeking Alpha, Oct-26; Federal Register 2026-15633; Holland & Knight, Aug-26).

**Risk 2 — Securities-litigation overhang from the 2026 demand disclosures (Probability: High | Impact: Low–Medium)**
A **§10(b) class action has been filed** covering purchasers from **12-Feb-2026 onward** (Howard Smith complaint, Jul-26), and investigations have broadened after a **28.31% single-day decline** and a further ~25% drop post-Q3'26 (Glancy Prongay, 29-Oct-26; Pomerantz, Nov-26; Frank R. Cruz; Kessler Topaz). Directionally: low cash severity relative to $20B cap, but it hardens disclosure conservatism and raises headline risk into 2027 guidance.

**Risk 3 — Ex-U.S. reimbursement compression and FX on the ATTR-CM ramp (Probability: Medium-High | Impact: Medium)**
International TTR growth is now the marginal growth driver ("ongoing ATTR-CM launches in Japan, Germany, and Italy"), which is precisely where price is administratively set. Japan's annual NHI cuts, German AMNOG re-assessment and the EU HTA Regulation's joint clinical assessment all cap ex-U.S. pricing, with JPY/EUR translation adding volatility. Note the company already reports constant-exchange-rate growth, signalling FX sensitivity.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Patent — offensive strategy has largely failed.** The Federal Circuit affirmed non-infringement in *Alnylam v. Moderna* (No. 23-2357, 4-Jun-2025), and Alnylam conceded the first Pfizer/BioNTech round after claim construction (IPF Ray; JD Supra). The LNP patent campaign is now a **non-event for upside and carries no liability** — remove it from the bull case.
- **Patent — new defensive/IP-challenge phase.** AMVUTTRA became **eligible for patent challenges on 13-Jun-2026** (DrugPatentWatch), with 13 U.S. patents filed 2022–24 and none yet expired (GreyB). Expect ANDA/BPCIA notice letters and IPRs against a product generating >$4.5B annualized revenue — the single most underappreciated legal tail.
- **FDA promotional enforcement — two untitled letters.** FDA's OPDP issued an untitled letter on the AMVUTTRA TV ad (**9-Sep-2025**) and a second over efficacy/survival claims on the AMVUTTRA **website** (FDA media 192265), both alleging the materials misbrand the drug under the FD&C Act (FiercePharma; MMM). Materially: enforcement is aimed at a **survival claim**, i.e., the core commercial message of the ATTR-CM launch. Escalation to a warning letter or a DOJ/FCA off-label theory is the tail risk.
- **No evidence found** of DOJ, SEC enforcement, FCPA, AML or sanctions matters — the SEC-adjacent exposure is purely private civil litigation.
- **Collaborator/partner regulatory dependence:** Regeneron's **Livsayis (cemdisiran) FDA approval 19-Nov-26** ($-economics via milestones/royalties) and Roche's zilebesiran remain third-party execution risks rather than legal ones.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Promotional/misbranding compliance (AMBER).** Repeat OPDP letters within ~12 months on the flagship brand is a pattern, not an anomaly — it invites FDA scrutiny of medical/legal/regulatory review and of promotional claims made by field forces during the ATTR-CM launch.
2. **Government-pricing program compliance (AMBER).** 340B duplicate-discount and MFP/Medicaid gross-to-net mechanics are the live audit surface; Q3'26 included a **$10M one-time Medicaid-related gross-to-net adjustment** — a small but telling data point.
3. **Disclosure/securities (AMBER).** Open class period from 12-Feb-2026 means every forward statement on Amvuttra demand conversion is now litigable.
4. **FCPA/AML/sanctions (GREEN).** No high-risk-jurisdiction footprint, no disclosed investigations; a U.S.-centric supply chain keeps export-control and sanctions exposure immaterial.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Pure geopolitical risk is genuinely low — no China, Russia or emerging-market manufacturing dependence, a U.S. manufacturing base that benefits from Section 232 onshoring incentives, and no sanctions or export-control entanglement. The Medium rating is driven almost entirely by **domestic U.S. policy and legal exposure**, not foreign risk: a heavily concentrated single-franchise revenue base (~89% TTR, ~87% AMVUTTRA) sitting directly in the path of MFN pricing, Section 232 tariffs, IRA negotiation and 340B rebate reform, plus a filed securities class action and two FDA promotional letters.

**What would change the rating:** (a) execution of an MFN/TrumpRx agreement with explicit tariff exemption → improves to **Low-Medium**; (b) escalation of OPDP letters to a warning letter, or a DOJ/FCA inquiry into ATTR-CM promotion → **High**; (c) ANDA/BPCIA challenge to vutrisiran filed with a credible invalidity theory → **High**.
