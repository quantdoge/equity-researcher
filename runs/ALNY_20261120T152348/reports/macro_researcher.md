## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — hawkish reflation with a bear-steepening curve. This is a "risk-on in credit, risk-off in duration" regime.**

The US economy is *not* slowing. Q3-2026 real GDP grew **3.4% annualised**, up from a near-stall in Q4-2025 (+0.2%), with unemployment down to **4.1%** from 4.5% a year ago. Simultaneously, CPI has re-accelerated from **2.39% YoY in January to 3.82% in October** — 143bp of disinflation fully reversed, with three-month momentum running ~5.7% annualised.

The Fed has therefore pivoted back to tightening. After cutting to 3.63% through mid-2026, it hiked on **16 September** and held at **3.75–4.00% on 28 October** — but with **two dissents in favour of a hike**. The 18 November minutes showed "almost all officials" expect further hikes "in the months ahead."

**The single most important number:** with effective fed funds at 3.88% and CPI at 3.82%, the **real policy rate is approximately zero**. Policy is not restrictive on a real basis — which is precisely why the Fed is signalling more. The 2Y (4.74%) sits **86bp above** the effective funds rate, so the market is already pricing roughly three more hikes.

The long end is where the damage is. The 10Y has risen **69bp since 30 June** (4.44% → 5.13%), with the 30Y at **5.47%**. This is *bear steepening*, not inversion (2s10s +39bp) — a term-premium and fiscal story, not a growth scare. The debt ceiling will bind around **February 2027**, adding structural pressure at the long end.

Critically, **credit is easy**: the Baa-10Y spread is **1.52%**, down from 1.69% in mid-August and well inside its long-run ~2.0% average. This is the defining nuance of the regime — **capital is cheap for credit, expensive for duration.** That is exactly why XBI has rallied ($120.60 → $145.88) while a long-duration single name like ALNY has collapsed. **The macro backdrop for biotech as a sector is genuinely favourable; ALNY's problem is not macro.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

### The duration problem — the dominant transmission channel

ALNY is among the longest-duration assets in large-cap biotech. Its valuation rests on Amvuttra's ATTR-CM franchise extending into the 2030s plus an early-stage RNAi platform whose cash flows are a decade out. With the **real 10Y at ~2.4%**, the discount rate applied to those back-ended flows has repriced violently. Rule of thumb: a 100bp rise in the real discount rate destroys **15–25%** of value for a cash-flow stream with a ~15-year duration. The real rate has risen materially through 2026 — this alone justifies a substantial de-rating, *independently of any change in the fundamentals.*

| Macro Variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **10Y / real yields (5.13% / ~2.4%)** | **Strongly Negative** | Longest-duration name in the peer set; multiple compression is mechanical. |
| **Fed hiking path** | **Negative** | ~3 more hikes priced; each increment extends the duration discount. |
| **CPI at 3.8%** | **Negative (margin)** | 2,500-employee R&D base faces wage inflation; drug prices are payer/competition-set, so no offsetting pricing power. |
| **GDP growth 3.4%** | **Neutral** | ATTR-CM is a fatal, progressive disease — demand is non-discretionary. Revenue is nearly GDP-insensitive. Genuinely defensive. |
| **Unemployment 4.1%** | **Negative (mild)** | Tight labour market raises R&D/clinical wage costs. |
| **Credit spreads 1.52%** | **Positive** | Funding window wide open; the biotech capital cycle is *not* impaired. |
| **USD +3.9% in 2 months** | **Slightly Negative** | Translation headwind on ex-US revenue booked via partners (Sanofi, Regeneron); also pressures ex-US pricing. Modest — the majority of Amvuttra revenue is US-booked. |
| **Pharma tariffs (100% on imported branded drugs)** | **Neutral to Positive** | ALNY manufactures at its Norton, MA facility — largely exempt, and advantaged versus foreign-manufacturing peers. |
| **MFN drug pricing / IRA** | **Negative (medium-term)** | High-price rare-disease franchises are prime Medicare negotiation targets. Policy uncertainty has fallen, but the direction of travel is adverse. |
| **Self-funding status + $1B buyback (4-Nov-26)** | **Positive** | Removes the classic biotech rate trap — no forced equity raise at a 69% drawdown. |

### The critical context: macro is the amplifier, not the cause

ALNY is **-69% from its October 2025 peak of $491.22** and **-62% year-to-date**. But almost none of that is macro. Two identifiable idiosyncratic shocks did the damage:

- **30 July 2026 (-28.3%, 9.8M shares):** 2026 guidance cut on ATTR-CM headwinds — compounded by the Wainua (eptlontersen) Phase 3 failure, which raised class-wide doubts about TTR silencers.
- **29 October 2026 (-25.1%, 11.8M shares):** Q3 revenue *beat*, but management warned of slowing new-to-brand Amvuttra prescription growth.

This distinction matters enormously for the investment memo: **ALNY is falling while XBI rises.** The sector is in a policy-relief and M&A-driven rally (tariff exemptions secured, MFN framework agreed, spreads tight). ALNY's underperformance is a *company-specific credibility problem*, and the high-rate environment is what makes that problem so expensive — when growth durability is questioned, the market applies a higher discount rate, and there is no easy multiple support underneath.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

*(Nuance: unfavourable on the valuation axis, neutral-to-positive on the operating axis. Macro is a secondary driver here — I would not attribute more than ~25% of the drawdown to it — but it is a powerful amplifier that removes the cushion a re-rating would normally find.)*

**Why not "Neutral":** A zero real policy rate with the Fed explicitly guiding higher, a 5.47% 30Y, and a ~2.4% real 10Y constitute the worst discount-rate environment for long-duration biotech in two decades. Every incremental hike hits ALNY's terminal-value-heavy DCF harder than it hits the average healthcare name. The company's own low beta (0.27) is misleading — it measures co-movement, not rate sensitivity. ALNY is a *high-duration* stock even though it is a *low-beta* stock, and in a bear-steepening regime those two facts diverge sharply.

**Why not "Strongly Unfavourable":** Demand is genuinely non-cyclical; Zilebesiran (hypertension) and the cardio-metabolic pipeline are long-dated but real optionality. ALNY is self-funding, just authorised a $1B buyback, has negligible tariff exposure, and faces an open credit market. There is no solvency or financing cliff — the risk is multiple compression, not existential.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise: **A Q1-2027 Fed Pivot (BULL)**

Consensus — and the Fed's own minutes — is positioned hawkishly. With the real policy rate at zero and CPI at 3.8%, the market is priced for roughly three more hikes. That is a crowded, one-directional trade.

**The surprise:** the **debt-ceiling standoff expected around February 2027** collides with a growth scare. Q4-2025 already showed the economy can stall to +0.2%. A fiscal-brinkmanship-driven risk-off episode, or simply a rollover in the hot 3-month CPI momentum as the labour market softens from 4.1%, forces the Fed to abandon the hiking bias. The 10Y falls from 5.13% toward **4.25%**, and the real 10Y compresses by ~70bp.

**The transmission to ALNY would be violent and non-linear.** At a 69% drawdown with sentiment at a trough, an 70bp fall in real rates is worth a **40–60% re-rating** on duration alone — before any stabilisation in Amvuttra new-to-brand trends. Long-duration biotech is the highest-torque asset class in a real-rate rally, and ALNY combines maximum duration with maximum washed-out positioning and a live idiosyncratic catalyst set (5-year HELIOS-B data, cemdisiran approval, buyback support).

**Symmetry for the memo:** the bear mirror-image is the Fed following through to 4.50%+ and the 10Y reaching 6.0% on fiscal premium, costing another 20–25% of multiple even on flat fundamentals. But that path is *consensus*, whereas the pivot is not. The asymmetry here favours the upside surprise — and it is a **macro** catalyst, arriving independently of whether the ATTR-CM demand debate resolves.
