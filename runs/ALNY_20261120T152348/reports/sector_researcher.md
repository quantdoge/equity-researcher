## SECTOR BRIEF — ALNY (Alnylam Pharmaceuticals) | Data as of 20 Nov 2026

### 1. Sector Overview

ALNY sits in **Biotechnology — oligonucleotide/RNAi therapeutics**, a sub-sector of the $1.97T-revenue (FY25, 22-company sample) US-listed Healthcare complex, whose aggregate gross margin is ~65% and net margin ~13.5%. The modality is no longer speculative: Alnylam alone has delivered **seven FDA-approved RNAi products in under nine years** (latest: Livsayis/cemdisiran, approved 19 Nov 2026 and commercialised by Regeneron), and the class is now contesting large cardiometabolic indications (ATTR-CM, hypertension, MASLD) rather than only ultra-rare orphan disease. That migration is the pivotal sector fact: it multiplies the TAM but simultaneously converts RNAi economics from **orphan pricing (high price, negligible competition)** to **cardiology pricing (payer scrutiny, oral and gene-editing rivals, share warfare)**. Sector structure is oligopolistic at the platform level (Alnylam, Ionis, Arrowhead, plus licensees Regeneron, Novartis, AstraZeneca, Roche) but brutally competitive at the asset level, where a single indication (ATTR-CM) now hosts Alnylam's Amvuttra, BridgeBio's Attruby, Pfizer's Vyndaqel/Vyndamax, and Ionis/AstraZeneca's eplontersen. Capital intensity is low (CMO-based manufacturing, 82% gross margins) and switching costs are moderate (chronic injectables with mortality benefit), while barriers to entry are high on IP/formulation chemistry but falling on clinical execution — a classic **"high barriers at the gate, crowded inside"** configuration.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Platform optionality converts to royalty cash at zero commercial cost.** Livsayis (cemdisiran) approval gives ALNY milestones plus tiered royalties on a Regeneron-funded gMG launch; Regeneron's CIDEBsiRNA showed first-in-human liver-fat normalisation in MASLD (Nov 2026), and zilebesiran (hypertension, Roche-partnered) addresses a multi-tens-of-millions-patient pool. This is non-dilutive, high-margin revenue layered on existing R&D spend.
- **ATTR-CM class expansion + best-in-class durability data.** Five-year HELIOS-B data (AHA, Nov 2026) show sustained mortality/morbidity benefit for Amvuttra, supporting duration of therapy and share-of-voice versus oral stabilisers; ATTR-CM diagnosis rates are still structurally under-penetrated globally, so the pool is growing faster than the number of competitors.
- **Operating leverage is now visible in the P&L.** Operating margin went from **-83.9% (FY21) → -15.4% (FY23) → -7.9% (FY24) → +13.5% (FY25)** on 49.8% five-year revenue CAGR; Q3'26 operating income of $346M (23.7% margin) confirms the model scales without proportionate SG&A (SG&A flat at ~$322–348M/quarter since 2025).

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **ATTR-CM share loss is real and priced in brutally.** BridgeBio's Attruby is targeting 30–40% of the ATTR market and is "cruising toward blockbuster"; ALNY cut 2026 ATTR guidance on 29 Oct 2026, driving a **-25% one-day move** and a **-62% YTD share price** ($400 → $151). A second air-pocket occurred 30 Jul 2026 (-28% on the day, $287 → $205) — two guidance disappointments in one year destroys management credibility on the growth algorithm.
- **Growth deceleration and gross-margin erosion.** Revenue growth has collapsed from **+65.2% (FY25) to +16.8% YoY (Q3'26: $1,458M vs $1,249M)**, and gross margin fell to **75.2% in Q3'26 from 84.2% a year earlier** — royalty stacking to licensors (Ionis, Arbutus/Tekmira legacy, Dicerna/Novo) plus Amvuttra profit-share mechanics. Mix shift into cardiology caps pricing power versus the orphan franchise.
- **Regulatory, legal and policy overhang.** A Pomerantz investigation opened mid-Nov 2026 following the guidance cut (securities-litigation risk); the RNAi patent estate remains actively litigated (LNP/mRNA assertions against Pfizer and Moderna); and IRA drug-price negotiation eventually reaches high-revenue single-source drugs — a structural ceiling on the long-duration pricing that orphan economics used to guarantee.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (but narrowing)**

- **Technology leadership: unambiguous.** Alnylam is the reference siRNA platform — seven approved products, the broadest GalNAc conjugate estate, and partners (Regeneron, Roche, Novartis, Sanofi) effectively paying it to de-risk the modality. No pure-play peer matches its approval cadence.
- **Commercial execution: deteriorating.** Q3'26 growth of 16.8% is below the peer median of 15.6% only marginally, but versus its own 65% trajectory and versus Attruby's 126%, the company is now a **share donor** in its most valuable market rather than a share gainer.
- **Financial position: best-in-class for a biotech.** GAAP profitable, 23.7% Q3'26 operating margin, gross margins above the broad Healthcare average by ~17 points, and no reliance on equity issuance. This is the single strongest argument that the -62% YTD drawdown is a de-rating of expectations rather than a solvency story.
- **Key vulnerability:** revenue concentration in Amvuttra in an indication where the competitive moat is label/durability data (defensible but not exclusive) rather than exclusivity. Note Q3'26 net income of $2.17B against $346M of operating income implies ~$1.9B of non-operating gain (tax-related); **do not use the $16.19 EPS as a run-rate**.

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

**Rationale:** The RNAi modality itself is **Attractive** (early-to-mid growth, 20%+ TAM CAGR in cardiometabolic, high gross margins, platform leverage). But the *investable sector as priced today* is Neutral: the class has transitioned from an orphan-pricing oligopoly into a competitive cardiology category in under 24 months, which compresses multiples faster than revenue grows — visible in ALNY's -62% YTD and in SRPT's gross-margin/operating-margin collapse (83.2% → 59.9% GM, +11.5% → -29.9% OM). Sector-wide pricing power is now a function of data superiority, not modality novelty. The large-cap Healthcare aggregate (65.3% gross margin, ~13.5% net margin, low-single-digit growth, 2026–2030 patent cliff) is late-cycle and unexciting; the growth is confined to a handful of platform names now fighting each other.

**Watch items:** (1) Q4'26 Amvuttra script trends and whether the 2026 guidance reset holds; (2) Attruby share trajectory and Pfizer's defence of Vyndaqel; (3) any securities-litigation escalation; (4) zilebesiran and CIDEBsiRNA phase-3 readouts as the next leg of royalty value.
