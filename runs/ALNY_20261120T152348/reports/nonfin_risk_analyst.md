## ALNY — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**Alnylam Pharmaceuticals, Inc.** | Healthcare / Biotechnology | Cambridge, MA | ~$20.2B market cap | 2,500 FTE | Price $150.57 (Nov 20, 2026)

**Context:** ALNY is down ~67% from its late-2025 highs (~$450). It has absorbed two single-day collapses — **–29% on Jul 30, 2026** ($286.62 → $205.48) and **–25.1% on Oct 29, 2026** ($232.40 → $174.00) — both driven by AMVUTTRA demand deceleration and a **$200M cut to 2026 TTR guidance ($4.2–4.5B)**. Everything below should be read against that backdrop: the non-financial risk profile is deteriorating, not stable.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (negative watch)**

**Structural strengths (genuinely good):**
- **Independent Chair** — Amy W. Schulman (Managing Partner, Polaris Partners) is separate from CEO Yvonne Greenstreet. No CEO/Chair duality.
- **Board is overwhelmingly independent** — Greenstreet is the sole executive director among ~10; committee chairs are all independent.
- **Committee leadership is high-calibre and domain-relevant** — Audit chaired by Peter N. Kellogg (former Celgene CFO); Science & Technology chaired by Elliott Sigal, M.D., Ph.D. (former BMS CSO); Nominating/Governance chaired by David Pyott (former Allergan CEO); Margaret Hamburg, M.D. (former FDA Commissioner) sits on the board. This is a serious, credentialed biotech board, not a captured one.
- **Proxy filed Apr 6, 2026**; annual meeting held May 20, 2026; Class I directors elected. No dual-class structure identified.
- **Clean SOX certification** in the Q3 2026 10-Q (Exhibit 31.1) — no disclosed material weakness or significant deficiency.

**Why not "Strong":**
- **Board is old and long-tenured** — ages range 60–80 (Ausiello 80, Sigal 74, Pyott 72, Hamburg 70, Brandicourt 70, Kellogg 69). Four-plus directors at or above age 70 implies elevated near-term turnover and thin succession depth — a real vulnerability given the current crisis.
- **Insider selling pattern precedes the disclosures.** Executives and one director sold ~$18M of stock at $300–$333 in Feb–Jun 2026, *before* the July guidance cut and *before* the October demand warning. Two consecutive guidance disappointments within one quarter of heavy insider sales is the single most damaging governance fact pattern here.
- **A federal securities class action naming officers and directors is pending** (see §2). Even if ultimately meritless, it puts the board's disclosure oversight and the CEO's public assurances under scrutiny. CEO Greenstreet stated on the Jul 30 call that "our confidence in AMVUTTRA's growth trajectory has never been stronger" — nine weeks before a second cut. That quote will be Exhibit A.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **HIGH**

| Item | Status |
|---|---|
| SEC enforcement / DOJ (EDGAR full-text search) | **No actions found** — clean on this specific screen |
| Federal securities class action | **Filed.** Class period runs from **Feb 7, 2025**; alleges misleading statements on AMVUTTRA demand/growth |
| Plaintiff-firm investigations | Pomerantz LLP, Bronstein Gewirtz & Grossman, Glancy Prongay & Murray, Frank R. Cruz, Howard G. Smith, Kessler Topaz — at least 6 firms soliciting |
| FDA / product approvals | **Positive:** FDA approved **Livsayis™ (cemdisiran)** Nov 19, 2026 for gMG — 7th Alnylam-discovered RNAi approval in <9 years. But it is developed *and commercialized by Regeneron*; Alnylam receives milestones/royalties, not product revenue |
| IP litigation | Chronic, structural. Alnylam is both plaintiff and defendant in RNAi/LNP patent disputes — *Silence Therapeutics* (UK High Court), *Alnylam v. Moderna/Pfizer* (LNP patents; a 2024 D. Del. claim construction ran largely *against* Alnylam), historical *Tekmira* settlement ($65M), *University of Utah* inventorship dispute (won on summary judgment) |
| Pending binary catalysts | Pivotal readouts in Huntington's and next-gen ATTR assets; regulatory interactions on these are unresolved |

**Assessment:** The regulatory exposure is dominated by **securities litigation, not FDA risk**. The company's core regulatory relationships appear healthy — the FDA approval cadence is a genuine franchise-strength signal. The litigation is a headline and settlement-cost risk more than an existential one, but it is a multi-year overhang that will keep the disclosure narrative negative.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Single-franchise concentration is extreme.** AMVUTTRA ($1,130M) + ONPATTRO ($20M) = **~79% of Q3 2026 revenue of $1,458M**, and the TTR franchise is the entire growth thesis. The guidance cut was caused by ATTR-CM patient-start deceleration — a commercial-execution problem in the exact asset the company depends on. Diversification (gMG via Regeneron, AHP, PH1, pipeline) is real but immaterial to near-term revenue.
- **Commercial leadership vacuum at the worst possible moment.** CCO **Tolga Tanguler departed**, disclosed via 8-K on Nov 5, 2026 — six days after he publicly defended the AMVUTTRA launch on the Q3 call and one week after the guidance cut. Whether voluntary or not, losing the chief commercial officer mid-launch, in a market where Alnylam is fighting Pfizer's Vyndamax/Vyndaqel (generic protection to 2031) and Intellia's gene-editing approach, is a material key-person and execution risk. Diagnosis-rate investment and physician-facing expansion were explicitly *his* stated strategy.
- **Manufacturing/supply-chain and IP dependence.** RNAi oligonucleotide API is a specialized, concentrated supply chain with limited qualified CMOs; combined with an active patent-dispute posture across RNAi/LNP technologies, a single adverse IP ruling or supplier disruption would hit the highest-margin product line. **Cybersecurity: no disclosed breach was found in the sources reviewed, but I could not retrieve the 10-K risk factors to confirm incident history — treat this as an unverified gap, not a clean bill.**

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

The narrative has flipped from "best-in-class RNAi launch" to "management lost credibility." Specific damage vectors:
- **Two guidance cuts and two >25% single-day drops in one quarter** — the market no longer takes guidance at face value. Management's forward commentary is now discounted.
- **Six plaintiff firms and a filed class action** keep the allegations in circulation with every press release (latest Pomerantz alert Nov 19, 2026).
- **Institutional exit is visible** — Aberdeen Group cut its stake 17.7% in Q3; the Nov 2 authorization of a **$1B buyback** is itself an admission that management believes the market is mispricing the stock, but it also signals a lack of higher-return internal deployment options.
- **Competitive narrative risk:** the AstraZeneca/Ionis Wainua failure in CARDIO-TTRansform removed a competitor (positive), but that same event raised questions about TTR-knockdown depth that analysts are now applying to Alnylam's own next-gen asset.

Mitigant: no product recalls, no safety signals, no manufacturing quality events, and >90% patient adherence disclosed. The reputational damage is **financial-credibility damage, not patient-safety damage** — which is more recoverable but recovers more slowly.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical. The company remains revenue-generating and growing (+17% YoY), cash-generative, and has an independent, competent board with a real regulatory track record (7th FDA approval in nine years). But the combination of a **pending federal securities class action**, a **33:1 insider sell-to-buy ratio into two guidance cuts**, a **CCO departure six days after the miss**, **79% revenue concentration in one franchise**, and a **67% drawdown** puts this firmly in High territory. Any one of these is manageable; together they compound into a credibility crisis.

---

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Insider sales at $300–$333 (Feb–Jun 2026) immediately preceding the Jul 30 guidance cut and the Oct 29 demand warning.** The CEO's "confidence has never been stronger" statement on Jul 30 is now directly contradicted by the October cut. This is the core allegation in the class action and the highest-priority item for legal/board review. **Escalate.**
2. **Securities class action filed with a class period beginning Feb 7, 2025**, plus six firms soliciting. Requires quantification of settlement/defence-cost range and D&O coverage confirmation. **Escalate.**
3. **CCO Tolga Tanguler's departure (8-K, Nov 5, 2026)** — six days after publicly defending the launch. Confirm whether departure was voluntary; assess whether any severance/acceleration or non-disparagement terms signal cause. **Escalate.**
4. **Two guidance cuts within one quarter.** Ask management directly: what changed between Jul 30 and Oct 29, and what internal data was available at the earlier date? If patient-start data was already softening in July, the disclosure-controls question becomes serious. **Escalate.**
5. **Board age profile** — multiple directors at 70+, at precisely the moment the company needs active oversight and a CEO succession option. **Monitor.**
6. **Data gap:** 10-K risk factors and the DEF 14A could not be retrieved via the filing tool (tool returned "No 10-K found" despite the company being a long-standing SEC filer; the 10-Q returned only Exhibit 31.1). Risk-factor text on cybersecurity incidents, supplier concentration, and related-party transactions is therefore **unverified**. Recommend reading the FY2025 10-K risk factors directly before finalizing the memo.
