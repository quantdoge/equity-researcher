# Charter Communications (CHTR) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**As of: November 2026 (latest filing: 10-Q filed 11/06/2026; FY2025 10-K filed 01/30/2026)**

---

## Headline Finding

Charter has **essentially zero non-US revenue exposure** — it is a pure-play US domestic cable/broadband operator (41 states, ~57M homes/businesses passed post-Cox). There is therefore **no meaningful sanctions, FX, expropriation, or emerging-market transfer risk**. The risk that matters is almost entirely **domestic regulatory and legal**, and it has risen materially in 2026 following two transformative transactions (Cox, Liberty Broadband) and a major data breach.

Structural context: the stock has fallen from ~$172 (May 2026) to ~$103 at its October low and $134.06 on 11/10/2026, with a market cap of only **$18.1B against ~$107B of debt** — an equity that is now a thin sliver on a very large, heavily regulated asset base. Legal/regulatory outcomes are therefore highly levered to equity value.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

### Risk 1 — Franchise non-renewal and state-level regulatory conditionality (Probability: Medium-High | Impact: High)
The FY2025 10-K **newly discloses** that "some franchisors have alleged Charter failed to meet agreement requirements" — a meaningful escalation in language from the prior year. Franchises are non-exclusive and subject to non-renewal or termination, and are granted municipality-by-municipality, creating thousands of individual points of failure. Separately, the CPUC's August 13, 2026 approval came with "strong consumer protections, broadband investments, and digital equity commitments," and Charter entered **two settlement agreements in May 2026** with the California Public Advocates Office and another party (JD Supra). This establishes a template: states now extract affordability/build-out commitments as the price of approval, which is a structural drag on returns and a precedent for future transactions.

### Risk 2 — Politicised FCC enforcement / DEI two-sided exposure (Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium)
The FCC approved the Cox deal under Chairman Carr with an explicit framing: "Promotes Rural Builds, Creates Jobs, and **Protects Against DEI Discrimination**." The FCC has opened EEO/DEI investigations into peers (Disney/ABC, Comcast/NBCUniversal) and is applying pressure across telecom. Charter pre-emptively curtailed its DEI programs, which then triggered a **joint letter from the NYC Comptroller and other officials** demanding reversal. Charter is thus exposed on *both* sides: federal enforcement risk for retaining DEI policies, and municipal/state political and pension-fund pressure for removing them. Note also the **$15M FCC 911/network-outage settlement (July 2024)** — a demonstrated willingness by the FCC Enforcement Bureau to extract nine-figure-adjacent penalties, with an admitted rules violation and compliance plan.

### Risk 3 — US–China supply-chain / secure-networks exposure (Probability: Low-Medium | Impact: Low-Medium)
Network infrastructure sourcing sits inside the FCC's "rip and replace" secure-equipment regime. *Not independently verified in this pass* — flagged as a watch item rather than a quantified exposure. Of greater consequence: Charter's rural growth depends on federal subsidy programs (BEAD, Rural Digital Opportunity Fund), and the company is reportedly **pulling Cox properties out of RDOF** — subsidy-policy reversal is a live political risk to the rural build economics that underpin the Cox deal thesis.

**Also material — geopolitical-adjacent:** net neutrality. The FCC's Title II rules were struck down on appeal (agency lacked authority), which is *favourable* today, but a **state-level patchwork** (California and others) creates compliance cost and re-litigation risk on any change of administration.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Securities litigation (active):**
- ***In re Charter Communications Securities Litigation*, S.D.N.Y. No. 25-cv-06747** (Judge Lewis J. Liman), class period 7/26/2024–7/24/2025. Alleges Charter misrepresented its ability to manage the end of the FCC's **Affordable Connectivity Program (ACP)** and the resulting internet-subscriber declines, and lacked a reasonable basis for EBITDA-growth guidance. Defendants filed a **Motion to Dismiss on 3/31/2026**; briefing ongoing. This is the most direct litigation threat to current management.
- **Prior securities class action settled for $144M** with full release of claims against Charter and its officers/directors — evidence of willingness to pay to clear, and a benchmark for the current case.
- **Girard Sharp investigation notice (10/20/2026)** on behalf of former Liberty Broadband investors who received CHTR shares in the August 2026 acquisition — a potential second securities/fiduciary front arising from the Liberty combination.

**Data privacy / cyber (the most under-appreciated exposure):**
- A **May 2026 breach** — reportedly via a voice-phishing (vishing) attack on an employee's Microsoft Entra ID account on 4/1/2026 — was claimed by **ShinyHunters**, asserting ~40–42M records. haveibeenpwned reports ~4.9M unique email addresses in the leaked corpus. Charter has stated no sensitive personal information or **CPNI** was exfiltrated; that assertion is itself a litigation and regulatory risk if contradicted.
- **Class action filed 6/1/2026** (Westlaw), very early stage. Breach notification letters were sent in May 2026 (state AG postings, e.g., Massachusetts).
- Regulatory overlay: CPNI rules (FCC), state breach-notification statutes across all 41 states, CCPA/CPRA in California. Multi-state AG action is a realistic escalation path. Peer benchmark: Comcast's breach settlement received final court approval 8/20/2026.

**Regulatory approvals / antitrust:** FCC approval 2/27/2026 (party-line, 2-1 climate on related media items); CPUC approval 8/13/2026 as the final state hurdle; DOJ/state antitrust clearance obtained. Public Knowledge and other groups opposed the deal on competition grounds — **post-consummation antitrust/conduct scrutiny remains a live risk** given the combined entity's scale.

**Governance / related-party:** Cox Enterprises holds **~25.1%** with board and governance rights plus **preemptive rights** on equity issuance; Advance/Newhouse's rights were modified; Liberty Broadband's governance rights terminated. Charter is now effectively a controlled company with two large strategic holders able to dilute public shareholders — a structural minority-shareholder risk.

**Franchise/contract:** Alleged franchise-agreement non-compliance (see Risk 1). Charter also discloses that "several potential tax increases" (service taxes, income-sourcing changes, central property assessments) are live legislative risks, and spent **$2.23M on lobbying in Q3 2026 alone**.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **HIGH**

**Decomposition:**
- **Classic geopolitical risk: LOW.** No foreign revenue, no sanctions nexus, no FX/currency controls, no expropriation risk, negligible FCPA/AML surface. On the dimension most agents measure, Charter is among the least geopolitically exposed large caps.
- **Regulatory risk: HIGH.** Excessive concentration in a single regulator set — the FCC plus 1,000+ franchise authorities and multiple assertive state PUCs — amid an administration actively using telecom regulation as a political instrument (DEI, media ownership, subsidy conditions). California has demonstrated it will price approval in affordability and build-out commitments.
- **Legal risk: HIGH.** Three concurrent fronts (SDNY securities class action, May 2026 data-breach class action, Liberty Broadband investor investigation) plus a $144M settlement precedent and a $15M FCC consent decree.

**Why this nets to High rather than Medium:** the risk is not that Charter faces a hostile foreign government — it is that **~$107B of debt is serviced by a business whose returns are set by regulators and whose equity cushion ($18B market cap) is thin enough that a single adverse verdict or franchise ruling is materially dilutive.** A domestic-only profile does not mean low risk when the domestic regulator is the principal determinant of pricing power, capex obligations, and deal approval.

**Key monitoring triggers:** (1) ruling on the SDNY motion to dismiss; (2) any FCC Enforcement Bureau or multi-state AG action on the 2026 breach; (3) franchise renewal outcomes in CA/NY and any CPUC condition-compliance disputes; (4) Cox integration — subscriber losses (legacy Charter lost 156k internet subs in Q3 2026; $2.4B quarterly loss) driving further re-regulatory pressure; (5) any challenge to the Liberty Broadband share issuance.
