# Charter Communications (CHTR) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $134.06 | Market Cap ~$18.1B | EV ~$115B (pre-Cox) / ~$125B pro forma | Nov 10, 2026**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Charter is, on the evidence, the **disrupted rather than the disruptor** — a scale HFC operator whose revenue has gone from +7.5% (2021) to −0.6% (2025) to −1.5% pro forma in Q3 2026, and whose Q3 included a **$6.3B cable-franchise impairment** signaling that management itself has marked down long-term cash flows. The bull case is no longer "growth"; it is a **capex-cliff plus convergence option**: an $11.7B/yr Network Evolution (DOCSIS 4.0) build rolls off toward <$8B by 2028 exactly as $2B of self-help cost cuts and >$1B of Cox synergies land, potentially lifting FCF from ~$4.5B to $8–9B against an $18B market cap. The genuine growth engine inside the company is **Spectrum Mobile** (12.5M lines, +15.5% YoY, +508K pro forma in Q3) — a convergence product, not a network franchise.

**This is not a Cathie Wood-style growth stock.** It is a deep-value, FCF-inflection special situation that happens to be priced for terminal decline.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **Serviceable TAM:** US broadband + wireless + video/business connectivity ≈ **$300B+** revenue pool. Charter's pro forma run-rate is ~$66B revenue / ~$26.4B EBITDA — call it ~20% of the addressable pie.
- **Penetration is the problem, not the opportunity.** Charter passes **58.98M homes** and already penetrates **53.4%** — down **2.3pts YoY**. This is a *saturated* footprint, not a land-grab. The Cox deal adds ~6M passings of the same mature, contested profile.
- **The marginal TAM is being taken by someone else.** Fixed wireless access (T-Mobile/Verizon) and fiber overbuilders are winning share at a structurally lower cost-per-bit. Charter's answer is to spend $11B+/yr replicating what attackers built cheaper.
- **The one genuinely unpenetrated TAM is mobile:** 12.5M lines against ~30M connectivity relationships implies **~42% attach** — and wireless service revenue is a $200B US pool. This is the only line item compounding at a venture-like rate, and it is the correct lens for the convergence thesis.

**Differentiation verdict: weak.** Charter's moat is scale density and 100% US-based service (a real NPS/cost differentiator), not proprietary technology or data network effects. There is no innovation moat here — there is an installed base and a cost-reduction program.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Q4 2026 results — Jan 29, 2027.** The single most important print: first full quarter of Cox, first data on the $2B cost program, and 2027 guidance. Consensus EPS $9.76; watch pro forma broadband net adds vs. the ~400K FY26 loss guide.
2. **Network Evolution / DOCSIS 4.0 completion (2027).** Symmetrical multi-gig and DAA roll-off. This is the *only* credible mechanism to stop broadband share loss — and the capex cliff that funds the FCF story. First clean test: do net adds inflect positive within 2–3 quarters of market launch?
3. **$2B cost savings (18-month run-rate, ~mid-2028) + >$1B Cox synergies.** Q3 pro forma opex actually *rose* 0.2%, so essentially none of this is in the numbers yet. ~$3B against a base of ~$1.1B annualized EBITDA erosion = ~2.7x coverage — the margin-recovery thesis in one number.
4. **Capex cliff → FCF step-change.** $11.7B → <$8B by 2028 on a shrinking share count (116.9M weighted in Q3, −12.6% YoY). On <$8B capex and $26B+ EBITDA, FCF of $8–9B against an $18B cap is the entire investment case.
5. **Mobile monetization / convergence M&A.** 12.5M lines and 15%+ growth invites either MVNO-to-MNO economics or further footprint consolidation. Watch for a Comcast-style cable-wireless JV structure.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

*For a strict growth mandate, this is a Sell/avoid — it fails every gate I run: negative revenue growth, Rule of 40 of 7.5, 2.3pts of annual penetration erosion, no innovation moat, and a $6.3B admission that the franchise's long-term cash flows are worth less than carried.*

**But I cannot call it a Sell at 4.5–5x pro forma EBITDA and ~4x P/FCF.** The market has already priced terminal decline and effectively assigned zero value to the Network Evolution inflection, the $3B of self-help, or 12.5M growing mobile lines. P/B of 0.9x on an asset with 40% EBITDA margins and a 53%-penetrated, largely duopoly footprint is an asset-value floor, not a growth multiple. The asymmetry is genuinely attractive — the bull case ($250–345 per the outriders at TD Cowen/formerly RBC) is a 2–2.5x, and the bear case ($101–120 at Wells Fargo/Bernstein/Wolfe) is roughly −15%.

**Why Hold and not Buy:** the re-rating requires *proof*, and the proof is 4–6 quarters away. Q3 showed pro forma opex *rising* into a revenue decline; synergy realization was explicitly minimal; and management has just impaired the core asset. I want to see (a) pro forma broadband net adds inflect toward zero, and (b) the $2B run-rate actually show up in margins, before underwriting the FCF bridge. **Buy trigger: two consecutive quarters of improving pro forma broadband net adds alongside $400M+ of realized annualized savings — I'd expect that setup to emerge mid-to-late 2027.**

## 7. Key Risks

**Execution (highest severity).** The entire thesis is a self-help bridge: $3B of savings against ~$1.1B of annualized EBITDA erosion. Q3 evidence was adverse — costs grew while revenue fell. If savings slip or are reinvested into price to defend share (residential ARPU already −1.8% YoY to $117.52; broadband ARPU $70.16 and falling), the FCF bridge fails and the 2028 $8–9B FCF becomes ~$6B.

**Competitive / structural.** FWA and fiber overbuild are the real disruptors, and Charter's $11B/yr defensive capex is a *margin* problem no cost program fully offsets. The impairment says management expects lower franchise cash flows — I should take that seriously rather than dismissing it as non-cash.

**Balance sheet.** Roughly **$115–125B of pro forma debt/EV against $18B of equity** — leverage near ~4.5–5x. In a flat-to-declining revenue business, that is the single largest destroyer of equity value: small EBITDA misses translate into large equity moves, and refinancing $5B+ of annual interest at higher coupons is a persistent headwind (interest expense $1.34B/quarter and rising).

**Integration.** Cox closed Aug 20, 2026 into a deteriorating demand backdrop, with franchise-impairment risk on acquired assets and known legacy operating issues (LA Times reported tensions as the deal closed). The $34.5B price tag for a shrinking business is a capital-allocation risk I cannot underwrite.

**Estimate/consensus risk.** Sell-side models are visibly stale (FY27 revenue below FY25 pre-Cox), so "beats" and "misses" will be noise for several quarters. Price target dispersion of $101–$345 in the same month (November) is the widest I've seen — **treat all consensus figures as unreliable.**

**Data gaps:** Pro forma net debt and fully diluted post-Liberty-Broadband-merger share count are my estimates (EV/EBITDA ~4.5–5.0x), not reported figures; I could not retrieve the primary Q3 press release (403 on ir.charter.com) and relied on secondary reporting of the $6.5B pro forma revenue / $6.6B EBITDA / $6.3B impairment detail. Cox-adjusted subscriber comps prior to Q3 2026 are unavailable.

---

**Bottom line:** A structurally challenged incumbent trading at a distressed multiple with a real, datable FCF inflection in 2027–28. **Hold as an asymmetric option; do not buy it as growth.**
