### 1. Consumer Demand Signals — **Deteriorating (core), stabilizing (funnel)**

**Google Trends — "Charter Spectrum internet" (US, 12m, weekly):**
- Last 4 weeks (Oct 11–Nov 1): 61, 52, 55, 52 → avg **55**
- Prior 4 weeks (Sep 13–Oct 4): 63, 54, 57, 69 → avg **61**
- Jul–Aug peak: 75, **100** (wk of Jul 19), 79, 77 → avg **71**

Read: search interest is **~20% below the July/August peak**, but that peak was an event artifact — Spectrum pricing/branding rolled into legacy Cox markets on Sept 16, 2026, generating a one-time search surge. Normalized against the Feb–Jun 2026 baseline of ~50–56, current interest (~52–55) is **flat-to-slightly-down, not collapsing**. Verdict: search demand is **In-Line with the muted baseline**, with no evidence of an inflection to broadband growth.

**Competitive share-of-search (key cross-check):** "Xfinity internet" collapsed from an index of 100 (Aug 9) to **13–20** in Oct–Nov, while Spectrum held 44–69. Relative share-of-search has therefore flipped *in Charter's favor* over the last 8 weeks. This is a genuinely positive relative read — but I flag it with low confidence, as a decline of that magnitude in a competitor's branded search looks partly like a data artifact rather than pure share shift.

**Web traffic (spectrum.com, SimilarWeb public snippets):** Traffic comparison pages confirm spectrum.com remains the #1 or #2 US ISP site (behind/around xfinity.com) with ~8% last-month change. Engagement quality is weak: **2.8 pages/visit and 58.5% bounce rate** vs Google Fiber's 4.36 pages and 32% bounce — consistent with a price-shopping/comparison funnel rather than a high-intent purchase funnel. No monthly visit series retrievable (API-key gated).

**Hard fundamental corroboration:** Q3 2026 pro forma internet losses of **233,000** (legacy Charter −156k vs −109k a year earlier — the loss rate *widened* YoY; legacy Cox −77k). Pro forma revenue −1.5% YoY, adj. EBITDA −4%. Management named **T-Mobile fixed wireless** as a primary competitive drain.

### 2. Business Activity Signals — **Defensive / contraction-mode**

**Hiring:** Indeed shows ~2,080 Charter postings (105 in FL, 25 Orlando, 17 Denver); LinkedIn ~9,000+. **No historical trend series available**, so direction cannot be measured. However, the strategic read is unambiguous: management launched a leaner operating model targeting **$2B annualized run-rate opex savings over 18 months**, plus >$1B of incremental Cox synergies, via "removing management layers" and "dropping projects that don't help Internet growth." That is a **hiring-contraction signal**, not a growth-investment signal. Notably, cuts are explicitly *not* targeted at frontline service/sales — i.e., protecting the customer-acquisition engine, consistent with the turnaround framing.

**Patents:** USPTO PatentsView feed **failed** (parse error). No data. For a cable MSO this is a low-value signal anyway (capex and network spend matter far more than patent counts).

### 6. Overall Alt Data Signal: **BEARISH**
On fundamentals, every forward-looking feed points the same way: flat-to-declining search demand, widening YoY subscriber loss rates, a defensive cost-cutting posture, an impairment that writes down the acquired franchise, and a Q4 consensus that is only rising because of accounting consolidation. The only constructive signals are (a) relative share-of-search vs Xfinity, (b) $1.5B deleveraging, and (c) retail sentiment already priced for bankruptcy — the last two argue about *valuation floor*, not about *demand inflection*.

### 7. Confidence Level: **MEDIUM**
Strengths: two independent demand proxies (Trends + traffic) corroborate the hard subscriber data; competitive cross-check adds a differentiated read; earnings-surprise history is clean and long enough to be informative.
Gaps: **patent feed failed** (no data); job postings and social sentiment are **qualitative scrapes with no historical trend**, so direction is inferred from management commentary rather than measured; web traffic is snippet-level, not a monthly series; Google Trends captures only branded search, not wholesale/enterprise or mobile attach. Price and news data terminate **Nov 10, 2026** — anything since is unobserved.

---

**Data limitations & lag:** Google Trends is weekly and normalizes to its own peak (index, not volume) — cross-brand comparisons are relative only. Web traffic and job-posting feeds are search-scraped proxies, not API-grade series. Q3 losses are pro forma and Cox timing distorts YoY comparability. Alt data is a leading indicator, not a definitive one — and for a subscription business with a 1–2% monthly churn rate, search interest leads reported net adds by roughly one quarter, meaning any stabilization from the Sept 16 Spectrum-in-Cox-markets pricing event would not show in subscriber figures until **Q1 2027 (April 2027 print)** — after the Jan 29 catalyst.
