# CHARTER COMMUNICATIONS (CHTR) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**As of:** November 11, 2026 | **Price:** $134.06 | **Mkt cap:** ~$18.1B | **Employees:** 91,900 | **CEO:** Christopher L. Winfrey

**Context:** Charter has been fundamentally reshaped in 2026 — closing the ~$34.5B Cox Communications acquisition (Aug 20, 2026) and the Liberty Broadband merger (effective Aug 19, 2026) within days of each other, expanding to 45 states and adding ~$12.4B of assumed Cox net debt. The stock fell from $171.74 (May 1) to a low of $102.18 (Oct 26, −40%) before recovering to $134.06 — roughly half its value lost over the trailing year. The non-financial risk profile has deteriorated materially alongside it.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

**Evidence of concern:**
- **Ownership concentration is now extreme.** Cox Enterprises disclosed a **27.9% beneficial stake** in Charter Class A and holds **three board seats**; Cox's chief executive **Alexander C. Taylor became Board Chairman**. Charter, Cox Enterprises and Advance/Newhouse entered an **amended and restated stockholders' agreement** granting preemptive rights. Two strategic holders plus a contractual agreement is a control bloc that can dictate outcomes for minority holders.
- **Board reconstitution by merger, not by election.** Dallas Clement and Mark Greatrex (Cox) and Wade Davis (Jan 27, 2026) joined; Eric Zinterhofer moved from Non-Executive Chairman to Lead Independent Director. Shareholders have not yet voted on this board.
- **CFO departure at the worst possible moment.** Jessica Fischer stepped down **October 15, 2026** — eight weeks after closing two transformative deals and three weeks before the Q3 10-Q disclosed a $6.33B impairment. Kevin D. Howard was appointed **Interim** CFO (8-K, Aug 30, 2026) and remains interim.
- **The "Director Emeritus" arrangement.** Former CEO Thomas Rutledge holds a non-standard Director Emeritus/Executive Director role while selling down to 3,968 shares — an unusual governance artifact that blurs management/board lines.
- **Compensation reset at the trough.** Large option awards were issued Aug 20, 2026 at $0 exercise (Winfrey, DiGeronimo 210,860, Fischer 134,591, Haughton 71,782), plus a 436,877-option grant to new COO Nick Jeffery (Sept 1). Granting mega-equity immediately post-merger risks rewarding integration optics rather than durable per-share value.

**Mitigants:** Chairman and CEO roles are separated; a Lead Independent Director exists; and four insiders (including the CEO) bought stock with personal cash in April–May 2026 — a genuine, if losing, alignment signal.

**Verdict:** Structurally sound on paper, but substantively weakened by concentrated strategic ownership, a board reshaped by deal, and finance leadership churn.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **No SEC enforcement actions surfaced** in EDGAR full-text search (empty result) — a clean read, though the search tool is not exhaustive.
- **Securities litigation/investigations are active.** Girard Sharp LLP and Levin Law are investigating claims for former Liberty Broadband holders who received CHTR at a 0.236 exchange ratio; CHTR fell from $147.76 (Aug 20 close) to $105.14 (Oct 14), −29% in under two months, following pre-merger disclosures of 172,000 Q2 internet subscriber losses and −4.3% adjusted EBITDA. A federal securities fraud class action (KTM&C) is also pending. Historical precedent matters: Charter settled an accounting-related shareholder class action for **$144M**, another for $87.5M (2023).
- **FCC/state enforcement history:** **$15M FCC settlement (July 2024)** for 911 and network-outage reporting violations, with an admitted violation and compliance plan. Connecticut AG extracted **consumer protection commitments** as a condition of the Charter/Cox merger (Jan 28, 2026) — evidence that state regulators now impose behavioral conditions.
- **Privacy/cyber exposure is the live issue.** See §5.
- **Lobbying:** $2.23M disclosed for Q3 2026 — Charter is actively defending broadband/spectrum policy positions. Sector-wide net neutrality remains legally unsettled (Sixth Circuit vacated FCC rules), creating regulatory whiplash risk on Title II classification.

**Data gap:** The 10-K and DEF 14A could not be retrieved (tool error / SEC 403), so I could not verify board independence ratios, committee composition, or compensation metrics directly.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Cox integration failure risk (highest severity).** Two multi-billion mergers closed within 48 hours; a new operating model launched Nov 6 targeting **$2–3B of cost cuts** while simultaneously restructuring the commercial organization, integrating a ~$12.4B-debt asset, and migrating customers onto Spectrum systems. Cost-cutting of this magnitude concurrent with integration is a classic recipe for service degradation, billing errors, and employee attrition at a 91,900-person organization.
- **Cybersecurity — already realized.** In April 2026 ShinyHunters compromised an employee's **Microsoft Entra ID account via voice phishing (vishing)** and exfiltrated data later published in May. HIBP counts **4.9M unique emails**; attackers claimed **40–42M records**; independent analysis suggests ~13M individuals plus ~27,000 employees. This is an identity-layer/MFA control failure — and the company has since pivoted to selling "Secure Networking" to enterprises, making its own breach a direct brand liability. Regulatory consequences under state breach laws and SEC cyber disclosure rules remain open.
- **Subscriber/business-model erosion and network-investment risk.** 233,000 internet losses in Q3 (after 172,000 in Q2) against fixed-line competition from wireless 5G home broadband. Defending share requires capex that conflicts with the leverage-reduction and cost-cut narrative, and the $6.33B franchise impairment is an admission that the acquired asset base is worth materially less than paid.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **High**

- **Customer experience:** Charter/Spectrum has a long history of labor conflict (the multi-year IBEW Local 3 NYC strike, NLRB complaints including Case 20-RD-377081) and persistent "worst-in-industry" satisfaction perception. A cost-driven restructuring at the same moment as an integration is likely to amplify complaint volume and churn.
- **Data breach optics are poor:** an unpatched human-layer vulnerability, extortion ("pay-or-leak"), leaked customer names/addresses/phones, and a disputed record count. Expect continued negative coverage and consumer-class-action filings.
- **Investor-relations credibility:** the −29% post-merger collapse and the law-firm "investigation notice" drumbeat (four separate GlobeNewswire releases in October alone) create a persistent negative-news cycle that management cannot control.
- **ESG/consumer-advocacy:** state AG conditions in Connecticut signal Charter is now a named target for consumer-protection activism.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Four of the six risk dimensions are elevated simultaneously — governance (concentrated strategic control, CFO churn), regulatory (active securities investigations), operational (dual integration + realized breach), and reputational (labor, service, breach) — and they are **correlated, not independent**: the merger created the governance concentration, the impairment, the litigation, and the integration/cyber exposure at once.

Not rated *Critical* because: there is no restatement, going-concern qualification, or SEC enforcement action; Chairman/CEO roles are separated; the breach involved identity data rather than core network control; and insiders (notably the CEO) did buy with personal capital. But the margin to Critical is thin, and it narrows sharply if the interim CFO becomes permanent by default or if a formal securities class action is filed over the Liberty Broadband disclosures.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **CFO vacancy (interim) during peak integration and a $6.33B impairment.** Unresolved, unfilled finance leadership at the exact moment accounting judgments (purchase accounting, franchise valuation) are most contestable. *Escalate — request the Board's CFO succession timeline.*
2. **$6.33B franchise impairment one quarter after paying ~$34.5B for Cox.** Implies the deal's underwriting assumptions were wrong within 90 days. *Escalate — query auditors on goodwill/intangible valuation triggers and any further impairment testing.*
3. **Securities fraud investigations + pending class action over pre-merger subscriber disclosures.** *Escalate — obtain D&O disclosure and litigation-reserve detail; assess whether disclosure controls were adequate.*
4. **~$295M of insider selling versus ~$3.8M of buying, with zero insider purchases since May 15 and the ex-CEO effectively fully exited.** *Escalate — the buying window has closed without follow-through.*
5. **Material cyber breach (~4.9M confirmed, 40M+ claimed) from an MFA/vishing failure.** *Escalate — confirm whether this was disclosed as material under Item 1.05 of Form 8-K and quantify state AG/FCC exposure.*
6. **Related-party and control risk from Cox's 27.9% + Chairman + $6.0B preferred equity + stockholders' agreement, alongside Advance/Newhouse preemptive rights.** *Escalate — related-party transaction review; confirm minority-holder protections.*
7. **Leverage at the ratings cliff.** S&P tightened Charter's downgrade threshold to 4.25x debt/EBITDA and cut Cox to BB+; Moody's downgraded Cox senior unsecured to Ba1; Fitch downgraded Cox while affirming Charter. *Escalate — a downgrade raises refinancing cost just as buybacks and cost cuts compete for cash.*
