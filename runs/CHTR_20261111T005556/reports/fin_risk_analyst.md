### 1. Credit Risk Assessment
**Rating: Lowest‑Rung Investment Grade (BBB−/BB+ crossover) — deteriorating, and effectively Sub‑Investment Grade on an issue‑level basis.**

- S&P **affirmed** Charter but **tightened its debt/EBITDA downgrade threshold by 0.25x to 4.25x** (per S&P, reflecting Cox's weaker business). Fitch **affirmed** Charter while downgrading Cox; CCO Holdings senior unsecured sits at **BB+ / RR4** — i.e. the largest unsecured bond class is already speculative grade because of secured-debt seniority. Moody's downgraded Cox senior unsecured to **Ba1** on assumption by Charter. Source: S&P/Fitch/Moody's actions (Aug–Sep 2026).
- Leverage is **at/above the 4.25x downgrade trigger**: FY25 standalone net debt/EBITDA **4.56x** (tool), pro forma post‑Cox **~3.9–4.0x** on management/rating-agency EBITDA, but that EBITDA is *falling* (Q2'26 adj. EBITDA −4.3% y/y to $5.4bn; revenue −1.7%).
- Interest coverage is the binding constraint: **operating income/interest = 2.65x**; EBITDA covers interest only **4.2x**; after capex, EBITDA − capex = $9.55bn vs $5.04bn interest = **1.89x**.
- **Structural note:** equity is $20.5bn against $97.6bn goodwill/intangibles → **tangible net worth is deeply negative (~−$77bn)**; retained earnings −$5.4bn after years of buybacks. This is a normal cable structure, not fraud risk, but it means creditor protection rests entirely on EBITDA durability, not asset cover.
- **Mitigant:** post‑Cox weighted average debt life **11.1 years** — no refinancing cliff. This is materially better than a typical levered cable LBO and is the single strongest credit positive.

### 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue −20%

Applied to FY2025 base, assuming ~45% contribution margin on lost revenue (programming scales, fixed network/SGA does not) and no capex flex beyond $1.7bn:

| Line | Base | Revenue −20% | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $54.77bn | $43.82bn | −$10.95bn |
| EBITDA | $21.21bn | **~$16.3bn** | −23% |
| D&A | $8.71bn | $8.71bn | — |
| EBIT | $12.50bn | **~$7.6bn** | −39% |
| Interest | $5.04bn | $5.04bn | — |
| **EBIT / interest** | **2.48x** | **~1.5x** | ⚠️ approaches distress |
| **Net debt / EBITDA** | **4.56x** | **~5.9x** (≈5.5x pro forma) | ⚠️ ~1.3x through trigger |
| FCF | $4.42bn | **~$0.2bn** | ≈ zero |

**Consequences:** the 4.25x downgrade threshold is breached by >1 turn → multi‑notch downgrade to BB territory; all $5.1bn of FY25 buybacks halted immediately; deleveraging to the 3.5x target becomes arithmetically impossible without asset sales or an equity raise.

**Equity sensitivity (the critical point):** at $16.3bn EBITDA, EV at 5.5x = $89.5bn vs $96.6bn net debt → **equity = $0 (wiped out)**. At a distressed‑cable 6.5x, equity = ~$9.2bn ≈ **$68/share (−49%)**. At 6.0x, equity ≈ **$8/share**. Because EV/EBITDA is only **5.4x** today on a 4.56x levered balance sheet, the equity is a thin residual: **±0.5x of multiple or ±$1bn of EBITDA moves equity ±25–50%.** The 23.9% FCF yield is not a margin of safety — it is the market pricing exactly this fragility.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical: no maturity wall (11.1y WAL), positive and substantial FCF, 38.7% EBITDA margins, F‑Score 7, no dividend obligation, and secured debt still investment grade. The company will not default in a normal downturn.

But High is warranted on: 4.56x standalone leverage with **EBITDA now declining**, 2.65x coverage, $477m cash against $97bn debt, tangible equity deeply negative, a rating trajectory (threshold tightened to 4.25x) that points to a downgrade to sub‑investment grade, and an equity cushion so thin that a −20% revenue scenario wipes out shareholders while creditors take control of the narrative.

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Debt‑funded buybacks / returns exceeding FCF** — FY25 FCF $4.42bn vs $5.13bn repurchases; total debt still rose to $97.1bn. Shareholder returns were partly debt‑financed into a declining business. Aggravating factor: equity issuance is now effectively unavailable at 0.88x book.
2. **Leverage rising into an EBITDA decline** — the Cox deal (+~$12bn assumed debt, closed 20 Aug 2026) raised debt to **$109bn principal** while Q2'26 EBITDA fell 4.3%. Growth‑by‑acquisition used to mask organic subscriber losses.
3. **Rating threshold now 4.25x vs ~4.0x leverage and falling EBITDA** — a ~10% EBITDA decline breaches it. Crossover to BB+ is the base case, not the tail.
4. **Accelerating broadband subscriber losses** — S&P projects ~400k legacy Charter broadband losses in 2026 *and* 2027 (1–2% of base); Q2'26 net losses 172,000. Structural, not cyclical — fixed wireless and fibre are taking share permanently.
5. **Interest cost is 23.8% of EBITDA** and ~114% of pre‑capex cash flow after capex is deducted before interest; every 100bp on $109bn ≈ $1.1bn of EBITDA, or 5% of it.
6. **No near-term refinancing cliff, but no liquidity buffer either** — $477m cash. Real liquidity is the revolver; **exact revolver commitment, covenant terms and the full maturity ladder are data gaps I could not verify** (S&P page returned 403; balance sheet data ends FY25, pre‑Cox).
7. **Data gaps to flag:** no FY2026 full‑year statements available (only Q2/Q3'26 press figures), no pro‑forma post‑Cox balance sheet, no CDS spreads, no Sortino, and no verified covenant package.

**Bottom line for the investment memo:** CHTR is a levered, cash‑generative but structurally shrinking asset whose equity has become an option on cable EBITDA stabilizing. Credit is one notch from sub‑investment grade; equity has already lost ~78% and remains highly convex to small EBITDA revisions in both directions. Position sizing should reflect Option‑like payoff, not a value‑stock payoff.
