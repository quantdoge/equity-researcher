## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT — but not on fundamentals**

This is the critical distinction for construction. **The disagreement is about price, not about the business.**

**Consensus (11/11 agree):**
- The franchise is structurally impaired. FWA + fibre overbuild broke the local-monopoly economics; the $6.33B impairment is management's own admission that long-term cash flows are worth less than carried.
- Revenue and EBITDA are now shrinking organically (−1.5% / −4.0% pro forma Q3-26) while costs rose.
- Leverage (~4.0–4.8x) against a falling EBITDA base makes the equity a thin residual (~14–16% of EV).
- The savings plan ($2B opex + >$1B Cox synergies) is a target, not a result — Q3 opex actually *rose*.

**Where they split — and it is a 9-vs-2 split in the wrong direction for a buyer:**

| Camp | Agents | Argument |
|---|---|---|
| **Do not own at $134** | Sector, Geo Legal, Macro, Value, Quant, Alt Data, ESG, Fin Risk | The multiple is fair not cheap on an enterprise basis; the 24% FCF yield is a leverage artifact; a 0.8x EV/EBITDA derating wipes the equity |
| **Do not short at $134** | Short, (Growth sympathises) | Bear case is fully consensus; 15–20% short interest; no near-term debt catalyst; real optionality in the capex cliff |

**Synthesis: both camps land in the same place — CHTR is in "no-edge territory" at $134.** It is too levered and too structurally challenged to buy as a value stock; too cheap and too squeeze-prone to short. The specialists who did the deepest work (Value, Short, Fin Risk) each independently describe the same object: **a levered call option on cable EBITDA stabilising, priced near its strike.**

**Fraud/forensic check — resolved in CHTR's favour.** The short analyst's M-Score (−2.65), accruals (−7.2%) and cash conversion (3.22x) all clear. The bear case is *cash-flow durability*, not accounting. Per my framework, the fraud flag does **not** take precedence — but the balance-sheet and governance flags do.

**Flags that override the valuation argument (per my conflict-resolution rule):**
1. **Rating cliff.** S&P tightened the downgrade threshold to 4.25x vs ~4.0x pro forma leverage *with EBITDA falling*. A ~10% EBITDA decline breaches it. Sub-investment-grade crossover is the base case, not the tail.
2. **CFO vacancy.** Interim CFO during peak integration and impairment — an accounting-judgment vacuum at the worst possible moment.
3. **Insider signal.** ~99% sell-side by value; no purchase since May. The April cluster buy at $172–175 is deeply underwater and unrepeated.
4. **Wipeout convexity.** Fin Risk's −20% revenue scenario puts equity at ~$0. That is not a −40% bear case; it is a total-loss tail.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation

**Core recommendation: 0.0% of a diversified portfolio. Pass.**

Where an opportunistic/deep-value sleeve exists: **0.5% of total NAV**, hard-capped.

| **Long/short book** | **0% net** | Short analyst: pair against a fibre/FWA beneficiary rather than hold standalone; express short only above ~$220–240 |

**Kelly logic — why not larger.** Probability-weighted value across the agents' scenarios: bull $300 (20%), base $170 (40%), bear $85 (30%), wipeout ~$20 (10%) → **~$155, or +16% vs $134**. Against 59% annualised volatility, a Sharpe of −0.71 and a fat left tail (single-day gaps of −22%, −12%, −9% in 2026), full-Kelly sizing is negative. The correct treatment is **fractional-Kelly at ≤0.5%**, treating the position as a lottery ticket, not a value holding. A 0.5% position contributes ~0.30% to portfolio volatility and ≤0.5% to NAV at total loss — tolerable. A 2–3% position would contribute 1.2–1.8% portfolio vol for a name with 0.001 R² to the market, i.e. **undiversifiable** risk.

**The beta trap — do not size this as a low-beta name.** Three methods give beta of −0.10, +0.12 and +0.73. **Correlation summary for construction:** the name adds *undiversifiable* idiosyncratic risk. Its only genuine diversifier property — near-zero correlation to SPY — is precisely the property that makes it unusable as a hedge.

I should state the strongest case against my own conclusion: **the short analyst and growth analyst are right that the bear case is the consensus and the price already discounts most of it.** At 4.6x EBITDA with 11.1-year money at 5.3%, no maturity wall, 12.5M growing mobile lines and ~$3B of identified cost-out, this is a legitimate asymmetric option. Short interest of 15–20% of float amplifies that.

That is why this is a **PASS, not a SELL or a SHORT**. If I am wrong, I forgo a 2x on a 0.5% position — immaterial to portfolio outcomes. If I am right and size it at 2–3%, a wipeout scenario costs 2–3% of NAV for a name whose downside is set by the bond market. **The asymmetry of the *decision* (not the security) favours passing.**
