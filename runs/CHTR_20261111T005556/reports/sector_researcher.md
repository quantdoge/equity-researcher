# Sector Brief: Charter Communications (CHTR) — US Cable / Converged Telecom

**Note on methodology:** The vendor "Communication Services" aggregate is dominated by Alphabet and Meta (FY25 aggregated revenue $2.48T, avg gross margin 47%), which is not a meaningful reference set for a cable operator. I therefore built a purpose-built peer set of US telecom/cable operators (Comcast, Verizon, AT&T, T-Mobile). Data as of 10-Nov-2026.

---

## 1. Sector Overview

US cable/broadband is a **mature, ex-growth infrastructure sector now in a share-shift phase** rather than a growth phase. Cable has held ~65% of US fixed broadband connections for a decade, but fixed-wireless access (FWA) and fibre overbuild have ended the era of structural unit growth: coaxial connections fell from 79.3M to 74.5M locations (Jun-2021 to Jun-2025) while fibre rose from 21.1M, and major cable providers shed an estimated ~280k broadband subs in Q1-2026 alone. Revenue has decoupled from units — the peer set grew FY25 revenue at a median of only **+2.5%**, entirely from price/ARPU and mobile, not broadband volume. The sector is highly concentrated (top-3 cable operators control the large majority of cable broadband; Charter alone serves 34.3M internet customers post-Cox) with very high barriers to entry (capital intensity, franchising) — but FWA has lowered the effective entry barrier on the product customers actually value. This is a **late-cycle, deleveraging-and-cost-cutting phase**: the value driver has shifted from subscriber growth to FCF discipline, and management teams are being paid for cash conversion, not volume.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Self-help cost and capex cycle.** Charter targets **$2B annualised opex savings over 18 months** plus **>$1B of Cox synergies**, while pro-forma quarterly capex already fell to $3.2B from $3.6B, with a further **~$2.5B capex reduction planned** over 2–3 years. Every incremental dollar of capex reduction flows almost directly to FCF given ~38–40% adj. EBITDA margins.
- **Mobile convergence is the only real growth line.** Charter added **508k pro forma mobile lines in Q3-26** against 233k broadband losses — a favourable mix shift because mobile is delivered via MVNO (low marginal capex) and raises bundle stickiness. AT&T/Verizon/T-Mobile are all executing the same convergence playbook, and it is working at the revenue line even where broadband is not.
- **Deleveraging + distressed valuation optionality.** A **$5.5B debt swap cut the debt balance by $1.5B**, keeping leverage on plan at just under 4x (management basis; statistical D/EBITDA ~4.8x). At **5.9x EV/EBITDA and 3.3x forward earnings**, capital return or accretive consolidation is highly levered to any re-rating; buybacks at these prices are mathematically potent even at modest volumes ($19M in Q3).

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **FWA and fibre substitution is accelerating, not plateauing.** Charter's pro-forma internet losses of **233k in Q3-26** (156k legacy vs 109k a year earlier; 77k Cox) are worsening on a like-for-like basis, driven explicitly by fixed wireless, fibre overlap, mobile-only substitution and competitor promotions. There is no disclosed timeline for a return to broadband growth.
- **Price/promo war compresses ARPU and revenue.** Pro-forma revenue **fell 1.5% to $16.5B** and pro-forma adj. EBITDA **fell 4.0% to $6.6B** in Q3-26; Spectrum pricing had to be re-launched into Cox markets (Sept-2026), implying market-share defence via price. Advertising is also a drag.
- **Leverage and interest burden cap equity value.** FY25 interest expense was **$5.04B** against $21.2B EBITDA (**interest coverage 2.53x**, the weakest in the peer set). Q3-26 FCF fell **$947M YoY to $674M**. The **$6.3B franchise impairment** is the clearest signal that the market value of the legacy asset base has fallen — a structural, not cyclical, warning. Video/programming cost inflation (content owners hold pricing power) squeezes the remaining margin.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Average, and weakening**

- **Strong:** Unmatched scale in US cable broadband (34.3M internet customers, 41 states); highest operating margin in the peer set (24.4%); 40% adj. EBITDA margin; tangible self-help levers ($3B+ combined opex and Cox synergy savings); and a valuation that already discounts substantial continued erosion.
- **Weak:** It is the **share loser** in the sector's key growth product, with losses worsening YoY on a pro-forma basis; unlike VZ/T/TMUS it owns **no wireless network** (MVNO-dependent), so convergence economics are structurally inferior; leverage (4.8x) and 2.5x interest coverage leave the least strategic flexibility; and the $6.3B impairment signals the installed base is worth materially less than carried.
- **Verdict:** Scale and margin leadership protect cash flow, but Charter is a **price-taker in a market where the marginal subscriber is now won on price** — it has the sector's worst unit trends with the sector's most levered balance sheet. The Cox deal buys cost synergies and market position but does not fix the substitution problem.

## 6. Sector Rating: **Unattractive** (US cable broadband); **Neutral** for converged telecom

The cable broadband subsector is late-cycle, structurally challenged on units, and engaged in a price war against subsidised wireless attackers with lower capital intensity. Cash flows remain large and durable, but the terminal-value debate is unresolved and the burden of proof has shifted to management execution on cost cuts and broadband stabilisation. Converged national wireless operators (TMUS, VZ, T) screen better on relative unit momentum and balance sheet, though they too trade as low-growth bond proxies. Within this unattractive subsector, CHTR is the **highest-risk, highest-optionality name**: a 3.3x forward P/E and 5.9x EV/EBITDA offer genuine asymmetric upside if broadband losses trough — but nothing in the Q3-26 data yet supports that inflection.
