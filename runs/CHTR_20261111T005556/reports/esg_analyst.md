### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW–MEDIUM**

- No material Scope 1 exposure (fleet, backup diesel/gas); the real footprint is **purchased electricity** for the HFC/network and data-centre estate. Third-party trackers (TM Forum, 2024) note Charter *does not disclose a renewable-energy ratio*, versus Comcast at 7% — i.e. weak disclosure vs peers.
- Climate target: shareholder resolutions (NY State Common Retirement Fund 2019; Ceres) pressed for sustainability reporting and GHG reduction targets. Reporting has improved modestly, but I could **not verify an SBTi‑validated or net‑zero target** in the public record reviewed — treat as a disclosure gap, not a greenwashing finding.
- Scope 3 (customer premises equipment, purchased video content, construction contractors) is essentially unreported — the bulk of true footprint.
- **Physical risk is the more real E factor**: the Cox acquisition added Gulf Coast/Florida/Southwest plant, raising hurricane and wildfire restoration opex. This shows up in capex volatility, not in carbon metrics.
- Environmental penalties in the record are trivial ($3.4M, 4 records; Cox CA hazardous-waste $3.3M, 2018) — immaterial.

**Verdict: E is not a valuation driver. Score it as disclosure-laggard, financially immaterial.**

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Consumer protection is the defining social issue.** Violation Tracker: **$306.7M total penalties since 2000 across 109 records**, concentrated in consumer protection ($243.5M / 19 records). Headline items: **NY AG $174.2M (2018)** for misrepresenting internet speeds; CA multi-agency $18.8M (2020); Cox AZ AG $13.0M (2024); NY PSC $13.0M (2017); FCC $15M (Jul‑24) for 911/network-outage reporting failures; WA AG $1.0M.
- **Labour:** 35 employment-related records, $27.5M — dominated by wage-and-hour settlements ($18M federal 2010; repeated smaller cases). IBEW Local 3 ran a ~4-year strike (2017–2020) at legacy TWC; NLRB unfair-labour-practice findings and union-decertification disputes persist. Cox integration has begun with **~1,200 corporate layoffs** (~1% of workforce) and further integration cuts implied by the $2–3B cost programme — execution/safety risk during network integration.
- **Workplace safety:** cable technicians work at height and near live power; reported electrocution fatalities of Spectrum linemen, plus OSHA citations (2014–2024, small dollar). Contractor screening is a live liability — a Texas jury returned a combined **$7.37B verdict** (robbery/murder by a cable installer) against Charter/Spectrum; expect post-trial reduction/appeal, but the reputational and contractor-vetting exposure is real.
- **Affordability:** ACP ended Jun‑24, removing a federal $30/mo subsidy from a chunk of the base; Spectrum Internet Assist ($25/mo) is thin relative to peers. With broadband subscriber losses (−233k internet subs in Q3‑26) and political attention on connectivity pricing, this is a live regulatory-risk channel.
- Composite social picture: **High** risk, mostly driven by conduct/consumer issues rather than workforce.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10**

Rationale (worst-in-class on capital allocation and shareholder alignment):

1. **Control concentration without economic ownership.** Advance/Newhouse holds ~14.35% (18.6M shares) under agreements capping its stake at 19% and voting at 15%; the completed Cox deal installed **Alex Taylor (Cox family) as Chairman**, with Eric Zinterhofer as lead independent director — i.e. the chair is *not* independent, and two family/legacy holder blocs shape the board. Post‑Liberty Broadband absorption, the Malone‑era ownership architecture has been re-cut rather than simplified.
2. **Capital allocation is the core failure.** Years of debt-funded buybacks at $400–600/share, now $134; a **$34.5B Cox deal** followed immediately by a **$6.33B franchise impairment** in Q3‑26 — a self-evidenced overpayment — and leverage being pushed back toward ~3.5x only via a $2–3B cost-out that is itself a social risk. Value destruction of this scale on a levered balance sheet raises cost of capital directly.
3. **Controls failure.** SEC settled charges (Nov‑23, **$25M**) that Charter lacked internal accounting controls over stock repurchases executed under 10b5‑1 plans; two of five commissioners dissented. A controls failure located precisely in buyback execution is a serious governance signal.
4. **Compensation optics.** CEO Winfrey's 2023 package was **~$89M** (mega-grant year) versus ~$15.6M in 2022 — granted before the shareholder-value collapse. 2026 say-on-pay passed, but multi-year awards and large annual grants in a period of −40% TSR invite misalignment critiques.
5. **Shareholder rights are functional but narrow.** Say-on-pay passes; shareholder proposals fail decisively (2026 political-expenditure disclosure: ~18% support, 23.3M for vs 104.9M against, *down* from 25.5% prior year; other proposals 27–32%). No disclosed ESG-linked debt covenants.

G (3/10) is the single largest ESG drag: this is a governance-discount story, not a sustainability story.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US (dominant, ~100% of revenue):** FCC — Title VI franchise/certification, CPNI privacy rules, network-outage and 911 reporting, broadband consumer labels, Title II/net-neutrality oscillation, BEAD/subsidy programme design. State PUCs and local franchise authorities (NY PSC precedent of a $13M penalty) control renewal — a genuine licence-to-operate lever.
- **State AGs and privacy regimes:** CCPA/CPRA and ~15 state privacy laws treat ISP browsing data as sensitive (opt-in), a materially stricter standard than for edge providers; multi-state AG enforcement is routine for this name.
- **SEC:** climate-disclosure rule remains stayed; the operative SEC exposure here was accounting controls, not climate. Expect minimal CSRD exposure (no EU consolidation), so **no CBAM/CSRD-driven reporting cost** — a relative advantage vs European peers.
- **Antitrust/competition:** Cox cleared with conditions; a Comcast tie-up would face heavy scrutiny.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE (mild) for the thesis**

- **Not a disqualifier, but a discount-rate and terminal-value input.** Environmental factors are immaterial — do not let an ESG screen mark CHTR down on carbon; there is nothing there.
- **Three ESG factors genuinely touch the cash flows:** (a) consumer-protection conduct → churn, AG penalties, franchise renewal; (b) cyber → uninsured breach costs and CPNI regulatory risk on a business whose product *is* connectivity trust; (c) governance/capital allocation → the $6.33B impairment and $25M buyback-controls settlement are direct evidence that the board's capital-allocation process destroys value. That last point is the strongest ESG argument in the bear case and it is quantitative, not ideological.
- **Positioning:** for an ESG-constrained mandate, CHTR is uninvestable (Laggard, High Sustainalytics risk, no credible climate target, non-independent chair). For a value mandate, ESG should be modelled as a **~5–8% haircut to fair value** via consumer-protection/cyber contingency plus a governance discount — and monitored on three catalysts: the Cox-integration layoff/safety record, the breach-related regulatory outcome, and whether post-deal capital allocation shifts from buybacks to deleveraging.

**Data gaps (honest):** 10-K and DEF 14A retrievals failed (tool returned no filing / SEC 403), so board size, precise independence ratios, pay ratio, and any reserved breach/legal accruals are unverified; Charter publishes no green-tariff or verified Scope 3 data; the lead-sheath exposure is unquantified by the company.
