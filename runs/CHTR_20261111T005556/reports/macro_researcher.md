# Macroeconomic Assessment — Charter Communications (CHTR)

**As of 11 November 2026 | Price $134.06 | Market cap $18.07B | EV ≈ $127B**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Hawkish / risk-on markets — a "no-landing, second-wave inflation" regime that is maximally hostile to long-duration levered equities.**

The US economy is not slowing into recession; it is re-accelerating into an inflation problem. Real GDP grew **+3.4% annualized in Q3-2026** (GDPC1: $24,611B vs $24,408B) and **+2.1% YoY**, with unemployment at a stable **4.1%** (Oct-2026). But CPI inflation has re-accelerated to **3.82% YoY** (Oct-2026) and, critically, has run at roughly **+4.5% annualized over the last four months** (CPI 332.6 → 337.5 Jun→Oct). Core PCE is better behaved at **3.12% YoY**, or ~2.9% annualized over three months — the disinflation is real but too slow.

The policy path is therefore the mirror image of 2024–25. The Fed cut the funds rate to a **3.63% trough by mid-2026**, then was forced to reverse: the effective rate rose to 3.75% (Sep) and **3.88% (Oct)**, with the FOMC holding at **3.75–4.00% on 28 October 2026** while stating "inflation remains elevated." The September SEP median implies **4.00–4.25% at end-2026 and end-2027** — i.e., the market is priced for a *hiking bias*, not cuts.

The bond market has done the tightening for the Fed. The **10Y has risen ~50–75bp since late June (4.38% → 4.88–5.13%)**, the **30Y sits at 5.47%**, and the curve is **positively sloped (+39bp 2s10s)** — this is *bear steepening on term premium*, not an inversion/recession signal. Credit, by contrast, is wide open: **HY OAS is only 334bp** (vs 265bp in early September). Equities are at highs (S&P 7,550). So risk assets are euphoric while the long end reprices — an unusual and dangerous mix for a BB-rated, $108.6B-levered company.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Charter is the most rate-sensitive name in Communication Services: **~100% domestic revenue, $108.6B debt principal, 5.3% weighted average cost of debt, 11.1-year weighted average life**, all sitting under an equity stub of just $18B. Note reported beta of 0.73 *understates* true rate sensitivity — the stock's correlation to the index is low precisely because idiosyncratic broadband collapse has dominated.

**Interest rates / discount rate — STRONGLY NEGATIVE (dominant factor).**
With pro forma EBITDA of ~$26.4B (4 × $6.6B) against a $126.7B EV, CHTR trades at **4.8× EV/EBITDA**. The equity is a thin residual levered ~7×:
- **Every 0.1× of EV/EBITDA ≈ $2.6B ≈ 15% of market cap.**
- A derating from 4.8× to 4.0× ($21B of EV) takes equity value to roughly **zero**.

This is why a 75bp rise in the 10Y is not an abstraction here: it is a direct, mechanical threat to the entire equity. `[MACRO RISK: HIGH]`

**Refinancing cost — NEGATIVE, but slow-burn and partly offset.**
$108.6B at 5.3% ≈ **$5.7B annual interest**, coverage 4.15–4.7×. With an 11.1-year WAL, only ~9%/yr rolls, so a 100bp repricing costs ~$100M/yr initially. But **30Y UST at 5.47%** plus a BB+/Ba2/BB+ high-yield curve means new long paper clears near 6.5–7% — a permanent FCF tax as the stack turns over. Offsetting this: **HY OAS at 334bp means the primary market is open**, and management is actively exploiting it — the **10 Nov 2026 tender for eight note series (up to $1.5B)** and Q3's exchange of $5.5B secured notes for $4.0B cash plus new notes (~$1.5B deleveraging, booked as a debt gain, consistent with bonds trading below par). Higher rates are simultaneously a *headwind* on coupons and an *opportunity* to retire debt at a discount.

**Inflation / pricing power — MODERATELY NEGATIVE.**
3.8% CPI supports nominal rate increases, but Charter is losing volume while raising price: **−233,000 pro forma internet subs in Q3** (34.3M total), pro forma revenue **−1.5%** and EBITDA **−4.0%**. Inflation is being passed through into *churn*, not revenue. Cost side: ~91,900 employees at ~4% wage inflation, plus programming/retransmission escalation — only partly offset by the **$2B run-rate opex savings plan** (+$1B Cox synergies). Content and labour inflation hit before the $2B lands (18 months).

**Housing turnover — NEGATIVE (underappreciated).**
Charter's gross connects are move-driven. With the **30Y at 5.47%**, 30-year mortgage rates are ~7%+, freezing existing-home turnover. Household formation is stalled even at 4.1% unemployment — a structural drag on gross adds that no amount of pricing fixes. This is the macro channel most directly visible in the −156k legacy Charter internet losses.

**Growth — NEUTRAL / slight positive.** +2.1% real GDP and 4.1% unemployment support ability-to-pay and small-business demand; a 3.4% Q3 is not a recession for a subscription business. But 2% growth with 3.8% inflation is the *worst* mix for a capital-intensive, levered, share-losing incumbent.

**Currency — NEUTRAL.** Essentially all revenue is USD; the +3.4% broad-dollar rally since September (117.9 → 121.9) is immaterial to revenue and trivially helpful on imported CPE/network gear. Not a thesis variable.

**Tariffs / trade — WATCH ITEM (unquantified).** Set-top/CPE and network equipment are imported; with capex guided to **≤$11B in 2027** from ~$12–13B, the exposure is real but shrinking. I could not retrieve a reliable 2026 tariff schedule for telecom equipment, so I flag this qualitatively rather than quantify it. The decision to move legacy Cox customer service back onshore over 18 months raises cost modestly but reduces trade/geopolitical exposure.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The economy is fine; the *regime* is wrong for this security. A hawkish Fed with dots at 4.00–4.25%, 3.8% CPI re-accelerating, a 5.1% 10Y and a 5.47% 30Y create three simultaneous headwinds: (i) a discount-rate assault on a 4.8× EV/EBITDA multiple supporting a ~7× levered equity stub; (ii) a rising cost of refinancing $108.6B of long-dated debt; and (iii) frozen housing turnover suppressing the gross connects that drive broadband. The mitigants — tight 334bp HY spreads, +2.1% real GDP, 4.1% unemployment, and a credible $2–3B cost programme — keep this from being "severe," but they are second-order relative to the multiple math. Note the stock is **−53.5% from its $254.11 52-week high**, so much of the rate damage is already in the price; the rating reflects *forward macro risk to the equity stub*, not a claim that the decline is unrecognised.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario That Would Matter Most

**Bear surprise (higher probability of the two, and asymmetric): inflation entraps the Fed.**
If CPI holds above 4% into H1-2027, the Fed delivers the two hikes its own dots imply, taking funds to **4.50–4.75%**, and term premium pushes the **10Y to ~5.75% and the 30Y to ~6.25%**. Nothing in Charter's operating story offsets that: refinancing ~$10B/yr steps up by another ~$150M of annual interest, and the EV/EBITDA multiple compresses 4.8× → 4.0×. At $108.6B of debt against a $26.4B EBITDA base, that 0.8× turn **eliminates essentially the entire $18B equity**. The same sensitivity runs in reverse, which is the bull case: core PCE already at ~2.9% three-month annualized suggests disinflation is intact, and if the 10Y returns to 4.2% the multiple could recover to 5.5–5.6×, **roughly doubling the equity** from $18B toward $35B — before any credit for the $2B savings plan or the discounted-debt tenders. Charter is, in macro terms, now a leveraged bet on the long end of the Treasury curve rather than on broadband.
