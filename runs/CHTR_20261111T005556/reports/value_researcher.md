# Charter Communications (CHTR) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price: ~$134 | Market cap ~$18.0B | Net debt ~$96.5B | Enterprise value ~$114.5B**
*FY2025 actuals (filed 2026-01-30). Stock is down ~84% from its $825 (2021) peak.*

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW, and actively narrowing**

Charter's historic moat was textbook: local franchise/licensing barriers, enormous fixed-cost scale in a passings-based network, and high switching costs on a bundled triple-play. That moat produced the classic hallmarks — 39% EBITDA margins and ROIC consistently above WACC.

**The evidence that the moat is being breached is now unambiguous:**

| Metric | Q1 2025 | Q1 2026 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer relationships | 32.16M | 31.68M | −1.5% |
| Internet customers | 29.7M | 29.6M | −120k in the quarter |
| Penetration of passings | 56.3% | 54.0% | **−2.3 pts** |
| Monthly residential ARPU | $120.07 | $118.44 | **−1.4%** |

The ARPU line is the decisive datapoint. A business with genuine pricing power does not cut price 1.4% *while* losing subscribers. Broadband losses were ~403k in FY2025, ~120k in Q1 2026, and ~233k (pro forma) in Q3 2026 — i.e., **accelerating**. Fixed wireless access (T-Mobile/Verizon) has permanently broken the local-monopoly economics, and FTTH overbuilders are taking share in the densest, most profitable footprint. Revenue has gone from +7.5% (2021) → +4.5% → +1.1% → +0.9% → **−0.6% (2025) → −1.0% (Q1 2026)**.

The one genuine growth engine is Spectrum Mobile (12.1M lines, +1.8M LTM), but mobile is a low-margin reseller business riding Verizon's MVNO — it adds revenue, not moat.

**Conclusion:** Still an efficient-scale asset locally, but the moat has narrowed materially for five straight years and management is now competing on price. This is a "harvest the asset" pattern, not a franchise being widened.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**The good:**
- **ROIC ~11.3%** for four straight years (2022–2025) against a WACC of roughly 6–7% — the business still earns above its cost of capital.
- EBITDA margins have *risen* (37.4% in 2021 → ~38.7% in 2025); operating margin 21.0% → 24.4%. Costs are being cut aggressively.
- FCF conversion (FCF/net income) = 88%, which meets the >80% bar.
- Asset base is irreplaceable: 58.7M passings, $46B of net PP&E.

**The disqualifying issues:**
- **Leverage:** net debt $96.6B = **4.6x EBITDA**. Interest expense of $5.04B consumes **24% of EBITDA**. Total equity is $20.5B against $97.6B of goodwill — tangible book equity is deeply negative. There is no margin-of-safety cushion on the balance sheet; the equity is a thin levered stub (16% of EV).
- **Capex intensity:** $11.66B of capex on $16.08B of operating cash flow. FCF fell from $8.7B (2021) → $6.1B → $3.5B → $3.2B → $4.4B (2025) → **$1.4B in Q1 2026, down YoY**. Roughly $3.2B/yr is rural line-extension growth capex, meaning true maintenance capex is lower — but it is still heavy.
- **No dividend**, and the FCF has been spoken for by buybacks and debt service.
- **GAAP earnings quality:** Q3 2026 reported EPS of **−$20.50** vs. a ~$8.94 estimate — roughly a $2.8B loss, almost certainly a goodwill/intangible impairment against that $97.6B goodwill balance. Book value (0.9x P/B) is therefore not a meaningful floor.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **The "cheapness" is a leverage illusion**

The optical multiples scream deep value: **P/E 3.7x, EV/EBITDA 5.5x, P/FCF 4.2x, FCF yield ~24%**, versus a sector-peer median P/E of 25x.

But look at the enterprise rather than the stub:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| EV / EBITDA (2026E ~$20.9B) | **5.5x** |
| EV / unlevered FCF (EBITDA − capex − taxes ≈ $7.5B) | **~15x** |
| FCF / EV | **3.7%** |
| Net debt / equity market cap | 5.4x |

Because 84% of the enterprise is funded with debt, the equity multiple is arithmetically distorted. **You are only getting a 24% FCF yield because you are buying the last, most levered 16% of the capital structure of an enterprise yielding 3.7%.** For a business with *declining* revenue and no pricing power, 15x unlevered FCF and 5.5x EBITDA is not distressed — it is roughly fair.

**Intrinsic value (own DCF; the provided DCF tool returned erroneous output):** assuming unlevered FCF of $7.5B → $10.0B by 2029 as the network-upgrade and rural-build capex rolls off, then −3%/yr perpetual decline, discounted at 6.5% WACC:

- EV ≈ $110B → **equity ≈ $13B → ~$98/share**
- Base case (capex normalizes faster, EBITDA flat, FCF $7.5–8B): **$150–190/share**
- Bull case (FCF $9B, buybacks shrink float 20%/yr, re-rate to 7x EBITDA): **$300+/share**
- Bear case (EBITDA erodes 3%/yr, capex stays high, refinancing at higher coupons): **$50–90/share**

**At $134, the stock trades near the low end of my base-case range, not at a 20–30% discount to intrinsic value.** Critically, the *dispersion* of outcomes ($50 to $300) is so wide that no honest margin-of-safety claim can be made. The asymmetry runs the wrong way: ~25% upside to my midpoint versus 40–60% downside in the bear case.

**A structural note on the threshold:** to buy the enterprise at a genuine distressed 4.5x EBITDA, the EV would have to fall to ~$94B — which implies equity of roughly **negative $2.5B**. There is no share price at which this becomes statistically cheap on an enterprise basis.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Adequate operators, poor capital allocators**

- CEO Chris Winfrey (since 2023) is a long-tenured internal operator running a credible playbook: price/packaging discipline, cost cuts, mobile bundling, rural expansion. Execution is competent.
- Ownership/control sits with Advance/Newhouse and the Malone-affiliated group (~25% voting). Insiders are aligned with long-term ownership — a positive.
- **But the capital-allocation record is the single worst item in this analysis.** Charter spent **$15.4B on buybacks in 2021 (avg ~$650) and $10.3B in 2022 (avg ~$450)** — roughly $40B+ cumulatively at 15–20x FCF — funded substantially with debt. Today the stock is $134. That is tens of billions of dollars of shareholder capital destroyed at the top, precisely the empire-building-adjacent behavior Buffett warns about. Buying back stock at 23% FCF yield today is defensible; the prior cycle was not.
- Buybacks have genuinely shrunk the diluted count from 193M (2021) to ~132M now, a 32% reduction — but the debt taken on to do it is what now threatens the equity.

**Capital allocation score: 4/10** — value-destructive at the cycle peak, now more rational out of necessity.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID** *(not a conviction short)*

CHTR fails my test on three of the four pillars that matter most:

1. **Moat:** Narrow and narrowing — penetration down 2.3 pts, ARPU negative, subscribers accelerating lower. Buffett's newspaper lesson applies directly: when distribution economics break, price cuts follow, and price cuts never restore the franchise.
2. **Balance sheet:** 4.6x levered with $5B of fixed interest on a declining EBITDA base. No cushion, negative tangible equity, and a $97.6B goodwill balance that is now being written down.
3. **Price:** The enterprise trades at 5.5x EBITDA / ~15x unlevered FCF — fair, not cheap. The low equity multiple is a leverage artifact, not a margin of safety.

The genuine bull case is real and I want to state it plainly: if the DOCSIS 4.0 upgrade and rural-build capex roll off to ~$9B and FCF inflects to $7–8B, buying back 35–40% of the float annually at these prices would compound equity value very fast. That is a legitimate fat pitch — *if* the broadband cash flow proves durable. But it depends on a 10-year competitive judgment that lies outside my circle of competence with any confidence.

**Where I would revisit:** I would need the equity at a price where the *enterprise* is genuinely mispriced — roughly **$80–95/share** (EV/EBITDA ~5.0x, ~35% FCF yield) — or, more importantly, evidence that internet net adds have stabilized for two or three consecutive quarters *and* capex is visibly declining. Absent either, the cheapness is a trap. I would also need the buyback to be funded from FCF rather than incremental debt.

**Monitoring triggers for a reversal to Buy:** (1) two consecutive quarters of positive internet net adds; (2) ARPU stabilization; (3) capex guided below $10B; (4) net leverage below 4.0x.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to the bear/avoid case (i.e., what could make me wrong):**
- **FCF inflection.** Capex falling to $9B would lift FCF to ~$7B — a 39% yield on today's market cap. Buybacks at that level would be enormously accretive.
- **Fixed wireless hits capacity ceilings.** FWA is spectrally constrained; T-Mobile/Verizon may cede share back, letting cable stabilize.
- **Asset scarcity value.** 58.7M passings and the only scaled cable network outside Comcast make CHTR a plausible strategic target; a take-private or merger premium is not negligible.
- **Cable同业 consolidation** (Comcast/Charter-style combinations) could re-rate the sector.

**Risks to the ownership case:**
- **Refinancing risk.** $96.6B of debt rolling into a higher-rate market with EBITDA flat-to-down could push interest above $6B, permanently impairing the equity stub. A downgrade spiral is the tail risk.
- **Accelerating cord-cutting/broadband substitution** — Q3 2026's 233k pro forma loss suggests deterioration, not stabilization.
- **Impairment cascade.** A write-down of the $97.6B goodwill would push book equity toward or below zero, removing any valuation anchor and potentially triggering covenant scrutiny.
- **Value-trap dynamics.** Management's demonstrated willingness to buy stock at 15–20x FCF means FCF could keep being deployed into a melting ice cube rather than into debt reduction.
- **Mobile is not a moat.** Spectrum Mobile rides Verizon's MVNO; margin and strategic control sit with a competitor.
