## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — "good growth, sticky headline inflation, rising *real* yields, easy credit." Risk-on for earnings, risk-off for long-duration equity.**

The US is running above-trend growth (real GDP +2.1% y/y in Q3 2026, +3.4% annualised q/q) with unemployment down to 4.1% from 4.4–4.5% a year ago. But the Fed has *reversed course*: effective fed funds bottomed at 3.63% in June–August 2026 and is now 3.88% (Oct 2026, implying a target range of 3.75–4.00%) — a 25bp hike delivered into a 3.8% headline CPI. The 2Y at 4.74% sits ~86bp above fed funds, so the market is pricing roughly three more hikes, not cuts.

The critical detail is *what* is driving the bond market. 10Y breakevens have **fallen** from 2.40% (June) to 2.20% (Nov 10) while the 10Y nominal has climbed to 5.13% — so the entire move is a **real-rate/term-premium shock** (10Y real ≈ 2.93%). Core CPI is anchored at 2.57% y/y and stable; the headline spike from 3.30% (July) to 3.72% (Sept) is food/energy-driven, i.e., a supply shock the Fed cannot address with rates without breaking something else. Credit conditions remain wide open (HY OAS 3.34%, cycle tights of 2.60% in late August), and the curve is positively sloped (+39bp 2s10s), so there is **no recession signal** — but household surveys are screaming: University of Michigan sentiment at 48.1 (Sept 2026), down from 71.7 in Jan 2025. That divergence between GDP/credit and the consumer is the defining tension for a discretionary name.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

ON Holding is a Zurich-based, CHF-reporting, ~$11.4bn-cap footwear/apparel brand (beta 2.08) with an approximate revenue mix of ~60–65% Americas, ~24% EMEA and ~12–13% APAC (last disclosed splits; not re-verified this quarter). Q3 2026 (reported Nov 10): net sales CHF 931.4m, +17.2% reported / +17.8% constant currency; gross margin 72.6% reported but 66.9% ex-CHF 52.6m of US tariff refunds; DTC +25%, wholesale +12.2%, APAC +31.1%. FY guidance: low-20s sales growth, ~66% gross margin ex-refunds.

| Macro variable | Impact | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Real 10Y at 2.93% / hiking Fed | **Strongly negative** | Pure valuation/duration effect. ONON's value is heavily weighted to post-2030 cash flows; a 100bp rise in the discount rate plausibly costs 15–25% of fair value, amplified by beta 2.08. Empirical proof: the stock is –45% from its Jan-2025 high ($63.80) and –25% YTD ($46.48 → $34.70) *while EPS grew* — that is multiple compression, not fundamental deterioration. |
| Headline CPI 3.8%, core 2.5% | **Neutral-to-positive** | ONON has genuine pricing power — full-price selling in a promotional market, 66.9% underlying gross margin. Its affluent, performance-driven customer is the least inflation-squeezed cohort. The core/headline gap means *input-cost* (not wage) inflation, which ONON has passed through. |
| UMich sentiment 48.1 | **Negative** | Americas is ~two-thirds of sales and the highest-margin region. Sentiment at recessionary levels alongside 3.8% headline inflation implies the low/mid-income consumer is cutting discretionary spend. ONON is taking share today, but a wholesale-channel destock (12.2% growth vs 25% DTC) is the transmission channel if it breaks. |
| USD firming (+3.2% broad since Sept 1; USD/CHF 0.8192) | **Modestly positive** | ~60%+ of revenue is USD-denominated but reported in CHF, so dollar strength is a translation tailwind. Note Q3 was actually a 60bp *headwind* (17.8% cc vs 17.2% reported), showing a 1% USD/CHF move ≈ 0.6% of CHF revenue. Partially self-hedged: COGS is USD-denominated (Vietnam/Indonesia sourcing), so CHF strength lowers CHF cost of goods — the residual damage is to the CHF cost base (Zurich HQ, ~4,000 staff) and to EUR/APAC translation. |
| Tariffs / trade policy | **Negative, high variance** | CHF 52.6m of US tariff *refunds* added ~570bp to Q3 gross margin — a one-off that inflated the headline beat and is the likely reason the stock fell 7.5% on the print. Implies ONON imports essentially all US volume and carries material tariff cost in the base case. Policy remains the single largest non-cyclical swing factor for margins. |
| Credit conditions (HY OAS 3.34%) | **Neutral** | ONON carries minimal debt / net cash — no refinancing risk, no interest-cost sensitivity. Rate exposure is entirely via the discount rate and consumer financing, not the balance sheet. |
| CHF safe-haven flows | **Negative asymmetry** | Risk-off episodes hit ONON twice: multiple compression on a high-beta name *and* CHF appreciation compressing CHF-reported revenue. |

**Idiosyncratic context:** the stock gapped –20.3% on Aug 11 (44m shares vs ~4m normal), rallied +25% to $38.62 by Nov 4, then fell 7.5% on the Q3 beat (Nov 10). This is a name whose price is set by *guidance revisions and multiple*, not by the macro cycle directly — which is precisely why the discount rate matters so much.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **Unfavourable**

The operating environment is survivable; the *valuation* environment is hostile. Three forces dominate:

1. **Real yields at 2.93%** with the Fed hiking again — the worst possible backdrop for a long-duration, 2.08-beta compounder. This is the primary cause of ONON's de-rating, not tariffs.
2. **Headline inflation + sentiment at 48** — a squeezed US consumer that is ~two-thirds of revenue, with tariff-driven price increases on imported footwear layered on top.
3. **Trade policy** — unresolved, non-diversifiable, and directly margin-relevant.

**Mitigants that keep this from being outright bearish:** core inflation is anchored at 2.5%, credit is freely available, GDP is above trend, unemployment is falling, and ONON is gaining share with pricing power and record underlying margins. If real yields stabilise, the stock has significant re-rating leverage — the entire de-rating would be recoverable.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (Bear, asymmetric)

**"A real-rate shock without inflation."** Breakevens are already falling (2.40% → 2.20%) while nominals grind to 5.13%. If fiscal supply/term premium pushes the 10Y through 5.75% *with breakevens still falling*, real yields reach ~3.4% — and the Fed, still hiking into an energy-driven headline CPI above 4%, cannot help. Under this path: consumer discretionary multiples compress a further 15–20%, ONON's multiple (already de-rated to roughly high-teens/low-20s forward P/E) goes to mid-teens while FY27 estimates get cut on a US wholesale slowdown, and equity weakness drives safe-haven CHF toward 0.75 — a ~8% translation headwind on ~60% of revenue. Beta 2.08 turns a 10% index drawdown into a 20%+ ONON drawdown. For a stock that has already shown it can gap –20% on a single print, this is the fat tail.

**The bull mirror image is equally live and worth flagging:** the energy spike reverses, core at 2.5% lets the Fed pivot late-2026, breakevens stay anchored at 2.2% and nominals fall to 4.5% — ONON's 20%+ growth with record margins re-rates violently off a depressed base, and USD/CHF at 0.82 adds a translation tailwind.
