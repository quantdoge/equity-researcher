# ON Holding AG (ONON) — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment
*Prepared Nov 2026. Company: Swiss-domiciled (Zurich), NYSE-listed, ~CHF 3.5bn 2026E net sales, present in 90+ countries.*

**Concentration insight:** ~52–58% of revenue in one tariff-exposed market (US), and ~90% of footwear from one origin country (Vietnam). These two facts dominate the risk profile — far more than the Swiss domicile.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**R1 — US tariff / rules-of-origin regime on Vietnam-origin goods (Probability: Certain / recurring; Impact: Medium-High)**
- The IEEPA "reciprocal" tariffs (Vietnam 20%, Switzerland 39%) were struck down by the Supreme Court on 20 Feb 2026; collection of the 20% Vietnam reciprocal rate ceased 24 Feb 2026. A 10% Section 122 baseline applied, then a **12.5% Section 301 "forced labor" tariff took effect 24 July 2026** — Vietnam footwear now ≈25% all-in vs ≈58.5% pre-ruling, with a **40% penalty rate where CBP finds transshipment/origin evasion** (Tariffstool; USTR FLIP 301 final action; Trade Compliance Resource Hub).
- **Direct financial evidence:** On received **CHF 52.6m of US tariff refunds** in 9M26 (+570bps to Q3 gross margin, taking GM to 72.6% vs 66.9% ex-refunds), and disclosed a further **CHF 4.5m of post-quarter claims accepted by CBP** not yet recognized (Q3-26 6-K, 10 Nov 2026). A CHF ~57m swing is ~4% of 2026E revenue and a large share of earnings — i.e., policy, not operations, is currently a top driver of reported margin. Guidance is now presented both including and excluding refunds (FY26 GM ~67.5% incl. vs ~66% excl.).
- Secondary: Swiss-origin shipments face a **variable additional US tariff of up to 12.5%** (SECO, 24 July 2026), with a US–Switzerland/Liechtenstein framework intended to **cap at 15%** (Supply Chain Dive).

**R2 — US consumer/regulatory concentration (Probability: Medium; Impact: High)**
The US is >half of revenue and is where tariffs, class actions and forced-labour enforcement all land. Americas growth has decelerated to +4.5–12.4% while EMEA/APAC grow 18–32% — so the risk is a slowing core market coinciding with policy cost shocks.

**R3 — China/APAC dependence for future growth (Probability: Medium; Impact: Medium)**
APAC is now >20% of sales and growing >30% cc, explicitly "supported by strong demand in China, Japan, and South Korea." Exposure: China consumer weakness, US-China coercion of companies complying with Western rules (Beijing's expanding blocking statutes — FT), and JPY/KRW/CNY translation. Note FX is already material: 9M26 reported +15.1% vs +21.8% cc (≈670bps drag), and certain subsidiaries have shifted functional currency from CHF to USD.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Active securities/regulatory enforcement: none identified.** No DOJ, SEC, FTC or antitrust action against On surfaced in searches. (Caveat: SEC EDGAR blocked direct retrieval of the FY2025 20-F risk factors/legal-proceedings section — 403 error — so this is a public-source conclusion, not a filing-verified one.)
- **Bologna et al. v. On Inc.**, No. 3:25-cv-01852, filed 20 Oct 2025 (D. Or.): proposed consumer class action alleging a design defect causing CloudTec shoes (Cloud 5/6, Cloudmonster, Cloudtilt, Cloudsurfer, etc.) to squeak; NY GBL, California UCL/CLRA, breach of warranty. Low monetary materiality, but reputational and warranty-policy pressure on the core franchise (ClassAction.org; BBC).
- **"Swissness" / origin marketing class action**: a proposed class action reportedly challenges how much "Swiss" identity On can market before it becomes deceptive ("How Much SWISS Can You Sell? On Faces Class Action," JD Supra). Material to brand positioning, given Swiss-law restrictions on "Swiss made" claims.
- **Product-chemistry compliance:** EU-wide PFHxA restriction on textiles/apparel/footwear; **Denmark bans PFAS in clothing and footwear from 1 July 2026**; expanding US state PFAS and Prop 65 regimes (Eurofins; White & Case; Greenberg Traurig).
- **Supply-chain / forced-labour compliance:** UFLPA entity list and enforcement remain active, and the US's *current* tariff instrument is explicitly a forced-labour statute — raising documentation/audit requirements on Vietnamese and Chinese inputs.
- **Swiss sanctions regime:** Switzerland aligns with EU sanctions (incl. Russia/Belarus), which applies to group entities — manageable given minimal Russia/CIS exposure, but is a compliance obligation.
- **Positive:** Sept 2026 board authorization of up to **USD 1bn** Class A buyback through Dec 2029 (no purchases yet); cash CHF 1,339m at 30 Sep 2026 — ample capacity to absorb legal/tariff costs.

## 5. Overall Rating: **MEDIUM** geopolitical/legal risk

**Rationale:** Domicile and governance are low-risk (Switzerland, NYSE, €/US-standard disclosure, net cash). Legal exposure is presently **immaterial in dollar terms** — consumer class actions and product-chemistry rules, with no government investigation identified. What pushes the rating to Medium is **geographic concentration plus policy dependence**: ~50–55% of revenue in the US and ~90% of footwear from Vietnam, in a tariff regime that has been rewritten three times in twelve months and whose refunds already move reported gross margin by ~570bps. A deterioration in US–Vietnam origin enforcement or a reversal of tariff relief is the key downside; conversely, supplier diversification, pricing power and the 15% Swiss framework cap are meaningful mitigants.

**Key monitoring triggers:** CBP origin/transshipment actions on Vietnamese footwear; sustainability of tariff refunds in FY27 guidance; ruling on the "Swissness" class action; Denmark/EU PFAS enforcement; China APAC growth deceleration.
