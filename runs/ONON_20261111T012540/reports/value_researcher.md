## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW (widening)**

On designs and sells premium performance footwear, apparel and accessories (CloudTec/Puma-derived "Cloud" cushioning, Helion foam, Speedboard). FY2026E revenue ~CHF 3.5bn, up from CHF 425m in 2020 (**~48% CAGR**). Founded 2010, Zurich, founder-led.

**Moat sources — real but not structural:**
- **Intangible/brand:** 15 years of credibility in run specialty, now validated on the biggest stages (podiums at New York, Frankfurt Marathon wins). Q3'26 delivered record net sales in *every* region and *every* channel/category, with **DTC +25% to 42.2% of sales** and **apparel +38.7%** — evidence of pull, not push.
- **Pricing power is the single best moat proof:** gross margin 56.0% (2022) → 59.6% → 60.6% → **66.9% in Q3'26 ex-tariff-refunds**, with management explicitly noting they "held full price" in a *promotional* market, with underlying GM up ~320bps YoY. This is Nike-class-plus (NKE ~44%), and far above adidas. Very few footwear brands sustain 65%+ GM.
- **Efficient scale in run specialty:** limited shelf space, high service requirements, and a wholesale partner network that rewards a differentiated technical product.
- **ROIC > WACC:** 17.0% (2025), 13.8% (2023), 7.5% (2022) — and *ex-cash* ROIC is materially higher (~20%+ given CHF 1.43bn cash). Rising ROIC alongside falling capital intensity confirms the moat is widening, not eroding.

**Moat verdict:** Narrow. Genuine, widening brand equity and pricing power, but **no switching costs, no network effects, no cost advantage** — and footwear brand heat has a long history of decay (Under Armour, Hoka's post-2023 deceleration, Lululemon footwear). The moat must be re-earned every season through product.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good, not perfect**

- **Founder-operator alignment:** Co-founders Caspar Coppetti and David Allemann (Exec Chairman) still run the business; Olivier Bernhard (three-time world duathlon champion) drives product. Rare, genuine founder-led culture.
- **Capital allocation discipline:** growth funded organically; no empire-building M&A (only a small distributor buyout); no leverage; cash compounds on the balance sheet. Investor Day 2026 laid out three growth engines (Performance/Run, Lifestyle/Sneaker, Apparel/Her) rather than a diversification spree.
- **Execution track record:** margin expansion of ~700bps gross in four years *through* a tariff shock, plus a 3x revenue increase since IPO without diluting the brand — strong evidence of pricing discipline ("On Premium Playbook").
- **Deductions:** (i) **Dual-class structure** — Class B voting shares held by founders/execs confer control with a minority economic stake; a controlled company that public holders cannot influence; (ii) **CHF 1.4bn cash earning little, with no buyback** even at a 40% drawdown — a mild capital-allocation lapse; (iii) SBC dilution ~1%/yr; (iv) one poorly-explained ~CHF 130m+ charge in Q2'25 that investors should demand clarity on; (v) management has now guided down twice (23%+ → low-20s) — credibility needs rebuilding.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **BUY** (accumulate; sizing discipline required)

This is a high-quality, founder-led, net-cash compounder with widening gross margins and a genuine (if fashion-sensitive) brand, trading at ~12.5x EV/EBITDA and ~21x normalised earnings for ~17% growth. That combination is rare and offers a ~33% discount to base-case intrinsic value of ~$52.

It is **not a Strong Buy** because:
1. The moat is **Narrow and re-earned seasonally** — not the durable, slow-changing moat I prefer.
2. **FY2026 earnings are flattered:** CHF 52.6m of tariff refunds are non-recurring; blended 2026 reported EPS overstates run-rate earnings by ~$0.16–0.18/share.
3. **Downside is not protected by assets:** the bear case (~$36) is today's price; if growth breaks toward mid-single digits, DECK's 8x EBITDA multiple implies ~$20–25.

**Practical discipline:** Buy below ~$38 (25%+ discount). Add materially below $32, where the price approaches bear-case value and the risk/reward becomes asymmetrically favourable. At ~$45+ (12–15% discount) the stock becomes a **Hold**. Position size as a quality-growth allocation, not a core Buffett-style holding.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Brand heat / fashion risk (highest risk).** Running is a fad-prone category with no switching costs. If Cloudtilt/Cloudsurfer lose cultural relevance, pricing power (the entire margin story) collapses fast. Hoka/Deckers is the live cautionary template: ~20% growth decelerating to high-single digits, now 8x EBITDA.
2. **Tariffs are structural, not transitory.** The 2026 refund is a one-time release; the underlying US tariff regime remains a permanent COGS headwind. Q2'25 proved fragility: a 32% revenue increase produced a *net loss*, with GM at 61.5%.
3. **Growth deceleration.** 30% (FY25) → ~17% (FY26E); wholesale is softening and channel inventories are elevated industry-wide. A further guide-down would compress both the multiple and the E.
4. **Currency.** CHF reporting with the majority of revenue in USD/EUR: a strong franc mechanically depresses reported CHF revenue/EPS and compounds the apparent slowdown.
5. **Competitive intensity.** Nike is rebuilding running, adidas is resurgent, plus Hoka, New Balance, Brooks. The premium price point is the first casualty in a promotional cycle.
6. **Earnings-quality/working-capital risk.** Rising DSO (~47 days), inventory at ~161 days, refund-flattered quarter, SBC, and an unexplained 2025 charge — reported numbers require adjustment before use.
7. **Governance.** Founder control via Class B supervoting shares limits shareholder recourse; key-man dependence is real.
8. **Volatility.** Beta 2.08; the stock has fallen 55% peak-to-trough in 12 months. Expect drawdowns of this magnitude as normal, not exceptional.

---

**Bottom line:** A better business than its chart suggests, at a price that finally asks little of it. Buy — but with the humility that this is a Narrow-moat growth franchise bought at a discount, not a Wide-moat toll bridge bought cheap. The durability of the *brand*, not the multiple, is what will decide the return.
