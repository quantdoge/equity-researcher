**Price $34.70 | Mkt cap ~$11.4B | EV ~$11.1B | 52-wk range $26.37–$51.06 | Beta 2.08 | Net cash balance sheet**

## 1. Growth Story

On is the fastest-scaling brand in global performance sportswear — a Swiss engineering-led challenger using proprietary cushioning (CloudTec/Helion), **LightSpray** robotic manufacturing, and a premium DTC model to take share from Nike and Adidas in running while extending into apparel (+39% YoY in Q3'26), outdoor, training and tennis. Four-year revenue CAGR of **47.9%** has taken the business from CHF 725M to CHF 3.0B with gross margins now near **67% underlying** — best-in-class versus Nike (~44%) and Adidas (~51%). The stock, however, has de-rated ~40% from its early-2026 highs as growth decelerated from 30% to ~17%, leaving an elite-margin, high-teens grower trading at ~2.6x EV/Sales and ~14x EV/EBITDA. **The dislocation between business quality and multiple is the opportunity.**

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

| Market | Size | On's position |
|---|---|---|
| Global athletic footwear | ~$150B | ~2% share |
| Activewear / performance apparel | ~$400B+ | <0.5% share — **the open frontier** |
| Performance running (core) | ~$50B | High-single-digit, gaining |
| China/APAC | ~$60B footwear+apparel | **<5% of On sales** — the largest untapped pool |

At ~$4.3B FY26E revenue, On has captured roughly **1% of a ~$550B combined TAM**. Even a path to 3–4% share supports a **$15–20B revenue business** — implying the consensus FY29 estimate of $5.8B is *conservative*, not aggressive. Three vectors extend the runway beyond core running: (1) **apparel** at 39% growth and rising mix, which raises revenue per door and lifetime value; (2) **owned retail + e-commerce**, which structurally lifts gross margin (DTC gross margins run well above wholesale); (3) **geography** — EMEA is the profit base, the US is the brand battleground, and Asia is almost virgin territory.

**Differentiation:** On is not a fashion cycle — it's an engineering story with a manufacturing moat. LightSpray (robotic, single-piece uppers) is genuinely defensible IP and a cost/margin lever, not a marketing slogan. That is what justifies a 67% gross margin versus Nike's 44%.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY**

**The setup:** This is a high-quality compounder that has been repriced from "priced for perfection" to "priced for deceleration," and the deceleration has now been priced in twice.

| Valuation | Multiple |
|---|---|
| EV/Sales FY26E | **~2.6x** (3.1x at 1:1 FX) |
| EV/Sales FY27E | ~2.2x |
| EV/EBITDA FY26E | **~13.6x** |
| P/E FY26E (consensus $1.456) | 23.8x |
| P/E on YTD run-rate adj. EPS (~$1.95) | **~17.8x** |
| P/E FY28E ($2.152) | **16.1x** |
| PEG | **~0.9–1.0** |
| P/B | 7.1x (net cash, minimal dilution) |

**Why Buy and not Hold:** For a business delivering ~18–20% revenue growth, 67% gross margins, expanding operating leverage (9.1% → 12.5% → ~15%+ operating margin), 45%+ DTC mix, and a sub-1% TAM penetration, a **~14x EBITDA / ~16x FY28 EPS / 0.9 PEG** multiple is a genuine mispricing. Consensus FY29 of $5.8B revenue implies only ~14% CAGR from here — far below the 47.9% four-year CAGR and below what a 1%-to-3% TAM share move mechanically produces. **FY26 EPS consensus of $1.456 looks stale** against $1.57 of YTD adjusted EPS through nine months — the estimate base is behind reality, which is the fuel for upside revisions.

**Why not Strong Buy:** Rule of 40 at 38.4 (and likely ~32–35 in FY26 as growth slows) fails the threshold. Growth has structurally halved. Google Trends says baseline brand demand is softening YoY, and the stock is a beta-2.08 name in a consumer discretionary sector where a spending slowdown is a live risk. I want one clean, tariff-refund-free gross margin print before upgrading.

**Position sizing implication:** Core growth holding, scaled up on evidence, not a max-conviction position yet. Add aggressively below $30 (Sept'26 low $26.37 was a capitulation print); the ~$42.5 median PT is a reasonable 12-month target, with $47–60 the bull case if FY27 guidance lands at 20%+ with 20% EBITDA margins.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Growth durability — the primary risk.** Revenue growth has fallen 30% → 17% in four quarters. Q3 beat was margin-driven, not demand-driven. If FY27 guides to mid-teens, the multiple compresses further regardless of margin quality. **A growth investor's thesis lives or dies here.**
2. **Tariff and FX whack-a-mole.** Q3's headline 72.6% gross margin included CHF 52.6M of US tariff *refunds* — a one-off. Underlying 66.9% is real, but tariff policy reversal and CHF strength (reporting currency) both hit reported margins hard. Do not capitalize the 72.6% print.
3. **Competitive intensity.** Nike is restructuring back toward performance running; Hoka/Deckers remains the closest structural competitor; Adidas is resurgent in lifestyle. On's premium price point is its moat *and* its exposure — a consumer trade-down hits a $180 shoe before a $100 one.
4. **Brand concentration / key-person and channel risk.** A single-brand company at a 2x-beta multiple has no diversification cushion. Wholesale partners (Foot Locker, JD, Dick's) retain markdown leverage.
5. **Working capital / FCF quality.** FY25 FCF fell to CHF 253M from CHF 446M on a CHF 163M working capital drag despite +30% revenue. The 8.4% FCF margin is the weak leg of the Rule of 40. Watch inventory turns in Q4 — inventory growing faster than sales would be a red flag on the "full-price sell-through is healthy" claim.
6. **Multiple/volatility risk.** Beta 2.08, ~40% drawdown from early-2026 highs, and the stock fell 7.5% on a quarter where it beat EPS by 40%. Expect continued 20%+ swings around prints.

---

**Bottom line:** Buy the de-rating. The business is executing better than the stock — margins are inflecting upward faster than revenue is decelerating, DTC mix is at a record, and the multiple now embeds a pessimism the TAM math doesn't support. Confirm with the Q4 print (~March 2027) and a tariff-clean gross margin; if those land, this re-rates toward $45–50.
