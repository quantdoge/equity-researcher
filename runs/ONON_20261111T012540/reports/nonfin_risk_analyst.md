## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — On Holding AG (ONON)

**Price $34.70 | Market cap ~$11.4B | Beta 2.08 | 52-wk range $26.60–$51.06 | Assessment date: Nov 2026**

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: WEAK

This is the dominant non-financial risk at ONON. The company is fundamentally **founder-controlled with fused executive/board authority**, and it just executed a destabilising CEO transition.

**Evidence:**

| Factor | Finding |
|---|---|
| Dual-class control | Class B **voting-rights shares (324.99M outstanding) exceed Class A economic shares (308.47M)**, with Class B carrying 10x votes (AGM approved conversion of 16.25M Class B → 1.625M Class A). Founders' extended team controlled **59.4% of voting power** at IPO and remain in control. |
| CEO/Chairman duality | **Both** co-CEOs — David Allemann and Caspar Coppetti — hold the title **Executive Co-Chairman of the Board**. There is no independent chair. This is duality squared. |
| Board independence | All incumbent directors re-elected at the 28-May-2026 AGM with no disclosed dissent; as a Swiss foreign private issuer it is not bound by NYSE majority-independent board rules. Board compensation cap CHF 2M vs. executive cap CHF 30M — the board has limited financial leverage over management. |
| Executive turnover | **CEO Martin Hoffmann departed 1-May-2026** after ~5 years (announced 25-Mar-2026, stock −11% that day); replaced by the two co-founders. **New CFO Frank Sluis** started 1-May-2026; **new President/COO Scott Maguire**. Three of the top four roles turned over within 12 months, while the departing CEO had been CFO for 13 years prior — i.e., the CFO seat has now changed hands twice in a little over a year with no long-tenured financial steward. |
| Related-party / alignment | Founder shareholdings are very large (Bernhard 5.23M; Allemann 2.84M; Coppetti 2.44M Class A) — genuinely aligned, but also means minority holders cannot influence outcomes. |
| Statutory result anomaly | AGM approved a **2025 statutory net loss of CHF 67.9M**, leaving only CHF 31.5M profit carried forward — despite record revenue. Recommend verification of the driver (plausibly equity-comp/one-offs), but a loss year alongside 20%+ growth warrants a look. |

**Assessment:** Governance is not "adequate." A majority-controlled company with co-CEOs who also co-chair the board, and a board that just waved through a founder-driven CEO replacement after a growth warning, gives public shareholders essentially no governance recourse.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**No active SEC enforcement actions or litigation releases were identified** (EDGAR full-text search returned zero hits for ONON). Positive datapoint.

However, other regulatory exposure is material and rising:

- **US tariffs (primary regulatory/trade risk).** The Q3-2026 profit beat was **materially flattered by CHF 52.6M of US tariff refunds** — reported gross margin 72.6% vs. **66.9% ex-refund**. That is a ~570bp gap between the headline and underlying business. FY guidance is ~66% ex-refunds. The market saw through it: shares fell ~7.5% on the print despite an EPS beat ($0.66 vs $0.50 est.).
- **Section 301 forced-labour duties** now apply at **12.5% to both Vietnamese and Chinese-origin goods** (since 24-Jul-2026), narrowing the benefit of the China→Vietnam sourcing shift that the entire footwear industry has relied on. On is a third-party-manufactured, Asia-sourced business with no US manufacturing footprint; it has no tariff hedge.
- **UFLPA / supply-chain due diligence** and incoming EU forced-labour rules create import-detention and disclosure risk for any Asia-sourced footwear brand.
- **Disclosure practice.** ONON pre-announced/updated guidance intra-quarter repeatedly in 2026 (early-March growth warning, 22-Sep positive pre-announcement that lifted shares 7.5% on 43M shares). Frequent mid-quarter resets create Reg FD / selective-disclosure optics risk and, more importantly, signal volatile internal forecasting.
- **Data gaps:** No 10-K, 10-Q, 20-F, or DEF 14A was retrievable via SEC filing tools for ONON (foreign private issuer filing 6-K/20-F with an incomplete EDGAR metadata record). Risk-factor and legal-proceedings language could **not** be directly reviewed — a genuine limitation of this report.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Leadership/key-person and execution risk at the C-suite — highest operational concern.** A first-time-in-role CFO (8 months in) and a new COO now run financial and operational execution while two founders share the CEO chair and the board chair. The company has guided and then missed/reset twice in 2026. Separately, co-CEO + co-Chairman concentration means governance failure and operational failure are the same failure mode.

2. **Tariff-exposed, Asia-concentrated, wholly outsourced supply chain.** 100% third-party manufactured, concentrated in Vietnam/China/Indonesia, with no US production and no disclosed hedging. Section 301 duties at 12.5% on both Vietnam and China origin, plus new US tariff actions, mean gross margin is a policy variable, not a management variable. The FY26 ~66% margin guide already depends on tariff outcomes, and Q3's beat came from a **refund** — the definition of non-recurring.

3. **Direct-to-consumer execution and store-expansion risk.** DTC is now **42.2% of sales and grew 25%**, and management conceded DTC came in *slightly below* plan on soft online traffic; new store openings (Munich, Miami) slipped into Q4. The growth algorithm is shifting from wholesale (12.2% growth) to a capital-intensive, fixed-cost retail model that management has never run at scale. Wholesale also benefited from **pulled-forward holiday shipments**, borrowing from Q4.

*(Secondary: brand durability complaints — see §5 — and foreign-exchange/CHF reporting exposure.)*

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment

**Moderate, with a clear and rising brand-dilution vector — but no active boycott or ESG fire.**

- **Quality/durability criticism is the live issue.** Customer-review channels carry sustained complaints about premature wear, holes, and foot pain relative to price. For a premium-priced ($150–250) performance brand, durability complaints are the classic leading indicator of discounting and share loss.
- **Brand-scarcity vs. scale tension.** On is under direct press scrutiny (e.g., NYT, April 2026) over whether it can expand from performance running into lifestyle/fashion — tennis, training, "all day," young movers — without losing the technical credibility that justifies its price. This is the same trap that damaged Lululemon and Under Armour. Enthusiastic multi-category expansion plus wholesale re-acceleration is how premium brands get diluted.
- **Promotional environment.** Management emphasises maintaining full-price selling "in a highly promotional environment" — a strength today, but it means margin is being defended by scarcity discipline, which expansion directly undermines.
- **No labour/ESG campaign against On specifically was identified**, and no product recall, data breach, or consumer-safety action was found. No negative ESG controversy score surfaced.
- **Management credibility is itself now a reputational asset under stress:** two large single-day drawdowns (Mar 25, Aug 11: −20%) and a "beat" received as a miss because it was tariff-driven.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE-HIGH**

**Rationale:** Financial trajectory remains strong (Q3 record sales CHF 931.4M, +17.2%; GM record ex-refunds; EPS beat) and insider alignment is exceptionally good — the founders put $10.6M of their own money in at the lows. That caps the rating. But the **governance structure is weak and un-remediable by minority holders**, the **top team is 75% new**, and the **earnings quality of the most recent quarter depended on a tariff refund**. A 2.08 beta with two −11%/−20% policy- and guidance-driven gap events in eight months is not a low-risk profile. I would not characterise this as High/Critical — there is no fraud indicator, no enforcement action, no solvency or going-concern question, and insiders are buying — but governance and earnings-quality issues are structural, not transient.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Co-CEO / Co-Chairman fusion of power in two founders** who also control ~59% of voting power. No independent chair, no shareholder recourse. **Escalate to governance committee** — this is a permanent discount factor, not a fixable item.
2. **Q3-26 earnings beat was tariff-refund-dependent** (CHF 52.6M; 72.6% reported GM vs 66.9% underlying). Treat headline EPS $0.66 as *not* a clean number. Request the refund's recurrence/counterparty and any clawback exposure.
3. **CFO/COO newness plus near-zero personal shareholdings** (CFO 997 shares; COO ~7,300) **immediately after a CEO exit.** Verify any severance/acceleration terms for the departed CEO and the 2027 compensation cap (CHF 30M) structure — is it performance-conditioned?
4. **2025 statutory net loss of CHF 67.9M** despite record revenue. Unexplained by the materials reviewed. **Get the driver.**
5. **Sequential guidance resets and mid-quarter pre-announcements** in 2026 (March warning, September positive pre-announcement). Escalate as a disclosure-controls / credibility issue and stress-test your model on management's low-20s growth guide rather than Q3's momentum.
6. **Pulled-forward wholesale revenue** into Q3 from Q4 — verify the size of the Q4 wholesale hole before extrapolating Q3 momentum.
7. **Diligence gap to close:** no 10-K/20-F/10-Q/DEF 14A was retrievable — the risk-factor, legal-proceedings, and full related-party transaction disclosures were **not** reviewed. Have compliance pull the FY2025 20-F and the FY2025 Compensation Report (which discloses a CHF 67.9M loss year and the 2027 pay cap) directly from EDGAR before this rating is finalised.

**Bottom line for the investment memo:** On passes the fraud screen and the insider screen decisively, and its operating momentum is real. It fails the governance screen. The non-financial risk here is not "will the brand break" — it is **"a founder-controlled, dual-class company with fused CEO/Chair roles and an untested management bench just missed expectations twice and reported a tariff-refund quarter."** Position sizing should reflect that shareholders have no governance lever if execution slips again.
