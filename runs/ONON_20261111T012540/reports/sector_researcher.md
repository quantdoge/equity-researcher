# Sector Brief: On Holding AG (ONON)
## Industry: Apparel – Footwear & Accessories | Sector: Consumer Cyclical | Domicile: Switzerland

---

## 1. Sector Overview

ONON competes in the global **athletic footwear and performance apparel** market, valued at roughly **$94.5bn in 2026 and forecast to reach $120.2bn by 2030 (~6.2% CAGR)**. The headline market is **mature and slow-growing**: US footwear retail sales grew only **+1% in H1 2026**, and Circana expects the US industry to close 2026 "generally flat." However, the market is bifurcating sharply — **performance footwear rose +6% and running shoes +13%** in H1 2026 while fashion/lifestyle sneakers stagnated. Concentration is high at the top (Nike, adidas, Deckers/HOKA, Puma, New Balance) but **eroding**: Nike's revenue has fallen from $51.4bn (FY2024) to $46.4bn (FY2026) and Puma's from €8.8bn to €7.0bn, ceding ~$8bn of combined scale that challengers are absorbing. Barriers are **brand-led rather than capital-led** — contract manufacturing in Vietnam/Indonesia/China keeps capex low, so share shifts can happen fast, as HOKA and On have both proven.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Performance running is the one genuinely growing pocket of a flat market.** Running footwear +13% and performance +6% in US H1 2026 vs. total market +1%. On is the purest listed play on this shift, with running-derived credibility (Cloudsurfer 3, Cloudtilt) rather than retro-fashion exposure.
- **Premiumisation and full-price selling.** ONON held full-price sales "in a promotional market" (Q3 2026) and lifted gross margin guidance. Gross margin reached **62.8% in FY2025** — roughly **1,090bps above the peer median of 51.9%** — evidence that brand heat, not discounting, is driving volume.
- **Share capture from incumbents plus channel and geographic whitespace.** Nike and Puma are both shrinking (Puma revenue −20.5% in FY2025, operating margin −5.2%), releasing shelf space in run specialty and general sporting goods. On is still early in APAC/China and in apparel attach, where DTC mix gains carry structurally higher margin.

---

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Tariffs and input-cost/FX pressure.** US tariffs on Southeast Asian footwear supply chains remain the sector's biggest swing factor; On's Q3 2026 gross margin of **72.6%** was materially flattered by **tariff refunds**, so that level is not a clean run-rate. A stronger CHF also translates offshore earnings down.
- **Selective consumer spending in a discretionary category.** Circana's flat-2026 outlook reflects consumers "making intentional purchasing decisions." Footwear is a deferrable purchase — a weak labour market or tariff-driven price increases at the $150–180 price point are direct volume risks.
- **Promotional risk from incumbent clean-up.** Nike (8.2% operating margin, down from 13.6% in FY2024), Puma (loss-making) and Crox (−1.5% revenue, net loss) all have inventory and margin repair ahead. Their markdowns can compress the whole category's pricing, and On's premium positioning is only defensible while discounting stays contained.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

**Rationale for "strong":**
- **Best-in-class growth by a wide margin** — +30.0% vs. a peer median of +0.4%, and the only large-cap name besides Deckers growing at a double-digit rate. Adidas, Nike and Puma are collectively flat-to-shrinking.
- **Highest gross margin in the comp set (62.8%)**, above Deckers (57.7%) and Lululemon (56.6%) despite being a fraction of their scale — a genuine pricing-power signal, not a scale artefact.
- **Expanding, not just high, margins** — +680bps of gross margin and +550bps of operating margin since FY2022, achieved while absorbing tariff costs and heavy brand investment (SG&A up from CHF 600m to CHF 1,517m).
- **Structural share gainer** — the ~$8bn of revenue lost by Nike and Puma since FY2024 has to land somewhere, and On is capturing a visible slice of it.

**Where it is only "average" — and the risks to the rating:**
- **Operating margin (12.5%) still sits ~1,060bps below Deckers (23.1%) and ~740bps below Lululemon (19.9%).** On has brand power but not yet Deckers' or Lululemon's DTC-driven operating leverage; SG&A at 50% of revenue is the gap. The bull case is margin convergence; the bear case is that growth spend never levers.
- **Growth deceleration from ~30% to high-teens** removes the multiple support that a 45x-earnings stock (as recently as FY2025) required.
- **FY2025 net income fell to CHF 203.7m from CHF 242.3m** despite 30% revenue growth — FY2024 was flattered by a below-the-line tax benefit, but the optics still show earnings lagging revenue.
- **High beta (2.08)** — the equity is a discretionary-consumer proxy and will amplify any sector de-rating.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE** (premium performance footwear sub-segment) — **NEUTRAL** for the broad apparel/footwear category

**The distinction matters and should be carried into the investment case.** As a *category*, athletic footwear deserves a **Neutral**: 1% US retail growth, a flat 2026 outlook, 6.2% long-run nominal CAGR, heavy incumbent markdown risk and unresolved tariffs. There is no rising-tide here.

But ONON does not compete in the average of that category. The **performance/running niche is growing 6–13%** and is undergoing a durable share transfer from Nike, adidas and Puma to a small set of challengers (On, HOKA, New Balance). Within that niche the structure is favourable: high gross margins, brand-led barriers, low capital intensity and demonstrated willingness among runners to pay $150–180 at full price. ONON is the strongest-positioned listed pure-play on that sub-cycle, offset by a decelerating growth trajectory and a still-unproven path to Deckers/Lululemon-level operating margins.

**Cycle position:** broad sector **late-cycle/mature**; On's niche **mid-cycle growth** with several years of international and apparel runway remaining.
