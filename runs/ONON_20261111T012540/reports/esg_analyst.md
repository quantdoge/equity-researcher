## 1. ESG Materiality Map

Footwear/apparel is an **asset-light, Scope-3-heavy, brand-dependent** model. The financially material factors are concentrated in the supply chain and the brand promise — not in own operations.

| Factor | Materiality | Transmission mechanism to cash flow / WACC |

| **Scope 3 supply-chain carbon & 2030 SBT delivery** | **Critical** | Brand premium is partly built on sustainability positioning; CSRD/CA disclosure forces public gap; greenwashing litigation risk |

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM** (Transition & Scope 3 sub-rating: **HIGH**)

**The core finding: On is decarbonising per unit while growing so fast that absolute emissions keep rising, and its own report concedes it is off-track against its 2030 science-based target.**

Disclosed trajectory:
- **FY2024:** absolute Scope 1+2 emissions **+13% y/y**; Scope 3 *economic-intensity* emissions **+3% y/y**. On's mitigation: emissions grew slower than production volumes, and average emissions per product fell.
- **FY2025:** per-product carbon emissions down a further **2%** — with explicit management acknowledgement that "we need to accelerate progress to meet our 2030 science-based target," and that the gap can only be closed by scaling renewable energy across **Tier 1 and Tier 2** supply chains.
- At ~25–30% annual revenue growth, a 2%/yr per-unit reduction is arithmetically insufficient: absolute Scope 3 is rising. **This is the single most important ESG fact about ONON.**

Positives (genuine, not marketing):
- **100% renewable electricity** for offices and stores achieved (2024).
- **Coal phased out** across Tier 1 suppliers (2024) — a meaningful, verifiable Scope 3 intervention that many peers have not completed.
- **23% recycled/renewable content in midsole compounds in 2025**, beating a 2027 target two years early.
- **29.7% preferred materials** across all products, traced to source via TextileGenesis.
- **84% of polyester** from recycled/renewable sources (2024, up from 80%).
- **LightSpray™** — robotic one-piece upper replacing a ~200-step assembly process, with first production facility opened in Zurich (2025). This is the most credible structural decarbonisation lever in the portfolio (material efficiency + reshored, renewable-powered production), though it adds Swiss cost base.
- **CleanCloud®** — EVA midsole foam partly derived from captured carbon emissions.

**Hidden liability to flag:** On's *own* CleanCloud lifecycle documentation references a "Pathway 2b (Cut-off Approach)" scenario in which the CleanCloud material's carbon footprint is **higher than fossil-based EVA**. That is an unusually candid disclosure, but it is also precisely the kind of technical caveat that activist litigation or a short-seller report could weaponise against a brand whose marketing leans on this material. **Greenwashing risk is asymmetric here.**

**Footprint estimate (analyst estimate, not company-disclosed):** On does not publish a consolidated absolute tCO₂e total in the reviewed materials. On ~CHF 3.0bn revenue and ~25–30m pairs equivalent, I estimate a total footprint of roughly **400,000–500,000 tCO₂e, >95% Scope 3**, i.e. **≈130–170 tCO₂e per CHF m revenue**. Materially below heavy industry, materially above asset-light software — the issue is trajectory, not level.

**Direct carbon-price sensitivity: LOW.** Unlike a CBAM-exposed industrial, On faces almost no *direct* carbon cost pass-through. The exposure is regulatory disclosure + brand + cost of Tier 2 renewable-energy transition. That is a real but *slow-burn* cost of capital issue, not a cash-flow cliff.

**Physical climate risk: Low-to-Medium.** Retail/store footprint is globally diversified. The concentrated exposure is the **Vietnam footwear supply base**, which faces material heat-stress productivity loss, typhoon/flood disruption, and groundwater stress. On does not disclose a quantified physical-risk scenario analysis.

**Environmental controversies:** No spills, no pollution fines, no remediation liabilities identified.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM**

**Supply chain (the material axis).** On's footwear production is heavily concentrated in **Vietnam**, a moderate-risk jurisdiction (reasonable minimum-wage compliance; restricted freedom of association / no independent unions). On discloses more than most peers:

| Metric | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 factories meeting Global Living Wage Coalition benchmark | 7 of 10 Vietnam footwear suppliers (~80% global footwear volume) | **80.9% of assessed Tier 1** factories, covering **77% of total production volume** |
| Wage vs. local legal minimum (Vietnam Tier 1) | 184% of local minimum | Above legal minimum, all factories |
| Target status | **Missed** 100% footwear target | **Target extended to 2026** |

**Assessment:** The 100% living-wage target has now been **missed and rolled forward once** — a credibility ding, but note the direction of travel is positive: the number of factories assessed more than doubled in 2025. Vietnam Tier 1 wages at ~184% of the legal minimum are genuinely strong; the residual gap is at Tier 2 and in apparel/accessories, which On is only now beginning to assess.

**Policy architecture is above-peer:**
- Human Rights and Labor Rights **Due Diligence Policy** developed with the **International Organization for Migration**.
- Supplier Code of Conduct, Responsible Business Policy, Responsible Exit Policy, Materials Policy — all publicly posted.
- Shift from static audits to a **continuous social compliance scorecard** with findings integrated into sourcing/buying decisions — this addresses the single biggest weakness of audit-based regimes (point-in-time theatre).

**Labour/people risk:** Headcount has roughly doubled to 4,000+ during hypergrowth. No disclosed turnover, injury (TRIR/lost-time), or gender-pay metrics — a **disclosure gap** that becomes a CSRD problem (own-workforce social metrics are mandatory under ESRS S1). Culture dilution during scale-up is a real risk, and the 2026 leadership reshuffle (below) amplifies it.

**Product safety / consumer protection:** Chemical content (PFAS, VOCs, azo dyes) is the primary vector — see Regulatory. On's premium price point and athlete endorsements (Hellen Obiri's NYC Marathon win in LightSpray™) raise the reputational cost of any product-safety failure. No recalls or CPSC actions identified.

**Community / social licence: low risk, real asset.** Right To Run: CHF 2.5m donated to 75+ organisations reaching ~292,000 people (2024); 195,361 community members reached via 100 organisations (2025); 3,029 employee volunteer hours.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**ESG is not a thesis driver for ONON — it is a monitorable tail risk and a modest cost-of-capital consideration. It is not a reason to own or avoid the stock.**

**Why neutral rather than negative:**
- No realised controversies, fines, or litigation — the ESG "accident waiting to happen" is absent.
- No ESG-linked debt, so no financing covenant risk.
- Compliance costs are real but small relative to a 62.8% gross margin and CHF 1.89bn gross profit.
- Traceability and supplier-engagement infrastructure is a genuine relative advantage entering the ESPR/CSRD era.

**Why neutral rather than positive:**
- Absolute emissions are rising against a public science-based target in a business growing >25%/yr. The math does not close without Tier 2 renewable procurement that On has not yet demonstrated at scale.
- Governance has moved *backwards* in 2026: professional CEO out, founders back in as Co-CEOs **and** Co-Chairs of a dual-class company. Governance-screened and SFDR Article 8/9 funds face structural limits on position size.
- PFAS deadlines (CA 2025/2027, EU 2027) land directly on the outdoor/waterproof growth vector.

**What would move us to Negative:** a formal greenwashing challenge (EU Green Claims, state AG, or NGO complaint) targeting CleanCloud® or "preferred materials"; disclosure of a forced-labour finding in the Vietnam chain; or FY2026 disclosure that absolute Scope 3 rose again with the 2030 SBT formally abandoned or restated.

**What would move us to Positive:** verified Tier 2 renewable energy contracting with quantified absolute-emissions decline (not just intensity); achievement of the 100% living-wage target in 2026; LightSpray™ scaled beyond pilot volumes; and introduction of ESG metrics into executive variable pay.

**Monitoring checklist for the FY2026 Impact Report:** (1) absolute tCO₂e, not just per-product intensity; (2) Tier 2 renewable energy %; (3) living-wage coverage — did the 77%→100% roll-forward hold?; (4) PFAS-free certification across outdoor range; (5) any change to Class B voting rights or board independence following the 2026 AGM's approved Class B conversion.
