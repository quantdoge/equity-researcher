# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Citigroup Inc. (NYSE: C)
**Sector:** Financial Services (Banks – Diversified) | **Mkt Cap:** ~$220B | **CEO:** Jane Fraser | **As of:** Oct 2026

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate (with notable weaknesses)**

- **CEO/Chair duality reintroduced:** In October 2025 the board elected CEO Jane Fraser as Chair, reuniting roles that had been separated for nearly two decades. John Dugan (Chair since 2019) transitioned to Lead Independent Director — a partial structural offset — but the move concentrates power in one individual who also received a **one-time $25M retention stock award**.
- **Compensation optics:** Fraser's 2025 comp was approved at ~**$42M**, up ~22% YoY. 2026 say-on-pay support fell, with a proxy adviser recommending a "no" vote — a material decline in shareholder endorsement of pay-for-performance alignment.
- **Board:** Independent chair governance was the norm for ~18 years; the board retains institutional process, but independence is now structurally weaker.
- **Insider activity (see §3):** benign, compensation-driven pattern; no disposals of concern.
- **No fraud indicators surfaced** in EDGAR enforcement search (tool returned empty — see gaps).

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **Active consent orders (elevated):** The **October 2020 Federal Reserve cease-and-desist and OCC consent orders** (risk management, data governance, internal controls) were **still in effect as of Dec 2025**. Fines to date: **$400M (2020 OCC)** plus **$135.6M (July 2024: $75M OCC + $60.6M Fed)** — an unusual second penalty levied specifically for *insufficient remediation progress*. A Feb 2024 Fed rebuke on counterparty credit risk complemented this.
- **Positive trajectory:** OCC withdrew the 2024 order amendment tied to the $900M Revlon manual-transfer incident (Dec 2025); the Fed closed three trading-risk management notices (Dec 2025); Reuters (Feb 2026) reports Citi targets **completing consent-order work in 2026**, with compliance spend declining after 2025.
- **Other actions:** CFTC $1.5M fine (Sep 2025, inaccurate large-trader reports, 2015–23); UK FCA ~$14.8M (£12.5M) for trade-monitoring failures; earlier data-governance and $900M Revlon payment failures.
- **Emerging exposure:** Expanded stablecoin/digital-payments infrastructure with Coinbase (Sep 2026) adds a new, fast-moving regulatory surface (crypto-asset, settlement, AML/CFT).
- *Gap: EDGAR enforcement-search tool returned no records; cannot independently confirm absence of SEC/DOJ actions.*

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis (last 12 months)

- 25 Form 4 filings: clusters on **2026-10-02** (10 filings), **2026-07-02** (10), plus April/May singles.
- Sample detail (director form, Oct 1, 2026) shows **acquisition of 6 common + 160 deferred shares** — routine deferred-compensation accretion, not disposition.
- **Assessment:** Clustering at quarter-end aligns with equity award/vesting cycles; **no evidence of insider selling pressure**. Caveat: transaction-level buy/sell attribution by officer was not fully available from filing metadata.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Transformation execution overhang:** A multi-year, cost-heavy remediation program sits under a **still-live consent order;** the 2026 completion target is the single biggest operational dependency, and 2026 reductions in compliance spend increase slippage risk.
2. **Cyber and third-party exposure:** Nov 2025 **SitusAMC supply-chain breach** affected Citi among major banks; an **unverified dark-web claim of a "Citigroup Securities data breach"** (mid-2026) remains unconfirmed — unreliable posture signaling comes at a bad time given the data-governance consent order.
3. **Manual-process/payments-operations risk:** The $900M erroneous manual Revlon transfer (2022) legacy drove OCC-mandated straight-through-processing redesign; residual manual-payment risk is both financially and regulatorily consequential.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Moderate**

- Repeated headline regulatory fines, the 2020 control failures, and the unusual "fined for slow progress" penalty keep a negative compliance narrative alive.
- **Pay-vs-job-cuts tension:** ~$42M CEO package (+22%) and $25M award alongside 2026's **widespread middle/back-office layoffs and offshoring** is a classic flashpoint; rising say-on-pay opposition corroborates stakeholder unease.
- Russia exit **completed Feb 2026** (sale to Renaissance Capital) — removes a long geopolitical overhang, but total legacy cost ~$1.2B+ and a 13% Q4-2025 profit decline drew negative press.
- Consumer risk surface: card delinquencies ticking up; aggressive **Bitcoin/stablecoin positioning** (BTC target $113K; Coinbase tie-up) adds volatile, consumer-facing reputational risk — a bet that cuts both ways.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **Moderate** *(regulatory dimension: High; trending improving)*

The profile is dominated by the still-active 2020 consent orders and the unprecedented second fine for slow remediation — a genuine High on the regulatory axis. Offsetting: regulators have begun withdrawing pieces (OCC amendment; three Fed notices), 2026 completion is telegraphed and feasible, Russia risk is closed, insider activity is benign, and no fraud or whistleblower signals appeared. Net assessment: **Moderate**, one rating notch above peer-bank norms, with clear escalation paths below.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Consent-order slippage:** Any missed 2026 completion target or new regulator rebuke (e.g., on counterparty credit or data governance) → escalate regulatory rating to **High/Critical**.
2. **Unverified cyber breach claim:** The dark-web claim re: Citigroup Securities must be resolved; **if confirmed, cyber/operational risk escalates to Critical**.
3. **CEO/Chair duality + pay:** Reunified roles, $25M retention award, and declining say-on-pay support warrant board-level monitoring in the next proxy cycle.
4. **Digital-asset buildout:** Stablecoin/Coinbase infrastructure expansion introduces unproven operational and AML/CFT risk — escalate if any supervisory objection or incident occurs.
