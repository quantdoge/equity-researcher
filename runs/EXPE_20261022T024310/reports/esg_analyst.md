## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **Medium**

**Positives**
- **SBTi-validated near-term targets** (1.5°C classification): −75% absolute Scope 1+2 GHG by 2030 vs 2022 base; **78% of suppliers by emissions** (purchased goods/services, capital goods, business travel) to set science-based targets. This is a credible, third-party-validated commitment, not just marketing.
- **−31% YoY reduction in total GHG emissions** reported in the 2024 Global Impact Report — real trajectory, partly structural (cloud migration, office consolidation).
- Energy use ~179,378 GJ (2025) with ~73% renewable (self-reported).
- Asset-light marketplace model means direct operational footprint is small relative to revenue; carbon intensity per dollar of gross bookings is structurally low for Scope 1+2.

**Negatives / watch items**
- **No SBTi-validated net-zero target** (Net-Zero Status: "–" in the registry) despite a public 2040 net-zero ambition and "carbon neutral since 2017" claim. Neutrality since 2017 rests substantially on **offsets** covering Scope 1/2 plus business travel — a claim increasingly challenged by regulators and investors (greenwashing/claim-substantiation risk).
- **Scope 3 is the real exposure.** Flights, hotel stays and vacation rentals sold on the platform generate emissions orders of magnitude above Scope 1+2. Expedia's influence is indirect (supplier targets, Travalyst disclosure frameworks) — meaningful but not controlling. I could not retrieve absolute Scope 3 tonnage (data gap).
- **Physical risk** is demand-side: hurricane/wildfire/flood disruption in core leisure destinations, plus resort safety incidents (see Social).

**Verdict:** Genuine, validated ambition with a structurally hard-to-abate value chain. No stranded-asset or CBAM exposure. Not a carbon-cost-driven credit risk, but an offsets/greenwashing reputational risk.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **Medium-High**

**Product safety — the single largest social liability**
- Litigation alleges **Expedia/Vrbo rents thousands of properties that violate its own fire-safety rules**, tied to a Dominican Republic rental fire that killed four people. Separately, families of three young women who died of **carbon monoxide poisoning** at a Dominican Republic resort booked through Expedia have sued, alleging negligently installed/faulty water heaters. Vrbo/Airbnb-style listings do not universally require CO detectors.
- These are **wrongful-death claims with punitive-damage potential**, plus a documented gap between published safety policy and enforcement — the classic "policy without controls" fact pattern that invites both litigation and regulatory action (FTC/DSA marketplace due-diligence duties).

**Consumer protection**
- A **bait-and-switch/drip-pricing class action settlement has been approved**; a separate proposed class action alleges Expedia **artificially inflates hotel prices (a "10–25% hotel price rule") and restricts price competition**. Consumer-protection exposure is recurring, not one-off.

**Labour**
- ~**1,500 roles cut in Feb 2024 (~9% of workforce)**; further 2025 restructuring of tech, product and finance teams; a WARN notice covering **162 permanent layoffs**; reported pattern of eliminating long-tenured roles while hiring ~250 new positions. Employee-review themes: "great benefits and culture, but high stress and layoff concerns." Repeated restructuring raises attrition, institutional-knowledge loss and execution risk — directly relevant to a product/AI-led turnaround thesis.

**Human rights**
- Lodging marketplaces carry inherent trafficking/exploitation exposure (sector-wide litigation trend against hotel brands). No EXPE-specific trafficking action was identified in my search — an absence of evidence rather than evidence of absence, and a tail risk given the size of the Vrbo listing base.

**Diversity**
- **Female CEO** (Ariane Gorin) and **4 of 11 directors are women (~36%)** — above average for large-cap boards. Workforce-level gender/ethnicity representation data was not retrievable (gap).

**Positives**
- "Open World" strategy: accessibility filters (elevators, service animals, sign-language staff), LGBTQIA+ welcoming-property search, access for underserved travellers; Travalyst Coalition and Glasgow Declaration signatory; Accor/UNESCO sustainable-tourism partnership.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Structure — the core weakness**
- **Dual-class control:** 114.5M common shares vs **5.5M Class B shares carrying supervoting rights**; Diller holds voting control via an irrevocable proxy arrangement with the Malone Group. **Three of eleven directors are elected solely by common stockholders** — a structural device that caps minority influence and sustains a governance discount.
- **Board independence: 7 of 11 (~64%)** — below the ~75–85% typical of large caps. Non-independent: Diller (Chairman), Gorin (CEO), Dara Khosrowshahi, Alex von Furstenberg.
- **Chairman/CEO separation is intact** (Diller Chairman, Gorin CEO) — a genuine positive.

**Tenure and interlocks**
- Diller 21.2 yrs, Khosrowshahi 21.2 yrs, Jacobson 18.9 yrs — long tenures limit refreshment and independence of judgment. **Khosrowshahi is Uber's CEO**, an interlock in an adjacent travel/transport ecosystem; von Furstenberg shares a PPLI directorship with Diller.

**Compensation**
- **Say-on-pay passed with ~89% support** (129.9M for / 15.4M against) — solid but not resounding. Support was dented in prior years by a **discretionary modification of 2021 PSUs** (post-performance partial payout). FY2025 PSUs were redesigned into three consecutive one-year measurement periods tied to **revenue growth and Adjusted EBITDA margin** — more frequent checkpoints, but **no ESG metric in executive pay**, a gap versus ESG-leaning peers. 2025 PSU payout was 176.9%. Clawback policy and stock-ownership guidelines exist (positive).

**Shareholder voice**
- **No shareholder ESG proposal on the 2026 ballot** (three management proposals only) — consistent with the ~47% national decline in ESG proposals, but also reflecting the difficulty of advancing proposals against a controlled company. **PAR Capital (activist, ~1.6%)** is the notable engaged holder; Vanguard/BlackRock/State Street hold ~19% combined but have limited leverage under the supervote structure. Board grade from third-party analysis: **B+**.

**Anti-corruption/ethics:** formal program presumed but not independently verified (gap).

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **FTC Unfair or Deceptive Fees Rule (US, effective 2025):** explicitly covers short-term lodging **including online marketplaces such as Vrbo**; requires total price upfront, bans drip pricing. Direct hit to ancillary/resort-fee display economics and to conversion; compliance build cost in 2025–26.
- **State price-transparency and consumer-protection laws** (parallel regimes) plus **state AG activity**.
- **EU Short-Term Rental Regulation (EU) 2024/1028:** host registration and data-sharing obligations, applicable from **20 May 2026** — affects Vrbo inventory in the EU, a core profit region; risk of listing attrition and compliance infrastructure cost.
- **EU Digital Services Act:** marketplace duties (host verification/KYC, illegal-content and safety transparency) — the direct regulatory hook for the Vrbo safety issue.
- **EU Platform-to-Business Regulation (2019/1150):** CJEU proceedings involving Airbnb Ireland, Expedia, Google Ireland and Amazon on platform-to-business obligations — contractual transparency and terms-of-service risk with hotel partners.
- **EU CSRD:** Expedia's EU subsidiary turnover and headcount place it in scope on a phased basis; sector guidance for tourism adds Scope 3 and social reporting plus limited assurance costs. Management time and audit fees rise; disclosure quality improves.
- **California SB 253/261:** in scope on US revenue thresholds — Scope 1/2/3 reporting and climate-risk disclosure.
- **CBAM:** immaterial (no goods imports).
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified (gap) — cost-of-capital transmission is via equity governance discount rather than loan pricing.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **Average** (governance-overhang; social risk elevated)

Third-party corroboration: **S&P Global ESG Score 39** (CSA 33) for Hotels/Resorts & Cruise Lines — mid-pack, below sustainability leaders; questionzero score **5.2/10**, "in line with average." Environmental leadership is offset by a dual-class governance structure that would normally mark a Laggard, and by rising consumer-protection/product-safety litigation that marks Social as Medium-High. Net: solidly Average, with identifiable risk flags rather than a broad ESG-risk designation.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **Neutral**

**Why not Negative:** none of these factors plausibly moves near-term cash flows by more than low-single-digit percentages against a ~$32.7B market cap. Legal accruals are small relative to EBITDA; environmental leadership (validated 1.5°C-aligned targets, −31% emissions) is a genuine positive for European institutional demand and for B2B/Egencia corporate-travel RFPs where supplier carbon disclosure is increasingly a procurement criterion. Female CEO and 36% female board support access to ESG-mandate capital.

**Why not Positive:** the dual-class structure is a permanent governance discount — minority holders cannot force change, which caps any re-rating from ESG improvement and limits the effectiveness of PAR Capital's engagement. No ESG metric in executive pay means sustainability execution depends on management goodwill, not compensation alignment. The structural Scope 3 problem and the offsets-based neutrality claim leave greenwashing headline risk. Rising US consumer-protection enforcement (fees, price claims) and EU STR/DSA obligations land squarely on the two growth engines — Vrbo and international.

**Bottom line for the thesis:** ESG is **not** the swing factor in EXPE. Treat governance (dual-class, 64% independence, long tenure) as an embedded valuation discount and a constraint on activist-driven value creation; treat Vrbo safety/consumer-protection litigation and EU STR/DSA compliance as **watch items with bounded but non-trivial downside**. The environmental program is a modest positive for franchise access and enterprise demand. If the Vrbo safety litigation broadens into a class action or regulatory action on host verification, re-rate to **Negative**; if the company adopts SBTi-validated net-zero targets and adds an ESG metric to PSUs, re-rate toward **Positive**.
