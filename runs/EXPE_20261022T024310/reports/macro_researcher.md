## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: TRANSITIONAL — late-cycle re-acceleration, risk-off tilt.**

The regime has inverted versus 2025. The Fed cut from 5.25–5.50% down to 3.50–3.75% by December 2025, and that easing cycle supported exactly the kind of high-beta consumer cyclical that EXPE is. Through 2026 that has stalled: headline CPI re-accelerated to **3.40% y/y (Aug 2026)** from 3.0% in Sept 2025, with **energy CPI up 16.28% y/y** following the Middle East escalation of 28 February 2026 and Strait of Hormuz disruption. The 10-year Treasury has risen to **~5.15%** from ~4.02% a year ago — a ~113bp bear steepening driven by inflation expectations and term premium, not by growth optimism. FOMC commentary through 2026 has shifted from a cutting bias to a **hiking bias**; the Chandler Asset note of 9 October 2026 argues "the case for another Fed rate increase this year grew stronger," while the WSJ reports a cooler inflation print "eases pressure for a rate hike next week" — i.e. a live, coin-flip hike at the late-October meeting. Real GDP is still solid (Q3 2025 +4.4%), and consumers are nominally spending, but sentiment is deteriorating ("consumer gloom" alongside rising holiday-spending forecasts) and the fuel shock is squeezing real discretionary income.

For EXPE this is the least comfortable of macro mixes: demand is holding up, but the discount rate, the dollar and fuel costs are all moving against it.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

EXPE is an **asset-light, commission-based travel intermediary**. It does not own aircraft, hotels or inventory — so it has almost no direct fuel-cost line. Its macro exposure is therefore almost entirely **demand-side and translation-side**, plus a valuation channel. That matters: a fuel shock hits EXPE indirectly, through airline economics and consumer budgets, not through its own P&L cost base.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating

### NEUTRAL (with a negative skew)

**Justification:** The demand side is genuinely favourable — Q2 2026 bookings +12%, revenue +14%, EBITDA +23%, and consumers are explicitly "prioritizing travel-based spending" even as confidence falls. That offsets the financial-conditions side. But three variables are all moving adversely at once: **hike expectations, a 5.15% 10Y, and a strengthening dollar** — each of which hits a high-multiple, internationally exposed consumer cyclical through a different channel (multiple, funding cost, translation). The rating is Neutral rather than Unfavourable only because the company's operating momentum is strong enough to absorb a moderate macro deterioration, and because a ceasefire-led oil retreat would quickly reverse the fuel and inflation headwinds.

The skew is negative because EXPE's valuation leaves little cushion: a hike at the late-October FOMC plus sticky energy inflation would compress the multiple at the same time as it squeezes the consumer.

## 5. Material Surprise Scenario (Bear)

**"Oil shock meets hiking Fed — the double squeeze."**

The live risk into the late-October FOMC is a second 2026 hike on inflation-expectations grounds (per DWS's credibility framing) **combined with** a re-escalation in the Middle East that pushes jet fuel well above the $3.95/gal February peak. In that path: airfares surge through Q4 2026 and Q1 2027, airline capacity is cut, US consumer confidence — already weak — breaks down into actual spending retrenchment, and EXPE's bookings decelerate from +12% toward low single digits while take rates compress as consumers trade down to budget lodging. With 10Y through 5.25% and the dollar pushing 104+, reported international revenue takes a further translation hit. Under that combination EXPE retests the February 2026 low around **$185–200**, roughly −30% from today.

*Upside mirror case (lower probability but real):* a durable Middle East ceasefire plus a cooler inflation print at the October FOMC removes the hike and lets the 10Y drift back toward 4.60%. Oil retreats, the dollar softens, and EXPE — with Q2 momentum already proven — re-rates through $320 on multiple expansion rather than earnings.
