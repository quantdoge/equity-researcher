## EXPEDIA GROUP (EXPE) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $285.64 | Market cap ~$37B | EV ~$37.1B | Sector: Consumer Cyclical / Travel Services**

---

### 1. Growth Story

Expedia is the world's #2 online travel platform ($120B+ annualized gross bookings) executing a self-help margin story that the market is still pricing as a declining asset. The thesis is not "travel grows" — it's that EXPE's take-rate is inflecting upward as mix shifts from paid-search-dependent retail bookings toward **B2B distribution (+21% gross bookings in Q2 2026) and high-margin advertising**, while gross margin has already expanded ~790bps in four years to 90.1% and operating margin more than quintupled to 12.7%. The kicker: at ~11.6x 2027E adjusted EPS and 12x free cash flow, with ~4–5%/yr share shrinkage from buybacks, EXPE trades at roughly *half* the EV/EBITDA of Booking Holdings and *less than half* of Airbnb — an incumbent growing high-single to low-double digits being valued as if AI had already killed it.

### 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **Global online travel market:** ~$626B (2024) → ~$1.46T by 2032 at an **11.1% CAGR**; alternative estimates put 2025 at ~$692B growing to ~$1.98T by 2035. OTA-intermediated spend specifically reaches ~$1.32T by 2033.
- **EXPE's capture:** ~$120B+ gross bookings ≈ **8–10% of online travel**, and only ~5–6% of *total* global travel & tourism spend. Even in its mature core, penetration is single-digit.
- **The real TAM isn't retail bookings — it's the three underpenetrated adjacencies:**
  1. **B2B distribution** — tech platform powering ~60,000 partners (airlines, banks, corporate travel, OTAs, retailers). Growing 21% vs 5% for total bookings. Structurally higher-margin because it bypasses consumer marketing spend.
  2. **Advertising & media** — Expedia Media Solutions monetizing its first-party travel intent data. Near-100% incremental margin; this is where the "data network effect" moat actually compounds. Recent hiring (Lead BD Manager, Advertising) confirms resourcing.
  3. **Alternative accommodation (Vrbo)** — the highest-growth, highest-ADR supply category and the direct counter to Airbnb's $108B market cap.
- **Penetration math:** each 100bps of op-margin expansion on a $16B revenue base is ~$160M of EBITDA — against a 2,200bps gap to Booking's 34.5% operating margin. **The margin runway alone is worth more than the current EBITDA.**

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (asymmetric value-plus-growth; not a frontier-compounder Strong Buy)

EXPE is not a Cathie Wood-style disruptor — it is the incumbent being disrupted, and it **fails the Rule of 40 at 28.7** against Airbnb's 48.6 and Booking's 47.2. I cannot award Strong Buy to a business growing 7–10% with a 12.7% operating margin in a category where the leader runs 34.5%.

But the *mispricing* is too wide to ignore. The market pays 16x EBITDA for EXPE versus 37x for Airbnb — a 2.3x discount — while EXPE carries the largest margin catch-up runway in the sector, has beaten estimates in 6 of 7 quarters by an average 11.5%, has seen its 2030 EPS estimate revised **+9.2%**, and shrinks its share count ~5%/yr against $3.1B of FCF. At 11.6x 2027E adjusted EPS for ~18% EPS growth (PEG ~0.6), the market is implying near-zero perpetual growth. Consensus 2030 revenue of $20.8B is a 7.2% CAGR — undemanding against an 11% TAM CAGR. **Target: mid-$300s (13–14x 2027E EPS), ~20–25% upside; re-rating to 16–17x on sustained B2B/advertising mix shift gets to $400+.** Position size modestly and add on Q3 confirmation — this is a re-rating trade, not a 10-year hold.

### 7. Key Risks

1. **AI/agentic disintermediation (the thesis-breaker).** EXPE spends ~$8.95B/yr on sales & marketing — largely a paid-search and metasearch funnel. If discovery migrates to Google AI Mode/AI Overviews and LLM shopping agents, that funnel's value collapses and OTAs get commoditized into pure inventory suppliers. The Google I/O read-through is currently favorable (agentic flow needs EXPE supply), but this is unproven and the market's discount is rational, not irrational.
2. **Deceleration already embedded in guidance.** H1 2026 revenue grew 14%; the raised ~$16.22B FY26 guide implies H2 growth of only ~7%. Booking growth of just +5% in Q2 (vs revenue +14%) means volume is soft and the beat is coming from monetization — a lever with a ceiling.
3. **Competitive gap widening, not narrowing.** Booking grows 13.4% at 34.5% op margin with 100% gross margin structure and deeper non-US supply. Airbnb grows 10.8% at a 37.8% FCF margin. EXPE remains structurally #2 with a weaker tech platform, thinner international share, and Trivago (metasearch) exposed directly to AI search disruption.
4. **Adjusted-earnings quality.** ~40% of "adjusted" EPS is add-backs; GAAP diluted EPS was $9.81 in 2025 vs ~$16.0 adjusted. Buybacks partly offset SBC dilution rather than genuinely compounding per-share value. On GAAP the stock is 29x, not 11.6x.
5. **Sell-side momentum is deteriorating at the target level.** Recent October 2026 actions were all *cuts* (Melius $355 from $372; Wells Fargo $275; Truist $288 from $309) and Morgan Stanley initiated **Underweight at $235** in September. The +9.2% long-run EPS revision is bullish, but near-term conviction is splitting — expect volatility around Nov 4.
6. **Cyclical and currency exposure.** Beta 1.36; record gas prices, cross-border headwinds, and a consumer spending downturn would hit bookings directly. B2B growth also brings partner concentration and credit risk at lower take rates.
