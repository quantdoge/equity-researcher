## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE (low-A / mid-BBB), Negative Momentum**

ELV remains solidly investment grade: 2.3x net leverage, 5.2–5.8x interest coverage, $44.8B of book equity (36% of assets), no near-term maturity wall (current portion of debt only **$375M** vs. $30.4B long-term). But the *trajectory* is unambiguously negative. **S&P lowered ELV to 'A-' from 'A' citing a lower capital adequacy assessment, outlook stable.** (Fitch's entity page surfaces a 'BBB+' context; Moody's not verified — treat non-S&P ratings as a data gap.)

Every credit metric has deteriorated for four straight years while revenue compounded:

| | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | TTM Q3'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating margin | 6.45% | 5.40% | 5.10% | 5.03% | **4.07%** | **3.69%** |
| EBIT / interest | 10.9x | 9.9x | 8.5x | 7.7x | **5.8x** | 5.2x* |
| Gross debt / EBITDA | 2.48x | 2.55x | 2.54x | 3.12x | **3.44x** | 3.46x |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 2.00x | 1.82x | 1.92x | 2.33x | **2.46x** | 2.30x |
| Free cash flow | $7.28B | $7.25B | $6.77B | $4.55B | **$3.17B** | n/a |

*Revenue +63% (2020→2025) while operating income grew +5%. Margin fell 238bp. This is margin-dilutive growth, and it is the single most important fact in this report.*

The one genuine offset: **net debt fell from $23.7B to $20.4B over nine months of 2026** and gross debt from $33.2B to $30.8B. Deleveraging is underway; leverage direction in 2026 is the first positive turn in the series.

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue −20%

Base (TTM): revenue $201.8B, EBIT $7.46B (3.69%), interest ~$1.44B, EBITDA $8.88B. Cost structure: benefit costs $148.2B (variable), SG&A $42.8B (assume 60% variable / 40% fixed → $17.1B fixed), D&A $1.5B.

| | Revenue | EBIT | EBIT margin | Interest coverage | Net income |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base | $201.8B | $7.46B | 3.69% | 5.2x | $5.14B |
| **A: −20%, cost flex as assumed** | $161.4B | **$3.13B** | 1.96% | **2.2x** | ~$1.3B |
| **B: −20%, 30% of SG&A fixed (cost cuts)** | $161.4B | **$3.93B** | 2.44% | **2.7x** | ~$1.9B |
| **C: −20% + 150bp adverse MLR** | $161.4B | **$0.74B** | 0.46% | **0.5x** | **−$0.5B (loss)** |

**The important conclusion is not the base case — it's that all three cases are broken.** Even the benign cost-cutting case halves coverage from 5.2x to 2.7x. In Case C the company fails to cover interest from operations and posts a net loss. Health-insurer revenue shrinkage is also *adversely selective* — the healthy exit first and the MLR worsens, which is precisely why Case C is the realistic downside, not Case A.

Secondary effects:
- **Buyback capacity disappears.** FCF likely turns negative (capex $1.1B is sticky). Management has ~$4.13B of discretionary returns ($1.53B dividend + $2.61B buyback) to cut — the buyback alone restores ~$2.6B. This is the primary mitigant.
- **Regulatory capital.** The S&P downgrade was driven by a capital-adequacy assessment. A revenue shock compresses subsidiary RBC ratios, inviting a further downgrade → higher funding cost → pro-cyclical spiral. This is the real transmission channel, not the debt/EBITDA math.
- **Goodwill impairment.** Goodwill + intangibles of **$39.2B = 88% of book equity ($44.8B)**. A large Carelon/legacy write-down is non-cash and covenant-neutral but would wipe out the majority of book value and mark the acquisition strategy as value-destructive.
- **Covenant:** debt/total capital 40.7% vs. ~60% — ELV would need roughly $22B+ of additional debt or equity destruction to trip it. **No covenant risk in any modeled scenario.**

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE** (trending toward Moderate-High)

Balance-sheet integrity is not in question — this is a solvent, cash-generative, investment-grade company with a well-laddered debt stack and falling net debt in 2026. The risk is **earnings-quality erosion**: a structural margin decline (645bp → 369bp), an interest-coverage ratio that has halved, and FCF that has fallen 56% in four years while returns of capital now exceed FCF.

**Relative context vs. UNH (FY2025):**
| | ELV | UNH |
|---|---|---|
| Net debt/EBITDA | 2.46x | 2.18x |
| EBIT/interest | **5.79x** | 4.74x |
| Current ratio | **1.24** | 0.79 |
| FCF yield | 3.57% | **5.05%** |

ELV is *less* leveraged and *better* covered than UNH on interest, but converts earnings into cash far worse. **Caveat: current ratio is a near-useless liquidity gauge for managed care** — UNH's 0.79 with −$24.3B working capital is normal because medical-claims-payable are non-debt obligations funded by premium receivables. ELV's 1.24 is not a strength; do not read it as one.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Interest coverage has halved** (10.9x → 5.2x) with interest expense compounding at ~15% annually while EBIT shrinks. FY2025 interest rose 18% on a 8.7% EBIT decline.
2. **Returns exceed free cash flow.** 2025: $4.13B returned vs. $3.17B FCF = **130% FCF payout**, funded from the balance sheet. Sustainable only while leverage falls elsewhere.
3. **Free cash flow down 56% since 2021** ($7.28B → $3.17B). The driver is working capital: −$3.28B in 2025 vs. +$0.78B in 2021. Receivables grew 16.7% ($13.1B → $15.2B) on 12.6% revenue growth — **collection quality is deteriorating and days-in-receivables is rising.**
4. **S&P downgrade to 'A-'** explicitly tied to capital adequacy — a credit signal, not a market-noise event.
5. **Piotroski fails on accruals quality:** FY2025 net income $5.66B vs. operating cash flow $4.29B (accruals of $1.37B). Earnings are running ahead of cash.
6. **Capital allocation has destroyed value.** $13.1B of buybacks 2021–2025 at an average of ~$493/share (shares −11.6%) versus a $400.84 current price, funded partly with $8.3B of incremental debt. Middle-of-the-fairway capital misallocation.
7. **Goodwill/intangibles at 88% of equity** — heavy impairment surface, non-cash but book-value-relevant.
8. **Event risk is severe and unhedged:** five >7% single-day drawdowns in 34 months, negative 3-year Sharpe.
