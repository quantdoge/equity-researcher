## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — **this is the dominant risk in the name**

**Active / material:**

1. **CMS intermediate sanctions (imposed 27-Feb-2026) for "substantial and persistent noncompliance" with Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment data reporting.** Sanctions include **suspension of MA/Part-D enrollment and marketing**. Effective date was rolled from 31-Mar → 30-May → 1-Jul-2026 as Elevance demonstrated remediation progress; press coverage through mid-2026 describes repeated **reprieves, not resolution**. Enrollment freeze remains a live tail risk into 2027. (Status as of Nov-2026 not confirmable from available sources — **escalate for confirmation**.)
2. **Financial exposure:** Elevance booked **~$935M** (range +$565M / –$585M) as the estimated cost to remediate/avoid sanctions, plus potential CMS recovery of risk-adjustment overpayments. Stock fell ~14% over 2–3 Mar-2026 on the sanction notice (see §5).
3. **DOJ/FCA risk-adjustment enforcement is a sector-wide priority in 2026** — Kaiser affiliates $556M, Independence Blue Cross $22.5M (Sept-2026), Complete Health $14.1M; DOJ/OIG guidance explicitly targets chart-review and diagnosis-capture practices. Elevance (as Anthem) has prior FCA risk-adjustment litigation history. A qui tam surfacing from the current CMS findings is a plausible, non-trivial tail event.
4. **PBM regulation:** The **2026 Consolidated Appropriations Act** requires PBMs to remit **100% of rebates/fees** to plan clients; FTC settled with Express Scripts (Feb-2026); multiple states ban spread pricing and impose transparency. CarelonRx is directly in scope → margin compression and compliance build-out cost.
5. **Government-program dependence:** Medicaid (~1.75% guided FY26 operating margin, negative), Medicare Advantage and Individual/ACA. Enhanced ACA subsidy expiry, 2027 rate filings, state Medicaid rate adequacy and redetermination timing are all exogenous swing factors the company does not control.
6. **Legacy conduct overhang:** 2015 Anthem cyberbreach (~78.8M records) and the blocked 2017 Anthem–Cigna merger remain reputational/regulatory precedents.
7. **EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned zero hits for ELV** — no SEC enforcement action surfaced in this dataset (possible tool limitation; treat as weakly positive).

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Risk-adjustment data infrastructure is the single point of failure.** CMS found "substantial and persistent" noncompliance in diagnosis-data submissions — this is a systems/process/controls defect, not a one-off. Remediation is consuming ~$935M and senior management attention, and any relapse triggers an immediate MA enrollment freeze (a direct revenue-growth shock) in the company's most profitable growth franchise.
2. **Heavy reliance on government program pricing it does not control.** Medicaid (~negative margin), MA rates, and ACA subsidies are set politically. Q2-2026 showed how fast this transmits: **adjusted operating margin 3.6% vs 5.0% y/y**, net income –24%, and a 8.5% single-day stock drop; nine-month operating gain fell **25.7%** even as adjusted EPS rose — a quality-of-earnings gap investors are already discounting.
3. **Carelon/PBM integration and regulatory re-engineering.** CarelonRx must rebuild rebate/fee economics under the 2026 CAA and a patchwork of state spread-pricing bans, while Carelon Services carries risk-based provider contracts. Execution failure here removes the diversification that currently offsets Health Benefits margin erosion. (Also: **cyber** — a company with a 78.8M-record breach in its history and 96k employees is structurally a high-value target; no new 2026 incident was identified, but this is unverified, not cleared.)

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Moderate, event-driven**

- The Feb–Mar 2026 sanctions story was covered by STAT, Reuters, Fierce Healthcare and trade press as a *"can't enroll new Medicare members"* headline — one of the most consumer-legible forms of insurer criticism. It landed as a **~14% two-day drawdown** (2–3 Mar-2026) on ~5x normal volume, confirming the market treats it as franchise-damaging, not technical.
- November 2026 weakness is **sector-driven, not idiosyncratic**: ELV –7.6% on 18-Nov alongside Centene –7.0% ("insurers fell for a second day"), with the sell-side actually turning *more constructive* (upgrade 18-Nov; CFO guiding ≥12% 2027 EPS growth at the UBS conference 13-Nov). No ELV-specific reputational event in the last 60 days.
- The company is running visible reputation-management activity (Harris Poll AI-in-healthcare survey, Carelon clinical-outcomes messaging) — consistent with a management team trying to reframe the narrative.
- No evidence found of a 2026 data breach, product recall, or state AG action specific to Elevance.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: idiosyncratic, unresolved, regulator-imposed operating restriction (CMS intermediate sanctions) with a quantifiable but wide-ranged cost ($0.35B–$1.5B) and a binary escalation path (MA enrollment suspension); a sector-leading DOJ risk-adjustment enforcement wave that makes an FCA suit a live tail; and a business model whose margins are set by payers it does not control, evidenced by a 140bp y/y margin collapse. Offsetting: independent board chair, CEO open-market buying into the drawdown, stable investment-grade ratings, no SEC enforcement hits found, and improving Q3 trends.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🔴 CMS intermediate sanctions (27-Feb-2026) not demonstrably closed.** Repeated deadline extensions ≠ resolution. **Requires confirmation of current sanction status from the Q3-2026 10-Q and CMS enforcement-action page before the investment committee relies on 2027 MA growth guidance.** Any recurrence of enrollment suspension is a direct hit to the highest-multiple segment.
2. **🔴 Risk-adjustment/FCA tail.** Given DOJ's 2026 enforcement intensity and the CMS finding of persistent data noncompliance, a qui tam or CID is a credible scenario. Diligence question: has the company received any DOJ Civil Investigative Demand or subpoena related to RADV? (Not answerable from available data — **ask management directly**.)
3. **🟠 CFO sale of $4.66M at 52-week highs (22-Oct-2026) while a $935M regulatory remediation is ongoing.** Post-earnings and window-compliant, so not an accounting red flag — but the asymmetry against the CEO's buying warrants a question on 10b5-1 plan adoption and whether the plan was amended in 2026.
4. **🟠 Classified board + former CFO director** — limits shareholder recourse if remediation slips, and weakens the independence of the audit/governance perimeter during the exact period it matters most.
5. **🟠 Evidence gaps to close:** 10-K Item 1A risk factors, DEF 14A compensation metrics and board independence determinations, and the last two 10-Qs were **not retrievable** in this run. Absence of findings on litigation, cyber and related-party transactions is an artifact of tool failure, **not** evidence of absence.

**Bottom line:** ELV is not a fraud or governance story — it is a **regulatory-execution story**. The investment case now hinges less on medical cost trend than on whether CMS closes out the risk-adjustment sanctions cleanly; until that is confirmed in writing, non-financial risk should cap position sizing and the 2027 margin-recovery thesis should be haircut for sanction tail risk.
