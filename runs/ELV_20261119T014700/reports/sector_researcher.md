## SECTOR BRIEF — ELV (Elevance Health) | Medical – Healthcare Plans (US Managed Care)
*Pricing/data as of 18-Nov-2026 close ($400.84)*

---

### 1. Sector Overview

US managed care is a ~$1.6T administered-price industry in which the federal government and state Medicaid agencies — not the market — set the clearing price for roughly 60% of revenue. The six national risk-bearing payers in this peer set generated **$1.17T of 2025 revenue**, with the top three (UNH $448B, CI $275B, ELV $199B) taking **78.8%** of that aggregate — structurally concentrated, consistent with the AMA's repeated finding that insurance markets are "stubbornly concentrated, limiting competition." Critically, the sector is **mid-cycle in a policy-driven margin trough, not a demand trough**: revenue is non-discretionary and growing double digits, but medical loss ratios have repriced upward (utilisation normalisation, specialty/GLP-1 drug costs, provider consolidation) while CMS payment updates lag trend. Every one of the six peers suffered operating-margin erosion between 2023 and 2025 (range −103bp to −581bp), and management teams are now explicitly guiding 2026 as the trough with recovery in 2027.

**Structural backdrop / Porter's Five Forces**

| Force | Intensity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Rivalry | **High** | 5 national players + regional Blues + provider-sponsored plans; competition via MA bids, Star ratings, network breadth |
| Buyer power | **Very High** | CMS sets MA/Part D rates administratively; states set Medicaid capitation; large employers self-insure with sophisticated consultants |
| Supplier power | **High & rising** | Hospital/physician consolidation, drug pricing; unit cost growth running ahead of premium yield |
| Entry threat | **Low–Moderate** | Licensing, capital/reserve requirements, Star-rating scale economics; but well-funded MA entrants (ALHC, Devoted) and provider-sponsored plans |
| Substitutes | **Moderate** | ASO/self-funding, ICHRA, reference-based pricing, direct contracting, public-option rhetoric |

Moat is scale + vertical integration (UNH/Optum, ELV/Carelon), not pricing power. Capital intensity is low; regulatory and reputational intensity is very high.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Medicaid rate catch-up is a visible, quantifiable 2027 earnings driver.** ELV guides FY2026 Medicaid operating margin to ~**−1.75%** and calls 2026 the definitive trough, with states implementing rate increases effective for 2026–27. ELV has raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to **≥$29.00** and committed to **≥12% adjusted EPS growth in 2027** off a ≥$26.75 baseline (≈$30+).
- **MA re-pricing plus competitor retreat improves industry supply/demand.** The CY2027 final rate announcement delivered a **+2.48%** net payment increase (~$13B; ~$26B gross) after a near-flat (+0.09%) advance notice. Simultaneously UNH and HUM are **exiting MA plans covering ~1M members in 2027** — capacity withdrawal that lifts pricing discipline and Star-rating economics for scaled survivors like ELV.
- **Secular demand + non-risk revenue mix shift.** MA penetration is roughly half of Medicare eligibles and rising; ageing demographics are non-discretionary volume. ELV's Q3-2026 revenue growth was driven by **CarelonRx product revenue and service fees** — capital-light, fee-based revenue that is not exposed to MLR volatility and carries a higher multiple.

---

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Administered pricing systematically lags trend, with binary policy risk.** The CY2027 advance notice proposed only **+0.09%** net; risk-adjustment/overpayment-rule tightening is live; political pressure on Medicare spending and vertical integration is escalating. This is not theoretical: on **18-Nov-2026 the entire group sold off sharply** — ELV **−7.6%**, UNH **−10.2%**, HUM **−13.0%**, CNC **−7.0%** — on Medicare-rate/overpayment policy reports and Humana's 2027 MA exits. (The precise trigger was not definitively confirmed; the sector's policy beta is unambiguously the dominant driver.)
- **Medical cost trend above premium yield.** FY2025 operating margins compressed in every peer: ELV 5.10%→4.07%, UNH 8.71%→4.24%, HUM 3.58%→1.12%, CNC 1.90%→**−3.91%**, MOH 4.62%→1.72%. Drivers: inpatient/outpatient utilisation normalisation, behavioural health, specialty pharmacy, and provider consolidation.
- **ACA individual-market and Medicaid unwind.** Expiry of enhanced subsidies repriced the individual market violently — **Centene posted a FY2025 operating loss of −$7.6B and a −$6.67B net loss (−3.43% net margin)** on revenue *growing* 19.4%. ELV is also shrinking: Q3-2026 revenue grew just **+0.7%** as MA, Medicaid and Employer Group risk membership declined — a hard deceleration from FY2025's +12.6%.

**Two benchmark facts that matter more than the headline P/E:**
1. **ELV has the best margin durability in the group** — highest net margin (2.84%), second-highest operating margin, and the *mildest* two-year margin erosion (−103bp vs −246bp median). Its FY2025 earnings were the least impaired of the six.
2. **That quality is already paid for.** ELV trades at **0.43x EV/Revenue vs a 0.28x peer median** — a ~54% premium. Its apparently cheap 16.0x GAAP P/E exists because the denominator held up, not because the stock is marked down. On adjusted EPS (~$29.00 FY2026E) ELV is ~**13.8x**, and ~**13.4x** on a 2027 baseline of ~$30 — undemanding, but no longer a statistical bargain.
3. **ELV is losing multi-year share:** 2023→2025 revenue CAGR of **7.8%** vs UNH 9.7%, HUM 10.4%, CNC 12.5%, MOH 15.4%, CI 18.7% — the slowest in the group. FY2025's +12.6% was subsidy/redetermination-inflated and has rolled over to +0.7% in Q3-2026.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (relative), not dominant (absolute)**

**For:** Best-in-class profitability (4.07% op / 2.84% net margin vs 1.72%/1.04% medians); superior margin durability through the down-cycle; diversified Blue-card franchise across 14 states with leading MA and Medicaid positions; Carelon/CarelonRx provides a growing fee-based earnings offset and a credible vertical-integration answer to Optum; balance sheet supports $5.0B remaining buyback authorisation and AM Best "bbb+" senior unsecured with stable outlook; FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance *raised twice*, with Q3 benefit expense ratio improving 70bp YoY to 90.6%.

**Against:** Sub-scale vs UNH (2.2x revenue) without a services arm of comparable earnings power; heaviest Medicaid concentration among the publics, so *most* levered to the state rate cycle it is currently troughing in; slowest 2-yr revenue growth = share donor; revenue growth decelerating to +0.7%; stock has lagged the peer median by ~20pp over 12 months despite superior margins, indicating the market discounts GovernMent-pricing exposure more than it rewards margin quality.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

**Why not Attractive:** No pricing power (administered rates), a structurally negative regulatory skew, MLR inflation that has out-run premium yield for three straight years, and demonstrated policy beta (a single day in November erased 7–13% of market cap across the group). Earnings recovery in 2027 is a *rate-cycle* recovery, not a competitive re-rating.

**Why not Unattractive:** Demand is non-discretionary and volume growth is demographic; cash generation is robust (ELV FY2026E operating cash flow ≥$6.5B); the cycle is measurably troughing (Medicaid margins at −1.75%, MA rates improving +2.48%, competitor capacity exiting MA); and valuations at 13–14x adjusted earnings for the highest-quality operator leave limited downside.

**Positioning implication:** Within a Neutral sector, prefer **margin-durability + self-help** names. ELV screens best on that axis (best net margin, mildest erosion, clearest 2027 EPS bridge, lowest adjusted multiple of the quality cohort) but warrants only a modest premium given the 0.43x EV/Revenue valuation, sub-sector-laggard growth, and outsized Medicaid policy exposure.
