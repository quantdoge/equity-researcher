### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional, risk-on in credit / risk-off in rates**

The US economy in late 2026 is a split screen. **Real activity is solid** — Q3 2026 real GDP of $24,611bn (chained 2017$) grew **3.4% annualised QoQ (+2.1% YoY)**, and unemployment has *fallen* to 4.1% from 4.4% a year ago. **Credit is wide open**: high-yield OAS at 3.43%, roughly 80bp off the late-August tights (2.63%) but still consistent with no recession pricing.

But the **inflation and rate picture has deteriorated sharply**. October headline CPI re-accelerated to **3.82% YoY** while core CPI is only **2.68%** — a 114bp gap that says the re-acceleration is **supply/energy-driven, not demand-driven**. The Fed stopped cutting at 3.63% in July 2026 and has since **hiked ~25bp back to 3.88%** (August–October). The long end has done the heavy lifting: the **10Y is at 5.13%** (from 4.79% on 1 Sep and 4.48% on 1 Jul), and the **30Y at 5.47%**. This is a classic **bear steepening** — term premium expansion on fiscal supply concerns, not a growth scare. The 2s10s is **+39bp and positively sloped**, so the curve is *not* signalling recession.

For a domestic, low-beta, rate-sensitive, policy-exposed health insurer, this is a **headwind-heavy mix**: multiples compress with the long end while reimbursement is set by a federal government under fiscal pressure.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL (negative skew)**

The *operating* macro backdrop is neutral-to-supportive: 3%+ real GDP, 4.1% unemployment and contained medical CPI all support commercial membership and fee revenue, while the CY2027 MA rate (+2.48%) and higher investment income are tangible tailwinds.

The **financial and policy backdrop is unfavourable**: a re-accelerating headline CPI has put the Fed back in hiking mode, the 10Y at 5.13% compresses a 13–15x multiple, the 2027 cost trend (9–11%) dwarfs the medical CPI print, and the November Medicare-spending proposal shows that **reimbursement — a fiscal variable — is the single largest swing factor for ELV's earnings**, bigger than any monetary variable. ELV's 0.71 beta and domestic revenue base make it defensive on *growth* but not on *policy*.

Note the market has already partly priced this: ELV is $400.84, up ~26% from $318.20 a year ago but ~11% below its $450.69 October high, and roughly 20% below its March-to-October trend line despite raised guidance.

### 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**Bear case (higher probability than consensus): a "policy + cost" double hit.** Headline CPI is supply-driven, so the Fed's 25bp of hikes does not fix it; energy stays elevated, the 10Y pushes toward 5.5%, and the Medicare spending proposal is codified into CY2028 MA benchmarks while commercial trend prints at the high end (10%+). ELV's 90.2% MLR target breaks upward, the ≥12% 2027 EPS growth target ($~30) gets cut, and the stock de-rates to ~11x — a further 20–25% downside from here. The trigger to watch is **Q4 2026 MLR guidance in January 2027**, plus whether Congress retroactively extends the ACA enhanced PTCs or lets the individual market shrink further.

**Bull case: the inflation shock proves transient.** If the headline-core gap is energy/food only, core at 2.68% lets the Fed reverse the 25bp of hikes by Q1 2027, the 10Y falls back to ~4.5%, and an early-2027 PTC extension restores exchange membership — ELV re-rates to 17x on $30 of 2027 EPS (~$510), +27%. The asymmetry favours the bull case *if* the 10Y breaks below 4.80%; above 5.30%, the bear case dominates.
