## ESG ANALYSIS — ELV (Elevance Health, Inc.)

**Sector:** Healthcare / Managed Care (Medical – Healthcare Plans) | **Market cap:** ~$86.6B | **Revenue:** $197.6B (FY2025) | **Employees:** 96,615 | **Members:** 45.2M medical; ~104M consumers served
**Data basis:** CMS regulatory documents, SEC 8-K/10-Q filings, 2026 proxy data, state insurance department orders, trade press, company 2025 Impact Report. *Gap noted:* the 10-K risk-factor extract and DEF 14A were unavailable through the filing tool; governance data was reconstructed from proxy summaries and the annual-meeting 8-K.

### 1. ESG Materiality Map — What Actually Moves Cash Flow Here

For a managed-care insurer, ESG is predominantly a **G (compliance/conduct)** and **S (product integrity/access)** story. Environmental factors are financially immaterial to the P&L; governance failures and social/product controversies are the ones that hit revenue and margins directly.

| Factor | Materiality | Transmission mechanism to cash flow |
|---|---|---|
| **G – Regulatory compliance & billing integrity** | **Critical** | Risk-adjustment accuracy drives MA revenue; CMS sanctions can freeze enrollment (direct revenue loss); DOJ False Claims Act tail risk |
| **S – Product safety / member access & affordability** | **High** | Star ratings, prior-authorization and parity disputes drive quality bonus payments and state fines |
| **S – Labour & workforce (96.6k employees)** | Medium | Medical-cost trend and service capacity; modest wage exposure |
| **G – Board/compensation/shareholder rights** | Medium | Cost of capital, proxy risk, reputational |
| **E – Own operations (Scope 1/2)** | **Low** | ~12.5k tCO2e total; immaterial cost line |
| **E – Scope 3 / supply chain & climate-health** | Low–Medium | Supplier data demands, procurement costs; climate-driven morbidity feeds medical cost trend over long horizon |

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- **Carbon intensity:** Scope 1 emissions of **12,478 MT CO2e** (latest year), down from 14,390 and 18,854 in prior years — a credible ~34% decline. Against $197.6B revenue this is **~0.06 tCO2e per $1M revenue**, among the lowest intensities of any large-cap sector. Scope 1 is fuel and refrigerants in offices/data centres only; there is no manufacturing or heavy-asset base.
- **Targets:** −46.2% absolute Scope 1 by 2030 vs 2019; **net zero across Scopes 1, 2 and 3 by 2030**; 100% renewable electricity sourcing. *Analyst caveat:* a 2030 net-zero Scope 3 target for a company whose Scope 3 is dominated by purchased clinical services and its investment portfolio is not credibly achievable through abatement alone — it implies heavy REC/offset reliance. Treat as **aspirational, not a risk mitigant**, and a mild greenwashing/goal-integrity concern rather than a financial one.
- **Physical climate risk:** Minimal direct exposure (no plants, distributed office footprint). Indirect exposure runs through climate-sensitive morbidity, Disaster Supplemental funding, and provider disruption — a long-dated medical-cost variable, not a near-term valuation input.
- **Controversies:** None of consequence. No spills, no environmental enforcement actions found.
- **CBAM / EU CSRD:** Not applicable — essentially domestic US revenue base.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

This is where Elevance's ESG risk concentrates, and it is also where the market's attention has been.

**(a) Billing integrity / member and taxpayer harm — the dominant issue.** CMS issued an **intermediate sanctions notice on 27 Feb 2026** for "substantial and persistent noncompliance" with Medicare Advantage **risk-adjustment data** requirements spanning roughly **2015–2023**, alleging Elevance submitted diagnosis-code corrections on a flash drive format CMS had "explicitly rejected," resulting in overpayments. CMS threatened to **suspend enrollment and marketing from 31 March 2026** — a direct revenue-freeze mechanism. Elevance **wired $342.2M to CMS on 27 May 2026**, disclosed an estimated **~$935M total remediation cost (±$565M/−$585M)**, and the review was **closed 9 July 2026 with CMS confirming in writing that sanctions would not be imposed**. Resolution is a material de-risking event, but the underlying finding — seven years of data-submission noncompliance — is a genuine conduct failure, not a technicality.

**(b) Mental health parity.** Anthem (Elevance subsidiary) agreed to pay **$12.88M** in July 2025 to settle a class action alleging improper denial of behavioural-health claims; Georgia's Insurance Commissioner issued **~$25M in fines across 11 insurers in January 2026** for parity violations, with Anthem among them (~$1–3M individual estimate). Parity enforcement is a live, repeatable state-level revenue leak.

**(c) Access, prior authorization and affordability.** Elevance is exiting **181 counties** in MA, and the **expiry of enhanced ACA marketplace subsidies in late 2025** is shrinking the individual market — both reduce the "access" leg of its social licence while pressuring membership. Prior-authorization practices remain the industry's highest-volume reputational flashpoint; Elevance joined the June 2025 AHIP streamlining commitments, which is a mitigant of intent but not yet of outcome.

**(d) Positives that carry real commercial logic.** >**$1B** committed to affordable housing (**$640M over five years**), framed explicitly as reducing total cost of care via social drivers of health. This is unusually well-aligned CSR — housing stability lowers avoidable utilisation — and supports Medicaid/MA rate advocacy.

**(e) Labour.** 96,615 employees; CEO-to-median pay ratio **286:1** (median $79,007). No material strike, safety or turnover controversy surfaced. Supply-chain cyber exposure sits here: the **Conduent vendor breach (Oct 2024–Jan 2025) exposed ~25M people including Anthem members** — a third-party data-governance failure with litigation and notification cost, on top of the legacy 2015 Anthem breach.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **CMS / Medicare Advantage:** Highest-severity channel. Risk-adjustment audits (CMS's aggressive large-scale audit strategy), Star Ratings, and intermediate sanctions with enrollment-freeze power. Industry-wide DOJ False Claims Act focus on risk adjustment (Kaiser affiliates $556M; multiple 2026 settlements) means **whistleblower/qui-tam tail risk persists even after CMS closure**.
- **State insurance regulators:** 50-state market conduct and **mental-health-parity enforcement** (Georgia has now assessed $40M+ across 2025–26). Rate approval risk in Medicaid and individual markets.
- **Federal fiscal/legislative:** Expiry of enhanced ACA subsidies (late 2025) and Medicaid rate/adjustment policy are the largest single swing factors on 2027 margins per management commentary.
- **Privacy/cyber:** HIPAA/OCR breach exposure given the third-party vendor footprint.
- **ESG disclosure:** **EU CSRD not material** (US-centric revenue); **CBAM not applicable**; SEC climate rule exposure minimal given immaterial Scope 1/2. No ESG-linked debt covenants identified — AM Best rates the new shelf **bbb+ / stable**, so no ESG pricing penalty in the cost of capital.

---

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (with elevated governance-conduct risk)

Elevance is **not a Laggard**: separated chair/CEO, 10-of-11 independent board, ~96% say-on-pay, no dual-class structure, near-zero carbon intensity, meaningful and commercially rational community investment. It is **not a Leader** either: the 2025–26 record includes a seven-year CMS data-compliance failure that triggered a sanction threat, a parity class action, state parity fines, a star-ratings downgrade, and a repeat shareholder-proposal pattern on political spending. Environmental leadership claims (2030 net-zero including Scope 3) are not credible on abatement alone. Net: structurally sound governance, **operationally unreliable conduct controls**.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (slight negative skew on governance)

**For the thesis, ESG is not a valuation driver — it is a risk-premium argument.**

- **The known bad news is largely paid for.** The $342M is cash out the door and sanctions were avoided; the ~$935M total remediation estimate caps the identifiable exposure at roughly 0.5% of revenue and is already reflected in guidance. Resolution on 9 July 2026 removes the single largest overhang.
- **What remains is a repeatable-compliance discount, not a tail event.** Star-ratings deterioration, state parity enforcement, and Medicaid/ACA policy are the live margin variables — and management's own Q2 2026 print (a ~20% EPS beat that still drove an 8.5% share decline on Medicaid concerns) confirms the market is pricing policy and conduct risk, not ESG scores.
- **Positives for a long-only ESG-constrained holder:** no environmental liabilities, no stranded assets, no dual-class control, independent chair, pay that flexed to 86.5% of target, and an affordable-housing program genuinely tied to cost of care. This is a name that passes most ESG screens comfortably.
- **Where I would push back on management:** (1) the durability of conduct controls in risk adjustment — request the remediation auditor's findings and whether clawback provisions were applied to any 2021–2025 incentive awards; (2) the credibility of a 2030 Scope 3 net-zero target, which should be re-based to a science-aligned Scope 1/2 commitment; (3) political-spending disclosure, the one governance item where the board remains resistant despite repeated proposals.

**Bottom line:** ESG neither supports nor blocks the investment case. Underwrite ELV on Medicaid rate recovery and MA margin, and treat governance/conduct as a **modest, ongoing discount-rate add-on** rather than a thesis-breaker — with DOJ False Claims Act follow-on litigation as the one low-probability, high-severity risk worth monitoring quarterly.
