# Elevance Health (ELV) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price $400.84 · Market cap ~$87B (217.6M diluted shares) · Sector: Healthcare / Managed Care Plans**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — **Moat: Narrow (and narrowing)**

Elevance is the second-largest U.S. health insurer (~44.9M medical members), operating Blue Cross/Blue Shield plans in 14 states plus the Carelon health-services arm (Q3'26 revenue $19.3B, ~38% of total).

**Sources of advantage:** scale in administrative cost per member; durable BCBS brand and state incumbency (30+ year contracts, high re-bid win rates); Carelon's vertical integration into pharmacy/specialty (CarelonRx, CarelonRx specialty) which gives it a cost lever most regional plans lack.

**Why not wider:** this is a regulated utility-like business wearing a growth company's clothes. Premiums in Medicaid are *set by states* and in Medicare Advantage *set by CMS* — there is no true pricing power, only a lagging catch-up mechanism. A 4% operating margin is the arithmetic result.

**The moat is narrowing — this is the single most important finding:**

| Metric | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 2026E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Operating margin | 6.45% | 5.40% | 5.10% | 5.03% | 4.07% | ~3.7% |
| ROIC | — | 13.6% | 13.2% | 12.0% | 10.5% | ~10% |
| ROE | — | 16.2% | 15.2% | 14.4% | 12.9% | ~11% |

ROIC has fallen ~310bps in three years and now clears WACC (~8.5%) by only ~200bps. A moated business shows the *opposite* trend. Revenue compounded 9.5% (2021→2025) while net income went **nowhere** ($6.10B → $5.66B). In 2026 revenue growth has stalled to **+1.8% YTD** as membership fell 487K year-over-year. Management itself calls 2026 the Medicaid margin trough (Medicaid operating margin −1.75%), with recovery guided for 2027.

---

## 2. Financial Quality Score — **6 / 10**

**What's good:** revenue is highly predictable and contract-based; investment-grade balance sheet; Carelon provides ~$0.9B/quarter of steady operating gain; dividend raised every year ($1.13 → $1.72/qtr, ~11% CAGR) at only ~24% of adjusted EPS; diluted shares down 11.5% since 2020 (250.8M → 217.6M).

**What's not:**

- **FCF quality is poor and volatile.** 2025 FCF $3.17B on $5.66B net income = **56% conversion** (working capital drain of $3.28B). 2024: 76%. 2023: 113%. 2026 recovers (~$6.5–7.0B, helped by a +$1.06B WC swing in Q1), but the underlying conversion is ~85% at best — not the >100% you want.
- **Owner earnings are flat-to-down:** $6.42B (2022) → $6.44B (2023) → $6.12B (2024) → $6.09B (2025) → ~$5.5B (2026E).
- **Earnings quality flag:** FY2025 GAAP EPS $25.12 vs adjusted $30.29 — a **17% gap** ($5.17/share of add-backs). That is wide, and it is the number management and the Street quote.
- **Goodwill is 88% of equity** ($39.5B vs $44.8B). Tangible book value is only ~$5.6B against $30.8B of debt.
- **Capital returned exceeded cash generated:** 2025 dividends $1.53B + buybacks $2.61B = $4.13B vs FCF $3.17B.

Balance sheet is adequate, not fortress: total debt $30.8B, ~3.1x EBITDA, large offsetting investment portfolio (~$37.8B cash + investments at Q3'26). Note: the net-debt figure is sensitive to how investments are classified ($20.4B on a cash-only basis vs. net cash on a look-through basis); I use the conservative $20B.

For context, ELV traded at 15–18x for most of the past decade. The de-rating from ~$530 (mid-2024) to $400 reflects real Medicaid/ACA margin damage, not just sentiment.

**Owner-earnings DCF** (base owner earnings $5.7B, cost of equity 9.5%, reflecting policy/regulatory risk):

|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | 3% | 3% | ~$415 |

**Cross-check on earnings:** $32.5 × 13–15x = $423–488. Analysts' post-Q3'26 targets cluster $451–523 (median ~$495).

**Intrinsic value estimate: ~$445/share (range $415–490).** Implied expectations at $400.84: the market is pricing ~3–4% perpetual owner-earnings growth and permanent ~10% ROIC. That is *not* an unreasonable assumption — which is exactly the problem. There is no pessimism to exploit.

**Margin of safety at $400.84: ~10%.** My discipline requires 25–30%. **Buy point: ~$335 or below.**

## 4. Management Quality — **B / B−**

**Positives:** Gail Boudreaux (CEO since 2017) has shown genuine pricing discipline — walking away from underpriced Medicaid contracts and repricing state rates rather than buying revenue. Guidance is consistently set conservatively and beaten ("at least $29" raised repeatedly; "at least 12%" 2027 growth). Dividend growth is measured and covered.

**Negatives:**
- **Capital allocation has been mediocre.** ~$12.4B of buybacks over five years at an average cost well above today's $400 (2024 alone: $2.9B at ~$480–520). Share count fell 11.5% but EPS has grown 0.4% annualized over four years — the buybacks masked earnings stagnation rather than compounding it.
- **Serial M&A:** $39.5B of goodwill accumulated (Beacon, BioPlus, Paragon, MMM) while ROIC fell from 13.6% to 10.5%. Empire-building has not obviously earned its cost of capital.
- **Compensation framing** relies heavily on adjusted EPS, and the GAAP gap has widened.

Insider ownership data was not available in this dataset — a gap I would normally require before underwriting management alignment.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

This is a *decent* business at a *fair-to-slightly-cheap* price — which is precisely the combination Buffett says to avoid. Three conclusions drive the verdict:

1. **The moat is narrowing, not widening.** A business whose ROIC fell from 13.6% to 10.5% and whose operating margin fell from 6.5% to 3.7% in five years has not earned a premium, and its returns now barely clear the cost of capital.
2. **The 2027 "recovery" is a margin mean-reversion story, not compounding.** Guided +12% EPS growth off a trough is low-quality growth — it depends on state Medicaid rate increases arriving on schedule and CMS not tightening MA risk adjustment. Both are political variables, not business variables.
3. **No margin of safety.** At $400.84 against ~$445 intrinsic value, the upside is ~10% with meaningful downside if Medicaid rates lag another year (bear case $340–360).

**What would make this a Buy:** price below **~$335** (25% discount to base intrinsic value), *and* evidence of two consecutive quarters of Medicaid margin expansion with membership stabilizing. At $340 you would be paid ~10.5% owner-earnings yield plus 1.8% dividend for waiting out a regulated-cycle trough — that is a Buffett trade.

**What would make this a Sell:** Medicaid rates still misaligned into 2027, OR the adjusted-to-GAAP gap widening beyond ~20%, OR debt/EBITDA above 3.5x with buybacks continuing to exceed free cash flow.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Medicaid policy risk (largest).** FY2026 Medicaid operating margin −1.75%. Rate-setting under the 2025 reconciliation law is uncertain; state budget stress and 1.2–2M projected enrollment reductions create both volume loss and adverse selection.
2. **ACA marketplace.** Median 2027 premium filings up ~14–15%; if subsidies lapse, healthy members exit and the risk pool deteriorates — ELV has meaningful marketplace exposure.
3. **Medical cost trend / MLR.** FY2026 consolidated MLR ~90%. A 100bp miss on MLR costs roughly $2B of revenue-equivalent margin — catastrophic against a $7.5B operating base. This is a business with almost no shock absorbers.
4. **Medicare Advantage.** Running ~2% margin against a 3–5% long-term target; V28 risk-model changes and rate notices cap upside.
5. **Balance-sheet thinness.** Goodwill 88% of equity; tangible equity ~$5.6B; ~3.1x leverage. An impairment or two bad years would pressure both the dividend-growth and buyback stories.
6. **Membership attrition** (−487K YoY) has already cut revenue growth to ~2%; continued losses remove the operating leverage the DCF assumes.
7. **Regulatory/litigation/cyber** — PBM reform, 340B, antitrust scrutiny of vertical integration (Carelon), and state contract concentration.
8. **Data caveats:** peer managed-care multiples were unavailable (the sector-peer feed returned pharma comparables), insider ownership data was not returned, and the FCF-based DCF tool produced invalid output. Valuation here rests on manual owner-earnings work and the reported quarterly trend.

**Bottom line:** A narrow-moat, capital-intensive, policy-exposed insurer at 12–14x adjusted earnings with a 7–8% normalized FCF yield. The price is reasonable, not compelling. **Hold; buy below ~$335.**
