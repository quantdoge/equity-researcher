## Elevance Health (ELV) — Geopolitical & Legal/Regulatory Risk Assessment
*Market context: $400.84/share, ~$86.6B market cap, 0.71 beta, ~96.6K employees (company profile). Note: the 10-K was not retrievable via the filing tool (returned only an Exhibit 31.1 certification from the Q3-26 10-Q), and state-level revenue splits were not disclosed in the sources I could access — flagged as gaps below.*

---

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

Elevance is effectively a **single-country business**: FY2025 operating revenue was **$197.6B**, split between **Health Benefits ($167.1B)** and **Carelon ($71.7B, incl. intersegment)**, with no disclosed non-US revenue segment (Elevance Q4/FY2025 release, 28 Jan 2026). ~45.2M medical members, of which ~2M Medicare Advantage (KFF Health News, 26 Jun 2026).

| Geography | Revenue exposure | Risk level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States (core)** | ~100% | **Medium-High** | Regulatory/political, not geopolitical. Blue-branded plans across ~14 states + FEP, Medicaid, MA, ACA. State-level concentration not disclosed in retrieved sources. |
| **Puerto Rico** | Small (within US) | **Medium** | MMM Healthcare LLC (MA plan) is a named plaintiff in star-ratings litigation — territory fiscal fragility, hurricane exposure. |
| **India, Philippines, Ireland, Puerto Rico (Carelon Global Solutions)** | De minimis revenue; cost/ops only | **Low-Medium** | Captive capability/BPO centers (Carelon Global Solutions careers pages, LinkedIn). Operational & data-privacy/vendor risk, not revenue risk. |
| **China / Russia / sanctioned jurisdictions** | **None material** | **Low** | No disclosed operations; no sanctions, export-control, or currency-repatriation exposure. |

**Key point:** ELV has essentially **zero direct geopolitical exposure** — no meaningful US-China, Russia, or EM footprint, no tariffed manufacturing, no currency controls. Its "geopolitical" risk is *domestic policy risk* (federal/state), which is where all the real variance sits.

---

### 2. Top 3 Policy/Geopolitical Risks

**① Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment enforcement escalation — Probability: High (already occurring) | Impact: High**
This is the dominant legal risk and it is no longer tail-risk; it is realized:
- CMS imposed **intermediate sanctions on 27 Feb 2026** for "substantial and persistent noncompliance" with risk-adjustment data submission rules, threatening to **suspend new MA/MA-PD enrollment** (effective 31 Mar 2026), later extended to 30 May and repeatedly pushed back (CMS sanction notice 27 Feb 2026; Becker's; Healthcare Dive; Fierce Healthcare).
- Elevance **wired $342.2M to CMS on 27 May 2026** as "remittance of the total overpayment amount" — apparently the first time CMS has successfully compelled an MA plan to repay alleged overpayments (KFF Health News, 26 Jun 2026).
- Company disclosed in an **April 2026 SEC filing** that its "current best estimate" of potential exposure is **~$935M** — i.e., roughly ~1% of market cap still unprovisioned above the cash already paid (KFF, citing the filing).
- Underlying **DOJ False Claims Act case (United States v. Anthem Inc., S.D.N.Y. 1:20-cv-02593, filed 26 Mar 2020)** remains live, with heavy discovery/deposition fighting through Nov 2026 (Georgetown Health Care Litigation Tracker). DOJ's 1 May 2025 complaint named three national insurers plus brokers. Precedent is worsening: Kaiser paid **$556M** (Jan 2026); Aetna/Kaiser ~$674M in one quarter (RiseHealth). Elevance's defense — that CMS knew of the coding practices for years — is plausible but has not been tested.

**② CarelonRx / PBM regulation — Probability: High | Impact: Medium-High**
Carelon is now ~36% of revenue and the growth engine (Carelon revenue +33% in FY2025), and its pharmacy arm is squarely in the crosshairs:
- **Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026** (signed 3 Feb 2026) established **national PBM standards** on transparency, rebates/compensation, and pharmacy participation (CarelonRx Q1-26 legislative report; Epstein Becker Green).
- **~1,600 state PBM bills** tracked in the 2026 session — a record — with 2027 shaping up to be more aggressive; **California SB 41** creates a comprehensive PBM framework reshaping supply-chain incentives (AMCP; RxBenefits).
- Margin-model risk: spread-pricing bans and rebate pass-through directly compress CarelonRx economics, at a time when Health Benefits margins are the problem.

**③ ACA subsidy expiry + Medicaid rate adequacy — Probability: High | Impact: Medium**
- **Enhanced premium tax credits expired 31 Dec 2025**, driving a 2026 enrollment decline with **healthier enrollees disproportionately dropping coverage** — i.e., adverse risk-pool selection (KFF; Health System Tracker; CRS R48290). ELV's 2025 benefit expense ratio rose 150bps to 90.0%, driven by elevated ACA medical trend (FY2025 release). 2027 marketplace premiums are rising sharply.
- **Medicaid**: FY2026 segment margin guided to ~**-1.75%**, with management calling 2026 the trough and targeting 2–4% long-term, contingent on state rate increases; the company has signaled **Medicaid market exits** (Healthcare Finance News; Becker's). State budget cycles are the swing factor, and CMS's 2026–2027 Rate Development Guide governs state capitation setting (medicaid.gov).
- **MA rates**: CY2027 final rate announcement landed at **+2.48% / >$13B**, better than the near-flat advance notice, but still below trend — and ELV is **exiting 66 counties and entering 10** for 2027 (KFF; CMS rate announcement). Reported prior-year county exits ran into the ~181 range (ABA Health Lawyer, 2026).

**Litigation asymmetry worth flagging to the PM:** ELV is simultaneously *defendant* on risk-adjustment (potentially $935M+) and *plaintiff* on star ratings (claiming $516M owed *to* it). Net legal cash flow could swing ~$1.4B depending on how both tracks resolve — material versus ~$86.6B market cap but not existential.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Risk-adjustment data integrity (RED, highest severity).** A CMS finding of "substantial and persistent noncompliance" plus an active DOJ FCA suit is the single clearest compliance signal in the file. Precedent settlements (Kaiser $556M) suggest elevated settlement probability.
2. **PBM transparency/spread-pricing (AMBER).** New federal PBM standards plus a record state legislative wave create disclosure and conduct liability — including potential state AG actions against CarelonRx.
3. **Third-party/data-privacy (AMBER).** Offshore Carelon Global Solutions operations (India, Philippines) and vendor ecosystems create HIPAA/vendor-contract and OCR exposure.
4. **FCPA / AML / sanctions (GREEN).** No material foreign operations, no sanctioned-jurisdiction exposure, no export-controlled products. Effectively not applicable at scale.
5. **Watch item — political contribution/lobbying optics (GREEN/AMBER).** Heavy MA advocacy spending ("dark money" criticism of MA payment lobbying, KFF, Mar 2026) is a headline/reputational rather than legal risk.

### 5. Overall Rating

| Dimension | Rating |
|---|---|
| **Geopolitical risk (cross-border, sanctions, EM, trade)** | **Low** |
| **Legal & regulatory risk (US)** | **High** |
| **Blended assessment** | **Medium-High** |

**Rationale:** ELV is one of the cleanest large-cap names on *classic* geopolitical risk — no China, Russia, EM, tariff, currency-control, or expropriation exposure; revenue is ~100% US. But the US regulatory and enforcement picture is unusually hostile and *company-specific*: an unprecedented CMS intermediate sanction tied to MA risk-adjustment data, a six-year-old DOJ False Claims Act case with a **~$935M** disclosed exposure estimate and **$342M** already repaid, a second front of star-ratings litigation worth **$516M** in claimed rebates, a structurally re-priced ACA individual market post-subsidy-expiry, a negative-margin Medicaid book, and a fast-tightening PBM rulebook aimed directly at CarelonRx — the segment carrying the growth story. A purely domestic payer should not be "Low" on regulatory risk in this policy cycle.
