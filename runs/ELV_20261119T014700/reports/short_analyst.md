# FORENSIC SHORT-SELLER ANALYSIS — ELEVANCE HEALTH (ELV)

**Price $400.84 | Mkt cap $86.6B | 18-Nov-2026 | Healthcare Plans**

---

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **LOW** (deteriorating, not fraudulent)

The reported P&L and the cash flow statement have decoupled. Net income has been flat-to-down for five years while operating cash flow has been cut almost in half. That is the central fact of this analysis.

| FY | Revenue ($B) | Rev growth | Op income ($B) | **Op margin** | Net income ($B) | **OCF ($B)** | **FCF ($B)** | **FCF/NI** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 138.6 | — | 8.95 | **6.45%** | 6.10 | 8.36 | 7.28 | **1.19x** |
| 2022 | 156.6 | +12.9% | 8.45 | **5.40%** | 6.02 | 8.40 | 7.25 | **1.20x** |
| 2023 | 171.3 | +9.4% | 8.75 | **5.10%** | 5.99 | 8.06 | 6.77 | **1.13x** |
| 2024 | 176.8 | +3.2% | 8.89 | **5.03%** | 5.97 | 5.81 | 4.55 | **0.76x** |
| 2025 | 199.1 | +12.6% | 8.11 | **4.07%** | 5.66 | **4.29** | **3.17** | **0.56x** |
| 2026E | 200.1 | +0.5% | — | ~3.7% (TTM) | ~4.8 | — | — | — |

Five consecutive years of operating margin compression (238bp cumulative). **FY2025 is the tell**: revenue +12.6%, operating income **-8.7%**, OCF **-26%**. They bought $22B of revenue at a negative incremental margin and paid for it in cash.

Working-capital drag: +$0.78B (2021) → +$0.25B (2022) → **-$0.54B → -$1.72B → -$3.28B**. A ~$6.6B cumulative swing in three years. For a health insurer this is the signature of falling days-in-claims-payable and/or accelerating claims outflows — i.e., cash leaving faster than premiums arrive.

**Cash conversion (OCF/NI): 1.37 → 1.40 → 1.35 → 0.97 → 0.76.** 2025 net income exceeded operating cash flow by **$1.37B**. Composite earnings quality score: **45/100**. FCF/NI at 0.56x is the lowest in the five-year window by a wide margin. Using the 2021-23 average FCF conversion of ~1.18x, 2024-25 FCF is roughly **$6B below** what reported earnings imply.

`Piotroski F-Score 6/9` — and critically, all three *quality* components fail: no ROA improvement (F3=0), poor accruals (F4=0), gross margin deterioration (F8=0). The score is propped up by leverage, equity and no-dilution factors.

## 2. Key Red Flags

**R1 — Cash flow divergence (severity: high).** OCF -49% over two years vs net income -5%. Confirmed above.

**R2 — The adjusted/GAAP gap is now an economic cost, not an accounting artifact (severity: high).** FY2026 guidance: **GAAP EPS ≥$22.00 vs adjusted ≥$29.00** — a **$7.00/share (24%) gap**, ~$1.5B. A material component is the **$935M CMS accrual**. Through 9M26 GAAP EPS is **$21.01** against a FY guide of ≥$22.00 — implying ~$1.00 of Q4 GAAP EPS against ~$8.00 of Q4 adjusted EPS. Virtually the entire year's add-backs are dumped into Q4. Excluding a probable regulatory penalty/refund from "adjusted" earnings is aggressive: it is a cash cost of doing business, and investors are being asked to capitalize a $29 number that the company itself says is $22.

**R3 — Revenue growth has collapsed to stall speed (severity: high).** Quarterly YoY: 14.8% → 14.0% → 12.3% → 9.5% → 2.6% → 1.4% → 1.4%. The 2025 growth engine (ACA/Medicaid enrollment + rate) is gone. 2026E revenue +0.5%. A company at 13.8x adjusted / 18.2x GAAP with zero revenue growth and negative GAAP EPS growth has no multiple support.

**R4 — MLR above the guardrail (severity: high).** Benefit expense ratio guidance 90.2% ±50bp; reported MLR 91.3% (up from 89.5%), Q4'25 MLR 93.5%. Medicaid segment operating margin guided to roughly **-1.75%** for 2026, with management calling it a "trough year." In a business with ~4% operating margins, a 180bp MLR move is nearly half of total profit.

**R5 — Goodwill is 90% of book equity (severity: medium).** Goodwill + intangibles **$39.5B** vs equity **$44.0B**. Tangible book is **$4.5B** — price/tangible book ~**19x**. Carelon/Wellpoint carrying values have essentially no cushion; note goodwill already ticked *down* $0.8B in 2025, which deserves scrutiny.

**R6 — Leverage rising into a cash-flow decline (severity: medium).** Total debt $25.8B → $33.2B (2022→2025); net debt $23.7B = **7.5x 2025 FCF** and 5.5x OCF. Interest expense +76% since 2021 ($0.80B → $1.40B) and now consumes **17% of operating income** (was 9%).

**R7 — Distributions exceed free cash flow (severity: medium).** 2025: dividends $1.53B + buybacks $2.61B = **$4.13B vs FCF $3.17B (130%)** and 96% of OCF. Share count fell 246.8M → 217.6M since 2021 (~12%), mechanically adding ~3pts/yr to EPS. Strip out buybacks and 2025 EPS decline is materially worse than the reported -2.2%. Capital allocation discipline is also questionable: the heaviest buyback year (2024, $2.9B) was executed at ~$500/share, near the all-time high.

**R8 — Guidance credibility (severity: high).** FY2025 adjusted EPS was originally guided to **$34.15–$34.85**, cut ~13% to ~$30 mid-year; actual GAAP came in at **$25.12** and even the consensus in our dataset ($29.99) was missed. FY2026 OCF is guided to **≥$5.5B** — against a 2025 actual of **$4.29B**, a required +28% step-up that we cannot verify (no quarterly cash-flow disclosure available). This is the number I would watch most closely: it is the one guide management has already failed to hit.

**R9 — Director selling into strength (severity: low).** Director Robert L. Dixon Jr. sold 60,667 shares at **$401.77** (11-Jun-26) and further stock at $403.16, near the recovery highs; the CFO and Chief Digital Officer have also sold post-earnings. Scale is small (~$8.8M over 24 months per third-party aggregation) and appears largely 10b5-1/vesting-related. **Not a governance red flag at this size — I will not overstate it.**

**R10 — Buybacks at $300+ while the CMS liability is unresolved (severity: low-medium).** 3.7M shares repurchased in Q1'26 for $1.1B at "just over $300," before the size of the CMS matter was settled.

**Honest read:** this is *not* a Beneish-style manipulation short. Total accruals are genuinely small relative to a $121B asset base, receivables are clean, and there are no auditor changes, restatements or related-party flags in the data. The M-Score sits at the edge of the warning band almost entirely because of margin decay (GMI) and sales growth (SGI). **The red flag here is operating deterioration and cash leakage, not accounting fraud.** A short-seller who pitches ELV as a fraud will lose money; one who pitches it as a melting margin story with a shrinking cash cushion has a case.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

- **Pricing power is gone at the margin.** Gross margin 27.9% → 25.6% (2025); operating margin in structural decline for five years. In managed care, margin is the residual between negotiated premium and medical trend — and medical trend is currently winning.
- **Government program concentration.** Medicaid at negative segment margin and ACA marketplace exposure leave ELV structurally dependent on state and federal rate-setting. The CMS accrual is direct evidence that regulatory counterparties can reach into earnings retroactively.
- **Vertical integration (Carelon) is the bull's moat argument** — internal pharmacy benefit and care delivery to capture margin. It is real, but note that as total margin compressed, Carelon's growth is partly intercompany revenue that does not fix the core MLR problem.
- **No inventory, no channel-stuffing vector, no obsolescence risk** — this is a structurally slower-moving moat than a hardware or software business. That cuts both ways: erosion is gradual, so a short needs a catalyst.

## 5. Management Red Flags

1. **Serial optimism, serial cuts.** FY2025 guided $34.15–$34.85, delivered ~$25 GAAP. That is the single most damaging governance data point in this file.
2. **Adjustment creep.** The non-GAAP gap has widened to $7/share and now embeds a regulatory penalty rather than just acquisition amortization.
3. **Q4 back-loading.** ~$7 of the year's ~$8 of Q4 adjustments land in one quarter — consistent with annual true-ups and a soft "big bath" dynamic that resets the 2027 base for the "at least 12% growth" promise.
4. **Countervailing:** FY2026 guidance has been *raised* three times ($26.75 → $29.00 adjusted; $19.85 → $22.00 GAAP), and both the CEO and CFO certified the Q3 10-Q with no disclosed material weakness or significant deficiency. Insider selling is routine-scale.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary

**Credible bear case: YES.**

The market is being asked to pay **13.8x "adjusted"** for a company whose **GAAP multiple is 18.2x**, whose GAAP EPS is guided **down ~12%** in 2026 after a ~2% decline in 2025, whose revenue has gone from +12.6% to +0.5%, whose operating margin has fallen every year since 2021, whose MLR is running above its own guardrail with Medicaid at a negative margin, and whose **free cash flow has halved in two years to $3.2B while it distributed $4.1B**. Net debt is 7.5x FCF. Goodwill is 90% of book. The $935M CMS accrual is unproven and third-party analysis flags the risk it is understated; any increase is a direct hit to a $1.5B adjustment pool that investors are capitalizing as earnings.

The mechanism that makes this shortable is **estimate risk, not fraud risk**: the same pattern that produced a 16% consensus miss in FY2025 is set up again, and the FY2026 OCF guide of ≥$5.5B requires a 28% step-up from a number that has fallen two years running.

**Where the bull case is stronger than the bear case, and I concede it:**
- The stock is already **-26% off its September-2024 peak of $544** and was down **-50%** at the July-2025 trough ($269). A large part of the de-rating has happened.
- Management is *raising* guidance, not cutting it, and is explicitly framing 2026 as the trough with ≥12% adjusted EPS growth guided for 2027.
- 2.0x book, 0.71 beta, ~1.65% dividend yield, investment-grade balance sheet with $9.5B cash and 5.8x interest coverage — this is exactly the kind of defensive name that gets bid in a risk-off tape.
- The accounting is **clean** on every test I can run: Beneish "low risk," no receivables red flag, no auditor or related-party issues.

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **SHORT — moderate conviction** (not a Strong Short)

**Not "Strong Short"** because there is no fraud, the accruals are small in absolute terms, the de-rating is well advanced, and the tape has repeatedly squeezed this name +49% off the low. **Not "No Edge"** because the cash-flow divergence is quantified, multi-year, and unresolved, and because the earnings being marketed to investors ($29 adjusted) are 32% above what the company actually earns ($22 GAAP) with the difference now containing a live regulatory liability.

**Suggested expression:** put spreads or a paired long (e.g., a managed-care peer with cleaner cash conversion) rather than a naked short — the binary CMS resolution and the defensive bid make a naked short poorly risked.

**What would break the thesis (monitor, in order):** (1) FY2026 OCF landing at or above $5.5B — this would invalidate R1/R8; (2) the CMS matter resolving at or below the $935M accrual; (3) two consecutive quarters of MLR at or below ~89.5% with Medicaid margin turning positive; (4) FY2027 adjusted EPS growth actually tracking ≥12%.

**Data gaps I could not close:** no quarterly cash-flow statement (so 9M26 OCF is unverified against the ≥$5.5B guide); no 10-K prior-period reserve development table (the reserve-release hypothesis is *untested*, and I have not asserted it); peer multiples unavailable in this dataset; the cause of the 7.6% drop on 18-Nov-2026 is unresolved — evidence points to a sector-wide managed-care selloff rather than ELV-specific news, given the 11-Nov guidance reaffirmation.
