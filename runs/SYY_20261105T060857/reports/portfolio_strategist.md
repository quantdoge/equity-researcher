**Sizing arithmetic under a 1% single-name risk budget:** a 1% position with a 29% vol, binary-event name contributes ~0.29% of portfolio volatility fully correlated into a single FTC decision, and carries a realistic 35% chance of a −10% to −15% outcome (−15 to −20bp of portfolio) plus tail spread-widening on a downgrade. **What a holder should do:** if SYY sits in a defensive income sleeve, trim to **≤1.0%** (from a typical 1.5–2.5% staples weight) and re-underwrite at the FTC decision. Do not add on the −9.3% dip — that is a capital-structure event, not a valuation dislocation (Alt Data makes the same call).

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector / concentration.** The Consumer Defensive label is misleading. SYY is a 3.66%-operating-margin distributor whose equity is now **~79% of the size of its own acquisition** — the position functions as a **leveraged, binary bet on an FTC ruling**, not as staples ballast. Sizing it at a staples weight is a category error and the single largest construction risk here.

**Factor exposure.** Adding SYY adds:
- **Positive:** cheapest name in its complex — P/E 21.0x vs distribution peers **USFD 37.4x, PFGC 47.9x**; EV/EBITDA 12.2x; FCF yield 5.24%; div yield 2.83%.
- **Negative:** the **worst momentum in the peer set** (−9.23% 12-1, 6/7) and the **highest realised volatility** (7/7, 28.96% vs ~22% peer median). Cheap + negative momentum + deteriorating ROIC is the textbook **value-trap** factor triplet. It also adds zero growth exposure and negative quality *trend* (level-good, direction-bad).
- **Net:** increases the portfolio's unintended cheap-cyclical/negative-momentum tilt and its single-name event-vol bucket simultaneously.

**Correlation.** SYY is genuinely diversifying on paper — 0.24–0.28 to PG/KO/WMT/COST and 0.075 to SPY (the last is part of the benchmark defect the Quant Analyst flagged; **do not treat SYY as a low-beta hedge on that number** — use the 5-yr monthly beta of 0.64). Critically, **SYY correlates 0.45 with USFD and 0.42 with PFGC** — so **do not initiate SYY alongside USFD/PFGC**, and note that the analyst-preferred trade in this complex is the *inverse*: **long USFD / short SYY** (Short Analyst), which the market has validated (+42.0% vs −1.1% over 14 months).

**Practical verdict:** a portfolio already long staples gains little. A portfolio long USFD/PFGC would be netting its own best idea. Either way, **the fit argues for zero.**
