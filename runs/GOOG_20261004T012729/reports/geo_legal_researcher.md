## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1. US antitrust remedies & appeals — Probability: High (already in-litigation) | Impact: High**
Judge Mehta's Sept 2025 *remedies* ruling let Google keep Chrome/Android but banned exclusive default-search deals (the Apple revenue-sharing engine) and imposed data-sharing/conduct oversight. DOJ + 35 states appealed that ruling (DC Circuit, appeal filed 2026), seeking harsher structural remedies. Separately, Judge Brinkema's E.D. Va. ruling found Google monopolized ad exchanges/publisher ad servers, with a **September 2026 remedies trial** at which DOJ is seeking ad-tech divestiture. A forced divestiture of the ad stack (~$30bn+ revenue) or an adverse DC Circuit result would be the single largest value risk.

**2. EU/UK regulatory fragmentation — Probability: High | Impact: Medium-High**
The EC handed Google its **first DMA penalty — €890m (23 July 2026)** — for self-preferencing in Search (Flights/Hotels) and Play Store restrictions, with a 60-day remediation order; Google is challenging the DMA designations and has cited ~€114bn in claimed European business losses. Antitrust fines are a recurring cost of doing business in Europe (historic Shopping/Android/AdSense fines ≈ €8.2bn), and the UK CMA's SMS designation imposes parallel conduct requirements. Revenue impact is manageable, but each remedy reshapes how Search/Android monetize in a ~25%-of-revenue region.

**3. US–China / Taiwan supply-chain & market risk — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium**
Google services (Search, YouTube, Play) remain barred in mainland China; growth is foreclosed. Rising risk is upstream: US AI-chip export controls and Beijing's 2026 counter-sanctions regime (with extraterritorial reach) complicate Google Cloud/TPU business, and TPU fabrication via TSMC concentrates hardware into Taiwan-strait risk. US tariffs on 22 countries plus the fragile Trump–Xi truce add volatility to hardware costs (Pixel manufacturing) and cross-border data flows.

*Other watch items (lower impact):* Russia judgment-enforcement litigation (contained via UK injunctions, Jan 2025); Turkey/Indonesia/South Korea platform-fee disputes.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **US v. Google (search, D.D.C.):** Liability found Aug 2024; Sept 2025 remedies — no Chrome/Android divestiture, but exclusivity bans, data licensing and a conduct-compliance regime. DOJ + 35 states **appealing** (2026).
- **Ad-tech antitrust (E.D. Va.):** Partial liability against Google; **remedies trial September 2026**; divestiture proposed; Google appealing the adverse portion ("we won half this case").
- **EU DMA:** €890m fine (first-ever DMA penalty), 23 July 2026 — real-time risk that non-compliance re-runs (fines up to 10% of global turnover); reported €2.95bn ad-tech fine (Sept 2025) also pending/appealed.
- **UK CMA:** Google designated **SMS in general search & search advertising**; separate ad-tech market investigation active.
- **India CCI:** ₹936.44cr Play-Store billing fine (NCLAT partly upheld Mar 2025; Google at Supreme Court) + ₹1,337.76cr Android fine — India is a high-growth, low-monetisation market, so fines sting disproportionately.
- **Russia:** YouTube throttled since Jul 2024; Russian courts issued astronomical penalty judgments — Google obtained UK injunctions (Jan 2025) to block enforcement; ECHR case (*Google LLC v. Russia*) remains pending.
- **US securities litigation (new):** Multiple class actions (Robbins, Frank R. Cruz, Glancy Prongay, Faruqi & Faruqi) allege Alphabet misled investors on the **timely launch of Gemini 3.5 Pro** (putative class period May 19–Jul 16, 2026; lead-plaintiff deadline **30 Nov 2026**).
- **AI governance:** Trump administration moved to *voluntary* AI safeguards (Oct 2026); EU AI Act systemic-risk obligations (GPAI) bind Gemini since Aug 2025 — divergence raises compliance cost and headline risk.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **Sanctions:** Direct Russia exposure minimal (subsidiary in bankruptcy, ad/cloud sales ceased), but legacy judgment-enforcement and cloud reseller channels need monitoring; OFAC enforcement posture is aggressive.
- **Export controls:** Google Cloud/Vertex AI/TPU offerings must screen China-linked customers as US AI-chip controls tighten; hardware import tariffs exposure.
- **Privacy (GDPR/DMA):** Repeated fines (French CNIL €150m cookie fine, Irish DPC ad-personalisation cases) and DMA self-preferencing findings signal structural compliance strain, not one-off lapses.
- **Disclosure/class-action risk:** The Gemini 3.5 Pro matter suggests heightened investor scrutiny of AI-product timelines — forward AI statements are now litigation-sensitive.
- **FCPA/AML:** Moderate — exposure runs through emerging-market ad sales, Play developer payouts, and cloud government contracts; no current enforcement cited in materials reviewed.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Alphabet's sovereign risk is low (US-domiciled, no expropriation exposure anywhere material), but **regulatory/legal risk is the stock's dominant co-factor**: it now holds active adverse antitrust rulings in the US on both Search and ad tech, is fighting the first-ever DMA fines in the EU, and faces parallel regimes in the UK and India — while a DOJ appeal and a September 2026 ad-tech remedies trial keep structural-divestiture tail risk alive. Nothing above is existential (no fine remotely approaches balance-sheet capacity), but remedies that alter Search distribution or ad-tech ownership would directly impair the ~75% of revenue that is advertising. Rating is **High**, not Very High, because the most aggressive structural outcomes (Chrome/Android divestiture) have already been rejected once and cash generation remains exceptionally strong.

**Modeling implication:** Discount US/EU ad revenue growth by ~100–150bps for remedy-driven friction and bake a $10–25bn multi-jurisdiction fine/legal-claim reserve into base case; stress case = ad-tech divestiture + EU non-compliance penalties (up to 10% global turnover).
