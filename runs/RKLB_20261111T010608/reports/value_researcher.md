## ROCKET LAB USA (RKLB) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $62.71 · ~652M diluted shares (Q3'26) · Market cap ≈$40B · Net cash $4.3B · EV ≈$36B*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW (developing, unproven)**

Rocket Lab is a genuinely differentiated franchise, but it does not yet possess a Buffett-quality moat, because a moat must ultimately be demonstrated in returns on capital — and RKLB has never earned a positive return on capital in its public history.

**Evidence for a moat:**
- **Intangible assets / know-how:** Electron is the West's dominant dedicated small-lift vehicle (50+ launches, high reliability record). Vertical integration is deep — in-house Rutherford engines, carbon composite tanks, flight software, Photon satellite buses. This is real engineering capital, not a slide deck.
- **Switching costs (partial):** Space Systems (reaction wheels, star trackers, separation systems, solar) is designed *into* other companies' satellites, creating multi-year design-in stickiness. This segment sells to competitors, which is a structurally attractive position.
- **Scale/efficient-scale signals:** Record backlog of **~$3.6B** (Q3'26, +52% QoQ) against TTM revenue of $882M — ~4x coverage. Over **$515M of Neutron launch contracts** signed with constellation operators in Q3'26 alone. Government/Space Force/SDA work carries multi-year, hard-to-displace program incumbency.
- **Moat direction:** **Widening** on capability (Neutron, backlog, margin), but **unproven** on economics.

**Evidence against a wide moat:**
- Launch is fundamentally a **price-taking service** where the reference price is set by SpaceX (now a ~$1.9T public entity in this peer set). Falcon 9 rideshare economics cap what Electron and even Neutron can charge.
- **No demonstrated pricing power:** gross margin of 34–36% is respectable but nowhere near moat-business levels, and it *dipped* to 33.6% in Q3'26 as revenue mix shifted.
- Competition is intensifying (Firefly, Stoke, Blue Origin, plus SpaceX's cadence advantage). Capital intensity is permanent: growth requires continuous heavy capex.

**Circle-of-competence check:** The technology is understandable; the *terminal economics* of the launch market a decade out are not forecastable with confidence. That alone should cap position sizing.

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **3 / 10**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $245M | $436M | $602M | **$882M** |
| Revenue growth | +16% | +78% | +38% | **+73% (Q3'26 YoY)** |
| Gross margin | 21.0% | 26.6% | 34.4% | **36.2%** |
| Operating margin | −72.7% | −43.5% | −38.0% | −27.6% |
| Net income | −$183M | −$190M | −$198M | **−$227M** |
| Free cash flow | −$154M | −$116M | −$322M | ≈−$250M |
| ROIC | −24.3% | −22.3% | **−11.6%** | negative |
| Diluted shares (wtd) | 482M | 496M | 531M | **652M** |

**What is good:** Revenue growth is *accelerating* (38% → 73%), gross margin has expanded ~10x from 9% in 2022 to 36%, operating leverage is real (op margin −164% → −28%), and **Q3'26 delivered positive GAAP operating cash flow (+$7.2M)** for the first time. Capex moderated to $31M in Q3'26 from the Neutron build-out peak. This is a company crossing from "story" to "operating business."

**What is disqualifying for a quality score:**
- **No free cash flow, ever.** FCF has been negative for five straight years, cumulatively ≈−$950M. Owner earnings (NI + D&A − capex) were **−$311M in FY2025** — Buffett's core metric is deeply negative.
- **ROIC of −11.6% and negative every year.** By my framework, ROIC below WACC for five years means the moat is not yet earning its keep, full stop.
- **Serial dilution is the business model's silent tax:** weighted shares 452M (2021) → 652M (Q3'26), **+44%**, with equity jumping $2.26B → $5.80B in Q3'26 alone (~$2.3B equity raised). Per-share value creation lags enterprise value creation materially. SBC runs ~$71M/yr (~8% of revenue).
- Revenue quality: working-capital swings are large (−$84M in Q2'26, +$16M in Q3'26); backlog conversion timing is lumpy.

**Balance sheet (the real bright spot):** Cash and short-term investments **$4.61B**, total debt **$120M** → **net cash $4.33B**, current ratio 8.6x, equity $5.80B. Solvency risk is now essentially off the table for this decade. Book value per share ~$8–9.

### 3. Valuation Analysis — **Intrinsic value ≈ $18–30/share; price is 2–3x that**

Current multiples are outside any normal valuation framework:

| Metric | RKLB | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| EV / TTM revenue | **~41x** | Mega-cap A&D peers trade 2–3x |
| EV / TTM gross profit | **~112x** | No mature industrial sustains this |
| P/E | n/a (loss) | — |
| P/FCF | n/a (negative) | — |
| P/B | **~7–19x** | vs. tangible-ish book of $8–9/sh |
| FCF yield | ≈ **−0.6%** | Negative |

*(Tool-based DCF returned nonsense because base FCF is negative; peer-multiple comparison was against mega-cap industrials — not meaningful comps. I therefore built the valuation from scratch.)*

**What the price requires (reverse DCF, 12% discount rate, net cash $4.3B added):**

- **Base case — 30% revenue CAGR to 2036** → ~$12.2B revenue, 15% FCF margin → $1.8B FCF, 20x exit → PV of equity ≈ **$16B ≈ $23/share**
- **Bull case — 40% CAGR for 10 years** → ~$25B revenue, 20% FCF margin → $5.1B FCF, 22x exit → PV ≈ **$40B ≈ $58/share**
- **Bear case — Neutron slips again / launch pricing compresses** → 18% CAGR, thin margins → **$8–14/share**

**The current $62.71 price is essentially the bull case, undiscounted.** To justify it you must underwrite, simultaneously and for a decade: ~40% revenue CAGR, 20%+ terminal FCF margins in a capital-hungry, price-competitive industry, *and* a 22x exit multiple — while accepting further dilution. That is not a margin of safety; it is a requirement for perfection. Note that Piper Sandler's November note flags the stock as likely "rangebound" with a **$58** target, while the high targets ($100–122) are theme-driven terminal-value bets.

Even the sell-side median (~$85–100) implies paying ~35x 2030 revenue six years forward.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good (B+ / 8 of 10 on operating, 6 of 10 on capital allocation)**

**Strengths:**
- **Founder-operator:** Sir Peter Beck is a technically credible, long-tenured founder-CEO with a large personal stake and demonstrated willingness to bet the company on vertical integration. Skinner-box honesty is better than average for the sector.
- **Opportunistic capital allocation:** Raising ~$2.3B of equity in Q3'26 at $60–90/share — near all-time highs — rather than issuing debt is exactly the right move when your currency is expensive. That is a genuine value-accretive decision for existing holders and materially de-risks the Neutron program. Note that this is a large part of why book value jumped.
- **Executed on Electron:** cadence, reliability, and margin expansion from 9% → 36% gross margin is evidence of operational discipline.
- **No empire-building M&A:** acquisitions (Geost, SolAero, Sinclair) have been capability tuck-ins, not diworsification.
- No dividends or buybacks — appropriate at this stage.

**Deductions:**
- **Chronic schedule slippage:** Neutron has gone from 2024 → mid-2025 → late 2025 → Q4 2026 targets. Forecast credibility is a management-quality issue, and this is the single largest driver of equity value.
- **Dilution:** +44% share count in five years, ~8% of revenue in annual SBC. Shareholders own a shrinking slice of a growing pie.
- No insider transaction data was available in this dataset — an unverified gap I would want closed before committing capital.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID at $62.71**

*(Not "Strong Sell" — the business is improving fast and the balance sheet is fortress-like. This is a valuation verdict, not a business-quality verdict.)*

Rocket Lab passes the "understandable, well-run, improving business" test far better than it did two years ago. It fails the two tests that matter most to me:

1. **No moat proven by returns on capital.** ROIC has been negative for five years. A business that has never converted growth into owner earnings is still unproven, however impressive the top line.
2. **No margin of safety — the opposite.** At ~41x EV/sales and ~112x EV/gross profit, the market has already paid for the bull case. My central intrinsic value estimate is **$18–30 per share**, implying the stock trades at roughly a **2–3x premium** to fair value. A 20–30% *discount* would mean buying near **$15–20**.

Put differently: to earn a 12% return from here, Rocket Lab must become a ~$25B-revenue, $5B-FCF company by 2036. That is possible — it is the SpaceX-adjacent outcome — but it is a venture-capital probability distribution, not an investment with a margin of safety. **If you already own it, this is a rational point to harvest gains (the stock is up ~17x from $4 in early 2024); if you don't, wait.** I would become interested near $20–25, and only in a small position.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (i.e., risks that it's *worse*, not better)

1. **Neutron execution:** The entire valuation bridges on a medium-lift vehicle that has never flown and has slipped at least three times. A failure or a further 12-month slip plausibly takes the stock to $20–30.
2. **SpaceX price umbrella collapse:** Aggressive Starship/Falcon pricing or higher rideshare cadence could permanently cap Electron and Neutron pricing, preventing the 20% FCF margin the price requires.
3. **Continued dilution:** If FCF stays negative into 2028, expect more equity issuance — and, ominously, at lower prices if the narrative breaks.
4. **Lumpy, low-margin government concentration and working-capital swings** — backlog is record, but conversion to cash is unproven.
5. **Valuation-driven volatility:** Beta 2.61. This stock has drawn down 40–50% twice in the last two years (96 → 66; 37 → 16). Owner psychology, not fundamentals, will set the near-term price.
6. **Data gaps acknowledged:** no reliable peer EV/Sales set (tool comps were mega-cap industrials), no insider-transaction history, and no FY2024-era filings text review. None of these change the verdict, given the size of the valuation gap.
