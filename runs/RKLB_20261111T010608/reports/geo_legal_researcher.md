## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**#1 — U.S. federal budget/appropriations concentration (Probability: High | Impact: High)**
Rocket Lab has become a direct prime to the U.S. national-security space enterprise: SDA award of ~$805M for missile-tracking satellites (Tranche 3, total SDA awards >$1.3B), an up-to-$816M Space Force missile-defense satellite prime (July 2026), and a record **$266M multi-launch U.S. Space Force contract** (RSLP-Kodiak, Alaska) announced November 2026 — all inside the Golden Dome architecture (Pentagon cost estimate expanded to ~$185B, March 2026). This is a *demand* tailwind but a *political* single point of failure: a continuing resolution, a Golden Dome re-scoping, or a program-of-record cancellation would hit backlog conversion directly. Backlog is $3.57B with only ~33% recognized within 12 months — i.e., politically appropriable.

**#2 — Iridium acquisition regulatory execution (Probability of delay: High | Impact: High if blocked/re-termed)**
The $8B Iridium merger (signed June 28, 2026; $27.00 cash + RKLB stock per share) is now the dominant legal/regulatory event. Iridium shareholders approved it with ~99.6% of votes (Sept 24, 2026), but closing is guided to **mid-2027 or later** pending **FCC consent** (applications filed Aug 10, 2026, DA-26-985) **and foreign regulatory approvals**. Competitors are actively intervening: **SpaceX, Globalstar and Ligado** have pressed the FCC over vertical-integration and supplier-conflict issues — Globalstar's November 4, 2026 petition asks the FCC to examine Rocket Lab's dual role as Globalstar's primary spacecraft-bus supplier (17 platforms under MDA Space) *and* prospective owner-operator of a direct rival constellation. Outcome risk is delay/condition rather than outright block, but this is a live, adversarial administrative record with a 2027–28 financing/stock-consideration overhang.

**#3 — U.S.–China export controls and critical-minerals chokepoints (Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium)**
Space Systems (solar cells, reaction wheels, star trackers, avionics, now laser comms) requires gallium, germanium, rare earths and permanent magnets — precisely the categories China has restricted since 2023 and re-escalated in October 2025, with a pause running only to ~November 10, 2026. Rocket Lab is ITAR/EAR-compliant and U.S.-manufacturing-weighted (a relative advantage versus European peers), but tariffs and licensing friction hit unit margins on satellite hardware and can delay customer deliveries. Secondary China vector: any U.S. restriction on Chinese-owned customers/spectrum counterparties could reduce addressable commercial demand; conversely, geopolitically driven "de-risking" by allied governments is a net tailwind for a U.S.-flagged, Five-Eyes-sited launch provider.

**Watch item (not top-3):** LC-1 is a *single* launch complex in one jurisdiction. New Zealand is a low-risk, treaty-allied democracy, but a regulatory/licensing interruption by the NZ Space Agency, a marine-mammal/environmental judicial review, or a natural event at Māhia would halt Electron cadence with limited near-term fallback (LC-2 Wallops is being re-vectored to Neutron).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action — resolved at district court.** *In re Rocket Lab USA, Inc. Securities Litigation* (C.D. Cal., filed Feb 27, 2025; class period Nov 12, 2024 – Feb 25, 2025; Judge George H. Wu), triggered by the Bleecker Street short report (share price −9.8% on Feb 25, 2025). Per the May 7, 2026 10-Q: **"On April 16, 2026, our Motion to Dismiss the securities class action was granted with prejudice. Plaintiffs have 30 days to appeal."** No confirmed appellate filing identified; the 30-day window has lapsed absent a docket update I could not verify.
- **Follow-on derivative action** arising from the same short-seller allegations remains on the defence counsel docket (Skadden, per firm bio). Low expected cost; nuisance/indemnification and D&O dynamics rather than balance-sheet risk.
- **FAA mishap oversight.** Following the September 2025 Electron anomaly, the FAA supervised Rocket Lab's mishap investigation under an FAA-approved plan and **authorized resumption of launches on Oct 25, 2025** (36 days). Demonstrates a cooperative, low-friction regulator relationship — a meaningful mitigant to the "single failure could ground the fleet" concern.
- **Iridium merger approvals stack:** HSR/antitrust, FCC transfer-of-control (Form S-4 filed; DA-26-985 public notice, comment cycle open), **foreign telecom/landing-rights approvals across Iridium's ~100+ market footprint**, and Iridium's U.S. government/intelligence-community customer consents. Expect national-security conditions and potential FCC-mandated conduct commitments (supplier firewalls, delivery priority).
- **Export/launch licensing regime:** ITAR/EAR on launch vehicles and spacecraft components; FAA launch/re-entry licences (LC-1 and LC-2); NZ *Outer Space and High-altitude Activities Act* licensing and Overseas Investment Office consents for Māhia land; ITU/FCC spectrum for any owned constellation post-Iridium.
- **Capital-markets disclosure risk:** $3.912B of ATM proceeds in 9M-26, collared-forward settlements, and a terminated $3.6B bridge (with a $27.3M extinguishment loss) raise disclosure-control and dilution-governance scrutiny if the Iridium financing is re-cut.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

**No sanctions, FCPA, AML or DOJ/SEC-enforcement red flags identified.** Rocket Lab sells to U.S. and allied governments and to Five-Eyes/European commercial customers; it has no material Russia, China, Iran or broader EM operations, so bribery/facilitation-payment and sanctions-breach exposure is structurally low.

Residual focus areas:
1. **ITAR deemed-export/foreign-person access** — a New Zealand launch site staffed by non-U.S. persons adjacent to U.S.-origin technology is a perennial Technology Control Plan and deemed-export audit surface. This is the single most likely source of a future export-control finding.
2. **Vertical-integration/self-dealing optics** — post-Iridium, Rocket Lab would be simultaneously a merchant supplier to competing constellation operators (Globalstar, MDA Space) and an owner-operator. Regulatory firewall commitments will need enforcement, and any delivery-priority dispute becomes an FCC/commercial litigation matter.
3. **Government-contract compliance (FAR/DFARS, cost accounting, contingent-fee/ethics rules)** — inevitable as prime awards scale past $1.3B; organizational-conflict-of-interest (OCI) rules become live given its component-supply role across the defense industrial base.
4. **Data privacy** — low; no consumer data footprint of consequence.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

**MEDIUM.**

Rationale: operational and customer geography is almost entirely U.S./Five-Eyes/allied (U.S., NZ, Canada, Germany, Australia) — no sanctionable, expropriation-prone or currency-control-exposed jurisdictions, and no nationalisation risk on U.S./NZ assets. Litigation is trending *favorably* (class action dismissed with prejudice). The "Medium" rather than "Low" rating is driven by (a) very high U.S. Government revenue concentration inside a volatile appropriations/Golden Dome political cycle, (b) the adversarial FCC/antitrust record and multi-jurisdiction approval path for the $8B Iridium transaction, and (c) ITAR/critical-minerals supply exposure to U.S.–China coercion. Key monitoring triggers: FCC order timing and any conditions in the Iridium docket; FY27 appropriations for SDA/Golden Dome; and any Electron/Neutron anomaly triggering a new FAA grounding.
