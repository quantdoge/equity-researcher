## 1. Sector Overview

Rocket Lab sits at the intersection of two very different markets: the ~$110B-revenue (FY25, 34-company sample) US **Industrials/Aerospace & Defense complex**, growing a mid-single-to-high-single-digit rate with ~10% net margins, and the far smaller but faster **space launch/space systems** market — sized at ~$18.3B in 2025, projected ~$22.2B in 2026 (+21%) and ~$32.2B by 2032. The sub-sector is in an **early-growth, capital-intensive expansion phase**: revenue is compounding 30%+ across credible pure-plays, but almost all of them burn cash and post negative operating margins. Structure is highly concentrated — SpaceX is now listed (SPCX, $1.89T market cap, $18.7B FY25 revenue, 49.4% gross margin), roughly **50x Rocket Lab's market cap and 31x its revenue**, and sets the price umbrella for the entire industry. Demand is currently **supply-constrained, not demand-constrained**: Falcon 9 bookings are reportedly closing out, giving remaining providers unusual pricing power after a decade of deflationary $/kg.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Government launch demand is stepping up in absolute terms.** The Space Force launch program is expected to expand by **91 missions and ~$17B**, against an FY26 USSF budget request of **$26.3B**; the military satellite market grows from ~$18.4B (2025) to ~$19.9B (2026). This is contracted, multi-year, cost-plus-adjacent demand — ideal for backlog conversion.
- **Tight near-term launch supply = pricing power.** With Falcon 9 manifest effectively sold out, incremental capacity commands premium ASPs. Rocket Lab's backlog jumped **~52% in one quarter to ~$3.6B**, i.e. **4.1x TTM revenue** — exceptional forward visibility for an industrial.
- **Proliferated LEO / responsive space architecture shift.** Defence and commercial buyers are moving from few-large-satellites to many-small-satellites plus dedicated responsive launch. This rewards exactly Rocket Lab's stack (Electron dedicated small lift, Photon/spacecraft buses, components sold to third parties — evidenced by the NASA ESCAPADE Mars spacecraft build).

---

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Neutron slippage to 2027 removes the medium-lift answer to Falcon 9.** The addressable-TAM step-change and the profitability inflection both push right, sustaining cash burn. Share count rose from 528.7M (Q3'25) to 652.3M (Q3'26), **+23.4% dilution in twelve months** — the sector's structural funding model is equity issuance.
- **Rivalry is brutal and asymmetric.** SpaceX at 49% gross margin and $18.7B revenue can cut price; Starship's falling $/kg plus rideshare cannibalisation threatens dedicated small/medium launch economics. Chinese state-subsidised launch adds further capacity. High fixed costs + a dominant price-setter is a poor structural combination.
- **Margin compression and input-cost inflation.** Rocket Lab's gross margin fell to **33.6% in Q3'26 from 37.0% a year earlier** as mix shifted and Neutron costs ramped — while aerospace-grade materials, skilled labour and range/launch infrastructure costs rise, and hot CPI prints pressure the long-duration multiples these stocks trade on (RKLB beta 2.61; -58% from its $150 May 2026 peak).

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

**Rationale:**
- **Clear #2 in Western commercial launch** and the only Western player with a funded medium-lift vehicle (Neutron) capable of attacking Falcon 9's core market. In small dedicated lift, Electron is the effective non-SpaceX benchmark.
- **Growth and margin superiority are structural, not cyclical:** 5-yr revenue CAGR **76.5%**; gross margin from **-3.0% (FY21) to 34.4% (FY25)** demonstrates genuine operating leverage and vertical integration (in-house engines, avionics, spacecraft buses, components).
- **Backlog coverage of 4.1x revenue** with firm, high-ASP, government-anchored contracts gives earnings visibility no pure-play peer matches (LUNR -7.9%, RDW +10.3% growth).
- **Diversification** across launch + space systems cushions single-market launch volatility — unlike ASTS/RDW/LUNR which are single-asset bets.
- **Caveats:** still GAAP-loss-making, dependent on a single unproven vehicle for its next leg of growth, and facing the industry's only true scale competitor. Share count +23% YoY is a real per-share drag. Competitive position is strong **relative to peers**; relative to SpaceX it is challenger, not leader (~3% of the two companies' combined revenue).

---

## 6. Sector Rating

**Attractive (space launch/space systems sub-sector) — Neutral (broad Industrials).**

Demand is visible, government-backed and growing ~20%+ near term; capacity is tight enough to restore pricing power; and barriers (capital, ITAR/export control, FAA licensing, launch range access, flight heritage) are genuinely high. Offsetting this, the sub-sector is **structurally concentrated** — a single dominant player with 49% gross margins sets price — and returns on capital across pure-plays remain negative, meaning equity issuance is the funding model. The broad Industrials aggregate is mid-cycle and unremarkable (FY25: $1.09T revenue, 33.0% gross margin, 10.4% net margin).

**Bottom line for the memo:** an attractive *demand* market with a difficult *competitive structure*; own the #2 with vertical integration and backlog, but underwrite a 2027 Neutron catalyst and a ~42x sales multiple that leaves no room for execution slippage.
