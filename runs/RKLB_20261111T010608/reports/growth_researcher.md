# Rocket Lab USA (RKLB) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Price $62.71 | Mkt cap ~$41B | EV ~$36.2B (net of $4.7B cash) | Beta 2.61**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Rocket Lab has evolved from a small-launch niche player (Electron) into a vertically integrated **space infrastructure platform** — launch + spacecraft platforms + components + laser comms + on-orbit management — and the model is now inflecting violently: revenue grew **73% YoY in Q3 2026** on a $602M base, backlog jumped **52% sequentially to $3.57B** (3.6x LTM revenue), and gross margin has structurally expanded from **-3% (2021) to 34% (GAAP) / 38.4% (non-GAAP)**. The thesis is that Rocket Lab becomes the credible #2 to SpaceX across the entire value chain — the only Western company that both launches and builds the satellite. The bear case is that **Neutron, the medium-lift vehicle that unlocks ~10x the TAM, has slipped again to an undated 2027 first flight**. You are paying today for a rocket that has not yet flown.

## 3. TAM Assessment

- **Launch:** Neutron's addressable market is the medium-lift segment — constellation build-out, national security (NSSL Lane 1), and commercial. Rocket Lab has **raised Neutron's base price to $70M** and has **all 16 first missions contracted (~$515M booked in Q3 alone)**. At 25 launches/yr × $70M, Neutron alone is a **$1.75B revenue line** — roughly 3x current total company revenue.
- **Space Systems:** satellite manufacturing, components, laser comms (Mynaric), robotics/optics (GEOST) — riding SDA/defense constellation demand. Already ~$860M annualized.
- **TAM:** global space economy ~$600B+ by mid-2030s; Rocket Lab's serviceable slice (Western launch + spacecraft systems) is plausibly **$40–60B** by 2030.
- **Penetration:** current revenue ~$1B = **~2% of SAM**. This is a penetration story, not a share-gain story.
- **Differentiation:** the only integrated Western alternative to SpaceX — Electron's 50+ consecutive successful launches, dedicated small-launch reliability (unlike rideshare), vertical integration in components, and now end-to-end "build + launch + operate." **SpaceX is now public at ~$18.7B revenue (33% growth)** — a useful anchor that the market pays extreme multiples for scaled space infrastructure, but also a reminder that RKLB is a distant #2.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Neutron first flight — 2027 (undated).** Replacement forward dome manufactured; no firm date set. Success = the single largest re-rating event in the story. Failure/delay = further multiple compression. *Binary.*
2. **Q4 2026 results, ~Feb 25, 2027** — guide $270–290M; first look at FY27 revenue/Neutron cadence. Street FY27 revenue $1.36B.
3. **Neutron production ramp** — second vehicle build, LC-3 pad at Wallops, VA; hiring surge already visible.
4. **Mynaric / laser-comms scaling** into SDA constellations — the highest-margin space-systems lever; margin expansion as integration costs annualize out.
5. **Government awards** — NSSL Phase 3 task orders, SDA Tranches, Golden Dome architecture. Each is a step-function backlog event.
6. **Potential index inclusion** and any opportunistic capital raise (they have $4.7B — a raise would be for acceleration, not survival).

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (accumulate; not Strong Buy)

The **fundamental thesis is strengthening while the stock has de-rated 58%** — that is the setup. Revenue is accelerating (38% → 63%), backlog is up 52% in one quarter to $3.57B, gross margins have expanded 37 points in four years, operating cash flow has turned positive, and the balance sheet holds **$4.7B in cash** — meaning Neutron is fully funded with no existential financing risk. Forward revenue CAGR of ~35% to $2.7B by 2030 is a credible path, and at **26x 2027E sales / ~20x 2028E sales**, the multiple is demanding but no longer absurd for a company with 3.6x revenue in contracted backlog.

What keeps this from Strong Buy: **Neutron has slipped three times and is now undated**, the first flight is a binary event, valuation still embeds flawless execution (288x 2027E EBITDA), dilution is running ~29%/yr, and the Google Trends decay says the retail bid that drove the May peak is gone. I would build the position in tranches — a core holding now, with adds on any Neutron-related drawdown toward $45–50, where you are paying ~19x 2027E sales for the same backlog.

**Why not Hold/Sell:** at 5-year revenue of $2.7B (management-track) and 20%+ EBITDA margins, a 12–15x sales exit implies a $500–600B... *check* — $2.7B × 12x = $32B, below today's EV. The stock therefore needs revenue **above** consensus by 2030 (i.e., Neutron working at scale, $4B+) to generate real upside. That is exactly the Cathie Wood bet: the market is mis-modeling the post-Neutron inflection. Median PT ~$105 (+67%) reflects that optionality.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Neutron execution (highest severity).** Three delays, now undated. A fourth slip or a first-flight failure would compress the multiple materially. This is a first-flight rocket — expect anomalies.
2. **Valuation risk / rate sensitivity.** 26x 2027E revenue, 288x EBITDA, beta 2.61. Any growth disappointment gets punished 30–50% (already demonstrated: -58% drawdown).
3. **Competitive threat from SpaceX.** Falcon 9 rideshare pricing, Starship's eventual cadence, plus Blue Origin, Firefly, Stoke. Neutron enters a market where reusability is already entrenched — pricing power may prove weaker than $70M implies.
4. **Margin reversal.** GAAP GM *declined* sequentially (36.1% → 33.6%) on Mynaric consolidation and loss of tariff refunds. Low-margin early Neutron units will pressure margins further — the "expanding margin" narrative could stall for 12–18 months.
5. **Dilution.** +29% share count YoY; SBC $18-28M/quarter. Per-share value creation lags enterprise value creation.
6. **Cash burn.** $322M FY25 FCF outflow; elevated investment guided to continue. The $4.7B buffer is ample, but burn plus dilution limits optionality.
7. **Government concentration & budget risk.** Space Systems growth is increasingly defense-driven (SDA, NSSL, Golden Dome) — exposed to appropriation cycles and program recompetes.
