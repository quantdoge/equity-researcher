### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Positives:** Rocket Lab operates under genuinely tight environmental permitting (NZ EPA/EEA & Continental Shelf Act marine consents; FAA launch licences; NEPA review at Wallops/MARS). New Zealand's 2026 statutory review concluded environmental risk remains low up to 1,000 launches provided debris is not deposited on sensitive features — i.e., the regulator has effectively validated the operating model. Rocket Lab is also on the *solution* side of debris (it has flown active-debris-removal and rideshare customers, and its space-systems segment sells to debris-tracking operators).

**Negatives:**
- **Zero public emissions disclosure.** No Scope 1, 2 or 3 inventory; no net-zero target; no CDP submission. DitchCarbon scores it 3/100 against a 15 industry average. For a company scaling to ~1,000 launches, direct combustion/upper-stage emissions plus Scope 3 from composites, aluminium and electronics are unquantified.
- **Atmospheric science risk is real and rising.** NASA TM-2024 documents reentry emissions from vaporising debris and spent stages rising from ~1,000 t/yr toward >30,000 t/yr — a regulatory narrative that could tighten upper-stage disposal rules mid-decade.
- **Disclosure retreat.** The 2021 Impact Report (published Aug-2022) was removed from the website in 2024 with no public explanation. That is a governance signal as much as an environmental one, and it removes the only baseline against which progress could be measured.
- **Materials/manufacturing footprint.** Neutron's carbon-composite structures and additive-manufactured engines are energy-intensive; the Oct-2026 tank-dome void defect shows process-control immaturity at scale.

**No environmental fines, spills or remediation liabilities** surfaced in searches — a genuine positive, though absence of evidence partly reflects weak disclosure.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Safety:** High-hazard operations (cryogenics, hypergolics, high-pressure composites, launch/range ops) with a good-to-date record — **no fatality or major OSHA citation identified in my searches**. The safety culture is evidenced structurally by rapid, disciplined mishap investigations (return-to-flight in weeks, not months) and by FAA-approved mishap plans. This is the single most important social factor and it reads favourably. *Caveat: I could not verify TRIR/lost-time rates — Rocket Lab does not publish them.*

**Labour & human capital:** ~2,600–3,500 employees scaling fast through acquisitions. Mynaric (Munich) came out of a financial restructuring with prior equity wiped out — integration and retention risk in a scarce laser-comms talent pool. Rocket Lab maintains a written affirmative action programme, a documented supplier code of conduct (suppliers must comply with applicable labour and environmental law), and gender targets in internship/mentorship intake. Pay ratio is wide (~280:1 in FY2024: $20.1M CEO vs $71.8K median) but FY2025 CEO pay fell to $6.8M, heavily equity-weighted — defensible pay-for-performance alignment.

**Community/social licence:** Māhia (NZ) and Wallops (VA) operations create marine exclusion zones, noise and range closures — ongoing but well-managed; NZ stakeholders have continued to grant expanded launch rights. The bigger social question is **defense**: SDA/Golden Dome missile-tracking satellites and HASTE hypersonic testing place Rocket Lab squarely in weapons-support supply chains. This is not "controversial weapons" in the cluster-munition/landmine sense, so most screens won't exclude it — but EU and Nordic mandates with "military equipment" or "missile systems" revenue thresholds will.

**Diversity:** Mixed signal — targets exist, but the company de-emphasised public DEI reporting after 2022. Human-rights due diligence on 3TG/conflict minerals in space-systems supply chains is not evidenced publicly.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Deductions:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality** — Beck has been Chairman since May-2021 while serving as President & CEO. No independent lead director is clearly evidenced. For a company whose entire thesis rests on one person's technical bets, this is the core weakness.
- **Founder concentration** — ~8–9% beneficial ownership plus reputational control; a 5M-share (~$465M) sale was executed under a pre-set 10b5-1 plan (procedurally clean, but dilutes alignment optics).
- **Disclosure opacity** — ESG report discontinued; 2021 Impact Report deleted. Governance of sustainability reporting appears ad hoc rather than board-owned.
- **Litigation history** — a Bleecker Street short report (25-Feb-2025) alleging deception over Neutron timelines triggered a securities class action; it was **dismissed with prejudice** (May-2026) and a related management suit was dropped. Resolved, but the episode shows forecast credibility risk on the company's single most important programme — and the Oct-2026 Neutron delay to 2027 rhymes with it.
- **Shareholder-rights erosion** — the 2026 AGM included a merger of a wholly-owned sub into Rocket Lab USA to amend its charter and **remove a pass-through voting provision**. Minor in dollar terms, negative in tone.

**Credits:**
- **No dual-class share structure** — one share, one vote. This is a meaningful positive versus SpaceX/peer founder vehicles and against the sector norm.
- **Majority-independent board** with recent refresh (Merline Saintil, Jon Olson added); independent Audit and Compensation Committees.
- **Compensation is genuinely equity-weighted** (88% of CEO pay) with FY2025 pay cut ~66% year-on-year — real pay-for-performance, not pay-for-tenure.
- **Insider alignment** — founder still operating the business 20 years in.
- **Clean capital-markets conduct** — the $1.944B ATM and Iridium financing were disclosed and stockholder-approved; Iridium shareholders approved; no related-party self-dealing surfaced.
- Holding-company reorganisation (May-2025) was transparently executed.

**Net:** structurally sound for a founder-led defense-tech, but below investment-grade governance because of duality, key-person risk and ESG disclosure withdrawal.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **United States:** SEC climate-disclosure rule is stayed/being unwound — so **no mandatory federal GHG reporting** near-term. Exposure instead comes from **FAA/ODRP** launch licensing and debris-mitigation rules (upper-stage disposal), **NEPA** environmental assessments for LC-2/Wallops and Neutron cadence, and SEC Reg S-K human-capital disclosure. **California SB 253 / SB 261** are the live items: SB 261 (climate risk report) applies at >$500M revenue doing business in California — Rocket Lab is likely **already in scope**; SB 253 (>$1B revenue, Scope 1/2/3 assurance) is crossed as revenue annualises near ~$1B in 2026–27. This is the single most concrete, dated ESG compliance obligation on the company.
- **New Zealand:** EEA & Continental Shelf Act marine consents, NZ Space Agency launch licence, EPA oversight at Māhia; 2026 review raised permitted launches toward 1,000 with low-risk finding — permissive but condition-laden.
- **European Union (via Mynaric, Munich):** Germany's Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs export-control approval was a closing condition — meaning future Mynaric sales into EU defense customers face **German export licensing**. EU **CSRD** likely *not* triggered on turnover thresholds yet (EU revenue well below €150M), but EU institutional and defense customers increasingly impose **Zero Debris Charter / ISO 24113** supply-chain requirements. **CBAM:** negligible — Rocket Lab imports no CBAM-covered goods at scale.
- **ESG-linked debt:** none identified. The $1.775B Iridium facility and convertible/ATM financing carry **no sustainability-linked margin ratchets** — so no direct cost-of-capital penalty, but also no external pressure mechanism. As a US defense contractor, federal ESG/DEI-related contracting scrutiny (2025–26 policy reversal) is a *tailwind* to disclosure restraint, not a risk.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD**
*(E: Laggard | S: Average | G: Average–)*

Rocket Lab fails the three tests that define an ESG Leader in this sector: (i) **no emissions inventory** in an industry where launch and reentry emissions are under active scientific and regulatory scrutiny; (ii) **no sustainability report since 2022**, with the prior report withdrawn; (iii) **no public decarbonisation or safety-performance targets**. Aerospace & Defense is itself a structurally high-risk ESG sub-industry. Its social record is clean and its governance is founder-tight but not abusive (single-class shares, equity-heavy pay, majority-independent board) — which is why it is a Laggard rather than an "ESG Risk" name. The trajectory is **deteriorating on disclosure** even as operations scale.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL to the thesis — but with one asymmetric, material risk**

**Why neutral:** none of the ESG factors above threaten near-term cash flows in a way that changes the equity story. There is no carbon-price exposure (no EU/ETS-costed production), no ESG-linked debt covenant, no major environmental liability, no fatality record, and no dual-class overhang. Exclusion-driven selling is a small, quantifiable drag — ESG-mandated AUM ownership of RKLB is low, and its shareholder base is retail/growth, not sustainability-labelled. The Iridium and Mynaric deals improve strategic durability without creating ESG covenants.

**Where ESG becomes financial:**
1. **Licence-to-operate is the real ESG risk, and it is priced as an operating risk.** FAA mishap groundings and NZ consent conditions gate launch cadence; the Oct-2026 Neutron tank-dome defect has already cost ~$3–4B of market value and pushed first flight to 2027. Investors should treat regulatory/debris-compliance and manufacturing quality as *ESG-driven* valuation variables, not just engineering ones.
2. **SB 253/261 compliance is a dated, non-optional cost** (~$2–5M/yr plus assurance) arriving just as revenue crosses $1B — and Rocket Lab currently has **no inventory to report**, so first-year effort is front-loaded. Mild negative to 2027 margins and a governance-credibility test.
3. **Key-person and capital-allocation concentration.** CEO/Chair duality plus an ~$8B, debt-funded Iridium integration raises the governance discount. The Bleecker Street episode — dismissed, but followed by a second Neutron delay — argues for a haircut on management timeline guidance rather than on ESG per se.
4. **Defense screens.** Golden Dome/SDA/HASTE exposure will keep RKLB out of EU/Nordic "military equipment" and controversial-weapons screened mandates — a structural, permanent exclusion, but one that matters little to the marginal buyer today.

**Recommendation:** Do **not** change the rating on ESG grounds; ESG is not a thesis-breaker for RKLB. Do **downgrade the credibility weighting on management timelines** and add a standing risk flag on **launch-licence interruption and manufacturing process control** — those are the channels through which ESG actually hits this P&L. If ESG disclosure quality matters to the mandate, note that RKLB would currently fail most sustainability-data screens and is unlikely to improve before its first SB 253 cycle.

**Data gaps to close before relying on this:** verified Scope 1/2/3 figures (none exist publicly), TRIR/lost-time injury rates, board composition and independent-lead-director status from the 2026 proxy (SEC fetch blocked), conflict-minerals/3TG due diligence on space-systems suppliers, and Mynaric's EU export-licence pipeline.
