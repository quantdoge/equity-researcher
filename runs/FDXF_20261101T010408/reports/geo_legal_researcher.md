## FDXF — FedEx Freight Holding Company, Inc.
### Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

---

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

FedEx Freight is the largest North American LTL carrier, spun off from FedEx Corporation on **June 1, 2026** (80.1% of shares distributed pro rata to FedEx holders; FedEx retained **19.9%**). The 10-K states plainly: *"While our operations are limited to North America, we are indirectly impacted by broader macroeconomic and international trade policies"* and *"Our operations are limited to the United States, Canada, and Mexico"* (10-K, Item 1 Business / Item 1A; 10-Q Q3 TY2026).

| Country / Region | Exposure | Basis | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | ~93–96% (est.) | HQ Memphis; ~355 terminals of which **320+ in the US**; coverage of **98% of US ZIP codes**; all 50 states. US/non-US is the only split tagged in the 10-K XBRL. | **Low** |
| **Canada** | ~3–5% (est.) | Cross-border, intra-Canada service; FedEx Freight Canada Corp.; provincial transport board licensing; CBSA regulation | **Medium** |
| **Mexico** | ~1–3% (est.) | Cross-border and intra-Mexico LTL; USMCA-dependent; maquiladora/nearshoring freight | **Medium–High** |
| **Puerto Rico / USVI** | <1% | Service via alliances only (no owned infrastructure) | **Low** |
| China / Russia / EU / EM | **0% direct** | No operations; no sanctions exposure | **Nil** |

> **Data gap, stated honestly:** FDXF does not disclose revenue by country in the 10-K — only a US vs. non-US tagged split plus a note that *"We are subject to income taxes in the United States, Canada, and Mexico."* The percentages above are my estimates derived from terminal counts, tractor fleet location, and FedEx's historical segment disclosure (FedEx Freight FY2025 revenue ≈ **$8.89B**). Treat Canada/Mexico as an upper bound of ~7%.

**Structural point:** the geographic risk here is not "emerging markets" or "sanctions" — it is **trade-policy risk on a single land border**. This is a pure-play North American freight beta.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**#1 — USMCA Non-Renewal (HIGH probability / HIGH impact)**
On **July 1, 2026**, the USMCA Free Trade Commission held its mandatory six-year joint review. USTR Ambassador Greer's statement: *"The United States did not agree to renew the USMCA in its current form. As a result, the USMCA is not renewed… the Agreement remains in force pending resolution of these issues or until the Agreement's termination."* A third bilateral negotiating round with Mexico was held in Mexico City the week of July 20, 2026. Because the agreement now sits on **annual review** footing rather than a locked 16-year horizon, tariff/trade terms facing FDXF's cross-border book reset every year. FDXF's own risk factor concedes: *"given the amount of North American trade that moves by truck, any new or revised restrictions on trade could have a significant impact on supply and demand in the LTL freight transportation industry."*

**#2 — Tariff Escalation with Canada and Mexico (HIGH probability / MEDIUM-HIGH impact)**
USTR has already imposed **Section 338 tariffs on Canada**, with further announced escalations taking effect. The 10-K states the US has *"impos[ed] and threaten[ed] to impose tariffs on many goods imported into the United States (including certain goods from Canada and Mexico)"* and that Canada and Mexico have retaliated. Reported effect: *"These actions have contributed to weakness in the global economy and in the transportation industry which has led to lower shipments, adversely affecting our results of operations."* Independent survey data (PRNewswire, 2026) finds trade disputes *"show no signs of abating."* Transmission mechanism: fewer cross-border loads, plus demand destruction in US manufacturing/retail freight that feeds domestic LTL too.

**#3 — Nearshoring Shortens Length of Haul (MEDIUM probability / MEDIUM impact — underappreciated)**
Explicit in the 10-K: *"as companies move manufacturing closer to consumer markets and expand the number of distribution centers, we transport shipments shorter distances, which adversely affects our revenue per shipment."* Mexico nearshoring is a **revenue-per-shipment headwind even when it is a volume tailwind**. Compounding: US negotiators have made **Chinese investment in Mexico** a central USMCA demand — any rules-of-origin tightening or FDI screening could disrupt maquiladora-origin freight, a core Mexico LTL vertical.

*Secondary but real:* fuel-price/supply shocks (no hedging — *"We have no derivative financial instruments to reduce our exposure to fuel price fluctuations"*), and exposure to Canadian provincial licensing/border-agency friction.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Spin-off tax contingency — the single largest legal line item.** The distribution is intended to be tax-free, but the 10-K flags *"significant tax liabilities that could result from the Spin-Off or related transactions failing to qualify as tax-free."* Practically this imposes **Section 355(e)-style behavioral restrictions on the company**: limits on M&A, large buybacks, and share issuance for the statutory window. This is a genuine strategic handcuff on capital allocation and a common source of post-spin shareholder litigation.

**"Nuclear verdicts" / insurance market failure — material and specific.** The 10-K documents that *"the commercial trucking industry has experienced a wave of blockbuster or so-called 'nuclear' verdicts, including some instances in which juries have awarded hundreds of millions of dollars."* Consequences disclosed: several insurers have **stopped offering auto liability coverage to trucking companies entirely**, excess coverage availability is *"decreasing,"* premiums *"increasing significantly,"* and carriers are *"challenging insurance claims more frequently."* FDXF is self-insured below retention. This is a structural, worsening legal-cost risk, not a one-off.

**Regulatory stack:** DOT/FMCSA (hours of service, ELD mandate, CSA/SMS safety scoring — publicly visible scores that can affect customer relationships and driver hiring); TSA & CBP (cargo security, customs brokerage); Canada Border Services Agency and **provincial transport boards** (licensing to move goods inter-provincially); EPA and **California** vehicle emissions rules — note the disclosure that California's Clean Air Act preemption **waivers "have been recently curtailed leaving businesses with questions on enforceability,"** creating a two-sided compliance uncertainty across jurisdictions.

**Labor:** FDXF is the non-union FedEx unit but the 10-K confirms *"Labor unions have in the past attempted to organize employees at FedEx Freight and could attempt to do so again."* Pending federal risk: the **Faster Labor Contracts Act passed the House in June 2026**, which would accelerate initial CBA negotiation timelines post-election. Separately flagged: **reclassification risk for FedEx Custom Critical independent service providers** — a litigation theory FedEx has fought for two decades.

**IP/brand:** FDXF **does not own** the FedEx name, logo, or purple/orange trade dress. It operates under a **Trademark License Agreement** with Federal Express plus an IP cross-license. Reputational or compliance events at parent FedEx can impair FDXF's brand, with limited control.

**Related-party structure:** 19.9% FedEx retained stake, Transition Services Agreement (with disclosed risk of failing to exit *"in the expected timeframe and at the expected cost"*), and a **$4.1B cash dividend to FedEx** funded partly by **$3.7B of senior notes** (Feb 2026) and a $600M term loan. Leverage and governance-overhang risk rather than geopolitical, but it belongs in the legal/structure file.

No active DOJ/SEC/FTC investigation against FDXF is disclosed in the filings reviewed, and no material pending litigation was identified in the 10-K's Item 3 excerpt available to me — though **I could not retrieve the full Item 3 Legal Proceedings text** (SEC.gov returned 403s and the filing mirror truncated before p.21). Treat litigation quantum as unverified.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM (low end)**

**Rationale for the rating:** FDXF scores *very well* on the axes that usually drive geopolitical risk — **zero emerging-market exposure, zero Russia/China sanctions exposure, zero currency-control or repatriation risk, no expropriation risk, single-currency (USD) revenue base, and a diversified customer book** (top 5 customers = 9% of revenue; top 25 = 21%; largest = 4%). Rule of law across all three operating jurisdictions is strong.

It scores *poorly* on three concentrated axes: (1) it is a **pure derivative of US–Canada–Mexico trade policy at precisely the moment USMCA has been allowed to lapse into annual review** with Section 338 tariffs already live on Canada; (2) it carries a **binary spin-off tax contingency** that constrains strategy; and (3) it operates in the one US industry facing an acknowledged **liability-insurance market breakdown**.

**Net:** Medium. If USMCA bilateral talks collapse or Canada/Mexico tariffs escalate materially, this migrates to **High** quickly — and the equity's post-spin drawdown (~40% from June 2026 levels per Seeking Alpha) suggests the market is already pricing some of that. Conversely, a renewed USMCA with tighter China-in-Mexico rules would remove the dominant overhang and pull the rating to **Low**.
