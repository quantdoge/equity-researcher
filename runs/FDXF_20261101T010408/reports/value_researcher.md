# FDXF — FedEx Freight Holding Company
**Price $126.04 (30-Oct-26) | Mkt cap $18.8B | ~149.5M sh | Net debt ~$3.9B est. | EV ~$22.7B**

*Business: the largest North American less-than-truckload (LTL) carrier, spun off from FedEx on 1-Jun-26. ~$9.6B run-rate revenue, ~40,000 employees, national terminal network across US, Canada, Mexico, PR, USVI.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW**

**What's genuinely good.** LTL is a structurally attractive freight sub-industry. A national hub-and-spoke terminal network with ~40,000 employees is capital- and time-intensive to replicate — this is a real **efficient-scale / cost-advantage moat**, and FDXF owns the densest national network in the industry. LTL pricing has been unusually disciplined since 2023; FDXF posted **revenue per shipment ex-fuel +10.1%** in the transition period. Industry consolidation behind a handful of national carriers (FDXF, ODFL, SAIA, XPO, ArcBest) supports that.

**Why not Wide.** (a) The service is fundamentally commoditized — customers route freight on price/transit trade-offs, not brand. (b) **Margins are structurally below best-in-class**: ~15.5% adjusted operating margin versus ODFL's ~25% EBIT / 32% EBITDA. That gap is an execution/service-quality gap, not a scale gap. (c) **Moat trend is negative over five years**: operating margin fell from 18.8% (FY23) → 18.6% (FY24) → 15.1% (FY25) → 6.1% GAAP (FY26), and volume is shrinking (shipments −1.4%, tonnage flat) even as price rises. (d) ROIC is now only modestly above any reasonable WACC (~9.2% in FY26 on a debt-laden balance sheet; 13–15% normalized vs. ~9% WACC) — a moat should produce much wider spreads.

**Verdict:** a solid franchise with a defensible cost position, but it is a cyclical price-taker, not a compounding moat machine.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

Trailing multiples are meaningless (P/E 28.8× on distorted EPS; P/FCF −88.9×; EV/EBITDA 18.0× on depressed EBITDA). I value on normalized transition-period economics.

**Base case (calendar 2027):** revenue $9.7B × 13.0% adj. operating margin = **$1.26B EBIT**; +$470M D&A = $1.73B EBITDA; −$237M interest; 25% tax → **~$768M net / ~$5.14 EPS**. At $126 = **24.5× P/E, 13.1× EV/EBITDA**, 4.1% FCF yield.

**Bull (mgmt 15% margin target, $10B revenue):** ~$6.30 EPS → 20×; EV/EBITDA ~11.6×; FCF ~$1.0B → 5.3% yield.
**Bear (freight recession, 10% margin, $9.2B revenue):** ~$3.40 EPS → 37×; net debt/EBITDA ~2.8×, with **no dividend cushion**.

**Intrinsic value:** DCF on normalized FCF of ~$800M growing 5%, 9% WACC, 2.5% terminal → **$71–85/share**. Cross-check on multiples (13–14× normalized EBITDA less net debt) → **$117–133/share**. Blending, **fair value ≈ $95–110, midpoint ~$102**.

**At $126, FDXF trades at a ~20–25% PREMIUM to my midpoint intrinsic value.** The peer screen shows FDXF at a 21% discount to sector median P/E (36.5×) — but that comparison is on corrupted trailing earnings and against mega-cap industrials; the proper LTL comps (ODFL ~27–29× forward, ~20× EV/EBITDA) carry double the margins. FDXF's discount to ODFL is deserved.

**No margin of safety exists at $126.** The stock is ~33% below its June high of $188 but has already rallied 15% off the October low — we would be buying after the bounce, not into the discount.

## 4. Management Quality — **Good, but unproven as allocators**

- CEO John A. Smith is a long-tenured FedEx Freight operator with genuine LTL credibility — the operational improvements (claims ratio, yield) are visible and real.
- **The separation capital structure is the blemish:** loading ~$4.3B of debt onto the spin to fund a distribution to the parent transferred leverage risk to new shareholders. Equity is negative as a result — a Buffett-unfriendly starting point.
- No dividend, no buyback history, no standalone capital-allocation record. Credited for early debt repayment ($100M) — a disciplined signal.
- **Insider ownership and incentive structure unverified** (I did not obtain the DEF 14A within budget). Treat as unknown, not negative.
- **Structural overhang:** FedEx retains shares and has stated it will dispose of them within 24 months of separation. That is a multi-year supply pressure and largely explains the post-IPO decline.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate at $126)

This is a competitively sound, cash-generative LTL franchise with a narrow moat, improving yields, and a credible margin-recovery story. But it fails my entry test on three counts: **(1) price is above intrinsic value, not below it** — I require a 20–30% discount; **(2) leverage is ~2.3× normalized EBITDA with negative book equity and no dividend buffer**, in a cyclical industry; **(3) there is no standalone operating track record and revenue growth is price-led, not volume-led.**

**Buy discipline:** I would be interested at **$80 or below** (~25% discount to the $102 midpoint, ~7× normalized EBITDA), which is where the DCF and the bear case stop fighting the price. **Sell/reduce** above ~$145, where even the 15%-margin bull case is fully discounted. Fair value $95–110; entry $80; trim $145.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Leverage in a downturn** — ~$4.3B debt / ~2.3× EBITDA; a 10%-margin recession year leaves ~2.8× leverage with zero dividend flexibility. Refinancing risk is the tail risk.
2. **Freight cycle** — LTL tonnage is flat-to-down; if pricing discipline breaks (as in 2016 and 2019), the 15% margin target evaporates.
3. **Volume erosion** — shipments −1.4% while yield +10.1%. Sustained volume loss would eventually break pricing power.
4. **Standalone cost creep** — TSA (transition service agreement) costs, IT build-out, and public-company overhead are still rolling in; the guided Dec-quarter margin of ~8.4% versus 15.5% in the prior four months shows how much quarter-to-quarter volatility to expect.
5. **FedEx share overhang** — the 24-month disposal program is a persistent technical drag.
6. **Governance/analytics gaps** — staggered board, no written consent, and the anti-takeover provisions reduce the chance of an external value-realization event.
7. **Data-integrity risk in the reported series** (unreconciled debt figures, broken FY26 P&L). My normalized estimates should be re-underwritten against the actual 10-Q and 10-K.

**Bottom line:** a good business at a full price. Buffett's rule applies — a wonderful-enough company at a fair price still isn't a buy when the price sits *above* fair value. **Hold; buy only on a meaningful drawdown toward $80.**
