## NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — FDXF (FedEx Freight Holding Company, Inc.)

**Context:** LTL freight pure-play, spun off from FedEx Corp. effective June 1, 2026 (1 FDXF share per 2 FDX shares; FedEx distributed 80.1%, **retaining ~19.9%**). ~$18.8B market cap at $126.04, ~40,000 employees, 149.5M shares out. As a four-month-old public entity, disclosure history is thin, no DEF 14A has yet been filed, and it self-identifies as an emerging growth company.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

Evidence:

- **Board is not majority-independent of the former parent.** Of the ten directors, roughly **half have FedEx ties**:
  - **R. Brad Martin** — FDXF Chairman, *while simultaneously serving as Executive Chairman of the FedEx board*. A chairman with a live fiduciary duty to the 19.9% seller is a structural conflict on any related-party, TSA-renewal, or M&A matter.
  - **Samantha Smith** — a *current FedEx employee* (Staff Director, Global Public Policy, since Nov 2020) who was explicitly expected to **continue her FedEx employment after the spin-off** while serving on the FDXF board. A sitting employee of the controlling-block shareholder on the subsidiary's board is an unambiguous independence failure.
  - **Robert A. King** — ~40-year FedEx veteran, Corporate VP of **Internal Audit** until Jan 2025. His presence materially compromises the appearance of audit-committee independence — the former head of the parent's internal audit now oversees the spinco's controls. He received an 8,189-share grant on June 1, 2026.
  - **Stephen Gorman** — resigned from the FedEx board to join FDXF (cleaner, but still a FedEx-designated seat).
  - **John A. Smith** — CEO and director (chair/CEO roles are separated, which is a modest mitigant).
- **Entrenched charter.** Staggered three-year board for the first five annual meetings; **66⅔% supermajority** to amend key charter provisions; **no stockholder action by written consent**; directors **not removable without cause** until the fifth AGM; the 20% special-meeting right is **not available until after the fifth AGM** (i.e., ~2031); advance-notice windows and a 3%/3-year proxy-access gate. For a company whose largest holder is a recent parent, this is a notably shareholder-unfriendly package.
- **Corporate-opportunity doctrine waived** in favor of FedEx and of directors/officers with FedEx relationships — directors may take opportunities that arguably belong to FDXF.
- **Capital extraction at separation.** FDXF paid a **~$4.1B cash dividend to FedEx** prior to the effective time. This is the single largest related-party transaction in the company's history and directly drives the leverage that management must now deleverage out of — a classic spin-off value-transfer pattern that warrants scrutiny.
- **No compensation disclosure yet.** Absence of a DEF 14A means no say-on-pay track record, no verified incentive metrics, no beneficial-ownership table. Unassessable ≠ acceptable.
- Positive notes: clean Sarbanes-Oxley §302 certifications (Smith, Oct 29, 2026) with no disclosed material weakness or significant deficiency; a plausible independent contingent (Jeffrey Davis — ex-CFO Dollar Tree/JCPenney; Cindy Miller — 30 yrs UPS, ex-Stericycle CEO; Amy Salcido — Kyndryl spin-off experience; John Sauerland — Progressive CFO; Donald Frieson — ex-Lowe's supply chain).

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **No SEC enforcement actions or DOJ matters identified.** EDGAR full-text search for FDXF returns nothing. *Caveat:* full-text search is weak for a newly registered CIK, so this is "no evidence found," not affirmative clearance.
- **No disclosed material litigation** surfaced in available excerpts; the 10-Q excerpt retrieved was the CEO certification rather than the legal-proceedings note — a genuine evidence gap I could not close within budget.
- **Structural regulatory changes from separation:** FDXF must now stand up its own DOT/FMCSA authority, hazmat permits, EPA/CARB fleet-emissions compliance, and state labor-classification posture independently, and it operates under transitional services agreements with FedEx rather than owned back-office functions.
- **Sector exposure:** LTL is governed by DOT hours-of-service, CDL/qualified-driver rules, emissions standards (notably CARB's truck-age rules affecting California terminals), and NLRB/state contractor-classification regimes. No pending FDXF-specific approvals identified.

### 3. Insider Activity Analysis

- **Net: neutral-to-negative; no open-market insider buying detected.** Approximately 30 Form 4s since the spin, concentrated in two spinoff-administrative clusters (June 3 and July 1, 2026), plus two in August (Aug 5, Aug 11).
- Available transaction detail shows these are **equity grants, not open-market purchases** — e.g., director Robert A. King "acquiring 8,189 shares on June 1, 2026, recorded as a grant."
- **Zero Form 4s appear after August 11, 2026** — meaning insiders did **not** buy into the September–October drawdown to the $103 low. For a newly independent management team talking up a 15% margin / >$1B FCF target, the absence of any conviction buying at a 45% discount to the post-spin high is a soft negative signal.
- No insider selling is indicated either, so I read this as **inert rather than alarming** — most shares are likely still locked or held subject to post-spin retention expectations. **Gap:** per-transaction price/quantity detail was not retrievable; treat direction as inferred.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Key-person and executive-bench fragility, already realized.** In **September 2026 — roughly 90 days after becoming independent — FDXF terminated EVP/Chief Specialized Services & Commercial Officer Michael B. Lyons after an internal investigation found he violated the Code of Conduct.** The commercial and pricing function is the profit engine of an LTL carrier; that seat was then backfilled in a *reconfigured* form by Michael Rodgers (Chief Commercial and Technology Officer) on Sept 14, 2026. Even accepting management's statement that the conduct was unrelated to financial reporting, controls, strategy, or customer relationships, losing the top commercial officer to a *for-cause* termination in the first quarter of independence is a live execution risk across pricing discipline and account retention.
- **Unproven stand-alone cost structure and systems dependency.** Separation costs of **$80M** hit the first standalone period, and the company still relies on FedEx for transitional IT, network, and back-office services. Targets of 15% operating margin and >$1B annual FCF are unvalidated; Q1 quality of earnings was additionally flattered by comparison against a lapped **$33M facility-sale gain**. Cost-duplication and dis-synergy risk in a business that previously bought services at parent scale is material.
- **Cyclical volume plus a non-union labor model under fresh attack.** LTL volumes and yield are industrial-economy sensitive; management's Q1 beat leaned on **higher yields and fuel surcharges**, not structural volume. Meanwhile, separation turns FDXF into a **single, cleanly targetable non-union carrier** — historically the Teamsters' organizing focus — at a moment when unionized freight peers have just reset wages sharply higher (5-year CBA ratified June 30, 2026). A successful organizing drive at even a subset of terminals would structurally raise the cost base against non-union pricing. Add driver scarcity and terminal-network/real-estate concentration as compounding factors.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Moderate, rising**

- **The market verdict is harsh.** FDXF first closed at $149.53, peaked at $188.46 (June 9, 2026), and bottomed at $103.48 (Oct 28, 2026) — a **~45% peak-to-trough decline** and still **~16% below the first-day close** even after a **+14.9% post-earnings snap-back** on Oct 30. Sell-side framing includes "priced for a clean quarter it hasn't had yet" and a premium multiple with "execution and leverage risks unproven." A broken-IPO dynamic creates its own reputational drag on management credibility and on any future equity issuance.
- **The executive termination is a genuine culture signal.** Press coverage (FreightWaves, Trucking Dive, local Memphis media) framed it straightforwardly as a chief commercial officer fired for a Code of Conduct violation. The disclosure itself was appropriate — Item 8.01, voluntary, on the same day — which is credit to the board. But it is the kind of item that invites follow-on reporting.
- **Offsetting:** no product recall, no data-breach disclosure, no ESG controversy, no consumer-facing brand exposure, and institutional sponsorship is broad and rising (State Street opened a 4.79M-share position worth ~$724M; multiple new 13F holders). Brand risk here is investor-facing rather than customer-facing.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

No fraud indicators, no regulatory enforcement, and clean control certifications keep this out of "Critical." But it earns **High** on three independent counts that compound: (a) a board where half the seats are FedEx-affiliated, the chairman is also the parent's executive chairman, and a sitting parent employee sits as a director; (b) a **for-cause termination of the top commercial officer 90 days into independence**; and (c) an unproven, leverage-laden standalone cost structure against an unvalidated margin target, with a charter engineered to delay shareholder recourse until ~2031.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Samantha Smith** — current FedEx employee serving as an FDXF director. Demand the board's independence determination and her committee assignments; if she sits on Audit or Compensation, escalate to a formal governance objection.
2. **Robert King** — former FedEx Corporate VP of Internal Audit on the FDXF board; confirm he is **not** on the Audit Committee. If he is, the audit committee is not independent of the control environment it oversees.
3. **R. Brad Martin's dual chairmanship** (FDXF Chairman + FedEx Executive Chairman) — a standing conflict on TSAs, the ~19.9% residual stake, and any future commercial agreement. Demand disclosure of how conflicts are recused.
4. **The $4.1B pre-spin cash dividend to FedEx** — the root of the leverage profile. Requires a full articulation of debt covenants, funded-debt terms, and any FedEx guarantees or cross-defaults.
5. **Michael Lyons termination** — the Code of Conduct basis is undisclosed. Obtain the full 8-K and any subsequent disclosure; verify independently that no customer, pricing, or channel-partner conduct is implicated, and monitor for follow-on litigation or regulatory inquiry.
6. **No DEF 14A / no compensation disclosure** — cannot verify incentive alignment. Escalate as an information gap blocking a complete governance opinion.
