# FDXF — FedEx Freight Holding Co. | Alternative Data Signal Report
*As of ~Oct 31, 2026 (post-Q1 FY27 print, Oct 29 AMC)*

**Context first:** FDXF is a **June 2026 spin-off** from FedEx (IPO/spin 2026-06-01). That matters enormously for alt data: history is ~5 months, "prior-quarter trend" baselines are contaminated by pre-separation carve-out accounting, and most third-party datasets (SimilarWeb panels, hiring trackers, app data) did not track the entity pre-June. Every signal below carries that caveat.

→ **Direction: sustained step-up (+~105% vs pre-spin baseline), flat-to-firming over the last 4 weeks.** Critically, search interest did *not* decay back to the 36-ish baseline 5 months post-spin, and the Oct print month is running 15–25 pts above September. That's consistent with the revenue beat and management's "improving volumes" language rather than a one-off listing-day curiosity.

**Google Trends — "LTL freight" (sector proxy, 12-m):**
Peak 82–89 (mid-Aug) → **51–59 in October** (last 4 weeks: 53, 59, 58, 57). → **Direction: down ~30% from summer peak.** This is a sector-level caution flag: shipper search interest in the LTL category is cooling into year-end, meaning FDXF's relative outperformance is company-specific (yield/surcharge-driven) rather than market-tailwind-driven.

**Web traffic:** *Data gap.* SimilarWeb proxy returned no usable fedexfreight.com time series (returned unrelated domains). Cannot validate shipper portal traffic. This is the single biggest hole in this report — for an LTL carrier, my.fedexfreight.com login/shipment-creation traffic is the cleanest demand proxy and I could not obtain it.

## 2. Business Activity Signals

**Hiring (Indeed/LinkedIn proxy):** Postings are dominated by **hourly operational roles** — Driver Apprentice-City ($27–34/hr), Freight Handler ($21–26/hr), Freight Inspector — across Memphis, Dallas, Irving, Charlotte, Indianapolis, Effingham, Gypsum. → **Direction: steady, dock/driver-skills hiring only; no visible acceleration in sales, pricing, or engineering roles.** This is a "capacity maintenance, not expansion" hiring profile. Quantitatively weak (search proxy, not a posting count series), so treat as directional only. No evidence of a pre-holiday hiring surge, which would have been the bullish tell.

**Patents:** Not gathered — immaterial for an LTL network operator (asset/operational business, not IP-driven). Deliberately skipped rather than reported as a null.

**Macro freight alt-data (the real signal for this name):**
- **Cass shipments: +1.9% y/y in September**, second consecutive positive month **after 42 straight months of decline** — the first genuine inflection in 3.5 years.
- **Cass TL linehaul: +10.1% y/y**, highest absolute reading in 4 years; contract rates still adjusting higher even as spot softens.
- Cass expenditures +20.4% y/y — pricing power, not just volume.
- **Q3 2026 LTL peer tonnage was mixed:** XPO shipments +5.7%, Saia +1.1%, **Old Dominion −2.4%** (and lighter weight per shipment across the group).

→ **Direction: inflecting positive, but unevenly.** Rate is the tailwind; volume is only nascent. This supports FDXF's reported "higher yields and fuel surcharges" beat — i.e., the beat is **price-led, not tonnage-led**, which is a lower-quality (and more cyclical-fragile) beat.

- Revenue **$2.44B vs $2.38B est** → beat
- Adjusted operating income $510M on $3.3B transition-period revenue

**Surprise history:** beat rate **66.7% (2 of 3)**; average surprise **−20.7%**, but that average is destroyed by a single pre-spin artifact (**Apr 10, 2026: $0.374 actual vs $1.42 est, −73.7%** — a carve-out accounting distortion, not an operational miss). Ex that print, the last two quarters are +1.3% and +10.4%.

**Next print:** calendar shows a stale 2026-12-02 entry with EPS est $1.35 / rev est $2.379B — *identical to the October consensus*, i.e. an unrefreshed placeholder. I would not treat that date or those numbers as live.

→ **Verdict: Tracking ABOVE consensus** on the reported quarter and on forward guidance, corroborated independently by Cass rate data (+10.1% y/y) and by the price-led beat. But the alt-data corroboration is **pricing-driven**, and the sector search trend is softening — so "above" for the holiday quarter, with rising risk of a volume air-pocket in Q1.

## 7. Confidence Level: **MEDIUM-LOW**

**Why not higher:**
1. **Five-month operating history.** No prior-year seasonal baseline; "improving volumes" cannot be validated against an FDXF-specific comp.
2. **Web traffic feed failed** — the best shipper-demand proxy for this business model is missing.
3. **Hiring data is a search proxy**, not a posting count; it shows no acceleration, which weakens the growth thesis.
4. **The beat is yield-led, not tonnage-led.** Cass shipments are +1.9% and peer tonnage is mixed-to-negative; sell-side will eventually test the durability of price over volume.
5. **Sector search interest is decelerating** into the holiday quarter — the one alt series pointing the wrong way.
6. Next earnings date/estimates in the calendar are stale placeholders.

**What would upgrade this to High:** a clean SimilarWeb traffic series trending up m/m, a Cass shipment print >+3% for October, or FedEx Freight job postings broadening beyond hourly dock/driver roles. **What would flip the call:** an October Cass shipment reversal, or a Q1 guide-down narrative as yield comps normalize.

**Data limitations:** All trend data lags 1–4 weeks (Google Trends 3–5 days, Cass ~2 weeks after month-end, SimilarWeb 3–4 weeks). Social and hiring are keyword-search proxies with no quantitative floor. Nothing here is a substitute for the next Cass print or the December volume update.
