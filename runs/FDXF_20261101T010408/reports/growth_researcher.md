## FDXF — FedEx Freight Holding Co. | Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $126.04 · Mkt cap $18.84B · EV $24.96B · Spin-off from FedEx Corp., June 1, 2026*

---

### 1. Growth Story

FedEx Freight is North America's largest LTL (less-than-truckload) carrier — ~$8.8B revenue, 40,000 employees — newly liberated from FedEx Corp. and pursuing a **self-help margin re-rating** rather than TAM expansion: management targets adjusted operating margin of **~15% versus ~12% today**, on a 4–6% revenue growth algorithm, driven by yield discipline (5.9% GRI), network densification, and an 85% operating ratio. The growth "innovation" here is not a proprietary platform — it is pricing power in a structurally tightened LTL oligopoly (post-Yellow bankruptcy) plus the freedom to optimize a network that was previously subordinated to FedEx's parcel agenda. **This is a margin-compounding spin-off, not a disruptive-innovation compounder — it fails my Cathie Wood screen but may pass my EPS-CAGR-at-a-discount screen.**

**3-yr revenue CAGR: -4.46%.** Every headline FY26 figure is distorted: cost of revenue booked at $0 (100% "gross margin"), $1.41B working-capital drain from separation, $9M/month transitional-services payments to FedEx, and spin-related costs compressing operating income. **The clean data point is the post-spin stub: four months to Sept. 30 revenue $3.3B, +9.2% YoY — an inflection from five consecutive down years — with yield up and adjusted EPS $2.15 (-24% on transition costs).** Forward consensus: EPS $4.67 → $5.40 → $6.58 → $7.64 (FY30), an ~18% CAGR; revenue to $10.4B by FY30 (+18% over four years).

### 3. TAM Assessment

US LTL is a **~$55–60B revenue pool growing ~3% in tonnage** — GDP-plus, pricing-led. FDXF holds roughly **15% share** as the #1 player; there is no penetration S-curve to ride. The realistic upside vectors are (a) yield capture above inflation, (b) operating-ratio convergence toward ODFL-like 70s from ~88% today, and (c) cross-border Mexico/Canada freight as nearshoring shifts manufacturing. Addressable margin pool, not revenue pool, is the prize: each 100bp of operating margin on $9B of revenue is ~$90M of pre-tax profit, ~20% of current net income.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Dec. 2, 2026 — Q2 FY27 earnings:** first full three-month standalone quarter; watch yield vs. tonnage split and TSA cost burn-off. Guidance for the following quarter (~$2.3B revenue, $0.65–0.75 adj EPS) sits well below the $1.35 consensus — a likely downward estimate reset already in the price.
- **Jan. 2027 — 5.9% GRI takes effect:** LTL peers are pulling GRIs forward; a full-year benefit lands in FY28.
- **FY27 margin print vs. the 15% medium-term target:** each quarter of operating-ratio improvement is the single biggest re-rating lever.
- **Freight cycle inflection:** Q2 2026 LTL tonnage +3% YoY, tender rejections ~17%, diesel $5.64/gal — capacity is tightening.
- **Potential index inclusion / coverage broadening and first capital-return policy** (currently no dividend, no buyback).

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (speculative, self-help re-rating — sized as a value/cyclical position, not a growth-compounder position)

The case is not TAM capture; it is **EPS compounding at ~18% CAGR purchased at a discount**. At $126 FDXF trades at **27x FY27E, 23x FY28E and 16.5x FY30E**, versus ODFL 35.7x P/E / 21.0x EV/EBITDA, SAIA 36.2x / 16.0x, and XPO 71.8x / 22.9x. On normalized FY25 EBITDA the EV/EBITDA is **13.4x** — a 30–40% discount to the LTL peer set that is only earned until management proves it can run standalone. The stock has already de-rated 42% from its $188 June high to $108, then jumped **+15% on Oct. 30** on the first clean report and the 5.9% GRI — a classic washed-out bottom with fundamentals turning. My implied FY30 value at 22–25x on $7.64 EPS is **$168–191 (~35–50% upside over ~3.5 years)**. I would not pay up for this as a growth name; I would own it for margin convergence.

### 7. Key Risks

- **Leverage and balance sheet:** net debt ~$6.1B, **negative book value per share (-$3.32)**, no dividend/buyback capacity — the spin was levered.
- **Standalone execution:** $9M/month TSA payments to FedEx, a multi-year IT/TMS buildout, and a first-ever standalone quarter — the April 74% EPS miss shows how fragile the transition is.
- **Guidance vs. consensus gap (~$0.65-0.75 vs. $1.35):** a consensus reset could produce another leg down before recovery.
- **Cyclicality:** LTL is a GDP-levered, industrial-end-market business; a freight recession would break the 4–6% growth and 15% margin targets simultaneously.
- **Competitive:** ODFL and SAIA have structurally superior operating ratios and decades of standalone cost culture; XPO is aggressive on yield.
- **Margin-target credibility:** management's framework was built pre-spin; no independent track record supports it yet.

**Data gaps:** LTL-specific forward consensus is thin (3–5 analysts on out-years), FY26 GAAP statements are spin-distorted (gross margin, FCF, operating income), and no LTL-pure-play forward multiple table was available — peer figures above are trailing.
