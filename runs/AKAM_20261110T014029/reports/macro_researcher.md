### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Reflation with a term-premium problem (risk-on in credit, hostile in duration)**

The US economy is running hot in nominal terms rather than cooling. Real GDP re-accelerated to **+3.4% saar in Q3'26** (from +2.2% in Q2 and +2.5% in Q1), unemployment has *fallen* to **4.1%** (Oct'26) from 4.5% a year ago, and headline CPI has re-accelerated from **2.4% YoY in Feb'26 to 3.7% YoY in Sep'26** (+0.65% m/m in September alone, ~8% annualised). This is not a soft-landing tape — it is a re-acceleration.

The Fed cut from 4.33% through H1'25 to **3.63% by mid-2026, then stopped and partially reversed to 3.88% (Oct'26)**. The Treasury curve has bear-steepened: **2Y 4.74%** (86bp *above* effective fed funds — the market is pricing further tightening), **10Y 5.13%**, **30Y 5.47%**, 2s10s **+39bp**. The long end is being driven by term premium/fiscal supply, not growth optimism. Yet credit is wide open: **IG OAS at 93bp** (near cycle tights, and *tighter* than the 0.73–0.75% June levels would suggest at first glance — spreads have actually widened modestly from 74bp in June to 93bp in November, still historically tight). The **broad USD index at 121.88** has rallied **+3.2% since 21 August 2026**.

Net: an environment that rewards nominal earnings growth, punishes long-duration cash flows, and squeezes dollar-translated overseas revenue.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Negative — US dollar (+3.2% since August).** AKAM is **~50% international**: Q3'26 international revenue was **$563M of $1.122bn**, up 7% reported but **9% in constant currency** — a ~2pt FX drag on the international book, ~1pt at group level (group +6.4% reported vs +7% cc). A persistently strong dollar is a ~100–150bp revenue headwind into FY27, partially self-hedged because a large share of operating costs (engineering, data-centre leases) is also non-USD. This is the cleanest, most direct macro transmission channel to AKAM.

**Negative — long-end rates / discount rate.** AKAM is not a levered rate-sensitive business in the REIT/bank sense, but two channels matter. (i) *Valuation*: at $80.29 with FY26 non-GAAP EPS guided to **$6.57** (lowered 2.2% on 5 Nov), the stock is ~12.2x / an **8.2% earnings yield versus a 5.13% 10Y — only ~310bp of equity risk premium for a 5–6% compounder**. With 30Y at 5.47%, the multiple is capped; this is why the stock could not hold its May'26 re-rating. (ii) *Duration of the FCF story*: Q3'26 free cash flow was **–$172M** (from +$100M in Q2) on the edge/AI capex build, with operating margin down to **9.7% from 15.7%**. Value depends on a 2027+ FCF inflection, which a 5%+ long bond discounts harder.

**Positive/neutral — credit conditions.** IG OAS of 93bp and AKAM's own access to capital are supportive: in **May 2026 AKAM issued $2.2bn of 0.00% convertible senior notes due 2030 and 2032** (plus $1.15bn of legacy converts due 2027). Essentially zero cash interest cost and no meaningful refinancing wall until 2030 — a genuine structural advantage in a 5%+ rate world, and a reason AKAM's rate sensitivity is far below that of a typical capex-heavy infrastructure name. Tight spreads also mean enterprise customers are not budget-constrained by financing costs.

**Neutral-to-negative — nominal growth vs. cost inflation.** Firm demand (3.4% real GDP, 4.1% unemployment) supports media/streaming, gaming and retail traffic volumes — and **Delivery revenue, while still contracting, improved to –3% YoY in Q3 from –6% in Q2**. But AKAM is a large consumer of electricity, data-centre capacity and AI-era hardware; with CPI at 3.7% and AI demand inflating server/memory and power costs, input costs are rising faster than its deflationary delivery pricing. The 600bp operating-margin compression is partly depreciation from capex, but power and component inflation are a real, under-modelled share of it.

**Neutral — tariffs/trade.** Minimal direct exposure: software and services are not tariffed. Indirect exposure via imported network/server hardware and via enterprise customers' capex caution. Low.

**Positive — the AI capex/inference cycle (macro-adjacent, dominant driver).** Management disclosed **>$14bn of multi-year Cloud Infrastructure Services commitments signed in 2026 YTD** and launched Akamai Inference and Agent Sandbox. AKAM is a leveraged play on *enterprise inference* rather than frontier-model training — a less crowded, more durable slice of the AI capex boom. This is the variable that actually drives the stock, and it is only loosely tied to Fed policy.

**Important framing:** AKAM's beta is **0.66**, and yet the stock went $74 (Nov'25) → $165 (13 May'26) → $80 (9 Nov'26), **–51% from the high**. That round trip was driven by company-specific news (AI narrative in, weak Q4 guide of **$1.16bn / +5.7% YoY, 3.5% below consensus** out), not by the macro cycle. Macro explains the *multiple ceiling*; idiosyncratic execution explains the *drawdown*.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL** (mild unfavourable tilt)

Scorecard: FX **negative**, long-end rates **negative** (valuation, not solvency), input-cost inflation **negative**, demand/credit **positive**, direct refinancing risk **positive (0% converts)**. The offsetting factors (half the revenue base is non-discretionary security/CIS with $14bn of locked-in commitments; zero-coupon debt; tight credit) keep this from being Unfavourable. Note clearly for the committee: **AKAM's risk here is ~80% company-specific (delivery erosion, negative FCF, guidance credibility) and ~20% macro (USD, term premium).** Do not let a Neutral macro call mask the execution problem.

### 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **Bull: the September CPI spike proves transitory**

The single most asymmetric macro surprise would be a fast disinflation. The Sept'26 0.65% m/m print is doing enormous work: it is what stalled the Fed at 3.63%, pushed the 2Y to 4.74% (pricing hikes), and held the 10Y at 5.13%. If Oct/Nov CPI revert to ~0.2% m/m — entirely plausible given the September bump looks like energy/tariff pass-through rather than demand-pull, with unemployment already at 4.1% — the market repricing is violent: the 2Y falls toward 3.75%, the 10Y toward 4.50%, and the broad dollar gives back its 3% August–November rally.

For AKAM that is a **triple hit in reverse**: (i) ~1–2pt of FX tailwind on the 50% international book; (ii) at $6.57 EPS, an 8.2% earnings yield against a 4.50% 10Y restores a ~370bp ERP, enough to support a move from 12.2x to 15x — **+23% on multiple alone** — and the $14bn CIS backlog gets re-underwritten at a lower discount rate just as the FCF inflection lands; (iii) cheaper capital for the edge build. A $100–110 stock on an unchanged fundamental story is the plausible bull case.

The mirror-image bear — 10Y through 5.75% on term premium, USD to 128, enterprise IT budgets trimmed — would force AKAM to fund negative free cash flow with equity or converts at a depressed price, extending the drawdown. Given the stock already sits at 12x with a $14bn contracted backlog, I judge the disinflation upside the more under-priced of the two.
