### 1. Geographic Risk Map

Akamai discloses revenue by U.S. vs. international only, and states that "no single country outside of the U.S. accounted for 10% or more of revenue" in FY2023–FY2025 (FY2025 10-K, filed Feb 2026). Country-level splits below are therefore company-reported where available and **estimates** where not — flagged accordingly.

| Geography | Revenue exposure | Basis | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | **~50%** (Q3'26: $559M of $1,122M; FY'25: $2.139B) | Reported | Low |
| **Europe (EU + UK)** | **~20–25% (est.)** — largest international bloc; no single country >10% | Estimated from intl. total (~$563M/qtr, 50.2% of Q3'26 revenue) | **Medium** (sovereignty regime, Data Act, GDPR) |
| **Japan / Asia-Pacific (incl. India engineering)** | **~15–20% (est.)** | No country ≥10%; Japan historically largest APAC market | Low–Medium (FX, India DPDP, China absent) |
| **Latin America** | **~5% (est.)** | Estimated | Medium (Brazil/Argentina FX, political volatility) |
| **Israel** | Immaterial revenue; **material R&D footprint** (Guardicore 2021, Noname Security ~$450M 2024) | Company/IVC data | Medium (conflict, reserve-duty staffing) |
| **Russia / Belarus** | **~0%** — business terminated, state-majority customers exited, sanctions compliance program | Akamai public statement | Low (legacy/residual) |
| **China** | **Negligible** — no domestic CDN licence; served via partners | Company disclosures | Low–Medium (US-China tech decoupling optionality foregone) |

**Key point:** Akamai is one of the more geographically balanced large-cap software/infra names — roughly a 50/50 U.S./international split with deliberate country-level diversification (no non-U.S. country at 10%). The *geographic* risk is moderate; the *jurisdictional-regulatory* and *counterparty* risks are where the real exposure sits.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**(1) EU digital-sovereignty regime squeezing non-EU cloud providers — Probability: High / Impact: Medium**
The European Commission adopted a proposal for an EU cloud sovereignty framework (the **Cloud and AI Development Act, CADA**) on 3 June 2026 as part of a broader "European Technological Sovereignty Package." It is procurement-driven and risk-based, meaning global hyperscalers can keep participating but face enhanced sovereignty criteria (EU-controlled operations, data residency, non-EU-government-override protections) for public-sector and regulated workloads. Akamai's edge/cloud infrastructure is physically embedded in EU member states, and it has responded commercially (EU Data Act addendum effective 12 Sept 2025; a published "Europe's digital sovereignty" commitment). Risk: margin compression and possible exclusion from EU public-sector tenders as CADA transposes (~2027–2029), plus associated compliance build-out costs across ~27 jurisdictions. Sources: European Commission digital-strategy page; HLC/CIDA analyses; Akamai EU Data Act legal page.

**(2) Single-counterparty AI-cloud concentration, funded with debt — Probability: Medium / Impact: High**
On 24 Sept 2026 Akamai announced an **$11.6B seven-year agreement with Anthropic** (up to $20B cited in press coverage), requiring **~$5.5B of capex**, and issued Anthropic a **warrant exercisable at $111.33/share for ~5% of common stock** (Reuters, 24 Sept 2026). S&P immediately placed Akamai's ICR on **CreditWatch Negative** and subsequently **downgraded the company to 'BB+'** (S&P Global Ratings), citing materially higher financial risk. Akamai followed with a **$2.2B convertible senior notes offering** (Nov 2026: $1.1B 2028s + $1.1B 2031s). Geopolitical/legal transmission channels: (i) U.S. AI-diffusion/export-control rules on advanced accelerators and cross-border compute could constrain where the contracted capacity can be sited; (ii) customer-concentration and take-or-pay-style commitments create litigation/covenant risk if Anthropic's demand or funding environment shifts; (iii) any U.S. government scrutiny of frontier-AI infrastructure deals (antitrust, national-security conditions) attaches reputational risk to the counterparty relationship. Sources: Akamai press release; Reuters; S&P Global Ratings (3642383, 3656742); GlobeNewswire 11/09/2026.

**(3) Trade/tariff and sanctions friction against a ~50% international revenue base — Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium**
The 10-K/10-Q risk factors explicitly flag "export or import controls, tariffs and other trade restrictions" and FX as risks to international operations. Concrete exposures: (a) **FX translation** — management attributes ~1pt of Q3'26 growth to FX, and growth is disproportionately international (+7% intl vs +5% U.S.); (b) **EU sanctions escalation** (21st Russia package, July 2026) raises dual-use export-classification risk for a company selling security/network technology into dozens of jurisdictions; (c) **Middle East conflict** affecting the Israeli R&D base (Guardicore/Noname) through reserve duty and continuity risk; (d) **content-blocking/ takedown orders** in Turkey, India, Russia-adjacent markets — Akamai's MSA expressly reserves rights to suspend service to comply with "judicial or other similar governmental demand," which is both a legal shield and a revenue/reputational exposure. Peer precedent for sanctions risk in this exact sub-sector: the 2026 OFAC settlement and DOJ non-prosecution agreement with Extreme Networks (Russia sanctions violations) — a directly analogous enforcement template for any U.S. network-infrastructure vendor with legacy emerging-market customers.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Litigation: ordinary course only.** The FY2025 10-K and 2026 10-Qs state Akamai is "subject to legal proceedings, claims and litigation arising in the ordinary course of business" and does not expect ultimate costs to be material. **No pending material proceedings, DOJ/FTC/EC investigation, or securities class action is disclosed.**
- **Recent IP dockets (all non-material):**
  - *SunStone Information Defense v. Akamai* (2:26-cv-00353, E.D. Tex.) — patent complaint filed 1 May 2026, **closed 12 Aug 2026** (i.e., dismissed/resolved within ~3 months; no disclosed payment).
  - *Akamai v. MediaPointe* — Federal Circuit **affirmed Akamai's win** invalidating streaming-patent claims (25 Nov 2025).
  - *Akamai v. Limelight* — long-running foundational patent saga (Supreme Court, Fed. Cir.), now resolved; Akamai's CDN patent estate has proven defensible.
- **Regulatory posture:** GDPR/EU Data Act (addendum live Sept 2025), EU–U.S. Data Privacy Framework certification, NIS2 supply-chain obligations, EU AI Act obligations phasing in from Aug 2026 (relevant to new Inference/Agent Sandbox products), and U.S. state privacy laws. No disclosed enforcement action.
- **Historical compliance marker:** SEC **Non-Prosecution Agreement (2016)** with Akamai relating to FCPA books-and-records/internal-controls failures, resolved after self-reporting and remediation (SEC Press Release 2016-109). This is the company's only public corruption-related enforcement event, and it is 10 years stale — a mitigating, not aggravating, factor.
- **Securities/financing:** $2.2B convertible notes (Nov 2026) plus BB+ rating bring covenant, dilution (Anthropic warrant ~5%) and disclosure-intensity risk — a financial-legal rather than geopolitical item, but it materially raises the cost of any adverse legal outcome.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

| Flag | Severity | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **Sanctions/customer screening on cloud & hosting** (Linode/Akamai Cloud) | **Medium** | A global hosting/edge platform must screen self-serve customers against OFAC/EU lists and police abuse (malware, phishing, sanctions-evading hosting). Akamai publishes AUP and abuse-reporting channels; the *Extreme Networks* OFAC/DOJ resolution shows active enforcement against network vendors. |
| **FCPA/UK Bribery Act third-party & channel risk** | Low–Medium | 2016 NPA history; extensive channel-partner model in emerging markets. Akamai maintains a published Anti-Bribery & Anti-Corruption Policy covering FCPA/UKBA. |
| **Data-localization & sovereignty commitments** | Medium | EU Data Act addendum, DPF reliance, and customer-specific residency commitments (e.g., the Anthropic MSA commits Akamai to manage "the physical location of the infrastructure"). Mis-siting data in an AI build-out is a realistic breach vector. |
| **Intermediary liability / content takedown** | Low–Medium | DMCA §512 / EU DSA hosting-provider posture; exposure rises with the larger share of first-party cloud (vs. pure CDN) workloads. |
| **Customer concentration (Anthropic)** | **Medium-High** | Not a compliance flag per se, but a contractual/legal concentration risk embedded in the largest contract in company history, with an equity warrant attached. |

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale.** Akamai scores well on the classic geopolitical metrics: ~50/50 U.S./international revenue with **no non-U.S. country at or above 10% of revenue**, no Russia/Belarus business, effectively no China exposure, a clean current litigation posture, and a defensible patent estate. That caps the rating below "High."

What keeps it at a solid **Medium** (rather than Low) is the *direction of travel*: (i) the EU's CADA/sovereignty framework explicitly targets non-EU cloud providers in exactly the public-sector and regulated segments Akamai is now pushing into with its cloud-infrastructure pivot; (ii) the transformation of the growth story into an $11.6B, seven-year, single-counterparty AI infrastructure contract funded with $5.5B of capex and a 5% warrant — which has already cost the company investment-grade status (BB+) and creates real covenant, siting, and export-control dependencies on U.S. AI policy; and (iii) half of revenue now sits outside the U.S. into a tariff/sanctions environment that is tightening, not loosening.

**Watch items for the next 12 months:** CADA trilogue text and public-procurement carve-outs; any U.S. BIS action on cross-border AI compute siting; Anthropic deal financing/covenant terms in the 10-K; EU AI Act high-risk obligations (Aug 2026); and any sanctions enforcement touching cloud/hosting self-serve customers.
