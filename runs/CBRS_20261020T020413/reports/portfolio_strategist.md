## 2. Consensus Assessment: **HIGH** (on the only question that matters)

There is no dissenting bull. Eleven agents, zero buy recommendations. The split is not *whether* to own CBRS — it is **how** to be negative:

- **Unanimous (11/11):** no margin of safety at $120.66. Even Growth's fair-value math ("fairly valued *if* the FY28 consensus ramp is delivered") and Value's *bull* case ($107) sit at or below spot. That is the single most important output in the packet.
- **Majority (7/11):** risk rating HIGH or worse (Geo, Fin, Nonfin, Value, ESG, Quant, Short).
- **Genuine disagreement:** Value says the price is ~2x intrinsic value; Short says the structural short "was right and is now late" and concedes the bull case strengthened post-drawdown; Growth wants accumulation below $100. I resolve this in favour of **not owning and not shorting large** — the fundamental downside is real but a 114%-vol, 22%-net-cash, heavily-shorted name that has already fallen 61% is not a short-seller's entry.
- **Cross-agent corroboration on earnings quality:** Fin Risk, Short, and Quant independently reached the same conclusion — FY2025's +$237.8M "profit" rests on ~$363–384M of non-operating income against a −$145.9M operating loss, and the accrual gap (NI vs OCF ≈ $248M) fails. Three desks converging on an accounting artifact is the highest-quality signal in this packet and I weight it heavily.
- **Unresolved conflict:** lock-up mechanics. Nonfin says 19.4M shares unlocked 14-Oct with tranches to come; Growth says ~290M shares mid-Nov; Fin Risk says 226–237M early Nov. All three agree on *direction* (material incremental supply within weeks) — treat the magnitude as unverified, the risk as real.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **0.00% now**; conditional path to max 2.0%

**Kelly test result: f\* ≤ 0.** Using Value's scenario set (bear $44 / base $59 / bull $107, weighted 20/50/30): expected return **≈ −47%**. A wager with negative expectancy takes zero capital regardless of how attractive the option-like payoff feels. This is the rare case where the sizing decision is arithmetic rather than judgment.

**Risk-budget arithmetic (the real constraint).** For a portfolio with 15% vol and ~0.40 correlation to CBRS:
| Weight | Portfolio vol | Share of total risk budget consumed |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0% | 15.34% (+34bp) | **~6.3%** |
| 2.0% | 15.75% (+75bp) | **~12.9%** |

One percent of capital buys 6% of the risk budget and, on the current evidence, negative expected return. That trade fails on both axes.

**Conditional sizing ladder** (requires the Section 6 triggers, not price alone):
- **Starter 0.75%** — only after Q3 (12-Nov) *and* the November lock-up tranche clears, with sequential backlog growth confirmed.
- **Build to 1.5%** — requires two consecutive quarters of GAAP gross margin >25% *and* a named customer base that is no longer ~90% two UAE entities + OpenAI.
- **Hard cap 2.0% (~13% of risk budget).** Above this, CBRS becomes a portfolio-level bet on a single export licence and a single customer relationship.
- **Zero for value, income, ESG-mandated, or low-vol mandates.** Non-negotiable — governance 3/10 and index ineligibility disqualify it on mandate grounds, not on merit.

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector/factor — this is an accretive-to-nothing position right now.** CBRS is bottom-decile on value, momentum, quality, *and* low-vol simultaneously. Any book with a quality, momentum, or value tilt is actively de-diversified by adding it. For AI-compute sleeves already holding NVDA/AVGO/AMD, CBRS adds a name with 0.34–0.43 correlation — so it provides *some* idiosyncratic diversification — while contributing **3.25 beta** and **114% vol**. Net: the correlation benefit does not remotely pay for the variance.

**Beta budget.** A 1% CBRS position contributes as much market beta as ~3.25% of an S&P-tracking allocation, from a company with $0.51B of revenue against a $765B capex pool. That is an inefficient use of beta.

**Factor-timing risk.** Every agent flags late-expansion cycle placement: revenue growth, margins, and market caps are at record highs across the complex *simultaneously*, credit spreads are widening (+66bp in six weeks), and the 10Y is at 5.09%. Adding a sub-scale, negative-margin, single-customer challenger at this point in the cycle is adding the most rate- and sentiment-sensitive asset in the sector at the moment of maximum macro hostility.

**Index/passive drag.** Dual-class (Class B 20 votes; ~95% of votes on ~47% of shares) likely bars S&P 500 inclusion, which caps structural passive demand and raises cost of equity. For any benchmark-aware mandate, this is a persistent structural underweight signal.

**Concentration verdict:** the portfolio-level risk here is not "semis overweight." It is that CBRS embeds **three uncorrelated tail risks in one line item** — a BIS licensing decision, a single customer's workload routing, and a TSMC single-source failure. Correlated tail risks should be sized *smaller* than standalone risk models suggest, not larger.

## 5. Entry Strategy: **Do not enter. If/when entering, three tranches — never at once.**

The tape disqualifies any "buy the dip" logic: **nine consecutive down sessions** from $208.49 (21-Sep) to $120.66 (−42% in 20 sessions), the last on 11.5M shares with a new intraday low. A stock making new lows on expanding volume with a dated supply event 3–4 weeks out is not a candidate for passive accumulation.

**Sequenced entry protocol:**
1. **Tranche 1 (0.75%) — after 12-Nov earnings.** Required: Q3 revenue ≥$215M; **backlog sequentially above $25.4B** (flat QoQ last quarter — the single most diagnostic number in the file); Q4 guidance ≥$290M, corroborating the +38% q/q ramp the FY guide requires.
2. **Tranche 2 (0.75%) — after the November lock-up tranche clears** and the stock holds a higher low on *declining* volume.
3. **Tranche 3 (to 2.0%) — on fundamental, not price, evidence:** GAAP GM >25% for two quarters, no customer >40%, and the first 10-K clean on internal controls (management has already admitted material weaknesses and ineffective disclosure controls at 30-Jun-26).

**Counter-case I will not dismiss:** the macro desk's asymmetry argument is real — a 50bp real-yield decline plus a cleared float could produce a +40–60% squeeze in 4–6 weeks. That argues against *shorting*, not for *owning*. We forgo the squeeze; we do not pay 6% of risk budget for a lottery ticket with negative expected value.

## 6. Risk Management Rules

**Stop-loss (fundamental, not price).** A price stop is meaningless at 114% vol (daily σ ≈ 7.2%, ~$8.70). If a position exists: hard stop at **−30% from cost** as an absolute backstop, but the primary exit is thesis-based.

**Thesis invalidation — exit fully on any of these:**
1. **Backlog does not grow sequentially** at Q3, or Q4 revenue lands below ~$290M / FY26 guide is cut.
2. **Any BIS action, licence denial, or Supplement No. 8 exclusion** touching MBZUAI, G42, or an OpenAI deployment → revenue step-down of 40%+; exit same day, no averaging.
3. **A second OpenAI workload routed to NVDA/AMD.** The first ("Ultrafast") already converted concentration risk from footnote to demonstrated competitive event.
4. **GAAP gross margin below 20% for two consecutive quarters** — confirms the pivot to owned cloud is structurally dilutive, not transitional.
5. **The FY2025 ~$367M non-operating gain proves to be recurring/remeasurement rather than one-time** — this would confirm the Short desk's earnings-quality thesis and invalidate the entire profitability narrative.
6. **Continued insider selling with zero open-market purchases** through the next lock-up wave.
7. **First-filed securities class action** naming the IPO/customer-concentration fact pattern (four firms investigating; a filing is a realistic 12-month outcome).

**Trim trigger.** (a) On any position: trim 50% at **$150–160** (below Value's *bull* case of $107 plus a modest momentum premium — do not hold through a full round-trip); trim to zero at **$178+** (the "perfection case" level, where no scenario supports the price). (b) **For existing holders: reduce now.** The Value, Fin Risk, and Quant desks all independently conclude the 61% drawdown has made this *less absurd, not cheap* — trimming into any bounce above $140 is the disciplined action.

**Monitoring dashboard:** 10Y TIPS yield (the primary driver — a move back below 4.7% nominal is the one macro trigger that would upgrade the whole complex); HY OAS (4.5% = risk-off confirmed); BIS Federal Register actions; monthly Form 4 cadence; backlog progression.

## 7. Final Portfolio Action: **PASS**

**Do not initiate. Establish no position at $120.66.**

The reasoning is not that Cerebras is a bad company — it is a genuinely differentiated architecture with a $25.4B backlog and a fortress balance sheet built from IPO proceeds. It is that **eleven independent desks converge on zero margin of safety**, even the most constructive fair-value work (Growth; Value's bull case) lands at or below spot, and the position would consume ~6% of portfolio risk budget per 1% of capital for negative expected return. The three tail risks — BIS licensing, OpenAI routing, TSMC single-source — are unhedgeable and individually capable of a 40%+ step-down.

**Existing holders:** reduce to ≤1.0%, or exit entirely if CBRS plus existing AI-compute exposure exceeds the sleeve's risk budget. Do not add into weakness. **Short sellers:** the structural short has largely paid; residual catalyst shorts around the 12-Nov print are defensible at small size but carry genuine squeeze risk against 22% net cash and a 3.25 beta — not a portfolio-level position.

**Re-engage when:** price <$85–90 (Value's bull case less a margin of safety) **or** the Nov-12 print plus the lock-up clearance deliver *simultaneous* sequential backlog growth, GM stabilisation >25% GAAP, and a broadening customer base. Absent those, CBRS stays on the watchlist as a call option on inference architecture share — one we are content to observe rather than own.

**Data gaps I could not close:** exact lock-up tranche schedule and share count (three desks disagree); the nature of the FY2025 ~$367M non-operating gain (no 10-K retrievable; three desks flag it as unverified); Q3 segment-level cloud-vs-systems economics. Each is a material diligence item before any capital is committed — but none of them changes the direction of the answer at this price.
