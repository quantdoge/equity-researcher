**Operating footprint:** Production data-center pairs in **North America, South America, Europe, Asia and Australia** (FY2025 10-K). Employees ~29,187; HQ Santa Clara, CA. **Key structural point:** ~85% of revenue is generated outside Asia-Pacific ex-Japan, and ~59% in the US alone. This is a *low-geopolitical-beta* revenue mix — the dominant risk is **US policy/regulatory**, not emerging-market instability.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US federal budget, DOGE-era procurement scrutiny and contract concentration
**Probability: High (ongoing) | Impact: Medium**
- ServiceNow has been growing its government mix; the 10-K explicitly flags that "a growing portion of our revenue is generated from sales to government customers" (Risk Factors — "Doing business with the government").
- The **GSA OneGov agreement (Sept 3, 2025)** positions ServiceNow as a platform for "AI-first modernization" — a tailwind, but it also concentrates the company inside a politically contested modernisation agenda. The DOGE-driven efficiency push cuts both ways: fewer agencies, re-competed/consolidated contracts, and "termination for convenience" exposure.
- Political risk vectors: government shutdowns, continuing-resolution funding gaps, protest of award, and GSA schedule price-reduction demands (Most-Favored-Customer / price transparency clauses typical of OneGov deals).
- **Mitigant:** Federal is a minority of total revenue (<15% estimated); commercial/enterprise growth (20.5% subscription growth in Q3'26) is the dominant driver.

### Risk 2 — EU digital-sovereignty and AI Act regulatory burden (EMEA = 25.6% of revenue)
**Probability: High (rules already enacted) | Impact: Medium**
- The **EU AI Act becomes fully enforceable in August 2026**, with penalties up to **€35M or 7% of global turnover**; Article 50 transparency duties (disclosure of AI interactions) apply to *deployers* from 2 Aug 2026, and ServiceNow's own "Now Assist" AI features must be classified under the four-tier risk framework.
- Structural pressure: EU sovereign-cloud and data-residency initiatives ("Buy European," Gaia-X-adjacent procurement) could disadvantage US hyperscaler/SaaS vendors in public-sector deals — a direct headwind to EMEA public-sector growth.
- **GDPR / EU–US Data Privacy Framework:** ServiceNow relies on the DPF and SCCs. A successful legal challenge to the DPF (the "Schrems III" line of litigation) would force re-papering of EU transfers and could trigger customer contract renegotiations.
- **Mitigant:** Local EU data centers and an existing GDPR/DPF compliance program reduce operational disruption; this is a cost-of-compliance risk, not an existential one.

### Risk 3 — US–China / APAC tension and data-localisation in Asia-Pacific (11.5% of revenue, fastest-growing share)
**Probability: Medium | Impact: Low–Medium**
- APAC & Other is the smallest but fastest-growing segment. Exposure runs through Australia, Japan, India, Korea and Singapore — all relatively high rule-of-law — rather than mainland China.
- Residual vectors: Chinese data-security/cybersecurity-review rules affecting any local R&D or support staff; US export-control "deemed export" rules on AI/encryption functionality; and possible Chinese retaliation against US software vendors (e.g., replacement lists, procurement bans) if bilateral tension escalates.
- **Low direct sanctions exposure:** no disclosed Russia operations, no sanctioned-country revenue, and no dual-use hardware. ServiceNow is not a plausible primary target of OFAC/BIS action, though it must screen customers and resale partners.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation / investigations**
- **Securities-fraud solicitations (Oct 29, 2026):** Following Q3'26 results, NOW fell ~19.5% ($29.23/share) after guiding Q4 cRPO growth to 18.5% vs. 21% delivered. **Levi & Korsinsky** and **SueWallSt** have each announced "investigations into potential violations of federal securities laws." At present these are **plaintiff-firm solicitations, not filed complaints**; the theory (guidance miss attributed to pulled-forward growth) is a weak basis for scienter. Expect a formal class action to be filed within the 60-day window; likely immaterial financially but a governance headline risk.
- **Ordinary-course litigation:** In each recent 10-Q (Mar/Jun/Sep 2026), the company states it "is party to certain litigation and other legal proceedings" but that no pending matter, if adversely determined, would be material. **No disclosed DOJ, SEC, FTC or EU antitrust investigation** into ServiceNow itself.
- **M&A regulatory risk (now largely resolved):** The **$7.75B Armis acquisition** (announced 2025) required customary regulatory approvals and **closed April 20, 2026**. The earlier **Moveworks** deal (~$2.85B, 2025) also cleared. Both add cybersecurity/OT and AI assets — increasing exposure to cyber-sector FDI screening regimes (e.g., CFIUS, EU FDI screening) on *future* deals rather than current ones.

**Regulatory posture**
- **Politically exposed customers:** Government, financial services, healthcare and critical infrastructure are named verticals — sectors where data-breach notification, public-procurement integrity and AI-governance obligations are tightening.
- **New debt indenture (May 15, 2026, U.S. Bank trustee):** $7.52B debt load with rising interest costs. Standard incurrence covenants; not a legal-risk driver but tightens flexibility for further debt-funded M&A.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

| Area | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **FCPA / anti-bribery** | **Low.** Third-party reseller channel is the main vector; no disclosed FCPA inquiry. DOJ's expanded Corporate Whistleblower Awards Pilot Program (2025) raises reporting risk for any channel-partner misconduct. |
| **Sanctions / export controls** | **Low.** No Russia/Iran/sanctioned-country revenue; SaaS delivery limits physical export exposure. Encryption/AI "deemed export" controls apply to engineering staff. |
| **Data privacy** | **Medium.** GDPR, UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA and a widening set of US state laws apply; as a processor for government and healthcare customers, breach or sub-processor failure carries notification and contractual liability. DPF invalidation is the tail risk. |
| **AI governance** | **Medium (rising).** EU AI Act (Aug 2026), plus emerging US state AI laws and customer-side model-risk requirements — ServiceNow is both a provider and a deployer of AI. |
| **Government contracting integrity** | **Medium.** Procurement-integrity rules, organizational-conflict-of-interest and price-transparency obligations under the GSA OneGov vehicle. |

**No material red flags identified** — no disclosed bribery, sanctions, or fraud investigation, and no going-concern or internal-control material weakness in recent filings (SOX 302 certifications clean, Oct 28, 2026).

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **MEDIUM** (trending Low–Medium)

**Rationale:** ServiceNow's risk profile is unusually benign for a company of its size. Revenue is ~59% US, ~26% EMEA, ~11.5% APAC — no meaningful exposure to sanctioned states, no manufacturing supply chain exposed to tariffs, and a clean regulatory enforcement record. The **Medium** rating is driven by three factors rather than any single severe exposure:

1. **Concentration in the US federal vertical** during a period of active procurement disruption — a high-probability but bounded-impact risk.
2. **Europe's regulatory build-out** (AI Act enforcement, data-sovereignty procurement, DPF litigation), which raises cost of compliance and could slow EMEA — 25.6% of revenue.
3. **A fresh securities-litigation tail** from the October 2026 guidance-driven selloff, plus integration/regulatory exposure from the $7.75B Armis and Moveworks acquisitions.

**Monitoring triggers:** (i) filing of a formal securities class action and any SEC subpoena; (ii) EU AI Act enforcement guidance on enterprise AI workflow tools; (iii) DPF challenge outcome; (iv) GSA OneGov scope reductions or contract protests; (v) CFIUS/EU FDI review of any follow-on acquisition.
