# Macroeconomic Assessment — ServiceNow, Inc. (NOW)
**As of 29–30 October 2026 | Prices: NOW $109.67 | Data: FRED**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-Off / Late-Cycle Reflation ("No-Landing With a Term-Premium Problem")**

The U.S. economy is *not* slowing into recession — it is re-accelerating into a supply-constrained, high-rate equilibrium. Real GDP grew **+3.4% SAAR in Q3'26** (24,611.1 vs 24,408.0 chained $bn), with YoY growth of **+2.1%**. Unemployment has *fallen* to **4.2%** from 4.5% a year ago. But CPI inflation has **re-accelerated to +3.72% YoY in September** (+0.65% m/m, ~8% annualised), up from +3.30% in July — and the October 29 inflation print is explicitly cited as the trigger for the day's equity selloff.1,2

The critical macro fact for NOW is the **rates dislocation**: the Fed cut the funds rate to **3.75%**, yet the 10Y Treasury has *risen* to **5.05%** (Oct 28) from 4.55% in mid-July — a **+50bp bear-steepening in under three months**, with the 30Y at **5.37%**. The 2Y (4.65%) now sits **+90bp above fed funds**, meaning the market is pricing a **hiking cycle, not a cutting cycle**. This is the worst possible rate regime for long-duration, high-multiple growth equities: the discount rate is rising while the growth *narrative* is being questioned.

That combination explains everything in NOW's tape. The stock fell **27% in one session** (Oct 29: $150.13 → $109.67) on a Q4 cRPO guide of 18.5% against a 21.5% revenue guide — a *deceleration* signal for enterprise software.3,4 Since the January 2025 high (~$234), NOW is **-53%** despite still compounding subscription revenue at **+20.5%** (to $3.99B) and raising its AI contract target to **$1.7B**. That gap between operating performance and share price is almost entirely a **multiple/discount-rate event**, not an earnings event.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

ServiceNow is a **long-duration, low-capex, net-debt-lite-but-not-debt-free** subscription business. Revenue is recurring (subscription ≈97% of total) and contracted, so it is structurally *protected* on the top line; it is structurally *exposed* on the multiple. Reported debt load is **$7.52B with "surging interest costs"**5 — small versus $113B market cap but no longer trivial at 5%+ yields.

| Macro Variable | Impact on NOW | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **10Y at 5.05% / bear steepening** | **STRONGLY NEGATIVE** | NOW's value is ~85% terminal-value; a 50bp rise in the discount rate on a 20%-grower compresses fair value by ~15–20%. This is the dominant driver of the -53% drawdown. |
| **Fed pricing hikes (2Y 4.65% > FF 3.75%)** | **NEGATIVE** | Kills the "rate cuts → multiple re-rating" thesis that supported software in 2025. Removes the main macro catalyst. |
| **Inflation +3.72%, re-accelerating** | **NEUTRAL / MIXED** | *Positive:* multi-year contracts carry 3–5% embedded escalators, so NOW has genuine pricing power and real revenue is inflation-linked. *Negative:* CPI prints like Oct 29 directly pressure high-multiple equities and force CIOs to scrutinise seat-based expansion. |
| **Real GDP +3.4%, unemployment 4.2%** | **POSITIVE (fundamental)** | Enterprise IT budgets are pro-cyclical; no recession means no wave of seat contraction or deal slippage. Note the tension: the *guide* slowed while the *economy* accelerated — that is company/competitive-specific, not macro. |
| **Broad USD 121.3 (+2.8% in 6 weeks)** | **MODERATELY NEGATIVE** | Roughly 40% of revenue is non-U.S.; a firmer dollar is a 100–150bp reported-revenue headwind. NOW reports constant-currency, so the *operational* read is fine, but reported numbers and consensus EPS take the hit. |
| **$7.52B debt / 5%+ long rates** | **NEGATIVE (second-order)** | Rising coupon and refinancing cost reduces FCF-to-equity and eliminates buyback optionality. Not solvency-threatening given ~$4B+ quarterly subscription revenue and strong FCF, but it caps financial engineering. |
| **Fiscal / public-sector exposure** | **WATCH ITEM** | Government is a core vertical for Now Platform (ITSM/GRC). With a 5.37% 30Y yield signalling fiscal stress, federal/vendor-budget consolidation is the single largest idiosyncratic macro risk not captured in FRED data. |

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

**Rationale:** The macro regime is actively hostile to NOW's valuation architecture even though it is broadly supportive of its fundamentals. Rising long-end yields, a market pricing Fed *hikes*, re-accelerating inflation, and a firming dollar all push the discount rate and the FX translation the wrong way for a 20%-growth, ~7x forward sales software company. The October 29 repricing (-27%) is the market doing macro work: simultaneously de-rating the sector for the guide-down *and* for the inflation print that landed the same morning. Until the 10Y stabilises below ~4.75%, NOW's multiple is capped regardless of cRPO delivery.

**What would change this to Neutral:** 10Y back to 4.50–4.60%, CPI back below 3.3%, and a 2Y that stops implying hikes. All three are visible in the next two CPI prints.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**BEAR (higher probability, ~35%): "The 2027 Fed Hike."**
Core services inflation, tight 4.2% unemployment and a +6% nominal GDP economy force the FOMC to **reverse course and hike in H1 2027**, taking fed funds back to 4.25% and the 10Y to **5.75–6.00%**. On a 20%-grower, that is a further 20–25% de-rating in the multiple alone. NOW trades to **~$80–85** (-25%) *on no change to estimates*, and the entire SaaS complex de-rates with it — Salesforce (-6%) and Workday (-5%) already moved in sympathy on Oct 29.6 The tell-tale signal is already live: the 2Y at 4.65% versus fed funds at 3.75% means the bond market has *begun* pricing this.

**BULL (lower probability, ~20%): "Term Premium Normalises + AI Monetisation Inflects."**
The Oct 29 CPI print proves to be an energy/shelter artefact, the 10Y mean-reverts to **4.30%**, and the raised **$1.7B AI contract target** converts into cRPO re-acceleration above 22% in Q1'27. With the stock at ~7x forward sales (versus 15x+ at the 2025 peak) and beta of 0.99, the re-rating is violent: **$175–190 (+60–75%)** within four quarters. Seven of the last ten times NOW fell >10% in a day, it was higher six months later7 — the base rate for a macro-driven, fundamentals-intact drawdown favours this, but *only once the rate shock stops*.

**Bottom line for the investment memo:** NOW is a macro *victim*, not a macro *problem*. The business is defying the cycle; the stock is being repriced by the long end of the Treasury curve. Position sizing should be treated as a **rates trade with software attached** until the 10Y breaks back below 4.75%.
