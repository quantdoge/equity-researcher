## NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — SERVICENOW, INC. (NYSE: NOW)

**Sector:** Technology / Software – Application · **HQ:** Santa Clara, CA · **Employees:** 29,187 · **Market Cap:** ~$113B · **Beta:** 0.99 · **CEO/Chairman:** William R. McDermott

**Context setting the analysis:** NOW shares fell ~19–20% in a single session on 29 Oct 2026 after Q3'26 (subscription revenue +20.5% to $3.99B, EPS miss) guided Q4 cRPO growth of 18.5% against a 21.5% revenue guide. Shares are down roughly 54% over twelve months amid a sector-wide AI-disruption de-rating. Non-financial risk here is therefore *amplified*: governance or trust failures would land on an already-wounded multiple.

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE** (with flags)

**Positives:**
- 9 director nominees, **6 independent** under NYSE standards; all Board committees fully independent; a **Lead Independent Director** role exists (2026 DEF 14A).
- Dedicated **Chief Ethics & Compliance Officer** (Bill Burtis) — formalized ethics infrastructure, above median for a company of this age.
- Founder Frederic Luddy retains meaningful equity (~773k+ shares) — founder alignment persists.
- Sobriety of disclosure: timely 8-K on the 2024 executive matter; active security transparency via trust.servicenow.com.

**Negatives:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality** — McDermott holds both roles. Concentration of power is only partly offset by the Lead Independent Director.
- **3 of 9 directors are non-independent** (McDermott plus affiliated seats) — elevated vs. best practice.
- **Key-person dependency is severe.** McDermott (age 65) is Chairman, CEO, and the company's principal public narrative-holder. No publicly announced successor or COO-level bench of equivalent stature since the 2024 departure.
- **Governance failure on record (2024):** President/COO **CJ Desai resigned 24 July 2024** after an internal investigation found policy violations tied to a **government contract sales process and the hiring of a former US Army CIO**. A **DOJ investigation was reported as ongoing** at the time. This is a genuine culture/internal-controls red flag in the company's strategically critical federal business — not a technicality.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Cybersecurity — actively exploited critical vulnerability in their own product.** **CVE-2026-6875, a sandbox escape in the ServiceNow AI Platform**, was disclosed 13 July 2026 and, per the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security (AV26-693, updated 20 July 2026), **is being exploited in the wild**. Affected Brazil, Australia, Zurich and Yokohama branches. For a multi-tenant platform hosting government, healthcare and financial-services tenants, a sandbox escape is a *cross-tenant isolation* risk — the highest-severity class of SaaS vulnerability. Mitigant: mature trust portal, published advisories, fast patching cadence.
- **Key-person and succession concentration.** Strategic direction, AI narrative and investor credibility are fused to one 65-year-old executive holding both Chairman and CEO titles, with no visible successor.
- **Platform/partner dependency on foundation-model and hyperscaler providers.** The OpenAI and Anthropic relationships and the "Control Tower Alliance" (AWS, Cisco, HiddenLayer et al.) create concentration on third parties whose pricing, availability or competitive posture ServiceNow does not control — while those same partners are potential competitors in agentic workflows.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Elevated**

The dominant reputational vector is not scandal but **the AI-disruption narrative**, and management has limited ability to control it:
- **Pricing-model transition (9 April 2026):** five legacy tiers collapsed into three AI-native tiers (Foundation / Advanced / Prime). This is a live **customer-cost backlash and renewal-renegotiation risk** — community discussion centers explicitly on "will customers pay."
- **Backlog deceleration** read by the market as evidence of AI-driven seat erosion — a self-reinforcing narrative that pressures the brand with CIO buyers.
- **ESG/brand controversies:** none identified of the product-recall, safety or environmental type. Employee/customer sentiment is broadly constructive (partnership announcements, customer wins like Stagwell).
- **Legal-notice pressure:** plaintiff-firm press releases name the company publicly after every guide-down — reputationally noisy even when meritless.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Reasoning: there are **no fraud indicators, no SEC enforcement history, no confirmed large-scale litigation, and no accounting-integrity concerns** (Q3'26 SOX 302 certifications are clean and unqualified, with no disclosed material weaknesses). Governance is structurally sound — independent committees, Lead Independent Director, dedicated compliance officer. That floors the rating at Moderate rather than High.

It does *not* reach Low because of four compounding factors: an **unresolved DOJ matter**, **CEO/Chair duality with acute key-person concentration**, a **critical CVE exploited in the wild against their own AI platform**, and **insiders selling into strength with zero open-market purchases** into a 54% drawdown.

Note also $7.52B of debt with rising interest costs — not itself a non-financial risk, but it **reduces the shock-absorbing capacity** if a compliance or cyber event requires remedial spend.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **DOJ investigation status (2024 government-contract hiring matter) — unresolved.** No public resolution identified. Potential FCPA/false-claims exposure in the federal segment, and a proven internal-controls failure at the President/COO level. **Request: current status, any accrual, and remediation evidence.**
2. **CVE-2026-6875 exploited in the wild.** Confirm remediation coverage across the installed base, whether any tenant data was accessed, and any customer notification obligations. This is a *lower-probability, high-severity* tail risk.
3. **CEO/Chairman duality + age 65 + no announced successor.** Engage on succession planning depth.
4. **Insider pattern:** director and officer sales at $135–$148 in Aug 2026; **zero open-market purchases** through the drawdown to $109.67.
5. **Securities-litigation watch:** solicitation received post-Q3; monitor for an actual filed complaint and any 8-K.
6. **April 2026 pricing-tier overhaul** — monitor renewal rates, customer churn commentary, and CIO cost backlash for evidence of pricing power erosion.
