# ServiceNow, Inc. (NYSE: NOW) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price:** $109.67 (close 10/29/2026) | **Market cap:** ~$114.4B (1.043B diluted sh.) | **EV:** ~$113B | **Net cash:** ~$1B
**Context:** −53% from the Jan-2025 high (~$234), −31% in one session on 10/29/26 (record drop) after Q3'26 results. *Note: all per-share data in the feed is split-adjusted (5:1 applied retroactively); figures below are internally consistent.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **WIDE** (narrowing at the margin)

ServiceNow is the system of record for enterprise workflow — starting in IT service management and now spanning HR, legal, security operations, CRM, and AI-agent governance ("Control Tower"). The moat rests primarily on **switching costs**, reinforced by **intangible assets** (regulatory/compliance certification, 20 years of embedded process logic) and a growing **data/ecosystem advantage**.

**Evidence the moat is real and has widened over 5 years:**
- Revenue: $4.5B (2020) → $13.3B (2025) → **$16.2B est. FY26**, a 25% 6-yr CAGR with no down year.
- **cRPO $13.65B (+20.5%)** and **total RPO $29.7B (+22%)** — contracted backlog growing in line with revenue, which is unusual at this scale and is the single best moat proof point.
- **680 customers with >$5M ACV, +23% YoY**; 19 deals >$5M net-new ACV in Q3'26, **+135% YoY**. Deal sizes are *rising*, not saturating — the opposite of a commoditizing product.
- Gross margin 77.5% FY25; non-GAAP operating margin 31.5% guided FY26; very high gross renewal rates (historically ~98%).

**Where the moat is under genuine attack.** The market's objection is not irrational:
- ServiceNow's historical pricing is **seat-based**. If AI agents replace human service-desk/IT users, the per-seat denominator shrinks. Management reports **~50% of net-new business now comes from non-seat/hybrid pricing** (tokens, infrastructure, connectors) — evidence of adaptation, but also of a business model in transition, with **less revenue predictability per contract**.
- **AI net-new ACV target of $1.7B for 2026 (+70% YTD)** is impressive, but it is partly *substitution* — offsetting seat disinflation rather than pure increment.
- **cRPO growth guided to 18.5% for Q4 vs. 20.5% delivered in Q3** — this deceleration, not the quarter, is what broke the stock. A wide moat can still produce decelerating growth; the question is whether 21% → 18.5% is a cycle or a slope change.

**Judgment:** Wide moat on switching costs, with the terminal value of the seat-based layer now legitimately in question. This is a better business than 95% of software, but I cannot underwrite its economics 10 years out with the same confidence I could in 2021. Moat trend: **widening economically, narrowing perceptually.**

**The SBC problem is the key analytic issue.** GAAP net income ($1.44B TTM) and owner earnings ($1.62B in FY25) look terrible against a $114B market cap — 71–79× earnings. But that is because $2.3B/yr of real compensation is paid in stock while cash flow remains enormous. Management neutralizes it with buybacks ($2.225B in Q1'26; $1.84B in FY25) and diluted share count is essentially flat (1.015B in 2021 → 1.043B). **True shareholder-available cash is therefore between $2.4B (FCF − SBC) and $4.75B (raw FCF).**

**Deductions from a perfect score:** gross margin down ~5 points (AI compute/acquired mix), GAAP operating margin compressing despite scale, FCF growth decelerating to ~7% in 9M'26 versus 20% revenue growth, and the Armis acquisition adding interest cost that caused the non-GAAP EPS miss ($1.00 vs $1.02). A 10/10 business does not let EPS miss on acquisition financing.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Multiples at $109.67:**

| Measure | Now | Comment |
|---|---|---|

| **P/E (non-GAAP FY26E ~$3.85)** | **~28.5×** | Reasonable for 20%-grower |

| EV/EBITDA (non-GAAP) | ~19.6× | Attractive |
| **FCF yield (FY26E ~$5.3–5.7B)** | **~4.6–5.0%** | Fair, not cheap |
| FCF yield **after SBC** | ~2.1% | Expensive |

**Intrinsic value (my own two-stage DCF; the built-in DCF tool naively extrapolates 28% FCF growth for a decade and returns an absurd $387/share, which I discard).** WACC 10%, terminal growth 2.5%:

- **Base case** — FCF $5.3B, growth 16%→10% over 5 yrs, 7% next 5: **EV ~$128B → ~$124/share**
- **Conservative case** — growth 12%→7%, flat margins, terminal 2.5%: **~$95/share**
- **SBC-deducted owner-earnings case** — base $2.4B, 15% growth: **~$70/share**
- **Bull case** — 20% for 5 yrs, 10% next 5: **~$170/share**

**Blended intrinsic value: ~$105–125/share.** The stock at $109.67 sits **inside** that range — fair value, not a discount. Implied expectations are undemanding (the price now discounts ~13–15% long-term FCF growth), which is the good news; the bad news is there is **no margin of safety**. Street targets (median ~$152, range $110–$201) embed a re-rating I am unwilling to underwrite.

## 4. Management Quality: **8 / 10**

- **Bill McDermott (Chairman/CEO since 2019)** — an operator, not a promoter, though his rhetoric ("Super Intelligence Control Tower," "rule of 57") is promotional. Execution is verifiable: beat-and-raise in 8 of the last 8 quarters.
- **Capital allocation is genuinely shareholder-oriented:** bought back **$2.2B of stock in Q1 2026** into the AI-fear selloff rather than issuing equity or overpaying for M&A. That is the behavior of an owner.
- **Dilution is contained** — share count flat over five years despite $2.3B annual SBC.
- **Blemishes:** SBC at 15% of revenue is too generous; the **Armis acquisition** (goodwill $1.48B → $4.70B) added leverage and interest expense that diluted EPS — the first sign of empire-building I've seen. No dividend (appropriate at this growth rate). Guidance for a **36% FCF margin** in FY26 looks optimistic against 22.6% delivered in 9M'26 — watch for a credibility gap.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (accumulate below ~$88)

**This is a wonderful business at a fair price — and a fair price is not a buy.**

The 53% drawdown has converted an un-ownable 65× GAAP / 15× EV/Sales story into a defensible ~28.5× non-GAAP earnings, 7× EV/Sales, ~4.8% FCF-yield franchise with net cash and $29.7B of contracted backlog. That is a dramatic improvement in the entry math. But my base-case intrinsic value of ~$124 and conservative value of ~$95 bracket the current price; the SBC-adjusted owner-earnings case (~$70) is materially below it. Paying ~$110 for a business whose seat-based revenue model is being repriced by AI, whose GAAP margins are compressing, and whose FCF growth has slowed to single digits offers **no 20–30% margin of safety**.

- **Buy zone: $85–88** (≈30% discount to base-case intrinsic value; ~4.5% EV/Sales). At that price the market would be paying only for the existing RPO base.
- **Trim/sell above: ~$155–170** (approaching bull-case value; the Street's $152 median target).
- **What would make me a buyer tomorrow at a higher price:** two consecutive quarters of cRPO growth *re-accelerating* above 20% **with** gross margin stabilizing ≥75%. That would prove the hybrid-pricing transition is additive, not dilutive — I would then underwrite the 20% growth path rather than the 14% one, lifting intrinsic value to ~$170.
- **What would make me avoid it entirely:** cRPO growth below 15% for two quarters, or gross margin below 70%. That would signal the moat is being priced away, not merely re-based.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI disintermediation of the workflow layer (highest severity).** Agentic platforms from Microsoft/Salesforce/OpenAI could reduce the number of human seats consuming ServiceNow workflows. The hybrid pricing shift mitigates but does not eliminate this; it also lowers revenue visibility.
2. **Deceleration is already in the numbers.** cRPO guidance of 18.5% for Q4 vs. 20.5% in Q3. If that slope continues, FY27 revenue growth lands mid-teens and the ~$124 base case falls toward ~$95.
3. **Margin compression.** Gross margin down ~5 points YoY and GAAP operating margin down to ~9.5%. If AI compute costs and SBC both scale with revenue, cash ROIC of ~50% erodes and the terminal-value assumption breaks.
4. **Guidance credibility.** The 36% FY26 FCF-margin target implies ~$3B of Q4 FCF versus $2.0B last year. Failure here would re-open the whole AI-narrative discount.
5. **SBC dilution / valuation optics.** 15% of revenue paid in stock means GAAP EPS may keep falling even as the business compounds — sustaining a 70×+ GAAP P/E is a persistent overhang.
6. **Competitive and integration risk.** Armis increases leverage, adds interest cost, and competes in a crowded security market; large-deal concentration is rising (19 deals >$5M in one quarter).
7. **Multiple risk.** Software is in a documented bear market on AI fear. The de-rating from 15× to 7× EV/Sales may not be finished, and a further leg down to 5–5.5× ($80–88) is entirely plausible — which is precisely why patience pays.

**Data notes / gaps:** I relied on the Q3'26 press release and earnings coverage rather than a full 10-K risk-factor review on the Armis transaction; precise Q3'26 net cash was estimated from the FY25 balance sheet plus disclosed buyback and financing activity. Peer-multiple comparison is limited (the sector peer set returned is semiconductor-dominated and not comparable to application software); I substituted historical EV/Sales and my own DCF.

**Bottom line:** A great company, honestly managed, now priced roughly at fair value. I want to own it — at $88, not $110. **Hold.**
