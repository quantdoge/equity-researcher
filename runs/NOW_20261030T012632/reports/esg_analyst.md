# ServiceNow, Inc. (NYSE: NOW) — Full ESG Analysis

**Sector:** Technology / Software – Application | **HQ:** Santa Clara, CA (US) | **Employees:** ~29,187 | **Market cap:** ~$113bn
**Context note:** NOW fell ~19–20% on 29 Oct 2026 on a Q4 cRPO growth guide-down. ESG factors are *not* the driver of that de-rating, but the selloff is relevant to two governance items (CEO pay optics and a securities-investigation solicitation).

The key insight for software: **the material ESG risk is social and governance, not environmental.** ServiceNow's own footprint is trivial in absolute terms; its ESG risk sits in (a) the trust layer around the data it processes and the AI it ships, and (b) human capital/governance.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

Verified FY2025 data (Apex third-party opinion, limited assurance):

| Metric | FY2025 | Trend |
|---|---|---|
| Scope 1 | 2,485 tCO₂e | — |
| Scope 2 (market-based) | 901 tCO₂e | Location-based 84,164 t → heavy REC reliance |
| Scope 1+2 vs 2019 (~16,597t) | **~3,386t, –80%** | Beats SBTi target (–70% by 2026, 1.5°C validated) |
| Scope 3 | **317,094 tCO₂e** (6 of 15 categories) | FY22 217k → FY23 285k → FY24 286k → **FY25 317k (+46% vs FY22)** |
| Renewable electricity | 100% (RE100) | Market-based vs location-based gap shows REC dependence |

**Strengths:** SBTi-validated 1.5°C targets; net-zero by 2030; third-party-verified carbon-neutral cloud since 2022; ~75% of footprint attributed to suppliers with a 65%-of-spend SBTi-supplier target; carbon intensity of roughly **~23–25 tCO₂e per $1m revenue** (on ~$13–14bn revenue) — among the lowest in Technology.

**Weaknesses / forward risks:**
- **Scope 3 is rising in absolute terms** and the target is *intensity*-based (per unit value added), not absolute. This is exactly the gap EU CSRD (ESRS E1) and California SB 253 will expose.
- **Offset reliance** underpins the "carbon-neutral cloud" marketing claim — live greenwashing/claim-substantiation risk under the EU Empowering Consumers Directive (enforcement from Sept 2026).
- **AI build-out is the emissions wildcard.** GPU-heavy workloads and third-party model inference inflate purchased-goods-and-services and capital-goods Scope 3, plus customer-attributed energy. Expect Scope 3 to keep climbing even as S1+2 stays near zero.
- No TNFD-aligned or quantified biodiversity disclosure (low materiality, but a data gap for some EU mandates).
- No stranded-asset, water-stress or physical-climate exposure of consequence.

**Verdict: Low.** Environmental factors do not threaten cash flows. The risk is disclosure/claims litigation and customer-supplier audit friction, not carbon cost.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Human capital / restructuring — the most under-priced S factor.**
- Headcount ~29,187, essentially flat year-over-year after years of 20%+ growth (Revelio: 29,155 in 2025) — a sharp deceleration in hiring.
- **WARN filings:** 424 jobs across two California locations; four CA WARN notices affecting 307 workers, most recently **13 Oct 2026**. This is a company repositioning toward AI while shrinking parts of the org — the classic "AI efficiency" narrative that carries attrition, morale and employer-brand risk precisely when the market is questioning the AI growth story.
- Workforce composition: ~36.7% female globally; US mix ~3.5% Black, 6.4% Hispanic, 28.4% Asian. Black/Hispanic representation trails US labor-force parity and large-cap tech peers — a pipeline risk for a company whose product is engineering-led, and a data point that will get *harder to benchmark* if the EEOC's proposed EEO-1 elimination (June 2026) proceeds.

**Litigation cluster (see §5)** — employment discrimination, wrongful termination, an ERISA 401(k) fee case and a RICO suit. None individually material; collectively they signal HR/compliance process strain during rapid scale and restructuring.

**Human rights / licence to operate.** DHS/ICE operates ServiceNow ITSM in production (DHS PIA-059 is public). ServiceNow has a Human Rights Statement and data-governance committees, and it also sells to CBP/defence. This is a **norms-based screening** exposure: a modest but real pool of European and Swiss institutional capital excludes or engages on immigration-enforcement enablement. Not a cash-flow issue for a company whose federal business is growing >30%; it is a marginal cost-of-capital/ownership-base issue.

**Product & AI.** Genuine two-sided exposure. Positively, ServiceNow is monetising governance itself — AI Control Tower, AI Passport, policy enforcement with NVIDIA, the Control Tower Alliance (Anthropic, AWS, Cisco, HiddenLayer), EU GPAI Code of Practice signatory, ISO/IEC 42001 mapping. Negatively, every agentic workflow it ships embeds customer-facing accuracy, bias and liability risk, and the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations (Aug 2026–2027) land squarely on its regulated-industry customers.

**Verdict: Medium.** No systemic social failure, but human-capital execution during an AI pivot plus a persistent employment-litigation drumbeat.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.5 / 10**

**Positives**
- **Single class of common stock** — no dual-class structure, one share one vote. Better than most founder-led software peers.
- **All three key committees (Audit, Leadership Development & Compensation, Nominating/Governance) are entirely independent.**
- Majority-independent board: **6 of 9 nominees independent** per the 2026 proxy.
- Robust anti-corruption infrastructure (Code of Conduct, Speak Up line, third-party GRC controls); no FCPA/UK Bribery Act enforcement or material bribery allegation found.
- Executive pay is overwhelmingly equity/PSU-weighted, linking realised value to TSR; ESG/purpose goals have featured in incentive design.
- Clean Section 302 certifications on the Q3 FY2026 10-Q; no disclosed material weakness.

**Negatives**
- **Combined Chairman & CEO.** Bill McDermott has held both roles since 2022. This is the single clearest governance defect: it concentrates agenda-setting, board-leadership and management authority, and is a standing trigger for proxy-advisor dissent.
- **CEO pay of $51.6m in 2026, +37% year-over-year, pay ratio 251:1.** Awarded in a year in which the stock is down ~20%+ and guidance was cut. Even with equity weighting, the optics are poor and the +37% increase is hard to reconcile with a 20%+ share-price decline. **Watch say-on-pay support for erosion** (a sub-80% result would be a meaningful signal).
- **Shareholder rights are thin.** The board recommends *against* the 2026 proposal granting shareholders the right to act by written consent; the company also lacks a broad special-meeting right. Management-controlled access to the ballot.
- **Dilution.** Shareholders approved amendments to the 2021 Equity Incentive Plan increasing shares available — with ~14.5% opposition (654.7m for / 110.9m against), a non-trivial protest vote.
- **Guidance/disclosure volatility.** The Oct 2026 guide-down that erased ~$25bn+ of market value has already drawn a securities-investigation solicitation (SueWallSt). Routine claims-firm marketing today — but a filed securities class action would add a disclosure-controls governance question.

**Why 6.5 and not lower:** independent committees, single-class shares, no controlling family, no related-party or accounting scandal, and a board that has refreshed. Why not higher: dual role, an outsized and rising CEO package at a bad moment for shareholders, and a defensive posture on shareholder rights.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**Why neutral, not positive:**
- The ESG risks that exist are **immaterial to cash flows**: total quantifiable controversy exposure <1% of market cap; carbon costs are near-zero for an asset-light SaaS model; no stranded assets, no CBAM, no environmental remediation liability.
- The governance defects (Chair/CEO duality, $51.6m pay up 37% into a 20% drawdown, resistance to written consent) are **real but priced by governance-aware capital, not by the broader market** — and are correctable by a single board action (appointing an independent chair).
- **The dominant risk to the NOW thesis is commercial, not ESG**: a 20%+ post-earnings drawdown on decelerating cRPO growth, AI monetisation credibility, and $7.52bn of debt with rising interest cost. ESG neither offsets nor amplifies that.

**Why not negative:**
- Single-class share structure, fully independent committees, no accounting or bribery scandal, no data breach of consequence, best-in-class operational carbon, and a genuinely differentiated position on AI governance — the one place where an ESG capability is a **revenue asset** rather than a cost (AI Control Tower, AI Passport, NVIDIA policy enforcement, Control Tower Alliance). As AI regulation bites, governance tooling becomes a non-discretionary enterprise purchase, and ServiceNow is selling the compliance layer.

**What would move the call:**
- → **Negative:** a filed securities class action with disclosure-control findings; a material GDPR fine; say-on-pay falling below 70%; Scope 3 restated upward or the carbon-neutral claim withdrawn under regulatory pressure; a serious AI-agent liability incident traced to the Now Platform.
- → **Positive:** appointment of an independent chair; absolute (not intensity-only) Scope 3 target; disclosure of workforce attrition and AI-driven reskilling metrics; federal/ICE revenue concentration addressed in risk disclosure.

**Positioning view:** ESG is **not a reason to own or avoid NOW**. For ESG-constrained mandates, NOW screens as *eligible but not best-in-class* — an "ESG-improver" candidate rather than a core sustainability holding. The engagement agenda for a large holder is short and specific: **independent chair, absolute Scope 3 target, and human-capital disclosure through the restructuring.** All three are achievable within 12 months and each would be incrementally value-accretive via lower cost of equity.
