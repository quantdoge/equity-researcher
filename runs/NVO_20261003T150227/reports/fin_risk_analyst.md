# NVO — Novo Nordisk A/S: Financial Risk Assessment
*ADR (NYSE), Healthcare / Drug Manufacturers — General · Data through FY2025 (reported in DKK; price/market data in USD)*

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment: **Investment Grade** ✔

Novo Nordisk carries **Moody's Aa3 / S&P AA** ratings (stable outlook) per its most recent interim report, with EUR 15.0bn + CHF 1.09bn bonds outstanding. The ratings are consistent with the fundamentals: leverage is still low and interest coverage enormous. However, the **credit trajectory deteriorated sharply in 2025** — net debt rose ~8x in two years, and free cash flow collapsed — so the credit profile is strong but *no longer strengthening*.

Tail risk is currently *elevated*, not residual.

|---|---|---|---|

| EBIT (60% incremental-margin deleverage) | 134.7 | ~97.6 | Coverage still ~23x |
| EBITDA | 156.7 | ~120 | Net debt/EBITDA → ~0.9x |

| FCF after 90.1 CapEx + 51.8 div | 29.0 | **~ −50 to −55 (negative)** | Self-funding gap of ~55bn |

**Conclusion:** A −20% revenue shock does **not** threaten solvency — coverage stays >20x and net leverage stays below 1.5x, consistent with a mid-AA rating. **The binding constraint is liquidity, not solvency**: with CapEx and dividends effectively fixed commitments, a revenue shock would push FCF deeply negative and force incremental net debt of ~DKK 55bn/yr, pushing net debt toward 160bn+ (ND/EBITDA ~1.3–1.5x) within two years. Manageable, but the credit rating would come under negative pressure and the dividend would be at risk in a prolonged downturn.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **Moderate** (trending higher)

Solvency risk is genuinely low (AA-rated fortress, low leverage, 30x coverage). The rating is capped at Moderate — not Low — by five converging pressures: the 8x debt build, the FCF collapse, an uncovered dividend, structurally negative working capital with a thin cash buffer, and a 74% drawdown signalling impaired market confidence. In a −20% scenario this becomes **High** on a liquidity basis.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **FCF collapse + capex cliff:** CapEx nearly doubled in one year to DKK 90bn; FCF fell 58%. Watch whether the capacity build translates into revenue — if it doesn't, the 2025 capex level is a multi-year earnings/cash drag.
2. **Dividend not FCF-covered:** DKK 51.8bn dividend vs. 29.0bn FCF — payout now funded by debt (buybacks were cut to DKK 1.4bn to compensate).
3. **Leverage inflection:** Net debt up ~8x in two years (12.6 → 104.5bn DKK); interest expense tripled in one year into a 4.65–5.0% rate environment.
4. **Negative working capital / thin cash:** Current ratio 0.80, cash ratio 0.13 — fine for a pharma model, but leaves no buffer for a working-capital shock at this capex scale.
5. **Market repricing / competitive risk:** −74% drawdown; decelerating growth (+6.4%) and margin slippage as Lilly's obesity franchise advances. Equity-funded M&A risk is high given cash-generative operations.
6. **Data gaps flagged:** (a) FCF-yield tool mismatched currencies (DKK FCF vs USD market cap — true yield ~2.5%, not 17.5%); (b) no granular debt-maturity schedule/CDS spreads available; (c) Altman Z uses book equity (indicative only); (d) credit rating confirmed via web sources (H1-2026 report), not a primary data feed.

*Bottom line: Novo Nordisk remains investment grade and solvent through severe stress, but 2025 marked a structural inflection from a net-cash-like, low-risk balance sheet to a debt-funded, capex-heavy, FCF-negative equity story. The credit is safe; the risk profile is no longer.*
