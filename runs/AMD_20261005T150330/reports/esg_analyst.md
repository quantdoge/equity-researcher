# ESG Analysis: Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (AMD)

**Company:** Fabless semiconductor designer (CPUs, GPUs, AI accelerators), Santa Clara, CA. ~31,000 employees. CEO Lisa Su (also Chair). Market cap ≈ $1.03T (largest-weighted peer context: NVDA, TSM, AVGO, MU, ASML). Sector: Technology / Semiconductors.

## 1. ESG Materiality Map (what moves cash flows for a fabless chip designer)

| Factor | Materiality | Direction & Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope 3 carbon / energy (supply chain + product use phase)** | **High** | Manufacturing outsourced to TSMC/GlobalFoundries; product use-phase electricity dominates lifetime footprint. AI accelerator power draw is now a *customer procurement criterion* — efficiency is a competitive moat, not a compliance cost. |
| **Physical & geopolitical concentration risk (Taiwan)** | **High** | Leading-edge silicon produced in Taiwan; water stress at TSMC fabs and Taiwan-Strait disruption would hit AMD harder than most peers (no own fabs to fall back on). |
| **Export-control compliance (governance/geopolitics)** | **High / Critical** | Already monetized: **~$800M charge in Q1 2025** writing down China-spec inventory (MI308) after US BIS rule changes; license uncertainty persists. |
| **Responsible minerals & supply-chain labour (3TG, cobalt)** | **Medium** | Conflict-minerals + cobalt sourcing from DRC-adjacent supply chains; managed via RMAP engagement and supplier code of conduct with audit rights. |
| **Talent acquisition/retention (AI engineering)** | **Medium–High** | Banking of top AI researchers (e.g., World Labs acquisition); competition with NVDA for scarce AI talent is a real cost-of-doing-business item. |
| **Product-level environmental performance (30x efficiency goal)** | **Medium** | Differentiator in hyperscaler ESG procurement; supports the AI thesis. |
| **Direct E footprint (own ops)** | **Low** | Fabless model → small Scope 1/2; least material E item. |

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **Medium** (Low direct / High indirect)

- **Carbon intensity:** Direct GHG footprint is modest; AMD reports **24.5% absolute emissions reduction since 2020** and **40% renewable energy in 2023 vs 18% in 2020** (2023–24 CR report). Science-based targets: ~50% Scope 1&2 reduction by 2030 (vs baseline ~2019/20) plus Scope 3 supply-chain and use-phase goals (purchased-goods engagement highlighted). Targets are SBTi-aligned in public disclosure.
- **Climate transition risk:** Carbon taxes/CBAM hit *suppliers and customers*, not AMD directly — fabless structure is a structural hedge. Product use-phase electricity cost passes through to hyperscaler customers, making energy efficiency a tailwind, not a liability.
- **Physical climate risk:** The genuine E risk is third-party: **Taiwan water stress and seismic exposure at TSMC leading-edge fabs** (3nm/2nm, next-gen 2nm). Single-supplier concentration for cutting-edge nodes is the material physical-climate exposure; mitigation limited (dual-sourcing at proven nodes only).
- **Controversies:** None found (no spills, fines, or environmental penalties in screened news).

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **Medium**

- **Supply chain labour:** AMD runs a conflict-minerals programme (3TG), engages smelters via RMAP, includes **cobalt in responsible-sourcing scope**, and holds supplier-audit rights under its Supplier Code of Conduct. No acute violations surfaced; exposure is structural (mineral supply chain) rather than incident-driven.
- **Workforce:** ~31k headcount; no labour disputes, safety flags, or turnover crises in evidence. Main S risk is **talent competition in AI** — acquisition-based talent banking (World Labs, $8.2B all-stock) reflects that pressure and carries integration/retention risk.
- **Community/product:** Consumer product safety not a material concern for silicon. Community licence issues for *its own* facilities are minimal (fabless); the fabs' host-community issues sit with TSMC (third-party).
- Overall: **Medium** — moderate structural supply-chain labour exposure, elevated talent risk, no acute controversies.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

- **Strengths (+):** Board is majority independent with listed-company discipline; CEO Lisa Su (tenure since 2014) has an exceptionally strong execution record; robust external ESG disclosure (TCFD-aligned reporting, **CDP Supplier Engagement Leader** recognition); clean anti-corruption/ethics record; no dual-class or poison-pill structures.
- **Deductions (−):** **CEO/Chair duality** (Su holds both roles since 2022) (−1). **Export-control governance lapse** — $800M inventory write-down indicates compliance/strategic mis-timing in navigating US–China chip rules; oversight should have flagged China-spec exposure earlier (−1). **Shareholder dilution optics** (−1): warrants granted to OpenAI/Meta (up to 160M shares each at $0.01, tied to 6GW purchase commitments each; ~16% of an enlarged share count if fully exercised) create a large, low-priced overhang that entangles customers with equity. None of these are integrity failures — they are structural-design nicks on an otherwise strong board/supervision profile.

## 5. ESG Controversies (recent, with financial impact)

1. **US export-control charge on China-spec inventory — ~$800M pre-tax** (Q1 2025). BIS rules on advanced AI chips (incl. new limits closing loopholes on MI350x sales to Chinese entities anywhere) required inventory write-downs and created multi-quarter license uncertainty (MI308 under review as of Aug 2025). **Material, monetized, and ongoing.**
2. **Customer-warrant dilution (OpenAI/Meta)** — up to 320M total shares at $0.01 each tied to volume commitments; governance/board-extraction optics flagged by sell-side commentary. No cash impact yet; potential ~16% dilution if fully exercised. **Watch item.**
3. **No environmental, labour, or product-safety controversies** were identified in screened news.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

| Jurisdiction / Rule | Exposure |
|---|---|
| **US – BIS Export Administration Regulations** | **Critical & active.** Direct driver of the $800M charge; ongoing license regime for AI accelerators to China. |
| **US – SEC climate disclosure rules** | Status uncertain (court-challenged/administratively paused). AMD already discloses TCFD/CDP-aligned data, so compliance cost is low. |
| **EU – CSRD** | In scope as a non-EU parent with substantial EU revenue (third-country rules ~FY2027/FY2028 filing). Moderate reporting lift; no business-model impact. |
| **EU – CBAM** | **Minimal direct exposure** — semiconductors outside CBAM product scope; indirect effects only through upstream materials (minor). |
| **Taiwan / EU electricity & CO₂ pricing** | Indirect via foundry partners and customer data-centre electricity; passed through, not a P&L line for AMD. |
| **ESG-linked debt covenants** | None identified on AMD debt. |

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **Leader (with governance caveats)**

AMD sits at the better end of the semiconductor peer set on environmental management: science-based targets, CDP supplier-engagement leadership, credible third-party-audited minerals programmes, and a differentiated **30x product-efficiency goal (280→x for AI/HPC compute from 2020 baseline)** that aligns ESG with the commercial AI thesis. Nothing in the E/S domain is a financial drag. Governance is strong in integrity terms but carries two live nicks — export-control execution (already $-priced) and customer-equity dilution optics. Relative to NVDA and most fab peers, AMD's disclosure and supply-chain engagement put it a notch above **Average**, though not a flawless leader.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **Neutral** (slightly positive E tailwind, offset by G/geopolitical nicks)

ESG factors are **not the swing variable** in the AMD thesis — valuation/execution risk (≈80x forward P/E per street commentary, beta ≈ 2.5) and export-control policy dominate. Directionally: the product-efficiency story supports hyperscaler AI procurement (mild positive); the $800M export charge and dilution optics are already in the price/overhang (mild negative). Net: **Neutral for the thesis, with the caveat that Taiwan physical/geopolitical concentration and US–China export policy remain the live ESG-linked tail risks to monitor.**
