# AMD (NASDAQ: AMD) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**Date: October 2026 | Price ≈ $624–630 | Market cap ≈ $1.0T+ (crossed $1T Sept 2026)**

*Method note: 10-K/DEF 14A full text was not retrievable via the filing tool; governance and risk-factor conclusions below rely on EDGAR metadata, Form 4 records, proxy/news coverage, and regulatory searches. Honest gaps flagged inline.*

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Strong** (with watch items)

- **CEO/Chair separation**: Lisa Su is CEO (since 2014), not board Chair — no duality. An independent Lead Director (Nora M. Denzel, appointed Lead Independent Director Nov 2022, chairs Nominating & Governance) provides counterbalance.
- **Board refresh & expertise**: Tim Ryan (former PwC US Chairman — deep audit/financial expertise) appointed to the board Aug 19, 2026, joining the Audit & Finance Committee. Denzel's move onto the audit committee adds to a competent, well-resourced board.
- **Alignment**: Comp is equity-heavy; no say-on-pay controversy or related-party transactions surfaced. No unusual 8-K/conflict items.
- **Watch items**: (a) Two large acquisitions in ~18 months (ZT Systems completed Mar 31, 2025; World Labs signed Sept 26, 2026) stress test deal-governance and integration oversight; (b) CEO and other executives are selling heavily and consistently (see §3) — all under Rule 10b5-1, so low fraud signal, but volume warrants monitoring.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **No active SEC enforcement, DOJ investigation, or administrative actions found** — EDGAR full-text search for AMD returned zero regulatory actions. No securities class actions surfaced in news screens.
- **The dominant regulatory exposure is U.S. export controls (BIS)**: policy tightening on advanced AI chips has forced AMD to "rewrite its AI strategy in real time," with reported revenue/market-impact estimates of ~$800M+ from the lost China AI opportunity. AMD has intermittently secured licenses (e.g., Instinct MI308 export approvals cited in its Q3'25 earnings call), but license-granting is discretionary and politically driven — a standing earnings/guidance risk.
- **Pending approvals**: World Labs transaction subject to regulatory clearance, closing expected end-2026 — low block risk, but expiry/timing risk exists. CFIUS exposure limited (both US-centric businesses).
- **Data privacy/other**: no GDPR/CCPA enforcement actions surfaced.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Foundry/geopolitical concentration**: sole-source advanced-node manufacturing at TSMC (Taiwan) plus shared CoWoS advanced-packaging capacity and HBM supply dependencies — a single-node failure or Taiwan disruption has outsized impact.
2. **M&A integration load**: ZT Systems ($4.9B, closed Mar-25) and World Labs ($8.2B all-stock, ~6% dilution at current cap, closing end-2026) in rapid succession — integration, culture, and key-person retention (Fei-Fei Li) are live execution risks.
3. **Key-person dependence**: Lisa Su remains the strategic architect of the turnaround; while the bench (Paper-master/CTO, Norrod/data-center) is deep, succession would be a reputational and strategic event.
4. *(Secondary)*: cyber/security incident history — AMD has disclosed critical CPU vulnerabilities (e.g., "Sinkclose," Aug 2024) with criticism of its remediation scope; no confirmed external exploitation reported.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment

- **Brand trajectory is strongly positive**: AMD is the market-share winner in x86 servers (≈34.5% in Q2-26 vs 3.2% in 2018), hit a $1T market cap, and is being pulled into enterprise AI wins (first HPE order of AMD "Helios" AI racks, Sept 2026). Consumer goodwill is high.
- **Pressure points**: (a) security-vulnerability disclosures and patch cadence (Sinkclose pattern) — modest trust erosion in enterprise/professional segments; (b) China export controversy — AMD is white-listed/restricted by US policy rather than at fault, but repeated on-again/off-again licensing generates negative headlines and customer churn in China; (c) Nvidia-dominated AI narrative keeps AMD in "distant #2" framing. No ESG/scandal-grade controversies (no product recalls, labor, or environmental incidents) surfaced.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **Moderate**

Balanced view: **governance and compliance controls are strong** (split CEO/Chair, refreshed board, clean enforcement record, disciplined 10b5-1 selling); **the elevated risks are structural** — export-control regime dependence, TSMC/Taiwan concentration, rapid debt-free M&A, and key-person reliance — all of which are priced risk in a $1T semi leader but can crystallize quickly (licensing revocation, Taiwan event, deal failure, exec departure). This sits at the top of "Moderate," one notch from "High" if export-control exposure or integration missteps escalate.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Sustained one-way insider selling at all-time-high valuation** (CEO >$100M+ in scheduled sales over 12 months; zero buys). Low fraud signal, high sentiment signal — escalate if 10b5-1 velocity accelerates or sales begin outside plans.
2. **$8.2B World Labs close** (end-2026): watch regulatory timing, key-person retention, and any earn-out/valuation adjustments disclosed in the 8-K/proxy.
3. **China licensing posture**: any BIS rule change cutting off AMD's incremental China AI licenses is a direct earnings-event risk — this is the single largest non-financial tail.
4. **Foundry single-point**: any escalation in Taiwan Strait tensions or a TSMC capacity allocation shift favoring competitors would hit AMD disproportionately (no second-source advanced-node option).
5. *No fraud indicators* — no accounting irregularities, whistleblower reports, or audit-committee red flags identified; recommendations are monitoring-driven, not integrity-driven.
