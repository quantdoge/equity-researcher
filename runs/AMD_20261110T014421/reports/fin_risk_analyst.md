### 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE (High Quality)**

AMD is structurally one of the least levered large-caps in the market. Net debt is *negative-to-trivial*, interest is a rounding error against earnings, and the company was upgraded during 2026 (S&P to **'A'**, Moody's to **A1**). Debt/EBITDA on a Moody's-adjusted basis ~0.5x. There is no refinancing cliff, no covenant pressure, and no plausible operating scenario in which AMD cannot service its obligations.

Crucially, **solvency risk is not the risk in this name.** The risk is entirely equity-duration risk: a $830B market cap resting on ~$7.6B of TTM net income.

The Sharpe of 1.19 is respectable *only* because of extraordinary realised returns; the risk-adjusted quality is undermined by a 5.13% risk-free rate, meaning AMD's excess return per unit of vol is thinner than the raw Sharpe suggests.

**The credit survives all four. The equity does not.** Even in the –50% case, AMD burns perhaps $2–3B/yr against $15.8B of cash and a business that has positive gross profit — a multi-year runway with zero refinancing need. But at 109x TTM earnings, a –20% revenue outcome collapses EPS toward ~$1.50–1.80, and the terminal multiple would compress simultaneously. **A 60–75% equity drawdown is a live outcome in the –20% case** — worse than the current 3-year max drawdown, because that drawdown occurred from a smaller earnings base.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Split assessment, because a single word hides the real story:
- **Balance-sheet / credit risk: LOW.** This is a fortress. Net debt ~0.1x EBITDA, $15.8B cash, A/A1 ratings, 48x coverage, Z-score in the safe zone, F-score 8.
- **Equity / market risk: HIGH.** Beta 2.43, 65% vol, 55% realized drawdown, 0.85% FCF yield, 109x earnings.

Blended: **Moderate** — but the composition matters enormously. AMD will not go bankrupt; it can lose half its market value without straining a single covenant.

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Working-capital build is deteriorating sharply — the single most important new signal.** Q3 FY26 operating cash flow collapsed to **$1.20B** (from $2.37B, $2.96B, $2.66B in the prior three quarters); FCF fell to **$554M**. The culprit is a **–$3.36B working capital drag** in one quarter. **Inventory jumped +35% QoQ ($8.47B → $11.46B) and receivables +26% ($7.28B → $9.21B) against revenue growth of only +16%.** DIO rose from ~147 to ~169 days; DSO from ~57 to ~63 days. Either AMD is pre-building for a demand ramp it is highly confident in, or channel inventory is accumulating ahead of a digestion. This is the classic leading indicator of a semiconductor down-cycle, and it is happening while the stock trades at 109x.
2. **Net cash has evaporated.** Net position moved from **–$1.71B net cash (Mar-26) to +$1.51B net debt (Sep-26)**, with total debt up $5.0B in two quarters (incl. $1.25B of 4.600% notes issued Aug-2026). Absolute leverage remains trivial, but the *direction* is the only Piotroski miss (F5 = 0). AMD is now funding buybacks and working capital with debt rather than cash.
3. **Valuation is the dominant risk factor.** 109x TTM earnings and a 0.85% FCF yield at a 5.13% 10Y Treasury. The earnings yield (~0.9%) is roughly **one-fifth of the risk-free rate** — the entire valuation is a long-duration bet on sustained AI accelerator share gains.
4. **Concentration / policy exposure.** Q2 FY2025 shows the plumbing: gross margin fell to 39.8% and AMD posted an **operating loss of –$134M** on $7.69B revenue, consistent with a China-related export charge (~$800M). A single policy action moved a profitable quarter to a loss. Margin volatility from export controls remains a live, unhedged risk.
5. **Goodwill & intangibles = 59% of equity ($40.6B).** Tangible book is ~$27.8B. An impairment would be non-cash, but it would signal the Xilinx-era acquisition base is not earning its keep.
6. **Zero dividend, buybacks decelerating** ($1.32B FY25 → $428M across the last three quarters) — management is conserving cash, which corroborates flag #1.

### Data Gaps (explicit)
- **No CDS spread data** available; credit assessment relies on agency ratings found via search (S&P 'A', Moody's 'A1'), not a live feed.
- **FY2026 annual statements not yet filed** — all figures use TTM (Q4'25–Q3'26) or FY2025 annuals; the leverage/liquidity/Z/F-score tools returned FY2025 (period end 2025-12-27) and are therefore **stale by three quarters** on a balance sheet that has since added $5.0B of debt.
- **No debt maturity schedule** beyond short-term ($0) vs long-term ($7.08B) split — cannot confirm the exact refinancing wall, though at 0.13x net leverage it is immaterial.
- No covenant terms, no segment-level margin detail, no customer-concentration disclosure retrieved.

**Bottom line:** AMD's financial risk is *not* a survival question — it is a valuation-duration question. Stress-test the balance sheet and nothing breaks; stress-test the price and a 60%+ drawdown is a routine beta outcome. The Q3 FY26 cash-flow reversal and $3.4B working-capital build are the tell that the operating cycle may be peaking precisely when the multiple is most extended. **Watch inventory days and DSO next quarter before anything else.**
