# AMD — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
**Advanced Micro Devices (NASDAQ: AMD) | Sector: Semiconductors | Nov 2026**

---

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

Revenue by geography, FY2025 (total ~$34.6B; aggregated from 10-K disclosures via Bullfincher/BusinessQuant):

| Geography | Revenue | % of Total | Risk Level | Drivers |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| United States | $11.36B | 32.8% | **Low** | Home market; hyperscaler/cloud demand; policy beneficiary |
| China (incl. Hong Kong) | $7.75B | 22.4% | **Very High** | Export controls, MI308 licence regime, retaliation risk, 15% USG revenue share |
| Taiwan | $5.19B | 15.0% | **High** | Billing to ODMs/OEMs + TSMC single-source for advanced nodes; Strait risk |
| Singapore | ~$4.3B | ~12% | **Medium** | Regional billing/distribution hub; diversion & transshipment scrutiny |
| Europe / Japan / Korea / RoW | ~$6.0B | ~17% | **Low–Medium** | Mature rule of law; Section 232 tariff exposure on US-bound goods |

**Critical nuance:** geographic revenue is booked to the *customer's* billing location, not end demand. The 15% Taiwan figure largely reflects shipments to Taiwanese ODMs (server/laptop assembly) whose finished products ship worldwide; likewise a portion of "China" revenue is assembly-and-reexport rather than Chinese end consumption. **True China end-demand exposure is materially below 22%** — management's guidance explicitly **excludes any China AI-chip revenue** (LeverageShares; Seeking Alpha). Conversely, AMD's *physical supply* exposure to Taiwan is far higher than 15%, since advanced EPYC/Instinct silicon is fabricated at TSMC.

Latest quarter context: Q3 FY26 revenue $13.38B, Data Center +97% to $8.5B (S&P Global Market Intelligence; Seeking Alpha) — growth is US-hyperscaler-led, which *dilutes* China's share over time.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US–China export-control regime and the 15% revenue-share arrangement (Probability: ~Certain / ongoing; Impact: High)**
AMD holds BIS licences for MI308 exports to China and has agreed that **the US government receives 15% of revenue from licensed MI308 sales to China** (FT; PBS NewsHour; CBS News; Reuters, Dec 2025 — "AMD chief says company ready to pay 15% tax on AI chip shipments"). The arrangement is codified in AMD's own filings (SEC 10-Q, amd-20260926.htm: "U.S. government will receive 15% of the revenue generated from licensed MI308 sales to China"). Historical magnitude: AMD flagged a **~$1.5B 2025 revenue hit** from China export controls (CNBC) and warned losing access to the China-compliant chip could cut revenue by **~$800M** (Benzinga). The licence regime is discretionary, revocable, and legally untested — a CRS study (R48642) documents "gaps, workarounds and countermeasures." **Any tightening is a direct revenue and multiple risk; policy is set by executive action, not statute, so it can shift without Congressional process.**

**Risk 2 — Taiwan Strait concentration / TSMC single point of failure (Probability: low annually (<5%); Impact: Catastrophic)**
AMD is fabless; leading-edge CPU/GPU wafers come from TSMC in Taiwan. Even a blockade, quarantine, or shipping disruption without kinetic conflict would halt supply of AMD's highest-margin products. Taiwan is also AMD's #3 billing geography ($5.19B). Compounding: the 2026 US–Taiwan trade agreement and Section 232 semiconductor measures create tariff-driven pressure to relocate packaging/assembly to the US (STR Trade; Global Policy Watch) — a multi-year cost headwind. Note also commentary that Trump's midterm loss may *harden* China policy on Taiwan/chips (Asia Times, Nov 2026).

**Risk 3 — US tariff and Congressional-scrutiny overhang (Probability: High; Impact: Medium)**
- **Section 232:** Proclamation 11,002 (Jan 14, 2026) imposed a **25% ad valorem tariff on a narrow set of advanced logic semiconductors and derivatives, effective Jan 15, 2026** (White House; Reuters; White & Case; EY). Chips imported into the US — including AMD product returning from Asian assembly — face the levy, with margin/price-mix consequences.
- **Political:** Democratic midterm gains put "AI chip exports … as target in Democratic Congress" (Bloomberg, Nov 6, 2026). The **Nvidia CEO spy/honeypot scandal and a reported $2.5B GPU diversion scheme via Thailand** have triggered broader scrutiny of chipmaker sales practices — AMD was cited as a *potential share gainer* but analysts warn it "could also be subject to greater scrutiny over their sales practices" (MarketWatch, Nov 9, 2026; Yahoo Finance).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation — clean balance sheet, which is a genuine positive:**
- Q3 FY26 10-Q (filed Nov 4, 2026): *"During the quarter ended September 26, 2026, there were no material legal proceedings."* FY2025 10-K: *"As of December 27, 2025, there were no material legal proceedings."* (SEC filings)
- **Adeia Corp.** — sued AMD over 10 semiconductor IP patents; **settled March 9, 2026 via a multi-year IP licence agreement that "resolves all outstanding litigation between the companies"** (Adeia press release; Reuters/Westlaw). Outcome: recurring licensing cost, no damages hit disclosed.
- **Nex-X Silicon LLC v. Advanced Micro Devices**, filed Oct 7, 2026, W.D. Tex. (7:26-cv-00411) (Justia docket). Newly filed patent assertion; likely immaterial but part of a pattern of PAE activity against AMD's IP estate.
- No open securities class action, DOJ, SEC, or FTC enforcement action identified in filings or news search.

**Regulatory / merger control:**
- **EU:** $4.9B **ZT Systems** acquisition cleared unconditionally by the European Commission (Global Competition Review; TechMonitor). **Enosemi** (silicon photonics) acquired 2025 without identified challenge.
- **China SAMR** remains the key gating regulator for any future AMD AI-infrastructure M&A — Beijing has a documented history of selectively using AML review in semiconductor deals as leverage (Southern California Law Review, Sept 2026). Future Helios/AI acquisitions should be modelled with SAMR delay risk.
- **BIS/DOJ export enforcement** is escalating into the supply chain: regulators are "no longer focused only on the original exporter" but increasingly target **distributors, resellers and freight forwarders** (BIS enforcement commentary, 2026; Sayari; Kharon). AMD's heavy use of ODMs, distributors and Singapore/HK intermediaries is exactly the profile under review.
- **China countermeasures:** MOFCOM export controls on 16 US entities effective **Nov 10, 2026**; new draft **Special Provisions on Protection of Chinese Trade Secrets** (Oct 26, 2026) that contemplate sanctions on foreign companies; the Anti-Sanctions Law. Mitigant: the US–China tariff pause was extended (China suspended its 24% retaliatory tariff and rare-earth controls until **Jan 10, 2027**) — a fragile truce that expires within ~2 months (Bloomberg Law; Global Times; Nikkei).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

| Flag | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **15% revenue share to USG on China AI-chip sales** | Highly unusual quasi-fiscal arrangement. Creates disclosure, audit, and reputational exposure; may itself face legal challenge as an unauthorized exaction. Not an FCPA issue (no foreign official), but it invites political and investor scrutiny. |
| **Third-party diversion / smuggling** | Highest-probability compliance event. Industry-wide diversion schemes (e.g., $2.5B Thailand route) mean AMD's distributor and ODM channel is a live enforcement target for BIS/DOJ. Any diversion finding = penalties, licence revocation. |
| **China trade-secret / anti-sanctions exposure** | New MOFCOM rules could expose AMD's China JVs and employees to compelled disclosure or retaliation; PIPL/DSL data-localisation obligations apply to design and telemetry data. |
| **Dual-use end-use/end-user monitoring** | MI308 licence conditions require verified end users; enforcement risk is administrative and fast-moving. |
| **IP/PAE litigation cadence** | Adeia (settled), Nex-X (pending) — persistent but manageable; monitor for injunctions on Instinct/Radeon. |

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale for High (not Very High):**
- **Positives:** No material legal proceedings per the most recent 10-Q and 10-K; ZT Systems and Enosemi cleared without conditions; revenue base is diversified across US (33%), China (22%), Taiwan (15%) and Singapore/Europe (~29%); China AI revenue is *already excluded from guidance*, so the market has partly de-risked the export-control tail.
- **Negatives:** ~37% of revenue is booked in China + Taiwan, both exposed to the single most acute geopolitical fault line in tech; physically single-sourced at TSMC; operates under a discretionary, executive-branch-controlled licence regime with a 15% revenue-share toll; faces a new 25% Section 232 tariff on advanced logic chips; and the US–China truce lapses Jan 10, 2027, creating a defined catalyst window.

**What would move this to Very High:** revocation/suspension of MI308 licences; a Taiwan quarantine or shipping disruption; formal BIS/DOJ diversion investigation naming AMD; or China adding AMD to an unreliable-entity list. **What would move it to Medium:** permanent removal of China from the model with no offsetting licence revenue, plus TSMC Arizona capacity for leading-edge AMD parts.
