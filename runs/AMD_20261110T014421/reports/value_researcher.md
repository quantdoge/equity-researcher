# AMD — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $508.58 | Market Cap: $830B | EV: ~$824B | Nov 9, 2026**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW**

AMD is a genuinely transformed business from its 2015 near-insolvency. But moat quality is mixed and segmented:

**Where the moat is real:**
- **x86 server CPU (EPYC)**: A structural duopoly with Intel. Switching costs are high in hyperscale servers (qualification cycles, software stacks, socket-level redesign). AMD has converted this from a challenger position into sustained share gains, evidenced by Data Center revenue of $8.5B in Q3'26 (+97% YoY).
- **Embedded / FPGA (Xilinx)**: The strongest moat in the portfolio — aerospace & defense, industrial, automotive and communications designs with 5–15 year qualification cycles and brutal switching costs. Growing 29% YoY at $1.1B/quarter with high margins. This is a real wide-moat *niche*, but only ~25% of revenue and its growth is the slowest of the three segments.

**Where the moat is thin:**
- **AI accelerators (Instinct MI450 / Helios)**: AMD is the credible #2, and customers (OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, Oracle) badly want a second source to escape NVIDIA lock-in. That is **demand pull, not a moat**. AMD wins because customers want competition — which structurally caps its pricing power. The decisive evidence: non-GAAP gross margin 56% versus NVIDIA's ~70%+ for functionally comparable accelerators. That 1,400bp gap *is* the CUDA software moat, and ROCm 10 does not close it. AMD is also entirely dependent on TSMC for leading-edge capacity — a shared dependency, not a proprietary advantage.
- **Semi-custom gaming**: Structurally declining — Gaming revenue fell 40% YoY to $773M. The moat was real but the moat is shrinking.

**Has the moat widened over 5 years?** Yes in CPUs and embedded, but the business mix has shifted *toward* the segment where its advantage is weakest. That is a net negative for moat durability, even as revenue explodes.

**The ROIC problem.** Reported ROIC of **5.5%** is far below WACC (~9–10%) — the hallmark of an un-moated business. To be fair, this is distorted by **$41.8B of goodwill and intangibles (66% of $63B equity)** from the $49B Xilinx deal. Ex-goodwill, invested capital is ~$15B against ~$3.1B NOPAT → **ROIC ~20%**, which is genuinely good. But the honest read is that AMD paid a full price for Xilinx and has spent four years growing into it. Goodwill impairment risk is non-trivial if embedded growth stalls.

AMD trades at roughly **2x the multiple of NVIDIA** — the dominant, more profitable, faster-growing incumbent with the entrenched software moat — and at a **426% premium to the sector peer median P/E of 36.5x**. That is only defensible if AMD takes enormous durable share from NVIDIA.

**Owner-earnings DCF.** Starting from ~$8.0B of FY2026 owner earnings (TTM net income $7.62B + D&A $3.05B − maintenance capex ~$1.0B − SBC $1.6B), 9% WACC, 3% terminal growth:

|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 25% | 10% | **~$280** |
| Base | 30% | 12% | **~$333** |
| Bull | 35% | 15% | **~$441** |
| Super-bull | 40% | 18% | **~$583** |

Using the tool's built-in 29.3% historical growth assumption over 10 years, intrinsic value is **$780B, or $477/share** — *below today's price* despite assumptions I consider too generous.

**The implied expectation.** To justify $508 today you must believe AMD compounds free cash flow at **~40% annually for five years and ~18% for the following five** — i.e., that AMD roughly doubles its revenue twice over and holds peak-cycle margins throughout. And at a 2.49 beta, CAPM would put AMD's cost of equity near 16%, not 9%; at a 10% WACC even my bull case drops to ~$400.

**Current price vs. intrinsic value: a ~30% *premium* to my $390 midpoint.** A 25% margin of safety would require a price near **$290**. "A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy" — this trades well above fair.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Excellent operator, improving-but-concerning allocator**

**Lisa Su is one of the finest CEOs in semiconductors.** The 2016–2026 turnaround — from ~$4/share and near-bankruptcy to $830B — came from disciplined roadmap execution (Zen, EPYC, Instinct) rather than financial engineering. She called the data center pivot years before the market did. Jean Hu is a credible, conservative CFO.

**Capital allocation is the soft spot:**
- The **$49B Xilinx acquisition** was strategically logical (embedded + adaptive computing) but struck at the top of the cycle, and four years on it has produced mostly goodwill rather than incremental returns. Retained earnings were just $723M by FY2023.
- **M&A pace is accelerating at precisely the wrong time**: ZT Systems, plus announced deals for **World Labs and Taalas** — buying AI startups at peak AI valuations. This is the early signature of empire-building, and history says semiconductor roll-ups at cycle peaks destroy value.
- **The OpenAI warrant is a genuine governance flag**: up to **160 million shares (~10% of the company)** vesting on GPU purchase volumes *and share-price milestones*. This converts a customer relationship into a dilution liability, and it is circular financing — AMD effectively funds its own revenue growth with equity. Shareholders bear the cost; the revenue is round-tripped.
- **Insider activity**: ~22 sales and one gift in September 2026; Su sold 95,000 shares (~$48M) in September, $57.6M in June, 85,000 shares in March. All under Rule 10b5-1 plans and diversification-consistent — not alarming, but notably there is **no open-market insider buying at these levels**.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (Avoid / Trim)

This is a **wonderful business and a poor investment at this price**. The framework's discipline is explicit: price matters more than quality.

**The case against owning it here:**
1. **No margin of safety — the opposite.** Price is ~30% above my $390 midpoint intrinsic value and 110x trailing GAAP earnings.
2. **The price already embeds a super-bull outcome.** Even 40% FCF growth for five years barely clears today's quote. There is no scenario short of permanent AI dominance that generates attractive forward returns from $508.
3. **Cyclical peak multiples.** 17x EV/sales and 73x EV/EBITDA at the apex of an AI capex boom, in a business where Gaming is already down 40% YoY — a reminder that AMD's end markets do turn.
4. **2x NVIDIA's multiple for a weaker moat.** You are paying more for the challenger than for the champion, with lower gross margins and a software ecosystem that remains the industry's weakest link.
5. **Negligible shareholder yield.** No dividend, buybacks that only offset SBC, and a 10% warrant overhang to a single customer.

**What would make me a buyer:** a price near **$280–$320** (a true 25% margin of safety), or evidence that gross margins are sustainably crossing 60% — which would prove real pricing power against NVIDIA rather than merely benefiting from customers' desire for a second source.

**What would change my *quality* view (not just price):** three consecutive quarters of gross-margin expansion *with* Data Center share gains, ROCm adoption metrics that show CUDA displacement, and a halt to large AI M&A.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to owning it (bear case — supports the Sell):**
- **AI capex digestion.** A pause in hyperscaler spending would hit a stock priced for 40% perpetual growth; at 73x EV/EBITDA, a single guidance miss can compress the multiple by half. The Oct 30–Nov 5, 2026 drawdown from $578 to $404 (−30% in four sessions) is a preview of this fragility.
- **Customer concentration and circularity.** A handful of hyperscalers plus OpenAI drive the growth; the warrant structure means AMD's revenue and its dilution are linked to the same counterparties.
- **Competitive response.** NVIDIA has ~6x AMD's R&D scale and can price defensively or extend CUDA lock-in; a price war would hit AMD first given its thinner margins.
- **Inventory and rackscale execution.** $7.9B of inventory and a pivot to lower-margin rack-scale systems raise obsolescence and margin-mix risk.
- **Goodwill impairment.** $41.8B of goodwill (66% of equity) hangs on embedded and datacenter cash flows.

**Risks to my Sell call (what would make me wrong):**
- AMD genuinely captures 20–25% share of a $500B+ AI accelerator market with 60%+ gross margins, in which case intrinsic value could exceed $700 and today's price was cheap.
- A sustained supply agreement with a single mega-customer that converts the warrant dilution into far larger profits.
- Continued multiple expansion driven by index flows and momentum rather than fundamentals — the stock can stay expensive far longer than a valuation-driven manager can stay patient.

**Bottom line:** Own AMD's products, admire its CEO, and wait for its price. At $508 with a $390 intrinsic value and an implied 40% decade-long FCF CAGR, the risk/reward is inverted. **Sell, and revisit below $320.**
