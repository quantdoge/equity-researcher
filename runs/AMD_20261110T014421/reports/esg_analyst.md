# AMD — Full ESG Analysis
**Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.** | Semiconductors | US | Mkt cap ~$830bn | 31,000 FTE | CEO & Chair: Lisa T. Su

*Data sources: AMD 2025-26 Corporate Responsibility Report & ESG disclosure pages, Climate Transition Plan, Q3-26 Form 10-Q filing, SEC 8-K/proxy-related disclosures, third-party ratings and news. | Rank | Factor | Why it is financially material for AMD | Driver |

**Key insight:** AMD is a *fabless* designer. Its own operational footprint is immaterial; its ESG risk is almost entirely **value-chain (Scope 3) and regulatory (export-control)** in nature. ESG matters here through revenue access (perf/watt, China licences) rather than through direct cost.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW–MEDIUM**

**Positives**
- Scope 1+2 emissions ~46,605 tCO₂e (2023) vs 61,754 t baseline (2020) — ~24.5% cut; ~30% progress against a **50% reduction by 2030** goal (2020 baseline). Small absolute base.
- Supply-chain goals largely **met**: 100% of manufacturing suppliers have public GHG reduction targets (target 100%); 82% of manufacturing suppliers sourcing renewable energy (target 80%).
- **Product efficiency goal exceeded**: 38x energy-efficiency improvement for AI training/HPC vs 30x target; early progress on 20x rack-level efficiency goal by 2030 (est. 4x in 2026).
- Published **Climate Transition Plan**; discloses to CDP, TCFD, SASB, GRI; filed under California **AB-1305** climate disclosure framework.
- Fabless model → no owned fabs, no large combustion fleet, low environmental-controversy history (no spills/fines of note found).

**Negatives**
- **Supply-chain carbon intensity is behind pace**: −8% vs a −25% target for 2024–2030 — trajectory is roughly one-third of the required rate. This is the metric that matters, and it is the one lagging.
- Reliance on TSMC (Taiwan) concentrates both **physical climate risk (water stress, typhoon)** and geopolitical risk in one node.
- Absolute Scope 3 disclosure remains limited; AMD does not fully separate certain emissions categories — a transparency gap that will become a compliance issue under CSRD-style regimes and AB-1305.
- **Product-level energy demand** of AI accelerators attracts increasing regulatory/NGO attention (data-centre power, grid interconnection), a reputational tail-risk.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **LOW–MEDIUM**

- **Safety/labour:** asset-light, no manufacturing workforce → low injury-risk exposure; no material labour controversies found. Layoff/attrition news searches returned sector-wide noise, not AMD-specific workforce actions — a genuine positive (no restructuring-driven social controversy, unlike peers in 2025-26).
- **Talent:** 31,000 employees in a hyper-competitive AI-talent market. Retention is the real social risk; AMD's 2023 materiality refresh explicitly *raised* the importance of "workforce recruitment, retention and development" — management acknowledges it.
- **D&I:** CEO Lisa Su is the most prominent woman leading a major semiconductor company and was reported as the first woman to top AP/Equilar's CEO pay ranking — strong symbolic and practical D&I signal. However, granular workforce-representation data was not retrievable; treat as unverified.
- **Human rights/supply chain:** first Human Rights Saliency Assessment (2023); human-rights-in-supply-chain rose in materiality. Residual exposure to forced-labour risk in Asian assembly/test and to conflict-mineral/rare-earth sourcing — no active controversy identified, but a structurally under-controlled area.
- **Product security:** the 2024 **"Sinkclose"** CPU vulnerability (affecting EPYC/Ryzen) required microcode patches; no known exploitation. Managed competently, but it demonstrates firmware-level attack surface as a recurring product-safety risk. AMD elevated **responsible AI and product use** to a top-tier material issue — appropriate.
- **Pay equity / social licence:** ~$55m CEO compensation creates internal-equity optics risk (see G).

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Supporting the score**
- **One share, one vote** — single class of stock, no dual-class structure, no controlled-founder bloc. This is a meaningful advantage versus much of the US tech universe.
- **Majority-independent board** with a designated **Lead Independent Director (Nora M. Denzel)**, which mitigates the combined Chair/CEO structure.
- Active **board refreshment**: board expanded from 8 to 9 directors in Jan 2026 (appointment of KC McClure, ex-CFO of Salesforce), following retirements; committee reassignments in Aug 2026. Third-party data suggests ~7 of 9–11 directors independent.
- Independent committee structure, SOX 302/406 certifications clean in the Q3-26 10-Q; no restatements, no material weakness, no auditor issues identified.
- Executive pay is heavily **equity/performance-weighted** (2025: $1.29m salary, $3.13m bonus, ~$8.95m stock awards, total ~$55.2m) — aligned with TSR rather than cash.

**Detracting from the score**
- **Combined Chair/President/CEO** — Su holds all three. Lead independent director is a partial, not full, mitigation; AMD is an outlier among large-cap semis in not having separated the roles.
- **Executive pay magnitude**: ~$55m total and a **CEO Value Creation Equity Award with a $75m target value granted March 2026** — an unusually large single grant even for a $830bn company. If hurdles are relative-TSR and multi-year, alignment is defensible; the quantum nonetheless raises dilution and "pay-for-luck" concerns and invites say-on-pay dissent.
- **Board size (9) and relatively low turnover historically** limit the freshness of oversight for a company whose business model has changed radically (AI data centre).
- **China sales architecture is the biggest governance question.** Agreeing to remit **15% of MI308 China revenue to the US government** in exchange for export licences (confirmed by CEO commentary, Dec 2025) is a policy-dependent, discretionary revenue stream. It creates earnings-quality concerns, licence-revocation cliff risk, and elevated scrutiny of AMD's sales practices — amplified after the November 2026 NVIDIA leadership/spy scandal, where analysts flagged that AMD and Broadcom "could also be subject to greater scrutiny over their sales practices." No evidence of wrongdoing by AMD, but the compliance surface has widened.
- No poison pill or entrenchment devices identified.

## 5. ESG Controversies (with financial impact estimates)

| Incident | Status | Estimated financial impact |
|---|---|---|
| **China export controls on MI308 / 15% revenue remittance** | Live, ongoing | ~**$800m inventory/related charge** taken in 2025 when licences were withheld. Ongoing: 15% of licensed China AI-chip revenue diverted — if China AI revenue runs $1–2bn p.a., that is **$150–300m of gross profit leakage (~0.5–1.2% of revenue)**. China was historically ~20–25% of AMD revenue. Structural risk: licence can be revoked at policy discretion |
| **"Sales practices" scrutiny spillover from NVIDIA scandal** | Emerging (Nov 2026) | No quantified exposure; AMD stock *rose 4%* on the news. Risk is investigative/compliance cost and potential disclosure obligations — a tail risk, not a base case |
| **Sinkclose CPU vulnerability (2024)** | Remediated | Immaterial direct cost (microcode patches); no known exploitation; reputational only |
| **Antitrust/patent history (Intel)** | Legacy, closed | 2009 Intel settlement paid AMD $1.25bn — a historical *positive*, not a liability. No current material antitrust action identified |
| **Minor litigation (§1782 application, Oct 2026)** | Routine | Immaterial |

**Assessment:** AMD has no environmental or product-safety scandal and a clean social record. Its controversy profile is **geopolitical and regulatory, not operational** — unusual for the sector, and importantly, this is a risk the market already prices as "China policy risk" rather than as "ESG risk." That's double-counting risk, not unrecognised risk.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE (upper end — "Leader" on product/environment, "Average" on governance)**

**Evidence:** MSCI ESG Rating **AA** (as published on AMD's own disclosures page); alignment with GRI, SASB, TCFD, CDP and UN SDGs; product energy-efficiency goals exceeded; supply-chain renewable and GHG-target goals met.
**Why not Leader:** scope-3 intensity trajectory is behind plan (−8% vs −25% required pace); combined Chair/CEO; outsized CEO award; and an unresolved, discretionary China revenue architecture that no rating agency fully captures.

**Peer context** (Technology, by size): NVDA $4.3tn, MSFT $4.0tn, TSM $2.0tn, AVGO $1.5tn, AMD $0.83tn. On governance, AMD's **single-class share structure is a genuine advantage** versus dual-class peers; on environmental controversy, it is cleaner than most; on regulatory/political entanglement, it is more exposed than any of them because of the China licence arrangement.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (ESG is not a valuation driver; one regulatory factor is)

**Reasoning**
1. **Nothing in E or S is thesis-changing.** AMD's direct footprint is trivially small and its product-efficiency performance is an *asset* to the bull case (perf/watt is why Meta's Helios rackscale and frontier-model wins exist). There is no stranded-asset, remediation, or product-safety liability of consequence.
2. **Governance is above average, not exceptional.** One-share-one-vote and a lead independent director offset Chair/CEO duality; the $75m award and $55m pay are a dilution/optics irritant, not a thesis risk. No entrenchment devices.
3. **The one material ESG-adjacent item is the China export-control regime**, and it is genuinely material to cash flows: an $800m charge already incurred and a 15% revenue remittance on licensed sales. But this belongs in the **policy/geopolitical risk** section of the memo — it is already visible, already priced as headline risk, and management has a licence in hand as of Dec 2025.
4. **Therefore:** ESG neither adds nor subtracts from the investment case. It should **not** be used as a reason to upgrade (no "ESG leader" re-rating argument is defensible given scope-3 lag and pay governance) nor to exclude (MSCI AA, clean controversy record, no dual class).

**What would change the call**
- *To Negative:* revocation or tightening of China licences (revenue cliff); a formal sales-practices investigation; a material Scope 3/forced-labour supply-chain finding; say-on-pay failing on the $75m award.
- *To Positive:* separation of Chair/CEO; credible Scope 3 absolute reduction or SBTi validation of the supply-chain target; quantified customer preference for AMD on perf/watt explicitly tied to contract wins.

**Bottom line for the memo:** AMD is an **ESG-neutral holding** — clean on environment and social, decent-but-improvable on governance, with its only genuinely material ESG-adjacent exposure (China export licensing) better analysed under geopolitical risk. Treat ESG as a **monitoring item, not a valuation input**.
