# Macro Assessment — Advanced Micro Devices (AMD)

**As of 10 November 2026 | Price $508.58 | Market cap $830bn | Beta 2.49**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **TRANSITIONAL, tilting RISK-OFF**

The US is in a **reflation / "no-landing" regime that has forced the Fed back into a hiking cycle** — the first tightening turn in three years. Real GDP is accelerating (Q3-26 annualised ≈ +3.4%), unemployment is stable at 4.1%, and CPI has re-accelerated to **3.72% YoY in September** (3-month annualised ≈ **4.6%**). The Fed hiked on 16 September 2026 to 3.75–4.00% (IORB to 3.90%) and again in October (effective 3.88%); the September minutes and Governor Barr's guidance both point to **at least one more hike**.

Critically, the bond market's repricing has been **almost entirely in real yields, not breakevens**:
- 10Y TIPS real yield: 2.20% (30 Jun) → **2.95% (5 Oct)** → 2.63% (6 Nov)
- 10Y breakeven: 2.24% → 2.36% → **2.23%**

That is the single most important macro fact for AMD. Inflation expectations are *anchored* while the **discount rate applied to long-duration equities has risen ~75bp at the peak**. This is a term-premium/fiscal-supply shock, not an inflation-expectations shock — and it is precisely the kind of shock that de-rates high-multiple growth stocks.

Credit is the tell: **HY OAS widened from 2.65% (1 Sep) to 3.42% (6 Nov), +77bp**, though still historically tight. Equity volatility in AMD has run far ahead of credit — a classic sign that the stress is in *valuation*, not in *fundamentals*.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Positives**
- **Nominal growth + AI capex (strong positive).** Q3-26 Data Center revenue +97% YoY to $8.5bn. Corporate IT budgets and hyperscaler capex are nominal-GDP-linked and are *benefiting* from the reflation — this is AMD's dominant driver and swamps the macro drags in magnitude.
- **No refinancing risk.** AMD carries minimal net debt. Unlike REITs, utilities or levered growth names, higher credit spreads hit AMD only through the *discount rate*, never through solvency or covenant risk. This is the key mitigating structural fact.
- **Resilient labour market (neutral-positive).** 4.1% unemployment means no demand destruction in PC/enterprise; the consumer and SMB legs of Computing & Graphics hold up.

**Negatives**
- **Real yields / discount rate (strong negative — the primary macro headwind).** At ~16x sales and with a 2.49 beta, AMD is one of the longest-duration large caps in the market. A 50bp rise in the real discount rate is worth roughly **8–12% of fair value** mechanically. The Oct 2 → Nov 5 drawdown of **−36% ($633.91 → $404.40)** is the empirical demonstration: the earnings disappointment was the trigger, but a stock that had run 5.3x in eighteen months with zero valuation cushion supplied the amplification.
- **Section 232 semiconductor tariff (direct, material).** A **25% ad valorem tariff on certain advanced computing chips and derivative products took effect 15 January 2026**. AMD is fabless — leading-edge wafers come from TSMC Taiwan — so US-bound advanced silicon now carries a 25% duty, plus tariff exposure on derivative products (servers/accelerators containing those chips). Mitigants are TSMC Arizona output and possible product-scope exemptions, but this is a **structural COGS and gross-margin drag** that consensus may still under-model, and it is a *policy* risk that can escalate by proclamation at any time.
- **USD strength (negative).** Broad dollar +3.4% in two months. Roughly half of AMD's revenue is booked outside the US, with China the largest single overseas market (~a quarter of sales). A firming dollar is a translation headwind and a pricing headwind versus Asian competitors.
- **Input-cost inflation (negative).** 4%+ PCE deflator with tight leading-edge capacity means TSMC wafer price increases, HBM/DRAM cost inflation and advanced-packaging scarcity. AMD's pricing power in accelerators is real but not unlimited against NVDA.
- **China / export controls (negative, episodic).** The Nvidia sales-practice scrutiny has been read as a relative positive for AMD/AVGO, but it raises the probability of tighter enforcement across the whole accelerator complex.

---

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The macro backdrop is a **triple squeeze on a maximum-duration asset**: rising real yields (+75bp), a firming dollar (+3.4%), and a 25% tariff on its core imported product — all while the Fed is *still hiking* and the long end is unstable. Strong nominal growth and tight absolute credit spreads prevent this from being "severe," and AMD's net-cash balance sheet removes the tail risk. But on a relative basis, AMD is close to the worst possible macro archetype: high multiple, high beta, import-dependent, and ~50% non-US revenue.

I would upgrade to Neutral on any one of: two consecutive core inflation prints below 0.2% MoM, a 10Y break back below 4.50%, or a formal Section 232 exemption for advanced logic.

## 5. Surprise Scenario (bear, and underpriced)

**The long end breaks 5.50% before year-end and the Fed delivers two more hikes.**

The mechanism: September CPI at 0.65% MoM and 4.6% annualised forces the December and January FOMC to move to 4.25–4.50%; simultaneously, heavy Treasury supply pushes the term premium up, taking the 10Y through the 5.31% October high to 5.50%+ and the 30Y above 5.75%. Because breakevens are already anchored at 2.23%, *every basis point of that move lands directly on the real discount rate.*

**Transmission to AMD:** earnings estimates stay intact — AI capex is contracted and backlogged — but the multiple compresses anyway. A 100bp real-yield shock from 2.63% back toward 3.60% is worth **20–30% of fair value** on a 2.49-beta, 16x-sales name. That means AMD revisits the **$350–400** zone *on unchanged fundamentals*, with HY OAS widening through 4.25% as the confirming signal and the AI-capex narrative itself coming into question as hyperscaler financing costs rise.

The tell to watch: if the 10Y makes a new high while AMD's earnings revisions stay positive and the stock *fails to make a new high*, the de-rating is a discount-rate event and the drawdown will be deeper and longer than the November V-shaped recovery implies.
