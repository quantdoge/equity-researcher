### 1. Consumer Demand Signals — **Deteriorating**

| Google Trends (US, 12-m, 0–100) | 12-m peak | Nov 2025 | Current (Nov 1) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "AMD Ryzen" | 100 (Apr 12) | 20 | **12** | **−40% y/y; −79% vs Q2 avg** |
| "AMD EPYC" | 100 (May 24) | 38 | **17** | **−55% y/y; −76% vs Q2 avg** |
| "AMD stock" | 100 (May 3) | 53 | **62** | **+17% y/y; ~2x Aug–Oct base** |

**The key divergence:** product-brand search interest (Ryzen, EPYC) collapsed from an April–May 2026 peak to 12-month lows by Aug–Oct and has *not* recovered — while *financial* search interest ("AMD stock") has re-accelerated sharply into the print. The attention AMD is attracting right now is **trader attention, not buyer attention.** That is a narrative-driven setup, not a demand-driven one.

Caveat on causality: the Apr–May spike has the signature of a product-launch/news cycle, so part of the decay is launch normalization, not demand loss. But the y/y comparison to a clean Nov 2025 baseline (−40% Ryzen, −55% EPYC) is unambiguous deceleration.

**Web traffic:** amd.com visits **−19.9% m/m** (SimilarWeb). Low weight — amd.com is a corporate/info site and consumer purchases route through Newegg/Amazon/Micro Center — but directionally consistent with the Ryzen decay. **Caveat: the reading is undated, so it may be 1–2 months stale.**

**Notable:** "AMD Instinct MI300" Trends data is unusable — heavily zero-inflated (interleaved 0s and 100s), no valid signal.

### 4. News Sentiment — **0.87 (Positive) — but treat as inflated**

20 articles/14 days, 14 positive vs 1 negative. **This score is not trustworthy as a contrarian indicator**: the article set is almost entirely from a single session (Nov 9), dominated by the +13% rebound, the Oppenheimer upgrade to Outperform with a $600 PT, and the Google Cloud EPYC win.

The *substantive* dispersion beneath the headline score is the real signal:
- **Bull:** Buy, $620 PT (server CPU TAM quadrupling to $220B by 2030); Buy, $705–765 fair value (Helios ramp, ~100% server growth in 2027).
- **Bear:** *"The Party Is Ending"* — **free cash flow collapsed to just 4% of revenue**, inventory build, potential dilution from AI-partnership warrants. This is the single most important non-consensus item in the flow.
- **Rebuttal:** inventory build = aggressive supply lock-in ahead of demand, not weakening fundamentals. Unresolved.

Narrative whipsaw is extreme: **−28% peak-to-trough, −17% on Nov 5, +13% on Nov 9.**

### 6. Overall Alt Data Signal: **NEUTRAL** (bullish DC fundamentals / bearish consumer + FCF quality)

The non-consensus insight from alt data is the **split**: consensus is entirely fixated on AI data-center momentum, which alt data corroborates (100% beat rate, DC +97%, AI-infrastructure hiring, Google Cloud design win). Meanwhile **every consumer-facing proxy is at a 12-month low** and no one is discussing it. That is a modest drag on the Client segment, and — combined with FCF at 4% of revenue and a +27% sequential revenue bar — it argues that the *risk* into Feb 2 is to the revenue line and to cash conversion, not to the EPS headline.

**Actionable framing:** the setup is a stock priced on a narrative that is currently being fed by traders rather than customers. Not a short — the beat cadence is too reliable — but the 100% beat rate is fully known and the search-interest divergence suggests upside surprise capacity is narrowing.

### 7. Confidence Level: **MEDIUM-LOW**

**Data quality caveats:**
- Google Trends is a weak proxy for a hyperscaler-procured B2B business — DC demand simply doesn't show up in search. Treat Ryzen/EPYC decay as a **Client-segment** signal only.
- Patents unavailable (2 failed attempts); job postings qualitative with no count trend; web traffic undated and a poor proxy for a corporate site.
- News sentiment is keyword-counted and single-day clustered — mechanically inflated.
- Social data is anecdotal, not a scored panel.
- Google Trends is weekly and lags 3–5 days; SimilarWeb lags ~30–60 days.

**Bottom line:** High confidence in the *fundamental* momentum read (it's confirmed by reported numbers). Low confidence in the *consumer* decay read as a revenue predictor. The most valuable output here is the attention-quality divergence — financial search interest at 2x baseline while product search interest sits at 12-month lows — and the FCF/inventory debate that the sentiment score is masking.
