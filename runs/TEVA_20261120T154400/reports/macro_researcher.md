# Macroeconomic Assessment — Teva Pharmaceutical Industries (TEVA)

**As of 20 November 2026 | Price $39.23 | Market cap ~$45.9bn | Beta 0.84**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional, tilting risk-off — a "re-acceleration, not recession" regime.**

The defining feature of this cycle is that the disinflation trade is over. The Fed cut from 4.33% (mid-2025) to a 3.63% trough, held there from January through August 2026, and has now **resumed hiking** — 3.75% in September and 3.88% in October 2026. This is not a cutting cycle; it is a stalled easing cycle that has reversed.

The trigger is visible in the CPI path: YoY inflation bottomed around 2.4% in February 2026 (327.46 vs 319.68) and has re-accelerated to **3.82% in October 2026**, with the sequential prints running hot (+0.64% m/m in September, +0.40% in August, +0.35% in October). Meanwhile growth is *not* decelerating — Q3 2026 real GDP came in at **3.4%** (Q2 2.2%, Q1 2.5%), and unemployment has *fallen* to **4.1%** from 4.5% a year ago. Strong growth plus sticky inflation plus a hiking Fed is the least friendly combination for long-duration, leveraged equities.

The bond market has already voted. The 10Y Treasury yield has risen from 4.48% on 1 July 2026 to **5.13%** today — **+65bp in under five months** — and the 30Y sits at 5.47%. The curve is now positively sloped (2s10s +39bp), but this is **bear steepening**, not a healthy re-normalisation: it reflects rising term premium and fiscal supply concerns, not easier policy. The dollar has moved with it — the broad USD index is up ~3.9% since 10 September (118.08 → 122.65), and EUR/USD has fallen ~5% over the same window (1.1629 → 1.1034).

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

TEVA is a **global generics and specialty pharma manufacturer** with roughly 55% of revenue in North America, ~25% in Europe and ~20% in international markets, an Israeli cost base, ~$16.6bn gross debt ($12.9bn net as of Q2 2026), and a beta of 0.84. That combination makes it simultaneously **rate-sensitive, FX-sensitive and policy-sensitive** — but with genuinely defensive revenue.

### 3a. Interest rates — NEGATIVE (the dominant channel)

- **Discount rate:** At 5.13% on the 10Y and rising, the present value of TEVA's terminal-value-heavy pipeline (LAI schizophrenia franchise, AUSTEDO, AJOVY, biosimilars) is being compressed. This is a duration effect on the equity, not a cash-flow effect.
- **Cost of debt:** TEVA refinanced **~$4.9bn of debt in Q3 2026** — precisely as the 10Y moved into the 5.1–5.3% zone — and repaid ~$1.8bn in October 2026. The **positive** is that it removed near-term refinancing risk and the Fitch/Moody's/S&P upgrades to investment grade (BBB-) tightened spreads. The **negative** is that a large slug of legacy low-coupon debt has been reset at cycle-high rates, so **interest expense steps up in 2027–2028** even as headline debt falls. Debt reduction remains the primary use of free cash flow, which caps EPS accretion.
- **Net read:** The IG upgrade materially de-risks the balance sheet, but the *refinancing tailwind of the 2025 easing cycle has been forfeited*. Rate sensitivity is now a headwind, not a tailwind.

### 3b. Inflation — NEGATIVE, and structurally so

This is the most under-appreciated point. TEVA does **not** have pricing power that tracks CPI. Its generics business is structurally deflationary: Q3 2026 global generics revenue fell **−16% YoY in local currency**, driven by lenalidomide generic competition. So the company is absorbing a 3.8% CPI environment in its cost base — manufacturing labour, energy, API and logistics — while its largest segment faces price *deflation*. The offset is mix: innovative brands grew 33% in local currency and non-GAAP gross margin expanded 345bp to 58.7%. That is excellent execution, but it is **company-specific margin mix, not macro pricing power** — it cannot be extrapolated if inflation stays at 3.8%+.

### 3c. Currency — NEGATIVE (near-term translation)

The dollar's 3.9% rally since September is a direct translation headwind. With ~45% of revenue outside the US, a 5% EUR/USD decline alone is worth roughly **1.0–1.3% of group revenue** on translation. Q3 2026 revenue was −2% YoY *in both dollars and local currency*, meaning FX was not yet a drag in the reported quarter — but the move happened *after* quarter-end (September–November), so the full translation hit lands in **Q4 2026 and Q1 2027**. Partial natural offset: TEVA's Israeli shekel cost base becomes cheaper in USD terms as the dollar strengthens, cushioning margins.

### 3d. Trade policy — NEUTRAL to modestly POSITIVE

Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs took effect 31 July 2026, but **generic pharmaceuticals, biosimilars and their ingredients are expressly excluded**, with a two-year duty-free window from 1 August 2026. That spares TEVA's core generics franchise — a meaningful relief versus the 100%-duty tail risk that was being priced in 2025. The offsetting negative is the **finalised Most-Favored-Nation pricing agreement** with the US administration, which carries a prospective pricing commitment on certain future innovative launches. That caps upside on the highest-margin growth engine. Net: tariff relief now, MFN pricing drag later.

### 3e. Growth — NEUTRAL to POSITIVE

3.4% GDP growth and 4.1% unemployment mean stable prescription volumes and no demand destruction. Healthcare is non-cyclical; TEVA's beta of 0.84 confirms it. A growth slowdown would barely touch volumes — but it would also not help, since the company's problem is competitive erosion in generics, not demand.

### 3f. Credit conditions — POSITIVE

Investment-grade status plus no drawn revolver gives TEVA access to capital markets at a spread that has narrowed even as base rates rose. In a tightening credit environment, that is a genuine relative advantage over leveraged generic peers.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL (with a clear negative tilt)**

TEVA's defensive revenue base, investment-grade balance sheet, generics tariff exemption and strong innovative-brand momentum offset the three macro headwinds — a 5.13% and rising 10Y, a 3.8% inflation rate it cannot pass through, and a 3.9% dollar rally. Nothing in the macro picture is *supportive*: there is no rate cut, no disinflation and no FX tailwind on the horizon. But nothing is existential either — the refinancing risk that dominated 2023–2025 has been retired. The macro backdrop is a mild drag on a fundamentally improving story, which is consistent with the stock's behaviour: up ~91% over twelve months (from $20.52 on 3 Nov 2025 to $39.23) but down from its $44.57 high as rates pushed back.

**Important caveat:** the −8.7% drop on 19 November ($42.23 → $38.54) was **idiosyncratic, not macro** — the announced departure of R&D head Eric Hughes. Do not attribute that move to the macro backdrop.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**Bear surprise — a term-premium / fiscal supply shock (probability: meaningful, underpriced).**

The market is treating the 10Y at 5.13% as a plateau. The more dangerous path: sticky 3.8% inflation plus heavy Treasury supply pushes the 30Y through 5.75% and the 10Y to 5.60–5.75% by Q1 2027. TEVA would be hit twice — the equity de-rates on the discount rate (a 100bp move on a terminal-value-heavy pharma franchise is worth roughly 10–15% of value), and the ~$2–4bn of remaining 2027–2028 maturities get refinanced at 6.5%+ rather than 5.5%. Against a $45.9bn market cap and $12.9bn net debt, that combination plausibly takes the shares back to the **low $30s** — unwinding most of the twelve-month re-rating while the underlying business keeps improving. The tell to watch is the 30Y auction tail and the 2s30s slope, not the Fed.

**Bull surprise (the mirror image):** the August–September inflation spike proves to be a tariff-driven goods-price artefact that reverses hard in Q1 2027. The Fed pivots back to cuts, the 10Y falls to 4.2%, the dollar gives back its 3.9% rally, and TEVA gets both a multiple re-rating *and* an FX translation tailwind on top of 33% innovative-brand growth — a plausible path to **$50+**.
