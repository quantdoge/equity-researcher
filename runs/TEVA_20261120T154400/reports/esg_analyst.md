## TEVA — ESG ANALYSIS
**Teva Pharmaceutical Industries Ltd.** | Healthcare / Generics & Specialty Pharma | Israel (Tel Aviv), NYSE+TASE dual-listed | Mkt cap ~$45.9B | 32,008 employees | CEO Richard Francis

*Evidence base note: SEC filing-text tool returned no 10-K/20-F/DEF 14A for TEVA (foreign private issuer, data gap); I substituted SEC-filed proxy excerpts, the Q1-26 10-Q provision figure, company sustainability disclosures, and press/AG-office releases. Self-reported ESG data is cross-checked against litigation outcomes wherever possible.*

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives (verified against SLB targets):** Scope 1+2 GHG **−39% vs 2019** (target −25%); Scope 3 **−32% vs 2020** (target −25%), both ahead of schedule; **60% renewable electricity**; net-zero-across-value-chain commitment (2045) and 100% renewable ambition; **73% of applicable sites** at safe antimicrobial discharge levels; CDP Water score improving.

**Concerns:** Scope 3 (~90% of footprint) is supplier-dominated and only **66% of significant suppliers by spend** have had sustainability evaluations — the largest unmeasured exposure. Absolute emissions remain meaningful given sterile/API manufacturing in Israel, Hungary, and Asia. Physical/geopolitical climate risk is real and not climate-related alone: a missile incident near the **Neot Hovav** site required Teva to clarify operations; Israeli site concentration is a physical-risk node.

**Verdict:** Medium — well-managed disclosure and genuine decarbonisation, but third-party supply chain and water/AMR are not yet controlled, and site geography adds physical risk.

---

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

This is Teva's weakest pillar, and it is not about D&I — it is about **product harm and labour disruption**.

- **Opioid and pricing conduct = social harm with a price tag.** The $4.25B opioid settlement (max cash over 13 years, plus ~$50M in donated medicines) and the price-fixing findings (Teva allegedly raised prices on ~112 generics and colluded with competitors) are the definition of social-externalities-turned-liabilities. Public-health credibility with state AGs, payers, and IDNs is impaired.
- **Access to medicines is the counterweight:** ~8M people reached via product donations, ~399,000 via health-system strengthening, $36.4B in generic savings across 21 countries. This is a genuine, quantifiable social licence — materially supportive of the generics franchise.
- **Labour:** ~8% of the workforce (~2,600 roles) to be cut under the restructuring targeting ~$700M savings by 2027; 250 Israel-based TAPI roles eliminated (over a third of that unit locally); ≥10% of Israeli staff called up for reserve duty. Execution risk, morale/talent attrition in the very R&D and commercial functions the Pivot to Growth depends on (note the Nov-2026 R&D leadership departure).
- **Board diversity:** 33% female directors — adequate, not leading.

**Verdict:** High — human-capital risk during a strategic pivot plus a documented history of consumer/patient harm.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Strengths:**
- **11 of 12 directors independent** under NYSE rules; **all committee members independent**.
- **Independent Chairman (Dr. Sol Barer)** — no CEO/Chairman duality.
- **Single class of shares** — no dual-class structure, no super-voting rights; one-share-one-vote alignment.
- Say-on-pay: **~90% of votes cast** approved updated CEO terms in 2025 — strong shareholder endorsement.
- Board refreshment: **7 new directors in five years**; Dr. David Brailer added as independent director effective Nov 3, 2026.
- Compliance infrastructure: **>99% annual compliance/ethics training completion**; **100% of third-party representatives** screened through the due-diligence tool.

**Why not 8–9:**
- **Staggered/classified three-year board terms** — entrenches the board and blunts activist or takeover accountability. A clear governance negative, especially post-2017 impairment era.
- **Compensation scale:** CEO total pay **$19.2M FY2025, +24% YoY, pay ratio 257:1** — granted while the company was cutting 8% of staff and paying antitrust penalties. Politically and optically problematic; watch for say-on-pay erosion.
- **Control-environment history:** a record $225M criminal antitrust penalty and a $450M FCA civil settlement are not one-off compliance slips — they evidence a multi-year failure of internal controls over competitive conduct. Remediation appears real (compliance spend, divestitures, training) but the track record caps the score.
- Israeli Companies Law gives minority-protective tender-offer mechanics, a modest positive.

**Aggregate:** Teva carries **$4,680M** of legal settlement provisions (accrued expenses + long-term liabilities, per Q1-2026 10-Q) — roughly **10% of market capitalisation**. That is a large but now largely *quantified* liability, which is materially better than an open-ended one.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US — FDA/cGMP:** highest-frequency, highest-severity regulator. Any Form 483/import alert is an immediate revenue event.
- **US — IRA Medicare drug negotiation:** *AUSTEDO* is exposed, with price effects from 2027 (management flagged IRA impact on Austedo in 2027 guidance). This is the single most consequential "S"-adjacent policy item for the growth thesis.
- **US — state AG antitrust and drug-pricing transparency laws:** the multistate action shows states are the primary conduct enforcer; continued scrutiny of generic pricing is structural.
- **EU — CSRD:** Teva's EU turnover comfortably exceeds the €150M threshold for non-EU parent groups, so it will be in scope (timeline pushed by the 2025 Omnibus simplifications, so likely FY2028/FY2029 reporting on FY2027/28). Compliance cost is modest; the real effect is that Scope 3, AMR discharge, and supplier audits become **assurance-grade** disclosures.
- **EU — CSDDD supply-chain due diligence:** relevant to API sourcing and human-rights/labour standards in Indian and other low-cost API supply chains. Teva's 66%-of-supplier-spend coverage is short of what CSDDD will demand.
- **CBAM:** immaterial — Teva is not an importer of CBAM-covered goods at scale; indirect exposure via packaging/chemical inputs is negligible.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** **~$7.5B of sustainability-linked bonds** (2021 and 2023 issuances, including the largest-ever SLB at the time) tied to climate **and access-to-medicines** KPIs. **All 2025 targets were met — no coupon step-up.** This is the standout governance feature: Teva has voluntarily put its cost of capital behind its ESG promises, and delivered. It also aligns with the **2026 return to investment grade**.
- **Israel:** environmental permitting and Ministry of Environmental Protection oversight at Neot Hovav/manufacturing sites; Israeli Companies Law minority protections.

---

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (disclosure trending Leader; conduct history Laggard)

Teva is a **bifurcated** ESG story. On **disclosure, climate execution, board independence and ESG-linked financing** it sits in the **top decile of its sector** — ranked top 10% of sector across most major benchmarks, improved scores on 5 of 6 priority ratings (Sustainalytics, MSCI, S&P Global, EcoVadis, CDP Water), TIME World's Most Sustainable Companies two years running, and it *hit every SLB target it set*. On **business conduct and product safety** it is a **documented laggard**: the largest antitrust cartel penalty in US history, a multi-billion opioid liability, and a repeating cGMP remediation cycle.

Net: not a Leader (conduct record disqualifies it), and clearly not an ESG Risk name (the liabilities are provisioned and governance is genuinely independent). **Average, with improving trajectory** — but the improvement is fragile because it depends on *absence* of new conduct findings.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**Why not Negative:**
1. The dominant ESG liabilities are **already provisioned and scheduled** — $4.68B, ~10% of market cap, paid out over years with no near-term maturity wall. The market has largely priced this. Tail risk is lower than the headline history suggests.
2. **ESG is accretive to cost of capital, not dilutive:** $7.5B of SLBs met with no step-up, plus the 2026 upgrade to investment grade. Teva is being *rewarded* by capital markets for ESG delivery.
3. **Access-to-medicines is a commercial asset**, not a cost — it underpins the generics franchise's relationships with governments and payers and is the main defence against pricing-policy hostility.
4. Nothing here impairs the Pivot to Growth: the 32% innovative-portfolio growth, the $6–7B 2030 innovative-revenue target, and the biosimilar expansion are unaffected by E, S, or G factors.

**Why not Positive:**
1. **Unquantified multistate settlement.** Terms of the Nov-16-2026 deal with 45 states, DC and two territories were not disclosed. Given Sandoz's $400M comparandum, a surprise charge of several hundred million dollars is a live estimate risk and is *not* clearly reflected in the existing provision.
2. **IRA is the binding "S" constraint** — AUSTEDO pricing from 2027 directly limits the highest-growth, highest-margin asset. This is a policy tax on the growth story that ESG analysis cannot offset.
3. **Restructuring vs. pivot execution:** cutting ~8% of staff, losing the R&D head, and eliminating a third of Israel-based TAPI roles while simultaneously trying to become "a high-velocity biopharma" is a human-capital contradiction. Watch attrition in R&D/commercial.
4. **cGMP tail risk is binary and site-specific** — a sterile-site import alert would hit revenue faster than any ESG score moves.

**Bottom line for the memo:** ESG does not change the investment case in either direction — it is a **bounded, disclosed, mostly-provisioned set of legacy liabilities attached to an improving operating story.** Positive ESG momentum (SLBs met, investment-grade regained, top-decile sector ratings) modestly supports the multiple; the residual risks to underwrite are (a) the undisclosed multistate price-fixing figure, (b) IRA impact on AUSTEDO, and (c) manufacturing-quality recurrence. **Rate Neutral; treat governance/conduct as a monitored risk, not a thesis driver.**
