## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NONE**

CoreWeave rents GPU capacity (largely NVIDIA) in purpose-built AI clusters, plus orchestration software. It is a capital-intensive asset lessor with a software veneer. Applying the five moat sources:

| Moat source | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Intangibles** | Weak. Brand matters at the margin ("ClusterMAX Platinum" 3x, top MLPerf inference scores), but there are no patents or licenses protecting the economics. |
| **Switching costs** | Moderate *contractually*, not technically. $132.5B backlog = multi-year commitments, but GPUs are fungible; the lock-in is scarcity of capacity, not product stickiness. |
| **Cost advantage** | Claimed operational speed in deploying large clusters; not demonstrable in the financials. |
| **Network effects** | None. |
| **Efficient scale** | None — hyperscalers (MSFT, GOOGL, AMZN, ORCL), neoclouds (Nebius, Crusoe), and NVIDIA itself are all adding capacity. |

**The decisive test — has the moat widened? No. It has narrowed, measurably:**

- Gross margin: **74.3% (2024) → 71.7% (2025) → 67.7% (Q3 2026)**
- Adjusted operating margin: **16% (Q3 2025) → 10% (Q3 2026)** despite revenue up 168%
- Revenue grew 42% sequentially in Q3 — and margins *fell*.

A business with pricing power does not deliver 25x revenue growth over three years while losing 660bps of gross margin. That is the signature of a commodity supplier racing to add capacity before rental rates deflate. Note also that Q3 revenue was flattered by customer **prepayments/deposits of ~$9B expected in the next three months** — growth is being funded by customers because the balance sheet alone cannot carry it.

**Circle-of-competence verdict:** I can understand this business. What I cannot do is underwrite the durability of GPU rental economics 5–10 years out, because the asset is a depreciating semiconductor whose replacement arrives every 18 months (Blackwell → Vera Rubin transition is already underway, with management citing 4.8x throughput gains on the new generation — which devalues the installed base). That is the definition of a business
