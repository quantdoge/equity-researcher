# Sector Brief: AI Cloud Infrastructure ("Neoclouds") — CoreWeave, Inc. (CRWV)

**Classification note:** FMP tags CRWV as *Technology / Software – Infrastructure*. That label is misleading — the `get_sector_peers` output returned Apple, Nvidia and Microsoft, which are not comparables. CoreWeave is an **AI infrastructure / GPU-cloud provider** ("neocloud"): it borrows money, buys NVIDIA GPUs, builds or leases powered data centres, and rents capacity on multi-year contracts. I have therefore benchmarked it against its actual comp set — Nebius (NBIS), IREN, Applied Digital (APLD) — plus the hyperscalers it both competes with and sells to (MSFT, ORCL).

---

## 1. Sector Overview

The neocloud sector is a capital-intensive intermediary layer sitting between NVIDIA and the AI labs/hyperscalers. Global hyperscaler capex is running at roughly **$700B in 2026** (CNBC), with J.P. Morgan projecting **$900B in 2026 and $5.5T cumulative by 2030**, and Goldman estimating total global AI investment above **$1T in 2026**. The directly addressable GPU-as-a-Service market is far smaller but compounding fast: **$37.4B in 2026 → $224B by 2032, a 34.8% CAGR** (MarketsandMarkets); AI infrastructure-as-a-service is ~$103B in 2026. Concentration is high but shifting — hyperscalers hold the majority of capacity, while CRWV has emerged as the largest pure-play neocloud at **$5.13B FY2025 revenue**, versus $0.53–0.71B for NBIS/IREN/APLD. Barriers to entry are capital, **power and permits** (not technology) — which is precisely why incumbents with energised shells and signed interconnection queues command a premium. Porter's Five Forces read **unfavourable**: supplier power from NVIDIA is extreme (sole-source high-end GPUs plus HBM shortage), buyer power is extreme (Microsoft is both anchor customer and competitor), and rivalry is intensifying as hyperscaler in-house silicon (TPU, Trainium, MTIA) becomes a credible substitute at the inference layer. The one offsetting force today is **temporary scarcity** — CRWV reports being sold out through 2027.

**Cycle position: late-acceleration / peak-capex.** The evidence is in the equity tape rather than the fundamentals. CRWV closed at **$58.33 on 12 Nov 2026, down 57.8% from its 6 May intraday high of $138.25 and down 51% in six months** — while revenue was growing 168%. That is multiple compression, not demand destruction. The market has begun discounting a 2027–28 digestion phase: Nebius posted a blowout quarter yet fell 14% on a cautious outlook and a larger capital budget, dragging CRWV down ~10% and IREN ~7–10% in sympathy. The sector has rotated from pricing *scarcity* to pricing *capacity glut risk*.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

1. **Hyperscaler capex is still accelerating, not levelling.** ~$700B in 2026 against $900B (JPM) and $1T+ global AI investment (Goldman). CRWV's **$132.5B backlog** — roughly **10x FY2026E revenue** — plus **$9B of imminent customer prepayments** means its growth is contractually underwritten, not speculative. Roughly **$11M of annual revenue per megawatt** gives a hard, calculable return on contracted power.
2. **Genuine near-term pricing power.** Capacity is sold out through 2027, and reports indicate neoclouds have been **raising NVIDIA GPU rental prices, including on four-year contracts** — a notable reversal from the 2024–25 price war. Q3'26 revenue of $3.66B (+168% YoY) came in on "strong pricing," per Zacks, and QoQ growth **re-accelerated to +42.2%** (vs +23.9% in Q2), which is the signature of a supply-constrained market.
3. **Power and permit scarcity is a durable moat.** The binding constraint is energised land, not GPUs. The Texas data-centre moratorium debate illustrates it: a one-year delay on 400MW costs ~$1.4B of revenue, but CRWV's Denton campus is largely insulated. Incumbents with signed power and shell capacity will capture the 2027–28 build regardless of who has the best chip access.

---

## 3. Key Headwinds

1. **Leverage and the depreciation mismatch — the defining risk.** Q3'26 interest expense was **$846M, or 23% of revenue** (up 3.2x from $268M in Q2'25). Annualised that is ~$3.4B against a ~$14.6B revenue run-rate. Weighted average cost of debt is **7.9%**, with $6.47B cash and $5B undrawn term loans. GPUs depreciate over 3–6 years while contracts run 4–6 years — a structural asset/liability duration mismatch that leaves little room for a demand air-pocket. Rising rates directly compress equity value.
2. **Severe customer concentration plus vertical conflict.** CRWV's anchor customer is Microsoft — a partner that is simultaneously building its own capacity and could in-source. News flow on 12 Nov noted "a late-breaking report about that partner turned the rally upside down within hours." The buyer is also the competitor; this is the weakest structural position in the value chain.
3. **Margin compression and overcapacity risk.** Gross margin has fallen from **74.3% (FY24) to 71.7% (FY25) to 67.7% (Q3'26)** as lower-margin, longer-duration hyperscaler contracts dilute the mix. Simultaneously, the whole group is asking "how much AI capacity is too much," and the NVIDIA Rubin transition raises obsolescence risk on the H100/H200 fleet that underpins current contracts.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (within a structurally challenged sector)**

**Rationale for "strong":**
- **Scale leadership** — $5.13B FY25 revenue vs $0.53–0.71B for NBIS/IREN/APLD; largest pure-play by a wide margin.
- **Best-in-class unit economics among peers** — 67.7% gross margin at scale and +5.7% operating margin in Q3'26, versus a peer median near −77%. Sequential margin *improvement* (Q1 −6.9% → Q2 −1.9% → Q3 +5.7%) shows operating leverage is arriving.
- **Contractual visibility** — $132.5B backlog, ~10x forward revenue, plus $9B imminent prepayments and sold-out capacity through 2027. No peer has comparable visibility.
- **Funding access** — $6.47B cash, $5B undrawn term loans, 7.9% weighted average cost of debt. It has already locked in capital at rates the smaller neoclouds cannot reach.

**Caveats that cap the rating:** the strength is *relative*. CRWV's absolute returns are still negative (net margin −19.4%), it carries the sector's heaviest debt load, and its anchor-customer relationship is structurally conflicted. Its competitive advantage rests on contracted power and NVIDIA allocation — both of which erode as hyperscalers build out.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

**Why not Attractive:** the growth is real and enormous (34.8% GPUaaS CAGR, ~$700B 2026 hyperscaler capex, sold-out capacity), but the sector's *returns on capital are poor to negative* across the board. Every pure-play in this set runs an operating loss except CRWV, and CRWV's positive figure is achieved only after $846M/quarter of interest and ~$2B/quarter of depreciation. Value is being transferred to NVIDIA (supplier) and to debt holders, not retained by the neoclouds. The Nov 2026 de-rating — CRWV −58% from peak, NBIS −30% in a month — is the market beginning to price this correctly.

**Why not Unattractive:** demand is contracted, not hoped-for. A $132.5B backlog and sold-out-through-2027 capacity mean near-term revenue is largely locked. Pricing power is currently positive. The barriers — power, permits, NVIDIA allocation — are real and rising. This is not a bubble in demand; it is a question of how the economics are split.

**Where in the cycle:** **late-acceleration, approaching peak capex.** The transition from "scarcity rents" to "capacity digestion" is the key 2027 risk. Within a Neutral sector, CRWV is the highest-quality operator and the most levered balance sheet — the best vehicle for the tailwind and the most exposed to the headwind.
