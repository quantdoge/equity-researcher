# Bloom Energy Corporation (BE) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**Price $200.40 | Market cap ~$59.2B | Beta 3.64 | 2,214 FTE | Industrials – Electrical Equipment**
*Assessment window: ~Nov 2025 – Nov 2026. Note on data gaps: the 10-K and DEF 14A full texts were not retrievable (tool returned "no filing found"; SEC.gov direct fetches 403'd), and EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned zero hits. Governance items below are sourced from Form 4s, the 2026 proxy summary, court/law-firm dockets, and press — they should be re-verified against the FY2025 10-K once accessible.*

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

**Concentration of power.** Founder K.R. Sridhar holds Chairman **and** CEO simultaneously — no independent chair. The company has a dual-class structure (Class B = 10 votes/share), making it a controlled company exempt from majority-independent board requirements. This is the single largest governance discount in the name.

**Shareholder dissent is measurable and material.** Prior **say-on-pay support was only ~62%** — a level that typically signals an activist onramp. The trigger was the large 2024 multi-year equity package for Dr. Sridhar; the Compensation Committee then granted a *further* new long-term performance PSU award in 2026. Reported 2025 CEO pay of $3.5M (36:1 ratio) understates the economics because equity is valued at grant, and the grant was deliberately skipped in 2025. Pay structure is being smoothed around a contested mega-award rather than reset in response to the 62% vote.

**Financial leadership churn — a control-environment flag.** The 2026 proxy names Daniel Berenbaum as *former* CFO, with Maciej Kurzymski serving as Chief Accounting Officer and **Acting Principal Financial Officer** for a period, before Simon Stephen Edwards was appointed CFO in late August 2026. CFO vacancy/CAO-as-acting-PFO at a company guiding to $4.4–4.6B revenue, in the middle of a disclosure controversy, is a genuine internal-controls warning sign.

**Board composition.** Directors include John Chambers and Jeffrey Immelt (former CEOs of Cisco and GE) — strong operating pedigree, but both were among the heaviest *sellers* of stock in 2026 (below). Audit/financial-expert depth on the board is not evidenced in available data.

**Transparency friction.** A Delaware Chancery action (*Jacob v. Bloom Energy*) resulted in the court ordering Bloom to respond to a stockholder's **§220 books-and-records inspection demand**. Courts rarely compel production absent a credible purpose — this is a direct signal that investors cannot get answers through normal channels.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **ELEVATED**

- **Active securities class action (filed 2026).** Class period **Feb 27, 2025 – July 8, 2026**, over statements that Bloom was "not dependent on China" for scandium and related disclosure. Lead-plaintiff deadline **Sept 28, 2026**; multiple firms (Rosen, Levi & Korsinsky, Kessler Topaz, Kaplan Fox, Glancy Prongay) are competing, plus **derivative-demand investigations** (Bronstein Gewirtz; standing for holders since Feb 27, 2026). No 10b-5 settlement reserve is disclosed in available data.
- **No SEC/DOJ enforcement action identified** in EDGAR full-text search — a genuine mitigant, but the §220 order and derivative demands often *precede* regulatory attention.
- **Permitting / environmental enforcement.** The flagship Oracle **Project Jupiter** (New Mexico) site still lacks an approved air permit; regulators and a local environmental group have specifically contested **Bloom's emissions calculations** (NOx/GHG). Oracle cancelled the on-site gas plant in May 2026 and later promised 35% lower emissions. Precedent exists: Delaware's DNREC issued a **$40,000 penalty and Secretary's Order** (2019) to the Bloom-owned Diamond State Generation project for air-permit violations.
- **Export controls / geopolitical regulatory exposure.** Beijing requires an export licence for *every* scandium shipment. Any de minimis Chinese content in the electrolyte makes Bloom a downstream subject of Chinese export-control policy — effectively a foreign regulator holding a kill switch over US data-center power contracts.
- **Legislative dependence.** The OBBBA modified the 45V hydrogen credit but preserved/extended fuel-cell investment tax credits. Bloom's economics and much of its "backlog" are credit-sensitive; a future Congress or midterm shift can reprice the entire demand curve.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Single-point-of-failure raw material with an adversarial supplier state.** Scandium is essential to stack performance and life; Bloom's own patents link insufficient scandium to accelerated cell aging, which undermines the 10–15 year service contracts underpinning revenue. Hunterbrook's trade-data work traces ~20 tonnes of scandium-bearing electrolyte membranes re-entering Oakland from a Thai subsidiary of China's Three Circle/Glory Winner in Sept–Oct 2026 — after a nine-month gap — on bills of lading with **redacted shipper/consignee names** but matching "BE PALLET" markings. Bloom publicly claims supply supports up to 25 GW without China. If the reports are accurate, the gap between public statement and shipping record is a disclosure problem *and* a supply continuity problem. A near-term supply model also suggests global scandium supply (~240t) versus Bloom's implied need (~220t at 5GW) does not clear.
- **Backlog quality and counterparty circularity.** The marketed **>$20B "backlog" is unaudited**, versus **RPO of $492.6M** (2.5% of backlog) — a >40x gap where peers run ~2x. It reportedly includes non-binding servicing revenue and un-granted tax credits. **74% of Q4'25 revenue ($574M of $778M) came from JVs Bloom part-owns with Brookfield**, with footnotes conceding the customer "may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user." Counterparty concentration in a financier that buys your boxes is a classic revenue-quality risk.
- **Flagship project execution and cash conversion.** Oracle's Project Jupiter has no air permit and no gas pipeline (SemiAnalysis reportedly pushed first power 2027→2029); AEP's $2.65B order slipped from end-2028 to "no later than 2030," backstopped by an unnamed hyperscaler with a cost-plus walk-away. Meanwhile **Q3'26 free cash flow was –$181M on $153M capex** despite record revenue — growth is being funded by working capital and customer deposits. Technology/manufacturing scale-up to multi-GW, plus key-person dependence on a founder-CEO who is also the technical visionary, completes the picture.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

Bloom has now been the subject of **serial short-seller and investigative attacks across two separate cycles**: Hindenburg (2019, servicing liabilities) and Hunterbrook (July 2026 "Bloom's Big Lie"; October 2026 follow-up), plus Crossroads Capital. The current allegation set is the damaging kind — not a valuation dispute but an accusation that the CEO repeated a specific factual claim ("no China supply chain") at least five times to investors, podcasts, and on stage with the WSJ, while trade data shows otherwise. That narrative is sticky and it directly implicates the founder's credibility.

Secondary reputational exposures: the **Delaware ratepayer story** (20-year obligated above-market contracts costing over 5x original projections, jobs 600 short) is the sort of local-press fact pattern that resurfaces in every permitting hearing; environmental-justice opposition in New Mexico is organized and litigious; and the stock's status as the **best-performing S&P 500 component** (added Sept 2026) at ~140x trailing earnings makes it a permanent media piñata. Short interest is ~6.0–6.2% of float (17.19M shares, down 18% MoM) — a large, well-researched, adversarial audience that will test every disclosure.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Strong operating momentum (Q3'26 revenue +138% to $1.24B, product GM 38.4%, guidance raised three times) is real, and no regulator has yet acted. But this is a company where **the growth narrative, the disclosure record, and the insider behaviour are pulling in different directions simultaneously**: a controlled founder-led board with 62% say-on-pay support, CFO/CAO turnover, a court-ordered books-and-records production, an active securities class action, a critical input sourced (at least partly) from a jurisdiction with an export off-switch, a flagship backlog anchored on two slipping projects and a related-party financier, and management plus the entire board selling stock with zero buys. Any one of these is manageable. The correlation among them is the problem: a single adverse disclosure event on scandium or on backlog conversion would validate several at once. Beta of 3.64 and a ~30% drawdown from the autumn peak show the market has begun pricing exactly that.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Credibility/whistleblower-grade disclosure conflict.** Company states it is "not dependent on China" for scandium (stated ≥5 times since Feb 2025); contemporaneous bills of lading with redacted counterparties, "BE PALLET" markings, and Thai-depot records point to resumed China-linked inflows. **Escalate:** request the Audit Committee's supply-chain verification memo; treat any restatement or 8-K correction as a negative catalyst. Watch for SEC informal inquiry or DOJ referral.
2. **Backlog/RPO asymmetry (>40x vs ~2x peers) and related-party revenue (74% of Q4'25 from Brookfield JVs).** Priority-1 revenue-quality diligence item; escalate to the accounting-forensics desk regardless of the short report's provenance.
3. **Court-ordered §220 books-and-records production (*Jacob v. Bloom*)** — evidence shareholders were stonewalled; typically precedes derivative litigation and can surface internal communications.
4. **CFO/principal-financial-officer churn immediately prior to a disclosure controversy** — controls and disclosure-committee effectiveness must be assumed impaired until demonstrated otherwise.
5. **Board-level selling with no buys** (Chambers ~$25M, Immelt $7.2M) and a discretionary sale without a 10b5-1 plan. Directors with no operational role liquidating near all-time highs is a governance signal, not a diversification story.
6. **62% say-on-pay + new mega-PSU grant** — proxy-fight and activist vulnerability; watch the 2027 proxy and any "against" vote on the compensation committee.
7. **Permitting dependency (Oracle Jupiter air permit; Bloom's emissions calculations contested)** — a permit denial would remove a material share of backlog and constitute a public, citable negative event.

**Suggested monitoring triggers:** any 8-K disclosing SEC/DOJ contact, an air-permit ruling in New Mexico, a 10-Q disclosure of a litigation reserve or RPO/backlog reconciliation, insider Form 4 purchases (would be a strong positive signal), and 13F/13D activity.
