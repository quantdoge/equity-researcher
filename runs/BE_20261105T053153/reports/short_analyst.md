## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **LOW** (deteriorating sharply; not fraud-flagged)

Bloom has just crossed into GAAP profitability and the reported numbers look spectacular — Q3'26 revenue +138%, gross margin 33.8% (+453bp), GAAP operating margin 18.2%. But the earnings quality **as of the most recent quarter is poor and getting worse**, which is the exact opposite of what the income statement implies.

| | FY2025 | 9M 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Net income | $(88.4)M | **+$517.2M** |
| Operating cash flow | $113.9M | **$272.9M** |
| Free cash flow | $57.2M | **$41.7M** |
| Cash conversion (OCF/NI) | n.m. | **53%** |
| FCF conversion (FCF/NI) | n.m. | **8%** |

**Q3 2026 alone: net income +$250.3M, operating cash flow −$27.1M, FCF −$180.6M.** Cash conversion of −0.11x on the best quarter in company history. The composite earnings-quality score is **0/100**.

The accruals bridge is the tell: $244M of 9M'26 net income did not convert to cash, a $356M single-quarter working-capital drag, and capex tripled to $153M in Q3.

**The Q4-loading pattern is more damning than the level.** FY2025 operating cash flow was $113.9M — but Q4'25 alone produced **+$421.4M**, implying the first nine months of 2025 ran **−$307.5M**. A >$700M swing in 90 days is not operations; it is year-end receivables collection, payables stretching, and customer prepayment/deposit timing. **Bloom's annual cash flow number is engineered in December.** Investors extrapolating "$57M FCF → $1B FCF" from the FY25 print are extrapolating a calendar artifact.

**Honest counterpoint I must concede:** that is where the manipulation signals *stop*. Q3'26 receivables fell sequentially from $891.9M to $634.7M *while revenue rose 16%* — DSO collapsed from ~120–138 days in 2025 to ~47 days now. Beneish **DSRI = 0.834** confirms receivables days are *falling*. Inventory days fell 186 → 163. There is **no channel-stuffing signature** in the standard forensic tests.

## 2. Key Red Flags

1. **Related-party revenue is now the core of the story.** Three customers were **43%, 13% and 12% of FY2025 revenue (68% total)** — and the 43% customer is a **related party through the Brookfield joint ventures**. Disclosed related-party revenue: **$892.0M, ~44.1% of FY2025 revenue.** Bloom's own footnotes concede these counterparties "may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user." This is the single most important fact in the file: the "customer demand" is substantially **Bloom's own financing partner buying Bloom's boxes**, with the ultimate end user unnamed.
2. **Unaudited $20B "backlog" vs audited RPO of $492.6M (as of 3/31/26) — a ~40x gap.** Among peers (GEV, Oracle, Microsoft, CoreWeave) the widest comparable backlog/RPO gap is ~2x. The backlog reportedly includes ongoing service revenue customers are not obligated to pay and tax credits not granted. A 40x gap between the marketing number and the auditor-reviewed number is the cleanest available measure of promotional inflation.
3. **CFO churn: four CFOs since the start of 2024**, including nearly a year with no permanent CFO — the same period the Brookfield JV structure was built.
4. **Project-level slippage after guidance raises.** Oracle's Project Jupiter (New Mexico) reportedly lacks an approved air permit and a gas pipeline; SemiAnalysis pushed first-power from 2027 to 2029. AEP's $2.65B order slipped from "end-2028" to "no later than 2030"; the campus reportedly lost developer Crusoe. Both slippages followed guidance increases.
5. **Dilution:** basic shares 230.2M (Q1'25) → **294.8M (Q3'26), +28% in six quarters**; diluted 324.3M. Another ~30M shares (10%) sit in the money.
6. **Interest expense fell 48% YoY ($14.4M → $7.5M) while gross debt rose 84%** — explained by the low/zero-coupon converts issued in Q4'25, not by capitalisation. **I checked this and it is not a flag**, but it confirms future dilution is structural.
7. **Unverified anomaly:** goodwill appears at $215.5M in Q2'26 and is zero in Q3'26. Likely a data artifact, but it should be reconciled to the filing before being relied on either way.

Anyone pitching Bloom as a balance-sheet short is wrong.

**Piotroski F-Score: 4 (weak).** Legitimate drivers only: no dilution avoided (F7=0), leverage up (F5=0), asset turnover down (F9=0). Growth-capex and convert-driven, not distress-driven.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment — **the moat is financing and speed, not technology**

- **Gross margin is expanding, not eroding** (14.8% FY23 → 29.0% FY25 → 33.8% Q3'26). Credit where due — that is genuine manufacturing leverage and pricing power. Moat-erosion-on-margin is *not* the bear case.
- **Scandium is a genuine single-point-of-failure.** China requires export licences on scandium and produces an estimated ~90% of supply. Bloom is, by its own CEO's admission, the **world's largest consumer**. Hunterbrook traced four China-linked supply routes despite repeated CEO statements of "no China supply chain." Global supply is ~240t against ~310t of demand, with Lockheed (F-35) competing for the same metal.
  - **Rebuttal I credit:** scandium is a by-product of titanium/nickel laterite mining, so supply is price-elastic; it is a small share of BOM; Bloom published a detailed rebuttal and the stock recovered 80% off the report low. This is a slow-burn constraint, not an off-switch tomorrow.
- **The real competitive threat is that the moat is a financing relationship.** Bloom's edge is speed-to-power for AI data centres. That edge is rented from Brookfield, whose own anchor tenant (Radiant) has no named CEO or disclosed customers. Meanwhile GE Vernova's turbine backlog, Caterpillar reciprocating engines, and grid interconnection queues are all compressing. When grid power arrives or turbine lead times shorten, Bloom's premium evaporates — and its ITC/PTC-dependent economics with it.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — the credible bear case

**Not "fraud." "Counterparty-financed demand plus a cash-flow mirage, priced as a secular compounder."**

- Bloom's 2026 earnings inflection is real but **8% of it converts to free cash**. Capex is now $153M/quarter and rising, working capital is a $356M/quarter drag, and the FY25 cash print was manufactured in Q4.
- 44% of FY2025 revenue came from a **related party Bloom co-owns**, where the ultimate end user is unnamed; 68% came from three customers. The two flagship projects behind the order book (Oracle Jupiter, AEP) have both slipped, post-guidance-raise.
- The **$20B backlog is unaudited and 40x the audited RPO**; peers run ~2x.
- **Valuation:** 14.7x 2026E EV/Sales, 9.1x 2027E, **63x 2026E EPS, 37x 2027E** — for a 34%-gross-margin equipment assembler. 2028 consensus EPS spans **$4.13 to $12.41** (3x dispersion); revenue $6.8B to $15.4B. That dispersion is an admission of no visibility.
- **Catalysts:** Q4'26 print (needs ~$1.4B revenue and a repeat of the year-end cash flog), any further Oracle/AEP permit or financing delay, a financing-market wobble for AI data centres, scandium export-licence tightening, and convert-driven dilution.
- **The tell:** Q3'26 beat consensus revenue by 15% and *raised guidance for the third time* — and the stock fell **19% in two sessions** ($238.53 → $193.30). Beat-and-raise followed by a −19% reaction is expectations exhaustion.

**The strongest bull arguments, stated fairly:**
1. Revenue +138% with GAAP operating margin 18.2% and expanding gross margin is genuine operating leverage — not an accounting construct.
2. **Negative accruals, Beneish −2.52, DSRI 0.834, falling inventory days.** The forensic models actively contradict a channel-stuffing or manipulation thesis.
3. Net debt of $295M against $2.49B cash; Altman distress is a book-equity artifact.
4. Third guidance raise, S&P 500 inclusion, and a stock already **−43% from its $351 June high**.
5. The Hunterbrook short has been public since July 8, 2026; the stock fell 36%, then rallied 80%. **The bear case is crowded and partially priced.**

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **SHORT** (not Strong Short)

**Position: modest short / put-spread structure, sized for a beta-3.6 squeeze.** Recommended sizing 50–60% of a normal short — this is a high-borrow-cost, index-inclusion-supported name with an 80% demonstrated bounce off the last short report.

The thesis is specific, documented and falsifiable, and it does not depend on alleging fraud — only on the observation that **a company reporting $780M of 2026 net income will likely produce roughly zero free cash flow, a large share of that income came from an entity it part-owns, and its order book is 40x its contractual obligations.** That is enough.

**What would kill the short and make me flip:** Q4'26 delivering >$1.2B of free cash flow *without* a Q4 working-capital lurch; RPO converging toward backlog in a 10-Q disclosure; related-party revenue falling below 20% with named third-party end users; or the Brookfield counterparties being replaced by direct hyperscaler contracts.

**Where I explicitly reject the bear case:** Altman "distress," a Beneish manipulation call, channel stuffing, and gross-margin erosion are all *not* supported by the data. Anyone shorting Bloom on those grounds is shorting the wrong thesis.

**Gaps I could not close:** audited 10-K text (fetch failed; tool reports no 10-K for this CIK), Q3'26 customer concentration (quarterly filings may not repeat it), whether receivables were factored to produce the DSO collapse, and the Q2'26 goodwill anomaly. Each should be reconciled before sizing up.
