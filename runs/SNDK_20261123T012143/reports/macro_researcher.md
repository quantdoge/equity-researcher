# Macroeconomic Assessment — SNDK (SanDisk Corporation)
**As of 20 November 2026 | Data: FRED, TrendForce, FOMC minutes (18 Nov 2026)**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional, tilting Risk-Off**

The US is in a *re-tightening* regime, not an easing one. The Fed cut from 4.33% (Jan 2025) to 3.63% by mid-2026, then **stopped and reversed**: effective fed funds is back to **3.88% (Oct-26)** with the target at **3.75–4.00%** after the 28 Oct hold. The **October minutes (released 18 Nov) show officials expect *further hikes*** to "stem inflation."

Three facts frame everything for SNDK:

1. **Inflation has re-accelerated.** CPI YoY went from **2.43% (Feb-26) to 3.82% (Oct-26)** — a ~140bp round trip. This is a second inflation pulse, and the Fed is responding.
2. **Rates are bear-steepening.** The 10Y has risen from **4.38% (26 Jun-26) to 5.13%**, the 30Y to **5.47%**, while 10Y breakevens *fell* to **2.15%** (from 2.36% on 1 Oct). Therefore **real 10Y yields ≈ 2.98%** — the actual tightening impulse is coming from *real* rates and term premium, not inflation expectations.
3. **The 2Y (4.74%) sits 86bp above effective fed funds.** The curve is pricing roughly 3–4 more hikes. Growth is still solid (Q3 real GDP +2.1% YoY, +3.4% annualised QoQ; unemployment 4.1%), so this is policy fighting inflation *with* the economy at trend — the worst combination for long-duration cyclicals.

Credit is the early-warning light: **HY OAS widened from 2.65% (1 Sep) to 3.52% (19 Nov)**, +87bp in eleven weeks, from historically very tight levels. Fin hovering, not cracking — but the direction is wrong for a capex-heavy, BB+ rated borrower.

**Verdict:** risk-on is over for high-beta technology. The equity market has already said so — the S&P 500 entered correction in the week of 20 Nov.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

SNDK is a **pure-play NAND flash** manufacturer: 11,100 employees, ~$90B market cap, **beta 5.65**, S&P rating **BB+** (sub-investment grade), manufacturing largely through the **Flash Ventures JV with Kioxia in Japan** (yen cost base, USD revenue). Revenue is levered to *price × bit growth*, so macro transmits through (a) discount rate, (b) end demand, (c) policy-driven trade barriers.

| Macro Variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Real yields (+3.0%) / 10Y 5.13%** | **Strongly NEGATIVE** | Beta 5.65, duration-heavy growth equity. A ~100bp rise in real yields since June mechanically compresses the terminal-value multiple on a stock whose earnings are at cycle peak. This alone explains much of the 73% drawdown. |
| **Fed hiking bias / 2Y 4.74%** | **NEGATIVE** | SanDisk is **BB+**, so its cost of capital is set in the HY market, not the IG market. HY OAS +87bp since September raises marginal funding costs for working capital and fab tooling exactly as industry CapEx is set to rise **26.7% YoY to $29.9B in 2027** (TrendForce). |
| **CPI 3.82%** | **NEUTRAL-to-NEGATIVE** | Little direct input-cost exposure (no commodity feedstock), but 3.8% CPI is what keeps the Fed hawkish, which loops back into the discount rate. Indirect, and negative. |
| **GDP +2.1% / unemployment 4.1%** | **NEUTRAL** | SanDisk's revenue driver is AI data-centre storage (portfolio +1,700%, ~378% revenue growth) and NAND price, not US GDP. Solid growth avoids a demand collapse but provides no upside catalyst. |
| **NAND pricing cycle (industry)** | **NEGATIVE** | TrendForce (19 Nov): Q4-26 contract increases **revised down to +8–13%** (from +15–20%); **Q1-27 flat to −5% QoQ**; broad declines from Q2-27. Spot NAND already **−20%**. 2026 deficit of 4–5% flips to oversupply in H2-27. |

| **USD strength (+3.1% since 30 Sep)** | **MIXED / slightly negative** | Non-US revenue (China CSPs, Asian handset OEMs) translates lower. Partly self-hedged: yen-denominated Flash Ventures costs fall in USD terms. Net modest headwind. |
| **Section 232 tariffs (25% on advanced logic semis, eff. 15 Jan 2026)** | **NEGATIVE (overhang)** | NAND is not "advanced logic," so direct incidence is narrow — but broader semiconductor *and derivative* tariffs were **deferred, not withdrawn**, leaving live risk on SSD/module imports. **Quantifying the drawdown:** on the adjusted series, SNDK went from ~$4.50 at the Feb-2025 spin-off to a peak of **$291.88 (25 Jun 2026)** — roughly 17x — and has since fallen **−72.8% to $79.24 (20 Nov 2026)**, including **−53% in the last month alone**. Year-to-date 2026 it is still **+130%** (from $34.40 on 2 Jan). This is a textbook late-cycle memory bubble deflating *into* a re-tightening Fed — the 2022 playbook, at higher velocity.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Three independent macro forces are aligned against the stock simultaneously:

- **Discount rate:** real 10Y ~3.0%, +75bp on nominal 10Y since June, with the Fed signalling more hikes. For a beta-5.6, BB+ rated, long-duration asset this is the dominant negative.
- **Cycle:** the industry's own supply response (CapEx +26.7% in 2027, QLC penetration, higher layer counts) plus Chinese CSP order cuts turns a 4–5% 2026 deficit into 2027 oversupply.
- **Financing:** BB+ credit into a widening HY spread environment while capex rises — the historically dangerous combination in memory.

The one genuinely supportive macro feature — falling breakevens (2.15%) — argues the inflation is supply-driven and the Fed may be close to done. That is a real offset, but it does not offset a NAND price downcycle beginning in Q1-27.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BEAR**

**"One more hike into a downcycle."** The October minutes explicitly lean toward additional tightening, and with the 2Y at 4.74% the market is pricing it — but not fully. If the **December 2026 FOMC delivers a 25bp hike to 4.00–4.25%** and the 10Y term premium pushes the long bond toward **5.50%** (real 10Y ~3.3%), the broad dollar breaks above 125 and global financial conditions tighten into a NAND market where Q1-27 contracts are already guided to **0% to −5%**.

The transmission to SNDK is non-linear because of the **beta of 5.65** and because consensus still anchors on a "cheap" multiple that rests on **peak-cycle EPS**. Analysts are already flagging that low memory multiples have historically marked peak pricing, not value. In this scenario I would expect a further **30–45% downside** from $79 as forward EPS estimates are cut (Q1-27 flat-to-down pricing plus Chinese CSP cancellations) *and* the multiple compresses. The stock's last two busts ended at −50% and −54%; it is at −73% now, which is precisely why a *macro* shock — not a company-specific one — is the marginal risk.

**Bull counter-scenario (lower probability, high magnitude):** breakevens at 2.15% say the 3.8% CPI is a supply/energy artefact. If the Fed's Oct hold proves to be the terminal rate and North American AI data-centre demand (SanDisk's long-term contracts, data-centre portfolio +1,700%) offsets Chinese CSP cuts, Q1-27 NAND pricing lands at the top of TrendForce's range (0%) rather than −5%. A 5.6-beta stock down 73% with 378% revenue growth on long-term contracts would re-rate violently. **Watch the December FOMC and the Q1-27 TrendForce contract print — those two events, not earnings, decide the next 30%.**
