## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US–China decoupling and Chinese retaliation (Probability: Medium-High ~40–50% over 12–24m; Impact: High)**
The Nov 2026 BIS rules target advanced AI compute and remote access, and sources do not name NAND/SSDs — so *direct* product-rule exposure is currently limited. But Beijing has rejected the rules, warned of countermeasures, and broadened its toolkit (rare earths, AI, biotech, solar); Eurasia Group's Bremmer expects the truce to be extended but warns tighter chip restrictions could prompt "measured Chinese retaliation." Reuters Breakingviews flags that the chip curbs themselves threaten the US–China trade truce. With 47.5% of revenue billed to China/HK, a Chinese move against US memory suppliers (import restrictions, procurement guidance, unreliable-entity listing, customs delays) would be the single largest swing factor. Note the Nov 2026 selloff was attributed to AI-capex and midterm/AI-regulation fears, not China — so this risk is **not priced**.

**Risk 2 — Japan manufacturing concentration vs. China's export controls targeting Japan (Probability: Medium ~25–35%; Impact: High)**
MOFCOM **Announcement No. 1 [2026]** (6 Jan 2026) prohibits export of **all dual-use items** from China to Japan where end use/end user relates to Japanese military capability — effective immediately, no wind-down, and **extraterritorial** (covers third-country transfers and in-country transfers). Criteria are deliberately broad and ambiguous, so "previously commercial or civilian" flows now need licensing. Because Sandisk's entire wafer supply sits in Japan, this is a supply-chain chokepoint (rare earths, gallium, specialty materials) that bypasses Sandisk's own compliance posture entirely. Compounding: China's rare-earth/semiconductor-material licensing regime requires Beijing approval for exports of items containing Chinese rare earths. Secondary: Yokkaichi seismic exposure.

**Risk 3 — Chinese NAND supply ramp (YMTC) and tariff broadening (Probability: High/ongoing; Impact: Medium)**
YMTC held ~14% of global NAND bit shipments in Q2 2026 and targets the top spot by end-2027; Citi calls Chinese capacity the biggest long-term risk to the memory thesis. This is a structural price/margin risk more than a legal one. On tariffs: Proclamation 11002 (14 Jan 2026), effective 15 Jan 2026, imposes a **25% ad valorem Section 232 tariff** on certain advanced computing chips and specified derivative products, under a two-phase approach. NAND appears outside the narrow Phase One scope, but **Phase Two broadening to memory would hit the 18.3% US revenue line** with no US fab to offset it (Sandisk has zero domestic front-end capacity). TrendForce already forecasts NAND contract prices flat to −5% QoQ in Q1 2027.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Active litigation:** *Sandisk Corporation v. Monolithic 3D Inc.*, N.D. Cal. 3:26-cv-05641, filed 11 June 2026 — Sandisk is **plaintiff** in a patent action. Immaterial.
- **Historical patent/IP disputes** (all resolved): Memory Technologies/Pendrell ITC 337-TA-1034; Round Rock (jury verdict for Sandisk); multi-patent NAND manufacturing case resolved 2017. Recurring pattern — Sandisk is a frequent patent litigant given its foundational flash IP estate.
- **Consumer class actions:** two proposed classes alleging defective SanDisk/WD portable hard drives, est. exposure up to $4.66M — immaterial.
- **Sector antitrust read-across — the item to watch.** In Nov 2026 a US judge denied motions to dismiss the DRAM price-fixing class action against Micron, Samsung and SK hynix, finding collusion plausible after a ~500% price surge ("acted like hawks flying in formation"). Sandisk is **not named** in those reports, but NAND has been subject to price-fixing class actions historically (Sandisk was a named defendant in an earlier NAND case), and Sandisk's revenue grew 175% in FY2026 and 378% in Q1 FY2027 on pricing. A NAND-focused follow-on suit, or EU/China/Korea competition scrutiny of the NAND oligopoly, is a plausible 2027 development. I found **no evidence of a current DOJ/FTC/SEC/MOFCOM investigation of Sandisk**.
- **Regulatory posture:** Section 232 (25% tariff, Phase One narrow); BIS advanced-compute export controls (NAND not named); MOFCOM Announcement No. 1 [2026] (dual-use to Japan); China rare-earth licensing; JCET/SanDisk Shanghai 80% divestiture (subject to PRC regulatory approval); Japan METI oversight of the Kioxia JV. S&P revised its Sandisk outlook in Aug 2026 on the stronger revenue trajectory.
- **Gap:** the FY2026 10-K legal-proceedings note and full risk-factor text could not be retrieved — SEC EDGAR and investor.sandisk.com both returned HTTP 403. My legal-contingency view is therefore built from secondary sources and should be re-verified against the 10-K Item 3 / Note disclosures.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Third-party/intermediary concentration in a high-corruption-risk market** — 47.5% of revenue billed to China/HK, routed partly through a 48%-owned affiliate (Unis Venture). Structurally FCPA/anti-bribery-sensitive; no allegation of wrongdoing found, but this is the profile that attracts DOJ scrutiny.
2. **Dual export-control regimes in conflict** — US EAR/BIS rules and China's blocking/anti-foreign-sanctions law plus MOFCOM dual-use controls now apply to the same transactions. Re-export of US-origin technology to the Japanese JV fabs, and China's extraterritorial controls over Japanese end users, create genuine compliance conflicts with no clean safe harbor.
3. **Deemed-export and JV governance risk** — 49.9% joint control of Flash Ventures means compliance failures at Kioxia-controlled operations are only partially within Sandisk's control.
4. **Transshipment/diversion risk** — Hong Kong billing at 25.3% of revenue is exactly the pattern US enforcement agencies scrutinize for diversion to restricted Chinese entities. No evidence of an issue; flag as a disclosure-quality concern (the 10-Q collapses geography to three regions, obscuring China exposure).

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** ~47.5% of revenue is China-exposed and ~100% of front-end wafer production sits in Japan, which is now itself the target of Chinese dual-use export controls — two independent chokepoints pointing in opposite directions. A broadening of Section 232 to memory, or a Chinese retaliation against US memory suppliers, would each be material. Offsets: NAND is not currently named in the advanced-compute export rules; the Shanghai back-end divestiture to JCET has reduced China-based capital at risk; the Kioxia JV is locked through 2034 with $31B+ of committed investment; and no material government investigation or large adverse legal contingency was identified. Litigation per se is **not** the driver — the rating is geopolitical and regulatory, not legal. Downgrade trigger to Medium would be a confirmed US–China truce extension plus formal exclusion of NAND from Phase Two tariffs.
