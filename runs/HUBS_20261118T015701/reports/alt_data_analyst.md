### 1. Consumer Demand Signals — **Sharply Negative, with a sector confound**

**Google Trends, US (weekly, 0–100)**

| Series | Jul-26 peak | Latest (w/e 8-Nov) | Δ | vs 12-mo avg |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "HubSpot" | 64 | **21** | −67% | ~40 → **−48%** |
| "HubSpot CRM" | 87 | **11** | **−87%** | ~54 → **−80%** |
| "Salesforce" (control) | 70 | 35 | −50% | ~60 → −42% |

Two things are true at once, and both matter:
- **The break is sector-wide.** Both HUBS and CRM step down in the *same week* (w/e 12-Jul-26, 4–12 pts in one week) and never recover. That pattern — simultaneous, permanent, across competitors — is characteristic of **AI answer engines displacing navigational brand search** plus a probable measurement shift. I would not read the raw decline as pure demand destruction.
- **But HUBS is decisively worse than the control.** −67% vs −50% on brand terms, and the *commercial-intent* term "HubSpot CRM" has essentially flatlined (−87%, three of the last four weeks at 11–19). If you strip out the shared component, HubSpot retains roughly half the relative search share it held in July. That is a genuine, idiosyncratic erosion of top-of-funnel intent.

**Web traffic (SimilarWeb, Oct-2026):** hubspot.com **−3.1% MoM**; **blog.hubspot.com −29.7% MoM**. The blog number is the single most bearish data point in this report. HubSpot built a ~$3B revenue business on inbound content marketing — that asset is being disintermediated by LLM-summarised answers in real time. Customer-portal domains (hs-sites.com) are stable, so this is a *funnel-entry* problem, not a churn problem (yet).

**Data limitation:** Trends lacks absolute volumes; the July break cannot be separated from platform artifacts with the tools available. Directional confidence high, magnitude confidence low.

### 3. Earnings Preview Signal — **Below consensus (on the metric that matters)**

Reported Q4'26 due ~10-Feb-27. Consensus: revenue $946.97M, EPS $3.89 — versus company guide of **$945–947M and $3.85–3.87**. Consensus sits at the *top of the revenue range and above the EPS range*: **there is no cushion**.

- **Revenue beats are compressing:** +2.9%, +1.9%, +2.1%, +1.5%, +2.4% over the last five quarters — steady, but the beat is thin enough that a 1-point growth miss overwhelms it.
- **EPS surprises are *widening*:** +0.6% → +3.3% → +2.7% → +3.3% → +10.1% → +8.0% → **+16.5%** (7/7 beat rate, +6.4% avg). This is real cost discipline/buyback leverage ($1B repurchased YTD, $1.3B cash, no debt) and is why Q4 EPS guide is ~12% *above* old consensus.
- **Growth staircase is deteriorating faster than the tape assumes:** +23.4% → +19.8% → +17.1% → **+11.8% guided**. And on the Q3 call: **reported NRR of just 101%**, net new ARR expected to *decline* in 2026, and no Q4 customer-adds guidance at all.
- **Direction of revisions: down.** TD Cowen cut PT to $215 from $260 (Hold) citing new-customer acquisition and NRR; multiple houses slashed post-Q3.

**Verdict: EPS likely In-Line/Above (cost lever intact); revenue and every forward metric tracking Below.** Net: **Below consensus**. Alt data (search + blog collapse, 101% NRR) leads the reported numbers here and argues net-new-ARR guidance is still too high.

### 6. Overall Alt Data Signal: **BEARISH on demand trajectory** (with a real, separate catalyst case)
The leading indicators — commercial-intent search (−87% on "HubSpot CRM"), blog traffic (−29.7% MoM), NRR at 101%, decelerating growth, and the absence of customer-adds guidance — all point the same way and all lead reported revenue by 1–3 quarters. Consensus revenue sits at the *top* of the guide. I expect the Feb-27 print to deliver another EPS beat alongside a below-consensus revenue/ARR outcome.

The bull case is not a data case: it is a **valuation and catalyst case** (founder-CEO returning with a mandate to refound, insider buying, ~10x FCF, $1.3B net cash, buyback, plausible acquisition target). Those are real, but they are not alt-data confirmed.

### 7. Confidence Level: **MEDIUM**
**Supports:** two independent, high-frequency datasets (Google Trends with a proper control; SimilarWeb) pointing identically; seven-quarter beat/miss history; on-the-record NRR of 101%; corroborated guidance cut.
**Undermines:** the July structural break in search is partly an AI-displacement/measurement artifact affecting the whole sector, so relative — not absolute — magnitude is what I trust; **job postings and patent data both failed**, removing my cleanest read on investment intent; web traffic is a single-vendor monthly estimate, not a time series; no usable peer multiples (the "peer" endpoint returned mega-cap semis, not CRM comps — CRM/BRZE/HUBS multiples should be sourced separately).

**Single most important thing to monitor:** whether blog.hubspot.com traffic stabilises in the Nov/Dec SimilarWeb prints. That number *is* the inbound funnel, and it is currently falling five times faster than the main domain. A flattening there would be the first alt-data confirmation that the Halligan "refounding" — including the seats-plus-credits pricing pilot now open to all EMEA customers — is repairing the top of the funnel rather than just re-accelerating AI narrative.
