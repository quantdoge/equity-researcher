## MACRO ASSESSMENT — HUBSPOT, INC. (NYSE: HUBS)

**As-of date: 18 November 2026.** All macro figures from FRED; equity data through 17 Nov 2026 close.

### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **TRANSITIONAL, tilting RISK-OFF for long-duration growth**

The US is in a **late-cycle reflation with a term-premium shock**: growth is above trend, the labour market is firming, inflation has re-accelerated, and the Fed has *resumed hiking* after an eight-month pause. This is the worst possible macro mix for a high-multiple, long-duration SaaS asset.

Key sequencing:
- **Growth is strong, not slowing**: Q3 2026 real GDP **+3.4%** (Q2 +2.2%, Q1 +2.5%) — a sharp recovery from the Q4 2025 stall (+0.2%).
- **Inflation re-accelerated**: headline CPI **+3.82% y/y** (Oct 2026). Core CPI is **~2.8% y/y but ~3.4% annualised over the last four months** — the *direction* is wrong.
- **The Fed is tightening again**: effective fed funds **3.88%** (Oct 2026), up from the 3.63% plateau that held May–Aug 2026, after 170bp of cuts from the 5.33% peak.
- **The long end has broken out**: 10Y at **5.13%**, up **+75bp since 29 June (4.38%)**. Critically, 10Y breakevens are only **2.21%**, so the 10Y *real* yield is **~2.92%**. **This is a real-rate / term-premium selloff, not an inflation-expectations selloff** — the most damaging variety for equity duration, because it cannot be resolved by disinflation alone; it requires fiscal credibility or a growth shock.
- **Credit is still benign but deteriorating at the margin**: HY OAS **3.34%**, up from a Sept low of **2.65%** (+68bp). Tight in absolute terms — no recession signal — but the direction matters for SMB customers.
- **The dollar is squeezing**: broad USD index **122.65**, **+4.0% since 9 September** (117.88) and +3.1% since June — a fast, pro-cyclical USD rally driven by the US hiking while the world holds.

**Curve shape: bear steepener, +39bp 2s10s.** Not an inversion/recession signal — the opposite: the market is pricing higher-for-longer real rates and rising term premium, which is the specific signal that de-rates long-duration equities.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact — HUBS

**Company context:** $10.56B market cap, **$216.30** share price, beta 1.14. The stock is **-73% from its Feb 2025 close of $819.71** and **-46% YTD** (from $401.30). It has printed at least *eight* single-day moves of -9% or worse in the last 15 months (e.g. -19% on 8 May 2026 and -19% on 6 Aug 2026) — a structurally de-rated, narrative-driven equity. Notably, it has **re-correlated to rates in November**: HUBS is **-18% from its 23 October high of $270.88** while the 10Y rose only ~20bp.

| Macro variable | Read-through for HUBS | Sign |

| **Fed resuming hikes (3.63%→3.88%)** | Signals the easing cycle that fuelled the June–October +59% rebound (from $170 to $271) is over. Compresses multiples across the whole CRM/SaaS complex and raises the bar for 2027 guidance. | **Negative** |

| **USD +4% in 9 weeks** | ~45–48% of revenue is international (Americas/EMEA/APAC). A sustained move of this magnitude is a **~1.5–2.5pt headwind to reported growth**, and makes USD list prices dearer for European/APAC customers already facing weak local demand. | **Negative** |

**Aggregate:** essentially every macro channel — discount rate, policy direction, credit, FX, input costs, seat-growth arithmetic — is pointing the same way. The only mitigants are the absence of leverage and a firm labour market.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

This is not a close call. The combination of **3.4% GDP + 3.8% CPI + a hiking Fed + 5.13% 10Y with 2.9% real yields + a dollar ripping** is close to the worst conceivable backdrop for a -73% de-rated, SMB-exposed, seat-priced, long-duration software name. The 10Y at 5%+ is doing more damage to HUBS's multiple than any plausible change in its own fundamentals.

**Important qualifier for the investment memo:** HUBS's problem is *mostly idiosyncratic*, not macro. It rallied +59% between June and October 2026 **while** the 10Y rose 60bp — proving company-specific catalysts (the 12 Nov announcement that co-founder **Brian Halligan returns as CEO to lead a "refounding"**, plus new CFO Karen Burgess and insider buying) can override macro for stretches. But the November tape shows that when rates gap higher, macro re-asserts control, and **beta to the long end is now the dominant risk factor**. Treat macro as the swing factor on *timing and multiple*, not the thesis itself.

### 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BEAR: "Term premium unanchored"**

**The setup:** breakevens are 2.21% while headline CPI is 3.82%. The bond market is positioned for disinflation *despite* four consecutive months of core CPI at ~3.4% annualised, 3.4% GDP, and a hiking Fed. That is a fragile consensus.

**The surprise:** Q4 2026 CPI prints 4.2%+ (tariff pass-through, energy, services), the Fed delivers two more 25bp hikes to ~4.4%, and the **10Y breaks 6%**. With the deficit and term premium already doing the work, the 30Y at 5.47% goes to 6.25%. **10Y real yields reach ~3.6% — above most equity risk-premium assumptions.**

**Transmission to HUBS:** a further 75–85bp on the long end plus widening SMB credit spreads would compress HUBS's multiple by another 25–35% (to the $140–165 area) *even with revenue in line*, because the entire de-rating would come from the terminal value. Simultaneously, SMB churn accelerates, and the "refounding" narrative gets no room to breathe — new management is judged on quarterly numbers into a credit-tightening tape. **This is the scenario where HUBS underperforms a falling market.**

**The mirror-image bull case (why I flag it, not why I expect it):** if 3.4% GDP is the top and Q1 2027 growth stalls to ~1%, unemployment breaks 4.6%, and the Fed reverses hard, the 10Y snaps back to 4.25%. Every basis point of that rally lands disproportionately on the most de-rated long-duration names — and HUBS, at -73% from peak and re-correlated to rates in November, would be a **+30–40% beta trade** on that pivot. Watch weekly jobless claims and the HY OAS: **3.34% → 4.25% would be the tell, and it would flip this rating to "Favourable" within a quarter.**
