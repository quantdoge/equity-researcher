## QXO, Inc. (NYSE: QXO) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
*Analyst: Non-Financial Risk. Data as of Nov 9–20, 2026. Note on sourcing: the 10-K and DEF 14A text feeds returned no extractable documents for QXO (tool limitation, not a filing absence), so narrative below rests on 8-K/10-Q exhibits, press releases, proxy-derived summaries and news flow. Gaps are flagged explicitly.*

**Company context:** $14.3B market cap; building-products distribution roll-up (Beacon Roofing ~$11B, closed Apr 2025; Kodiak; TopBuild ~$17B, closed 2026) now spanning ~1,150+ locations. Founder-led, targeting $50B revenue within a decade. Beta 2.36. Stock: $13.78, vs. $27.07 close peak (Feb 2026) and $10.04 low (Oct 19, 2026) — a ~50% peak-to-trough drawdown.

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: WEAK

| Factor | Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Chairman/CEO duality | Brad Jacobs is Founder, Chairman **and** CEO | Concentration red flag |
| Voting control | Jacobs beneficially owned ~**31.4% of voting power** (FY2024 10-K); a single holder effectively controls outcomes | Control block; minority has little leverage |
| Board | Seven directors elected 2026; Deloitte ratified as auditor; say-on-pay passed | Adequate hygiene, but no evidence of a strong independent lead director or disclosed succession committee |
| Compensation | Jacobs base salary $750,000 with **no discretionary equity grants** for FY2025; offset by very large PSU grants (6.2M+ PSUs, 2.0M vested Jan 2026) | Founder-aligned on paper; heavily performance/PSU-weighted, so equity-value sensitivity is high |
| Structural seniority | **$3B Series C convertible perpetual preferred** (Apollo/Temasek led), $10,000/share liquidation preference, upsized from $1.2B | Preferred sits ahead of common; terms (conversion, dividends, board designation) not verified — a material unknown |
| Capital structure complexity | $2.25B 6.75% senior secured notes due 2032 plus ~$3B senior notes for TopBuild, layered guarantor structure | Covenant/creditor constraints limit strategic flexibility |

**Verdict: Weak** — driven by three overlapping concentration vectors (founder control, dual CEO/Chair role, sponsor preferred with seniority), not by any evidence of self-dealing.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: MODERATE
- **SEC/DOJ enforcement: none found.** EDGAR full-text enforcement search for QXO returned zero actions. No subpoena, Wells notice, or restatement disclosed.
- **Deal-related litigation: live risk.** QXO's own merger registration statement discloses that *"securities class action and derivative lawsuits may be brought against QXO or TopBuild in connection with the mergers."* The TopBuild deal drew a **Levi & Korsinsky fiduciary-duty investigation** into "certain officers and directors of QXO." This is an investigation announcement, not a filed class action — treat as an early-stage flag, and expect plaintiffs' activity to scale with the drawdown.
- **Antitrust: cleared but structurally rising.** TopBuild cleared with ~99% of votes cast in favor; both deals closed. However, QXO is now the largest publicly traded roofing distributor and #2 in lumber/BMC. Every subsequent bolt-on moves into HHI territory the DOJ/FTC can challenge — and the 2026 HSR regime is in flux (2025 form vacated Feb 2026, new form under comment). Assume longer, costlier clearances and possible second requests on future targets.
- **Pending approvals:** CKX Lands transaction (Jacobs' side venture) requires CKX shareholder approval; expected close early 2027.
- **Data privacy/ESG enforcement:** nothing surfaced.

- Institutional flows are mixed, not panicked: BNY Mellon new $44.9M and Neville Rodie new $5.5M positions vs. CalPERS −22.4% and Carnegie −13.8%.

### 4. Top Operational Risks
1. **Serial-integration execution.** Three large, overlapping integrations (Beacon, Kodiak, TopBuild) inside ~18 months across 1,150+ branches with legacy ERPs, local branch P&Ls, and culturally independent operators. Reported margin gains have been partly attributed to inventory/purchase-accounting effects rather than durable efficiency — integration failure is the single largest value-destruction vector.
2. **Key-person dependency with divided attention.** QXO is built around Jacobs' dealmaking and capital-raising charisma. As of Nov 9, 2026 he is simultaneously becoming Chairman & CEO of a new platform (CKX Lands, renamed; his Jacobs Private Equity II to hold ~99.78% and invest $900M of a $1B round). No succession plan or COO-level counterweight is disclosed. There is no bench depth comparable to the founder.
3. **Technology, cyber and supplier concentration.** The "tech-enabled distributor" thesis requires consolidating dozens of acquired IT estates and customer payment data — precisely the environment where a breach originates. Combined with supplier concentration in roofing (GAF, Owens Corning, CertainTeed) and weather/storm-cycle-driven demand, plus branch-level safety exposure (warehouse/forklift/OSHA) in a 20,000+ person field workforce.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: MODERATE (elevated, but performance-driven not conduct-driven)
- **Negative narrative:** "rough start," sales declines at legacy Beacon, RBC price-target cut to $18 and a 7% single-day drop on weak channel checks, GAAP net loss of ~$123M in Q3'26 against revenue of $4.90B (+79%). Trades below book and below the value of the TopBuild consideration.
- **Mitigants:** Forbes' Most Trusted Companies in America listing (Oct 2026); overwhelming shareholder approvals; heavy institutional sponsorship; no product recall, no disclosed data breach, no consumer/ESG controversy, no labor action found.
- **Asymmetry:** the brand is now inseparable from Jacobs' personal reputation. A failed deal, a visible conflict of interest between QXO and his new vehicle, or an accounting surprise would damage both simultaneously — sentiment is thin after a 50% drawdown and a 2.36 beta.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**
Not Critical: clean enforcement record, no fraud indicators, uninterrupted Deloitte audit, zero discretionary insider selling, and no conduct/ESG scandal. But the aggregate of founder control, dual-CEO distraction, a sponsor preferred stacked ahead of common, three simultaneous mega-integrations, and a pitched deal-litigation environment puts this well above sector norm.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation
1. **Dual-CEO conflict (highest priority).** Jacobs becomes Chairman & CEO of CKX while remaining Chairman & CEO of QXO, with his own PE firm owning ~99.78% of CKX. Undisclosed target industry creates a live risk of QXO competing with, or transacting with, an entity he controls. Demand: time-allocation disclosure, related-party policy, and recusal protocol.
2. **Series C preferred terms unverified.** $3B at a $10,000/share liquidation preference ahead of common. Conversion price, dividend rate, and any board-designation rights must be confirmed — these determine how much of any recovery accrues to common.
3. **Fiduciary-duty investigation + merger litigation risk.** Levi & Korsinsky investigation and the registration statement's own securities/derivative-suit warning. Monitor for a filed class action with a class period tied to the 2026 guidance and TopBuild disclosures.
4. **31.4% voting control with no counterbalance.** Verify whether shareholders withheld votes on directors or say-on-pay at the 2026 annual meeting (QXO took the unusual step of posting an investor Q&A in May 2026, often a sign of engagement pressure) — results not confirmed in my data.
5. **Disclosure opacity.** Q3 guidance was released as *preliminary* ahead of the full 10-Q; GAAP −$0.14 vs. adjusted +$0.08 EPS. Reconciliations of inventory/purchase-accounting benefits and adjusted EBITDA definitions should be stress-tested before relying on the margin-expansion narrative.
6. **Data gaps to close:** no whistleblower complaints, SEC subpoenas, or cyber incidents were found — but absence of evidence here is weak evidence given I could not read the 10-K risk factors or the 2026 proxy in full. Recommend a direct read of Item 3 (Legal Proceedings) and the related-party footnote of the FY2026 10-K on filing, plus confirmation of the employee count (feed shows 7,794, which is inconsistent with a ~$20B run-rate, 1,150-location platform and is likely stale).
