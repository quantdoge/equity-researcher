## 1. Geographic Risk Map

QXO is one of the most **geographically concentrated large-caps in Industrials**: essentially a single-country business.

| Geography | Revenue exposure | Nature of exposure | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | **~97% of FY2025 net sales** (QXO 2025 Form 10-K, geographic note: *"Net sales in the U.S. accounted for approximately 97% of total net sales for the year ended December 31, 2025"*) | ~All branches/DCs, installation services (TopBuild), 49 US-jurisdiction subsidiary guarantors (DE, TX, IL, MI, NE, ID, OR, CO, WA, NH, NC…) | **Low–Medium** (stable rule of law, but maximum exposure to US tariff/housing/antitrust policy) |
| **Canada** | ~2–3% (residual; Beacon legacy branches; Q1-26 10-Q references operations in "provinces in Canada") | Branch network + **material sourcing** (softwood lumber, gypsum, insulation) | **Medium** — small revenue, disproportionate *input-cost* and M&A-approval exposure |
| **Mexico / Asia (sourcing only)** | No disclosed revenue; embedded in COGS | Imported building products, components | **Low–Medium** (tariff/UFLPA/forced-labor diligence) |
| **Europe (France)** | 0% — **Rexel hostile approach (Sept 2024) abandoned** | Optionality, not exposure | Low today; would add EU FSR/FDI screening if revisited |

**Key takeaway:** there is no emerging-market, Russia, China-operations, or OFAC-designated-country exposure. Country risk is not the issue; **US policy risk transmitted through trade and housing** is.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US building-materials tariffs and cross-border trade friction (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High)**
- Section 232 timber/lumber tariffs: 10% levy with scheduled step-ups to 30% and 50% (Brookings); Canadian softwood duties raised from 14.5% to **35% plus a 10% Section 232 tariff** on all timber and lumber imports (NAHB). New Canadian counter-tariffs (~Aug 19, 2026) cover **cement, Canadian lumber, plywood and wood products**.
- QXO is a *distributor*: tariffs hit COGS and working capital before they hit price. Q3 2026 evidence of pass-through is decent but not full — **companywide pricing +2.2% y/y, positive in 9 of 11 product areas** (8-K, 11/9/2026). Watch gross-margin spread and inventory-cost lag; also second-order volume destruction in new residential.
- Sourcing geography (Canada lumber/gypsum) makes this the single largest *external* margin variable, even though Canada is only ~3% of sales.

**Risk 2 — US housing/rates and energy shock from the Iran conflict (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
- ~Two-thirds of QXO demand is repair & remodel plus new residential/commercial roofing — highly geared to existing-home turnover, insurance claim cycles and mortgage rates.
- The ongoing **Iran war** has pushed oil prices sharply higher (CNBC, 11/7–11/9/2026), feeding freight/fuel and inflation expectations and complicating the Fed path. A housing re-slowdown would hit volume just as QXO carries acquisition debt and inventory at tariff-inflated costs.
- Political overlay: a **Democratic sweep in the Nov 2026 midterms** introduces uncertainty on tax, trade-relief and antitrust posture — two-sided but raises the probability of a more aggressive merger-review environment.

**Risk 3 — Canada as a regulatory chokepoint and nationalist-trade flashpoint (Probability: Medium / Impact: Low-Medium)**
- Every major QXO deal has required Canadian clearance: Beacon (Canadian Competition Bureau **early termination**, 2025); TopBuild (**"no action" letter from the Competition Bureau, May 28, 2026**, with parallel US HSR expiry). Canada remains a de facto second antitrust regulator for QXO's roll-up.
- US–Canada relations are deteriorating (Canada walked out of trade negotiations in August 2026; Carney–Trump friction — Politico, 10/30/2026), raising retaliatory-tariff and permitting risk on a small but strategic footprint.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Antitrust — the dominant forward risk.**
- Beacon ($11B, $124.35/sh): survived a hostile tender offer, poison pill and 14D-9 campaign; HSR early termination and Canadian clearance obtained; closed **April 29, 2025**.
- TopBuild (**$17B**, announced April 19–20, 2026): US HSR waiting period expired, Canadian "no action" letter (May 28, 2026), TopBuild stockholder elections completed June 2026, **closed July 1, 2026**.
- But advocacy pressure is on the record: **Economic Liberties publicly called on the FTC to investigate the QXO–TopBuild merger as anticompetitive (May 1, 2026)**. With management targeting **$50B of annual revenue** and $4–5.5B adjusted EBITDA by 2030 via further M&A, each incremental deal faces a higher second-request/hold-separate probability, a new (2026) HSR form, and state-AG attention in concentrated roofing/insulation metros.

**Shareholder litigation (deal-related, typical Delaware).**
- **QXO stockholder proposed class action in Delaware Chancery** seeking to block the TopBuild-related shareholder vote, alleging defendants "breached their fiduciary duties by failing to disclose all material information" (Law360, *QXO Stockholder Sues Over TopBuild Deal Disclosures*; QXO 8-K, 6/22/2026). Disclosure-only nuisance value expected; injunction risk pre-close is now moot.
- 2025 Beacon-board investigations/filings by plaintiffs' firms (Rigrodsky Law, Johnson Fistel, M&A class-action firm) challenging the $124.35 process — largely mooted by closing.
- Ongoing ordinary-course docket as *plaintiff*: breach-of-contract/debt-collection suits (e.g., QXO dba Beacon v. All Weather Roof, filed 11/4/2025; Beacon Sales Acquisition dba QXO v. HomePro Roofing, filed 6/19/2026). Immaterial.

**Governance / related-party conflicts (watch item).**
- Jacobs Private Equity II investment agreement and Brad Jacobs' performance-based RSU award create aligned-but-concentrated control dynamics.
- **Brad Jacobs' family office plus co-investors agreed to a proposed $1B cash investment in CKX Lands** ($900M from Jacobs), after which he would become CKX chairman/CEO **while remaining QXO chairman/CEO** (8-K 11/9/2026). This is a classic derivative-suit and proxy-advisor target (attention/conflict/related-party), and it is the single most likely source of new stockholder litigation against QXO outside M&A.

**Leverage/indenture.** $2.25B 6.75% senior secured notes due 2032 plus ~$3B new notes for TopBuild; management cites **>$2B liquidity, no maturities before 2031, ~two-thirds fixed rate**. Covenant restrictions on additional liens/debt could constrain the acquisition program — a legal-financial constraint rather than a litigation one.

**Regulatory posture otherwise benign:** SEC reporting/NYSE (shelf registration effective), state contractor-licensing regimes for TopBuild's installation workforce, OSHA/product-liability exposure inherent to roofing and insulation, and CCPA-style privacy obligations arising from the "tech-enabled" customer/contractor platform.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **UFLPA / forced-labor import diligence** — low but non-zero: imported lumber, plywood, gypsum and insulation inputs carry supply-chain traceability obligations; a CBP detention would be a localized but headline risk.
2. **Related-party/conflict disclosure (CKX Lands)** — medium concern; needs clean related-party disclosure and independent-director process.
3. **No FCPA/AML/sanctions red flags identified** — US/Canada-only operations; no OFAC-designated markets; no known DOJ/SEC/FTC enforcement action against QXO.
4. **Integration compliance risk (M&A velocity)** — Beacon, Kodiak, TopBuild absorbed within ~14 months; multiple legacy ERPs, contractor-classification, licensing and safety programs being consolidated. This is where a future enforcement action (wage/hour, OSHA, state licensing) most plausibly originates.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Geographic exposure is effectively the lowest-risk profile available (US ~97%, Canada ~3%, no EM/sanctions/Russia/China operations), which caps geopolitical risk at the country level. The Medium rating is driven by (i) high transmission of **US tariff and housing/rate shocks** into a thinly-margined distribution model; (ii) **antitrust scrutiny of a serial roll-up** that has already drawn public FTC-referral pressure, making each future deal slower and less certain; and (iii) **governance/related-party and Delaware litigation** surface that grows with deal cadence and CEO-side ventures (CKX). No material sanctions, corruption, expropriation, currency-control, or IP-ligation exposure.

**What would move the rating:** Up — an FTC second request or suit on a future acquisition, an adverse CKX/related-party derivative claim, or a tariff step-up that breaks pricing pass-through. Down — resolution of the TopBuild disclosure action, sustained positive price/cost spread, and diversification into a second regulatory regime (e.g., a completed European acquisition) executed with clean clearances.
