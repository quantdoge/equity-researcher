### 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **SUB‑INVESTMENT GRADE**
S&P assigns QXO **BB‑ / stable** (issuer credit rating), with S&P‑adjusted leverage expected "just over 5x." The trajectory since then has moved *against* the rating: QXO has completed three transformational deals in ~15 months — **Beacon ($11B, Apr 2025), Kodiak ($2.25B, Apr 1 2026), TopBuild ($17B, closed Jul 1 2026)** — pushing annual revenue from ~$57M (FY2024) to ~$16.3B pro forma. Gross debt rose from $0.6M (FY2024) → $3.91B (FY2025) → **$7.04B (Q2'26)** *before* the ~$6B committed financing for TopBuild.

The company is solvent on liquidity but **structurally dependent on continuous capital-markets access**: operating cash flow is thin, EBIT is negative, and the equity is almost entirely goodwill.

### 4. Downside Scenario Analysis (revenue –20%)
Pro forma base: revenue ~$16.3B, adjusted EBITDA ~$1.9B, gross debt ~$12.5–13B, interest ~$625M/yr at ~5%.

- Revenue –20% = –$3.26B. At ~25% contribution margin → **–$815M EBITDA**, partly offset by variable cost cuts ($250M) → **EBITDA ~$1.35B**.
- Net leverage: **~8.0–8.3x** (from ~5.8x) → **covenant waiver / equity cure territory**, 1–2 notch downgrade to B+/B.
- Cash coverage: (1,350 – 250)/625 = **1.76x** — serviceable but breach‑prone.
- **Goodwill impairment:** ~$10.6B of goodwill + TopBuild's added goodwill against $10.4B of book equity. A 20% revenue decline plausibly triggers a **$3–5B impairment, erasing 30–50% of book equity** — and goodwill impairment is a leading trigger for rating action in levered roll‑ups.
- **Severe case (–35%, housing-start collapse + data-center capex pause):** EBITDA ~$0.9B → net leverage ~12x, coverage ~1.0x, revolver draw, near-certain equity raise at a distressed price.

**Liquidity is not the near-term constraint** (cash ~$2.8B pre‑TopBuild, current ratio 4.1x, positive OCF); **leverage and refinancing dependence are.**

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating — **HIGH**
Justified by: 10.9x GAAP net leverage rising through acquisitions, negative EBIT and negative tangible book equity, distress‑zone Altman Z, weak Piotroski, sub‑cost‑of‑capital FCF yield, preferred dividends not covered by FCF, and 60%/beta 2.4 market risk. **Not "Critical"** because of genuinely strong liquidity, positive operating cash flow, BB‑ market access, a demonstrated ability to raise large equity and preferred, and a $300M synergy target.

### 6. Key Red Flags
1. **Three acquisitions in 15 months ($30B+ of deal value)** — integration and purchase‑accounting risk at a scale management has never run; pro forma revenue ~2.4x the FY2025 base.
2. **Negative tangible book equity (–$235M)** — shareholders own goodwill and future execution, not assets. Hard‑asset downside protection is absent.
3. **Interest coverage of 2.0x on EBITDA and –1.5x on EBIT**, with (EBITDA–capex)/interest at 1.44x.
4. **Dilution machine:** diluted shares 407M (FY24) → 767M (Q2'26), +88%; Piotroski F7 = 0. Each deal is part‑funded with stock — per‑share value accretion is unproven.
5. **Preferred stock seniority:** $3B / 4.75% convertible preferred consumes ~$144M/yr, ~1.0x TTM FCF. Common holders are structurally subordinated.
6. **Earnings quality:** quarterly EBITDA sign‑flips; gross margin swings 14%–25%. Reported results are not a reliable run‑rate.
7. **Retained earnings –$761M**, TTM net loss –$512M, and a stated intent to be "tech‑enabled" — capex/investment needs collide with a levered balance sheet.
8. **Refinancing dependence:** the model assumes continuous debt + equity access; a closed high‑yield/convert market stalls the strategy.
