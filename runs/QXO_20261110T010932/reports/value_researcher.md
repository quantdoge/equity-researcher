## QXO, Inc. (NYSE: QXO) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $13.78 (Nov 9, 2026) | Market cap ~$14.3B | ~1.04B shares | Down ~43% YTD from $25.52 January high*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — **Moat Rating: None**

QXO is no longer the small software company that Brad Jacobs recapitalized in 2024. In ~18 months it has become one of North America's largest building-products distributors through three transformational deals:

| Deal | Closed | Price | What it bought |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beacon Roofing Supply | Apr 2025 | ~$11B | #2 US roofing/waterproofing distributor |
| Kodiak Building Partners | Apr 2026 | $2.25B ($2.0B cash + 13.2M shares) | Lumber & building materials, pro contractor |
| TopBuild Corp. | Jul 1, 2026 | ~$17B | #1 insulation installer/distributor |

Pro forma Q3 2026 net sales were **~$4.90B (+79% YoY)** with Adjusted EBITDA of **~$557M (+85%)**. The company now serves an "$800 billion addressable market" and targets **$50B of revenue within the decade**.

**Why the moat is None:**
- **No pricing power.** Gross margins run 17–25%; distributors are pass-through intermediaries. FY2025 gross margin was 16.8%, and Q1 2026's 14.2% shows how purchase-accounting noise and mix shifts can gut reported profitability.
- **Fragmented, low-barrier industry.** The bull case rests on *consolidating* a fragmented market — the honest admission that no participant, including QXO, has durable structural advantage. Scale in distribution buys modest purchasing and freight leverage, not a moat.
- **Returns below cost of capital, consistently.** ROIC was **-0.42% (2025), -1.40% (2024), -12.99% (2023), -3.22% (2022)**. A moated business earns ROIC > WACC through the cycle. QXO has never done so. The 2025 figure is flattered by only nine months of Beacon and is *before* the goodwill from two more deals.
- **Cyclical, not franchise.** TopBuild's installation revenue is levered to new home starts; Beacon's roofing is levered to storm activity and re-roof deferral. Both are volume businesses.

The one genuine asset is **TopBuild's national insulation installation network** — the largest in the US, with real local density advantages and some switching friction with builders. That is a *narrow* moat inside an otherwise commodity portfolio, and it is diluted by the roofing and lumber segments. **Blended rating: None.**

### 2. Financial Quality Score — **3 / 10**

| Metric | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Q2 2026 | Q3 2026 (prelim) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $54.5M | $56.9M | $6,842M | $3,246M | ~$4,900M |
| Gross margin | 39.6% | 40.3% | 16.8% | 24.7% | ~23% (est.) |
| Adj. EBITDA | ~$0 | ~$0 | ~$430M | $301.9M | ~$557M |
| GAAP net income | -$1.1M | +$27.9M | **-$279.4M** | -$55.0M | **-$123M** |
| FCF | $0.5M | $84.8M | $183.2M | **-$248.1M** | n/a |
| Diluted shares | 329M | 408M | 613M | 767M | ~1,037M |

**Positives:** Adj. EBITDA is compounding rapidly and Q3 2026 beat expectations; interest coverage on an EBITDA basis is adequate (~2.8x); revenue is highly diversified across products and geographies.

**Serious negatives:**
- **GAAP losses are structural, not transitional.** D&A runs $165–200M *per quarter*, dominated by amortization of acquisition intangibles ($10.6B of goodwill + intangibles at 6/30/26). Net loss was ~$123M in the strongest seasonal quarter of the year.
- **Cash conversion is poor.** FY2025 FCF of $183M on $6.8B of revenue is a **2.7% FCF margin** — well below the 4–5% a healthy distributor generates. Q2 2026 burned **$248M of FCF** on a $418M working-capital build. The business consumes cash as it grows, and integration/transformation costs ($28M/quarter) plus transaction costs ($40M in Q3 alone) are recurring in practice.
- **Dilution is extreme:** shares outstanding went from 329M (2023) to ~1,037M — a **3.2x increase in under three years**. Per-share value creation is the only kind that counts, and this is the opposite of it.
- **Tangible book value is deeply negative** (goodwill + intangibles ≈ 47% of total assets pre-TopBuild; far higher now).

### 3. Valuation Analysis — **Intrinsic value ≈ $12–13/share; price offers no margin of safety**

**Capital structure (key estimate).** Last reported balance sheet (6/30/26, pre-TopBuild): total debt $7.04B, cash $2.77B, net debt $4.26B. The TopBuild close added a **$3B first-lien term loan (due 2033)** and **$3B of senior notes**, funded partly with balance-sheet cash. My post-close estimate: **total debt ~$13B, cash ~$0.5B, net debt ~$12.5B.** *(Gap flagged: the 9/30/26 10-Q is not yet filed — results are due Nov 12, 2026. This is the single largest uncertainty in the valuation and should be verified.)*

That puts **enterprise value at ~$26.8B**, or **~12.5x** my 2027E pro forma Adjusted EBITDA of ~$2.15–2.3B (Q3 2026's $557M annualized at its ~26% seasonal share, before incremental synergies). Peer distributors (Builders FirstSource, Watsco, Core & Main, pre-deal TopBuild) trade at roughly 10–13x. **QXO commands a peer-level multiple while carrying ~6x net leverage versus 1–2x for those peers — a premium it has not earned.**

**Scenario valuation:**

| Scenario | Adj. EBITDA | Multiple | EV | Net debt | Equity | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear** (recession, starts -25%) | $1.8B | 9x | $16.2B | $12.5B | $3.7B | **~$3.60** |
| **Base** (synergies delivered, flat cycle) | $2.3B | 11x | $25.3B | $12.5B | $12.8B | **~$12.30** |
| **Bull** (2030: $4.2B EBITDA, delevered to $10B) | $4.2B | 12x | $50.4B | $10.0B | $40.4B | **~$35.00** |

My base case intrinsic value is **~$12.30/share**; a reasonable range is **$11–13**. At $13.78 the stock trades at a **~12% premium to base-case value** and roughly 28% above where I would want to buy (a 20–30% discount implies an entry near $9). The bull case is real but requires flawless integration, full synergy capture, continued accretive M&A, *and* no recession over four years — priced against a bear case that wipes out ~75% of equity.

Note the leverage asymmetry: **equity is only ~53% of EV.** Every 1x turn of EBITDA multiple moves the stock ~$2/share; every 10% move in EBITDA moves it ~$2.30/share. This is a levered bet on the cycle and on management, not a defensive compounder.

*(Data gap: sell-side targets — Wells Fargo $15, Truist $15, RBC $18, Melius $17, D.A. Davidson $20 — are 9–45% above spot, but these are sum-of-parts and "playbook" targets that implicitly assume mid-cycle EBITDA and successful deleveraging. I do not treat them as intrinsic value.)*

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Mixed (5/10)**

**The case for:** Brad Jacobs has one of the best capital-allocation records in modern American industry — United Rentals, United Waste, XPO, GXO, RXO. He built each via disciplined serial acquisition, and he is personally and heavily invested in QXO. His 2024 capital raise (~$5B of committed equity from blue-chip investors) was a genuine vote of confidence, and the execution speed on Beacon, Kodiak and TopBuild has been extraordinary. He is honest about the plan: it is a roll-up, explicitly.

**The case against:**
- **Price discipline is questionable.** Beacon was bought after a public pursuit at a premium; TopBuild at ~$17B is roughly 17x EBITDA in a business with cyclical installation exposure. Paying full prices and manufacturing accretion through leverage and share issuance is the classic roll-up trap.
- **Per-share discipline is weak.** Tripling the share count to buy EBITDA works only if the acquired EBITDA is bought below the multiple QXO trades at. At 12–17x purchase multiples against a 12.5x market multiple, the arbitrage is thin at best.
- **Attention risk is now material.** On **November 9, 2026** — the same day as the Q3 preliminary release — Jacobs announced he will become chairman and CEO of **CKX Lands**, backed by a $1B investment from Jacobs Private Equity II, describing it as "the launch of my ninth company." He retains the same roles at QXO. Running two public acquisition platforms simultaneously, during the largest integration in QXO's history, is a governance concern I cannot dismiss.
- **Governance/insider alignment:** large insider ownership is a plus, but there is no buyback, no dividend, and compensation structures in roll-ups typically reward EBITDA growth rather than ROIC or per-share value.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (do not initiate; avoid)

QXO fails all three of my non-negotiable tests:

1. **Circle of competence** — *partially passes.* Distribution is understandable, but the value here depends on financing engineering and M&A execution, not on business economics.
2. **Durable moat / ROIC > WACC** — *fails decisively.* Four consecutive years of negative ROIC; a commodity intermediary with no pricing power.
3. **Margin of safety** — *fails.* Base-case intrinsic value ~$12.30 against a $13.78 price. You are paying a premium for leverage, dilution, and execution risk.

The stock is down 43% year-to-date and trades below stated book value (~$15.70/share). That looks cheap and is **not** cheap: book value is ~90% goodwill and intangibles from acquisitions made at premium multiples. There is no asset backing here.

This is a **speculative levered roll-up**, not a Buffett business. If I wanted exposure to building-products distribution, I would rather own a conservatively financed operator with positive ROIC. **Verdict: Sell.** I would revisit only below ~$9 (a 25% discount to base-case value) or upon evidence of *organic* margin expansion and leverage falling below 4x.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to the downside (i.e., risks that I am wrong to be negative):**
1. **Synergy surprise.** If QXO delivers $300M+ of cost synergies and pushes blended EBITDA margin toward 12%, EBITDA could reach $2.8–3.0B by 2028 — at 12x that is ~$21/share. This is the most plausible path to a higher value.
2. **Accretive M&A continues.** A $800B addressable market and a rising QXO currency could let Jacobs keep buying below his own multiple.
3. **Deleveraging.** Strong FCF in 2027–28 could take net debt from ~$12.5B to ~$9B, mechanically lifting equity value by ~$3.4B (~$3.30/share).
4. **Short squeeze / sentiment.** Beta of 2.36, ~$557M of quarterly EBITDA momentum, and sell-side targets of $15–20 create violent upside rallies (the stock rose 24% in the first three trading days of 2026).

**Risks to the downside (risks that reinforce the Sell):**
1. **Leverage in a downturn — the dominant risk.** ~6x net debt/EBITDA with a BB/BB+ rating (S&P assigned a 'BB+' issue rating with a '1' recovery rating to the new term loan — the '1' recovery rating tells you secured lenders expect to be made whole *ahead of* equity). A 25% EBITDA decline takes leverage to ~8x and puts the equity in covenant/impairment territory. Housing starts and re-roofing demand are both late-cycle.
2. **Organic growth is unproven.** Every dollar of the +79% revenue growth came from acquisitions. Management itself concedes accelerated organic growth is a 2027-and-beyond story "once the tech stack and basic integration work have matured." No one has yet demonstrated QXO can grow a legacy branch's sales.
3. **Integration overload.** Three major acquisitions in 15 months, ~7,800+ employees pre-TopBuild, ERP/tech-stack migrations, and now a CEO splitting time with a ninth company. Distributor integrations routinely destroy more value than the synergies they promise.
4. **Continued dilution.** At 12–17x purchase multiples, funding deals with stock at $13.78 is value-destructive; funding with debt at 6x leverage is risk-destructive. Both bad options remain on the table.
5. **Cyclical margin compression.** Q1 2026's 14.2% gross margin and near-zero Adjusted EBITDA in a $1.73B revenue quarter shows how quickly reported profitability deteriorates in the seasonally weak, deflationary part of the cycle.

**Monitoring triggers:** (a) the 9/30/26 10-Q for actual post-TopBuild debt; (b) organic (same-branch) sales growth disclosed for the first time; (c) net leverage trajectory and any rating action; (d) whether Jacobs' CKX role expands; (e) purchase multiples on the next deal.
