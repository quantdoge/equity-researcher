Cboe is a **predominantly US business by revenue**, with an international footprint that has *contracted* materially via divestiture. **Geographic revenue anchor points (sourced):** Q3 2026 net revenue $712.2M, of which **Europe & APAC = $73.3M (+6% YoY, +7% constant currency)** ≈ **10.3%** of net revenue — the only disclosed international line item. Prior periods: Q2 2026 $84.8M; Q4 2025 $69.9M (+24%); Q2 2025 $70.4M (+30%). Europe & APAC is a **single reportable segment alongside Options, North American Equities, Futures and Global FX.**

**Structural read:** Cboe has *deliberately reduced* its geopolitical surface area. The October 2025 strategic realignment led to the April 22, 2026 agreement to sell **Cboe Australia and Cboe Canada to TMX Group for ~US$300M** — Australia closed July 31, 2026 (gain $71.5M); Canada remains subject to approvals. Management states the realignment should be "substantially complete by year-end 2026" with ~$7.7M of compensation cost remaining. This removes two small, low-margin, non-core regulatory jurisdictions and concentrates exposure in the US, EU and UK — i.e., **high-rule-of-law jurisdictions with low expropriation/currency-control risk, but dense regulatory oversight.**

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US event-contract / prediction-market jurisdiction war (HIGHEST)**
- *What:* Cboe is scaling "Cboe Predicts": it received **SEC approvals to list and clear company-KPI binary event contracts**, launching November 2026 with **Robinhood, tied to 23 companies**. Simultaneously the industry faces a multi-front jurisdictional battle — **CFTC reaffirmed exclusive jurisdiction in a US Circuit Court filing (17 Feb 2026)**; the CFTC **withdrew its own proposed rulemaking in Feb 2026** citing "state regulatory actions and litigation"; the **CFTC has sued Wisconsin** over state gambling claims; Senators Reed and Hickenlooper (5 Mar 2026) pushed for review of military-related event contracts.
- *Probability of material adverse development:* **Medium-High (50–60%)** — state gaming enforcement or adverse appellate ruling is plausible within 12–24 months.
- *Impact:* **Medium-High.** This is Cboe's headline growth narrative. A hostile ruling could strand product investment and invite SEC/CFTC turf conflict. Not existential (core SPX/VIX untouched) but a narrative/multiple risk.
- *Read-through:* Highest **political/reputational** risk in the book. Election- and geopolitics-linked contracts attract Congressional scrutiny.

**Risk 2 — US securities/derivatives rulemaking & SRO regulatory-cost creep (STRUCTURAL)**
- *What:* Cboe operates **six national securities exchanges as self-regulatory organisations**, making it simultaneously a regulated entity *and* a regulator — an inherent conflict repeatedly probed (notably around the 2021 Chi-X Asia Pacific acquisition). Live items: the **Options Regulatory Fee (ORF)** sunset was extended only to **June 30, 2026** (Federal Register, Dec 2025); CAT funding fee schedules were amended in 2026 (e.g., SR-CboeEDGX-2026-029; Cboe Options CAT funding fees, May 2026); and the **SEC opened a Concept Release on CAT (S7-2026-12)** that could fundamentally reshape the utility.
- *Probability:* **High (~70%)** of continued rulemaking friction; **Low-Medium** of a single materially adverse rule.
- *Impact:* **Medium.** Regulatory fee revenue is real revenue at risk; compliance cost drags margin. Record **0DTE index-option volumes** (Q3 2026 index options ADV +24%) and the Oct 19, 2026 launch of **extended pre/post-market single-stock and ETF options sessions** invite market-structure scrutiny.

**Risk 3 — EU/UK regulatory divergence in the remaining international segment**
- *What:* Europe & APAC (~10% of net revenue) runs through **Cboe Europe B.V. (Dutch AFM/MoF authorisation)** and **Cboe UK (FCA)**. Exposure is to MiFID II/MiFIR review, consolidated-tape policy, and post-Brexit UK–EU divergence forcing duplicate infrastructure. The segment decelerated to **+6% in Q3 2026** (from +30% in Q2 2025) partly on softer data and access fees.
- *Probability of adverse change:* **Medium (40%)** — ongoing, incremental rather than abrupt.
- *Impact:* **Low-Medium.** ~10% of revenue; Europe is Cboe's largest pan-European venue by market share, so competitive relevance exceeds its revenue contribution.

*Explicitly considered and assessed as LOW for Cboe:* Russia/Ukraine sanctions exposure (no disclosed Russia operations or revenue); direct China/Taiwan exposure (immaterial); nationalisation or capital-control risk in any material jurisdiction. **The single largest "geopolitical" risk to CBOE is not foreign — it is US domestic regulatory and political risk.**

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **CAT cost-recovery impairment ($14.0M reserve)** — a rare instance of Cboe taking a credit reserve against a regulatory-industry receivable. Analytically this is a *litigation/regulatory-financing* risk, not a control failure, but it is the one item that moved the P&L this quarter outside operations.
2. **SRO structural conflict** — Cboe is both market operator and self-regulator across six exchanges. This is an enduring governance/optics flag (historically raised around Chi-X Asia Pacific) and a standing source of SEC scrutiny.
3. **Prediction-market state-gaming exposure** — new product line deliberately placed in a contested federal/state jurisdiction. Escalating state AG enforcement is a genuine, if bounded, compliance risk.
4. **Extended trading hours** — the Oct 19, 2026 pre/post-market single-stock and ETF options launch carries surveillance and Reg SCI operational-resilience obligations.
5. **Not flagged:** no evidence of FCPA/bribery, AML, sanctions, or data-privacy (GDPR) enforcement issues. Given US/EU/UK-only operations, **FCPA and sanctions exposure is inherently low.**

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **LOW–MEDIUM**

**Rationale.** Cboe's *geopolitical* risk is **Low**: ~90% US revenue, remaining exposure in the Netherlands, UK and Japan — all Tier-1 rule-of-law jurisdictions with negligible expropriation, currency-repatriation, sanctions or export-control risk. The divestiture of Australia (closed) and Canada (pending) further reduces jurisdictional sprawl. There is no Russia, China, or emerging-market tail risk in the revenue base.

Its *legal/regulatory* risk is **Medium**: the company lives inside the densest regulatory environment in global finance, operates as a self-regulator with an inherent conflict, is currently absorbing CAT funding-model impairment, and is simultaneously pushing a legally contested event-contract product into a live federal–state turf war. Offsets are strong — the S&P licence locked to 2051, no disclosed material corporate enforcement or antitrust actions, $2.66B cash, an undrawn $400M revolver, and a beta of 0.41 indicating the market does not price this as a high-risk name.

**Monitor list:** (i) Cboe Canada deal close and any condition imposed; (ii) further CAT reserve build or SEC CAT restructuring outcome; (iii) state AG / appellate rulings on event contracts; (iv) ORF sunset at June 30, 2026 and its successor fee regime; (v) Europe & APAC growth deceleration (+6% in Q3 2026 vs +30% a year earlier).
