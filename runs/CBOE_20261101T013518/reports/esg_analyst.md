## CBOE — ESG & Sustainability Analysis
**Cboe Global Markets, Inc. | Financial Services — Financial Data & Stock Exchanges | US (global ops) | Mkt cap ~$35.0B | ~1,661 employees**

---

### 1. ESG Materiality Map

Cboe is an asset-light, regulated-market-infrastructure business: it owns no plants, holds modest office space, and its key assets are (a) its regulatory licences/self-regulatory-organisation (SRO) status, (b) proprietary index IP (VIX, SPX), and (c) technology. Materiality is therefore heavily weighted to **G** and the **social "product" dimension**, with **E** largely immaterial financially.

| Factor | Materiality | Why it moves cash flows / cost of capital |
|---|---|---|
| **G – Regulatory & compliance oversight quality** | **Critical** | SRO status is the licence to operate; SEC/CFTC findings can gate product listing, fee changes and index IP economics |
| **G – Board independence, shareholder rights, compensation alignment** | **High** | Proxy-adviser pressure, ESG-index exclusion risk, takeover/entrenchment concerns |
| **S – Product suitability / retail investor protection** (0DTE options, prediction markets) | **High** (rising fast) | Single largest source of revenue-durability and litigation/regulatory headline risk |
| **S – Technology resilience & cybersecurity** (market integrity) | **High** | An outage or breach is an existential-trust event for an exchange; also a regulated obligation |
| **G – Anti-corruption, political spending/lobbying transparency** | **Medium** | 56% shareholder support for political-disclosure proposal; reputational/AGM risk |
| **S – Talent, human capital, diversity** | **Low–Medium** | 1,661 FTE; key-person/IP risk concentrated in index and technology teams |
| **E – Scope 1/2 emissions, data-centre energy** | **Low** | Immaterial cost/revenue line; no carbon-tax or stranded-asset exposure |
| **E – Scope 3 / financed emissions** | **Not material** | No lending, underwriting or balance-sheet credit exposure; index/ETP listings are not "financed emissions" in a bank sense |

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- **Operational footprint is negligible**: office- and data-centre-based operations, LEED-certified Chicago HQ, renewable-energy procurement and REC purchases, plus purchased carbon credits (company disclosure). No manufacturing, no Scope 1 process emissions of consequence.
- **No climate transition exposure**: no carbon-tax-exposed cost base, no stranded assets, no CBAM-relevant goods. Physical climate risk is limited to data-centre siting and business-continuity (Chicago, Kansas City, London, Amsterdam, Singapore).
- **Disclosure gaps (material to ESG-mandate holders, not to valuation)**: I could not verify quantified Scope 1/2 inventory, a science-based or net-zero target, or Scope 3 (Category 1/3 IT and travel) from accessible sources — the 2024/2025 stewardship reports are PDF-only and were not machine-readable in this session. The absence of a public, SBTi-validated target is a mild "ESG-laggard" flag versus European exchange peers (LSEG, Euronext) but carries **no measurable financial impact**.
- **Environmental controversies**: none identified in the review period. No spills, fines, or remediation liabilities.

---

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Key exposure — product safety / retail market structure.** This is the only social factor with real financial consequences:
- **0DTE (zero-days-to-expiry) options** now exceed **60% of SPX options volume**; 0DTE contracts were up **46% YTD in 2026** to >20M contracts. Cboe's own research rebuts "high volume = high risk," but regulators (SEC, and internationally ESMA/FCA) increasingly focus on short-dated retail speculation. Any rulemaking restricting 0DTE or retail options access would hit Cboe's highest-growth, highest-margin revenue pool (index options).
- **Prediction markets (Cboe Predicts)**: launched on S&P 500 outcomes with defined-risk, exchange-backed contracts, and Cboe is seeking SEC jurisdiction for binary options on corporate KPIs. Strategically sensible, but it embeds **gambling-adjacent, suitability, and insider-trading** controversy into a franchise whose brand is "trusted, regulated market operator." Management initially said it would avoid sports (Bloomberg, Nov 2025) — a deliberate reputational guardrail worth monitoring.
- **Human capital / diversity is a relative strength**: small, specialised workforce; the board is notably diverse (6 of 12 directors are women, plus two directors from under-represented groups by name/background); Associate Resource Groups, matched giving up to $15k/associate, paid volunteer days.
- **No supply-chain labour or community-relations exposure** (no manufacturing supply chain; community impact limited to charitable programmes).
- **Litigation history (mostly resolved)**: VIX-manipulation class actions were dismissed with prejudice and **affirmed by the Seventh Circuit (2022)** — no remaining material liability.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10** (Good, not best-in-class)

**Supporting evidence:**
- **Board**: 12 directors, all re-elected by majority vote to **one-year terms** (declassified board — a genuine positive) at the 14 May 2026 AGM. **Non-executive independent Chairman William M. Farrow III** — no CEO/Chairman duality. CEO Craig S. Donohue is the only member of management on the board.
- **2025 leadership transition handled in order**: Fred Tomczyk (CEO from 2023) handed to industry veteran Craig Donohue in May 2025; say-on-pay passed in both 2025 and 2026 with no disclosed material dissent; KPMG ratified as auditor.
- **Compensation**: heavily equity-weighted (≈95% performance/stock-linked at the top), modest absolute levels (~$18M CEO total comp) for a $35B-cap company — well aligned with long-term TSR; the Q3 2026 print (record diluted EPS $4.08, +43%; net revenue +18%) and raised organic-growth targets suggest the incentive plan is biting.

**Deductions (−3):**
1. **Shareholder rights are restrictive for a large-cap**: shareholders **cannot act by written consent** (proposal rejected at the 2026 AGM) and the special-meeting threshold is a demanding **25% of all shares outstanding**. The written-consent proposal drew ~44% support previously — meaningful minority dissent.
2. **Board responsiveness on political spending**: a 2025 political-spending disclosure proposal reportedly attracted **~56% support**, yet Cboe's disclosure remains FEC-minimum-based (lobbying ~$0.38M in 2026). Majority-backed proposals not adopted is a classic governance-quality discount in proxy-adviser scoring.
3. **Board independence nuance**: while no CEO/Chair duality exists, the **immediate former CEO (Tomczyk) continues to serve as a director** — a cooling-off consideration, and the board has had three CEO configurations since 2023 (Tilly's 2023 resignation over undisclosed personal relationships, Tomczyk interim, Donohue). **SRO oversight risk is structurally concentrated** in a small board with a single regulatory-experienced cohort.

No dual-class share structure; no poison pill disclosed; ethics/anti-corruption programme exists with an associate ESG committee reporting into board risk oversight.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US SEC**: exchange registration, Reg NMS, market-data fee rulemaking — **the dominant regulatory channel**; SEC climate-disclosure rule is not material to a company with immaterial emissions, and has been in litigation/rollback. Political-spending and write-consent proposals remain the live shareholder-engagement vectors.
- **CFTC**: event-contract/prediction-market jurisdiction; joint SEC–CFTC request for comment on "novel or emerging products" (2026) is the key binary for Cboe Predicts.
- **EU CSRD / EU Taxonomy**: moderate exposure via **Cboe Europe** (equities, ETP listings, Cboe Clear Europe). Group-level double-materiality reporting may be triggered through EU subsidiaries; cost is disclosure-grade, not cash-flow-grade. **CBAM: not applicable.**
- **UK FCA / ESMA**: market-operator conduct, operational resilience (DORA-style outsourcing/ICT rules) — relevant to data-centre and vendor dependencies.
- **US states**: California SB 253/261 scope questions (revenue >$1B "doing business" test) — a disclosure watch item, not a financial risk.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants**: none identified; capital structure is plain vanilla (dividend payer, $0.72/qtr), so no ESG-covenant step-up risk.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**For the thesis (long, post-Q3-2026 upgrade case), ESG is not a re-rating catalyst and not a de-rating risk — it is a monitored tail.**

- **No environmental or financing-channel penalty**: no ESG-linked cost of capital, no stranded-asset narrative, no CSRD-driven capex. Cheap on this dimension versus industrial peers.
- **The one ESG factor that is financially material is social/regulatory product risk** — 0DTE concentration (>60% of SPX volume) plus prediction markets put a **policy beta** into the growth story. If the SEC restricts short-dated retail options or the CFTC/state-gaming patchwork poisons event contracts, the multiple (already at a ~25% discount to its 5-year average P/E on the bull case) would face a narrative reset, not just a revenue haircut. Conversely, Cboe's "regulated, exchange-backed, defined-risk" positioning on prediction markets is a defensible, lower-controversy entry than Kalshi/Polymarket — a potential qualitative premium if the sports-gambling blow-up occurs elsewhere.
- **Governance is the marginal swing factor**: with ~56% support for political-spending disclosure and ~44% for written consent, expect continued proxy-adviser pressure and a rising risk of "against" votes on the compensation or governance-committee chair. This does not impair cash flows but can compress the ESG-mandate ownership base.
- **Positioning**: treat Cboe as an **ESG-tolerable compounder** — acceptable in a broad mandate, acceptable to most sustainability-screened funds (as Parnassus demonstrates), but it will never be an "ESG leader" holding. Escalation triggers that would change the implication to **Negative**: (1) an SEC/CFTC enforcement action on 0DTE or prediction-market marketing; (2) a trading outage or cyber incident tied to control failures; (3) a majority-supported shareholder proposal being ignored a second consecutive year, paired with a failed say-on-pay.
