## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE**

**Evidence supporting:**
- **Declassified board:** all 12 nominees elected to one-year terms at the May 14, 2026 annual meeting; all received majority support. No plurality/contested structure.
- **Shareholder accountability functioning:** say-on-pay **approved**, KPMG ratified as 2026 auditor. Management-backed positions carried.
- **Board acted decisively on a prior tone-at-the-top failure:** Sept 2023, then-Chair/CEO **Edward Tilly resigned** after a board-led investigation found he failed to disclose personal relationships with colleagues, violating company policy. The board's willingness to remove a sitting chair/CEO is a positive governance signal — but the episode itself evidences disclosure-culture weakness and previously excessive concentration of power (combined Chair/CEO role).
- **Structural mitigant:** as an SRO operator, Cboe maintains separate self-regulatory/regulatory oversight functions and subsidiary boards (Cboe Exchange, C2, BZX, EDGX, Cboe Clear Europe) — a meaningful independent-compliance architecture.
- **Capital discipline:** 16th consecutive dividend increase (Q3 2026 declared +19% to $0.86/qtr); divested Cboe Australia and Canada for $300M (Apr 2026) — portfolio simplification rather than empire-building.

**Weaknesses / open items:**
- **CEO tenure and independence of chair:** Craig Donohue became CEO in May 2025 (~1.4 years) and sits on the board. Proxy confirms he is a director; I could not verify whether the Chair role is independently held (proxy PDF and EDGAR HTML both failed to retrieve — see data gaps). Confirm before relying on board independence.
- **Compensation magnitude:** reported total CEO compensation ~$18.3M, heavily equity-weighted, at a firm with 1,661 employees. Equity alignment is good; *absolute* level and any one-time transition awards (plus a **COO retirement consulting arrangement through Dec 31, 2026**, per 8-K) warrant a pay-for-performance peer test.
- **Entrenchment tools remain:** shareholders *rejected* a written-consent-rights proposal the board opposed — the board retains a defensive posture on shareholder rights expansions.
- **High executive turnover since 2023** (CEO, COO) is a governance-continuity consideration.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**Enforcement history: CLEAN.** EDGAR full-text search returned **no SEC enforcement actions** naming CBOE.

Active and structural exposures:
1. **Prediction-market jurisdiction war (highest-severity regulatory item).** Kalshi formally **asked the SEC in Aug 2026 to slow Cboe's product launches**; the SEC/CFTC signed a coordination MOU (Mar 11, 2026) and the SEC nonetheless approved Cboe's **binary KPI options** (Amendment No. 1 order, published Federal Register Oct 16, 2026; 100+ metrics across 23 issuers; **zero fees through end-2026**). Kalshi's litigation is trending toward appellate/Supreme Court review. Regulatory outcomes here can *create or destroy* a growth vector overnight — a binary, non-diversifiable policy risk.
2. **Novel surveillance obligation on KPI contracts.** Issuer-specific binary contracts on revenue/EPS-type metrics create **MNPI and insider-trading detection risk** (trading ahead of KPI disclosure), plus manipulation and "event-contract" suitability scrutiny. Commentators are already flagging compliance and insider-trading risk. This is a live SEC-examination and enforcement-risk vector, and a first-mover reputational liability.
3. **SRO rule-filing dependence.** Revenue levers (fee schedules, product listings, RPC structures) all run through SR-CBOE rule filings subject to SEC approval and public comment — competitors and critics can (and do) use the comment process to delay.
4. **European/UK perimeter.** Cboe Europe, Cboe Clear Europe: ESMA/BoE/ECB supervision; EMIR-related approvals for the **Aug 2026 expansion of SFT clearing into EU/UK/Swiss/US fixed income** (new CCP default-fund and margin exposures); MiFID/MiFIR market-data and consolidated-tape rules; GDPR/CCPA data-privacy exposure from Data Vantage market-data products.
5. **Contractual-licensing risk treated as regulatory:** exclusive **SPX options rights secured through 2051** (S&P DJI) plus FTSE Russell, MSCI, DJI relationships. Long-dated licenses materially de-risk a former core regulatory/contractual vulnerability.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Product/revenue concentration in index options.** Index options (SPX, VIX, 0DTE) drive a disproportionate share of net revenue and are the growth engine behind the raised "high-teens" organic growth target. A low-volatility regime, structural decline in 0DTE retail activity, or a competitor's exclusive index license would hit the franchise disproportionately. The 2051 SPX license *locks in* the dependency while removing renewal risk.
- **Platform and technology resilience.** Exchange outages are reputation-defining events. Cboe has a documented history of exchange downtime (C1 order-handler hardware failures impacting FIX/BOE connectivity; the 2013 full-session failure) and peers (CME, 2025) show industry-wide fragility. Risk is amplified by a **1,661-person headcount** — thin relative to the mission-criticality of the infrastructure and heavily dependent on a small set of key engineers and on colocation/cloud vendors.
- **Key-person and clearing-house operational risk.** New CEO (1.4 yrs), a retiring COO (consulting only through Dec 2026), and a small senior bench; separately, Cboe Clear Europe's new fixed-income SFT clearing (launched Aug 24, 2026) imports **CCP operational, margin and settlement risk** into a business historically free of balance-sheet clearing exposure.
- **Cyber/vendor concentration:** high-value market-infrastructure target; market-data distribution and matching-engine third parties are single points of failure. No breach disclosed, but disclosure of an incident would be market-moving.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **MODERATE**

- **Positives:** clean enforcement record, strong operating narrative (Q3 beat, guidance raised twice in 2026), dividend growth, portfolio simplification. No product recall/crisis/ESG controversy of consequence; low carbon-intensity.
- **Pressure points:**
  - **"Regulatory arbitrage / incumbent protection" narrative** — being publicly characterized as the target of Kalshi's delay petition puts Cboe in the frame of the incumbent defending turf via regulators rather than product. Each escalation raises political and press risk.
  - **KPI binary options crackdown risk** — if any trade is shown to have front-run a KPI disclosure, Cboe's *listing venue* reputation (not just a product) is exposed.
  - **Retail-derivatives scrutiny** — ongoing media/academic criticism of 0DTE and short-dated options as "financial gambling" attaches to the largest index-options venue.
  - **Residual CEO-departure stigma** (2023 undisclosed-relationships resignation) still appears in coverage; the board's response mitigates but does not erase it.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

A clean regulatory record, adequate (recently stress-tested) board, functioning SRO compliance architecture, and long-dated index licenses offset by: (a) a genuinely unresolved prediction-market regulatory/political fight, (b) concentrated dependence on index-options volume and on market-infrastructure uptime, and (c) an early-tenure CEO with elevated absolute pay.
