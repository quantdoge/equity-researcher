# Sector Brief — U.S. Fast-Casual & Quick-Service Restaurants
**Target:** Chipotle Mexican Grill (CMG) | **Peer set:** CAVA, SHAK, WING, TXRH, MCD, YUM, DPZ

## 1. Sector Overview
The U.S. restaurant industry is in a **traffic-negative, value-led environment**. Through FY2025/2026, U.S. QSR traffic has been declining year-over-year (e.g., April 2025 U.S. QSR traffic −1.7%; April 2026 industry traffic −2.3% while sales only +1.5% per Black Box Intelligence), as inflation and ~$3.50 gasoline pressured consumers. Fast-casual is outgrowing QSR because it skews higher-income and leans on digital/takeout, but the group is not immune — same-restaurant sales decelerated sharply into late 2025 (CMG comps went negative, Wingstop went sharply negative in 2026). The cycle looks like the **late-normalization phase** of post-COVID restaurant demand: unit growth and digital adoption still drive low-to-mid-single-digit top-line growth, but same-store sales and margin expansion are under pressure from commodity/labor costs and a price-sensitive consumer.

## 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Digital/off-premises penetration** continues to grow and skews transaction economics favorably — CMG digital = 36.7% of F&B revenue FY2025 and 38.3% by Q2 2026 (Chipotle press release); Wingstop is ~mid-60s% digital.
- **Category mix-shift to fast-casual** with fresher/platform brands (CAVA FY2025 revenue +22.5% and Q1'26 same-restaurant sales +9.7%) growing well above QSR incumbents.
- **Strong unit-growth pipelines** — CMG added ~345 units to 4,056 (long-term target ~7,000); Wingstop added a record 493 net units (+19.2% unit growth, system sales +12.1%).

## 3. Key Headwinds
- **Negative/weak restaurant traffic** across the sector; basket/check growth is doing the heavy lifting (April 2026 industry traffic −2.3%, Black Box Intelligence).
- **Commodity inflation**, notably beef/chicken and now cheese/labor — SHAK restaurant-level margin slipped to 22.6% partly on beef pricing; CMG annual operating margin fell to 16.2% (from 16.9%).
- **Value-price competition / pricing-power ceiling** — MCD and QSRs leaning heavily on value bundles to defend traffic; fast-casual brands that raised prices (CMG comp −1.7% FY2025, transactions −3.2% in Q4) are giving back share.

## 6. Competitive Positioning
- **Digital:** Strong. CMG digital = 36.7% of F&B revenue (FY2025), rising to 38.3% (Q2'26). This is above most full-menu peers but below WING's digital-first model.
- **Throughput:** Historically a best-in-class leader, but the 2025 momentum broke — Q4 transactions fell −3.2% and FY comps went negative; rebound visible in Q2'26 (+2.2% comp, +9.3% revenue).
- **Pricing power:** High brand equity/AUV, but the 2025 volume-price trade-off (comp −1.7% with negative traffic) suggests pricing ran ahead of the current value-sensitive consumer. Peers caught up on price-value (MCD below, CAVA's affordability halo).

## 7. Benchmarking Summary (attributable numbers)

- **Margin:** CMG operating margin 16.2% (down 70bp YoY); expansion pause vs high-margin franchise/royalty peers (WING ~25.7% op. margin) and CAVA/SHAK restaurant-level ~25%/22.6%.
- **[Center] Competitive Position: AVERAGE-to-WEAKENING on 2025 momentum.** Stronger structurally (digital, brand, scale) than 2025 financials imply, but CMG is currently the growth laggard of the set with negative comps — a transitory valuation/positioning gap that hinges on the 5-point throughput recovery plan.

## 8. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE (with a normalized-growth caveat)**
Long-duration growth industry with durable digital/unit-growth tailwinds and structural winners (CAVA, SHAK, WING). However, we are mid-traffic-recession, so near-term comp expansion is limited; selectivity is required.
