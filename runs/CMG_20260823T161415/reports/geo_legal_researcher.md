## 2. Regulatory Risk by Jurisdiction

- **FDA / food safety (federal):** Core regulatory arena. 2020 DPA (monitorship, compliance program) expired per its 3-yr term (~2023), but FDA jurisdiction over supply-chain/prep/GMP and outbreak response remains the single most important regulatory lever given 2015–18 history. Any new large outbreak resets enforcement risk. Sources: DOJ (justice.gov) Apr 21, 2020; NBC News Apr 21, 2020.
- **California (state/local) — the key wage/regulatory jurisdiction:** Fast-food minimum wage now **$20.75/hr effective Jan 1, 2026** (with CPI indexing) and a **restored Fast Food Council** (2025 ballot initiative reviving the FAST Recovery Act) — the direct escalator for CMG's largest-cost labor market. Combined with aggressive CA Labor Code wage-hour/tip-pooling litigation (Section 351) and PAGA exposure, California is the highest regulatory-cost state. Sources: Advocate Magazine Nov 2024; wage-hour case law (Fisher Phillips; *Scott v. Chipotle*). *(Note: exact current CA fast-food wage rate should be double-checked against latest state data — flagged.)*
- **Other state/local minimum wages & paid-leave laws** (NYC, Seattle, Chicago, etc.): distributed compliance burden across 48 states; chronic wage-statement/labor-posting exposure.
- **Data privacy / breach-notification (multi-state):** Oct-2025 Workday/HR data incident triggered state AG breach notices (e.g., **Vermont AG, Dec 23, 2025**); exposure under state breach statutes and **CCPA/CPRA** (CA) for data-security and employee personal information. Source: ago.vermont.gov Dec 23, 2025; Claim Depot Dec 24, 2025; Bloomberg Law Feb 11, 2026.
- **Franchise laws:** **Low risk** — CMG is essentially ~100% company-operated (restaurant ownership, not franchising); franchise registration/fairness (FTC Franchise Rule) exposure is minimal.
- **Labor board (federal):** NLRB jurisdiction over the unionized stores (section 4).
- **No direct financial/sanctions regulator exposure** identified (no banking, no OFAC-sensitive counterparties surfaced).

## 3. Geopolitical & Supply-Chain Exposure

- **Avocado dependency (the dominant geopolitical input):** ~**50% of avocados sourced from Mexico**; the rest from **Colombia, Peru, Dominican Republic** (diversification over a ~7-yr program). Mexico supplies ~90% of US avocado consumption, so the chain's own Mexico share is below market — but guacamole/avocados remain its signature cost input. Sources: CNBC (CEO Scott Boatwright, Feb 4, 2025); WSJ Apr 2, 2025; Fortune Mar 3, 2025.
- **Tariff/trade policy (US–Mexico):** Trump administration imposed a **25% tariff on Mexican imports (February 2025)**, temporarily paused, and CMG has **factored a possible 25% Mexico tariff into 2025 financial expectations** (WSJ, Apr 2, 2025). Also noted **10% tariffs on Peru/Colombia origin** (per reporting). Key mitigant: diversifying origin to non-Mexico growers; but beef/limes/produce and some "made-in-Mexico" inputs still carry import-cost risk. Business Insider Feb 4, 2025 flags CMG "could get impacted by Trump tariffs anyway."
- **Pricing/consumer trade-down risk** (indirect): tariff-driven food inflation → margin vs. consumer price sensitivity; CEO has vowed to hold prices "as long as possible" (Fortune, Mar 3, 2025).
- **No meaningful US–China, Russia, or sanctions exposure** identified in this chain's footprint and sourcing. The geopolitics here is **hemispheric/Tariff-era**, not East-West.
- **Geographic footprint:** overwhelmingly **US-centric**. FY2025 revenue **$11.9B (+5.4%)**; **4,056 restaurants total as of Dec 31, 2025**, with a small international presence (Canada, UK, France, Germany). Sources: Chipotle IR Feb 3, 2026; Food Ingredients First Feb 12, 2026. Exact international revenue % not surfaced (see Data Gaps).

## 4. Labor / Unionization / NLRB Risk

- **Precedent-setting union activity at company-owned stores:**
  - **Augusta, Maine** — first Chipotle store to unionize (2022); Chipotle permanently closed the store, workers filed NLRB charges, and CMG reached a ~**$240,000 settlement** with former employees (Mar 2023). Source: Reuters Jun 23, 2022; AP News Mar 27, 2023; CNBC Jul 20, 2022.
  - **Lansing, Michigan** — the chain's only remaining unionized store (International Brotherhood of Teamsters); the **NLRB's Detroit regional director found merit (Aug 28, 2024)** to allegations CMG unlawfully disciplined a worker for union activity and told employees they could not get raises because of the union. Source: AP via The Chief, Aug 30, 2024.
- **Structural context:** 4,000+ company-owned stores with high crew turnover = the classic NLRB/union-organization target set; a **friendlier pro-union NLRB posture** (2024–25) raises the probability that individual-store complaints become liability and precedents. EPI (Jan 28, 2025) documents industry-wide union-busting scrutiny amplifying this.
- **FAST/independent-contractor and tip laws** in CA add labor-policy overlay.
- **Materiality:** Low–Medium *per store*, but with franchise-wide reputational/operational risk because **all stores are company-owned** (no franchisee buffer); a unionization wave at corporate units would directly hit a ~$2.4–2.6B labor line (proprietary point — see gaps).

## Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **Medium**

**Rationale:** No currently *material* adverse judgment looms (the high-profile portion-size securities suit was won by CMG), and geopolitical exposure is confined to **hemispheric tariff risk on a ~50%-Mexico avocado supply** that CMG has been actively diversifying. Offsetting to Medium: (i) recurring **wage-and-hour + EEOC** class/consent-decree costs across a company-owned, 4,000-store footprint; (ii) **California** regulatory escalation (fast-food minimum wage indexing + restored Fast Food Council); (iii) **residual food-safety** legal convexity rooted in the 2015–18 outbreaks and 2020 $25M DPA; (iv) **labor-relations/NLRB** vulnerability given 100% company ownership and recent adverse NLRB rulings; and (v) **data-privacy** exposure from the Oct-2025 HR breach. Tariff policy (US–Mexico) is the single most trackable external variable, with CMD management already building a 25% Mexico tariff into guidance.
