# Macroeconomic Assessment — Lattice Semiconductor (LSCC)

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Stance: Transitional (late-cycle re-acceleration with a hawkish Fed)**

LSCC is trading in the most hostile rates regime of the past four years, and it shows in the chart. The striking feature of this cycle is the **decoupling of growth from policy**: the US economy re-accelerated to 3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026 while inflation, instead of converging to 2%, **re-accelerated to 3.72% YoY**. That combination has forced the Fed to abandon its easing cycle and hike again.

The sequencing matters:

- The Fed cut from 4.33% (mid-2025) to an effective 3.63% through June–August 2026.
- Inflation bottomed at **2.39% YoY in January 2026** — the apparent all-clear.
- CPI then climbed steadily: 2.43% (Feb) → 3.29% (Mar) → 3.78% (Apr) → 4.17% (May) → 3.30% (Jul) → 3.35% (Aug) → **3.72% (Sep)**.
- The Fed **hiked ~25bp in mid-September 2026**, lifting the effective rate from 3.63% to 3.75% (Sep) and 3.88% (Oct) — target now **3.75%–4.00%**.

Meanwhile the long end has sold off hard on term premium and fiscal supply: the 10Y went from **4.45% (29 May)** to a peak of **5.31% (5 Oct)** before easing to **4.95%–5.13%**, and the 30Y sits at **5.47%**. The curve is bear-steepening (**2s10s +39bp**, no longer inverted) — historically pro-cyclical, but here it is being driven by term premium rather than growth expectations, which is the worse of the two varieties for long-duration equities.

This is precisely the environment where a 1.89-beta, high-multiple growth stock de-rates regardless of fundamentals — and LSCC's price action confirms it: peak **$155.27 (22 Jun)** → trough **$92.97 (30 Oct)**, a **−40% drawdown** that maps almost one-for-one onto the **+85bp** move in the 10Y over the same window.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**LSCC's exposure profile:** fabless FPGA designer (1,174 employees), revenue skewed to **Asia, industrial, automotive, and communications/computing**. Two structural sensitivities dominate — **discount rate** (high-multiple, long-duration cash flows) and **Asia/China revenue + Taiwan-based supply chain** (TSMC).

| Macro Variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **Fed Funds 3.75–4.00%, hiking** | **Negative** | The pivot back to hikes removes the multiple-expansion catalyst. Growth stocks need the *direction* of policy to be down; it is now up. |
| **10Y at 5.13% / 30Y at 5.47%** | **Strongly Negative** | The dominant variable. A ~60–85bp rise in the discount rate has already driven a ~40% drawdown. Empirically, each 25bp on the 10Y costs a high-multiple semi name roughly 5–7% of multiple. |
| **Bear-steepening curve (+39bp)** | **Negative / ambiguous** | Normally pro-cyclical and good for capex demand, but this steepening is term-premium and fiscal-supply driven, not growth driven. Long rates hurt LSCC; the steepening is not the benign kind. |
| **CPI 3.72%, re-accelerating** | **Neutral to slightly negative** | Input-cost pressure (wafer, substrate, packaging, freight) is real. But LSCC is demonstrating **pricing power** — Q3 2026 revenue of **$291.0M, +118.3% YoY**, a record, on supply-constrained FPGA demand. Pricing power offsets cost inflation. |
| **GDP 3.4% SAAR** | **Positive** | The single strongest tailwind. Industrial and auto capex, plus communications/compute buildout, is where Lattice's low-power FPGAs sit. Demand is not the problem — the multiple is. |
| **Unemployment 4.2%, full employment** | **Positive** | Supports end-market demand. Wage inflation is a negligible cost line for a fabless firm with ~1,200 staff, so the usual labour-cost channel is largely irrelevant here. |
| **USD firming (+3% since Sept)** | **Negative** | Lattice sells overwhelmingly into Asia/Europe. A stronger dollar is a direct translation headwind to reported revenue and a modest competitiveness drag. A 3% USD move ≈ 1.5–2% reported revenue headwind. |
| **Tariffs / export controls (Asia supply chain)** | **Negative — largest tail risk** | Fabless model means TSMC (Taiwan) is a single-point-of-failure. Section 232 semiconductor tariffs and China export-control escalation are the highest-variance macro input, and cannot be hedged. |

**Cross-check on rate sensitivity:** the 10Y bottomed at 4.45% on 29 May 2026 and LSCC peaked at $155.27 on 22 June — near-perfect coincidence. The 10Y peaked at 5.31% on 5 October and LSCC bottomed at $92.97 on 30 October. That is the cleanest evidence available that **this stock is currently trading as a rates instrument first and a semiconductor second**.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

**Rationale.** The rating is driven by the *valuation* channel, not the *earnings* channel, and the distinction is critical for the investment memo:

- **Fundamentals are excellent.** Record quarterly revenue, +118% YoY growth, an AI-adjacent FPGA franchise, and a re-accelerating economy at 3.4% GDP.
- **The macro is working against the multiple.** A hawkish Fed, a 5.13% 10Y, a 3.7% CPI that is moving the wrong way, and a firming dollar all compress the valuation a 1.89-beta name can sustain.
- **The stock's own behaviour validates the framing:** −40% peak-to-trough in four months *while fundamentals hit records*. That is a pure de-rating.

For a fabless, high-multiple growth semiconductor with heavy Asia exposure, I would characterise the current backdrop as a **strong demand environment in a hostile cost-of-capital regime**. Net: **Unfavourable**.

One nuance that keeps this from being outright bearish: the recent tape shows earnings can overpower rates. LSCC rallied **+11% on 3 November on 7.8M shares (~2.5x normal volume)** on the Q3 beat, and is back to $107.25 from the $92.97 low — a +15% bounce in three sessions. So the macro headwind is a ceiling on the multiple, not a floor under the price.

## 5. Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BEAR (Underappreciated)**

**The scenario: the Fed is not done, and the 10Y breaks 5.50%.**

The market is treating the September hike as a one-off. I think that is the wrong read. With **CPI at 3.72% and rising**, **GDP at 3.4%**, and **unemployment at 4.2%**, there is no slack anywhere in the economy to bring inflation down organically. The Fed's real policy rate is barely positive (~+15bp ex-ante on a 3.88% nominal rate against 3.72% CPI). That is not restrictive — it is approximately neutral in a 3.4%-growth economy.

If core CPI prints above 4% in the next two quarters, the Fed hikes again in December or Q1 2027. The 30Y at 5.47% is already screaming about term premium and fiscal supply; a second hike pushes the 10Y through 5.50% and plausibly to 5.75%.

**Transmission to LSCC:** using the observed empirical relationship (≈+85bp in the 10Y → ≈−40% in LSCC, i.e. roughly 12% of price per 25bp at the margin), another **+60bp** would pressure the multiple by **25–30%**, taking LSCC to **$75–80** *even if Q4 revenue beats again*. At that level the stock would be roughly −50% from its June peak and approaching its 52-week low of ~$61.73.

**Why this is a genuine surprise rather than consensus:** the market is positioned for the earnings story. The Q3 beat and the +11% single-day move show investors are anchoring on the +118% revenue growth and treating rates as a solved problem. The unresolved variable is that **inflation has risen for six of the last eight months** and the Fed has already broken its easing trajectory once. Nobody is priced for the Fed hiking twice.

**The bullish mirror-image scenario**, which I assign lower probability but is worth flagging: inflation rolls over sharply on base effects and energy, the Fed reverses to cuts by Q1 2027, and the 10Y falls back to 4.50%. LSCC would re-rate violently — a 60bp decline in the discount rate plus 100%+ revenue growth could drive the stock back through **$150** and toward new highs. This is the "everything goes right" case, and the stock's high beta cuts both ways.

**Position implication:** own the earnings, but size for the duration. The macro is not the reason to sell Lattice — it is the reason not to pay up for it at 50x+ in a 5%-plus long-bond world.
