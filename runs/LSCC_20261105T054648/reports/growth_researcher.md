# Lattice Semiconductor (LSCC) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Price $107.25 | Market cap $15.2B | EV ≈ $16.0B | Beta 1.89 | Nov 4, 2026**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Lattice has transformed from a cyclical low-power FPGA vendor into the **control, boot, security and power-management plane of the AI data center** — a socket it uniquely occupies because FPGAs are instant-on, deterministic and reprogrammable at the rack level, something ASSPs and MCUs structurally cannot match. The AMI acquisition (closed July 27, 2026, $1.65B) adds the dominant platform firmware franchise (BIOS/BMC/infrastructure manageability), converting a components business into a **silicon + firmware platform** with per-server content expanding every generation. The result: revenue growth went from **+2.7% in FY2025 to +118% in Q3 FY2026**, with guidance for +130% in Q4.

This is not a cyclical recovery — it is a genuine re-rating of the addressable socket.

## 3. TAM and Penetration

- Core low-power FPGA/control SAM: ~$6–7B (Lattice has historically framed SAM growing toward $11B+ by 2029–30)
- Server platform firmware/manageability (AMI: BIOS, BMC, rack orchestration): ~$2B, growing with AI rack complexity
- **Combined SAM: ~$9–10B today, expanding as AI rack content per server rises** (power sequencing → boot → security root of trust → thermal/cooling telemetry → full BMC consolidation)

**Penetration today is roughly 10–12%** of that combined SAM (FY26 revenue ~$1.0B). The critical nuance: this is a **content-per-system expansion story, not a unit story**. Every AI rack generation adds control sockets, so revenue can outgrow the underlying server market by 2–3x. Even the consensus $3.2B FY2030 revenue implies only ~30% SAM share — leaving substantial runway. The Arm AGI CPU collaboration (Oct 7, 2026), placing Lattice FPGAs *and* AMI firmware inside Arm's server CPU platform, is direct evidence of socket expansion beyond x86.

- **Ongoing — Mach-N2 secure control FPGA family and "Lattice Prompt" AI-assisted design tool**; new products already >70% YoY, targeting 25% of FPGA revenue.
- **Deleveraging → <2x net EBITDA by year-end**, with $50M already repaid in Q3 (long-term debt $869M). Balance-sheet clarity opens the door to broader institutional/index coverage (only ~10 analysts currently).

## 6. Growth Verdict: **STRONG BUY**

The market is **not** pricing in heroic outcomes. At ~$16B EV, LSCC trades at **16.0x FY26E sales → 9.9x FY27E → 7.9x FY28E**, and ~37x FY26E adjusted EBITDA — versus AMD at 109x EV/EBITDA growing 34% and AVGO at 47x growing 24%. On growth-adjusted EBITDA, Lattice is the *cheapest* of the three AI-semis compounders.

Reverse-DCF check: if FY2030 revenue merely meets the $3.2B consensus and the stock holds a 10x EV/S multiple, EV ≈ $32B → **~$220/share, ~20% IRR** from here. A modest beat ($4.0–4.5B at 11–12x) implies $45–55B EV, or **3x+**. Rule of 40 of ~119 with 72% gross margins and 41% operating margins means this is a *profitable* hyper-grower, not a hope trade.

Position sizing, not conviction, should reflect the 1.89 beta: this name will routinely hand back 30–40% (it already has once this year) while the thesis compounds.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Customer concentration in AI capex.** A large share of the +132% C&C growth sits in a handful of hyperscaler platforms. An architecture change or a socket loss at one customer is an existential-order risk to the growth rate.
2. **Altera is attacking the exact same socket.** Altera's Agilex 5/3 and MAX 10 explicitly market "every platform management socket from power sequencing to a consolidated BMC with hardware root of trust." Microchip and ASSP/BMC consolidation are secondary threats; in-house hyperscaler silicon is the tail risk.
3. **Acquisition integration and dilution.** $1.65B for AMI ($869M debt, $650M equity issued) at **20.7x book**. Non-GAAP is doing heavy lifting: **SBC was $50.3M in Q3 — 17% of revenue** — leaving GAAP EPS at $0.08 and GAAP operating margin in the low single digits. GAAP P/E of ~4,800x is meaningless, but a market shift toward GAAP discipline would compress the multiple fast.
4. **Not cycle-proof.** FY2024 revenue fell **−30.9%**. Channel inventory, industrial capex and communications spending can reverse abruptly; the current channel is described as "healthy," which historically marks a mid-cycle, not a floor.
5. **Stale estimates create asymmetric reaction risk.** Consensus FY2027 EPS of $2.51 is clearly too low relative to FY2026 non-GAAP of ~$2.50. That is upside if guidance is strong — but if management guides conservatively into a 16x sales multiple, the reflex sell-off will be violent.
6. **Execution on the 2028 security-retrofit ramp.** Material revenue from that program is two years out; if it slips, the FY2028 $2.0B consensus loses its newest leg.
