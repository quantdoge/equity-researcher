## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW**

Lattice is the leader in *low-power, small-density FPGAs* — iCE40, MachXO, CrossLink-NX, Certus-NX — used for power sequencing, interface bridging, and glue logic in industrial, auto, comms and, increasingly, data-center servers. The moat is real but narrow:

**Evidence for the moat:**
- Gross margin expanded from **60.1% (FY2020) → 68.2% (FY2025) → 67–70% quarterly in FY2026**. An 800bp expansion is genuine evidence of mix improvement and pricing power, not commodity behavior.
- Classic switching costs: FPGA design-ins are socket-level, locked by the Radiant toolchain, and product life cycles run 10–15 years. Once design-win, revenue is annuity-like.
- #1 share in a niche too small for AMD/Xilinx or Intel to prioritize.

**Evidence against:**
- **The moat did not protect revenue.** FY2024 revenue fell **-30.9%** and FY2025 grew only +2.7%. A wide-moat business does not lose a third of its revenue in a downdraft. This is a cyclical, inventory-sensitive parts business.
- **The moat is expensive to maintain.** R&D runs ~36% of revenue (FY2025 opex $341.6M less SG&A $153.6M ≈ $188M). Moat durability requires relentless reinvestment — the opposite of a See's Candies.
- The addressable niche is contested: Microchip (PolarFire), AMD (Spartan), Efinix, and Chinese entrants (Gowin) all press the same sockets.
- **The AMI pivot is itself an admission.** Management is paying $1.65B — more than 3x Lattice's own FY2025 revenue — to reposition from "low-power FPGA" to "secure management & control platform." That is not what a company confident in its widening moat does.

**Verdict:** Narrow moat, widening on margin, narrowing on competitive intensity. Understandable business — but no longer a *simple* one.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **5 / 10**

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | 9M FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 660.4 | 737.2 | 509.4 | 523.3 | 663.0 |
| YoY growth | +28.1% | +11.6% | **-30.9%** | +2.7% | **+91%** |
| Gross margin | 68.5% | 69.8% | 66.8% | 68.2% | 68.8% |
| GAAP op margin | 28.4% | 28.8% | 6.8% | **2.9%** | **11.0%** |
| GAAP net income ($M) | 178.9 | 259.1 | 61.1 | **3.1** | 52.4 |
| Free cash flow ($M) | 215.5 | 249.5 | 119.9 | 132.6 | n/a |
| ROIC | 29.5% | 29.5% | 4.7% | **1.9%** | n/a |
| SBC ($M) | 55.5 | 70.2 | 53.0 | **115.6** | — |

**What's good:** Gross margin stability at ~68% through a brutal downturn; positive FCF every year; modest share count (136.7M basic in FY2025 vs 135.2M in FY2020 — dilution genuinely contained); net cash position pre-deal.

**What's disqualifying at a high multiple:**
1. **Stock-based comp is 22% of FY2025 revenue ($115.6M) versus $3.1M of net income.** The entire GAAP profit is an artifact of what you choose to exclude. FY2025 FCF of $132.6M was only achieved because $115.6M of compensation was paid in paper.
2. **True owner earnings are far below reported FCF.** Lattice spent **$100M on buybacks in FY2025** — roughly the size of its SBC — merely to hold the share count flat. Owner earnings after neutralizing dilution ≈ **$30–35M**, not $132.6M. That is a ~440x multiple on genuine owner earnings.
3. **Growth is not converting to GAAP profit.** Despite +91% revenue in 9M FY2026, GAAP operating margin is 11.0% — and *declining* through the year (15.6% → 11.1% → 8.2%). Q3 FY2026 net income of $11.2M on $291M revenue is a 3.8% net margin. The incremental revenue is low-quality at the statutory line.
4. **ROIC through-cycle is unstable, not high.** 29.5% → 4.7% → 1.9%. Buffett's hallmark test (ROIC > WACC *consistently*) fails. Post-AMI, invested capital roughly triples while returns lag — ROIC will sit in single digits for years.
5. **The balance sheet has been downgraded from fortress to levered.** $925M–$950M of new debt funds AMI; S&P rates the company **BB (sub-investment grade)**. Post-deal net debt ~$800–850M and ~$38M/yr of interest (Q3 FY2026 interest expense: $9.64M in one quarter) against ~$66M of GAAP net income. Also, $650M of the consideration is stock — 8–12% dilution.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

At ~$100/share, $14.4B cap / $15.2B EV:

| Multiple | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| P/S (FY2026E ~$1.0B) | **14.4x** | Rich for a cyclical semi with 20% SBC |
| P/E GAAP (FY2026E ~$66M) | **~218x** | Meaningless but telling |
| P/E non-GAAP (FY2026E ~$2.49) | **~40x** | vs. mega-cap peer median 38.9x |
| EV/EBITDA (FY2026E ~$175M) | **~87x** | |
| P/FCF (FY2026E ~$180M) | **~80x** | |
| FCF yield | **~1.2%** | vs. 10-yr Treasury |

**Intrinsic value — owner-earnings DCF (10% WACC, 4% terminal):**

Starting owner earnings are the crux. Post-SBC, post-interest, and treating acquired-intangible amortization as a real cost of maintaining AMI's IP, I estimate **$150M (bear) to $300M (bull)** of sustainable FY2027 owner earnings.

| Scenario | OE base | Growth yrs 1–5 / 6–10 | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | $150M | 10% / 5% | **~$28** |
| Base | $250M | 15% / 8% | **~$42** |
| Bull | $300M | 22% / 10% | **~$71** |
| Super-bull (near-flawless) | $320M | 30% / 12% | **~$110** |

**Intrinsic value: ~$45–$70, midpoint ~$58.**

**What the current price implies:** You must underwrite **~30% owner-earnings growth for five years, then 12% for another five**, plus a 4% terminal — i.e., the super-bull case — just to justify $100. The market is not paying for the base case plus a margin of safety; it is paying for the best case with no discount. Note the stock traded at $37.84 in April 2025 — the same business, a year earlier, at *one-third* the price. That is the definition of a price that is a function of narrative, not value.

Analyst targets ($160–200, Rosenblatt/Baird/Stifel, 11/3/26) are multiple-driven, applying ~35–40x to non-GAAP EPS that excludes the 20%-of-revenue compensation expense. I do not underwrite those multiples.

**Requirement: a 25% discount to my $58 midpoint = buy only below ~$43.** We are 2.3x above that.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Mixed to cautious.**

- **The Anderson era (2018–2024) was genuinely excellent**: gross margin +17pts, ROIC to ~30%, disciplined, net-cash balance sheet. That earned real credit.
- **The Tamer era is unproven and the early marks are poor.** FY2025 GAAP operating margin of 2.9%, SBC doubling to $115.6M, and a 2024 collapse that management did not appear to anticipate.
- **The AMI deal is the decisive capital-allocation test, and it reads poorly on Buffett criteria:** $1.65B (~33x AMI's estimated ~$50M EBITDA; ~8x its $200M 2026 revenue) for a BIOS/firmware business *outside* the FPGA core competence; funded with ~$950M of debt that took the credit to BB; ~$650M of stock issued; announced as a pivot to a "$1 billion or greater" revenue trajectory. That is empire-building language. The strategic logic (control-plane adjacency in servers) may even be right — but price paid plus leverage plus dilution is precisely how shareholder value gets transferred away in acquisitive semis.
- **Shareholder returns:** no dividend, ever. Buybacks ($67M–$110M/yr) have served mainly to offset SBC rather than shrink the count. Insider ownership is low; this is a professional-manager company, not an owner-operator one.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

*(For a non-holder: do not initiate. For a holder: the thesis is now a momentum thesis, not a value thesis.)*

The business is better than average — a narrow-moat, 68%-gross-margin franchise with genuine design-win stickiness and a real AI/data-center tailwind (+118% Q3 FY2026 revenue growth is not nothing). But three things are disqualifying at this price:

1. **No margin of safety — the opposite of one.** Intrinsic value ~$58 (range $45–70) versus ~$100. The stock discounts a super-bull scenario. Buffett: *"A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy."* This is not even fair.
2. **Earnings quality is poor.** 20%+ of revenue paid in stock, GAAP operating margin *falling* while revenue doubles, owner earnings of ~$30M in FY2025, ROIC of 1.9%. The reported numbers you'd need to believe to justify 40x non-GAAP are not the economics the company actually generates.
3. **The capital structure changed for the worse.** BB-rated, ~$850M net debt, $38M/yr interest, 8–12% dilution, and a large integrating acquisition in an adjacent industry — all while the core FPGA business is two years off a -31% air pocket.

**Where I would get interested:** high-$30s to low-$40s (a 25–30% discount to the $58 base-case intrinsic value), *and* only after (a) two or three quarters showing AMI is organic-accretive with stable GAAP gross margin (watch Q3 FY2026's 67.4% vs Q2's 70.3% — a 290bp sequential decline that needs explaining), (b) SBC trending below 15% of revenue, and (c) debt reduced toward 1.5x EBITDA.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks that I am too bearish (thesis-invalidating):**
- **AI data-center ramp is larger and longer than my model.** Lattice's power-sequencing/control content per AI server rack could scale faster than 30%; if FY2028 revenue reaches $2.5B at 30% non-GAAP margins, my bull case is too low.
- **AMI is genuinely synergistic** — firmware + FPGA becomes a defensible secure-management platform with cross-sell, and the 33x EBITDA looks cheap in hindsight.
- **Takeout optionality.** A unique low-power programmable asset at $15B is affordable for a large strategic; a bid at a premium is a real possibility.
- Gross margin holding ~68% through the cycle is a positive I may be underweighting.

**Risks to owning it here (asymmetry is unfavorable):**
- **Multiple compression.** At 40x non-GAAP and ~14x forward sales, a de-rating to peer-median multiples costs 40–60% of the price with no change in the business.
- **Cyclicality repeats.** FY2024's -31% revenue decline shows what happens when inventory corrects; with BB leverage and $38M interest, the next downturn is materially worse than the last.
- **Customer/channel concentration and Asia exposure** — a large share of revenue ships into Asia; tariffs, export controls, or China competition (Gowin) hit directly.
- **SBC and dilution recurrence** — $650M of stock issued for AMI, plus ongoing 20%-of-revenue comp, means per-share value creation lags enterprise value creation.
- **Integration risk** — a 50%-of-market-cap acquisition, debt-funded, in an adjacent business, immediately after a cyclical trough.

**Bottom line:** A good niche franchise, temporarily supercharged by an AI narrative and transformed (for better or worse) by a leveraging, dilutive acquisition — priced at roughly 1.7x my estimate of intrinsic value. **Circle of competence: yes, I understand it. Margin of safety: none. Verdict: Sell.**
