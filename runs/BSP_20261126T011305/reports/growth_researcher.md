# Bending Spoons S.p.A. (NASDAQ: BSP) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $46.29 | Mkt cap ~$29.4B | Net debt ~$4.0B | Nov 2026*

## 1. Growth Story

Bending Spoons is not a software company that grows — it is a **machine that buys stalled internet franchises and re-rates them through operational surgery**. It has deployed ~$9.1B of acquisitions in 2026 alone (Airtable $1.29B, Miro $1.36B, Tractive $759M, Mailchimp $3.68B), rewriting acquired codebases with AI-assisted full-stack teams and cutting cost structures, which lifted revenue from $387M (2023) to $1.31B (2025) and is guiding to $3.07–3.09B for 2026. The disruptive asset is the *operator playbook*, not any single product: 2,187 employees generating ~$1.4M of revenue per head is roughly 5–8x a typical SaaS peer. But strip out the acquisitions and the underlying franchises grow **3%** — and decelerating from 6%.

**Two financial-quality flags:**

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

The correct TAM framing is not "SaaS" — it is the **universe of mature, cash-generative internet properties available at a discount**. Bending Spoons buys at 2.3–2.7x ARR and ~6.1x Mailchimp's segment operating income. The opportunity is enormous and structurally *expanding*: AI is compressing valuations of legacy SaaS and consumer software, creating a buyer's market. Mailchimp at $3.68B vs. Intuit's $12B (2021) is a 69% discount. 500M MAU across the portfolio and ~1M Mailchimp SMB customers are the raw material.

But penetration cuts both ways. This TAM has **no organic growth engine** — it is a flow of distressed assets, not a market being newly created. The constraint on growth is *deal supply and cost of capital*, not customer adoption. That is a fundamentally different (and less durable) growth curve than a platform compounding on network effects.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4'26 results — ~Feb 11, 2027.** Guidance already set at $984M–$1.0B revenue. Highest-probability beat: management has beaten two-for-two by wide margins.
- **2027 guidance (same call).** With Mailchimp (~$1.25B annualized) plus a ~$3.9B exit run-rate, pro forma 2027 revenue could reach **$5.0–5.2B** vs. $4.35B consensus — a potential 15–20% consensus reset.
- **Mailchimp close, early 2027.** First large deal where the seller already cut costs (Intuit lifted segment margin 44% → 65%). CEO Ferrari has pre-flagged *lower* returns than prior deals — this is the key execution test.
- **Airtable / Miro integration.** Closed Sep 4 and Oct 26, 2026; margin realization should show in H1'27.
- **Credit ratings from Moody's / S&P** on the first-ever bond issuance, and the **~180-day IPO lock-up expiry (~late Dec 2026/Jan 2027)** — a supply overhang.

## 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Google Trends (US, 5-yr):** Interest peaked at 100 during IPO week (late June 2026), collapsed to a base of 6–8 by late August, and now only spikes on news events (Oct 11 = 86; Nov 15 = 48 on Mailchimp). The pattern is **declining retail attention with event-driven bursts** — consistent with post-IPO hype decay rather than a building demand flywheel. Note this measures the parent brand, not product demand (Evernote, WeTransfer, AOL are the consumer-facing marks).
- **Hiring:** ~6 active postings on Indeed. Deliberate — the model is headcount *reduction*, not expansion. Efficient, but it caps organic growth by construction and supports the "3% organic" figure as structural rather than cyclical.
- **Revenue per employee:** $597K (2025) → ~$1.41M (2026E guidance). The strongest quantitative confirmation that the transformation playbook genuinely works.
- **Price action:** IPO at $40.50, range $31.78–$55.77 since. Stock *fell 1.6%* on a large Q3 beat — the market is already discounting the quality of M&A-driven growth.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

Valuation is not the problem. At **$46.29** the stock trades at **20x 2027E EPS ($2.30)**, ~7.6x 2027E EV/revenue (~6.6x pro forma), and ~22x 2027E FCF — genuinely undemanding for a company compounding revenue >50%. Analyst targets ($38–$72, consensus ~$50) show limited near-term upside, but the Mizuho $72 case maps to a legitimate pro-forma bull scenario.

The problem is **the durability of the growth**, which is exactly where a growth framework should be strictest. A 3%-organic, decelerating, debt-funded roll-up does not deserve a premium innovation multiple, and the Rule of 40 of 117 is an M&A accounting artifact. Meanwhile interest expense has grown to 62% of operating income and pre-tax profit is *falling* while revenue doubles — the earnings quality is deteriorating even as the top line surges.

**Upgrade to Buy if:** organic growth re-accelerates above 5%, leverage post-Mailchimp holds under ~3x, and 2027 guidance clears $5B with FCF >$1.3B.
**Downgrade to Sell if:** organic growth turns negative, leverage exceeds ~4x, or a deal is repriced/fails.

**Price framework:** Bear $30 (organic growth negative, leverage stress, multiple de-rates) · Base $55–60 (2027 revenue ~$5B, FCF $1.3B, 22–25x) · Bull $75–80 (Mailchimp margin upside + continued 2.5x ARR deal flow).

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Organic decay is structural, not cyclical.** 6% → 3% in one quarter. The portfolio is composed of *declining* franchises (Evernote, WeTransfer, AOL) being monetized via price increases — a finite runway with documented brand/consumer backlash ("the company ruining software on the internet"). Melting-ice-cube risk is real and compounding.
2. **Leverage spiral.** Net debt $4.0B and 2.1x at Q3; Mailchimp adds ~$3.3B cash funded with ~60% debt, pushing leverage toward ~3–3.5x. Every incremental deal must earn more post-restructuring than its debt cost — a threshold rising as rates stay elevated.
3. **Mailchimp is the hardest deal yet.** Intuit already extracted 65% segment margins; the CEO has pre-announced lower expected returns. The "cut 75% of staff" lever has been partly pulled by the seller.
4. **Shareholder dilution.** Shares went 502M (2023) → 599M (2025), plus a $1.3B private placement and $368M in stock to Intuit — roughly 5–7% annual dilution.
5. **AI disruption is double-edged.** AI enables the code-rewrite playbook, but it is also deflating the value of the legacy SaaS assets BSP is buying (the "Death Star to SaaS trade"). Bought-cheap can become cheaper.
6. **Execution bandwidth.** $9.1B of deals in twelve months across five major integrations (Airtable, Miro, Tractive, Mailchimp, AOL) strains a 2,187-person team with no slack by design.
