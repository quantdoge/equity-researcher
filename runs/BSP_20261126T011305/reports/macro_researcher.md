# MACROECONOMIC ASSESSMENT — BENDING SPOONS S.p.A. (NASDAQ: BSP)

**Prepared:** 26 November 2026 | **Reference price:** $46.29 (25 Nov close) | **Market cap:** ~$29.4B

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: RISK-OFF / LATE-CYCLE REFLATION — a hawkish regime flip.**

The defining macro feature is that the 2025 easing cycle has fully reversed. The Fed cut to 3.63% by mid-2026, then stopped; the effective funds rate has since risen to 3.88% (Oct 2026), and markets price a **95–97% probability of a 25bp hike at the 8–9 December FOMC**, to 4.00–4.25%. Governor Waller has publicly floated 50bp. The ECB held on 29 October but faces **euro-area HICP at 4.0% in October** (roughly double a year ago) and markets price >90% odds of a December hike — a synchronised G3 tightening impulse.

The trigger is an **energy shock**: Brent spiked to $135.51/bbl on 2 October before retreating to $91.80 (24 Nov). That pass-through has pushed US CPI to **3.82% YoY** (Oct), up from 2.70% in Nov 2025, with the last three months running at a **5.7% annualised** pace. Critically, 10-year breakevens are only **2.12%** — so the rise in the 10-year to ~5.1% is almost entirely **real yields (~2.8–3.0%)**, the highest in decades. The curve is **bear-steepening** (2s10s +39bp, 30Y 5.47%), not inverting — a term-premium/inflation-risk signal rather than a growth-scare signal.

Growth is *not* the problem: Q3 2026 real GDP grew **4.3% annualised** (+2.3% YoY), reaccelerating from a near-stall in Q4 2025 (+0.2%). Unemployment is a stable **4.1%**. This is the worst possible mix for long-duration equities: **firm growth removes the Fed put, while inflation keeps the discount rate rising.** The dollar is the release valve — the broad index has firmed **4.7% in ten weeks** (118.08 on 10 Sep → 123.60 on 20 Nov), and EUR/USD has fallen **5.6%** to 1.0981.

BSP is the archetype of what this regime punishes: a **beta 1.48, ~9.5x revenue, newly-IPO'd, acquisition-funded** growth asset with **$4.9B of debt** and a **$3.68B acquisition** to finance.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

BSP is an Italian-headquartered, USD-listed roll-up of proven consumer/SMB software (Evernote, WeTransfer, Meetup, StreamYard, Issuu, Eventbrite; Mailchimp pending). Q3 2026: revenue **$780M, +125% YoY**; adjusted operating income **$424M (54% margin)**; FY26 revenue guidance raised to **$3.07–3.09B**. Organic growth is only **~3%** — the model is M&A- and financing-dependent. That is the crux of its macro sensitivity.

### ⛔ Interest rates / discount rate — **STRONGLY NEGATIVE** (dominant factor)
The market has already run this experiment. Between 13 August and 2 October, the 10Y rose **68bp** (4.63% → 5.31%); BSP fell **34.6%** ($48.98 → $32.03). That is a **~0.5% de-rating per basis point** of 10-year yield — an extreme duration coefficient, consistent with a 9.5x-revenue, beta 1.48, no-dividend, post-IPO growth asset. The 10Y is now 4.91–5.13% with a December hike incoming. **Every 25bp of further tightening is worth roughly 12% of equity value** on this historical coefficient. This single variable dominates the entire macro assessment.

### ⛔ Debt cost & refinancing — **NEGATIVE**
Long-term debt **$4.91B**, net debt **$4.00B**, leverage **2.1x** (target band 2–3x). Q3 interest expense **$107M, +163% YoY** — implying an all-in cost near **8.5–9%**, already a high-yield-style coupon. Refinancing into a rising-rate market is a direct EPS headwind: each +100bp on ~$5–7B pro forma debt is ~$50–70M pre-tax annually. The **$3.68B Mailchimp deal** (announced 19 Nov, closing early 2027) is to be funded alongside a **$1.3B equity private placement** (32.5M shares at ~$40, ~5% dilution) — implying roughly **$2.4B of incremental debt**, taking pro forma leverage toward **~2.8–2.9x, the top of the stated band**. Financing an acquisition at the peak of a hiking cycle is the single worst-timed macro exposure in the capital structure. Rising rates also raise the hurdle rate for the *next* deal — and deals are 97% of growth.

### ⚖️ Inflation & pricing power — **MIXED, modestly POSITIVE**
Software has no commodity input; COGS are hosting and engineering labour (2,187 employees, largely Italian/European). The Bending Spoons playbook is explicitly *raise prices and cut costs* on acquired apps — 3.8% CPI provides air cover for subscription price increases with near-zero marginal cost. Nominal consumer spending is up **6.1% YoY** (PCE $22.56T vs $21.27T), supporting ARPU. **But** the offsetting risk is real: record $1.29T credit card balances, 5.0% delinquency, and a consumer "against the ropes" going into the holidays. Price hikes into a squeezed consumer raise churn on the consumer-facing apps (Evernote, WeTransfer, Meetup) — and churn is the one thing that breaks the roll-up math.

### ⚖️ Growth — **NEUTRAL / mildly positive**
US GDP +2.3% YoY and reaccelerating is a supportive backdrop for SMB and consumer software demand. But with only **3% organic growth**, BSP's revenue is decoupled from GDP — it is a function of capital markets and deal flow, not the business cycle. Growth is a *necessary but not sufficient* condition here.

### ⛔ Currency — **NEGATIVE**
EUR/USD at **1.0981**, down 5.6% in ten weeks and 3.5% since the July IPO. Two competing effects: (a) **translation** — a weaker euro reduces the USD value of EUR-denominated earnings for the USD-listed shares, a direct headwind to the quoted price; (b) **transaction** — if revenue is USD-heavy (US app-store and subscription billings) while opex is EUR-heavy, a stronger dollar is a *margin* tailwind. Net: negative for the share price, partly cushioned at the operating line. The broader **+4.7% dollar surge** also compresses USD-equivalent ARPU across BSP's global/EM user base (WeTransfer, Evernote). At beta 1.48, BSP also carries full market-FX risk into a USD-strong tape.

### ⚖️ Tariffs / trade — **NEUTRAL (relatively advantaged)**
The administration's proposed **15% levy on Europe**, China and 14 others targets industrial overcapacity in *goods*. Software is a service with no imported physical inputs — BSP is structurally insulated from the direct tariff line. The indirect costs are a European growth drag and a rising risk premium on a European-domiciled, US-listed issuer (plus EU retaliation/digital-services-tax risk). Relative to industrials, this is a **safe harbour**; in absolute terms it is background noise against the rates channel.

### ⛔ Credit conditions — **NEGATIVE**
Aggregate delinquency at 5.0% of household debt, record card balances, Fed tightening — credit is tightening at both the consumer and corporate level. For a serial acquirer, credit availability is oxygen. Wider spreads hit both the Mailchimp financing and the pipeline that supplies 97% of growth. A frozen high-yield market would strand the model.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Four of the seven channels — rates/duration, debt cost, FX, and credit — are negative, and the dominant one by an order of magnitude is the discount rate. The company's momentum is genuinely strong (Q3 beat, guidance raised ~10%, Berkshire's ~$330M/4% stake, Evercore's $48 target), but that is **idiosyncratic, not macro**. The macro tape is fighting it: BSP trades 22% below its $59.50 October high despite raising guidance, purely because the 10-year backed up. A favourable macro turn is not required for the thesis to work — but an unfavourable one is sufficient to break it.

---

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**Bear case — the December hike is not the last one.** 95–97% odds price the *December* move; they do not price the *path*. Waller has floated 50bp. If the Fed delivers 50bp on 9 December, or 25bp with guidance toward 4.75%+, and the 10-year breaks 5.50% toward 5.75%, BSP's demonstrated **~0.5%/bp** coefficient implies a **25–35% de-rating from $46.29 toward the low-$30s** — retesting the 2 October low of $32.03. The compounding damage is structural: the **$1.3B placement is struck at ~$40**, so a sustained price below that breaks the equity funding economics on the Mailchimp deal precisely as ~$2.4B of debt must be raised, pushing leverage to the top of the 2–3x band. That is a financing accident, not a sentiment wobble — and it is the more probable surprise given how fully the December move is already discounted.

**The offsetting bull trigger is already forming in the tape.** Brent has fallen **32% from its 2 October peak** ($135.51 → $91.80). If that flows through, headline CPI rolls from 3.82% toward 2.5% by Q2 2027, December proves to be the terminal hike, and the 10-year retreats to 4.25% — an 85bp decline that, on the same coefficient, is worth **+40–50% upside**. BSP is, functionally, a levered bet on the long end of the Treasury curve. Position sizing should reflect that, not the Q3 revenue print.
