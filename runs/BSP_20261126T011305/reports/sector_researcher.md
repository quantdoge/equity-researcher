## SECTOR BRIEF — BSP (Bending Spoons S.p.A.)

**Classification note:** FMP tags BSP as Technology / Software–Application, but the generic peer list (AAPL, NVDA, MSFT) is not a valid comp set — those are hardware/semis. BSP's true category is **permanent-capital software/IP acquirers** (Constellation-style) with a **consumer-subscription app portfolio**. I built a bespoke comp set accordingly. Data gaps flagged at the end.

---

### 1. Sector Overview

BSP operates at the intersection of two markets: the **$1.5T+ global software market** (structurally low concentration, tens of thousands of vendors, near-zero HHI) and, more precisely, the **market for orphaned software assets** — products with proven product-market fit but stalled growth, owned by distracted or over-levered strategic parents. That second market is in absolute oversupply in 2026: horizontal software multiples have compressed ~36% in six months and vertical software ~39%, driven by the "SaaSpocalypse" narrative that AI agents compress seat-based revenue (Bain estimates AI could automate 30–50% of activity across key enterprise functions; ~73% of SaaS vendors rebuilt pricing in 2026). For BSP — a *buyer* of those assets, backed by Berkshire's liquidity and public currency — multiple compression is a **procurement tailwind, not a threat**. The demand side of BSP's own end-markets (consumer subscriptions, productivity, marketing software) is growing low-single-digit, while AI-native competitors are growing massively faster from a small base.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Buy-side price dislocation is severe and exploitable.** Mailchimp: $3.68B from Intuit vs $12B paid in 2021 (**−69%**, ~3x ARR). Airtable: $1.29B EV vs ~$11.7B private mark (**−89%**), ~2.6x ~$500M ARR. Mailchimp/Airtable are its two largest-ever deals, both bought at 3x or lower — vs the 4.6x median EV/Revenue multiple that mature software peers trade at *in public markets*. BSP is buying wholesale and marking retail.
- **AI-driven engineering is already visible in gross margin.** Gross margin rose from 61.1% (FY23) → 63.9% (FY24) → **65.6% (FY25)**, +4.5pp in two years, on the "rewrite the codebase with AI tooling, standardise on Python/FastAPI" playbook. Management explicitly ties AI-driven engineering gains to *deal volume*, not just cost — i.e., the same 2,187 FTEs can absorb more acquisitions. Revenue per employee: ~$597K FY25A, ~$1.4M on the Q3'26 annualised run-rate.
- **Platform-tax relief and pricing power on owned apps.** Apple's external-payment-link regime (27% down from 30%, structurally lower in the EU) offers a gross-margin tail on BSP's mobile-heavy assets (Remini, Evernote, StreamYard), while its willingness to re-price long-neglected consumer subscriptions is a gross-margin lever peers are too reputation-conscious to pull.

---

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Organic growth is effectively zero — the model is bought growth.** Q3'26: revenue **+125% YoY to $780M but organic growth only +3%**, held back by Remini declines and disruption from AOL's adtech transition. The market's core question — "what does this compounder do without deals?" — is unanswered, and mail-delivery marketing software (Mailchimp) is arguably the *most* AI-exposed large asset in the portfolio.
- **Leverage plus dilution plus non-existent GAAP earnings.** Debt of ~$4.36B (Q1'26); interest expense $142.6M in FY25, **+337% YoY**; net income **−$0.1M on $411M of EBITDA** and $278M of operating income. The $1.3B private placement (32.5M shares, ~5.5% of shares out) to part-fund Mailchimp confirms the growth must be equity- and debt-financed. GAAP-to-adjusted bridge is enormous: Q3'26 operating income $172M vs adjusted $424M — a **$252M quarterly gap** of SBC and acquired-intangible amortisation. Beta 1.48.
- **Multiple compression risk on a premium equity.** BSP trades at **~25x FY25A revenue / ~80x FY25A EBITDA** — versus mature software comps at 2.7–6.0x revenue. Even on my FY26E revenue of ~$2.8B (annualising the $3.1B Q3 run-rate), that is ~11–12x. The stock is −22% from its 15-Oct high of $59.50 and has already round-tripped twice.

**Read-through:** BSP is the **fastest grower** (+94.7% vs +19.5% median) but the **lowest-quality grower** — the median peer's growth is largely organic, BSP's is ~97% inorganic (3% organic). Gross margin (65.6%) and GAAP operating margin (21.3%) both sit **below** peer medians (76.5% / 26.2%), the GM gap explained by app-store take rates and hosting inside cost of revenue; the op-margin gap is the amortisation/SBC burden of buying rather than building. On adjusted economics it is far cheaper: Q3'26 adjusted operating income annualises to ~$1.7B, implying ~19x EV — roughly in line with AppLovin's 26x EBITDA. **Share-gainers:** APP (organic, margin-expanding), then BSP (via M&A). **Share-losers:** MTCH, DBX, and by extension the asset class BSP buys.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Strong (in its niche) / Average (in the sector)**

- **Strong:** demonstrated ability to buy at 3x revenue or less, a proprietary operating playbook (full-stack rewrite + disciplined G&A) validated by 4.5pp of gross-margin expansion, a $3.68B marquee acquisition funded by an oversubscribed placement (new *and* existing holders), and Berkshire backing — a genuine structural advantage in deal access and cost of capital.
- **Average:** only 3% organic growth, a portfolio concentrated in assets the market believes AI will erode (Mailchimp, Airtable, Evernote, Miro, AOL), near-zero GAAP net income, and a rising leverage/dilution trade-off. On Porter's, supplier power (Apple/Google/cloud) and threat of substitutes (AI-native tools) are both **high** — BSP controls neither.
- Net: it is a **better allocator than operator-of-growth**, and should be underwritten as such.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **Neutral**

Aggregate software-application sector: **Neutral, tilting unfavourable** — trailing demand intact but multiple compression and seat-based disintermediation are structural, not seasonal. BSP's specific sub-sector (acquisition of distressed software assets at 2–3x revenue) is **Attractive**, because the same AI narrative destroying incumbent multiples is manufacturing his deal pipeline at generational discounts. Recommendation to the analyst: the sector beta is a headwind; the stock's return will be driven almost entirely by deal pricing, organic growth stabilisation, and the pace of share issuance.
