# BSP — Bending Spoons S.p.A. | Forensic Short-Seller Analysis

**Company:** Italian technology roll-up (AOL, Evernote, WeTransfer, Brightcove, Vimeo, Eventbrite, Remini, komoot, Tractive, Airtable pending). IPO'd Nasdaq 1 Jul 2026 at $29.00. Market cap ~$29.4B; net debt ~$3.98B → EV ~$33.4B. Price ~$46.29.

---

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment

**Rating: LOW — but with an important caveat.**

The composite earnings-quality score is **0/100** and the cash-conversion ratio prints at **−2,136** — but those readings are largely a *denominator artifact*: FY2025 GAAP net income was **−$136k** on $1.31B of revenue. Dividing cash flow by approximately zero produces a meaningless number. I flag this so the score isn't over-read. The real quality problem is not that cash is absent — it's that **reported profit is structurally unrepresentative** and the disclosure is unusually weak for a $29B company.

**I am explicitly NOT calling this "Fraudulent Risk."** The evidence does not support that. Capex is essentially nil ($5.2M in Q3 2026) — development spend is expensed, not capitalised. That is *conservative* accounting, and it means GAAP earnings are not flattered. The accruals ratio is **negative (−0.061)**, i.e. cash earnings exceed accounting earnings. Credit where due: this is a genuinely cash-generative business.

The "Low" rating reflects **opacity and unreliability**, not theft.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**a) Organic growth is 3%. Reported growth is 125%.**
Q3 2026 revenue $780.2M, +125% YoY — but company-disclosed **organic growth was 3%**. Trajectory: 7% (FY24) → 13% (FY25) → 6% (Q1 26) → 3% (Q2 26) → 3% (Q3 26). Roughly **97% of headline growth is purchased**. The 84% three-year revenue CAGR is a capital-deployment statistic, not an operating one.

**b) Net revenue retention is below 100% in every year disclosed: 93% / 91% / 95% / 94%.**
The installed base *shrinks* even as they raise prices. This is the most damaging single metric in the file. A roll-up whose existing assets erode revenue must keep buying growth — the model is a treadmill, not a compounding machine.

**c) Gross margin collapsed 1,400bp in one year.**
FY23 61.1% → FY24 63.9% → FY25 65.6% → Q1 26 67.9% → **Q3 26 52.9%**. Q3 gross profit grew 78% while revenue grew 125%. Operating margin fell from **37% to 22%** — operating income +34% on revenue +125%. Negative operating leverage, despite "AI-driven cost cuts" being the core pitch.

**d) Adjusted-vs-GAAP gap is 2,600bp.**
Management guides to an **adjusted operating margin of 48%** while GAAP operating margin is **22%**. Q3 2026 adjusted EPS **$0.42** vs GAAP **$0.13** — a 3.2x gap. The bridge is amortisation of acquired intangibles (~$600M annualised run-rate), which is ~37% of adjusted EBITDA. Adjusted EBITDA measures cash generated *before consuming the assets that were bought*. The FT called this "an odd kind of financial magic trick."

**e) Goodwill and intangibles are 77.8% of assets; tangible book value is −$4.86B.**
Assets $9.60B, goodwill+intangibles $7.47B, equity $2.61B. Tangible equity = **−$4.86B** (from −$2.51B at FY25). Assets grew $4.8B in nine months — nearly all acquisition. A goodwill impairment is a live risk if any acquired asset underperforms; NRR <100% says several are.

**f) Interest expense is eating the operating profit.**
Interest as % of operating income: 20.4% (FY23) → 25.6% (FY24) → 51.3% (FY25) → **77.5% (Q1 26)**. LTM interest ~$433M against LTM EBIT ~$888M = **~2.1x coverage**. Total debt $5.0B, up from ~$400M in nine quarters.

**g) Q1 2026 operating cash flow was restated down 63% — plugged through FX.**
$123.7M restated to $75.7M, with ~$48M of imbalance absorbed by the foreign-exchange translation line. A plug to FX is a textbook tell. Combined with **material weaknesses disclosed across all five COSO components**, no SOX 404 evaluation ever performed, and **no auditor review report on interim financials**, the control environment is not yet fit for a public company of this size.

**h) Receivables outgrew revenue in FY2024** — AR +127.9% vs revenue +73.4% (red flag triggered). Corrected in FY2025 (AR +68.4% vs revenue +94.7%), so this may be acquisition-mix rather than channel stuffing. Downgraded to a watch item.

**i) FY2023's 41.5% net margin was a tax artifact.** Net income $160.6M on operating income of only $84.3M — the delta was a $103.1M deferred-tax benefit. Anyone anchoring on "they used to earn 40% net margins" is reading a tax entry.

**j) R&D fell from 13.8% to 9.2% of revenue.** Harvest, don't reinvest — flatters near-term margins, erodes long-term product competitiveness. Inconsistent with the AI-platform narrative.

**k) Consensus implies Q4 revenue *declines* 7% sequentially** ($2.81B FY26E vs $2.09B actual 9M → ~$724M Q4 vs $780M Q3), despite a "raised outlook." And Remini — the AI app that drove the 2023–24 surge — is disclosed as *declining*.

## 3. Beneish M-Score & Accruals Analysis

**M-Score: −2.122 → "MODERATE RISK — possible manipulator"** (sits in the grey band between the −2.22 soft and −1.78 hard thresholds).

| **AQI** | **1.066** | Asset quality deteriorating — the $7.47B intangible build |
| **LVGI** | **1.194** | Leverage rising, as expected |
| **SGAI** | **1.128** | SG&A rising faster than sales — the opposite of the "cut costs" thesis |
| **TATA** | **−0.061** | Accruals *negative* — cash exceeds earnings. **A genuine quality positive** |

| GMI | 0.974 | Gross margin roughly flat on the annual measure |
| DEPI | 0.971 | — |

**Honest interpretation:** the M-Score is elevated principally by SGI and AQI — the two indices that a serial acquirer *cannot avoid* triggering. An acquirer growing revenue 95% through M&A will always score badly on SGI. The TATA reading is negative and the DSRI is clean, which is *not* the profile of a company stuffing channels or front-loading revenue. **The M-Score here is a structural artifact of the roll-up model, not evidence of manipulation.** I weight it accordingly.

**Accruals ratio by year:** +0.1085 (FY23, warning) → −0.0612 (FY24) → −0.0611 (FY25). Trajectory improving. Negative accruals mean reported earnings *understate* cash — the opposite of the classic red flag.

**Piotroski F-Score: 4/9 (Weak).** Fails positive ROA, ROA improvement, lower leverage, no dilution, and asset-turnover improvement. Passes positive operating cash flow, accruals quality, positive equity change, and gross-margin improvement. The "no dilution" fail is the notable one — see below.

**Altman Z-Score: 0.654 "Distress" — but this is misleading and I will not lean on it.** The tool's x4 uses *book* equity ($2.61B / $6.99B liabilities = 0.264). Substituting market cap ($29.4B / $6.99B = 4.21) recomputes Z to **~2.24 — the grey zone, not distress**. The company has $1.02B cash, $5.0B debt, and ~$460M of 9M-2026 free cash flow. **There is no near-term solvency event.** Net debt / EBITDA is roughly 2.5–3.5x depending on definition, against a 4.0x covenant; S&P upgraded to BB− with FOCF/debt comfortably above its 10% downgrade trigger. The balance sheet is levered, not broken.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

- **The moat is the M&A machine, not the products.** AOL, Evernote, WeTransfer, Brightcove and Vimeo are all declining consumer/SMB franchises. Bending Spoons' edge is operational: rewrite codebases, cut cost, raise price. The asset-level execution is genuinely impressive — Remini at 9x pre-acquisition revenue, Evernote ARPU at 2.5x 2022 levels, AOL bought at 4.4x operating income, Airtable at 2.7x ARR. These are real, cheap trades.
- **But price-raise-and-cut is a finite lever.** NRR of 91–95% says they have already run past the point where price increases offset churn. Each asset is being harvested, not renewed. R&D at 9.2% of revenue confirms it.
- **The AI claim is the weakest link.** "Full-stack developers rewriting codebases with AI" is a cost story, not a moat. It is replicable, and it is precisely what any PE-backed competitor can do. Meanwhile Q3's gross-margin collapse suggests AI inference costs or lower-margin acquisition mix — the AI thesis may be *costing* margin rather than creating it.
- **Commoditisation risk is high.** Every asset in the portfolio competes with free or near-free AI-native alternatives. Remini declining is the canary.

## 5. Management & Governance Red Flags

1. **Dual-class control: founders hold 82.71% of voting power** (Class A = 5 votes vs ordinary = 1). Public holders have effectively no governance recourse. This is an Italian *società per azioni* listed on Nasdaq — an unusual, shareholder-unfriendly hybrid.
2. **Foreign private issuer status** exempts them from SOX 404(b) auditor attestation, US proxy rules, and quarterly reporting obligations. Combined with material weaknesses across all five COSO components and no auditor review of interim statements, this is a company that has chosen the lightest possible disclosure regime.
3. **Three equity raises in five months.** IPO net proceeds ~$933M went to acquisitions, not deleveraging. Then $330M from Berkshire at $33 — a **13% discount to the $37.86 prior close**. Then a **$1.3B private placement** announced in November. A business that keeps selling stock is not self-funding; each raise dilutes and signals the acquisition treadmill needs external fuel. Share count already 713M in Q3 vs 587M in FY25.
4. **Insider Form 4s cluster in two windows:** five filings on 2026-09-25 and three on 2026-11-23. **I could not retrieve transaction direction or size** — the filing index returned metadata only. I will not assert net selling without the data. But the September cluster falls ~87 days post-IPO, consistent with an early lock-up release, and the November cluster follows the Q3 print. **Unverified — flag for follow-up.**
5. **No evidence of related-party self-dealing, auditor churn, or promotional language outrunning delivery.** Management's numbers have largely been delivered on. I found no fraud markers.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary

**There is a credible bear case, but it is a valuation-and-quality-of-growth case, not a fraud case.**

The thesis in one line: *a levered serial acquirer growing organically at 3%, with revenue retention below 100% and gross margin compressing 1,400bp, is being valued at 11.9x forward sales and ~22x adjusted EBITDA — multiples reserved for durable compounders.*

- **Valuation:** ~$33.4B EV / $2.81B FY26E revenue = **11.9x EV/Sales**. Comparables: Match 3.3x, SS&C 3.5x, Adobe 4.2x, Roper 6.1x. Even granting BSP a premium for acquisition skill, the gap is 2–3x. On adjusted EBITDA ($1.48B FY26E) EV/EBITDA is ~22.6x; on GAAP EBIT it is ~37x. ROIC has fallen from 24.9% to 0.5%.
- **Mechanical risks in the next 12 months:** lock-up release (~Nov 2026) frees ~318.5M shares, roughly **5.5x the true tradeable float of ~58M** — and screeners report float ~4x too high, so most holders don't know it. A $1.3B private placement is pending. Airtable must close and integrate. A goodwill impairment test on a $7.47B intangible base against sub-100% NRR is a live event.
- **The trigger:** one or two quarters where organic growth prints below 3%, or a covenant-relevant adjusted-EBITDA miss, and the "compounder" framing breaks. The stock already fell 16.5% on its first earnings print and is down 12.9% over 30 days.

**The other side — and it is strong:**
- **Berkshire Hathaway is a substantial, increasing shareholder** and added $330M in October at $33. The stock rose 41% on the news. Berkshire's diligence is the single most credible counter-signal in this file, and I do not dismiss it.
- **The asset-level economics are real.** These are genuinely cheap, well-executed trades. Danaher and TransDigm prove levered serial acquisition can compound — and Bending Spoons cites them as models.
- **Accounting is conservative on the dimension that matters most:** zero capitalisation of development, negative accruals, real free cash flow (~$460M in 9M 2026), capex of $5M. The earnings-quality models that score this 0/100 are penalising it for having a low GAAP net income, not for weak cash.
- **No solvency risk.** $1.02B cash, covenant headroom, S&P upgrade to BB−.
- **Forward P/E is not demanding:** 34.8x 2026E, **20.1x 2027E**. If AOL and Airtable integrate and adjusted EBITDA compounds to $2.4B in 2027, 20x is defensible.
- **The "cash conversion" and "distress" red flags are artifacts.** I've said so plainly rather than letting them inflate the case.

## 7. Contrarian Rating

### **SHORT** — moderate conviction. Not "Strong Short."

**Why not Strong Short:** I found no fraud. The restatement is a control deficiency, not proof of intent. Capex treatment is conservative, accruals are negative, free cash flow is real, the balance sheet is not impaired, and Berkshire is a genuinely informed anchor shareholder buying more. The Altman "distress" reading and the 0/100 quality score are artifacts I refuse to lean on. A Strong Short rating here would be reflexive bearishness, which is exactly what I'm supposed to avoid.

**Why not No Edge:** 11.9x forward sales for a 3% organic grower with sub-100% net revenue retention and 1,400bp of gross-margin compression is a genuine mispricing. The market is paying a durable-compounder multiple for a capital-deployment story whose fuel is increasingly external equity. The float structure (5.5x true float unlocking) and the pending $1.3B placement create dated, asymmetric downside.

**What would change my mind to No Edge:** organic growth re-accelerating above 6% with NRR crossing 100%; gross margin recovering to the high-60s; or evidence that Airtable/AOL close and integrate at the stated multiples without impairment. Two consecutive quarters of that and the "treadmill" framing fails.

**What would change my mind to Strong Short:** a goodwill impairment charge, a covenant waiver request, or audited FY2026 accounts revealing further cash-flow restatements.
