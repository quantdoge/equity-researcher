## BSP — Bending Spoons S.p.A. | Value Analysis
*Price $46.29 (25-Nov-2026) · Mkt cap ~$33.0B (713M diluted sh.) · Net debt $3.98B · EV ~$37.0B*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow (and I'm being generous)**

Bending Spoons is not a software company in the conventional sense. It is a **leveraged acquirer and operator of distressed consumer internet assets** — Evernote, WeTransfer, Meetup, StreamYard, AOL, Vimeo, Eventbrite, Harvest, and in 2026 Mailchimp ($3.7B, vs. Intuit's $12B purchase price), Miro and Airtable at reportedly 10–31¢ on the dollar. The playbook: buy proven-but-stagnant products cheaply, rewrite the codebase with a centralized engineering platform, cut headcount brutally (WeTransfer: −75% staff), reprice aggressively (Harvest: 1,000%+ list price increases), and harvest cash.

**What is genuinely good:**
- Capex is almost nil ($5.2M in Q3'26 on $780M revenue). Essentially every incremental dollar of gross profit falls to cash.
- Gross margin 65.6% (2025) and rising (61.1% → 63.9% → 65.6%), with operating margin ~21% and expanding as acquired cost bases are stripped.
- Demonstrated, repeatable ability to buy assets at fractions of prior valuations and re-rate their profitability.

**Why the moat is only Narrow:**
- **Product-level moats are weak.** These are assets that were already losing share to better-funded competitors before Bending Spoons bought them. AOL, WeTransfer and Evernote have no pricing power that survives a 10x price increase; revenue is being harvested, not compounded.
- **The edge is a management skill, not a structural asset.** Bargaining power over sellers, coding productivity and cost discipline are excellent but *non-transferable and non-defensible*. Anyone can bid for the next distressed asset.
- **The ROIC test fails.** ROIC: 11.6% (2023) → 8.5% (2024) → **7.6% (2025)**, against a WACC I'd put at 10–11% given beta 1.48 and ~10% coupon debt. By Buffett's hallmark — ROIC > WACC consistently — the moat verdict is negative today. Goodwill + intangibles of **$7.47B against $2.61B of equity** means reported capital is mostly the price paid for other people's franchises.

The honest description: this is a **publicly traded, founder-run PE vehicle with permanent capital**. Understandable — but the 10-year cash flows are not forecastable with confidence.

**Positives:** FCF conversion is outstanding on a cash basis — 2025 OCF $291M vs. net income of ~zero, because the P&L is crushed by $157M of D&A (of which the bulk is amortization of acquired intangibles) and $143M of interest. Growth in cash generation is real and steep: quarterly FCF went $72M → $130M → $256M during 2026. No inventory, negative working capital dynamics, zero dividends and zero buybacks — all cash is reinvested, which is correct.

**Negatives that keep this from being a high score:**
- **Growth is bought, not earned.** Organic growth is not disclosed in a way I can verify; the legacy cohort almost certainly shrinks on volume as prices are pushed. 2026 revenue of ~$3.1B vs. $1.31B in 2025 is deal-driven.
- **Earnings quality is poor.** Net income of −$136K on $1.31B of revenue, a ~50% effective tax/one-off drag, and interest expense now running ~$430M+/yr.
- **Balance sheet is the weak link.** Debt $5.0B at high-yield terms (reports of $1B + €500M notes at high-9%/low-10%, BB-), net debt $3.98B, tangible equity **−$4.9B**. This company is one credit-market closure away from being forced to stop buying — the engine of its growth.
- **Dilution.** Diluted shares: 502M (2023) → 599M (2025) → 620M (Q2'26) → **713M (Q3'26)**. ~+42% in under three years. Per-share value creation is materially below enterprise value creation.

### 3. Valuation — Intrinsic value **~$30–37/sh.; price $46.29 = 25–50% above IV**

Current multiples are not meaningful on trailing GAAP earnings (P/E n/m) or trailing FCF (1.9% FCF yield, 1.05% on stale FY25 data). The only sane anchors are forward cash flow and EV/EBITDA:

- Pricing: **P/B 12.7x** (on Q3'26 book of $2.61B), **EV/2026E EBITDA ~26x** ($1.42B), **EV/2027E EBITDA ~18x**, **EV/2026E FCF ~52x**.
- Peer median P/E of 27x is a meaningless comparison — no true comparable set exists for a distressed-asset roll-up.

**DCF (base case):** 2026E FCF ~$710M (9M actual $458M + Q4E ~$250M); 2027E ~$1.25B as Mailchimp/Miro/Airtable annualize and margins expand. Growth 25% → 20% → 15% → 10% → 5% (2028–2032), WACC 11%, terminal 3%:
→ **EV ~$25.1B, equity ~$21.1B, ≈ $29.6/share.**

**Sensitivity:** WACC 10% / terminal 3.5% → **$37.3/share**. Conservative case (20% growth fading to 3%, WACC 11%) → **$18–24/share**. Everything above $45 requires assuming the roll-up compounds FCF at 30%+ for five years with no deal-flow interruption and no equity issuance — i.e., paying for perfection in a business funded by 10% debt.

**What the price implies:** ~$46 requires ~30% FCF CAGR through 2031 plus terminal value at a premium multiple. That is a growth bet on Luca Ferrari's deal machine continuing to find 70%-off assets, not a value bet on a durable franchise.

### 4. Management Quality — **Excellent operator, unproven as a public-company steward**

- **Founder-led and owner-operator** (CEO Luca Ferrari; founders retained large stakes post-IPO), ~2,187 employees doing the work of far larger firms. SBC is modest ($55M in 2025, ~4% of revenue) — a genuine positive versus typical tech.
- **Capital allocation record is exceptional.** Buying Airtable/Miro at 10–31¢ on the dollar and Mailchimp at a ~70% discount to Intuit's price is the opposite of empire-building — it is disciplined, opportunistic, price-sensitive buying. Zero dividends, zero buybacks, no acquisitions at silly prices.
- **But:** the *opposite* side of that ledger is aggressiveness with stakeholders — 75% staff cuts, 1,000x price increases at Harvest, and pro-forma profit of only ~$22M on the $3.3B AOL/Vimeo/Eventbrite outlay (Forbes). Value creation is being pulled forward through price and cost, which raises churn, regulatory and reputational risk, and caps the durability of acquired cash flows. Leverage at ~2.5x and high-yield refinancing risk amplify any stumble. I'd rate honesty/discipline high, predictability moderate, and shareholder-alignment good but subject to continued dilution.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID at $46.29**

This is a high-quality *machine* run by exceptional capital allocators — and a stock with **no margin of safety**. My intrinsic value estimate of $30–37 (midpoint ~$34) sits 25–35% below the current price; even my aggressive 10%-WACC case ($37) fails to reach it. A Buffett-style buyer requires a 25% discount to IV — that is roughly **$25–28/share**, a level last seen at the October dip and barely above the $31 IPO price.

I am not short this business: cash generation is real, accelerating, and capital-light, and the deal pipeline could keep the compounding going for years. But "a great business at a fair price" is not "a great business at any price," and this one is a skill-dependent roll-up financed with 10% debt and a rising share count, trading at 26x forward EBITDA and 52x forward FCF. **Hold if you already own it from the IPO; do not initiate here. Revisit below ~$30.**

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Deal-flow dependence / reflexivity** — the growth engine requires continuous access to cheap distressed assets *and* cheap capital. A high-yield market closure or a rise in asking prices stalls the compounder.
2. **Leverage and refinancing** — $5.0B debt, ~10% coupons, BB-, $0.85B short-term maturities, tangible equity −$4.9B. EBITDA must keep growing to service it.
3. **Revenue durability under repricing** — 1,000% price increases and 75% headcount cuts invite churn, class actions, and regulatory attention (especially in the EU). Harvested assets decay; the P&L won't show it until it does.
4. **Goodwill impairment** — $7.47B of goodwill/intangibles on $2.61B of equity; a single bad deal forces a write-down and a credibility reset.
5. **Continued dilution** — 42% share growth since 2023; if future deals are stock-funded at lower prices, per-share value lags enterprise progress.
6. **Key-man risk** — the moat is four founders in Milan. There is no structural protection if the culture or leadership changes.
7. **AI disruption** — the "rewrite legacy codebases with AI" advantage is available to every competitor, and AI-native alternatives threaten exactly the utility products (notes, file transfer, email marketing) being harvested.

*Data notes: quarterly balance-sheet/cash-flow history is only available from Q2'26; organic growth is not disclosed, so my revenue-durability judgments are inference, not disclosure. Peer multiple comparison is not meaningful for this business model and was not relied upon.*
