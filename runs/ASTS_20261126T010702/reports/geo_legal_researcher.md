## AST SpaceMobile (ASTS) — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment

**Concentration:** revenue is effectively 100% US-sourced today (US government + domestic gateway build-out), while the *forward* contracted book is heavily weighted to sovereign-linked counterparties (Saudi, Japan, European JV). Geographic diversification is therefore real in backlog but unproven in cash.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**(a) Spectrum/regulatory "country-by-country" gating — Probability: High | Impact: High**
Commercial service in Saudi Arabia is explicitly "pending regulatory approvals in Saudi Arabia and **15 other markets**"; Japan's launch depends on final J-LEO rules and Rakuten/AST licences; India remains at consultation stage (TRAI). Each jurisdiction is a separate gate on a single global constellation — a single large-market denial defers revenue without reducing capex. US side: FCC granted AST/AT&T/Verizon/FirstNet supplemental-coverage-from-space authority, but Chairman Carr's July 10, 2026 comments on multi-player D2D competition drove ASTS's sharpest weekly decline in a month (SatNews, Jul 11, 2026), and the FCC continues to arbitrate spectrum boundaries between ASTS and SpaceX ("stay in their lane" order; PCMag).

**(b) US–China / great-power D2D competition and US policy favouritism — Probability: High | Impact: Medium-High**
The competitive field is now SpaceX/T-Mobile, Amazon/Globalstar-Leo and China's SpaceSail, all scaling faster on orbital count. ASTS's differentiator is MNO-licensed low-band spectrum, which makes it structurally dependent on favourable regulatory treatment in Washington (FCC, FirstNet, SDA) — an explicitly political variable. A policy tilt toward vertically integrated incumbents, or an FCC push against foreign-manufactured satellites (headline: "FCC looks to rule against foreign satellites"), would reshape the addressable market. Upside: ASTS manufactures in Texas, which is a tariff/export-control advantage versus foreign-built competitors.

**(c) Export controls, launch dependency and supply chain — Probability: Medium | Impact: High**
Satellite buses, phased arrays and encryption are EAR/ITAR-sensitive; foreign national access to programme data and any non-US component sourcing create licensing friction and disclosure obligations. Launch is third-party-dependent (BlueBird 11–13 manifested for Aug-2026 via Cape Canaveral), with the company's own 20-ton reusable rocket not deploying until **late 2028** — so schedule risk is geopolitical (launch-range, ITAR, provider capacity), not just technical.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action (active):** *Hunter v. AST SpaceMobile, Inc.*, No. 7:26-cv-00378 (W.D. Tex., Judge Counts). Class period **Mar 4, 2025 – Jul 15, 2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline **Nov 13, 2026**. Allegations: understated capital needs/increased debt and dilution, overstated liquidity sufficiency, overstated competitive durability in satellite D2C (including post-EchoStar transaction), and **slow user adoption in the U.S. and Japan**. Trigger event: July 15, 2026 pricing of **$1.0B 1.625% convertible notes due 2034**; shares fell 17% to $55.01 on July 16. Follow-on shareholder investigations (Grabar Law) indicate derivative-demand risk. Sizing: undisclosed; share-price collapse (~55–79% from October highs) enlarges the putative class.
- **Spectrum counterparty/regulatory risk:** L-band access via Ligado Networks (up to 40MHz) sits behind an FCC licence-modification request filed amid **Ligado's bankruptcy** — approval timing outside ASTS's control. Separately, a **$64.5M acquisition of 60MHz of global S-band ITU priority rights** closed with an entity holding those filings; analysts (Light Reading) have publicly called the asset "largely worthless," implying impairment/valuation-dispute exposure and ITU co-ordination risk.
- **Competitive regulatory docket:** FCC actions on EchoStar spectrum (~115MHz split between SpaceX and AT&T) and on D2D spectrum boundaries directly affect ASTS's 248-satellite authorisation and partner economics.
- **Government contracting compliance:** SDA tactical SATCOM award (~$30M) plus additional 2026 U.S. government awards bring FAR/DFARS, DCAA audit and security-clearance obligations.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Sovereign counterparty / FCPA-adjacent exposure** — the anchor commercial contract is with **stc group (Saudi sovereign-linked)** with a $175M prepayment, plus a Japanese sovereign-adjacent JV; third-party/intermediary diligence and books-and-records controls in Gulf contracting warrant scrutiny.
2. **Foreign strategic ownership + US national security** — Rakuten (Japan), Vodafone (UK), Samsung and Google hold equity; foreign ownership of a US satellite operator holding FirstNet/SDA work invites FCC/Team Telecom-style mitigation conditions (no public mitigation agreement found — a gap to verify in the 10-K).
3. **Export-control/data-handling** — cross-border engineering (Spain R&D centre, Luxembourg JV, Japan JV) touching US-controlled space technology and PPDR/public-safety use cases.
4. **Data privacy** — telecom metadata in dozens of jurisdictions (GDPR/UK GDPR in the Vodafone JV; Saudi PDPL; Japan APPI) with the JV structures complicating controllership.
5. **Disclosure-controls overhang** — the class action's core theory (capital-needs and adoption disclosure) is a governance flag for the 2027 proxy season.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Drivers: (i) a live federal securities class action with an expanded class period and a 17% single-day trigger; (ii) revenue recognition gated on 15+ national licensing decisions and a Japanese rulemaking; (iii) spectrum rights whose legal status depends on a bankrupt counterparty's FCC modification and contested ITU priority; (iv) concentrated exposure to Gulf sovereign and Japanese sovereign-adjacent counterparties alongside US national-security-sensitive government work. Offsets: US-based manufacturing (tariff/export-control shelter), FCC SCS authorisation already in hand with AT&T/Verizon/FirstNet, and a European JV validating regulatory groundwork in Spain. Recommend the memo treat regulatory-approval timing — not competition — as the primary de-risking catalyst to monitor.
