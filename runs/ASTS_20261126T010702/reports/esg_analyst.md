### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**
- **Carbon intensity:** No Scope 1/2/3 disclosure, no CDP response, no sustainability report identified. Absolute emissions small (1,126 staff; assembly not smelting), so transition risk from carbon pricing is **low**. Scope 3 (launch vehicles, semiconductors, aluminium) is the real footprint and is entirely undisclosed.
- **Physical climate risk:** Low for operations (Midland TX, launch sites); moderate indirect (launch-window weather, hurricane risk at Cape facilities).
- **Space sustainability (the material E factor):** SpaceX has filed with the FCC objecting to AST's modification application, arguing AST may not satisfy collision-risk-mitigation conditions — an explicit "space sustainability" challenge. BlueWalker 3 was among the brightest objects in the night sky; radio astronomers have flagged interference risk in radio-quiet zones. AST signed a NASA spaceflight-safety agreement (a real mitigant) and is exposed to the FCC 5-year deorbit rule. The live debate over NEPA review of launch emissions/reentry debris/light pollution is a **future permitting-cost lever**.
- **Controversies:** No spills, fines or remediation liabilities.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**
- **Positive:** Direct-to-standard-handset coverage of unserved regions is a genuine digital-inclusion benefit, and is the basis of partner (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Rakuten) and public-safety/FirstNet support — this is a **social licence asset**.
- **Labour:** 1,126 employees with very rapid headcount growth; no disclosed turnover, safety (TRIR) or union data. Manufacturing/launch integration at scale without published HSE metrics is a disclosure gap, not an evidenced problem.
- **Supply chain:** Satellite-component sourcing (incl. possible Asian contract manufacturers) has no disclosed human-rights or conflict-minerals diligence. Export-control/ITAR compliance is a latent risk for a space-hardware exporter.
- **Product/consumer:** High-power space-to-handset transmission raises RF-exposure and adjacent-band interference questions (the OOBE dispute with SpaceX is the commercial proxy).
- **Privacy/dual-use:** Secure government/maritime/defence applications plus a US Space Force award create surveillance and exclusion-screen sensitivity for Article 8/9 fund holders.

### 5. ESG Controversies (financial impact estimates)
1. **Securities fraud class action (active, escalating).** Class period **4-Mar-2025 → 15-Jul-2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline **13-Nov-2026**; multiple firms (Kessler Topaz, Levi & Korsinsky, Kaplan Fox, SBS). Allegations: overstated durability of D2C competitive position after SpaceX's Sept-2025 EchoStar spectrum purchase, understated capital requirements, and dilution-driven losses. Complaint alleges **$277M of insider sales** during the period. Stock peaked ~$133 (May-2026) and trades ~$26 (**-80%**). *Estimated cost: $15–40M defence over 2–3 years; settlement tail risk $50–150M (D&O-insured in part). Reputational/discount-rate impact exceeds the cash cost.*
2. **Guidance credibility breakdown.** Service launch slipped from late-2026 to **early 2027**; **~13 BlueBird/BlueWalker-3 satellites in orbit vs a 45–60 target**, after Blue Origin's New Glenn ground-test explosion. This is the fact pattern underlying the litigation and the primary governance red flag.
3. **Insider selling / change-of-control severance (Sep-2026).** Severance plan adoption preceded buyout speculation — a control-and-entrenchment optics issue, not an environmental one.
4. **SpaceX FCC challenge on debris and out-of-band emissions** — regulatory-competition conflict, unresolved; cost is delay, not fines.
5. **No environmental enforcement actions, sanctions, or bribery incidents identified.**

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD (governance-led)**
Weak governance architecture (controlled company, 71.6% voting bloc, CEO/Chair, incentive metrics tied to share price and capital raising), an unresolved fraud class action alleging $277M of insider sales, near-zero E/S disclosure, and a live space-sustainability/astronomy challenge. Genuine social upside (digital inclusion) and a strengthened regulatory position (FCC grant, Japanese sovereign support) prevent a "critical" designation — but they are offset by disclosure and alignment failures.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE**
The ESG risk here is **not** an environmental liability — it is a **governance discount applied to a story stock**. Three transmission channels:
1. **Cost of capital:** an active fraud class action plus serial convertible issuance argues for a higher discount rate on 2027–2031 cash flows than headline DCF assumes.
2. **Incentive misalignment:** PSUs measured on share price and capital raised, alongside alleged $277M of insider sales, mean management's payoff function has historically rewarded announcements over delivery — precisely the risk the market is now repricing.
3. **Execution/permitting dependency:** launch-provider concentration and orbital-debris compliance are unhedgeable operational gates that can push revenue a further 12+ months.

**Monitoring triggers:** outcome/settlement of the November-2026 lead-plaintiff consolidation; any change to dual-class sunset or board independence; first publication of Scope 1–3 and HSE metrics; FCC disposition of the modification/debris conditions; conversion/refinancing schedule for the 2032–2036 converts (all struck well above the current $26). A move to majority-independent board and a de-linked, TSR-plus-operational PSU framework would be the single fastest route to re-rating the G score toward 5–6.
