**As of 26 November 2026 | FRED data unless noted**

## 1. Macro Environment Summary: **RISK-OFF in long-duration assets; "reflation with a hawkish Fed"**

The US economy is **not** slowing into a recession — it is *re-accelerating while inflation re-accelerates*, which is the single worst combination for a pre-revenue-adjacent, capex-heavy, high-beta growth equity like ASTS.

The sequence that matters:

- The Fed cut from 5.33% (mid-2025) to 3.63% by spring 2026, then **reversed**. It hiked 25bp on **16 Sep 2026** to 3.75–4.00% and held on **28 Oct 2026**. Effective fed funds printed **3.88% in Oct-2026** (FRED FEDFUNDS). CME FedWatch showed **~90% odds of a further hike at the 8–9 Dec 2026 FOMC** (as of 18 Nov); Governor Cook stated on 20 Nov that policy "is not high enough" to lower inflation.
- **Inflation is sticky and rising**: CPI **+3.82% y/y in Oct-2026**, up from **3.72% in Sep** — nearly double the 2% target with core above headline.
- **Real GDP accelerated**: Q3-2026 grew **+4.3% annualised** (Q2 +2.2%, Q1 +2.5%, Q4-2025 +0.2%); y/y +2.3%. Unemployment is **4.1%** (Oct-2026), down from 4.5% a year earlier. This is a full-employment, demand-pull inflation economy — no growth offset to justify higher rates.
- **The curve is bear-steepening, not inverting**: 2Y 4.74%, 10Y **5.13%**, 30Y **5.47%**, 2s10s **+39bp**. The 10Y is up **+69bp since 30 Jun 2026** (4.44% → 5.13%). A bear steepener is a *pure discount-rate shock* — the most damaging shape for equities whose value sits in 2030+ cash flows. There is no recession signal here, and therefore no "rates will fall soon" rescue.
- **Credit is beginning to tighten at the margin**: HY OAS widened from **2.65% (1 Sep)** to **3.72% (24 Nov)** — **+107bp in under three months**, from historically tight levels. Still tight in absolute terms, but the *direction* is what closes financing windows.
- **The dollar is rallying**: broad USD index **123.60 (20 Nov)** vs 118.66 (1 Sep), **+4.2% in 11 weeks**.

ASTS has fallen from **$133.09 (28 May 2026)** to **$26.02 (25 Nov)** — **–80.4%** — and **–44% in the last month alone**. Beta is **2.85**. The proximate causes were launch delays and fresh share issuance, but the macro tape is the amplifier: this is precisely the profile (unprofitable, cash-burning, terminal-value-heavy, retail-flow-dependent) that gets sold hardest when the 10Y clears 5%.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**A. Interest-rate sensitivity — the dominant channel. NEGATIVE.**
ASTS is a textbook long-duration asset: Q3-2026 revenue was just **$42.3M** against a **$10.7B market cap** (≈250x sales), with essentially all enterprise value in 2028–2032 subscriber economics. Every 100bp on the long end of the discount curve hits this equity far harder than the market. With the 10Y +69bp in five months, a mechanical duration haircut of ~10–15% on the terminal value applies *before* any idiosyncratic news. The construction is also rate-sensitive operationally: satellite manufacturing, launch services and ground infrastructure are financed today and monetised years later, so the cost of capital compounds against the build-out.

**B. Refinancing risk — genuinely LOWER than the bears claim, but the forward window is closing. NEUTRAL-to-NEGATIVE.**
ASTS term-funded itself well *before* the window shut: **$1.0B of 2.25% converts due 2036 (Feb-2026)**, **$1.0B of 1.625% converts due 2034 priced Jul-2026** (effective conversion price **$149.20**), plus an earlier 1.625% tranche — i.e. low-coupon, long-dated paper, and **$3.2B of cash as of 30 Sep 2026**. There is **no near-term maturity wall**. That is a real, underappreciated macro positive.
The problem is the *next* raise. At a 3.72% and widening HY OAS and 5.13% 10Y, an unrated, cash-burning issuer cannot issue 1.6–2.25% converts again. With conversion prices at $149.20 versus a $26.02 stock, the converts are effectively straight debt for years — so incremental capex (Q2-2026 capex ~$610M, Q3 ~$310M) must come from **equity sold into weakness**. The Nov-2026 "fresh share issuance" reaction confirms the dilution tax is now live. Macro drives *the price at which ASTS must sell equity*, and that price has fallen 80%.

**C. Inflation on the cost structure — NEGATIVE, and under-modelled.**
3.8% CPI against multi-year fixed-price launch and manufacturing contracts means input-cost inflation with **no pricing power to pass it through**. ASTS's revenue model is wholesale capacity revenue-share with MNO partners (Vodafone, Verizon, AT&T, Rakuten and others) — long-term, contractually indexed-down arrangements, not spot pricing. So inflation compresses the spread between 2026 capex dollars and 2029 revenue dollars. Rising launch prices (Rocket Lab's CFO flagging *higher* Electron prices in Nov-2026 is a direct read-across) are a real cost headwind.

**D. Currency — NEGATIVE (modest today, material later).**
Revenue is USD-reported but economically global. The broad dollar's **+4.2% rally in 11 weeks** is a translation headwind on Japanese, European and emerging-market revenue, and it also reduces the USD value of partner revenue-share. Today this is small ($42.3M quarterly revenue); by the time revenue scales, FX could be a 2–4pt swing factor. Note the **$1B Japan J-LEO subsidy** — a fiscal/industrial-policy tailwind, but its USD value shrinks as the dollar strengthens.

**E. Credit conditions and risk appetite — NEGATIVE.**
Beta 2.85, heavy retail/momentum ownership. The widening of HY spreads and the November de-rating of the whole space complex (Rocket Lab –9% in a session, SpaceX below its IPO price on lender pushback) is the classic late-cycle liquidity drain from speculative long-duration equities. This is a flow-of-funds headwind, not a fundamentals one.

**F. Two genuine macro OFFSETS worth flagging.**
1. **High rates relatively favour satellite over terrestrial buildout.** Rural fibre and tower coverage requires enormous, rate-sensitive upfront capex; ASTS's proposition is coverage at a fraction of that capital intensity. A 5.13% 10Y makes MNOs *more* receptive to a wholesale satellite alternative for low-density geographies — a structural tailwind hiding inside a rate headwind.
2. **Solid employment (4.1%) and 2.3% real growth** support mobile data demand and MNO willingness to sign distribution deals. Government demand (Japan subsidy, US defence/ SDA-type budgets) is largely insensitive to the fed funds rate.

**Tariffs/trade:** moderate exposure via imported electronics and launch services; a secondary factor versus rates.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The macro regime is actively working against this specific business model on four of six channels — discount rate, cost of incremental capital, input-cost inflation, and dollar strength — with only partial offsets from balance-sheet pre-funding and solid end-demand. Note the honest caveat: **80% of the last month's decline is idiosyncratic** (launch delays, dilution, 45-satellites-by-mid-2027 credibility). Macro is not the cause; it is the reason the market will not pay up to forgive it. In a 4.0% 10Y world, ASTS at $26 would already be a different stock.

**What would flip this to Neutral/Favourable:** 10Y back below ~4.50%, HY OAS back under 3.00%, and CPI prints at or below 3.0%. None of those are imminent with core inflation running near 3.8% and a December hike ~90% priced.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise: **a violent disinflation-driven duration rally (BULL)**

The market is currently priced for the bear case: a December hike, a 5%+ 10Y, and a permanently higher term premium. The asymmetric surprise is the opposite — the same demand-pull inflation that is now squeezing ASTS could roll over quickly if growth decelerates off a **+4.3% Q3** base (that number is not sustainable) while the Fed's hikes bite into an already-levered consumer. A move from CPI 3.8% → ~2.5% by mid-2027, with the 10Y falling from 5.13% to ~4.00% and HY spreads retracing from 3.72% to ~2.80%, would do two things simultaneously: cut ASTS's discount rate by more than a full point and **reopen the convertible market at 2%-coupon terms**, removing the dilution overhang that is currently capping the stock.

Because ASTS's beta is 2.85 and options/retail flow dominate its marginal pricing, the re-rating would be non-linear: the November 2026 selloff has compressed the share price to **~0.17x its July 2026 convertible strike ($149.20)**. A disinflation shock of that magnitude, arriving alongside any launch-cadence proof point, could plausibly produce a **100%+ move within two quarters** — a pure macro-duration convexity trade.
**The mirror-image tail:** if the Fed is forced to take policy to 5%+ to break inflation, the convertible window stays shut, ASTS funds the satellite constellation with equity at $20–25 (15–20% dilution per raise), and the stock grinds toward the high-teens. I assign the bull surprise the higher *surprise value* — but the bear path the higher probability today.
