# Sector Brief: AST SpaceMobile (ASTS) — Satellite Direct-to-Device Communications

**Data note:** ASTS is classified by GICS as Communication Services / Telecommunications Services, which is misleading. Its revenue drivers, comps, and risks are those of a satellite D2D network operator. I have benchmarked against *both* the GICS sector aggregate and the true operational peer set (Iridium, Globalstar, Viasat; SpaceX/Starlink as the private benchmark). Current price $26.02, market cap $10.66B, beta 2.85.

---

## 1. Sector Overview

ASTS competes in satellite **direct-to-device (D2D)** — space-based cellular broadband connecting to *unmodified* smartphones, monetised via revenue-share with mobile network operators (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone). The sub-sector sits at the very front of its S-curve: ASTS has 12 BlueBird satellites in orbit against a target of ~45 by mid-2027, while SpaceX's Starlink operates ~11,100 satellites (Oct 2026) and has commercial D2D live with T-Mobile. Critically, the legacy satellite operators in the same GICS bucket — Iridium, Viasat, Globalstar — have already matured into low-single-digit growers, so "satellite telecom" as a whole is a mature, capital-intensive, mid-cycle industry *inside which* D2D is a genuinely new growth pocket. The FCC's proposed D2D spectrum auction (Oct 2026) is the single largest structural variable: it creates a rules-based path to scale but simultaneously invites the deepest-pocketed competitor to bid.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Regulatory unlocking of dedicated D2D spectrum.** The FCC's October 2026 proposal to open wireless spectrum for direct-to-device services converts a spectrum-constrained business into an auctionable, financeable one. ASTS already holds temporary D2D test authority and its partners' nationwide sub-1GHz spectrum.
- **Carrier demand is de-risked, not speculative.** All three US majors have chosen sides — T-Mobile/Starlink vs. **AT&T + Verizon/ASTS** — meaning ASTS's distribution is pre-contracted with two of the largest MNOs on earth. AT&T has begun prepping a beta D2D service with ASTS.
- **Revenue is inflecting fast off a near-zero base.** Q3 2026 revenue $42.3M, **+187% y/y and +34% q/q** (Q1 $14.7M → Q2 $31.5M → Q3 $42.3M). The addressable pool is ~5bn existing smartphone subscribers already owned by the MNO partners — no device replacement cycle required.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Launch execution risk is now the binding constraint — and it has already broken.** Launch delays drove a ~**80% drawdown from the $129.89 January 2026 peak to $26.02** (-55.7% in the last ~10 weeks alone). Rising from 12 to 45 satellites by mid-2027 depends on third-party launch cadence ASTS does not control; SpaceX, its principal rival, owns its launch.
- **Gross margin is *compressing* as revenue scales — the opposite of the operating-leverage thesis.** Gross margin fell from 53.4% (FY25) → 24.9% (Q1'26) → 25.2% (Q2'26) → **40.2% (Q3'26)**, versus 64.8% at Globalstar and 77.3% at Iridium. Mix is tilting to lower-margin government/MNO revenue. FY25 operating margin was **-405.7%**.
- **Capital intensity meets a closing financing window.** 9M 2026 net loss $554M vs. $342M for all of FY25; Q3 interest expense $30.3M (annualising ~$121M); diluted shares up **+17% y/y** (256.0M → 300.1M) with fresh issuance in Q4. Space equities are simultaneously de-rating (Rocket Lab -9%, SpaceX below its IPO price), so equity is both the most available and the most expensive currency.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **AVERAGE**

**Rationale — real strengths:** ASTS holds the only proven broadband-grade D2D technology (video-capable, not just SMS), a patent portfolio, ITU orbital priority, and exclusive-ish carrier relationships with two of three US majors. It is a legitimate first mover with a defensible technology position and no listed pure-play peer of comparable ambition.

**Rationale — structural weaknesses:** (i) **Scale asymmetry is brutal** — 12 satellites vs. Starlink's ~11,100, and SpaceX launches at internal marginal cost; (ii) **launch dependency** is the exact risk that has already delayed the model by ~5 years per sell-side revisions; (iii) **MNO bargaining power caps the moat** — ASTS captures a revenue share of partners' retail pricing, not the retail price, limiting gross margin at the very moment scale should be delivering it; (iv) **balance sheet** — accelerating cash burn, rising interest, 17% annual dilution; (v) terrestrial 5G buildout slowly shrinks the "unserved" TAM.

**Porter's Five Forces:** Rivalry **HIGH** (SpaceX), Supplier power **HIGH** (launch), Buyer power **HIGH** (MNO gatekeepers), Entry threat **MEDIUM-LOW** (spectrum/capital barriers), Substitutes **MEDIUM** (terrestrial 5G). Net: an attractive technology in a structurally difficult competitive position.

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

The D2D TAM is real, large, and newly legitimised by FCC spectrum action — an **Attractive** long-run opportunity. But as an investable sub-sector in late 2026 it is **capital-hungry, winner-take-most toward SpaceX, and dependent on a space-equity financing window that is currently shut**. Legacy satellite peers show what maturity looks like (4.9% growth, high margins); ASTS shows what the pre-scale phase costs (-399% operating margin, 80% drawdown). The risk-adjusted return profile is asymmetric in the wrong direction until launch cadence visibly inflects and gross margin resumes expansion.

**Key monitorables:** (1) Q4 2026 revenue vs. the $150–200M FY guide, (2) BlueBird launch cadence toward 45 by mid-2027, (3) gross margin direction now that commercial mix dominates, (4) FCC D2D auction rules and whether ASTS must bid against SpaceX, (5) dilution and debt terms in the next capital raise.
