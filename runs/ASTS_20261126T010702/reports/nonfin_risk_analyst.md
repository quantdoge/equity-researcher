### 2. Regulatory & Legal Risk Summary

**Adverse:**
- **Securities fraud class action** — *U.S. District Court, W.D. Tex., No. 7:26-cv-00378*, class period **4 Mar 2025 – 15 Jul 2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline passed 13 Nov 2026. Allegations: (a) capital/liquidity position misrepresented as sufficient, (b) dilution would come sooner and larger than signalled, (c) competitive durability overstated *after EchoStar's spectrum sale to SpaceX*, (d) US and Japan user adoption slower than represented. Multiple firms (Rosen, Kessler Topaz, SBS, Robbins, Henzel) are soliciting — high claim volume, low individual merit threshold, but a real settlement/opt-out overhang.
- **FCC Ligado L-band proceeding is contested and politically entangled.** AST's spectrum/partnership deal with Ligado (bankruptcy-court approved; supported by a ~$550M loan) requires FCC approval of Ligado's licence modification. **Iridium has formally opposed it**, and Capitol Forum reports critics fear the FCC will approve a Ligado/AST package as part of settling Ligado's ~$39B litigation. Outcome is binary and outside AST's control.
- **Competitive-regulatory asymmetry:** the FCC-sanctioned ~$17B EchoStar→SpaceX AWS-4/H-block transfer (≈65 MHz) hands Starlink a nationwide D2D spectrum position AST does not own outright — AST depends on *partner MNO spectrum*.

**Favourable:**
- **FCC granted AST commercial SCS (direct-to-device) authorisation in April 2026**, plus approval to operate a **248-satellite constellation** — the single largest regulatory de-risking event for the equity.
- **No SEC enforcement actions or DOJ matters found** in EDGAR full-text search. No short-seller report alleging accounting fraud surfaced.

**Pending/ongoing:** FCC SCS rulemaking (2026 NPRM opening ~482 MHz across 13 bands) — net positive for the category but lowers barriers for Starlink.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Launch access is the binding constraint, and it is externally controlled.** AST was heavily reliant on Blue Origin's **New Glenn**, which is grounded while the company rebuilds its launch pad after an explosion. Deployment milestones have been pushed back; independent analysis (Kratos) concludes AST has **too few satellites for beta service this year** and that the **2027 outlook is uncertain**; the "45 satellites by mid-2027" target looks increasingly stretched. Q3-26 revenue of **$42.3M** (+187% YoY) and a **$132.4M quarterly net loss** underline how early commercialisation still is.
2. **The "Blacklift" vertical-integration gamble.** AST is developing its own 20-ton reusable rocket using **Firefly Aerospace Miranda engines**, first flight targeted late-2028 or beyond. This converts a launch-contracting problem into a multi-year, capital-hungry, unproven development programme; industry commentary is openly skeptical. It introduces **new key-partner dependency (Firefly)** and significant technical/execution risk for a company that has never flown a rocket.
3. **Key-person and concentration risk.** 1,126 employees; a single founder who is Chairman and CEO and the public face of both the technology and the equity story; single manufacturing footprint (Midland, TX); revenue concentrated in a handful of MNO partners and **US government customers**. Loss of Avellan, or a failed Blacklift milestone, would be hard to absorb.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment

- **Severe price and narrative damage.** $26.02 versus a **$133.86 peak on 28 May 2026 — an ~81% drawdown in six months**; 1Y TSR −46.6%; 52-week low set 20 Nov 2026. "Down 44% in a month," "79% collapse," "hard landing" is now the dominant media frame.
- **Litigation optics.** A rolling drumbeat of plaintiff-firm press releases (Rosen, Kessler Topaz, SBS, Robbins) alleging **securities fraud** is a recurring headline generator through 2027.
- **Retail/meme dynamic.** Heavy retail-holder base, 20–55M share daily volumes, celebrity commentary (Cramer: "a very hard stock to own"), and a reported **presidential portfolio disclosure including ASTS** — all raise volatility and politicise the story. Sell-side remains split (B. Riley upgrade to Buy, ~$48 target, vs. Scotiabank sell).
- **ESG/consumer:** no product-safety, recall or data-privacy controversy identified. Reputational risk here is **investor-trust-driven, not customer-driven** — which is the more dangerous kind for a pre-scale capital-markets-dependent issuer.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

The regulatory *product* pathway is genuinely de-risked (FCC SCS authorisation, 248-satellite grant, zero SEC enforcement history). But the combination of **live securities-fraud litigation targeting exactly the disclosure issues that recur**, **a binary contested FCC decision (Ligado)**, **launch capability outside management's control**, **a speculative rocket programme**, and **founder control with $147M of insider monetisation** produces a non-financial risk profile well above what a $10.7B capitalisation normally carries. This is a high-variance, narrative-dependent asset where non-financial risk is the primary driver of outcomes.
