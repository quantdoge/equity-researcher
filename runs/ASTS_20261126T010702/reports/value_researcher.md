## AST SpaceMobile (ASTS) — Value Investment Analysis
*Data vintage: latest reported fundamentals Q3 FY2026 (filed 2026‑11‑09); price $26.02 (25 Nov 2026 close). The stock is down ~80% from its January 2026 intraday high (~$130) and ~70% from March.*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **None today** (a *candidate* Narrow/Wide moat exists only if execution succeeds)

**What the business is:** a pre-scale satellite operator building a low‑Earth‑orbit constellation ("BlueBirds") that connects directly to unmodified smartphones using partner MNO spectrum. Revenue is currently a mix of government/partner milestone work and early commercial service, not recurring subscriber revenue.

**Evidence for a potential moat (real, but unproven):**
- **Intangible assets/spectrum:** shared MNO spectrum plus controlled MSS spectrum targeting ~100 MHz in the U.S., 60+ MHz globally; 60+ MNO partners representing 3B+ subscribers (AT&T validation of space‑to‑ground beam handover; authorizations in Brazil, Japan, UK, Saudi Arabia, Canada).
- **Switching costs / network effects (latent):** carrier integration is sticky once built, and a wholesale D2D layer used by multiple carriers has a two‑sided network quality.
- **Capital/regulatory barriers:** DPAS priority rating, orbital slots, 100+ BlueBirds under launch agreements, and $1.0B of **non‑dilutive** Japanese government funding.

**Evidence against a moat, and why I score it None *today*:**
- **No pricing power demonstrated.** FY2025 revenue was $70.9M, yet Q4 FY2025 alone was $54.3M and Q1 FY2026 fell to $14.7M — revenue is lumpy and milestone‑driven, not recurring. Q2/Q3 FY2026 were $31.5M/$42.3M, both below consensus. That is the opposite of the predictable revenue stream a moat should produce.
- **Returns on capital are deeply negative and not converging:** ROIC −6.2% (2025), −28.8% (2024), −77.6% (2023). A moated business earns ROIC > WACC *consistently*; ASTS has never earned a positive return on capital.
- **A far stronger competitor with structural cost advantages:** SpaceX/Starlink direct‑to‑cell is vertically integrated with its own launch (the decisive cost variable in LEO). ASTS depends on third‑party launch and is now spending shareholder capital to develop its own 20‑ton reusable rocket ("BlackLift," engines from Firefly, service from late 2028) — an admission that launch access is its binding constraint, plus a multi‑year, multi‑billion‑dollar detour outside its core competence.
- **Trajectory: the moat has not widened in 5 years — it has merely not yet been disproven.** Coverage is still "intermittent, nationwide service activation expected before year‑end 2026"; the company has repeatedly missed launch timelines (2026 target flagged at risk).

**Verdict:** this is a legitimate, technically remarkable first‑mover franchise with a *possible* Narrow moat in 2029–2031, and a real possibility of no moat at all. Under Buffett's standard — a durable moat demonstrable in the financials — it fails today. It also sits at the edge of my circle of competence: outcomes hinge on launch cadence, orbital mechanics and binary financing events, not on consumer franchise durability.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

No conventional DCF is defensible: the DCF/owner‑earnings models are inapplicable to negative, accelerating‑loss cash flows. So I value it as a **probability‑weighted scenario of a 2030 steady state**, then demand a margin of safety.

- Market cap ≈ **$7.8B** (300.1M diluted shares × $26.02); FMP's all‑share‑class figure is **$10.66B**. Net debt $1.32B → **EV ≈ $9.1B–$12.0B**.
- **EV / annualized Q3 revenue ($169M) ≈ 54×–71×. EV / FY2026E revenue (~$164M) ≈ 56×–73×.** EBITDA and FCF multiples are negative. For reference, sector-peer median P/E is 23× — ASTS has no P/E at all.
- **What the price requires (reverse DCF):** to earn a 12% return with a 20× terminal FCF multiple, today's ~$11B EV requires ~**$1.1B of sustainable FCF by ~2032**, i.e., roughly **$4B of revenue at a 25–27% FCF margin** — *after* funding another $3–5B of satellites, each with a finite ~5–7 year life (so true maintenance capex at scale is enormous; EBITDA will materially overstate owner earnings).

**Scenario weighting (illustrative, 300M+ shares, additional assumed dilution):**

| Scenario | Prob. | 2030 revenue | FCF margin | Implied value/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bull — ~100 satellites fly, D2D becomes a wholesale standard | 30% | $4.0B | 25% | ~$36 |
| Base — real but sub‑scale revenue, heavy dilution, modest FCF | 40% | $2.0B | 15% | ~$6 |
| Bear — deployment slips, dilutive rescue financing, de‑rating | 30% | <$0.8B | neg. | ~$3 |

**Probability‑weighted intrinsic value ≈ $10–15/share** vs. **$26.02** — the stock trades at roughly a **80–150% premium** to my estimate. There is **no margin of safety**; there is a margin of speculative hope. (Caveat: my scenario weights are judgment, not precision — but every input would have to move favorably at once to justify today's price.)

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Mixed (founder‑led, well‑aligned, poorly capital‑efficient)**

**Positives:** Abel Avellan is a founder‑operator with substantial insider ownership and control (Class B/C structure); the partner‑first MNO strategy has been executed exceptionally well (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Rakuten, Google); the $1.0B non‑dilutive Japan award and DPAS rating show real government‑relations skill; note issuance was done into strength rather than distress.

**Negatives:** four consecutive years of cumulative cash consumption with **5.5× dilution** — growth financed almost entirely by shareholders, with per‑share value destroyed. Timeline credibility is weak (repeated launch slips; 2026 target at risk). "BlackLift" — building your own 20‑ton rocket, first launch late 2028 — is precisely the kind of capital‑intensive, competence‑stretching vertical integration Buffett warns against; it also expands the funding need. SBC of ~$170M/yr at ~$170M of revenue is egregious. No buybacks are appropriate here, but there is no record of disciplined capital allocation to judge.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **Sell / Avoid**

For a Buffett‑style portfolio this is not a "hold and wait" situation — it is a business that never qualified for purchase and now trades at roughly **55–70× sales with −$1.8B of trailing free cash flow, negative ROIC, $4.1B of debt, and a financing gap**. Even accepting the genuinely attractive bull case, probability‑weighted value lands far below the current quote.

I am **not** predicting the technology fails — I am saying I cannot underwrite the outcome, cannot compute a defensible intrinsic value, and am not being paid for that uncertainty. *"A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy"*; this one trades at a rich price.

**What would change my mind (revisit triggers):**
1. Continuously available, contractually recurring service revenue (not milestones) with gross margin >50% and positive contribution margin per satellite;
2. A funded path to 45–90 satellites **without** further large equity dilution (e.g., fully financed by MNO prepayments or non‑recourse project debt);
3. Evidence of durable pricing power against Starlink D2C — e.g., multi‑year wholesale contracts with fixed per‑subscriber economics;
4. Sustained positive operating cash flow, and ROIC trending above WACC.
At that point a 20–30× multiple on real, recurring, high‑margin revenue could be defensible; today, none of these is in hand.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (i.e., ways I could be wrong)
- **Technology/first‑mover payoff:** if ASTS's larger satellites deliver materially superior broadband economics and 60+ MNOs scale demand quickly, revenue could exceed my bull case and the "narrow moat" becomes a wide one.
- **Financing availability:** convertibles have been raised at favorable terms; continued capital‑market access at high share prices would dilute less than I assume (conversely, a closed window is catastrophic).
- **Government anchor:** DPAS priority, U.S. government demand, and the $1B Japan subsidy could effectively underwrite the buildout and de‑risk the equity.
- **Consolidation/strategic bid:** an MNO consortium, hyperscaler or prime contractor could pay a strategic premium for the spectrum/patent/partner position.
- **Competitive stumble:** Starlink D2C spectrum constraints or regulatory setbacks would materially improve ASTS's position.
- **Short‑squeeze/valuation regime risk:** with beta ~2.85 and heavy retail ownership, the shares can re‑rate far above fundamentals for extended periods — being "right" here may require a patience and volatility tolerance I do not get paid for.
