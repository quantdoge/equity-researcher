# IONQ — Geopolitical, Legal & Regulatory Risk Assessment
*As of the most recent filing/earnings cycle (3Q26, reported Nov 5, 2026)*

## Company Snapshot
IonQ is a College Park, MD–headquartered full-stack quantum platform and foundry (Technology / Computer Hardware; ~1,132 employees; market cap ~$14.0B; beta 3.30). FY2026 guidance was raised to **$505–515M** after 3Q26 revenue of **$173.8M (+336% YoY)** and backlog/RPO of **~$1.2B**. The company has transformed from a US-focused quantum-cloud lessor into a **vertically integrated, multi-jurisdiction hardware + foundry + space group** via 12 acquisitions, most importantly SkyWater Technology ($1.8B, closed July 31, 2026), Oxford Ionics (~$1.075B, closed Sept 2025), ID Quantique (closed Apr 2025) and Capella Space. (Sources: FMP company profile; IonQ 3Q26 release via Business Wire/Morningstar; 2Q26 release; Converge Digest; SEC EX-21.1 subsidiary list.)

**Concentration insight:** the single most important "geography" is not a country but a *customer class* — the US federal government and its allied equivalents. Wolfpack Research alleges that "backdoor earmarks" in the Pentagon budget represented **up to 86% of booked revenue** (Fortune, Feb 4, 2026; Wolfpack report). Even at a fraction of that claim, IonQ is fundamentally a **sovereign-demand story**, which makes appropriations cycles a geopolitical variable in their own right.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Export-control reclassification of IonQ hardware / cloud access (probability: Medium-High; impact: Very High)**
IonQ's own compliance page states Forte Enterprise and Tempo are classified **EAR99** — "generally exportable without a licence" — and was **last revised April 17, 2025**. That is materially stale. Since then: BIS quantum controls (Jan 2025) cover advanced quantum computers, related hardware, software and technology; Western export controls are explicitly "evolving into an industrial strategy" (IISS analysis via The Quantum Insider, Aug 2026); and **Executive Order 14411 (June 22, 2026), "Ushering in the Next Frontier of Quantum Innovation,"** elevates quantum to a US national-security priority (White House; Baker McKenzie). IonQ management publicly *welcomed* those EOs (2Q26 release) — but the same policy vector that creates demand also creates the licence regime. With **~50% of revenue international**, a move from EAR99 to a controlled ECCN, or a deemed-export/end-user restriction on cloud delivery to non-allied customers, is the single largest geopolitical cliff in the model.

**Risk 2 — US federal budget, appropriations and election-cycle volatility (probability: High; impact: High)**
Government contracts are "typically structured in phases, with each phase subject to satisfaction of certain conditions" (IonQ 10-K FY2025, via SEC full-text index), and the Wolfpack episode shows how fast earmark-dependent revenue can be repriced when control of Congress changes. The **November 2026 midterms produced a Democratic sweep** (CNBC, Nov 7, 2026), which changes defense/quantum appropriations priorities and oversight posture. Backlog of ~$1.2B is only as good as the appropriations behind it. Related: the 8-Ks of Oct 13/16, 2026 are unexplained here (see gaps).

**Risk 3 — Multi-jurisdiction investment screening and M&A execution risk (probability: Medium; impact: Medium)**
IonQ has completed at least three deals requiring foreign-direct-investment clearance — Oxford Ionics (UK Investment Security Unit final order, gov.uk), ID Quantique (Swiss/Korean nexus, UK ISU approval cited), and SkyWater (US, "final regulatory approval," Sept 2026). Quantum is now the archetypal "sovereignty-sensitive" sector: CFIUS, UK NSI Act, EU FDI screening and the new **Comprehensive Outbound Investment National Security Act (2026)** regime restricting US capital into Chinese quantum all narrow IonQ's future deal space and its ability to co-develop with non-allied partners. Every remaining acquisition in the pipeline carries a multi-agency approval tail.

*Honourable mention:* **Section 232 tariffs.** A 25% tariff on certain imported semiconductors and derivative products took effect **January 14–15, 2026** (Federal Register, Jan 20, 2026; Morgan Lewis). As a US Trusted Foundry, SkyWater is a relative tariff *beneficiary* versus imported competitors — but its own inbound equipment/materials and any non-US-content supply chain create cost and compliance friction, and this is flagged in IonQ's FY2025 10-K risk factors (SEC filing analysis, sec-api.io).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action — dismissed and affirmed.** *Defeo v. IonQ, Inc.* (4th Cir., No. 24-1709, decided **April 8, 2025**) — the Fourth Circuit **affirmed dismissal** of the SPAC-era fraud class action; Cooley publicised the win. **This overhang is cleared.**
- **Wolfpack Research short report (Feb 4, 2026).** Alleges IonQ misrepresented the extent to which revenue depended on now-cancelled Pentagon earmarks and questions insider sales; shares fell ~8–11%. IonQ called the report "false, misleading, and unsubstantiated." (Fortune, Feb 4, 2026; Barron's; DataCenterDynamics.)
- **Scott+Scott D&O investigation (Feb 12, 2026).** Announced investigation of IonQ directors/officers for breach of fiduciary duty in the wake of the Wolfpack allegations (Business Wire). **This is a derivative-demand/investigation posture, not yet confirmed litigation** — the highest-probability vector for a new lawsuit.
- **Capital-markets disclosure discipline.** IonQ's own 10-K carried the candid warning that it "has not produced a scalable quantum computer and face[s] significant barriers" (quoted in Fortune) — a fact pattern plaintiffs' firms use to argue the opposite was marketed.
- **Regulatory posture is broadly supportive, not adversarial.** No known DOJ/FTC/SEC enforcement action. EO 14411, DARPA/DoD programmes, Sandia and Anduril MoUs place IonQ on the *favoured* side of US industrial policy. Antitrust risk from consolidation is currently de minimis given IonQ's share relative to IBM/Google/Quantinuum.
- **IPO/SPAC lineage:** 2021 dMY Technology Group merger; dual-class-free NYSE listing. No delisting or listing-compliance issues.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Stale public export-control position.** The "EAR99, no licence needed" representation is dated **April 17, 2025** — before EO 14411 and the 2026 tightening cycle. If engineering, sales or investor materials still rely on that page, that is a disclosure and compliance gap.
2. **Deemed-export / foreign-national access.** Rapid hiring in the UK, Switzerland and Korea, plus the SkyWater foundry (ITAR-adjacent, Trusted Foundry), raises deemed-export and technology-control-plan exposure. This is the classic FCPA-adjacent/trade-compliance failure mode for defence-linked tech.
3. **Korean entity naming anomaly.** The subsidiary list shows "**ID Quantique Limited — Republic of Korea**" alongside the Swiss `id Quantique SA` and Delaware `ID Quantique Inc.` Structurally unusual; worth confirming local licensing for QKD/cryptography products (Korean crypto/network-security certification).
4. **Space segment compliance surface.** Capella Space brings NOAA remote-sensing licensing, FCC spectrum, ITAR/USML and export-controlled SAR imagery — a materially higher compliance burden than quantum compute, and a business acquired out of financial stress.
5. **No evidence of FCPA/AML/sanctions violations or OFAC exposure.** China/Russia revenue: none disclosed; D:5 storage is contractually prohibited.
6. **Insider-sale scrutiny.** Wolfpack's allegations on insider selling are the type of fact that attracts derivative suits independent of merit.

## 5. Overall Rating

**Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk: MEDIUM**
(with a **High** legal/regulatory sub-rating and a **Low** jurisdictional-risk sub-rating)

**Rationale:** IonQ's operating footprint sits almost entirely inside the US–UK–Switzerland–Korea–Japan allied bloc, so classic EM/nationalisation/currency-control risk is negligible. What elevates the rating is the **combination** of (a) ~50% international revenue resting on a revocable EAR99 classification in the single most policy-sensitive technology category in the world, (b) a revenue base tied to US federal appropriations now facing a changed Congress, and (c) an unresolved short-seller narrative with a live D&O investigation and a 12-acquisition integration burden that each required — and will require — national-security clearance.

**What would move this to High:** BIS moving quantum systems/cloud from EAR99 to a controlled ECCN; a securities class action filed off the Wolfpack report; or a CFIUS/ISU/EU screening block on a pending deal.
