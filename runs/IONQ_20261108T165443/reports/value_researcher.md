## IONQ — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $32.62 (latest available; stock data through 2026-02-27, fundamentals through 3Q26 filed 2026-11-05). Market cap ≈ $14.1B on ~431M shares.*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NONE** (potential narrow, not yet earned)

IonQ is a full-stack quantum company that became a vertically integrated manufacturer in 2026 by acquiring **SkyWater** (foundry). Two segments now:

| Segment (3Q26) | Revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| IonQ Platform (quantum computing, networking, sensing, security) | $90.3M | +127% |
| SkyWater Foundry Services | $83.5M | (acquired) |

**Genuine positives:** only trapped-ion firm advanced to DARPA QBI Stage C (up to $300M potential), a 256-qubit "Superion" system going on-premise at NVIDIA's Accelerated Quantum Research Center, $1.2B backlog, and the strongest balance sheet in the pure-play sector ($3.7B cash/investments pro-forma for the October 2026 raise). Intangible assets (patents, DARPA validation, government contracts) are real.

**But no moat is *demonstrated*.** The test is pricing power and returns on capital, and both say no:

- **Gross margin is collapsing:** 63.2% (2023) → 52.2% (2024) → 40.4% (2025) → **23.0% in 9M26**. The mix shift into foundry services and hardware deployments is dilutive, not accretive. A moated business does not cede 40 points of gross margin while revenue quadruples.
- **ROIC: −16.5% (FY25)**, worse in FY26 on a vastly larger capital base. Negative for four straight years. This is the single most damning fact against a moat.
- **No switching costs yet.** Quantum workloads are experimental; customers (governments, FIU, SDT, AFRL, D-Wave) run pilots and multi-vendor research programs.
- **Competition is the largest-capitalized in technology:** IBM, Google, Microsoft, Amazon, plus PsiQuantum. IonQ spends ~$1B/yr on opex against those balance sheets. Its differentiation (trapped ion) is scientifically credible but not commercially proven at scale.

*Moat verdict: none by Buffett's standard. What exists is a credible technology option, not a durable franchise.*

| Stock-based comp | $69.7M | $106.9M | $312.0M | **$523.9M (138% of revenue)** |

The score of 2 comes entirely from liquidity, not from the business. Cash went $998M (9/30/26) → **$3.7B pro-forma** after the October 2026 offering; total debt $154M, so net cash ≈ $3.55B. That buys roughly five years of runway — real, and it is why solvency is not the risk.

Everything else fails: revenue growth is bought (12 acquisitions in ~2 years), gross margin is falling as revenue rises, cash burn is accelerating (−$83M in 4Q25 → −$332M in 3Q26), and SBC exceeds revenue. **GAAP net income is meaningless here** — swings of +$564M (3Q26) and −$1.87B (2Q26) are mark-to-market on Series A/B warrant liabilities (a $896M non-cash gain in 3Q26), plus a $142M tax benefit. Ignore the reported EPS sign entirely; FCF is the truth.

**Dilution is the silent partner:** shares went 197.7M (2022) → 280.3M (2025) → ~431M today, **+118% in four years (~21%/yr)**. Goodwill and intangibles are $4.37B of $5.02B equity; tangible book value is ~$0.65B, or ~$1.50/share, against a $32.62 price (**~21x tangible book**).

### 3. Valuation Analysis — Intrinsic value ≈ **$22/share**; price ≈ **$32.62**

A DCF on negative FCF returns nothing useful (`base_fcf` −$299.6M; the tool's output is degenerate). So I value the option on future cash flows explicitly.

**Enterprise value:** $14.07B market cap + $154M debt − $3.7B cash ≈ **$10.5B**. Add ~$2.3B of non-debt liabilities (warrant and contingent-earnout obligations payable largely in stock) and the economic EV is closer to **$12.5B**.

**Multiples:** EV/FY26E sales ~20x; EV/FY27E sales ~10x; P/TBV ~21x; FCF yield negative. Peer-median P/E is 38x — meaningless against a loss-maker, so no useful relative anchor exists.

**What the price requires (reverse DCF, 12% discount, 3% terminal growth):** ~$950M–$1.1B of steady-state free cash flow. At a mature 20% FCF margin that is **$4.5–5.5B of revenue within ~10 years — roughly 9–10x 2026 revenue (a 25%+ CAGR for a decade)** — with gross margins roughly doubling from today's 23% and a ~$1.3B annual operating loss turning into a ~$1B annual profit. That is IonQ becoming a company with revenue comparable to a top-tier semicap equipment maker.

**Scenario-weighted intrinsic value** (2033 horizon, discounted at 12%):

| Scenario | Prob. | 2033 revenue | FCF margin | Exit multiple | Value/share today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear — commercialization slips, becomes a gov't/research contractor + foundry | 40% | ~$1.0B | 12% | 20x | ~$11 |
| Base — real but modest commercial market | 40% | ~$2.5B | 18% | 25x | ~$18 |
| Bull — quantum inflection, category leader | 20% | ~$6.0B | 25% | 30x | ~$51 |
| **Weighted** | | | | | **≈ $22** |

The bull case — generously weighted at 20% — is worth $51. The weighted intrinsic value of **~$22** sits **~33% below** the current $32.62. A 25–30% margin of safety implies paying **below ~$15–16**.

Note the sell-side: eight targets cluster $43–$80 (mean ~$55). Every one requires the bull case as base case.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment

**In favor:** founder-led (Niccolo de Masi, Chairman & CEO) with meaningful ownership — genuine skin in the game; exceptional velocity in product milestones (256-qubit system, DARPA Stage C, NVIDIA design win); and — importantly — **they have been disciplined issuers of expensive stock**, raising ~$2.7B in October 2026 at ~$69/share and ~$2B earlier in 2026 near the highs. Selling overvalued equity is accretive to continuing holders, and very few managements do it well.

**Against:** capital allocation looks imperial. Twelve acquisitions in two years, $4.4B of goodwill, and SBC of 138% of revenue means the per-share value of the franchise is being diluted almost as fast as revenue grows. GAAP results are dominated by warrant-derivative noise rather than operating discipline. Reporting "record quarter" alongside a 23% gross margin and a $1.29B operating loss is promotional. Verdict: **honest and capable operators, aggressive allocators, not yet proven stewards of per-share value.**

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL**

Not a Strong Sell — there is no solvency risk ($3.7B net cash), the technology is credible, and the backlog is real. But this fails every one of my criteria simultaneously:

- **Circle of competence:** the terminal value rests on a scientific-commercial inflection I cannot underwrite with confidence. That alone is disqualifying.
- **Moat:** none demonstrated; gross margin down 40 points and ROIC −16.5%.
- **Financial quality:** negative FCF of $689M on $380M of revenue; SBC above revenue.
- **Margin of safety:** the opposite — I estimate intrinsic value ~$22 against a $32.62 price. You are paying a ~50% premium to a *generous* probability-weighted value to own an option.

For a value portfolio, **sell / avoid**. The correct re-entry discipline is a price below ~$16 *and* evidence of durable gross margin above 40% with FCF approaching breakeven — not just revenue growth. Existing holders with cost basis far below should recognize they own a lottery ticket sized like a blue chip; position size, not conviction, should reflect that.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to my bearish view (i.e., what would make me wrong):**
1. **A genuine quantum advantage** shipped on the 256-qubit Superion roadmap, converting backlog into high-margin system sales and lifting gross margin back above 45%.
2. **Government anchor contracts** (DARPA QBI Stage C, up to $300M; AFRL; optical atomic clocks) scaling into a multi-billion program of record — this is the most plausible path to real revenue durability.
3. **Consolidation** as the only scaled, best-capitalized pure-play: IonQ becomes the acquisition vehicle for the sector, and the $3.7B cash is deployed at high returns rather than burned.
4. **Foundry leverage** if SkyWater wins third-party quantum manufacturing (Qolab, D-Wave) — a semiconductor-equipment-like business at 30%+ margins is worth far more than my base case.

**Risks to owning it (why I stay out):**
1. **Continued dilution** — 21%/yr share growth plus warrant overhangs (Series C struck at $69.46) silently erodes per-share value regardless of revenue.
2. **Burn acceleration** — $332M in a single quarter, and the foundry is capital intensive; the $3.7B cash pile is not infinite at this trajectory.
3. **Competitive obsolescence** — IBM's superconducting roadmap or Google/Microsoft breakthroughs could render trapped-ion architectures a niche, stranding $4.4B of goodwill.
4. **Multiple compression** — beta 3.3; the stock already fell ~60% from its ~$82 peak. At 20x EV/sales there is no valuation floor.
5. **Governance/promotional risk** — reported results dominated by non-cash warrant marks make it hard to track true operating progress quarter to quarter.

---

**Bottom line:** A genuinely important technology company and a poor value investment at $32.62. Great business or not, at 20x EV/sales with negative gross-margin trajectory, negative ROIC, and no demonstrable moat, there is no margin of safety — and a business that trades at a fair price is not a buy, let alone one trading at a large premium to an optimistic appraisal. **Sell / avoid.**
