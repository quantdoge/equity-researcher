## NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — TYSON FOODS, INC. (TSN)
*Prepared as of 16 Nov 2026, following the FY2026 Q4 print. Market cap ~$22.5bn; CEO Jeffrey K. Schomburger (effective 4 Oct 2026).*

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

The board is nominally majority-independent but functionally controlled, and the controlling family's presence has repeatedly intersected with disclosure and personal-conduct problems.

**Evidence:**
- **Voting control vastly disproportionate to economic stake.** Tyson Limited Partnership controls "nearly 100% of Class B stock, giving it 71.7% of the vote" (2026 annual meeting record). The family's economic interest is a low-single-digit percentage. Minority holders cannot meaningfully influence the board, a takeover, or compensation — this is the single most important fact in TSN's governance profile and it structurally caps the rating.
- **Chairman concentration and tenure.** John H. Tyson — board member since **1984**, Chairman since **1998**, and former CEO (2000–2006). He holds a **personal employment agreement as Chair** (a new three-year agreement was disclosed), meaning the board's leader is a compensated executive rather than an independent steward. He received 103,752 and 51,876 option awards in July 2026 alone.
- **Family board bloc.** At least four Tyson family members sit as directors (John H., Barbara, Olivia L., and John R. Tyson) of fifteen nominees — a family bloc larger than any single institutional holder.
- **Directorship held by an executive removed for cause-type conduct.** John R. Tyson was arrested for alcohol-related offences twice (2022; again June 2024), was **suspended as CFO** in June 2024, and was replaced by Curt Calaway. He nonetheless **remains a director and, since 2026, chairs the Strategy and Acquisitions Committee** — the committee overseeing M&A. A director with unresolved personal-conduct issues chairing capital-allocation oversight is a clear independence failure.
- **Legacy disclosure culture.** In 2005 the SEC sued Tyson Foods and then-Chairman Don Tyson over **misleading perquisite disclosures** — the same "family benefits / inadequate disclosure" failure mode that recurs as a theme in the current probe (see §2).

**Mitigants:** CEO/Chair roles are split; a planned CEO succession (King → Schomburger, announced May 2026, effective Oct 2026) rather than a forced exit; all 15 nominees elected in Feb 2026 with say-on-pay passing; a shareholder proposal drew only 3% support, consistent with a controlled company.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **ELEVATED**

- **Securities-disclosure investigations (highest-priority item).** On **3 Sept 2026** Tyson cut FY2026 revenue-growth guidance to 1.5–2.0% from 2.5–3.5% and trimmed beef projections. The stock fell **7.3% on 3.5x volume** ($55.81 → $51.76). At least **five firms are investigating** — Levi & Korsinsky, Schall Brown & Schwartz, Rosen, ZLK, and SBS Law — explicitly citing the sequence that guidance was reaffirmed in May 2026, a **$1bn senior notes offering** was issued, and then guidance was cut **one month later**. That "capital raise then reversal" pattern is the classic fact pattern for a 10b-5 claim and, potentially, an SEC inquiry.
- **Gap to note:** EDGAR full-text search returned **no** SEC enforcement action or formal investigation against TSN. The risk is presently litigant-driven, not confirmed regulatory action. I could not retrieve 10-K risk factors (see §8), so any disclosed subpoena or inquiry is unverified.
- **Beef antitrust — largely resolving.** Cumulative settlements of roughly **$200m** across purchaser classes: **$82.5m** (direct purchasers; claims deadline 30 Nov 2026), **$55m** Tyson share of an **$87.5m** settlement with Cargill (final approval 27 May 2026), and **$47m** (commercial/indirect). This is de-risking, but opt-out and remaining-class exposure persists.
- **Chicken antitrust (legacy).** 2019 DOJ Antitrust Division **grand jury subpoena**; criminal broiler-chicken indictment episode. Tyson was not convicted, but the industry remains under prosecutorial scrutiny.
- **Labor/worker safety.** DOL found illegal child labor across 13 meatpacking facilities in eight states (2023), including at Tyson-linked plants via sanitation contractors; OSHA has levied fines (~$263k in a cited action); a prior criminal OSHA violation involved a **$500,000 fine and probation** following a worker death. Recurrence risk is structural, not episodic.
- No material pending product approvals (FDA/FCC/EU) — not a regulatory-approval story.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Beef segment is in structural distress with no fast fix.** Q4 beef operating loss widened to **$171m** on **–12.7% volume**; US cattle herd is at multi-decade lows and high beef prices are expected to persist. The turnaround now rests on a **restructured beef network**, an **Amarillo shift restart**, and a novel **Allied Beef rancher-partnership** sourcing arrangement — an unproven counterparty structure that introduces execution and supplier-relationship risk at the exact point of maximum fragility. FY2027 guidance already assumes a **3.5–5.5% sales decline**.
2. **Labor and immigration exposure across 133,000 employees.** Meatpacking is the most labor-exposed industry in the food chain: elevated injury rates, OSHA citations, recurring child-labor findings via *contractor* sanitation crews, tight rural labor markets, and an aggressive immigration-enforcement environment. A single plant-level enforcement action or ICE-related disruption can halt a line; the 2026 industry ransomware trend (+62% incidents) compounds this.
3. **Leadership and key-person churn at the worst moment.** A brand-new CEO (from a **Prepared Foods** background) inheriting a beef crisis; a **COO change in 2026** (Devin Cole → Wes Morris); a CFO replaced after the predecessor's suspension; and multiple chief-accounting-officer-level changes. Add a **$343m pretax goodwill impairment** ($0.96/sh) in FY2026 — evidence that prior acquisition capital was overpaid, which bears directly on management's capital-allocation credibility, alongside $395m of buybacks and a dividend raise to $0.52/quarter.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **MODERATE–HIGH**

- The franchise is exposed to the three narratives that most damage food companies: **child labor in the supply chain** (recurring since 2023, and litigated by shareholder advocates), **price-fixing/"greedflation"** (now substantiated by ~$200m of beef settlements rather than mere allegation), and **corporate-governance nepotism** (twice-arrested heir on the board chairing M&A).
- The securities-fraud investigation headlines add a **credibility** overlay that is harder to repair than a product issue: investors are now asking whether guidance was managed around a debt offering.
- Offsetting: the portfolio (Jimmy Dean, Hillshire Farm, Ball Park, Tyson) is heavily **B2B/private-label and retailer-intermediated**, so consumer boycott and social-media backlash risk is structurally lower than for branded DTC peers. Prepared Foods is performing (margins expanding to 12.6%, outperforming the category by 150bp), which limits the reputational blast radius to the beef/commodity side.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Justification: no confirmed fraud, no SEC enforcement action, planned succession, antitrust liabilities crystallizing and being paid, and the incoming CEO buying stock with his own money. Against that: **71.7% family voting control**, a compromised independent director chairing M&A oversight, a **live five-firm securities investigation** into disclosure timing around a $1bn bond deal, and a beef business losing money at scale. Not Critical — but this is a name where the non-financial layer, not the income statement, will determine the next leg of the downside.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Timing of the Sept 2026 guidance cut vs. the $1bn senior notes offering and May reaffirmation.** Escalate to compliance/legal before any position sizing. Monitor for an SEC formal order or a consolidated class action filing (typically within 60 days of the first firm notice — i.e., late Oct/Nov 2026).
2. **71.7% voting control by Tyson Limited Partnership combined with a compensated Executive Chairman and a family bloc on the board.** Activism, strategic alternatives, or board refresh are effectively unavailable to minority holders. ESG/governance screens may force institutional selling.
3. **John R. Tyson as Chair of the Strategy and Acquisitions Committee** despite two alcohol-related arrests and removal as CFO. Direct conflict with board oversight credibility on capital allocation.
4. **$343m FY2026 goodwill impairment plus $395m buybacks** — capital-allocation discipline question for the incoming CEO.
5. **Recurring child-labor findings via sanitation contractors** — a single new DOL action, or a journalist-investigation cycle, would re-open the reputational wound.
