# TYSON FOODS (TSN) — Financial Risk Assessment
**Data as of FY2026 (period end 2026-10-03), price $63.95, market cap ~$22.5B**

---

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE (BBB), but thin-cushion**

Tyson is a low-beta, IG-rated protein processor with a genuine deleveraging track record: total debt has fallen from $9,787M (FY24) → $8,830M (FY25) → **$7,750M (FY26)**, a ~21% reduction in two years. Net debt/EBITDA of **2.80x** sits comfortably inside IG expectations and near management's stated 2.0x long-term policy. Fitch affirmed **BBB/F2, Outlook Stable** in July 2026, citing FY25 EBITDA leverage of 2.4x.

The problem is not leverage — it is **earnings quality and the absence of an operating buffer**. Gross margin is **5.7%** and operating margin **1.95%**. At that level, EBIT of $1,075M is a rounding error on $56.4B of revenue, and interest coverage of **2.65x** leaves very little room. This is a structurally cyclical commodity-spread business (cattle, hogs, feed, chicken) with a highly fixed cost base — the same dynamic that produced a **–$395M operating loss in FY2023**. Credit quality is therefore one bad cattle cycle away from pressure, not one bad quarter.

**Covenant read:** The revolver carries a springing first-lien net leverage covenant, reportedly not expected to be applicable at current levels — a meaningful mitigant. Specific covenant thresholds and the debt maturity ladder were **not available from my data sources (flagged gap)**; short-term debt of $848M is only 11% of total debt, implying limited near-term refinancing cliff, but this is inference, not verified schedule.

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue –20% (−$11,284M)

Base: EBIT $1,075M, EBITDA $2,473M, interest $406M, COGS 94.3% of revenue.

| Variable-cost assumption | EBIT | EBITDA | Net debt/EBITDA | Interest coverage | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 85% variable | **–$618M** | $780M | **8.9x** | Negative | Severe breach |
| 90% variable | **–$53M** | $1,345M | **5.2x** | ~0x | Covenant breach |
| 95% variable (generous) | +$511M | $1,909M | **3.6x** | 1.3x | At/below covenant edge |

**Conclusion: a 20% revenue decline breaches leverage covenants under every realistic cost-flex assumption.** Because fixed cost absorption is brutal in protein processing, there is no variable-cost ratio at which TSN escapes. Supportive evidence: FY2023 delivered exactly this outcome — operating income of **–$395M** and a net loss of **–$648M** on essentially flat revenue, from spread compression alone.

**Liquidity through the stress:** cash of $824M plus a springing revolver. OCF would fall from $2,433M toward ~$500–750M, against maintenance capex of ~$770M and a $704M dividend. FCF turns **negative**; the dividend would be cut or suspended, and the $414M buyback eliminated. Cash runway on the dividend alone is roughly **one year**. Solvency is not at risk (assets/liabilities 2.01x), but the equity story and the buyback are.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE** (upper end of the band)

Solvency is not the issue — leverage is falling, the rating is IG, liquidity is adequate, and FCF generation is real at 7.35% yield. What keeps this from "Low" is the **combination of a 1.95% operating margin with 2.65x interest coverage**: TSN has almost no capacity to absorb revenue or spread shock before covenants and the dividend come under pressure, and its own FY2023 results demonstrate the mechanism concretely.

---

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Interest coverage of 2.65x is the single biggest risk.** Deteriorating for two straight years (3.27x → 2.71x → 2.65x). For a BBB credit this is well below the 8x+ typically associated with the rating band, and it is low because of margin, not debt.
2. **Gross margin compression despite revenue growth** — 6.80% → 6.54% → 5.72%. Revenue up 5.8% over two years while gross profit fell 10.9%. Pricing is not covering input costs.
3. **Quick ratio of 0.60 and cash down 52% in two years** ($1,717M → $824M). Liquidity relies on perishable inventory ($5,578M) that cannot be liquidated at book in a demand shock.
4. **Goodwill and intangibles of $14,890M = 42% of total assets and ~85% of true book equity.** Tangible net worth is only ~$2,724M against $7,750M of debt — debt/tangible equity ≈ 2.85x. Impairment risk is live if beef/prepared foods forecasts disappoint.
5. **FCF improvement is partly capex starvation** — capex cut 60% from FY23 ($1,939M → $770M). Under-investment flatters near-term FCF and raises future cost risk.
6. **Dividend exceeds earnings** (155% of net income) and buybacks were $414M against $453M of net income — shareholder returns exceed earnings and are being funded by balance sheet capacity while leverage is only just below target.

Segment splits would materially improve the downside analysis — the Beef segment's spread exposure is the likely transmission channel for the stress case above.
- **Moody's:** search surfaced a headline referencing an outlook move to negative tied to an updated fiscal 2026 outlook, but the snippet was ambiguous and could not be verified against the full release. Treat as **unconfirmed**; Fitch's Stable BBB/F2 affirmation (July 2026) is the firmer data point.

**Bottom line for the memo:** TSN is a solvent, IG-rated, low-beta operator with a deleveraging story intact — but the credit and equity both hinge on a 1.95% operating margin. Own it for the FCF yield and balance-sheet repair, not for resilience. The correct risk framing is not "can they survive a downturn" (yes) but "how much of the equity and the buyback survives a repeat of FY2023" (most of the equity, none of the buyback).
