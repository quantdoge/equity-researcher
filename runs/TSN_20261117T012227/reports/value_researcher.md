# Tyson Foods, Inc. (NYSE: TSN) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price: $63.95 | Market cap: $22.5B | EV: ~$29.4B | Dividend yield: 3.25%**

*Note on data quality: the balance sheet feed shows FY2026 total equity of $35,438M against assets of $35,106M and liabilities of $17,492M — internally inconsistent. Implied equity is **~$17.6B** (assets − liabilities), which reconciles with FY2025's $18.2B. I use $17.6B throughout; the vendor's reported 0.64x P/B and 1.28% ROE are artifacts of the bad equity figure. Corrected: P/B ≈ 1.28x, ROE ≈ 2.6%. Separately, the DCF tool mis-scaled WACC by 100x, so all intrinsic value work below is my own.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **None** (Narrow only within Prepared Foods)

Tyson is the largest U.S. protein company: ~$56B of sales across Beef (38%), Chicken (31%), Prepared Foods (19%), Pork (11%), and International (4%). The economics of those pieces are radically different, and the aggregate is a spread business, not a franchise.

| Segment (FY2026, adj.) | Sales | Adj. Op. Income | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beef | $21,277M | **$(654)M** | (3.0)% |
| Chicken | $17,221M | $1,859M | 10.7% |
| Prepared Foods | $10,298M | $1,332M | 12.9% |
| Pork | $6,216M | $228M | 3.6% |
| International | $2,379M | $180M | 7.6% |
| Corporate + amortization | — | $(968)M | — |
| **Total** | **$55,370M** | **$1,977M** | **3.5%** |

**Moat evidence:**
- **No pricing power where it counts.** Beef and Pork (~49% of sales) are pass-through commodity conversion. The company loses money when cattle are scarce and makes windfalls when they are plentiful — the opposite of a moat. Beef has now lost money three consecutive years ($275M, $654M, and a $(1,215)M GAAP loss in FY26).
- **Gross margin of 5.7%** is the tell. A business with durable intangible assets or switching costs does not earn 5–6% gross margins. Gross margin was 13.9% in FY2021 and 12.5% in FY2022 — that was a cattle-cycle windfall, not a widening moat.
- **ROIC below WACC through the cycle.** FY26: 2.6%. FY25: 4.1%. FY24: 5.0%. FY23: (1.4)%. Even the record FY2022 (~13–14%) barely exceeded a 7–8% WACC by a wide margin for one year only. The hallmark of a moated business — *consistently* ROIC > WACC — is absent.
- **The one genuine asset: Prepared Foods.** Jimmy Dean, Hillshire Farm, Ball Park, Wright and Tyson brands in frozen/refrigerated prepared foods generate 12.9% margins, 45% of segment profit on 19% of sales, and grew operating income in FY26 despite flat volumes. That is a real, if narrow, brand moat — shelf position, category leadership, and household penetration. But private-label pressure and 0.1% volume growth in FY26 show it is not a pricing fortress.
- **Scale is a cost advantage, but a competed-away one.** Tyson is #1 in chicken and a top-tier beef packer; scale helps, but all four major beef packers have the same scale, so the benefit accrues to consumers and cattle producers, not shareholders.

**Moat direction over 5 years: narrowed.** Beef and chicken returns have compressed structurally; the branded portfolio has grown but not enough to lift consolidated returns.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **4 / 10**

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 53.3 | 52.9 | 53.3 | 54.4 | 56.4 |
| Gross margin | 12.5% | 5.0% | 6.8% | 6.5% | 5.7% |
| Operating margin | 8.3% | (0.8)% | 2.6% | 2.0% | 2.0% |
| GAAP net income ($M) | 3,238 | (648) | 800 | 474 | 453 |
| Adj. EPS | ~$8.92 | ~$(0.32) | ~$2.60 | $4.12 | $3.44 |
| Free cash flow ($M) | 800 | (187) | 1,458 | 1,177 | 1,663 |
| Owner earnings ($M) | n/a | (1,248) | 1,068 | 857 | 1,081 |
| ROIC | ~13% | (1.4)% | 5.0% | 4.1% | 2.6% |

**Strengths:** Revenue is genuinely defensive and non-cyclical in *demand* (protein is a staple; +4.6% five-year CAGR). Cash generation is real: $1.66B FCF in FY26, up $486M. Debt was reduced $1.08B. Liquidity $4.1B. Interest coverage on adjusted operating income is a comfortable 4.9x.

**Weaknesses — and they are serious:**
- **Earnings are cyclical and currently depressed.** GAAP net income of $453M on $56B of sales is a 0.8% net margin.
- **A chronic GAAP-to-"adjusted" gap.** FY26 adjusted EPS $3.44 vs GAAP $1.28 — a $2.16/share (~$760M) spread. Adjustments included legal contingency accruals booked as *reductions to sales* ($358M in FY26, $698M in FY25) and restructuring gains. When "one-time" items recur every single year, they are operating costs. I weight GAAP, and GAAP says this business earned a 2.6% ROE.
- **Capex is running below depreciation** ($770M vs $1,398M in FY26). That flatters FCF. Normalized maintenance capex is closer to D&A, which is why owner earnings ($1.08B) are a third below reported FCF.
- **The balance sheet is hollow.** Goodwill and intangibles of $14.9B against $17.6B of equity leaves **tangible book value of roughly $2.7B, or ~$7.70/share.** The company is essentially its acquisition history. With Beef earning negative returns, an impairment charge is a live risk.
- **Net debt $6.9B** = 2.8x depressed EBITDA, ~1.8x mid-cycle EBITDA. Manageable but leaves no room for another bad cycle without pressuring the dividend.
- **Dividend coverage is thin on a GAAP basis:** $704M paid vs $453M GAAP net income (155% payout); 60% of adjusted EPS. The just-announced raise to $2.08 annual (+2%) signals confidence but also that growth in the payout has stalled to a token increase.

**Score rationale:** Defensive demand and solid cash conversion earn points; 5.7% gross margins, sub-WACC returns, $15B of goodwill, and a persistent adjusted-earnings gap cap it at 4.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples are misleading because earnings are trough-level:**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E (GAAP FY26, $1.28) | 50.0x |
| P/E (adjusted FY26, $3.44) | 18.6x |
| P/E (FY27 guide midpoint ~$3.40–3.60) | ~18x |
| EV/EBITDA (trailing $2.47B) | 11.9x |
| EV/EBITDA (mid-cycle ~$3.8B) | ~7.7x |
| P/FCF (trailing $1.66B) | 13.6x |
| Owner earnings yield ($1.08B) | 4.8% |
| P/B (corrected) | 1.28x |
| **P/Tangible book** | **~8.3x** |

**Intrinsic value (my own work):**

*Normalized earnings power.* Mid-cycle adjusted operating income of ~$2.4B (Beef at breakeven, Chicken $1.7–1.9B, Prepared Foods $1.3B, Pork $200M, International $180M, less $750M corporate and $216M amortization), less $400M interest, taxed at 24%, plus after-tax intangible amortization → **normalized earnings power of ~$1.6–1.7B, or ~$4.50–4.80/share.** At a 7.5–8% cost of capital, that capitalizes to an enterprise value of ~$24–26B, or **~$49–55/share.**

*Owner-earnings DCF.* Base owner earnings of $1.5B, 3% growth for 10 years, 8% WACC, 2.5% terminal growth → **$62/share.** At a 9% WACC (appropriate for a commodity processor with 6% gross margins) → **$50/share.**

**Intrinsic value range: $50–62; base case ~$56.**

At $63.95 the stock trades at a **~14% premium to base-case intrinsic value** and at the very top of the range. There is no margin of safety — indeed, negative margin of safety. Note the market has already done the work: the shares are up 26% from the ~$50.60 October low, sit near the 52-week high of $68.75, and the 8.7% post-earnings pop on November 16 capitalized the *entire* beef recovery in one session.

**What the price implies:** The market is paying for Beef swinging from $(654)M to guidance of $(300)M to +$100M, *plus* a full chicken recovery, *plus* permanent Prepared Foods growth. FY2027 guidance actually calls for **sales down 3.5–5.5% and adjusted operating income of $1.7–2.15B — a midpoint of $1.93B, below FY2026's $1.98B.** Chicken profit is guided lower. The bull case requires two cycles to turn favorably at once.

**Reality check on the long-term record:** $1,000 invested five years ago is worth ~$749 today. Shareholders have been paid a dividend but have lost capital in a business that has not earned its cost of capital.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Grade: C+**

**Positives:**
- **Genuine insider alignment.** The Tyson family holds roughly 70%+ of the super-voting Class B shares (10 votes each) and the family name is on the building. Management thinks in decades.
- **Dividend discipline:** 13+ consecutive years of increases, now $2.08 annualized, sustained through the FY2023 loss. The company did not cut it in the worst year of the cattle cycle.
- **FY26 capital allocation was rational:** $1.08B of debt reduction, 7.8M shares repurchased for $427M at an average of ~$54.75 — a price at or below my estimate of intrinsic value. That is value-accretive.
- **Portfolio construction worked.** Hillshire ($7.7B, 2014), AdvancePierre ($3.2B, 2017) and Keystone ($2.2B, 2018) built Prepared Foods, now the highest-returning segment and 45% of segment profit. That is not empire building; that is a coherent strategic pivot into branded, value-added protein.

**Negatives:**
- **Governance is structurally poor for outside shareholders.** Dual-class control means minority holders have essentially no voice. This is a controlled company in practice.
- **Past buybacks destroyed value.** $702M repurchased in FY2022 at an average above $85/share — roughly 50% above today's price, in a cyclical business at peak earnings. Buying back stock at peak-cycle multiples is the classic capital-allocation error.
- **Leadership churn.** Jeff Schomburger is a recent CEO transition (from Donnie King), with CFO turnover as well. Strategy continuity — "brands, innovation, operating capabilities" — is sound rhetoric, but three guidance cuts in FY2026 alone suggest forecasting and operational control have been weak.
- **The adjusted-earnings culture.** A persistent $2/share gap between GAAP and "adjusted" results, with legal settlements and restructuring routinely excluded, reflects a management team that prefers to frame the business favorably.

**Verdict on management:** Honest stewards of a hard business, with real strategic vision in Prepared Foods and real discipline on the dividend — but with a demonstrated tendency to misjudge cycle timing in both capital spending and buybacks.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (leaning Sell at current price)

Tyson is a decent, essential business with a genuinely good branded segment bolted onto a commodity spread operation that has not earned its cost of capital over a full cycle. At $63.95 — 50x GAAP earnings, ~18x adjusted earnings, 12x trough EBITDA, and a ~14% premium to my base-case intrinsic value of ~$56 — **there is no margin of safety.** The beef recovery and the dividend are both already in the price, while FY2027 guidance explicitly points to *lower* sales and *lower* adjusted operating income than FY2026.

Per the discipline that "a business that trades at a fair price is not a buy," this is not a purchase. I would not initiate a position.

- **For existing holders:** Hold. The 3.25% dividend is covered by cash flow, the balance sheet is investment-grade, and the beef cycle is genuinely inflecting (guidance of $(300)M to +$100M vs $(654)M, with herd rebuilding underway and plant closures reducing the cattle requirement). Selling into the first sign of recovery forfeits the cyclical upside. But do not add.
- **For new money:** Wait. A 25–30% discount to base-case intrinsic value implies a purchase price of **$40–45/share** — roughly 10–12x normalized earnings and ~5% FCF yield, where the dividend alone compensates for the cyclicality. At $40–45, the tangible-asset and cash-flow support becomes a real floor.
- **Upgrade trigger:** Beef returning to sustained profitability *while* chicken margins hold above 10%, lifting ROIC durably above 10%. **Downgrade trigger:** a goodwill impairment in Beef/Pork, or a dividend cut.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to the downside (why this is not a buy):**
1. **The cattle cycle does not cooperate.** Herds are at a 75-year low after years of drought. Rebuilding takes 2–3 years from the breeding decision. Guidance of a beef profit in FY2027 assumes imports and plant closures bridge the gap; if cattle costs stay elevated, the loss persists and my $56 estimate falls toward $45.
2. **Chicken is deteriorating into the beef recovery.** More cattle supply means more beef on the market, which pressures chicken pricing. FY26 chicken operating income fell to $1.86B from $1.89B, Q4 fell 31%, and management guides FY27 chicken *lower*. The diversification that is supposed to smooth the cycle is failing precisely when needed.
3. **Goodwill impairment.** $14.9B of goodwill and intangibles against $17.6B of equity. Beef and Pork are earning negative or sub-cost-of-capital returns. A write-down would be non-cash but would confirm that the acquisition prices were never earned back.
4. **The GAAP/adjusted gap.** If legal contingencies and restructuring are genuinely recurring, true earning power is closer to $1.30–2.00/share than $3.44, and the stock is far more expensive than 18x.
5. **Leverage in a downturn.** $6.9B net debt with 2.7x interest coverage on GAAP operating income. Another FY2023-style loss would strain both the dividend and the buyback.
6. **Volume erosion.** Beef volumes fell 12.2% in FY26 on tight supply and record retail prices. Persistent consumer resistance to high beef prices could structurally shrink the largest segment.
7. **Governance.** Dual-class control means outside shareholders cannot force capital-allocation discipline if the family's interests diverge.

**Risks to the upside (why I would not short it):**
1. Beef guidance of up to +$100M may prove conservative; a fast herd rebuild plus import relief could deliver $300–500M, worth ~$1.00+/share of EPS.
2. Prepared Foods is a genuine compounder — $1.25–1.40B guided for FY27 — and deserves a higher multiple than the commodity segments.
3. At 1.28x book and 3.25% yield, a re-rating on any earnings recovery is powerful operating leverage: each $400M of segment profit improvement is ~$0.85 of EPS.
4. A strategic buyer or activist could force a separation of Prepared Foods from the commodity operations — a sum-of-the-parts move worth meaningfully more than $64.

---

**Bottom line:** A narrow-moat-at-best, family-controlled commodity protein processor with a genuinely good branded franchise inside it, currently priced as though the cycle has already turned. Fundamentals are stabilizing, the dividend is intact, and the balance sheet is sound — but at 50x GAAP earnings and a premium to intrinsic value, the patient investor's correct posture is to **wait for the price to come to them.** Fair value ~$56; buy price ~$40–45.
