## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**Red Flag #1 — Operating cash flow per share has gone *nowhere* through the greatest gold bull market in history.** This is the entire thesis in one line:

| | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | 9M-26 |

Gold went from ~$1,800 (2021 avg) to ~$3,400 (2025 avg), **+89%**. OCF per share went from $1.13 to $1.11, **-1.7%**. Revenue per share: **$3.80 (2021) → $3.43 (2026E), -10%** against a doubling in the commodity. Shareholders have captured *none* of the gold bull market.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

FY2025 is the tell: EBITDA **$842.7M**, less capex **$704.5M** (84% of EBITDA), less interest **$182.4M** (22%) = **105% of EBITDA consumed**. FCF: **-$6.7M**. "Cheap on EBITDA" is a category error for this asset base. 9M-26 FCF is finally positive at **+$69.3M** — a **0.8% annualized FCF yield** at a record $4,256/oz realized price.

**Red Flag #3 — 310% dilution in five years.** 285M shares (2021) → 630M (2025) → **1,167.5M** (Aug-26, post-Orla), **1,247.5M fully diluted**. The July 31, 2026 Orla merger alone issued **378.1M shares**. Plus **31.5M** shares of in-the-money convertible overhang ($172.5M notes at $6.30 vs $10.39 spot).

**Red Flag #4 — Margin compression on record revenue.** Q3-26 revenue hit a record $1.03B (+27% YoY), yet net income collapsed to **$67.5M from $310.1M in Q1-26 (-78%)**. Gross margin: 50.9% (Q1-26) → 39.2% (Q2-26) → **33.7% (Q3-26)**. Cost of revenue rose to 66.3% of sales. *Fair caveat:* part of this is Orla purchase-accounting (inventory step-up flowing through COGS) and should reverse. But the direction is adverse, and the market has noticed — the stock is **-45% from its $18.88 February peak**.

**Red Flag #5 — Headline EPS is not a reliable number.** Q2-26: reported EPS **$0.29** vs **adjusted $0.16** — **45% of earnings were non-operating** (Versamet share sale, $92M; Brazil disposal). I also found unreconciled gaps between income-statement net income and cash-flow-statement net income: Q1-26 **$310.1M vs $184.6M** ($125.5M gap); Q4-25 **$192.4M vs -$52.6M**. Separately, **Q4-25 data is internally corrupt** (revenue $136.7M, gross profit $3.5M, SG&A **-$61.9M**, operating income $137.6M > gross profit) — this is a data-feed artifact I flag rather than rely on.

**Red Flag #6 — FY2024 depreciation looks understated.** D&A was **$222.6M in FY24 (≈4% of PP&E)** versus **$526.6M in FY25 (≈6.7%)**. A $5.5B PP&E base should depreciate $400-500M/yr. At FY25's rate, FY24 pre-tax income would fall ~$150M and diluted EPS would be **~$0.45 rather than $0.68 (-35%)**. This mechanically explains why FY24 net income ($339M) *exceeded* FY25 ($225M) on 22% lower revenue. Some of the step-up is legitimately new Greenstone assets — but this deserves scrutiny, and it means the 2024 earnings base that 2026 is being measured against is soft.

## 3. Beneish & Accruals Detail

M-Score **-2.643** is comfortably clean, driven by DSRI 0.764 (receivables shrinking vs sales — the opposite of channel stuffing) and GMI 0.803 (gross margin *improved*). But two components are unreliable and should not be taken at face value: **SGAI of 0.258** is an artifact of the -$61.9M Q4-25 SG&A line, and **DEPI of 0.615** (depreciation rate halving) reflects M&A-distorted asset lives rather than a genuine accounting change. **TATA of -0.045** is genuinely reassuring — total accruals to assets are negative, so earnings are not being manufactured on the balance sheet. The problem is capital intensity, not accrual gamesmanship.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment — **No Moat, High-Cost Position**

A gold miner has no moat by construction: it is a price-taker with no customer concentration, no switching costs, and no technology advantage. The only durable advantages are ore grade and jurisdiction — and EQX is structurally disadvantaged on both:

- **AISC of $2,175/oz (Q2-26)** is materially above the senior peer group (Newmont and Agnico Eagle typically run $1,400-1,650/oz). EQX is a **high-cost producer**. That is fine at $4,256 gold and catastrophic at $2,800.
- **Operating leverage cuts both ways.** At $4,256 realized, margin is $2,081/oz. At $3,600, margin falls to **$1,425/oz (-32%)**. At $2,800, margin is $625/oz **(-70%)** and the company is near break-even after capex and interest. With a **beta of 2.49** — the highest in the gold complex — this is a levered gold-price proxy, not a compounding business.
- **Jurisdictional retreat is real progress** (Brazil sold Jan-26; Canada/US now dominant post-Orla/Musselwhite), but legacy exposure to Mexico (Los Filos — a mine with a history of community blockades, only now renegotiating 20-year land access) and Nicaragua remains.
- **The growth pipeline is the threat, not the opportunity:** path to 1.9Moz requires Valentine Phase 2 (**$436M**), Castle Mountain, South Railroad, Los Filos restart, Camino Rojo UG. Five years of history says this gets funded with shares. A 5% NCIB against a multi-billion-dollar build-out is a rounding error.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — Is There a Credible Bear Case?

**Yes, but it is a capital-allocation and commodity-cycle case, not a fraud case.**

*The bear case:* EQX is a serial diluter (share count +310% in five years) that has **never** generated cumulative free cash flow (-$735M, 2021-25) and has produced **flat-to-down operating cash flow per share through an 89% rise in the gold price**. It trades at ~10.4x 2026E EPS and ~8.4x EV/EBITDA on **peak-cycle, record-gold-price earnings**, with a 2.49 beta and a $2,175/oz cost base that leaves it marginal below ~$2,800 gold. Every dollar of "growth" has come from shareholders' pockets or lenders'. Consensus is thin and stale — 5 analysts for 2026, **1** for 2029-30 — and 2026E EPS of $0.996 requires Q4-26 EPS of ~$0.30 against actual Q3-26 of **$0.064**. The 0.87% dividend yield and a token buyback will be swamped by 31.5M convertible shares plus the next growth capex cycle.

*The other side — and it is strong:* the transformation is real. In 18 months management took a $1.4B net-debt balance sheet to **$214M net cash**, moved the asset base into Canada/US, doubled scale to ~1.1Moz pro forma, and started returning capital. At $4,256/oz the business throws off **~$1.5B+ adjusted EBITDA** (Q2-26 adj. EBITDA $358M/quarter). If gold simply holds, 2027E EPS of $1.38 puts the stock at **7.5x**, and FCF inflects hard as growth capex rolls off and purchase-accounting noise reverses. Shorting a beta-2.49 gold miner into a gold bull market is how shorts die — this stock ran from $5.40 to $18.88 in seventeen months.

*What the forensic work actually says:* the accounting is **clean**. Beneish -2.64, negative accruals, receivables down, inventory days falling, no related-party or auditor red flags. There is no Hindenburg angle here. The bear case is a legitimate but *well-understood* complaint about dilution and capex — and it is already 45% reflected in the price.
