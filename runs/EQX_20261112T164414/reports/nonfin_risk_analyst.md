## 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE

**Positives**
- **Chair/CEO separation maintained.** Charles "Chuck" Jeannes (former Goldcorp CEO, former Orla Chair) became independent Chair on July 31, 2026. **Ross Beaty stepped down as Chairman** — a genuine de-concentration of founder control and a structural improvement versus EQX's historical configuration.
- **Board reconstituted to 11 directors** at Orla close; independent proxy advisors recommended in favour of the Orla share-issuance resolution, which passed with **99.83% shareholder support** — no activist dissent, no proxy fight.
- **Capital returns are real, not cosmetic:** quarterly dividend ($0.0225), NCIB buybacks of $20.7M YTD, and ~$1.2B of debt repaid YTD with a **net cash position of $163M**. Alignment between stated discipline and capital allocation is credible.
- **No dual-class share structure** identified; no related-party transaction abuse disclosed.

**Concerns**
- **Leadership was effectively transferred to the acquired company.** Post-Orla, the President/CEO (Jason Simpson), COO (Andrew Cormier) and Chief Capital Markets Officer (Etienne Morin) are all ex-Orla; the Chair is ex-Orla Chair. Equinox was the nominal acquirer, but the operating brain trust is Orla's. This is functionally a reverse takeover of management and raises culture-integration and accountability questions about who owns the post-merger plan.
- **Simultaneous CEO and Chair turnover.** CEO Darren Hall retired effective **October 31, 2026**, the same quarter the Chair changed. Both transitions were orderly and pre-announced, but the combined effect is that the two most senior accountability seats turned over during the riskiest phase of integration.
- **Three management regimes in 13 months.** Legacy Equinox, Calibre (merged June 2025) and Orla (merged July 2026). Redundancy risk is visible: Daniella Dimitrov, ex-Calibre CFO, was moved to "Chief Corporate Development, Sustainability & Risk Officer" while legacy-Equinox Peter Hardie retained CFO. Bundling **sustainability and risk under corporate development** is a structurally conflicted reporting line — risk oversight should not sit beneath the growth function.
- **Compensation structure not verifiable** from retrieved filings; cannot confirm the buyback/dividend versus growth-capex weighting in executive incentives. Gap.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Integration execution across three merged companies.** Calibre (June 2025) and Orla (July 2026) closed 13 months apart, tripling asset and jurisdictional complexity while both the CEO and Chair seats turned over. Q3 2026 already showed the strain — output of 237,019 oz landed at the **low end of guidance**, with **Greenstone processing at just 1.03 g/t** and Valentine still ramping (1.82 g/t). Management now needs its **best quarter ever (~258.5 koz in Q4)** to hit guidance. Miss probability is material.
- **Los Filos is a stranded, unresolved asset.** >18 months of suspension, zero ounces in guidance, and a restart that depends on community agreements that have already failed once (the prior agreement was allowed to expire, triggering the blockade and indefinite shutdown). This is not a "ramp-up" problem; it is a social-licence problem with a repeat-failure history.
- **Geotechnical and technical failure at Mesquite.** Pit instability drove the Q3 miss at a mine processing **0.43 g/t** — extremely low grade, meaning thin margin for any further geotechnical disruption. Combined with single-asset JV concentration (Greenstone is a 60/40 JV with Orion, introducing partner-consent friction), physical and technical risk is elevated relative to peers.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: ELEVATED

EQX carries a **documented, organised, multi-front NGO campaign** — this is not generic ESG noise:

- **Los Filos / Mexico:** Earthworks, MiningWatch Canada, EKO (active petition: "Equinox Gold: Return the land!"), and the Business & Human Rights Resource Centre have all published on the mine. Mongabay (2025) reported Equinox **failed to implement a closure process** after overstaying its communal land agreement — a damaging specific allegation, not a vague complaint.
- **Brazil legacy:** The 2021 Maranhão tailings dam breach left ~4,000 residents without potable water; Equinox and subsidiary MASA **denied responsibility**, which is itself a reputational liability. Earthworks published "Equinox Gold Hides the Ugly Truth from Investors." The January 2026 Brazil sale removes the operating asset but not the reputational and potential legal residue.
- **Mitigants:** A dedicated Sustainability & ESG executive function exists, the company publishes tailings management disclosure aligned with GISTM-style practice, and there have been no product recalls, consumer-facing controversies, or disclosed cyber incidents.

**Net:** Brand risk is **concentrated in Mexican community relations** and is likely to re-escalate if the Los Filos restart is mishandled. For a company whose equity story is now "premier North American jurisdiction producer," residual Mexican and Nicaraguan exposure is a narrative vulnerability.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: strong balance sheet (net cash $163M) and genuine insider buying are real offsets that keep this out of "Critical." But the rating is driven by four compounding factors that are *simultaneous rather than sequential*: (i) ~33% of production in a sanctions-exposed jurisdiction the company itself flags; (ii) an unresolved, 18-month-suspended mine with a failed social licence; (iii) two mergers and a full CEO/Chair turnover inside 13 months; and (iv) a Q4 that must be a record quarter to hold guidance. The financial buffer buys time — it does not buy execution.

---

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Nicaragua sanctions/asset-seizure exposure (~33% of production).** Escalate to portfolio risk. There is no disclosure of a divestiture path or sanctions-mitigation structure. This is a binary, unhedgeable jurisdictional risk sitting inside a $12B market cap.
2. **Los Filos repeat-failure risk.** The prior land agreement expired once already. Monitor restart milestones; any new blockade should be treated as a permanent impairment signal, not a temporary disruption.
3. **Q4 2026 guidance credibility.** A record ~258.5 koz quarter is required. A miss would validate the "serial over-promiser" critique that already exists in sell-side coverage and would materially raise management-credibility risk.
4. **Risk function reporting line.** Combining Sustainability, Corporate Development and Risk under one officer who reports into the growth agenda is a genuine control weakness. Recommend engagement on whether risk has independent board-committee access.
5. **Brazil sale indemnity overhang.** Confirm what representations and indemnities Equinox gave on the Aurizona/RDM tailings liabilities — an unquantified contingent liability was transferred, not extinguished.

**Explicit data gaps:** No 10-K/40-F retrievable (FPI status), no EDGAR Form 4 detail (SEDI-only), and executive compensation structure unverified. Insider figures carry medium confidence; the direction (net buying) is well-corroborated across three independent sources and is reliable.
