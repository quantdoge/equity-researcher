## EQX — Equinox Gold Corp.: Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Data as of most recent disclosures available (Q3 2026 results, released 9 Nov 2026). EQX is a Canadian-domiciled foreign private issuer (40-F/6-K filer, not 10-K — no 10-K exists on EDGAR; note this as a disclosure gap vs. US peers).*

---

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

Portfolio has been **materially reshaped in the last 18 months**: Brazil sold (closed 23 Jan 2026), Nevada assets (Pan/Gold Rock/Illipah) sold Oct 2025, Calibre merged in (June 2025: Nicaragua, Valentine), **Orla Mining merged in 31 Jul 2026** ($3.57bn, 378.1m shares — adds Camino Rojo (Mexico), South Railroad (Nevada), Musselwhite (Ontario)). Q3 2026 revenue $1,047.0m; 9M26 revenue $2,678.4m (source: EQX 6-K, 9 Nov 2026).

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**#1 — Nicaragua sanctions escalation (Probability: Medium-High, ~40% over 18 months / Impact: High)**
The US has built a layered, escalating sanctions architecture aimed specifically at Nicaragua's gold sector: ENIMINAS designated (Jun 2022), the **General Directorate of Mines (DGM)** designated (Oct 2022) alongside amendments to **EO 13851** granting broad authority over "new investment in any identified sectors of the Nicaraguan economy," COMINTSA and Capital Mining designated (May 2024), and a **further tranche on 16 Apr 2026** designating regime-linked gold firms (EMSA, Minero, Thomas Metal, Xinxin, Brother Metal) plus Ortega/Murillo family members — triggered in part by the **2025 forcible seizure of a US-owned gold processing site** (Treasury press release sb0451; Reuters, 16 Apr 2026; Federal Register 2026-07699).
- EQX **explicitly names "Canadian and United States sanctions on Nicaraguan operations"** as a risk factor in its own disclosures (equinoxgold.com/cautionary-notes and repeated in 2025–26 investor decks).
- Transmission channels: (i) designation of a JV/offtake/logistics counterparty or of DGM-linked permitting officials; (ii) banking/refining de-risking (LBMA refiners refusing Nicaraguan doré); (iii) Canadian SEMA mirror-sanctions. Any of these could force a rushed, value-destructive exit. Note precedent in the same portfolio: EQX has now sold Brazil and Nevada in 12 months — management has demonstrated willingness to divest non-core, higher-risk ounces, which cuts both ways (option value vs. risk of a distressed sale).
- Mitigant: EQX's 2025 Conflict-Free Gold Standard report states risk-based sanctions monitoring with "no breaches identified."

**#2 — Mexico: permitting freeze, community/land access and security (Probability: High for delay, ~60% / Impact: Medium-High)**
- Los Filos was **indefinitely suspended in April 2025** after the land-access agreement with the Carrizalillo community expired and illegal blockades halted operations (EQX releases; Mexico Business News; BNAmericas). Los Filos produced only 33,013oz in 2025 vs. design capacity ~150–200koz.
- **25 June 2026: 20-year land access agreements signed with all three host communities** — a genuine de-escalation and the key catalyst for restart (EQX release; BNAmericas). Residual risk: three-community governance, historic pattern of recurring blockades (2020, 2021, 2025).
- National policy backdrop is adverse: Sheinbaum reaffirmed **no new mining concessions** (S&P Global, Jun 2025); an **open-pit mining ban bill** remains before Congress; and on **21 Jul 2026 SEMARNAT published a directive blocking new environmental permits for exploration/exploitation/processing in protected areas**. Los Filos is an open-pit + underground operation and Camino Rojo's underground project needs permits — tail risk to both growth plans.
- Guerrero remains one of Mexico's highest-security-risk states (extortion, illegal mining, cartel presence); Los Filos has previously reported illegal miners on concessions.

**#3 — Trade policy / gold-flow and resource-nationalism shock (Probability: Medium, ~30% / Impact: Medium)**
~60% of revenue is now Canada+US, exposing EQX to US tariff actions on Canadian and Mexican goods and to any US policy affecting gold imports/doré flows. Separately, both Mexico (concession review) and Nicaragua (state discretion over which companies exploit reserves via ENIMINAS/DGM) carry resource-nationalism optionality. A US-Canada trade rupture would hit EQX twice: cost inflation on Canadian mines and a re-rating of "safe jurisdiction" premium.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Brazil/CMOC mineral-rights transfer litigation (active).** A Brazilian court **halted the transfer of mineral rights** tied to the $1.015bn sale to China's CMOC (Reuters, 3 Mar 2026). Exposure: delay/impairment of the **up to $115.0m production-linked contingent consideration due 23 Jan 2027**, plus potential indemnity/escrow claims. Management still carries the receivable (6-K, 9 Nov 2026). This is the largest identifiable live legal item.
- **Los Filos community litigation/advocacy.** NGO campaigns (Earthworks, EKO "Return the land," MiningWatch) allege water/land damage and have pushed the company to file charges against protesters (earthworks.org). Historically the site has generated blockades, arbitration/negotiation cycles and OECD National Contact Point complaint risk. No quantified claim disclosed.
- **Nicaragua property-rights/rule-of-law.** The Treasury action of 16 Apr 2026 arose from the regime's **occupation and forcible seizure of a gold processing site** — direct evidence that asset security in Nicaragua is not underwritten by courts. EQX's own concession-based tenures are only as strong as regime goodwill.
- **US permitting.** Castle Mountain Phase 2 and South Railroad both accepted into the **FAST-41** federal permitting program (a positive, de-risking designation); South Railroad's Record of Decision was expected mid-2026 and a federal permit has been reported as received; Castle Mountain Phase 2 permitting targeted Dec 2026 (BLM scoping; CEQAnet). Residual risk: NGO litigation under NEPA/CEQA (California), which is the standard failure mode for California mining permits.
- **Canada (Valentine, NL).** The project carries a history of Indigenous consultation litigation (Innu Nation / Nunatsiavut) around its environmental assessment; the $436m Phase 2 expansion was approved in 2026 (Northern Miner). Ongoing duty-to-consult and benefit-agreement obligations, not a currently active injunction.
- **No active US securities class action, DOJ/SEC investigation, or FCPA matter identified** in searches. Company is a 40-F filer, so US-style 10-K risk-factor disclosure is thinner — a governance/disclosure transparency gap relative to US-listed gold peers.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Nicaragua = the file's only true compliance red flag.** Operating in a US-sanctioned gold sector with SDN designations on the state mining authority (DGM/ENIMINAS) creates potential secondary-sanctions and "facilitation" exposure for US persons (EQX is NYSE American-listed with a large US shareholder base), plus Canada SEMA exposure. Requires ongoing OFAC counsel sign-off, buyer/refiner KYC, and supply-chain tracing (LBMA Responsible Gold / Conflict-Free Gold).
2. **Doré sales channel concentration in Nicaragua** — counterparty designation risk could strand production with little notice; not currently disclosed as hedged.
3. **Guerrero security/illegal-mining environment** — third-party payments, security contractors, and extortion risk warrant FCPA/UK Bribery Act-adjacent controls; no evidence of a problem, but inherent to the asset.
4. **Brazil indemnities** — the court-blocked mineral-rights transfer creates open-ended post-closing liability while ~$115m remains receivable.
5. **Data gap:** EQX does not publish granular country-level revenue disclosure in a US-style 10-K; SEC EDGAR access to the 40-F/financial statement notes was blocked during this review. Findings rely on company releases/decks, Treasury/OFAC releases, and third-party reporting.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH** (improving — trending to Medium)

**Rationale:** The rating is held at High by **Nicaragua (~15–18% of revenue, Very High jurisdiction risk, live US sanctions escalation)** and **Mexico (~15–20% of revenue at normalized Los Filos output, adverse permitting regime and a demonstrated blockade history)** — together roughly a third of revenue in jurisdictions where the state, not the courts, determines outcomes. The Brazil exit, the ~$1bn+ of Canadian cornerstone assets, FAST-41 designations at Castle Mountain/South Railroad, and the June 2026 Los Filos land agreements are all genuine de-risking events.

**Key monitorables:** (1) any OFAC action touching Nicaraguan mining logistics/refining counterparties, or EQX commentary on divesting Nicaragua; (2) Los Filos restart date and ramp to ~150koz+; (3) Senate passage of Mexico's open-pit ban bill and any SEMARNAT permit ruling affecting Camino Rojo underground; (4) resolution of the Brazilian court block on mineral-rights transfer and collection of the $115m contingent payment due Jan 2027; (5) US tariff actions affecting Canadian/Mexican mine inputs.
