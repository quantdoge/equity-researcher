# Macroeconomic Assessment — Equinox Gold Corp. (EQX)

**Data as of 12 November 2026. All macro series from FRED unless noted.**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional, tilting risk-off for real assets — a reflation/inflation-reacceleration regime with a hawkish Fed pivot.**

The regime changed decisively in H2 2026. The Fed cut the effective funds rate from 4.33% (Aug 2025) to a 3.63% trough that held from February through August 2026 — then **reversed and began hiking**, taking the effective rate to 3.75% in September and 3.88% in October. Markets now price a **78% probability of another hike at the 9 December FOMC**.

The reason is inflation: CPI YoY bottomed at ~2.4% in February 2026 and has re-accelerated to **3.82% in October** (Sept: 3.72%, Aug: 3.35%), with the October print coming in at +0.4% m/m and above consensus. The October CPI release knocked gold down ~3% in a session.

Critically, this is **not** a growth scare. Q3 2026 real GDP grew at **3.4% SAAR**, unemployment is **4.1%**, and the 2s10s curve is **+39bp** (bear steepener, not inverted). Add WTI at **~$100/bbl** — up 43% from $69.60 on 6 July — and you have an energy-driven inflation impulse meeting a Fed that has lost the disinflation narrative. The long end is paying for it: 30Y at **5.47%**, 10Y at **5.13%**, and the 10Y TIPS real yield at **2.66%** — a cycle high, up ~59bp from 2.07% on 1 June.

For a gold miner this is the least comfortable configuration available: **gold's opportunity cost is rising, the dollar is bid, and the cost base is inflating simultaneously.** Gold peaked above $5,500/oz earlier in 2026 and now trades near **$4,013** (10 Nov) — a ~27% drawdown driven almost entirely by the real-yield and dollar move.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates & real yields — NEGATIVE (dominant variable).**
Gold is the archetypal zero-coupon real asset; the 10Y TIPS yield at 2.66% is the highest of this cycle and is the single best explanation for the 27% gold drawdown. The transmission to EQX is geared: from the 25 February high ($18.88) to the 17 July low ($8.61), EQX fell **54%** against gold's **24%** decline — a realised downside beta of roughly **2.3x**. On a constant-AISC basis, a 10% move in gold is worth roughly 25–30% on equity value. EQX's reported **beta of 2.49** confirms this is a long-duration, rate-sensitive equity, so the 30Y at 5.47% compresses its terminal value twice over: once through gold, once through the discount rate.

**Inflation — MIXED, net modestly negative at the margin.**
Gold is a genuine inflation hedge and 3.8% CPI supports the structural bid, but the *composition* of this inflation is hostile: it is energy-led, and energy is a direct input to mining. Diesel, electricity, grinding media and steel are 25–30% of AISC. A $100/bbl WTI versus $70 in July is plausibly a **$40–70/oz AISC headwind** if sustained through Q4/Q1. The offset is that EQX's price realisations are rising in nominal terms too, and Q3 delivered record revenue of **$1.0bn** and adjusted EBITDA of **$533M** with costs reduced — so operating leverage is still strongly positive at $4,000 gold.

**Currency — NET POSITIVE for unit margins, negative for the multiple.**
EQX sells gold in USD and pays costs in BRL (Aurizona, RDM, Fazenda, Santa Luz), MXN (Los Filos, Camino Rojo) and CAD (Greenstone, Valentine). The broad dollar's 3.4% rally since September is a headwind to the gold price but a **tailwind to the cost base**: the BRL has weakened 7.5% since early August and the peso 7.1%, so USD-denominated costs at the Brazilian and Mexican operations fall mechanically. Notably, BRL moved from 5.04 to 5.49 (a ~9% slide) in the two weeks to 6 November — disorderly enough to suggest Brazilian fiscal or political stress, which is a tail risk worth monitoring even as it flatters reported costs. Canadian exposure (Greenstone, Valentine, Vancouver HQ) is a genuine growth hub and CAD has been stable.

**Credit conditions & balance sheet — NEUTRAL, improving.**
This is the one macro channel running *in EQX's favour*. At $533M of quarterly EBITDA the company is deleveraging rapidly, has resumed capital returns ($0.0225/qtr dividend; $35M returned in Q3), and is integrating the Orla assets. Refinancing risk is low and falling. The caveat: any floating-rate term debt is repricing at a ~3.88% and rising policy rate, and the equity's cost of capital has risen regardless.

**Trade & tariff exposure — WATCH ITEM, mildly negative.**
US tariffs on steel and aluminium raise equipment, grinding media and capex costs. More consequentially, EQX's asset base straddles the US (Mesquite, Castle Mountain, South Railroad), Canada and Mexico — precisely the geographies exposed to US trade-action risk and to periodic renegotiation of USMCA. The 2025 precedent of a threatened bullion tariff showed how quickly policy can dislocate the gold market itself. Mexico also carries permitting and rule-of-law risk (Los Filos has a history of community/land access disputes).

**Equity-specific macro behaviour:** Perhaps the most informative datapoint is the decoupling since July — gold down 3.8% while EQX is up **20.7%** (from $8.61 to $10.39). That ~24pp spread is company-specific (Orla synergies, cost reduction, deleveraging, the Barron's "buy" call), not macro. It tells you the market is currently paying for execution, which means **macro is being treated as a headwind to be absorbed rather than the driver** — for now.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL** (negative skew over the next 1–2 quarters)

The **level** of the macro backdrop is still extraordinarily favourable: $4,000 gold against an AISC likely in the $1,700–1,900/oz range is a margin environment that would have been unthinkable in 2023. EQX is generating record revenue and EBITDA and returning capital.

But every **marginal** macro variable is currently moving against the stock:

- Real 10Y yields at a 2.66% cycle high and rising
- A live, 78%-priced Fed hike on 9 December
- A 3.4%-stronger dollar in two months
- A 43% oil rally inflating AISC with a one-to-two quarter lag
- EM FX stress (BRL, MXN) signalling a broader dollar-squeeze/risk-off dynamic
- Bear-steepening long end at 5.47% hitting a 2.5-beta long-duration equity

The offset that keeps this at Neutral rather than Unfavourable is the currency channel: local-currency weakness is a real, if underappreciated, cushion to USD unit costs, and the company's deleveraging insulates it from the credit channel that would otherwise make a hiking cycle dangerous. **Net: Neutral, with the balance of macro risk pointing down until the December FOMC is resolved.**

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (Bull, ~25–30% probability)

**The debasement decoupling: gold breaks back above $5,000 while real yields stay high.**

The consensus is that the Fed's hiking cycle kills gold. But 2025 already disproved the simple real-yield model — gold ran from ~$2,600 to over $5,500 with real yields firmly positive throughout, driven by record central bank buying (1,231 tonnes of demand) and reserve diversification.

The live surprise is that the **December hike is the last one, and it fails.** Consider the configuration already in the data: 30Y at 5.47% while 2Y is at 4.74% — the long end is steepening on **term premium and deficit concerns, not growth optimism**. If the Fed hikes on 9 December into a $100 oil price and a visibly softening consumer, and the long end keeps backing up anyway, the market will reprice from "inflation-fighting credibility" to "fiscal dominance." In that world, gold stops trading against TIPS yields and starts trading against the credibility of the sovereign balance sheet — which is precisely the 2025 playbook.

EQX is the highest-torque expression of that outcome: 2.3x realised beta to gold, a 2.49 market beta, an Orla integration adding volume, and a materially deleveraged balance sheet that converts a gold rally into free cash flow rather than interest expense. A move back to $5,000 gold on flat AISC would support $2.8–3.0bn annualised EBITDA and, at the multiple the stock commanded in February, a return to the **$16–18 range** — a 55–75% upside from $10.39.

**The boundary condition (bear, ~30% probability):** if the Fed delivers 50bp or more of additional tightening and the 10Y TIPS yield clears 3.0%, gold at $3,600–3,700 and WTI at $100+ would squeeze AISC from both sides. On 2.3x beta, EQX retests the **$8.00–8.50** July low — roughly 20% downside — *even while reporting record revenue*. That asymmetry, not the level of profitability, is the macro risk an investor is actually underwriting.
