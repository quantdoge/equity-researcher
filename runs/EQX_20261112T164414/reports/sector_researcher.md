# Sector Brief — Gold Mining (Basic Materials) | Target: Equinox Gold Corp. (EQX)

*Data note: latest full-year financials are FY2025 (Dec-31-2025); quarterly data through Q3 2026 (Sept-30-2026, reported Nov-9-2026). Prices as of Nov-12-2026. Margin comparisons are on reported (FMP-standardised) gross/operating lines; D&A sits inside cost of revenue for all names, but D&A intensity differs materially, so operating margins understate EQX's relative position less than they appear — see EBITDA note.*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

Gold mining is a ~3,600–3,700t/yr primary-supply industry with structurally inelastic supply: 10–15 year permitting/development lead times, declining reserve grades, and reserve depletion at the majors (Newmont's attributable reserves fell from 134.1Moz to 118.2Moz in 2025). Production is fragmented at the asset level but the corporate tier is consolidating — 2025–26 saw Newmont/Goldcorp-style logic repeated via Barrick–Newmont's Nevada Gold Mines JV expansion ($1.95bn consideration to Barrick) and mid-tier mergers such as Equinox's combination with Orla. Crucially, 2026 is a **record-margin year**: spot gold printed an all-time high of ~$5,595/oz in January 2026 and traded $4,500–4,900 through Q2, while global average AISC rose only to **$1,785/oz in Q1'26** (+5% q/q, +16% y/y) — pushing average AISC margins to a record **~$3,076/oz (+134% y/y)**. The sector's Basic Materials aggregate (22 largest names, FY2025) shows $484bn revenue, $159bn gross profit (32.9%) and $70.6bn net income (14.6% net margin). The sector is **late-cycle on margins, mid-cycle on volume** — the profit pool is expanding because of price, not ounces.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Record price-to-cost spread.** Average AISC $1,785/oz (Q1'26) against gold averaging >$4,500/oz implies ~60–70% cash margins per ounce — the widest in the industry's history. Every $100/oz on gold is worth roughly $90–95m of EBITDA to EQX on ~0.9Moz+ guidance.
- **Structural, price-insensitive demand.** Central-bank accumulation, ETF re-stocking and debasement/rate-cut logic; sell-side targets cluster at $4,275/oz (Reuters poll average for 2026) to $4,650/oz (Goldman, end-2026). BofA notes gold miners' **earnings yield of 12.0% is the highest of any sector** and the cheapest vs the S&P 500 in 20 years — a valuation tailwind for the whole peer group.
- **Consolidation and capital returns.** Majors are buying growth rather than building it (Nevada JV restructuring), which supports takeout premia for mid-tiers with permitted pipelines. Sector balance sheets have delevered and dividends/buybacks are now normal (EQX initiated and has since raised its dividend, returning $35m in Q3'26 alone).

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **28 consecutive quarters of AISC inflation.** Costs are ratcheting: royalties have doubled from ~6% to ~12% of AISC since Q1'21, with **sliding-scale royalty regimes** introduced in Ghana (up to 12% above $4,500/oz), Burkina Faso (10%), and Mali (9.5%) — a direct tax on the price rally, and EQX's Brazil/Mexico exposure carries similar resource-nationalism risk.
- **Energy and supply-chain shock.** Closure of the Strait of Hormuz drove US diesel +54% q/q and Perth wholesale diesel +96% in Q1'26. Large caps are hedged (Newmont, Evolution, OceanaGold report PPAs/fuel contracts); **junior and mid-tier producers are structurally more exposed** — a relative disadvantage for EQX.
- **Margin mean-reversion and M&A overpayment risk.** If gold normalises toward $3,500–4,000/oz, the sector's record margins compress violently because AISC is sticky downward. Reserve depletion plus a bidding war for quality ounces also raises the risk of value-destructive deals; EQX's own all-share Orla transaction lifted the share count from ~630m to ~1.04bn, diluting per-share leverage to gold.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Average** (improving, with execution risk)

**Rationale for "average":**
- *Negatives (dominant):* second-quartile-or-worse cost position (AISC $1,900–2,000/oz vs $1,785 average); margin gap of 10–20pts on EBITDA vs senior peers; multi-jurisdiction operational execution record is poor — 2026 guidance now points to the **low end** (870–920koz) after ramp-up problems at Greenstone (Canada) and Valentine, plus pit instability at Mesquite (USA); levered balance sheet ($30.8m quarterly interest, ~$1.7bn gross debt implied) magnifies the downside if gold corrects; heavy equity dilution from Orla (shares ~630m → ~1.04bn) which transfers gold-price upside to former Orla holders.
- *Positives:* genuine scale transformation toward ~1.1Moz near-term and a stated 2Moz path; jurisdiction mix improving toward Tier-1 Canada/US (Greenstone, Valentine, South Railroad — now federally permitted in Nevada); geographic diversification across Brazil, Mexico, US, Canada; dividend initiated and raised (Q3'26 $0.0225/qtr); trades at a clear discount to peers (EV/EBITDA ~8x on ~$1.6bn LTM EBITDA versus double-digit multiples for AEM/NEM), so the market has already priced much of the execution risk; Barron's and several sell-side notes argue the transformation is under-appreciated.
- *Net:* EQX is a **beta play on gold with an execution discount**, not a quality-compounder. It is a price-taker with below-peer margins and above-peer leverage — the classic mid-tier profile. Rerate requires Greenstone/Valentine to hit nameplate and a Q4-2026 record quarter (~258koz needed) to be delivered.

## 6. Sector Rating: **Attractive**

Rationale: record absolute and relative margins, 12% sector earnings yield (highest of any sector per BofA), 20-year-cheapest valuation vs the S&P, inelastic supply, and structural central-bank demand all argue for a constructive stance. The qualifier is cycle position — this is a **late-cycle margin peak**, with AISC in its 28th consecutive quarter of inflation and royalties/energy/resource nationalism eroding the spread. Within an Attractive sector, the preference is for **low-cost, Tier-1-jurisdiction producers (AEM, NEM, B, KGC) over high-cost mid-tiers like EQX**; EQX's case rests on self-help operational delivery and a valuation discount, not on sector tailwinds alone.
