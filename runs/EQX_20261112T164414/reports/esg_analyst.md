## EQUINOX GOLD CORP. (NYSE-A: EQX) — ESG ASSESSMENT

**Entity check:** Equinox Gold Corp., Vancouver; mid-tier/senior gold producer, ~3,700 employees, US$12.1B market cap (~US$10.39, 12-Nov-26); 52-wk range ~$8.48–$18.88; beta 2.49. Portfolio radically reshaped twice in 14 months: **Calibre Mining merger (17-Jun-25)** and **Orla Mining merger (31-Jul-26)**; **Brazil sold to CMOC for US$1.015B cash (closed Jan-26)**. Six mines now spanning Canada (Greenstone, Valentine), US (Mesquite, Castle Mountain, Pan), Mexico (Camino Rojo; Los Filos in care & maintenance).

### 2. Environmental Risk: **MEDIUM**

**Positives:** 2025 Significant Environmental Incident Frequency Rate of **0.00**; zero tailings/heap-leach incidents (2024, 2025) under its own reporting framework; external assurance at producing mines (ex-Brazil) against World Gold Council **Responsible Gold Mining Principles** and **Conflict-Free Gold Standard**; 60,113 tCO₂e reductions; 40 ha under restoration; dry-stack tailings and shared freshwater reservoir at RDM (now divested).

**Residual risks:**
- **Legacy tailings contamination — Aurizona (Brazil).** The 2021 failure of the Lagoa do Pirocaua sediment dam cut potable water to ~4,000 people; ANM ordered the dam's use stopped and state environment authority SEMA fined the company. Independent meteorological review (Dr. S. Emerman) found the storm's return period was **<1 year**, contradicting the "1-in-10,000-year event" defence, and attributed root cause to lack of inspection/maintenance. Equinox's own ESG reporting omitted the dam failure while community water complaints continued into 2022. **This is now CMOC's problem operationally, but indemnity/representation exposure and reputational scar tissue travel with the seller.** No quantified remediation provision is visible to us — *material disclosure gap*.
- **Water stress:** Camino Rojo (Zacatecas) and Los Filos (Guerrero) sit in high-stress basins; Mesquite/Castle Mountain are in California/Nevada desert settings under SMARA/state water rules. Cyanide heap-leach at Los Filos is ~500m from Carrizalillo's health clinic and soccer field.
- **Emissions/Scope 3:** No Scope 3 inventory or intensity (tCO₂e/oz) disclosed — a genuine data gap versus Newmont/Agnico. 60,113 tCO₂e of "reductions" is not a baseline-inventory disclosure.
- **Climate transition exposure is second-order:** gold is not a CBAM good; no EU CSRD obligation identified. Commercial risk runs through diesel/freight and Mexican grid, not carbon pricing.

### 3. Social Risk: **HIGH**

**Positive:** 2025 **zero fatalities**; TRIFR improved to 1.65 (2.21 in 2024); LTIFR 0.49 (2024); 98% national / 67% host-community employment; 93% national procurement; US$14M community investment and US$2.1B economic value distributed (2025); five benefit agreements with **seven Indigenous partners** at Canadian mines — best-in-class for the peer group; women 16% of workforce, 30% of board.

**Why High:**
- **Los Filos is a chronic social-licence failure, not an incident.** Operations were **indefinitely suspended 1-Apr-25** when Equinox let land-use and social-cooperation agreements with the **ejido of Carrizalillo** (≈2,800 residents) lapse. Reporting indicates the company sought austere cuts as a precondition for continued investment; a Mexican agrarian court called its ~US$90k compensation offer "ridiculously small and even laughable" (~1.5 pesos per ejido member per month), and Equinox withdrew the suit rather than match 2024 rents. Community members reported **death threats** (Mar-25, denounced by ~100 organisations); since Nov-25 the community has accused Equinox of **seeking to criminalise its protest**. In **Oct-25 PROFEPA issued temporary partial-closure notices** on areas of the Los Filos complex; the company reports four months of inspections and submitted corrective plans.
- **Safety tail:** a fatality was disclosed in the 2023 ESG report (seven LTIs); an **Oct-25 chute/pulling-mechanism failure at Valentine** seriously injured two workers (leg injuries, medevacked to Gander; provincial OHS investigation, stop-work order). Valentine is a brand-new, now-flagship Canadian asset — a bad first safety record in the highest-quality jurisdiction.
- **ASM sourcing:** a mercury-reduction programme (≈3 t avoided) implies operational artisanal/small-scale ore sourcing — an exposure to child/forced-labour and mercury tail risk that requires chain-of-custody assurance.
- 2026 operational setbacks (Mesquite **pit instability**, Canadian ramp-up issues) are geotechnical — but pit-wall failures at times sit at the E/S boundary.

**Rough financial impact of the social record:** Los Filos at ~150–180 koz/yr in C&M against a ~US$1,900/oz AISC and ~US$4,000/oz realized price represents roughly **US$300–380M/yr of forgone EBITDA** (our illustrative estimate) — i.e. the single largest ESG-attributable value destruction, an order of magnitude above all fines and legal costs combined.

### 4. Governance Quality: **5 / 10**

**Positives:** 30% board gender diversity; separate Sustainability & Risk executive function (Chief Corporate Development, Sustainability & Risk Officer) with ESG credentials; independent external assurance; NCIB buyback plus inaugural dividend (Mar-26, raised to $0.0225/qtr by Q3-26); >US$1.1B debt reduction since Q2-25.

**Deductions:**
- **Serial transformational M&A and CEO churn.** Greg Smith (to Jun-25) → **Darren Hall**, ex-Calibre CEO (Jun-25) → **Jason Simpson**, ex-Orla CEO, President from 31-Jul-26 and **CEO from 31-Oct-26**. Two mergers and three CEOs in 18 months is a strategy-continuity and accountability problem; ex-target CEOs sequentially assuming the top job also concentrates deal-making influence.
- **Board reconstituted twice** in 14 months (Calibre Jun-25; Orla Jul-26) — limited forward-looking independence track record at the current configuration; post-merger board independence and chair/CEO separation are **not verifiable from our feed** (no DEF 14A/10-K available — EQX reports on a Canadian 40-F/AIF basis, so US-style proxy disclosure and say-on-pay do not apply). *Disclosure gap.*
- Growth-by-accretion plus buybacks plus dividend simultaneously raises the question of capital-allocation discipline.
- No evidence found of ESG-linked debt covenants (neutral to slightly negative on lender discipline).

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **Mexico (highest):** SEMARNAT/PROFEPA enforcement (LGEEPA overhaul tightening permits, compliance liability and enforcement); CONAGUA water concessions; 2023 mining-law reform and proposed **open-pit mining restrictions**; 2023 labour-reform union-democracy rules. Two of six mines here, one paralysed.
- **Canada:** federal Impact Assessment Act (Greenstone), Ontario MECP, Newfoundland & Labrador OHS (active Valentine investigation); **Fighting Against Forced Labour in Supply Chains Act** annual reporting; Canadian Sustainability Disclosure Standards (IFRS S1/S2-aligned) phasing in — EQX will need Scope 3 and climate-risk disclosure.
- **United States (under-appreciated):** **California SB 253/261** likely apply — >US$1B revenue *and* doing business in California (Mesquite and Castle Mountain) → Scope 1–3 disclosure and climate financial-risk reporting from 2026. The **SEC climate rule is effectively off the table** post-vacatur, so California is the binding US requirement. Also SMARA/state reclamation bonding.
- **Brazil:** exited — residual liability transferred to CMOC, but ESG-screening investors may still ask about the Aurizona legacy.
- **EU:** minimal. No CSRD scope evidence; gold outside CBAM; no EU-taxonomy-driven debt pricing identified.
- **Voluntary/assurance:** WGC RGMP + Conflict-Free Gold Standard external assurance (a real credibility positive vs peers relying on self-report).

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (leaning Laggard on Social)

This is not a broken ESG story — 2025 delivered zero fatalities, a zero significant-environmental-incident rate, RGMP assurance and an exit from its weakest jurisdiction. But the company has **one unresolved, multi-year, value-destructive community conflict (Los Filos)**, a fresh serious-injury event at its flagship new Canadian mine, and a governance record of two dilutive mergers and three CEOs in 18 months. Peer set (Newmont, Agnico Eagle, Barrick) discloses materially more (Scope 3, TCFD/ISSB, board-level sustainability committees, US-style proxies) and has cleaner community records in OECD jurisdictions. EQX screens as mid-pack on E, weak on S, and below-average on G relative to senior peers.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL (with a negative governance watch item)**

- **The big ESG loss has already crystallised, not pending.** Los Filos' suspension is in the numbers; restart is now free optionality. Brazil's exit removed the worst tail (post-failure dam, Amazon-adjacent permitting) at a good price. Jurisdiction mix — Ontario, Newfoundland, California/Nevada, Zacatecas/Guerrero — is now majority Tier-1 by revenue, which should compress equity risk premium and improve index/ESG-fund eligibility over time.
- **What the market is underpricing is governance, not environmental.** Two mergers in 14 months, a CEO seat rotating through the acquired companies' CEOs, and a buyback + dividend + growth-capital programme running simultaneously is a per-share-value risk that ESG frameworks label "G". Demand clarity on capital allocation, board independence/chair separation, and PSU metrics.
- **Specific catalysts to monitor:** (1) Los Filos restart terms with Carrizalillo — a fair-rent, good-faith agreement would be a genuine re-rating signal on social licence; a forced/agrarian-court route would be a red flag; (2) resolution of PROFEPA partial closures; (3) Valentine OHS investigation outcome (charges/fines under NL OHS); (4) California SB 253 Scope 1–3 disclosure quality — a visible gap today; (5) whether Greenstone/Valentine community agreements survive the Orla/Calibre board churn.
- **Bottom line:** No ESG-driven exclusion or forced-seller dynamic, and no uninsurable environmental tail visible at current assets. ESG neither adds nor subtracts from the equity thesis; treat community relations in Mexico and M&A discipline as ongoing risk factors rather than thesis drivers.

**Data gaps (honest):** no 10-K/DEF 14A retrievable (40-F/AIF filer) — board independence, chair/CEO separation and executive-compensation alignment **unverified**; no Scope 1/2 absolute emissions, emissions intensity or Scope 3 inventory; no third-party ESG rating (LSEG/MSCI/Sustainalytics) available to triangulate; fine/remediation amounts for Aurizona and PROFEPA not disclosed. Peer ESG scores unavailable in our feed; comparisons to NEM/AEM/B are qualitative.
