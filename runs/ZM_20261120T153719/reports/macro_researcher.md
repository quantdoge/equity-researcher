### 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional, leaning risk-off — a re-inflation / term-premium regime, not a recession regime.**

The defining feature of this cycle is that the disinflation of 2025 has reversed. Headline CPI has re-accelerated from a mid-2025 trough to **3.82% YoY**, with the last three months running at a **~4.1% annualised** pace versus **~3.1%** over six months — the second derivative is the problem. The Fed finished its easing cycle in mid-2026 (funds steady at 3.63% April–August), then **reversed and hiked in September (FOMC 16 Sep: "Inflation remains elevated… a timelier return to the Committee's 2 percent goal") and again in October**, taking the effective rate to **3.88%**.

Critically, the **ex-post real policy rate is essentially zero** (3.88% nominal vs 3.82% headline). Policy is not restrictive on headline inflation, and the bond market has priced that: the **10Y has moved from 4.38% (29 Jun) to 5.07% (18 Nov), peaking at 5.31% on 5 Oct**, with the **30Y at 5.47%**. This is **bear steepening** — a term-premium and fiscal/inflation-risk story, not a growth story. The curve is **positively sloped (+39bp 2s10s), so there is no recession signal from inversion**; the message is higher-for-longer discount rates.

Growth itself is fine, even improving: **real GDP +3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026, +2.1% YoY**, after a near-stall in Q4 2025 (+0.2% SAAR). Unemployment has drifted *down* to **4.1%** from 4.5% a year ago. Nominal PCE is running **+6.3% YoY**. This is an economy that does not need rate cuts — which is precisely why long-duration equities are under pressure.

**For ZM this is the worst of both worlds: no growth recession to trigger Fed easing, and no disinflation to pull the long end down.**

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The single dominant transmission channel — the discount rate — is moving against ZM hard, and the offsetting channels are small. A 5.1% 10Y with a **positively sloped, bear-steepening curve**, a Fed that has resumed hiking, and an ex-post real policy rate of zero is close to a worst-case macro configuration for a 5%-growth, long-duration software multiple. The strong-dollar leg adds a near-term reported-revenue headwind into a Q3 FY27 print due **Monday 23 November**. ZM's net cash and interest income blunt the damage but cannot offset multiple compression of this magnitude — which is why the stock has de-rated ~22% since August despite stable operating trends.

**Important nuance:** ZM's *business* is almost macro-insensitive (recurring subscription revenue, 5% growth, 40% margins, net cash). Its *stock* is highly macro-sensitive. Do not conflate the two — but do not ignore the second one either.

### 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BEAR**

**"The 2027 hiking cycle."** The market currently assumes the Fed stops near 4.0%. Instead, the 3-month CPI pace (~4.1%) proves sticky on shelter, energy and tariff pass-through; the Fed delivers three more 25bp hikes to **4.75–5.00%** by mid-2027, and the term premium takes the **10Y through 5.50% and the 30Y toward 6%** on deficit-supply fears. In that world:

- Software de-rates another 20–25% on a mechanical discount-rate basis; ZM's already-broken August-to-November downtrend extends and retests the **$70 area** (2026 low: $70.70 in March).
- The **Anthropic IPO is postponed or repriced down**, removing the sum-of-parts support that the bull case leans on — the double hit of a lower multiple *and* a lower mark on the longest-duration asset on the book.
- USD breaks above 125 on the broad index as rate differentials widen, turning the FY2028 guidance reset into a currency-headwind story rather than a currency-neutral one.

**Mirror-image bull tail (why the distribution is fat both ways):** if the Q4 2025 near-stall (+0.2% SAAR) was the leading edge of a genuine slowdown rather than a data artefact, inflation rolls over fast, the Fed reverses back to 3.00% by mid-2027, the 10Y retreats to 4.20%, and long-duration software re-rates violently. With 5% growth, 40% margins, net cash and an AI stake marked up in a reopened IPO window, ZM would be one of the highest-beta expressions of that move. Given ZM's **1.02 beta to the market but far higher effective duration to the 10Y**, a 90bp decline in the long end is plausibly worth 30–40% of upside — a larger move than the bear case. The macro is currently positioned for the bear; the asymmetry favours patience over aggression.

---

**Bottom line for the investment memo:** the macro backdrop is a **headwind that is valuation-driven, not earnings-driven**. ZM's operating model is close to macro-insulated; its share price is not. Position sizing should reflect that the 10Y, not the P&L, is the marginal price-setter for this stock right now — and that the near-term event risk is idiosyncratic-plus-FX (23 Nov print, USD at 122.65) sitting on top of a hostile rate regime.
