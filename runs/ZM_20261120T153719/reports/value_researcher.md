## ZM (Zoom Communications) — Value Analysis
*Price $83.48 (Nov 20, 2026) · Market cap ~$25.0B (300M diluted sh.) · Net cash ~$7.76B · EV ~$17.2B*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW**

Zoom sells subscriptions for video meetings, phone, rooms and contact center, plus an AI Companion bundled at no extra cost. The economics are software-like: **77.0% gross margin** (FY26), dollar-renewal-driven revenue, and virtually no capital intensity (**capex $65M on $4.87B revenue — 1.3%**).

Evidence for a moat:
- **Switching costs** in enterprise: Zoom Rooms hardware/AV integration, Zoom Phone as a telephony system of record, admin/security workflows. Churn is low and revenue base is sticky.
- **Cost/scale advantage in gross margin**: 74.3% → 77.0% over five years despite a shrinking consumer base.
- **Ex-cash ROIC ~42%** (NOPAT $865M on invested capital of ~$2.05B, after SBC). The reported 11.4% ROIC is an artifact of a $7.8B cash pile, not weak economics.

Evidence against a **Wide** moat — and this is decisive:
- **Microsoft Teams is bundled "free" into M365**, Google Meet into Workspace. Zoom has no pricing power against a zero-priced substitute; enterprise buyers reportedly negotiate 15–35% discounts off list.
- **Growth has collapsed to low single digits**: revenue CAGR 4.4% FY22–FY26, with 3.1%, 3.1%, 4.4% in the last three years; FY27 guidance +4.5%, and the Q3 FY27 guide implied ~3.9%.
- Moat has **narrowed since 2020–21** (prosumer/SEG share lost to Teams); it has stabilized, not widened. Zoom is the best-of-breed niche, not the standard.

Verdict: a good, cash-generative franchise with a narrow, *stable* moat — not a great business in the Buffett sense.

Two real blemishes: (1) **SBC at 15.6% of revenue** — roughly $2.45/share pre-tax of genuine economic cost; (2) **GAAP earnings are not clean** — FY26 net income of $1.90B exceeded operating income of $1.12B, and Q2 FY27 net income of $1.54B vs $314M of operating income, both driven by large **non-cash tax benefits (DTA recognition)**. Ignore the 13.5x "P/E"; normalized net income is ~$1.1B (~$3.75/sh).

### 3. Valuation Analysis — Intrinsic value **$75–90 (midpoint ~$82)** vs price $83.48 → **no margin of safety**

The DCF utility returned a broken result (WACC mis-scaled), so I built the model manually. Base case: operating FCF of $1.60B less $0.76B SBC = **$0.85B**, growing 4.5% → 3.0%, terminal 2.5%, 9–10% WACC:

| Scenario | IV/share |
|---|---|
| SBC fully deducted, 10% WACC | ~$75 |
| SBC fully deducted, 9% WACC | ~$79 |
| SBC at ~70% (partial replacement cost), 9% | ~$89 |
| Reported-FCF basis (no SBC haircut, generous) | ~$125 (rejected — double counts) |

Cross-checks:
- **EV/operating FCF ex-SBC ≈ 20x** for a 4–5% grower. **Ex-cash owner-earnings multiple ~15.7x** with 4.5% growth → implied total return ~8%/yr. Adequate, not exceptional.
- Non-GAAP P/E 13.7x (FY27 EPS $6.08–6.12) vs sector peer median 28.3x — the discount is *earned* by low growth and SBC, not an anomaly.
- FCF yield 7.8% reported, but only **~4.9% after SBC**; EV/revenue 3.4x.

At $83.48 the market is capitalizing Zoom's cash at face value and paying a full price for a low-growth operating business. **My required 20–30% discount is not present.** Buy zone: **$62–66** (25% below midpoint IV); it traded at $70–73 as recently as Feb–Mar 2026.

### 4. Management Quality — **Good, with one reservation**

- **Eric Yuan, founder-CEO** with meaningful insider ownership — owner-operator alignment, long tenure, no debt-funded adventurism.
- **Capital allocation is disciplined where it counts**: $1.62B of buybacks in FY26 and ~$360M/quarter in FY27 at 13–15x earnings; share count down 5%. Zero dividends and no debt.
- **M&A discipline**: the failed $14.7B Five9 bid (2021) was a near-miss on value destruction; since then, conservative.
- **Reservation**: $7.8B (31% of market cap) sits in Treasuries earning ~4% while the stock buys back at ~14x earnings — the buyback should be larger, or a dividend initiated. Recent **insider selling** disclosed around the November drop.
- Guidance is conservative and consistently beaten (6 of last 6 quarters beat on revenue; 4 of 5 on EPS).

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

A high-quality, cash-rich, founder-led franchise — but **the price is fair, not cheap**, and a fair price is not a buy. Quality justifies owning it; valuation does not justify adding here. Existing holders should hold (net cash plus buyback provide a floor near $65–70); new money should wait
