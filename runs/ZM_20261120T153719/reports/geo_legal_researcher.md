## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical & Regulatory Risks

### Risk #1 — China-linked legacy and R&D dependency (Probability: High that it recurs; Impact: Medium–High)
The single most distinctive geopolitical vulnerability. Zoom's founder/CEO Eric Yuan is Chinese-born and the company long maintained a **sizable development team in China (Hangzhou)**. Court documents from the DOJ prosecution of former Zoom employee **Julian Jin** detail that in October 2019 Yuan travelled to Beijing, met with the **Ministry of Public Security Network Security Bureau**, and Zoom drafted a **"rectification plan"** to monitor and suppress content on Tiananmen, Hong Kong protests, and criticism of Chinese leaders — after China blocked the service in September 2019. Service was restored ~10 weeks later (17 Nov 2019). Prosecutors allege Jin **informed executives** of his takedown work, indicating executive awareness. *(Source: CyberScoop, 17 May 2023, reporting unsealed DOJ criminal complaint.)*

Mitigants: Zoom publicly stated key parts of the rectification plan were never implemented; it cooperated with US investigators; it has since **shifted R&D toward India and the US explicitly to offset geopolitical risk** (Nikkei). No direct China sales motion since ~2020.
Residual: Reputational taint with US Congress (House Select Committee on China has publicly attacked the company), elevated scrutiny in federal/state procurement, and latent follow-on litigation risk.

### Risk #2 — AI-era privacy litigation and the FTC consent-order overhang (Probability: High; Impact: Medium, ~$100–500M range)
A live, dated event: **Sheldon v. Zoom**, putative class action filed **25 September 2026** in Illinois, alleging **AI Companion** and **ZoomMate** generate **voiceprints** for every speaker — including guests who never created an account or accepted terms — without BIPA-compliant written notice and release. BIPA statutory damages are **$1,000 per negligent** and **$5,000 per reckless/intentional** violation; the Illinois Supreme Court treats loss of control over biometrics as the injury. Plaintiffs firms (Silver Golub & Teitell, Loevy, FKKS-tracked) are actively soliciting the class. Given Zoom's meeting volumes, uncapped per-violation damages create a headline-number tail even if settlement values land far lower. *(Sources: sgtlaw.com case page; claimdepot; FKKS BIPA tracker.)*

Compounding: this lands on top of a **20-year FTC consent order (Nov 2020)** mandating a data-security program and specific remedial measures following deceptive end-to-end-encryption claims — meaning any new security/privacy misstatement is both a fresh violation *and* an order-compliance breach. Also prior: **$85M** Zoom Meetings privacy/zoombombing settlement, **NY AG** privacy settlement (2020), and state AG inquiries from ~27 states.

### Risk #3 — EU data-transfer / AI Act regime friction (Probability: Medium; Impact: Medium)
EMEA is 15.8% of revenue and the most regulated slice. Zoom depends on the **EU–US Data Privacy Framework** (in force since 10 July 2023) plus SCCs for transatlantic transfers; DPF is subject to recurring legal challenge (the *Latombe* line), and an adverse ruling would force re-papering of EU customer contracts and potentially suspend transfers. Separately, **EU AI Act transparency obligations phase in from August 2026**, adding disclosure duties for AI Companion-class features. Zoom's **August 2023 terms-of-service change** (asserting rights to use customer content for AI training, later walked back amid backlash) remains the template for GDPR/consumer-protection complaints. No current EU DPA enforcement action against Zoom was located **[unverified / no evidence found]** — this is a *policy-drift* risk rather than an existing penalty.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **FTC 20-year order + a fresh privacy class action (BIPA) = repeat-offender optics.** Any adverse 2026–27 privacy finding invites FTC to treat it as an order breach, which carries civil penalty authority. Highest-priority red flag.
2. **Biometric consent gap for unregistered meeting guests.** Non-users who join by link never accept terms or see a biometric notice — a structural, not incidental, BIPA defect. Applies beyond Illinois if other states (Texas CUBI, Washington) pursue similar theories.
3. **China-state interaction / third-party risk.** The Jin/MPS conduct raises classic third-party-and-foreign-official touchpoints. No FCPA bribery charge exists, but the precedent shows local Chinese personnel can act in ways that create US criminal exposure for individuals and severe reputational exposure for the issuer. Warrants strong China-entity governance separation and FCPA/anti-corruption screening of PRC partners.
4. **Sanctions/export controls — low but non-zero.** Encryption software is EAR-controlled; the company has shown awareness by **prohibiting sales to Russian state agencies** (2021) and exiting Russia. Residual risk is partner-channel leakage into sanctioned jurisdictions (Russia, Iran, sanctioned Middle East parties) and OFAC screening of resellers.
5. **EU–US DPF reliance** as a single point of failure for ~16% of revenue.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **MEDIUM**

**Why not High:** 72% Americas revenue, zero manufacturing/tariff/commodity exposure, no Russia operations, no current government investigation into the company, and $7.8B of cash comfortably covers any plausible settlement in the $100–500M band.

**Why not Low:** a still-live **20-year FTC consent order**, a **fresh 2026 BIPA class action** with uncapped statutory damages, a **$150M securities settlement** concluded only in 2025, and an unresolved **China censorship episode** documented in DOJ filings that keeps the company on the radar of China-hawk legislators and federal procurement reviewers.

**Watch items (12–18 months):** (i) class certification in *Sheldon v. Zoom*; (ii) any FTC action framed as consent-order violation; (iii) an adverse EU data-transfer ruling affecting DPF; (iv) China-hawk legislative or procurement riders targeting the company; (v) AI Act transparency compliance from Aug 2026.
