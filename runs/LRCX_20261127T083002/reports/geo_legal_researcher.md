## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk #1 — MATCH Act / legislated export controls on chipmaking tools to China
**Probability: High (~65% some form enacted within 12 months) | Impact: Very High**

- The **MATCH Act** (Multilateral Alignment of Technology Controls on Hardware) was introduced **April 2, 2026** by Sens. Ricketts (R-NE) and Kim (D-NJ), co-sponsored by **Risch and Schumer** — a rare bipartisan, leadership-backed coalition. House companion led by Rep. Baumgartner (R-WA). (ricketts.senate.gov, 4/2/26)
- Mechanics: harmonizes U.S. controls with the Netherlands and Japan, **closes servicing and entity-specific loopholes**, and — per the Senate version — **directs Commerce to impose controls unilaterally after ~150 days** if allies don't match. The bill names every DUV lithography tool among covered equipment.
- **Catalyst timeline:** TS Lombard expects **three to four chip export-control measures attached to the FY2027 NDAA**; passage is a question of *"when," not "if,"* possibly slipping into early 2027 around the **Dec. 11 funding deadline**. The **Nvidia smuggling/leadership scandal** in November 2026 is the proximate political accelerant. (Motley Fool, 11/25/26)
- **Market has already repriced it:** LRCX fell **9.7% intraday to $177.06** and ~7% on 11/25/26 on MATCH Act headlines; the stock is down **~50–52% from its 52-week high** despite record results. (Street Insider; 24/7 Wall St.)
- **Quantification:** each 10pp of China revenue at risk ≈ **~$2.3B annualized** on the current run-rate (Sept-Q revenue $8.22B, +54–55% YoY). Note the mitigating nuance: much of Lam's China revenue is *already* outside the most restrictive tool tiers, and a meaningful share is **domestic Chinese customers** (CXMT, YMTC, SMIC) rather than banned global fabs. A *harmonized* regime that finally binds ASML/TEL could theoretically be net-neutral-to-positive for Lam competitively — Senator Ricketts' explicit framing is that U.S. rules are currently *"stronger than those of our close allies,"* putting "American companies last."

### Risk #2 — Taiwan Strait disruption
**Probability: Low (~5–10% p.a. for a kinetic/blockade event) | Impact: Severe**

Taiwan is **22.5% of revenue** and rising fast (+213% over two years, $1.67B → $5.22B), concentrated at TSMC's most advanced nodes — the single densest cluster of Lam's installed base and the leading edge of AI/HBM demand. Blockade, quarantine, or semiconductor-tariff escalation would simultaneously hit revenue *and* trigger non-linear supply-chain contagion across the whole WFE complex. Note also that the U.S. has imposed tariffs on Taiwan since April 2025 (CRS IF10275), adding a persistent friction layer.

### Risk #3 — Korean memory customer concentration & retaliation/second-order policy risk
**Probability: Medium | Impact: High**

Korea at 19.4% is effectively a bet on Samsung and SK hynix HBM capex. Two distinct exposures: (a) **cyclical** — memory is the marginal driver of this cycle, and a Samsung/Hynix capex pause hits Lam harder than diversified peers; (b) **policy** — U.S. pressure on Korean fabs operating in China (and Chinese retaliation via rare-earth/helium export controls or antitrust action against U.S. toolmakers) creates a channel where Lam loses revenue *without* any direct U.S. rule change.

**Secondary:** China's own retaliation toolkit (export controls on critical minerals; unreliable-entity-list designations of U.S. firms; an antitrust probe targeting a U.S. chip firm, as seen with Nvidia) is a live but hard-to-underwrite tail.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active/pending litigation**
- **PSK Inc. v. Lam Research** — Korean patent litigation over **bevel etcher equipment**; the parties have been litigating discovery/on-site inspection procedure. (The Elec) Modest dollar exposure, but operationally relevant to a core clean-product line.
- **Inpria Corporation (a JSR subsidiary) v. Lam Research** — federal **patent** suit naming Lam as defendant. (Justia federal dockets)
- Legacy U.S. trade-secret and patent disputes (e.g., Veeco, CIGNEX) appear resolved/historical.

**Regulatory posture**
- Primary regulator: **U.S. Bureau of Industry and Security (BIS)** under the EAR — this is the dominant regulator for Lam, far more consequential than the SEC or FTC.
- Lam has publicly engaged on rulemakings (e.g., its December 2024 statement on newly announced export regulations), confirming active policy exposure and lobbying engagement.
- **Congressional scrutiny:** Lam is **named specifically** in the House Select Committee on the CCP report *"Selling the Forges of the Future,"* which recommends harmonizing SME export restrictions — meaning Lam is a *target* of legislative attention, not just a bystander.
- **Antitrust:** no material FTC/DOJ/EC merger or conduct action identified in accessible sources.

**Disclosure gap:** Direct EDGAR access was blocked (HTTP 403) and the filing-text tool could not retrieve the FY2026 10-K, so I could **not** verify the Legal Proceedings footnote or confirm whether any **BIS administrative subpoena, enforcement action, or deemed-export violation** is disclosed. Historically Lam has operated under export licenses with conditions; any license denial or civil penalty would be a material negative surprise. **This is the single most important thing to verify in the 10-K before publishing.**

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating

# 🔴 HIGH

**Rationale:** Lam carries the **highest China revenue concentration of any major U.S. wafer-fab-equipment maker (~35%)**, and it is precisely that revenue pool which is the explicit legislative target of a bipartisan bill with leadership backing and a plausible path into the FY2027 NDAA. Combine that with **56% of revenue in Greater China**, **only 6.6% U.S. revenue** (no home-market buffer), and a stock already down ~50% on policy fear, and the geopolitical overlay is the dominant non-fundamental driver of the equity.

**Why not Very High:** the company is not sanctionable, has no identified material government investigation, holds net cash (~$3.7B), and — importantly — the *direction* of policy is toward **multilateral harmonization** that would bind ASML and Tokyo Electron, which currently enjoy lighter China rules. A level playing field is competitively *better* for Lam than the status quo, even if the absolute revenue pool shrinks. Demand visibility extends into 2028 and management guides WFE above **$200B in 2027**.

**What would change the rating:**
- ⬆️ **To Very High:** confirmation of a BIS subpoena/enforcement action; MATCH Act enacted *without* allied harmonization; a Taiwan quarantine event.
- ⬇️ **To Medium:** MATCH enacted *with* Dutch/Japanese alignment (competitively neutral, China mix drifts to ~20%), or China revenue mix falling below 25% organically.
