## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW–MEDIUM**

**Positives (verified against 2025 Global Impact Report / SBTi):**
- Scope 1+2 (market-based) **down 36% vs. 2019 baseline**, exceeding the 25% 2025 goal; target of –46.2% by 2030 and **net-zero operations by 2040**, full net zero 2050.
- **~60% renewable electricity** toward 100% by 2030; 13.7M kWh saved in 2025; >80M gallons of water saved.
- **SBTi-approved** near-term targets: Scope 3 use-of-sold-products intensity –63.8% per USD value added by 2034 (2022 base); supplier SBT coverage targets (95% of top direct suppliers by 2030).
- **CDP Climate A-** (upgraded from B), DJSI North America inclusion for a 4th consecutive year, Sustainalytics ~13.6 ("low risk"), S&P Global CSA 58. One public ESG screen lists MSCI **AAA** (unverified against MSCI directly — treat as AA/AAA band).

**Risks:**
- Absolute Scope 3 (8.97 MtCO₂e) dwarfs operational emissions (~0.1–0.2 Mt est.). Lam's climate risk is *transmitted* through customer decarbonisation requirements and upstream supplier emissions, both of which it is now being asked to underwrite contractually (EU CSRD value-chain data requests).
- Carbon-price exposure is negligible directly: even $100/t on ~0.15 Mt ≈ **$15M/yr**, or <0.1% of revenue. This is a disclosure/competitiveness risk, not a P&L risk.
- Physical risk: modest — Fremont (water stress/drought, seismic), plus typhoon/earthquake exposure via Taiwan/Korea/Japan customer and supplier concentration. No uninsured catastrophic loss in evidence.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Safety:** recordable injury rate **0.26 per 100 workers** in 2025 (goal <0.4), –7% YoY, lost-time injuries –17%. Best-in-class for a manufacturing/field-service footprint. Strong positive.
- **Labour/geopolitics:** China (35% of revenue) and Malaysia/Korea/India expansion create exposure to forced-labour and labour-standards scrutiny. Mitigants: formal **Human Rights Policy**, RBA-validated assessment (VAP) audits, Responsible Labor SAQs, country risk profiling, conflict-minerals (CMRT/RMI) due diligence, and a 2030 target for 75% of in-scope direct supplier facilities to hold valid audits.
- **Social licence/talent:** targets to upskill 70,000 students by 2031 and benefit 750,000 people via social-impact programmes; engagement/learning goals reset in 2025.
- **Conduct history:** the December 2018 resignation of CEO Martin Anstice over workplace-misconduct allegations is the single most material social incident in the company's history — it removed the CEO abruptly, drove a ~5.7% one-day share drop, and triggered board-led culture remediation under Archer. It remains the key reference point when assessing whether the board will act early on executive-conduct risk (evidence since 2018 is that it does).
- **Litigation (Violation Tracker, since 2000):** cumulative penalties ~**$1.85M**, dominated by a **$1.65M wage-and-hour** matter and a **$168k employment-discrimination** matter. Financially immaterial (<0.01% of revenue) but indicative of recurrent low-level employment-law friction in US manufacturing/field service.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL to the fundamental thesis, but a NEGATIVE TAIL-RISK on valuation**

- **E and G are not thesis drivers.** Environmental and governance factors do not move LRCX cash flows: carbon cost is <0.1% of revenue, no stranded assets, no ESG-linked debt, no activist or proxy contest, no fines above $2M. ESG screening will not exclude the name for most mandates; if anything, the leader-tier ratings widen the ESG-index buyer base.
- **The only ESG factor that matters is policy (export controls), and it is already the #1 driver of the drawdown** (shares ~52% off the high, –7% on China-curb headlines). Investors should treat "China export policy" as a governance/regulatory risk line item alongside the AI-cycle call, not as a separate ESG concern.
- **Quantified tail:** a repeat of the 2023 restriction produced $2–2.5B of annual revenue loss; a broad-ban scenario on 35% China revenue implies ~30% downside to the revenue base versus a $37B target. Any NDAA-embedded China provision in the current session is the key catalyst to monitor — LRCX is more exposed to a *consumption*-based China rule than ASML/TEL on the advanced-node side, but less exposed than AMAT on memory-heavy China.
- **Under-appreciated positive:** product-level energy/emissions efficiency (cryogenic etch, deposition uniformity) is becoming a customer purchasing criterion as fabs face their own Scope 1/2 caps. If carbon performance becomes a share-shift factor in WFE, Lam's SBTi-validated roadmap is a modest competitive asset rather than a cost centre.
- **Watch items:** (i) any BIS action naming a top-5 China customer; (ii) NDAA China-provision language; (iii) rare-earth/component supply commentary on the next earnings call; (iv) special-meeting-threshold proposal trajectory (repeat 30%+ support would signal rising governance friction); (v) first-year CSRD/UFLPA supply-chain findings from Malaysia.

**Bottom line: ESG-neutral for the investment case, ESG-negative as a tail-risk distribution. The rating is Leader; the risk is geopolitical, and it is already priced, in part.**
