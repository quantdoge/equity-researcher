**Price $185.01 (Nov 25, 2026) | Mkt cap $231B | Beta 2.01 | Down 57% from June peak**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Lam is the purest large-cap play on the **deposition + etch layer** of the AI compute buildout — the step where 3D NAND layer stacking, DRAM capacitor scaling, and advanced packaging are won or lost. After a brutal FY24 digestion year, the cycle inflected violently: Q1 FY27 revenue hit a record **$8.22B, +54% YoY**, with gross margin at **52.3%** and non-GAAP operating margin of **40.2%** — all-time highs. Management sized preliminary **2027 WFE at >$200B** (vs. ~$155B in 2026), led by DRAM. Yet the stock has been cut in half on a **one-quarter guidance shortfall caused by component shortages** plus a **bipartisan China export bill (MATCH Act)**. The thesis here is that a supply-chain timing problem is being priced as a demand problem — but with a genuine legislative tail risk that keeps this from being a table-pounding call.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **2026 WFE: ~$155B. 2027 preliminary: >$200B (+30%), led by DRAM.** SK hynix has publicly stated memory demand outruns supply *through 2030* — fabs take 2–3 years to build, so the order book is structurally visible.
- Lam's **served SAM** (deposition, etch, single-wafer clean/strip) is roughly **$58–62B of a $155B WFE market**, ~38% of total spend. Lam's systems revenue of ~$17–18B implies ~30% penetration of its served SAM — already high.
- **This is the critical caveat versus a classic Cathie Wood framework:** Lam's *share-gain* runway within etch/dep is narrowing, because they already own ~45–50% of conductor etch and a leading position in 3D NAND deposition. Incremental upside from here must come from **SAM expansion** (advanced packaging/HBM bonding, gate-all-around, new materials) and **WFE growth itself** — not from taking share in existing markets.
- **The installed-base annuity is the durable asset.** Lam's customer support/services business (~25% of revenue) grows with the cumulative installed tool base, is far less cyclical than systems, and is the piece that de-risks the trough. This is a genuine moat — 24,300 employees, decades of process recipes, and service relationships that don't churn.

Consensus implies FY31 revenue of $60.3B. Against a WFE market plausibly at $280–320B by then, that's ~19% WFE share — *below* Lam's current share. **Consensus is not asking for a heroic outcome, and the market at 19x forward is discounting something well below it.**

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q2 FY27 earnings — Jan 27, 2027.** The single most important event. Guide: **$8.6B ±$400M, adj. EPS $2.33**. Management attributed the soft guide to *component shortages*, with delayed shipments shifting into the March quarter. Watch: (a) whether those shipments are recovered, (b) China mix in the December quarter (expected to drop sharply from 35%), (c) FY27 2H framing. A clean beat-and-raise here re-rates the stock hard.
- **March 2027 quarter — the catch-up quarter.** If the ~$400M+ of deferred shipments lands as promised *and* 2027 WFE guidance firms to $200B+, estimates go up, not down. This is the highest-probability positive catalyst.
- **MATCH Act legislative path (H.R.8170, 119th Congress) — ongoing, binary.** Introduced Apr 2, 2026; cleared its first committee hurdle. Would ban immersion DUV and certain tool exports/servicing to China and force allied adoption. **The Netherlands is actively lobbying to kill it** (Reuters, Nov 25) — a genuine counterweight. Dutch/Japanese resistance is Lam's best hope; Japan's Tokyo Electron is aligned against it too.
- **Advanced packaging / HBM tool demand.** Lam has been pushing into hybrid bonding and advanced packaging — a new SAM that isn't yet in the WFE numbers and could be a 2027–28 estimate-revision driver.
- **Analyst PT resets post-China clarity.** With 20/21 at Buy and a $342 consensus target vs. a $185 stock, any reduction in legislative fear produces a violent squeeze. Heavy short-term volume (31M shares Nov 5 = 3x normal; 21M Nov 25) suggests crowded positioning has already been flushed.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY**

*(Not Strong Buy — the geopolitical tail is genuinely binary.)*

**The market has priced this as though the AI capex cycle has already peaked, and Lam's own numbers, orders, hiring, and end-market commentary say otherwise.** At $185 you are paying **19.2x FY27 and 15.3x FY28 earnings** for a business compounding revenue at 50%+ with 52% gross margins, 40% operating margins, a 47% Rule-of-40 score, a $200B+ WFE market inflecting upward on DRAM/HBM, and an installed-base services annuity underneath it. The 5-year average forward multiple is ~24x; you are 5 multiple turns below that, and the stock is trading at the *low end* of its 52-week range after a 57% drawdown.

**The asymmetry:** consensus targets cluster at **$290–$400** (Bernstein $350, Citi $435), conservative math to ~$291 requires only a return to 24x on FY28 EPS — **+57%**. Bear case at the Street's $127 low (15x on cycle-low EPS, MATCH Act enacted) is **−31%**. Roughly 1.8:1 upside-to-downside on a $185 entry with a clear January catalyst is a favorable setup.

**But I will not call it a Strong Buy, because the growth here is *cyclical*, not durable** — and a Cathie Wood framework must be honest about that distinction. The FY22–FY26 revenue CAGR was **9.7%** precisely because FY24 fell 14.5%. Lam is a superb operator inside a boom-bust industry, not a compounding platform. You are underwriting a 2–3 year AI capex window, not a decade.

**Sizing and tactics:** a half-position now, with dry powder for either (a) a fresh look after Jan 27 guidance, or (b) capitulation toward $150–160 on a MATCH Act headline. Do not chase above $230 without confirmation that the component-shortage catch-up actually landed. **Beta 2.01 — this is a volatile, high-conviction, position-sized bet, not a core holding.**

## 7. Key Risks

1. **MATCH Act / China export controls — the #1 risk.** China was **35% of September-quarter revenue** (~$2.9B annualized at current run-rate, ~$7B on an FY26 basis). The Act would restrict immersion DUV and servicing to China and force Dutch/Japanese alignment. If enacted in broad form, this permanently impairs a low-to-mid-teens share of revenue and accelerates China's domestic tooling self-sufficiency (Naura, AMEC) at US toolmakers' expense. The Netherlands' active opposition is the mitigation — but legislation risk here is one-directional and bipartisan. **Retrying dead-tool count: 0; this is a genuine, live, unresolved policy risk.**
2. **Component shortage = demand deferral in disguise.** Management says the $400M+ guide headwind is supply, recoverable in the March quarter. If the January print shows the shipments *didn't* return, the entire "temporary" framing collapses and FY27 estimates get cut hard from $9.66.
3. **AI capex digestion / customer concentration.** Results are levered to a handful of hyperscaler and memory budgets. The Oct 30 Intel weak guide and Amazon AI-spend concerns that triggered the sector selloff are the tell — **this stock trades on the AI-spend narrative, not on its own fundamentals.** A single major capex trim from a top-5 customer re-rates the whole group.
4. **Memory cycle risk.** The DRAM-led 2027 WFE ramp is the bull case, but memory capex is historically the most violent part of the cycle. If DRAM makers over-build into 2028, WFE could air-pocket exactly as the current order book clears — and Lam's multiple would compress against falling estimates (the FY24 −14.5% revenue year is the precedent).
5. **Peak-margin risk.** 52.3% gross margin and 40.2% operating margin are all-time records. At cycle-peak margins, a 19x P/E is not actually cheap — it's 19x on cyclically inflated earnings. Normalizing to a mid-cycle ~45% GM / ~30% OM on $35B revenue gets you to ~$8.50 EPS, or 22x today. **The "cheap" case is multiple-dependent, and multiple expansion is what died in June.**
6. **Beat cadence broken.** Seven straight beats, but the most recent at +0.9% and a guide cut relative to buy-side hopes. Earnings-driven momentum has reversed; the Jan 27 print is the swing factor.

### Bottom line
A high-quality, share-leading franchise with record margins and genuine AI-driven demand visibility, thrown into a bear market on a temporary supply execution issue *plus* a real legislative threat that could restructure its largest end-geography. **Buy at $185, sized for a 2.0-beta cyclical, with January 27 as the thesis-confirmation date.** If you believe the >$200B 2027 WFE number, you're being offered 19x forward on a 50%-growth business — and the market is only paying for the China risk, not the DRAM ramp.
