**Data vintage: 25–27 November 2026 | Price $185.01 | Mkt cap $231bn | Beta 2.01**

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: TRANSITIONAL → RISK-OFF for long-duration growth. A hawkish policy pivot inside an intact expansion.**

The regime has inverted versus 2025. Through 2025 the Fed was easing (4.64% Nov-24 → 3.63% Jun-26). That easing has now **stopped and reversed**: the Fed paused in October at a 3.75–4.00% target, and November FOMC minutes show *nearly all* officials expect a **25bp hike in December to 4.00–4.25%** (FedWatch ~95%). The 2-year at **4.74%** sits 86bp *above* the 3.88% effective funds rate — the market is pricing further tightening, not cuts.

The reason is inflation re-acceleration: headline CPI ran **3.72% YoY in September** (Aug 3.35%, Jul 3.30%), and the 3-month annualized pace is **~5.7% headline / ~3.75% core**. Core PCE printed **3.3%** (reported 25-Nov) — far from target.

Meanwhile the long end has repriced violently: the 10Y went from **4.44% (30-Jun) to 5.31% (5-Oct)**, and the **10Y real (TIPS) yield rose from 2.20% to 2.79% (+59bp)**. This is a **bear steepener driven by term premium and fiscal supply, not growth optimism** — the single most toxic backdrop for high-multiple, long-duration equities. Credit is *not* yet stressed (Baa–10Y spread 1.61%, +18bp off late-October lows but historically tight), and growth is genuinely strong (Q3 real GDP **+4.3% SAAR**; unemployment **4.1%**). This is not a recession signal — it is a **discount-rate and policy-uncertainty shock**, and that is precisely what has driven the AI/semiconductor complex into a correction (−$1.64T of market cap in six sessions; Nasdaq in correction since 6-Nov).

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Negative — Interest rates / discount rate (highest-magnitude channel).** LRCX's valuation is long-duration: ~most of its value sits in FY2028+ terminal cash flows, and the stock is a 2.01-beta instrument. The **+59bp move in real yields** mechanically compresses a 20x multiple toward ~17x on unchanged earnings — roughly a 15% de-rating, and the actual drawdown has been larger. Balance-sheet rate risk is *minimal*: **~$3.7bn net cash**, negligible refinancing exposure. So the rate hit is purely a multiple/discount-rate effect, not a credit effect. This is the correct way to read it — and it means the exposure is **reversible** if real yields fall.

**Negative — Policy/geopolitical: China + export controls + tariffs (highest-magnitude idiosyncratic channel).** China was **35% of September-quarter revenue**, and management guides that share **below 30%** into 2026. Fresh momentum on export-control legislation attached to the annual defense bill (catalysed by the Nvidia leadership scandal) has hit the whole equipment group, with LRCX down 7% on 25-Nov alone. Separately, the **January 2026 Section 232 proclamation imposes a 25% ad valorem duty on certain semiconductors *and semiconductor manufacturing equipment*** — a direct cost and demand friction for LRCX's own product shipments plus an input-cost channel via Asian-sourced components. This is the primary reason the stock has fallen **58% from its 30-June peak of $438.03 to $185.01** even as revenue grew **54% YoY to $8.22bn**.

**Negative — USD strength.** Broad dollar **+4.6% since June**. LRCX sells into the US, China, Korea, Taiwan, Japan and Europe — the large majority of revenue is non-US. A stronger dollar is a translation headwind and, more importantly, erodes LRCX's price competitiveness versus **Tokyo Electron** (JPY at 158.8, still historically weak, giving TEL a structural cost edge) and versus ASML.

**Positive — Cyclical demand / capex supercycle (the offsetting force).** This is where macro is a genuine tailwind. WFE is in a memory/HBM-led upcycle: management guides **2026 WFE at mid-$150bn and 2027 above $200bn**; SEMI forecasts record **$156bn** equipment sales in 2027; Gartner sees WFE **+29.4% in 2026 and +16.5% in 2027**. Q3 GDP at 4.3% and 4.1% unemployment underwrite that capex. December guidance implies **+61% YoY revenue growth** with 40% operating margins. Strong nominal GDP growth and AI-driven capex are powerful earnings tailwinds — but they arrive *while* the multiple compresses, which is why the shares have fallen on rising estimates.

**Neutral-to-positive — Inflation on costs.** Input-cost and wage inflation (core CPI 2.6%, core PCE 3.3%) is manageable given 40% gross/operating margins and genuine pricing power in a supply-constrained equipment market ("supply limits, not demand" is now management's framing). LRCX is a price-setter here, not a price-taker.

**Net:** Four of six macro channels are negative and they operate on the multiple; one is strongly positive and operates on the earnings. Given a 2.01 beta and an AI-sentiment shock, the multiple is currently winning.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**
*(Macro-driven, not company-specific. The operating backdrop is favourable; the financial backdrop is hostile.)*

Real yields +59bp, a Fed pivoting to hikes, a +4.6% dollar, 25% equipment tariffs, and active export-control legislation all compress the multiple simultaneously. The favourable WFE cycle does not offset these in the price today. **Valuation context:** at **20.3x FY2027 EPS versus a 5-year average of 25.4x**, the market is already discounting meaningful macro damage — which is why the risk rating is Unfavourable but not extreme.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise: **A Policy Error to the Downside**

Consensus assumes the December hike is a single "insurance" move, inflation rolls over in H1 2027 on base effects, real yields retreat, and the >$200bn 2027 WFE cycle re-rates the stock. **The underappreciated risk is that the Fed hikes in December into a decelerating economy whose marginal growth driver is a single, sentiment-fragile AI capex cycle.**

Mechanism: headline CPI at ~5.7% 3-month annualized forces the December hike *and* a hawkish dot plot → the 10Y breaks **5.5%** and the 10Y real yield goes to **3.2%+** → credit spreads widen from 1.61% toward 2.5% → AI capex is re-scoped for H2 2027 exactly as memory customers digest the HBM build → export-control legislation lands in the NDAA removing another 10–15 points of China revenue. In that world LRCX's multiple compresses to 14–15x on *falling* FY2028 estimates, implying **$120–135** (another −27% to −35%). The tell to watch is **Baa spreads above 2.0%** combined with **10Y real yields above 3.0%** — both currently just below those thresholds.

Symmetrically, the upside surprise: if December is "one-and-done," real yields fall back to 2.3%, and 2027 WFE exceeds $200bn, a re-rating from 20.3x to 25x on rising EPS supports **$300+**. **The asymmetry is now favourable for patient capital — but the macro trigger (real yields peaking) has not yet fired.**
