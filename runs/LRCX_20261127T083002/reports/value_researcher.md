# 1. Business Quality — Moat: WIDE (and widening)

Lam is one of four companies on earth that can sell a leading-edge deposition or etch module (Lam, Applied Materials, Tokyo Electron, and ASML in adjacent litho). Etch and deposition are ~25-30% of wafer-front-end capital intensity and are becoming *more* critical, not less, as device architectures go 3D (GAA, 3D NAND strings >200 layers, advanced packaging/HBM).

**Evidence of the moat:**
- **Switching costs:** a tool is qualified into a customer's process flow, recipe by recipe. Requalifying is a 12-18 month yield risk at a fab that costs $15-20B. This is the single best form of pricing power in industrials.
- **Installed base annuity:** customer support (spares, upgrades, service) is the least cyclical part of the P&L and scales with the ~50,000+ chamber installed base, not with new orders.
- **Pricing power, demonstrated:** gross margin **44.6% (FY23) → 47.3% (FY24) → 48.7% (FY25) → 50.5% (FY26)**. Operating margin **29.7% → 35.3%** over the same span. A commodity supplier cannot expand gross margin 590bps through a cyclical downturn and recovery. That is the moat, quantified and *widening*.
