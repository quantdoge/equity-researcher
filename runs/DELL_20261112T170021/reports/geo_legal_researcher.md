## 1. Geographic Risk Map

Dell operates in **170+ countries** (FY2026 10-K, filed Mar 16, 2026). Per the FY2026 shareholder letter/proxy, **~45% of net revenue came from outside the US in FY2026 (vs. ~50% in FY2025)** — a meaningful *de-risking* driven by the US AI-server ramp. FY2026 revenue: **$113.5B (+19%)**; FY2027 is running far hotter (Q1 $43.8B, +88%; Q2 $46.97B), with an **AI-server backlog of ~$95B**.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US data-center political backlash (Texas-centric) — **HIGH probability / MEDIUM-HIGH impact**
- Texas **Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa** (upset winner Nov 3, 2026) has **pledged laws restricting data-center development**, shielding citizens, and freezing electricity rates (Reuters, 2026-11-06; Anadolu, 2026-11-06).
- Reporting suggests **~13 GW of Texas data-center capacity "at immediate risk"** and that a **moratorium or its threat is enough** to change developer behavior (NBC DFW; Yahoo Finance).
- National context is hostile: post-midterm "anti-data-center wave" (CNN 2026-11-05); Reuters/Ipsos polling cited at **84% of US voters view AI as a threat to jobs** (Forbes, 2026-11-06).
- **Transmission channel:** ~55% US revenue concentration + AI-server backlog + Texas HQ/roots (Round Rock) = Dell is a second-derivative victim of hyperscaler/neocloud buildout delays. Press attributes part of the **~40% one-month drawdown** to "a Texas political threat" (24/7 Wall St., 2026-11-11).
- **Assessment:** Policy action is already signaled (high probability); the impact is indirect but large because AI-server demand is the entire equity narrative.

### Risk 2 — US–China tech decoupling & export-control whiplash — **MEDIUM-HIGH probability / MEDIUM impact**
- China's **rare-earth export controls** (Oct 2025) directly hit enterprise IT hardware supply chains; China **extended its suspension on Nov 9, 2026** as part of a détente, and BIS extended suspension of its **"Affiliates Rule"** (JD Supra / BIS). This is a *reversible pause*, not a resolution.
- Beijing has **activated its 2021 blocking statutes** against US sanctions (escalation optionality) and vowed to retaliate against US sanctions on Chinese firms (Bloomberg, 2026-11-07).
- **Nvidia leadership/spying scandal** involving Jensen Huang's lobbying on export controls creates headline risk of *tightening* US controls on advanced AI silicon — a direct constraint on Dell's AI-server allocation and addressable market (WSJ, 2026-11-09/11).
- China localization ("secure and controllable") and Huawei competition structurally erode Dell's China franchise. **Note:** confirmed the 45% non-US mix is *falling*, so relative China exposure is shrinking — a positive.

### Risk 3 — Tariffs, component concentration & GPU dependency — **HIGH probability / MEDIUM impact**
- Dell discloses reliance on **three largest contract manufacturers** and a **single dominant GPU supplier**; component cost inflation (memory/storage) and tariff pass-through are margin risks (10-K concentration disclosures).
- US computer/accessory/semiconductor imports running at a **~$1.04T annualized pace** (Sept 2026 data) — squarely in the tariff crosshairs; earlier trade-war episodes included **100% tariff threats on China-bound exports**.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Corporate law — redomiciliation to Texas (July 1, 2026).** Shareholders approved conversion from Delaware to a Texas corporation; Class C shares continue on NYSE. The Texas charter reportedly adopts a **3% ownership threshold for derivative proceedings** plus a Texas forum bylaw (Bloomberg Law). This **materially narrows shareholder-plaintiff exposure** but invites proxy-advisor/governance scrutiny — a favorable legal-risk development, with reputational cost.

**Litigation:**
- **ERISA class action:** *Lowbruck et al. v. Dell Technologies Inc., et al.*, filed **Jan 28, 2026** — challenges retention of "underperforming" investment options in a ~**$15B 401(k) plan** (PSCA, Feb 2026; SECfilingAI extract). Modest but recurring exposure; the same playbook has hit dozens of large plans.
- **Legacy Dell Class V / DVMT matter:** Delaware Chancery granted final approval to a **record $1 billion recovery for Dell Class V stockholders** (Robbins Geller). Historical, but confirms willingness of plaintiffs' bar to pursue Dell on structural transactions.
- **Consumer/product litigation:** recurring laptop-battery and consumer-protection class actions (classaction.org docket).
- **Antitrust:** Dell has appeared as a *plaintiff* (tag-along LCD price-fixing claims per The American Lawyer) rather than a defendant — no material antitrust defendant exposure identified.
- **Ordinary course:** 10-K states the company is involved in "various claims, suits, investigations, and legal proceedings" arising in the ordinary course, with no material accrual disclosed in accessible excerpts.

**Regulatory perimeter:** EU AI Act + EU Data Act/data-sovereignty rules (affects ISG positioning), GDPR/PIPL (services & support data), US federal AI/HPC procurement standards (NIST-funded deals, e.g., University of Alabama HPC center), BIS EAR deemed-export/re-export rules on advanced compute into 170+ countries, and **Pillar Two** global minimum tax (Irish/Singapore structures).

**Labor:** $0.2B severance in Q1 FY2027 and continuing headcount reductions (10-Q, June 2026) — WARN Act / EU collective-consultation exposure at the margins.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale.** Dell's *legal* risk is genuinely below-average for a $237B-market-cap multinational: no identified government investigation, ordinary-course litigation only, a modest ERISA suit, and a Texas redomiciliation that deliberately reduces derivative-suit exposure. Its *geopolitical* risk is the binding constraint — not through foreign exposure (which is shrinking, from 50% to 45% non-US) but through a **new domestic political risk vector**: the post-midterm, Texas-led backlash against AI data centers, layered on an AI-server backlog that is the stock's entire thesis (~40% drawdown in a month already discounts some of this).

**What would move the rating:** To **High** — enactment of a Texas data-center moratorium, or a BIS action restricting Dell's GPU supply following the Nvidia export-control imbroglio. To **Low** — a China trade truce that becomes permanent (rare earths + Affiliates Rule), plus a Texas administration that settles for rate-review rather than moratorium.
