## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT — but cleanly split, not randomly**

The disagreement is not about the company; it is about the **price**. Three consensuses exist simultaneously:

**Consensus #1 — Business quality is high (HIGH agreement: Sector, Growth, Value, Short, Alt Data).** Even the Short Analyst concedes "Rule of 40 genuinely satisfied," "balance sheet fortress-grade," "accounting is clean — anyone calling Rubrik a fraud is wrong." No agent found fraud, channel stuffing, restatement, or solvency risk. *This is resolved and not in dispute.*

**Consensus #2 — The price is not supported (HIGH agreement: Value, Short, Macro, Quant, Fin Risk).** Four independent methods converge on the same zone: Value's prob-weighted DCF **$88**; my own cross-check — **8x FY28E sales ($2.06B) + $0.54B net cash ÷ 196.5M shares = $86.5**; Short's bear **$75**; Macro's real-yield de-rating case **$65–75**. Four different frameworks, one answer: intrinsic value is **$75–90, i.e. 18–32% below spot.** At $110 the stock is **12.5x FY27E and 10.2x FY28E sales** against consensus growth fading to +21.7% (FY28) and +15.6% (FY31).

**Split #3 — Near-term direction (LOW agreement).** Growth and Alt Data say the 30-Nov print beats; Short says "a clean beat can still de-rate" and cites 27-Aug, when RBRK beat revenue by 7.8% and **fell 13% in one session**. Both are right. That is the trap.

**Conflict resolution:** Where the bull and bear cases address different variables, I weight them by durability. The bullish evidence is *quarterly and mean-reverting* (estimate staleness, beat cadence, product-launch news flow). The bearish evidence is *structural and slow-moving* (10Y real yield +69bp, NRR −1,100bp, SBC > FCF, decelerating ARR). Per the firm's hierarchy, the structural constraint dominates position sizing; the catalyst only affects *timing*.

**Net conviction: LOW.** This is a high-quality franchise at a full-to-excessive price, seven days before a binary event, in the worst macro mix for its factor profile.

**Factor exposure — actively harmful.** This single name adds: **high beta (1.79 regression, 1.65 quant, 2x index in the 5-Nov selloff)**, **high volatility (66.6%)**, **negative value (P/E −61.8, EV/EBITDA −77.4, P/FCF 85.3, negative book −$2.64/sh)**, **negative profitability (owner earnings −$76M)**, and **positive momentum (+24.2% 12-1)**. **Rate/factor correlation — the hidden overlap.** At a 1.5% FCF yield with ~85–90% of intrinsic value in 2031+ cash flows, RBRK is a **long-duration bond proxy**. In a book already holding high-multiple software or AI infrastructure, this is the same real-yield exposure at higher beta. The 10Y real yield at 2.89% (+69bp since June) is the single driver most likely to compress the position, and it is *not* diversifying anything.

**The apparent offset is illusory.** SPY correlation of only 0.376 (R² 0.128) looks like genuine diversification, but on a 1-year post-IPO sample with a single regime, that is estimation noise — and the practical evidence contradicts it: RBRK fell 11.2% on 5-Nov vs Nasdaq −5.5%.

**Credit is the one genuine strength.** $1.13B of **0.00% converts due June 2030** (conversion $124.76), $1.68B cash, no short-term debt, and a −20% revenue stress case still leaves FCF of ~+$118M. **There is no solvency catalyst on either side.** This also means the bear case is purely an equity-multiple event, reinforcing that position sizing — not credit work — is where the risk is managed.

**Verdict: portfolio fit is poor on every axis except idiosyncratic enterprise-software exposure, which is already available at better prices.** Recommended bucket: cap total "high-multiple unprofitable software" at 6–8% of book; RBRK ≤0.75% within it.
