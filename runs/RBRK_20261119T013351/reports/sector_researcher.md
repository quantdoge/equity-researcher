# Sector Brief — Rubrik, Inc. (NYSE: RBRK)
**Sector:** Technology → Software – Infrastructure (sub-industry: cyber resilience / data protection & security)
**Fiscal reference:** RBRK FY ends 31-Jan; latest reported FY2026 (Jan-26) plus Q1–Q2 FY2027

---

## 1. Sector Overview

Rubrik competes at the intersection of two adjacent markets: **backup & data protection software** (roughly $18–22bn, growing high-single to low-double digits) and **information security** (Gartner: **$244bn end-user spending in 2026, +11.6% cc**). The defining dynamic is that the legacy "backup" category is being re-priced as a **security** category — ransomware recovery, immutable air-gapped copies, and cleanroom restore are now board-level mandates, which supports premium pricing and gross margins in the high-70s/low-80s. The vendor landscape is **moderately concentrated** (estimated HHI ~1,200–1,400; top-3 share ~45–50%) across Veeam, Rubrik, Cohesity/Veritas (merged), Dell PowerProtect, Commvault and Druva — with the two largest by seat count (Veeam, Cohesity) **private**, which limits pure-play benchmarking. Structurally the sector is in a **consolidation/mature-growth phase**, where budget growth is being concentrated into fewer, larger platform vendors.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Security is the most defensive line in the IT budget.** Security spend is growing ~12% while total technology spending growth decelerated to **3.6% in Spring 2026** (ETR, down 100bps from 4.6% in Winter). Gartner's 2026 infosec forecast of $244bn (+11.6% cc) implies security outgrowing the overall software market by roughly 3–4x — the single most important demand backdrop for Rubrik.
- **Regulation converts backup from "nice-to-have" to a compliance requirement.** DORA (financial services operational resilience), NIS2, and SEC material-incident disclosure rules force documented recovery capability and tested immutability — a spec that legacy tape/backup architectures cannot evidence, accelerating refresh cycles.
- **Cloud/SaaS data sprawl and the AI data layer expand TAM.** Rubrik's recent releases (Azure Cosmos DB, Google BigQuery/Cloud Storage, GitHub & Azure DevOps cyber recovery, AWS Autonomous Business Recovery) push protection into AI application data and code pipelines — a new, largely unpenetrated data estate with the same immutability requirement.

---

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Platformization by larger, better-capitalised peers.** Palo Alto Networks (post-CyberArk, FY26 revenue $11.5bn), CrowdStrike ($4.8bn) and Microsoft are all pushing consolidated security platforms; CIOs are actively cutting point tools. Buyer power and vendor-consolidation pressure are rising, and Rubrik is the smaller party in most of those negotiations.
- **Growth deceleration and non-recurring revenue roll-off.** FY26 growth of **48.5%** was flattered by ~**$68m of one-time "material rights" revenue** (~7.7pp of growth); normalised growth was ~38–40%, in line with the 38–39% reported in Q1/Q2 FY27. Management's FY27 guide of **$1.685–1.693bn implies only ~28% growth**, so the exit rate is materially below the headline. Consensus multiple support depends on that landing near 25%+ rather than high-teens.
- **Hyperscaler-native and hardware-bundled substitution.** AWS Backup, Azure Backup and Dell PowerProtect (bundled into server/storage refresh economics) compress pricing at the low end, while GAAP profitability remains negative (**-26.2% FY26 op margin**), leaving little cushion if pricing pressure intensifies. Share count has gone from 154m to 205m in two years — SBC dilution of ~30% is a real cost even where non-GAAP margins look healthy.
- *(Additional)* Rising compute/storage costs from AI-driven infrastructure inflation (Gartner: AI infrastructure spend +53% in 2027 to $2.4tn) siphons budget away from non-AI software and raises Rubrik's own cloud COGS.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

**Rationale:**
- **Growth leadership is unambiguous.** 38–39% underlying growth vs a 21.7% peer median and 11–19% for the direct data-protection competitor (Commvault) — Rubrik is taking share, not riding a rising tide. Subscription ARR of **$1.66bn** with **net revenue retention >119%** indicates expansion revenue well above the ~110–115% typical of mature infrastructure software.
- **Gross margin of 80.1%** sits at the top of the comp set and has expanded ~1,800bps in two years, evidence of SaaS mix shift and pricing power rather than discounting — a strong signal in a market where hyperscaler bundling is a live threat.
- **Category positioning is defensible.** Rubrik is a Leader in Gartner's Backup & Data Protection Platforms MQ alongside Veeam, Commvault, Cohesity and Dell, and differentiates on **cyber recovery** (immutable air-gapped copies, cleanroom, blast-radius investigation) — a capability pure backup vendors (Veeam, Commvault) under-index on and pure endpoint/network security vendors (CRWD, ZS) do not cover natively. That positioning lets it be sold out of the security budget, which is the faster-growing pool.
- **Scale is the constraint.** At ~$1.3bn revenue Rubrik is 1/9th of PANW and is exposed to vendor-consolidation mandates that favour incumbents with broader suites; it is also dependent on public-cloud partners that are simultaneously competitors.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

*(Sub-sector: cyber resilience / data protection. Broad "Software – Infrastructure" would be Neutral, given ~3.6% aggregate tech budget growth and AI capex crowding out non-AI software.)*

**Justification:** ~12% mandated budget growth, regulation-driven refresh cycles, a consolidating vendor field where the top-3 are all share donors or neutrals, and gross margins structurally in the high 70s make this one of the better secular growth pockets in software. The sector is in **mid-cycle expansion**, not late cycle — penetration of cloud-native data protection is still well under half of enterprise data estates.

**Key risks to the rating:** (1) growth deceleration to the guided ~28% would compress the sector's premium multiple even with stable fundamentals; (2) platform bundling by PANW/Microsoft could cap pricing power faster than expected; (3) an IT budget recession would hit cyber later than other software, but not never.

**Data gaps:** Veeam, Cohesity/Veritas and Druva are private — share and margin data are estimates, not reported. Peer valuation multiples and consensus forward estimates were not available from the feed and are excluded; all peer comparisons are backward-looking reported GAAP figures on non-aligned fiscal year-ends.
