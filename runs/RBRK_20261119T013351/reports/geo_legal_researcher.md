## RBRK — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company:** Rubrik, Inc. (NYSE: RBRK) · Palo Alto, CA · CIK 0001943896 · Software – Infrastructure (data security / cyber recovery) · FY ends Jan 31 · 3,797 FTE · mkt cap ≈$22.6B · IPO 2024-04-25.

**Bottom line:** Rubrik is a *jurisdictionally simple* company — a US-domiciled, Americas-dominant software vendor with **no disclosed operations in Russia, China, or other sanctioned/restricted markets** and **no active government investigation or securities class action that I could identify**. Its geopolitical risk is therefore not "sanctions exposure" but (a) concentration in the US public sector and (b) the compliance cost of European data-sovereignty rules as it pushes EMEA/APAC growth. The most under-appreciated risk is that Rubrik is itself a Tier-1 target for state-linked actors.

**Reading the map:** the single most important geographic fact is that Rubrik's risk is *domestic*. There is no emerging-market currency-control exposure, no expropriation exposure, and no meaningful sanctioned-jurisdiction revenue to lose. What replaces it is dependency on the US federal budget cycle and on US cloud hyperscalers (AWS, Azure, GCP) — Rubrik's entire product architecture and November 2026 product launches are built on those three platforms, so US–China tech decoupling that harms hyperscaler cloud growth is a second-order risk to Rubrik's TAM.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US public-sector budget and procurement politics (Probability: Medium-High · Impact: Medium)**
Rubrik's federal/SLED business runs through FedRAMP/StateRAMP authorisations and Carahsoft, with a dedicated Public Sector Summit and a `Rubrik Security Cloud for Government` SKU. US federal IT budgets are in a fiscal-consolidation and continuing-resolution cycle (debt-limit pressure flagged for February 2027 by the Bipartisan Policy Center, per NYT, 18 Nov 2026). Procurement disruption — a government shutdown, a re-scoped cyber budget, or a shift in FedRAMP programme rules (FedRAMP is itself re-baselining impact levels through 31 Dec 2026) — would hit deal timing and, more importantly, force re-certification spend. This is a timing/revenue risk, not a solvency risk.

**Risk 2 — European data-sovereignty and cyber-regulation fragmentation (Probability: High · Impact: Medium)**
Rubrik is expanding in EMEA and must simultaneously comply with GDPR transfer limits, NIS2 (penalties to €10M or 2% of global revenue for in-scope customers), DORA for financial-services customers, and the EU Data Act. Rubrik has turned this into a marketing asset — dedicated NIS2 critical-infrastructure, DORA financial-services, and "Sovereign" pages promising "complete data sovereignty… without foreign dependencies." That framing cuts both ways: it concedes that customers (and regulators) may demand EU-resident, EU-controlled infrastructure. Building and maintaining sovereign/regional cloud tenancies is a structural margin drag on EMEA, and it creates an opening for EU-headquartered competitors. Watch for any EU-public-sector tender that excludes non-EU-controlled vendors.

**Risk 3 — Rubrik as a state-actor target / supply-chain compromise (Probability: Low-Medium · Impact: High)**
This is the tail risk that matters most for a security vendor. Rubrik holds immutable backup copies of its customers' most critical data — precisely the asset ransomware operators and state actors most want to destroy or extort. Rubrik carries a dedicated "Cybersecurity Risk Management and Strategy Disclosure" section in its FY2026 10-K, consistent with the SEC's cybersecurity disclosure rules. A breach or a defect enabling an attacker to delete backups would trigger: (i) securities class litigation, (ii) multi-jurisdiction regulatory action under GDPR/NIS2/DORA, (iii) contractual and customer-loss liability, and (iv) existential brand damage. No such incident is known — this is a forward risk, not an existing event.

*De-prioritised:* Russia/Belarus sanctions exit and China exposure are effectively nil; the residual channel-diversion risk (a reseller moving product into a restricted country) is real for any US software vendor but is typical-course, not elevated.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active litigation:** No material pending litigation disclosed in the filings I could access. The patent history is clean and closed:
- **RealTime Data v. Rubrik** (D. Del. 1:18-cv-01198, 4 patents) — filed Aug 2018, **closed Nov 2018**.
- **Commvault Systems v. Rubrik** (W.D. Tex. 6:21-cv-00634) — filed Jun 2021, **closed Aug 2021**.
- **Actifio v. Rubrik** — four backup-technology patents; Gibson Dunn (Rubrik's counsel) describes obtaining "a highly favorable settlement."
- Rubrik's S-1 confirms "patent disputes with two of our competitors which have since been resolved."

**Government investigations:** None identified. No DOJ, SEC, FTC, or EU/UK enforcement action found in news or filing metadata. Filing activity in the last 12 months is routine (Form 4 insider filings; no 8-K Item 1.05 cyber incident, no restatement flag — the 10-K's "Financial Statement Error Correction Flag" is `false`).

**Regulators to monitor:**
- **US:** FedRAMP PMO (authorisation continuity), CISA/SEC cyber-disclosure regime, and — given the Carahsoft federal channel — GSA schedule and FAR/DFARS supply-chain rules.
- **EU/UK:** national NIS2 competent authorities; EU financial supervisors under DORA; GDPR supervisory authorities (Rubrik acts as a processor for customer data → DPA and sub-processor obligations); ICO.
- **Patent/PAE:** Rubrik is a signatory to BSA's March 2026 House Judiciary comments on patent-assertion-entity reform — evidence the company sees PAE litigation as a live cost of doing business, not a hypothetical.

**Peer/industry regulatory posture:** Cybersecurity is in a *regulatory tailwind* phase — NIS2, DORA, SEC cyber disclosure, and sector rules mandate exactly the resilience Rubrik sells. The regulatory environment is currently an asset, not a liability, for this specific business model.

**Insider activity (not legal risk, but flagged):** CTO Arvind Nithrakashyap sold 12,820 shares twice in early November 2026 (~$1.50M and ~$1.51M, per Defense World). Consistent with post-IPO diversification; no 10b5-1 controversy identified.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

| Area | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Sanctions / export controls (EAR, OFAC)** | **Low.** Rubrik mitigates by contract: its Export & Availability policy shifts compliance onto customers and restricts availability by jurisdiction. Residual risk is reseller/Channel diversion into Russia, Iran, or China — the exact theory behind recent DOJ export-diversion prosecutions. Recommend confirming the 10-K states no known diversion. |
| **FCPA / UK Bribery Act** | **Low-Medium.** International growth is channel-led across EMEA/APAC and the Middle East, which is where third-party intermediary risk concentrates. No disclosed FCPA matter. This is the one compliance area where rising international mix mechanically raises exposure. |
| **Data privacy (GDPR / UK GDPR / CCPA)** | **Medium.** Rubrik is a processor of customers' most sensitive backup data; GDPR transfer mechanics and sub-processor disclosure are structural, ongoing obligations. Breach-notification clocks under NIS2/DORA for its regulated customers flow through to Rubrik contractually. |
| **SEC cyber disclosure (Item 1.05 / Reg S-K 106)** | **Watch item.** Any material incident must be disclosed within four business days — a legal event, not just an operational one. |
| **AI governance (EU AI Act)** | **Low, rising.** Rubrik is marketing AI-driven anomaly detection / "Security and AI Operations" (Rubrik Announced at Microsoft Ignite, Nov 2026). Automated decisioning in security tooling will attract AI Act transparency obligations over time. |

**No compliance red flags rising to a reportable concern.** The profile is that of a well-counselled, newly public US software company.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# LOW–MEDIUM (carry as **Low**)

**Rationale:** Zero sanctioned-market revenue, no expropriation or currency-repatriation exposure, no active investigations, and a closed litigation history. The factors that keep it from a pure "Low" are (i) revenue concentration in the politically-exposed US public sector and dependency on FedRAMP continuity, (ii) a high-value cyber-target profile with tail risk asymmetric to the upside of any normal legal reserve, and (iii) a single-country operational concentration in India for R&D (>1,000 staff). None of these is currently crystallising.

**What would change the rating:** a reported breach or backup-integrity failure (→ High/Very High); a formal DOJ/SEC inquiry; an EU public-procurement exclusion or NIS2/DORA enforcement action naming Rubrik; or US federal budget action that materially defunds cyber procurement.
