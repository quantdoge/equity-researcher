## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (operational: Low; supply chain: High)

**Reported metrics (FY2026 Sustainability Report, third-party limited assurance):**
- Scope 1: **9,822 tCO₂e** (negligible)
- Scope 2 (market-based): **0** — 100% of global electricity matched with clean electricity
- Scope 3: **10,700,940 tCO₂e**, up from **3,638,432** in FY2024 — **nearly tripled in two years**; **>99% of total footprint** (Greenpeace East Asia, 12 Jun 2026)

**Read-across:** Absolute Scope 3 is large but revenue-normalised intensity is very low for a hardware manufacturer (roughly low-double-digit tCO₂e per $1m revenue vs. hundreds for industrial peers) — because revenue is enormous, not because manufacturing is clean. The material fact is the *trajectory*: emissions are a direct function of GPU unit volume, and volume is compounding at >70%.

**Targets (SBTi-validated):** −50% absolute Scope 1+2 by FY2030 (easy — already near zero market-based); −75% Scope 3 *intensity per sold GPU* by FY2030. **The intensity formulation permits unlimited absolute growth** — a structural weakness in the target architecture that investors have flagged.

**Gap identified:** No disclosed supplier renewable-energy procurement (PPAs/investments). Greenpeace estimates only ~25% renewable electricity across NVIDIA's top East Asian suppliers, with 500+ ecosystem partners in Taiwan on fossil-heavy grids. NVIDIA says it engaged suppliers covering >80% of Scope 3 Cat-1 in FY25. Directionally adequate disclosure, weak abatement.

**Physical climate:** Fabless model transfers physical risk to counterparties (TSMC, SK hynix) — but concentration in Taiwan (water stress, seismic, typhoon, geopolitical) means a supply interruption is unhedged. Liquid-cooling closed-loop designs reduce customer water intensity; that is a commercial advantage, not a risk offset.

**Environmental controversies:** None material (no spills, no remediation liabilities, no environmental fines found).

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Supply-chain labour: High relevance, moderate evidence.** FY26 forced-labour/human-trafficking/child-labour statement published; regular supplier audits claimed. But the electronics contract-manufacturing footprint (Taiwan, Malaysia, SE Asia) carries well-documented migrant-worker recruitment-fee risk; no public audit-failure data.
- **Diversity: disclosure gap.** NVIDIA has not published EEO-1-style consolidated workforce composition (subject of a PRI collaborative engagement). Diversity claims are unverifiable — treat as an information risk for ESG-screened mandates, not a cash-flow risk.
- **Workforce:** headcount ~47.8k (2026) vs ~30k pre-AI boom — a 60% expansion in two years. Attrition and culture dilution are the under-appreciated execution risk in a company whose moat is software/hardware co-design talent.
- **Product/consumer safety: reclassified upward.** The core "product" risk is not injury but **diversion and misuse** of export-controlled accelerators — a social/product-governance issue now materialising in enforcement (see §5).
- **Community/social licence:** Strong in the US (national-champion status); deteriorating in China-adjacent markets.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10** (trajectory: was 6, now impaired; reform possible)

**Structural positives (the "box-checking" layer):**
- **Single class of stock, one share one vote** — no dual-class entrenchment
- All directors independent except the founder-CEO; **independent lead director** (Stephen Neal)
- Proxy access bylaw (3% / 3 years); annual say-on-pay
- Board has now acted decisively post-crisis: appointed **Harvey Jones (ex-DuPont CEO) as Chair**, named Colette Kress interim CEO, publicly ruled out reinstatement, and created a **National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee** (17 Nov 2026)

**Why the score is 4, not 7:**
1. **Oversight failed in substance, not form.** The Yale/Sonnenfeld critique is correct: ISS and Glass Lewis treated NVIDIA as a governance showpiece while the board missed a CEO conduct/national-security failure of the highest order. Checklist compliance ≠ diligence.
2. **Reactive, not proactive.** On 8 Nov the board issued a statement expressing "confidence in Jensen"; four days later he had resigned. The sequence suggests the outcome was forced by external revelation, not internal detection.
3. **Key-man dependence crystallised.** Founder-CEO with no succession plan; beta 2.22 reflects an operating model priced as an individual.
4. **Compensation and capital-allocation continuity** unproven under an interim CEO guiding a ~$390B supply commitment.
5. **Disclosure posture:** board recommended against a shareholder proposal seeking enhanced GHG/Scope 3 disclosure (SEC Rule 14a-6 solicitation, 2026); three shareholder proposals failed at the 24 Jun 2026 AGM and say-on-pay support was ~87% — below the 90% threshold that signals investor unease.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US export controls (BIS) — Critical.** "AI Diffusion 2.0" interim final rule (Nov 2026) imposes new licensing on advanced GPUs supplied to overseas data centres serving China; H200 China sales resumed only in Jan 2026; Commerce approved 879,000 NVIDIA/AMD chips in six months, some to entities on Pentagon lists. China data-centre revenue — historically ~20–25% of the segment — is now a policy variable, not a commercial one. **This is NVIDIA's largest single regulatory exposure and is properly classified as a governance/compliance issue.**
- **California SB 253/261 — High (and imminent).** HQ in Santa Clara, revenue >$1bn: mandatory Scope 1/2/3 disclosure. Directly explains investor pressure on the Scope 3 tripling.
- **EU CSRD — Medium.** Non-EU parent with substantial EU turnover: reporting obligations phase in for FY2028 (published 2029). NVIDIA already reports most required content, so incremental cost is low; the EU AI Act GPAI transparency obligations (models such as Nemotron) add product-level disclosure duties under Aug 2026 enforcement.
- **EU CBAM — Low.** No carbon-intensive goods imported directly; indirect pressure only via customer/supplier Scope 3 accounting.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants — None identified.** Balance sheet is net-cash generative; no sustainability-linked instruments.
- **SEC climate rule — Stayed/immaterial** relative to SB 253.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD (governance-driven ESG Risk)**

Not a laggard on environmental operations (100% renewable electricity, assured Scope 1/2, SBTi targets) and not a laggard on labour policy documentation. It is a laggard on the factors that actually move the valuation: **board effectiveness, key-man risk, compliance/ethics infrastructure, and regulatory conduct** — compounded by an active, multi-agency enforcement cluster and an unresolved antitrust theory against a ~90% share position. Third-party ESG scores (MSCI/Sustainalytics) were not retrievable in this run — expect them to lag the facts materially; do not rely on them.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE**

ESG factors now subtract from the thesis, and the channel is **cost of capital and policy risk, not demand**:

1. **Governance discount is real and durable.** Interim-CEO leadership through a $390B supply commitment, an unfilled permanent CEO search, and a $2.4T drawdown. Expect a 5–10% multiple haircut until a permanent CEO is named and the board's investigation closes cleanly.
2. **Compliance overhang is unquantifiable in a way the market dislikes.** Four concurrent government inquiries (SEC, DOJ, California AG, Congress) with a December export hearing. Skadden-style rule changes create *licence* risk, not just fine risk.
3. **Antitrust is the forgotten ESG factor.** A conduct/structural case against a 90% share is the largest single downside scenario in the name and is currently being analysed as "regulation," not "ESG." Our view: it belongs in the ESG risk register.
4. **E and S are neutral-to-slightly-positive** and should not be sold as a differentiator while Scope 3 nearly tripled and supplier renewables sit at ~25%.
5. **Watch items that would move us to Neutral:** permanent CEO appointment with external credibility; clean conclusion of the independent investigation; settlement/closure of the California AG and SEC matters; adoption of an *absolute* supplier-emissions or supplier-renewables target at the FY2027 report; say-on-pay back above 90%.

**Bottom line for the investment memo:** treat NVDA as carrying a **structural governance risk premium** that was invisible in ESG screens twelve months ago. The environmental and social profile is investable; the governance profile is not, at current disclosure. Downweight on ESG grounds — and note that no ESG data provider in the market has yet repriced this.
