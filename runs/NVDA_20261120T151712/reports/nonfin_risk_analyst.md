# NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — NVIDIA CORPORATION (NASDAQ: NVDA)

**Date of assessment:** November 20, 2026 | **Price:** $140.92 | **Market cap:** ~$3.37T | **Sector:** Technology / Semiconductors | **Beta:** 2.22

**Material development framing:** This assessment is dominated by a live, multi-front integrity event. Jensen Huang resigned as President & CEO on November 12, 2026, after disclosure of extramarital affairs — one with a subordinate executive, and one with Alice Huang, a businesswoman U.S. intelligence reportedly assesses as linked to Chinese intelligence, connected to Singapore-based cloud firm Megaspeed (a multi-billion-dollar NVDA chip buyer under investigation). The board unanimously accepted the resignation and has publicly ruled out his return despite political pressure. The stock closed at $140.92 on Nov 20 — roughly **41% below the Oct 6 record ($239.24)** and a fresh 52-week low, with >$700bn of market value erased. Trading volume on Nov 12 hit 748M shares vs. a ~100–130M normal run rate (5–7x), indicating institutional repositioning, not just retail noise.

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK** (structurally deficient; remediation underway under duress)

| Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **No independent board chair for 33 years.** Harvey C. Jones was appointed the company's *"first-ever Chair of the Board"* only on Nov 12, 2026 — the day of the CEO exit. | Founder-CEO effectively controlled board agenda, information flow, and succession for the entire public life of the company. This is the root cause of the current crisis. |
| **Board now 10 directors, none of whom are employees**, all independent, including the new chair. | Genuine improvement in form — but it was forced by catastrophe, not designed in advance. |
| **Succession vacuum:** CFO Colette Kress named interim CEO; permanent search only now beginning. | Critical conflict: the CFO who signed the disclosures now under SEC scrutiny is simultaneously interim CEO, board-facing compliance owner, and a likely witness/party. Remediation is being run by the person whose work product is being investigated. |
| **Board reflexes were reactive.** Export-control / national-security oversight committee formed Nov 17 — *after* subpoenas, not before. Outside law firm and "independent slate of directors" engaged to investigate damage. | The board had no national-security or export-compliance committee despite China contributing materially to revenue and export controls being the #1 known industry risk for years. |
| **Huang exited the board entirely (Nov 11).** Long-tenured directors (e.g., Tench Coxe, Sutter Hill) remain. | Removes contamination, but destroys institutional knowledge at the exact moment of a supply-commitment peak; tenure concentration raises refreshment questions. |

**Verdict:** Governance is the single weakest link and the proximate cause of value destruction. The newly independent board and chair are real positives and are acting decisively (refusing reinstatement, forming the export committee, hiring outside counsel) — but until a permanent CEO is seated and the SEC/AG matters resolve, this cannot be rated above Weak.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — **SEVERE / escalating**

**Active enforcement and investigations:**
- **SEC civil probe** (per WSJ) into whether NVIDIA misled investors in its disclosures. Reporting indicates the focus includes **financial reporting, risk disclosures, and internal controls** — not merely the affair. This is the most dangerous thread: it can migrate from disclosure to accounting.
- **California AG investigative subpoena** (Rob Bonta, Nov 19). Investigating whether NVIDIA or Huang's actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information, and whether NVIDIA "misled investors and the public." Bonta: *"raises bright red flags."* State-level subpoena power with parallel private litigation is a costly combination.
- **Congressional hearings, Dec 1:** Huang and General Counsel **Tim Teter** to appear before House Foreign Affairs, the Select Committee on the CCP, and House Intelligence. Chairs allege NVIDIA's "role in allowing dangerous PRC actors to access our most advanced chips."
- **House Democrats' inquiry** (Reps. Kamlager-Dove, Meeks) into Commerce Dept. approval of H200 sales to China and handling of the Megaspeed investigation, including a records-preservation demand to Secretary Lutnick.

**Pending regulation (direct revenue risk):**
- BIS interim rule (Nov 16) restricting third-country access to advanced AI chips; bipartisan momentum to attach tighter export controls to year-end legislation.
- NVIDIA has **halted H200 shipments to China** as Beijing restricts imports and Washington tightens. Commerce records show **879,000 NVDA/AMD chips approved for China in six months**, with some recipients on the Pentagon's military-company list — a live trigger for further restriction.

**Private litigation:** Multiple securities class actions in N.D. Cal. (filed from Nov 9), class periods spanning **Dec 19, 2024 – Nov 12, 2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline **Jan 8, 2027**. Allegations center on concealment of Huang's ties to a suspected foreign intelligence asset. Consolidated exposure is large but bounded relative to market cap; the *discovery* is the real risk, since it will probe internal controls and board knowledge.

**Additional red flag:** NVIDIA **declined to file its Q3 FY2027 Form 10-Q** with its results, stating it will file "in the coming weeks." This breaks with its own standard practice and was publicly flagged by Michael Burry. A deferred periodic report during an SEC disclosure probe is a serious controls signal.

*Note: EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned no historical SEC actions against NVDA. No DOJ investigation is confirmed — do not assume one.*

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis — **NET SELLING, HEAVY, AND POORLY TIMED**

- **~$1.4 billion** of insider sales in the run-up to the resignation (secondary aggregation), against **zero open-market purchases** identified. NVIDIA filed ~40 Form 4s over the trailing twelve months — an unusually high cadence, clustered around Jun 29 and Sep 18, 2026 (likely vesting/option events).
- **Director Mark Stevens (second-largest shareholder)** sold ~**$946 million** in the months before the scandal broke, including 1,022,239 shares at $230.51 (~$235.6M) on Sep 8, leaving ~28.9M shares.
- Another director sold **1,366,000 shares via a trust** on Sep 18; a separate board member reportedly unloaded **$407M**.
- **Most alarming detail:** reporting indicates the ~$946M Stevens sale **had no Rule 10b5-1 trading plan flagged**. If insider sales were not pre-scheduled in the weeks immediately preceding a governance implosion, that materially strengthens the securities class action and invites an insider-trading inquiry.
- **Mitigant:** Huang made **no open-market sales in 2026** — his exposure was aligned, which marginally offsets the pattern.

*Data gap/confidence:* The Form 4 feed returned accession numbers without parsed transaction detail. The buy/sell split above is sourced from secondary reporting and aggregators and should be verified against primary filings before publication. **Recommend escalation for verification.**

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Key-person risk — realized, not hypothetical.** Huang was the archetypal founder-CEO: chief negotiator of the $390bn supply book, the face of sovereign-AI deals, and the primary relationship holder with TSMC and memory partners. He is gone with no successor, and the board has foreclosed his return. A CFO with no operating-P&L track record now runs a company guiding ~75% growth. **Execution risk on the largest capex ramp in semiconductor history is now unmanaged.**
2. **Supply-commitment overhang + supplier concentration.** Commitments escalated **$119bn (April) → $279bn → $390bn (Q3 FY27)**, predominantly multi-year **HBM memory**. These are largely take-or-pay in substance, taken against demand forecasts. Dependency remains concentrated in **TSMC (CoWoS advanced packaging)** and a three-way HBM oligopoly (SK Hynix, Micron, Samsung), plus substrate commitments (Kinsus, Samsung Electro-Mechanics). If AI capex decelerates even modestly, this converts from revenue visibility into inventory/obsolescence write-down risk. It also concentrates Taiwan geopolitical exposure.
3. **Customer concentration and circular vendor financing.** Five customers account for **70% of $63.1bn of receivables**; NVIDIA simultaneously invests in customers (OpenAI, CoreWeave, Nebius, Anthropic; trimmed its Intel stake 25% in Q3) who then purchase its GPUs, alongside reported ~$110bn–$500bn financing/backstop structures. Nothing identified is improper — but revenue quality is partly contingent on NVIDIA's own capital, which is precisely the condition that made the telecom-equipment bubble so destructive.

*Cybersecurity: no material incident identified in this review — noted as a gap, not a clearance. Talent: elevated attrition risk following Huang's exit and the culture exposé below.*

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **SEVERE**

- **Brand trajectory inverted in twelve days:** from the gold standard of AI execution to a governance and national-security cautionary tale. Coverage now runs through the national security lens ("dangerous PRC actors," potential Chinese intelligence asset), not the technology lens.
- **Culture exposé (NYT, Nov 20):** "How Jensen Huang Made Humiliation Routine at Nvidia" — decades of belittling behavior, including a **confidential settlement with an executive** who alleged mistreatment. This opens a second, independent litigation and #MeToo-adjacent risk vector and undermines the "meritocratic engineering culture" narrative used to recruit talent and justify valuation.
- **Investor-sentiment damage is now reflexive:** Michael Burry publicly claiming vindication on AI-infrastructure names; Cathie Wood selling NVDA to buy Anthropic at IPO; Damodaran framing a $140 entry point. Seven consecutive down sessions into a VIX of 27.7.
- **ESG/governance scoring:** near-certain G-pillar downgrades and likely exclusion from sustainability-mandated funds — a mechanical, price-insensitive seller.
- **Stakeholder contagion:** hyperscalers, sovereign AI programs, and U.S. government customers are all now obligated to seek compliance assurances from an interim CEO under subpoena.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **CRITICAL**

Six independent, mutually reinforcing exposures are live simultaneously: (i) an SEC civil investigation touching internal controls and disclosures; (ii) a state AG subpoena alleging investor deception; (iii) three congressional committees plus a Commerce-oversight inquiry; (iv) a securities class action with a Dec 2024 class period; (v) an unprecedented CEO removal and vacant permanent leadership; and (vi) a **deferred 10-Q**. These are not diversifying risks — they share a single factual root (Huang's undisclosed relationships and China-adjacent counterparties) and a single evidentiary record. Each new disclosure worsens the others. Add a $390bn take-or-pay supply book under new, unproven leadership at a 2.2 beta, and the non-financial risk alone justifies a material discount to peers and a capped position size until leadership is permanent and the 10-Q is filed.
