## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT** (facts agreed, conclusions opposed)

**High agreement on the facts — this is the important part.** All eleven agents independently converge on the same description of the business: a genuine franchise with accelerating software (+43%), 25% bookings growth, evidence-archive switching costs, and a de-rated multiple — undermined by SBC at 17–23% of revenue, negative-to-thin GAAP operating income, a 77% FCF collapse, and federal booking lumpiness that has now bitten twice. **No agent found fraud.** Beneish −2.52, four consecutive years of negative accruals, and receivables growing +4.4% against +33.5% revenue is the cleanest line in the file, and I weight that heavily.

**Low agreement on the action.** Growth (BUY) and Value (SELL) are looking at the same company through different lenses, and the gap is almost entirely two assumptions:

- **SBC treatment.** Value expenses $634M of stock comp and gets ~$200/share. Growth adds it back and gets $378–540. Both are defensible; the truth is that SBC is a real cost to *shareholders* but not a cash cost to the *enterprise*.
- **Terminal multiple.** 25x exit vs 40x exit on 2028.

**Conflict resolution — where I side against the bulls.** Three independent agents (Fin Risk, Short, Alt Data) converge on a point Growth underweights: **the FCF conversion story is unproven and the near-term catalyst is skewed negative.** Growth's own data show it — FY25's entire $211M of operating cash flow arrived in Q4. That makes the 23-Feb-27 print a near-binary event on the single metric the thesis most needs repaired.

**Conflict resolution — where I side against the bears.** Value's $200 base case and the Quant SELL both embed a further de-rating that has *already* largely occurred. The Short Analyst — the agent with the strongest incentive to find a bear case — explicitly declines to short at $343 and names $450–500 as the level where he would. That is, functionally, a floor-to-target statement. Quant's own R² of 8.6% also means its factor scores describe a *crowding unwind in progress*, not a forecast of business deterioration.

**Net:** medium conviction, correctly described as **"own it small, prove it, then size it."** This is not a 4% position and it is not a pass.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **1.25% initial → 3.0% maximum**

**Kelly arithmetic.** Scenario-weighted expected return (bull +60% / 25%, base +10% / 45%, bear −40% / 30%) = **+7.5%** edge on roughly 1.5:1 odds. Full Kelly = 5.0%. Apply a **¼ fractional Kelly** for a 91%-idiosyncratic, 60%-vol name with an unresolved disclosure question → **1.25%**. That is the initial size.

**Volatility budget check (the binding constraint).** At 1.25% weight, standalone contribution is 0.76% of portfolio vol; at the 3% cap it is 1.82% — the practical ceiling for a single name whose risk cannot be hedged (R² 8.6% means beta-hedging removes almost nothing). A 3% weight with a 2:1 adverse move costs ~5% of portfolio — acceptable for a high-conviction name, too much for this one until Q4 clears.

**Why not 3–5% now (the "high conviction, low risk" bucket does not apply):** conviction is *contested*, and risk flags number four (FCF quality, governance/insider selling, securities litigation, federal concentration). Under the framework, medium conviction + multiple flags → 1–3%, and the lower half of it.

**Why not 0.5% or pass:** the business is not impaired, the bear case is largely priced, and the Short Analyst's own tripwire ($450–500) is 31–46% *above* spot. A sub-1% position would be uninvestable noise given a 60% vol profile — you would be paying full research cost for no P&L impact.

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector.** Filed as Industrials/Aerospace & Defense; **economically it is long-duration government software.** Do not book it against an A&D/ITA allocation — corr to ITA is only 0.30 and it is the worst-ranked name on value/momentum/low-vol within that group. Book it against the **growth / long-duration quality** sleeve. If the portfolio already carries a full growth-factor budget, this competes for it — it does not diversify it.

**Factor.** This is an unambiguous *addition* to high-beta (1.28–1.47), negative-momentum (−31.7%), low-quality-cash exposure. It is the precise opposite of a value or low-vol sleeve. **Two hard constraints:** (a) do not add if portfolio-level high-beta/negative-momentum weight already exceeds budget — AXON would be a fourth or fifth correlated bet on the same real-rate variable Macro described; (b) do not add if the portfolio is already >8% in sub-2% FCF-yield names.
