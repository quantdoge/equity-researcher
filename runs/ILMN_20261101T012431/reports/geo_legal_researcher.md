# Illumina, Inc. (ILMN) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*As of Q3 FY2026 (quarter ended 27-Sep-2026); latest filed 10-Q 30-Oct-2026*

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

Illumina reports revenue in five regions (appendix note in press release flags "Greater China region" as a carve-out from ROW because of the Unreliable Entities List). Exact regional dollars for Q3'26 sit in the investor-prepared-remarks tables (investor.illumina.com static file, 403-blocked for retrieval) — figures marked * are derived/estimated, and this is the one gap in this note.

| Country/Region | Revenue exposure | Legal/reg risk | Geopolitical risk | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** (San Diego HQ; mfg in US + Singapore; ~8,600 FTE) | Largest single market, ~45–50%* | Antitrust/securities docket, FDA LDT/IVD pathway, CMS reimbursement, state genetic-privacy laws | Low (home market); policy volatility moderate (tariffs, BIOSECURE) | **Medium** |
| **Greater China** | ~$308M FY24 (≈7%); FY25 plan was $165–185M; YTD'26 China contributed **+1.2pp** to growth but **–0.1pp** in Q3'26 → now ~3–4% | MOFCOM UEL listing; procurement approval | **High** — state-driven localisation, MGI/BGI preference | **High** |

Structural anchor: **~52–54% of revenue is shipped outside the U.S.** (FY25 Form 10-K, sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1110803/000111080326000024/ilmn-20251228.htm). FY25 consolidated revenue $4,343M; FY26 guidance $4.64–4.66bn.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1. China Unreliable Entities List (UEL) remains a structural, not transient, impairment — Probability ~80% persists through 2027; Impact: moderate (1–2% of revenue) but strategically large**
- MOFCOM listed Illumina on the UEL on 4-Feb-2025; an outright **ban on exporting Illumina sequencers into China took effect 4-Mar-2025** (Reuters). China was ~7% of sales / $308M in 2024 (Reuters, 10-Mar-2025).
- The **export ban was lifted effective 10-Nov-2025, but Illumina remains on the UEL**, meaning Chinese customers need government approval for instrument purchases (Illumina press release; Endpoints). This is the single most-cited risk factor in the Q3'26 8-K ("uncertainty regarding the impact of our inclusion on the 'unreliable entities list'").
- Consequence: China is being managed out of the growth algorithm ("ROW organic growth" excludes Greater China). Risk that Chinese clinical/agrigenomics demand permanently re-platforms to MGI/BGI (Complete Genomics), which is exactly the market Illumina cannot re-win if approval friction persists. Upside kicker: any full UEL removal would be a discrete re-rating event.

**2. US–China tariff and export-control whipsaw — Probability of further policy shifts: high; Impact: two-sided**
- **IEEPA tariffs were struck down**; Illumina booked **$79M of tariff-refund benefit to cost of revenue plus a $37M revenue reduction for refunds credited to customers in Q3'26** ($0.18 of EPS) — i.e., the refund alone was ~11% of Q3 non-GAAP EPS. Further tranches of refunds remain a possible upside (press release, 29-Oct-2026).
- But authority has migrated to **Section 232 and Section 122** (Section 122 tariffs ran 24-Feb-2026 to 24-Jul-2026 absent extension; Section 232 scope keeps expanding — Baker Donelson, BDO, Freshfields). Illumina's instrument/consumable supply chain (Singapore manufacturing, China-sourced components) remains exposed to new duties with no refund offset.
- Export-control dimension: sequencers are dual-use technology; tightening US controls on advanced bio-instruments to China cuts both ways (protects IP, but invites retaliation against Illumina's China install base).

**3. Legacy antitrust/litigation overhang resurfacing as new claims — Probability of incremental adverse outcome ~40%; Impact: low–moderate (quantified below)**
- The GRAIL saga (FTC 5th Circuit loss, EC prohibition, €432M gun-jumping fine in Jul-2023) is now largely closed: **divestiture of GRAIL completed 24-Jun-2025** via distribution of 85.5% of shares (Illumina release; Cravath; FTC).
- However, the Q3'26 8-K still flags "the risk of additional litigation or other liabilities arising against us in connection with previous acquisitions, including the GRAIL acquisition" — i.e., post-divestiture indemnities and securities follow-on suits remain live.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Antitrust / EU**: €432M EC gun-jumping fine (Jul-2023) — the largest ever at the time — was litigated; the **CJEU annulled the Commission's Article 22 referral approach on 3-Sep-2024**, undermining the jurisdictional basis of that file (McDermott, Norton Rose). Residual EU exposure is now mostly a tail.
- **Securities / shareholder litigation**: Grail-related federal securities class action **dismissed in Illumina's favour (Oct-2025, Cooley)**; a separate consolidated class action had motions to dismiss filed 11-Dec-2025 and pending (KTMC); a **$13.9M settlement** resolved an earlier securities class action (Bloomberg Law). Derivative claims against directors filed Oct-2023 (Stanford Securities Litigation clearinghouse). Aggregate quantum is small versus $35.6bn market cap.
- **Patent/IP war with BGI–Complete Genomics**: Delaware jury found Illumina **willfully infringed CGI patents, $334M verdict** (Quinn Emanuel); Illumina previously paid **$325M to settle 2022 U.S. litigation** with BGI units (Reuters). Cross-border patent fights (UK, Germany, US ITC) remain a recurring, competitively-motivated cost of doing business; a BGI import exclusion would be a partial offset.
- **China regulatory**: MOFCOM UEL/export ban (above) plus China's human-genetic-resources regime constrains what Chinese customers can do with Illumina platforms — a demand-side regulatory drag independent of UEL.
- **Product/regulatory**: FDA lab-developed-test rule, EU IVDR transition, and payor coverage decisions are the main non-geopolitical regulatory swing factors for the clinical franchise (now the growth engine per Q3'26 call).
- **Trade policy tailwind**: **BIOSECURE Act signed into law (Jan-2026)** restricting US federal agency contracting involving "biotechnology companies of concern" (BGI named; WuXi entities not listed) — structurally favourable to Illumina versus Chinese competitors in US-funded research (Ropes & Gray; Latham).
- **M&A/contract**: SomaLogic (closed 2025) integration and the **$30.0M payment to Standard BioTools (24-Jul-2026) terminating earnout/royalty/licence obligations** — clean, non-litigated resolution (10-Q Ex-2.1).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **No FCPA/OFAC/sanctions enforcement action or disclosed investigation identified** in the filings and news reviewed.
- **Watch items, not violations**: (i) dual-use export-control classification of sequencers and the UEL designation create a genuine "conflicting-requirements" hazard (US export rules vs. Chinese procurement approvals) that requires a standing compliance overlay; (ii) genetic-data handling under GDPR/China HGR/state laws — a data-privacy incident would carry outsized reputational and regulatory cost in clinical genomics; (iii) distributor/tender model in LatAm/MEA carries third-party/intermediary risk typical of the sector; (iv) tariff-refund accounting (IEEPA) is a new disclosure-sensitive area — refund estimates rely on "expected to be recovered" assumptions and could be challenged or reversed.
- Governance: activist overhang (Icahn, 2023) has subsided under CEO Jacob Thaysen; board-related costs of $3M appeared in Q3'26 non-GAAP bridge — immaterial.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

*Rationale*: The two genuinely high-severity exposures — the FTC/EU GRAIL antitrust war and the ~7%-of-revenue China ban — have both been defused in structure (GRAIL divested Jun-2025; China import ban lifted Nov-2025). What remains is a **persistent, mostly-priced China friction risk (~3–4% of revenue)**, a **two-way tariff regime** where the refunds booked in Q3'26 flatter the comparison, and a **litigation tail that is small relative to the equity**. Rated Medium rather than Low solely because of the China UEL's refusal to clear and the binary nature of US export-control/tariff policy on a company that manufactures outside the U.S. and sells 52%+ abroad. A full UEL removal, or an IEEPA refund reversal, are the two clean catalysts to watch.
