## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — AECOM (NYSE: ACM)
**As of market close Nov 17, 2026 | Price $80.53 | Market cap ~$10.4B | 51,000 employees | CEO: W. Troy Rudd**

**Context:** ACM is 38% below its Nov-2025 level ($129.61 → $80.53) and 40% off its $133.31 high, having printed three separate double-digit single-day declines (Feb 12, May 12, Aug 11–12, 2026) before rebounding +11% on FY26 results (Nov 17) and +43% off the $56.10 October low. This is a stock whose *non-financial* risk has already crystallized into price.

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (with material caveats)

**Evidence for:**
- Designated **Lead Independent Director** (Douglas W. Stotlar) offsets the combined **Chairman & CEO** role held by Troy Rudd.
- Functioning committee structure (Compensation & Organization Committee), annual say-on-pay, independent compensation consultant.
- **No SEC enforcement actions** returned against ACM in EDGAR full-text search — a clean enforcement record.

**Evidence against:**
- **Combined Chairman/CEO** at precisely the moment a securities class action and a data-breach suit land on the board's plate. The board's independence is structurally weaker when it must oversee disclosure litigation against the CEO's own statements.
- **CFO/COO role conflation**: Gaurav Kapoor holds "Chief Financial & Operations Officer" — an internal promotion (ex-CAO/Controller/Treasurer) combining the financial gatekeeper and operations P&L roles. The person responsible for cost-to-complete discipline is also the person signing off on the financials.
- **CLO turnover (Sept 16, 2026)**: David Gan → Senior Advisor, retiring 2027; Manav Kumar appointed. Framed as long-planned succession (>20 yrs tenure), but it removes the institution's top legal officer weeks after the securities investigation opened.
- **Disclosure-control optics**: On **Feb 10, 2026** Rudd and Kapoor explicitly *raised* FY26 adjusted EPS midpoint guidance to **$5.95**; by **Aug 11** it was cut to **$4.05** (−32%) with a $337M charge. Either controls did not surface a deteriorating project for six months, or disclosure was selective. That is a governance question, not merely an operating one.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Cost-to-complete / bid-estimation failure on legacy P3 work.** A **$337M pre-tax charge** on a design-build P3 Construction Management project **bid in 2019**, driven by delayed completion — producing an $84M GAAP quarterly net loss. A *second* CM project remains live: FY27 guidance absorbs **~$500M of expected cash use** on the two projects, with management flagging they weigh on H1 FY27 cash flow. Further charges are the single most probable negative surprise.
- **Claims and receivables recoverability / cash conversion.** Significant project claims have grown to **$680M**. "Longer-than-anticipated claim resolution" plus **Middle East payment delays** mean reported revenue quality is deteriorating; this is the operational mechanism behind the 98% collapse in operating cash flow. Watch for impairment or a revenue-recognition restatement — the closest thing to a fraud indicator in this file.
- **Cybersecurity — alleged breach, 1.22TB.** On **Sept 17, 2026** the BrainCipher/MetaEncryptor ransomware group listed AECOM, claiming ~1.22TB of data stolen. The company has not publicly confirmed scope or materiality in the sources available to me (an 8-K was filed the same day, contents unverified). Litigation has already been filed over "massive employee data breach." For a firm holding client-confidential infrastructure, defense, and government design data, this is a severe continuity and client-trust risk.
- *Adjacent:* key-person concentration in Rudd (Chairman/CEO) with no announced succession; heavy data-center/AI pipeline exposure against a national backdrop of **$64B of data-center projects blocked or delayed** by local opposition and environmental litigation.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — MODERATE-HIGH

- Brand is otherwise strong: **ENR #1-ranked Transportation** practice, record backlog, 18.2% segment margin (+110bps), dividend raised 10%, and marquee wins (NZ Northland Corridor, Irish Rail JV, data-center pipeline). New appointments (Dwight Pullen, Global Transportation CEO) signal bench depth.
- But the narrative is now dominated by **"$337m loss on a construction project," "securities fraud investigation," and "ransomware breach."** Trade-press and Reddit engineering forums are actively questioning how a firm of this caliber misestimates a project by that magnitude — reputational damage that matters in a bid-and-prequalification business where clients judge execution history.
- Client concentration in government and P3 environments means reputational damage translates into **prequalification and bonding friction**, not just headlines.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Positives (clean SEC enforcement record, net insider buying, Lead Independent Director, restored FY27 guidance, record backlog, no liquidity stress) are real but are *mitigants*, not offsets. What remains is an **active, filed securities class action**, a **demonstrated internal control failure** in project estimation, a **$680M claims balance of uncertain recoverability**, an **alleged terabyte-scale data breach with litigation**, and a **concentrated Chair/CEO + CFO/COO + fresh-CLO** leadership stack. That combination warrants High, short of Critical only because there is no disclosed SEC/DOJ subpoena, no restatement, and no covenant or going-concern pressure.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Guidance integrity.** EPS midpoint $5.95 (Feb 10) → $4.05 (Aug 11), a 32% cut within six months, while insiders publicly reiterated confidence. Filed class action. *Escalate to GC/IR for the exact class period and whether Feb–Aug statements are covered.*
2. **The second Construction Management project.** ~$500M of FY27 cash use tied to legacy CM projects; if the first burned $337M, the reserve adequacy on the second is not externally verifiable. **Highest-probability source of the next negative revision.**
3. **$680M project claims.** Growth of +$280M in one quarter against a 98% collapse in operating cash flow. Requires scrutiny of claim recognition policy, probability weighting, and Middle East sovereign collection.
4. **Cyber incident disclosure.** Alleged 1.22TB exfiltration (BrainCipher/MetaEncryptor, Sept 17, 2026) with employee class action filed; **public confirmation of scope/materiality is absent from my sources.** Verify whether Item 1.05 of Form 8-K was triggered and whether SEC disclosure timelines were met.
5. **Governance concentration during litigation.** Combined Chairman/CEO plus combined CFO/COO plus CLO transition — three key control roles in motion while defending disclosure litigation.
6. **Middle East collection and FCPA exposure.** Payment-timing delays in a high-corruption-risk theatre with a large public-sector book; no active enforcement found, but this is the exposure most likely to produce a *new* regulatory event.
