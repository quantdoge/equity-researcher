### 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **MEDIUM** (not fraudulent — but structurally overstated)

There is no accounting manipulation here. The problem is *narrative* earnings quality: a widening, recurring gap between "adjusted" and GAAP, and a fresh deterioration in cash conversion that the adjusted numbers conceal.

### 2. Key Red Flags Found

**R1 — Serial "one-off" project charges. The single most important issue.** FY26 Q3 (June-2026) carried a **$337M pre-tax loss on a delayed Construction Management project**, which flipped quarterly gross profit *negative* (−$34M on $3.586B of revenue). FY23 shows the same fingerprint: operating income collapsed from $646.8M to $324.1M (~$320M of charges). That is **two ~$0.3B write-downs in four years** in fixed-price/CM work — and management is now guiding FY27 *including* Construction Management. Any risk that recurs twice in four years is an operating cost, not a non-recurring item. Cumulatively ~$660M pre-tax, ~6% of market cap, all excluded from the number the stock is valued on.

**R2 — Cash conversion breaks down on the adjusted number.** FY26 CFO ≈ $535M (Q4 $366M + 9M $169M) against GAAP net income $293.9M = **1.82x — healthy**. But against *adjusted* net income (~$580M) it is **~0.92x, below 100%**. The adjusted earnings the sell-side capitalises are not fully converting to cash. Note the charge was non-cash, so it cannot explain a $341M FCF decline — the cash deterioration is *separate and unexplained by the charge*.

**R3 — Working capital absorption is scaling with backlog.** 9M FY26 operating cash flow was **$169M vs $625M** prior year (−73%); changes in working capital were −$278M vs −$142M. Receivable days: 107.9 → 100.1 → **105.4**. With backlog at a record $28.0B (+13%) and guidance for a 20%+ profit step-up, growth will *consume* more working capital. "Record wins" is being financed on the balance sheet.

**R4 — Capex up 40% in a declining-revenue year.** Implied FY26 capex ≈ $191M vs $137M in FY25. Capital intensity rising while revenue falls is a classic late-cycle efficiency signal.

**R5 — Balance sheet opacity and negative tangible equity.** Goodwill+intangibles $3.88B = **144% of book equity** ($2.70B); retained earnings are a **deficit of −$1.22B**; tangible book is roughly **−$1.19B**. Americas adjusted operating income fell 37% to $566M — a goodwill impairment indicator under ASC 350. (Fairness note: $566M + $337M ≈ FY25's level, so the Americas decline *is* the charge — ex-charge the segment was roughly flat. That materially softens this flag.)

**R6 — Capital allocation destroyed value, then stopped.** ~$442M of buybacks in H1 FY26 at an average ~$93 (Q1 $326M, Q2 $116M), then **$6 thousand** in Q3 as the stock fell to $60–70. Buy high, stop low. The 10% dividend hike ($0.31 → $0.34) on halved FCF raises payout to ~51% of FY26 FCF.

**R7 — Promotional language vs delivery.** "Record full-year wins," "13% backlog growth to all-time high," "adjusted EPS +20%" — against a 6.5% revenue decline, a 47.7% net income decline, and a negative-gross-margin quarter. Adjusted NSR grew only **3%** in Q4 versus 13% backlog growth: book-to-burn is flattering backlog while revenue conversion lags.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

**Moderate.** AECOM is a genuine #1-scale player in design/consulting with a $28.0B backlog — this is not a melting ice cube. But the franchise economics are fragile: gross margin of 5.8–7.5% on gross revenue means ~90%+ pass-through costs, so a **170bp margin move drove a 28% gross-profit decline**. That is symmetric operating leverage: tiny mix or fixed-price execution errors produce violent earnings swings, as Q3 proved. Engineering & construction remains structurally low-margin, labour-constrained, and fixed-price-exposed, with public-sector clients that hold pricing power. Moat: adequate. Pricing power: limited. **Data gap: no clean E&C peer multiples were retrievable (the sector feed returned mega-cap Industrials, not Jacobs/Tetra Tech/Stantec), so I cannot make a defensible relative-value claim.**

### 6. Short Thesis Summary

**The bear case that survives scrutiny:** AECOM is a thin-margin, fixed-price-exposed contractor whose management has twice in four years absorbed ~$0.3B project losses, reports them as one-offs, and asks investors to value the company on adjusted EPS that converts to cash at only ~0.9x. Meanwhile FY26 FCF halved, working capital absorbed $278M through nine months, capex rose 40%, and goodwill sits at 144% of equity over a $1.22B retained-earnings deficit. FY27 guidance requires adjusted EBITDA of $1.35–1.39B, ~25% above FY26, and adjusted EPS of $6.15–6.35 vs ~$4.50 — a margin expansion step far beyond the 10–20bp/yr the company historically delivers, on a revenue base that shrank 6.5%. Any third CM charge, or simply a "good but not great" FY27, exposes a GAAP EPS of $5.65–5.90 as the real number.

**The bull case is stronger than the bear case on valuation, and I will not pretend otherwise.** Record $28.0B backlog (+13%), Q4 adjusted operating margin on NSR up 20bp to 20.6%, Q4 adjusted EPS +20% (a 2.5% beat), Q4 CFO +87% and FCF +104%, a 10% dividend hike, and a fully funded $1.24B cash balance. At $80.53 the stock is **12.7x FY27 guided adjusted EPS and 13.9x guided GAAP EPS**, ~9x FY27 guided EV/EBITDA — after a 39% de-rating from $132. The market has already charged AECOM for the charge.
