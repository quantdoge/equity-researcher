# Sector Brief: AECOM (ACM) — Industrials / Engineering & Construction

**Prepared:** Sector Economy Research | **Price:** $80.53 | **Mkt cap:** $10.36bn | **FY end:** 30-Sep

---

## 1. Sector Overview

Engineering & Construction (E&C) spans two economically distinct sub-industries: **asset-light design/consulting** (AECOM, Jacobs, Tetra Tech, Stantec, WSP, Arcadis) and **self-perform specialty contracting** (Quanta, EMCOR, MasTec, Dycom, Primoris). The sector is **highly fragmented** — no single firm holds even low-single-digit share of a global addressable market measured in trillions, implying an HHI well under 100 (unconcentrated) — but it is consolidating around scaled, technically-credentialed players who win multi-year framework agreements. Demand is currently **bifurcated**: firms levered to grid/electrification, data centres and communications are compounding revenue at 13–20% (PWR +19.8%, DY +18.0%, EME +16.6%, MTZ +16.2%), while traditional government-services consultants are growing low-single-digit or shrinking (J +4.6%, KBR +0.6%, FLR −5.0%, TTEK −6.7%, ACM −6.5%). The sector sits **mid-cycle with a rotation underway**: the 2021–2026 public-works wave (IIJA/IRA) is maturing as obligations are spent, but a new private capex leg — AI data centres, generation and grid interconnection, water — is in early innings. U.S. data-centre construction spending alone reached **$58.1bn in 2026 YTD**, and global data-centre buildout is estimated to require **~$6.7trn of investment by 2030**.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **AI / electrification capex supercycle.** Data-centre, power-generation and grid-interconnect work is the fastest-growing demand pool in the sector, feeding design, permitting and program-management scope. AECOM's **Design business growth accelerated to 6% in Q4 FY26**, and its International backlog rose **28% YoY** to a record — direct evidence the tailwind is reaching even the slower-growing consultants.
- **Record backlog visibility across the group.** AECOM closed FY26 with **total backlog of $28.0bn (+13%)**, **$13.4bn of wins** and a **1.3x book-to-burn** ratio — roughly **1.85x annual revenue** (~22 months of coverage). Across peers, backlog/book-to-bill is at or near cycle highs, which de-risks forward revenue even where reported growth is soft.
- **Mix shift toward higher-margin advisory and program management.** Scaled consultants are rotating away from low-margin pass-through construction toward cost-reimbursable design and advisory. AECOM posted **Q4 segment adjusted operating margin of 18.2% (+110bps YoY)** and guides FY27 to a **record 17.6%**; EME (10.1% op margin, +86bps), DY (12.5%, +529bps) and TTEK (11.9%, +77bps) show the same structural margin expansion.

---

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Public-funding air pocket in the Americas.** The IIJA obligation cycle has peaked while FY2026–27 federal appropriations remain contested, and policy direction on data-centre and infrastructure incentives is shifting. AECOM's **Americas adjusted operating income fell 37% to $566m** in FY26 — the segment bearing the brunt of both funding timing and project-specific problems.
- **Fixed-price / at-risk construction execution risk is sector-wide, not idiosyncratic.** AECOM absorbed a **$337m pre-tax charge on a Construction Management project** in Q3 FY26, halving FY26 net income (−47.7% to $294m) and free cash flow (**$343.8m vs $685.2m**), with two CM projects still set to drag H1 FY27 cash flow. MasTec's gross margin compressed from 13.2% to 9.6% YoY and Fluor posted a **negative gross margin (−0.8%)** — confirming EPC/fixed-price contract structures as the sector's principal earnings-volatility vector.
- **Labour cost inflation and fragmented, price-competitive bidding.** Engineering/technical talent is the binding input; wage inflation compresses margins unless recovered through rate escalation clauses. With thousands of qualified bidders and framework re-tenders every 3–5 years, pricing power is limited, and growth must be bought through bid volume rather than price.

**Porter's Five Forces (design/consulting sub-sector):** Rivalry **High** · Buyer power **High** (public agencies, competitive tendering) · Supplier power **Moderate-High** (scarce engineering talent) · Entry threat **Low-Moderate** (licensing, bonding, track record) · Substitutes **Low-Moderate** (in-house design, design-build bundling, AI-assisted design).

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG** (structurally) — **AVERAGE** (momentum)

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Scale / franchise** | **Strong.** Largest global pure-play infrastructure design consultancy (~$15bn revenue, 51,000 staff), with framework positions across transportation, water, environment and government services, and a JV foothold (with Jacobs) on Irish Rail's consultancy framework. |
| **Backlog / visibility** | **Strong.** $28.0bn backlog (+13%), 1.3x book-to-burn, ~1.85x revenue coverage — among the best forward-visibility in the peer set. |
| **Margins** | **Strong on adjusted basis.** 18.2% Q4 segment adjusted operating margin, FY27 guide 17.6% (record); EMEA/Americas mix shift toward advisory is working. GAAP optics are poor (4.1%) purely due to the $337m CM charge. |
| **Growth** | **Weak.** −6.5% FY26 revenue vs +4.6% peer median; 3-yr CAGR of 2.5% vs 7.1%. Even adjusted, Q4 NSR grew only **+3%** — below the sector and far below the 13–20% of electrification-levered peers. |
| **Balance sheet / returns** | **Average.** FY26 FCF halved to $344m; dividend raised 10% to $0.34/qtr (1.7% yield) signals confidence, but the 31x FCF multiple leaves little cushion until CM cash drag clears. |

**Verdict:** AECOM owns a genuinely **strong asset** — the broadest global design franchise outside the contractors, with record backlog and the best adjusted margin in its niche — but it is **not where the sector's growth is** this cycle. It is a **share donor in a share-gainer's market**, and the CM charge has damaged management credibility, which explains the discount: at $80.53 against FY27 adjusted EPS guidance of $6.15–6.35, ACM trades at **~12.9x** forward adjusted earnings and roughly **7.5–8x FY27 adjusted EBITDA** ($1.35–1.39bn) — a steep discount to PWR/EME/DY, which the sector's growth differential partly justifies. The re-rating case rests on backlog conversion and margin delivery in FY27, not on sector beta.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

**Rationale.** Structural demand is strong and multi-sourced (AI/data centres, grid, water, resilience, Middle East giga-projects), backlogs are at records across the group, and margins are expanding as consultants shed pass-through revenue. Median peer growth of +4.6% and mid-cycle positioning support an Attractive rating.

**Two conditions on that rating:** (1) the attractiveness is **rotational, not uniform** — capital is rewarding electrification/communications exposure (PWR, EME, DY, MTZ) and penalising government-services consultants (J, KBR, TTEK, FLR, ACM); and (2) **fixed-price execution risk** is the sector's live earnings threat, as AECOM's $337m CM charge, Fluor's negative gross margin and MasTec's 360bp gross-margin compression all demonstrate. AECOM participates in the tailwind through its Design backlog but does not lead it.
