# AECOM (ACM) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $80.53 | Market cap: $10.46B | Net debt ~$1.7B (1.3x) | EV ~$12.2B | FY ends 9/30**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW**

AECOM is the world's largest pure-play infrastructure design and engineering consultancy (~51,000 employees; Americas + International segments). It sells technical labor and program management on transportation, water, environment, energy and government programs. FY2026: revenue $15.1B (−6.5%), net service revenue (NSR) ~$7.6–8.0B, **backlog $28.0B (+13%)**, Q4 adjusted operating margin on NSR a **record 14.8% (+270bp)**.

**Evidence of a moat:**
- *Intangible assets / qualifications*: top ENR rankings, 100+ years of brand, pre-qualification on government frameworks that take years to obtain.
- *Switching costs*: multi-year master service agreements and embedded program teams; clients rarely re-bid mid-program.
- *Cost/scale advantages*: global delivery footprint, proprietary design tools, and now AI-enabled productivity — the stated driver of margin expansion.
- *Proof in the numbers*: ROIC 15.3% → 16.9% (FY24/FY25) vs. WACC ~8.5%; margin on NSR has risen from ~11% (FY19–20) to ~14.8%. That is genuine operational improvement, not accounting.

**Why not Wide:** the moat did not protect shareholders from a **$337M pre-tax overrun on a Construction Management project** in Q3 FY26. A business with true pricing power does not eat a charge equal to ~5% of annual NSR. Bidding is competitive, end markets are procurement-driven, and the asset is people who can walk out the door. The moat is real but narrow, and it has **not widened** over the past 5 years — the margin gains came from portfolio pruning and execution, while fixed-price contract risk has recurred.

**Circle of competence:** Yes. Understandable, cash-generative, but intrinsically low-margin (~9% adjusted EBITDA on gross revenue) and cyclical.

**Peer context (hand-picked engineering consultancies, not the sector feed):** Stantec 11.2x EV/EBITDA, Tetra Tech 15.8x, Jacobs 22.1x. AECOM at **8.9x forward EBITDA is the cheapest in its true peer group** — the discount is the market's price for the CM charge, the Americas margin collapse (segment adjusted operating income −37%) and a mid-year guidance cut from $5.90–6.10 to $3.95–4.15.

**Intrinsic value range $70–98; base ~$88.** At $80.53 the stock trades ~8% below base-case IV and ~39% below its 2025 high of ~$133. **That is not a margin of safety.** My required entry for a business of this quality and uncertainty is 25–30% below base IV — i.e., **≤ $65**, which equates to ~10.4x FY27E EPS and a 7.7% normalized FCF yield.

## 4. Management Quality — **Above average, with a credibility problem**

- **Capital allocation is the strongest suit.** Share count down from ~159M (FY20) to 129.9M (−18%) while also raising the dividend from $0.18 to $0.34 quarterly (+89% since 2023 — the board just raised it another 10%). Buybacks are funded with real FCF, not leverage, and were aggressive when the stock was cheap.
- **Portfolio discipline:** exited the fixed-price construction and management-services businesses, sold non-core energy/EMEA assets, and repositioned toward higher-margin design and advisory (advisory targeted to double). CEO Troy Rudd (since 2020) and CFO Gaurav Kapoor have delivered a ~400bp margin improvement. That is real.
- **The mark against:** risk control and guidance discipline. Two legacy CM projects bid years ago have now produced a $337M charge and wiped out a third of FY26 earnings; the FY26 guide was cut ~33% mid-year. Management calls these "outliers" and says the remaining CM backlog is lower-risk — plausible, but unproven until the second project closes in the coming months.
- Insider ownership not verified in this pass; no founder/controlling holder. Assume standard institutional ownership and treat this as a gap in the thesis.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

AECOM is a good — not great — business with a narrow moat, honest FCF, a fortress backlog, and a shareholder-friendly capital allocator. It is now priced at ~13x forward earnings and 8.9x EBITDA, a discount to every credible comparable, after a 39% drawdown.

But the margin of safety is insufficient. Base intrinsic value ~$88 versus a price of $80.53 leaves only ~8% upside to fair value, and that estimate rests on management's own guidance being achievable just months after that guidance was slashed by a third. The Q4 print (+11% in a day) has already removed the panic discount. **A fair price is not a buy.**

**Actionable plan:** establish a buy list entry at **≤$65** (25%+ MOS), or upgrade to Buy if the second legacy CM project closes without further charges **and** Americas segment adjusted operating income returns to growth, at any price below ~$72. Buybacks of ~4–5% of shares annually plus 4–6% NSR growth and modest margin expansion should produce 10–12% annual per-share value growth from here — adequate, but a Hold at this price.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Further CM charges (highest near-term risk).** The second legacy project is scheduled to deliver "in the coming months." Another overrun would confirm systemic risk-control failure and break the "outlier" narrative.
2. **Americas margin erosion.** Segment adjusted operating income fell 37% — only partly the charge. If underlying Americas margins are structurally lower, my normalized EPS is too high.
3. **Fixed-price contract model.** AECOM is paid a fixed fee on work whose cost it cannot fully control under inflation. This risk is recurring, not episodic (2019–20 also saw large charges).
4. **Public funding dependence.** US federal/state, UK and Australian infrastructure budgets drive the backlog. Appropriations delays, fiscal austerity or political shifts (e.g., federal cost-cutting) would hit bookings.
5. **Earnings-quality gap.** Adjusted EPS relies on excluding amortization (~$100M+/yr) and SBC; the GAAP-to-adjusted gap is ~16%, and the adjusted definition has flexed as convenient.
6. **Cyclicality.** Late-cycle infrastructure spending; private-sector work would compress quickly in a recession.
7. **Goodwill/leverage.** $3.9B goodwill (impairment risk if Americas underperforms) and 1.3x net leverage with ~$190M annual interest — a second bad year plus buybacks could push leverage toward 2x.
8. **Talent risk.** The assets are engineers; advisory expansion puts AECOM in direct competition with management consultancies for the same people.
