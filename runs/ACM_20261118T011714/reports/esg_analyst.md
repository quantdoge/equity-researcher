# ESG Analysis — AECOM (NYSE: ACM)

**Sector:** Industrials / Engineering & Construction · **HQ:** Dallas, TX · **Market cap:** ~$10.4B · **Employees:** 51,000 · **CEO:** W. Troy Rudd (also Chairman) · **FY26 revenue:** ~$15.4B *(derived: Q4 FY26 revenue $3,878M, −7% YoY; Q4 net service revenue $1,920M, +3%)*

AECOM is a professional-services infrastructure consultancy, not a heavy contractor. That distinction drives the entire ESG profile: **low direct environmental footprint, high leverage to environmental regulation as a revenue driver, and material risk concentrated in government-contracting integrity and project execution.**

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives**
- **SBTi-validated science-based net zero by 2040** — 10 years ahead of sector; interim target ≥50% reduction across Scope 1, 2 and 3 by 2030 vs. 2018 baseline.
- **CDP Climate score A-** (per third-party ESG aggregator, verified Nov 2026).
- Maintains net zero in own operations, retiring carbon credits equivalent to residual Scope 1+2 — modest credibility caveat (offset reliance on the small residual).
- Published **UK Carbon Reduction Plan (Dec 2025)** — a hard procurement gate for UK public contracts; compliance is a competitive advantage.
- Asset-light model means no stranded-asset risk, no Scope 1 heavy industry, no CBAM-exposed materials production.

**Negatives / watch items**
- **94% of footprint is Scope 3** — mostly purchased professional services and subconsultants. This is hard to abate, hard to audit, and increasingly demanded by EU/UK public clients. Data quality risk is real.
- **High-hazard legacy work**: DOE Hanford waste treatment, nuclear and contaminated-site remediation. Environmental liability tail exists via legacy design-build and energy & construction entities.
- Actual environmental penalty record is trivially small ($44,500 across 2 records since 2000) — a genuine positive on operational compliance.

**Verdict:** Low *direct* intensity, Medium *indirect* (Scope 3) and *liability* risk. Net environmental positioning is a **tailwind**, since regulation creates the demand.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Safety:** Company claims "industry best-in-class lost workday case and recordable incident rates," repeatedly recognised by clients. **Gap: no hard TRIR/LWCR figure was verifiable from public sources** — I could not independently confirm the rate. Flag for the memo.
- **Safety enforcement:** 27 OSHA records since 2000 totalling only $496K — small individual penalties, consistent with a genuinely strong safety culture rather than a systemic failure. Legacy Tishman/Flint/Cleveland Wrecking entities dominate the list.
- **Labour:** 16 employment-related records, $2.6M total, of which $2.47M wage & hour across 13 cases. Recurring but immaterial in dollar terms; concentrated in legacy acquired entities.
- **Ethics/culture:** Named one of the **World's Most Ethical Companies for the 10th year (2026)** by Ethisphere, 6 consecutive years. Meaningful third-party validation of compliance culture — though it sits awkwardly beside the FCA record (see §5).
- **Product/project quality — the material issue:** FY2026 included a **$337M pre-tax charge** from a higher projected cost to complete a Construction Management project, plus a **second challenged CM project** expected to pressure H1 FY2027 cash flow. For a firm selling project-delivery expertise, a cost-to-complete blowout of this size is a product-quality and oversight failure, not merely a financial one.
- **Diversity data:** not verifiable in this window — gap.

**Verdict:** Safety is a relative strength; project-delivery quality and wage/hour compliance are the soft spots.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**Rationale**

| Factor | Assessment |

| Board independence | Board appears majority independent with independent Audit, Compensation & Organization, and Nominating committees. Exact ratio not verified (proxy/board pages returned 403). |
| **Chair/CEO duality** | **Negative.** Rudd holds both Chairman and CEO. AECOM previously had an **independent Chairman (Douglas Stotlar)** — that structure was eliminated. Reverting to combined leadership removes a key check on management. No confirmed lead independent director. |
| Shareholder rights | Single class of stock; no dual-class structure; annual advisory say-on-pay. Clean. |

**Net:** Structurally sound board and clean share structure, undermined by (a) re-concentration of chair/CEO power and (b) a repeated integrity pattern in the highest-risk part of the business. A 6/10 reflects "adequate but with two specific, fixable deficiencies."

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (Leader on disclosure, Laggard on integrity)

- **Climate/disclosure:** Leader-grade — SBTi-validated 2040 net zero, CDP A-, 94% Scope 3 transparency, UK CRP compliance. Better than most Industrials peers.
- **Social:** Average — strong safety culture, weak verifiable metrics, one large delivery-quality failure.
- **Governance:** Below average — chair/CEO combination plus $111M FCA history.

The two dimensions cancel. AECOM is neither an ESG-compounder nor an ESG excludable.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL to POSITIVE**

**Positive for the thesis:**
- A material share of revenue is **regulation-created and non-discretionary** — remediation, water/wastewater, CSO, rail decarbonization, waste management. Environmental regulation is AECOM's growth engine, not its cost centre. This is a durable, policy-insulated demand moat that a carbon-focused screen would entirely miss.
- Asset-light model means **no stranded-asset or carbon-tax exposure** on the balance sheet.
- Disclosure quality (SBTi, CDP A-) supports inclusion in ESG-mandated mandates and lowers cost of capital at the margin.

**Negative / risk to underwrite:**
- The **$337M CM charge** is the ESG issue that actually matters. It is a governance and oversight failure with realized cash impact, and management flags a second challenged project into H1 FY2027. This is an **execution-risk discount**, not an ESG discount — but it should be modelled in the base case, not treated as a one-off.
- **FCA history** ($111M, 7 matters, spanning 2016–2023) creates tail risk of debarment or a large settlement. Low probability, severe consequence. Worth a qualitative risk premium on a public-sector-concentrated revenue base.

**Bottom line:** ESG is **not a thesis driver and not a derailer** for ACM. There is no exclusion risk, no carbon transition liability, and a genuine regulatory tailwind. The correct use of this analysis is as a **risk-control overlay**: monitor (1) the second CM project's cost-to-complete, and (2) any new FCA/DOJ activity — those two items, not carbon, are where ESG becomes financial.

**Data gaps to disclose:** exact board independence ratio and lead-independent-director status (SEC/IR pages returned 403); hard TRIR/LWCR figures; diversity metrics; FY25 absolute Scope 1/2/3 tonnages and carbon intensity per revenue dollar; confirmation of whether any debt carries sustainability-linked terms.
