## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — Middle East conflict / Iran war disrupting the Gulf business *(HIGH probability / MEDIUM-HIGH impact)*
- A US/Israel–Iran conflict has been active since **February 2026**, with a **de facto closure of the Strait of Hormuz** to most shipping (Congressional Research Service, R48971). VP Vance stated in November 2026 that Washington does "does not intend to remain in the Iran conflict for years," indicating a live but open-ended engagement.
- AECOM maintains named offices in **Abu Dhabi and Riyadh**, plus AECOM's own *Middle East Property and Construction Handbook 2026* explicitly addresses "how the ongoing conflict in the Middle East affects construction costs and project activity in the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, and Qatar."
- **Impact channel:** not revenue loss on signed work so much as (a) project delays/cost escalation on Gulf programs, (b) receivable and contract-asset collectability, (c) mobilization cost inflation, (d) potential client payment disruption. Gulf markets are actively growing (Saudi $2.14T pipeline), so this is a *volatility* risk rather than a *demand* risk.
- **Assessment:** High likelihood of continued disruption through FY2027; ~13–15% of revenue directly exposed, with disproportionate exposure in the fastest-growing segment.

### Risk 2 — US federal procurement, FCA enforcement and government shutdown/funding risk *(HIGH probability / MEDIUM impact)*
- The US is ~70–72% of revenue and heavily public-sector. AECOM is a serial defendant in **False Claims Act** matters (see §3) and has a dedicated defense practice for "a series of claims brought against the company under the False Claims Act" (Hecker Fink attorney profile).
- Management is also explicitly courting a **$40–50B US military infrastructure repair/expansion program** — which would deepen, not reduce, federal counterparty and compliance concentration.
- **Assessment:** Near-certain continued FCA and audit activity; low-to-moderate single-case financial impact but meaningful tail risk of a large adverse finding or suspension/debarment, which would be a thesis-breaking event.

### Risk 3 — Sanctions/export-control and trade-fragmentation exposure across a 150-country delivery model *(MEDIUM probability / MEDIUM impact)*
- The sanctions environment has tightened materially in 2025–26: OFAC's "Operation Economic Outcast" targeting Iranian networks, secondary sanctions on third countries doing business with Iran, and coordinated Western measures on Israeli settlement-linked trade (12 Western countries, Sept 2026; UK expansion of Global Human Rights regime, Nov 2026).
- For a firm delivering design and program management across the Gulf, India, Africa and Asia, secondary-sanctions and restricted-party screening is a live operational constraint — particularly where Gulf clients have Iran-adjacent commercial ties.
- **Assessment:** Medium probability of a compliance incident; medium impact (reputational, potential penalties, and mandatory ring-fencing of revenue).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**A. Securities fraud / claims-accounting investigation — the dominant legal risk (ACTIVE, 2026)**
- **11 May 2026:** Q2 FY2026 results showed **operating cash flow of $4M, down 98% YoY**, adjusted FCF of **negative $27M**. CFO Gaurav Kapoor attributed this to "longer-than-anticipated claim resolution on certain projects" — "projects we bid in fiscal year 2019 and 2020, two projects," noting "we've been successful on each one of them. But it's just been very slow and dragged out."
- **12 May 2026:** The 10-Q disclosed **claims recorded in contract assets and other non-current assets of ~$680M at 31 Mar 2026, versus ~$400M at 30 Sep 2025** — a ~$280M increase. **Shares fell $9.55 (−12%) to $69.95.**
- **11 Aug 2026:** Q3 10-Q recorded a **net loss of $344.6M ($258.8M after tax)** related to these matters.
- **Multiple firms investigating/aggregating claims:** Pomerantz LLP, Bragar Eagel & Squire, Glancy Prongay & Wolke Rotter, Howard G. Smith, Frank R. Cruz, Bernstein Liebhard. A filed securities class action is the likely next step.
- **Why this is a legal-and-geopolitical crossover:** The two contracts were bid in FY2019–20 and involve delayed claim resolution across two clients — a pattern that plausibly reflects non-US public-sector counterparties with slow/contested payment processes. Note also the **FY2021 10-K already flagged** that $258.8M-after-tax charges related to contract assets and other non-current assets; the recurring nature of claim recognition is the core allegation.
- **Assessment:** High probability of a filed class action and SEC informal inquiry. Financial exposure uncertain but the $680M claims balance is ~4.5% of revenue and >2x annual net income — a material overhang.

**B. False Claims Act — recurring pattern, not a one-off**

**C. Commercial litigation — a favorable data point**
- *AECOM Technical Services v. Flatiron* (10th Cir., No. 25-1140, **14 Aug 2026**): Tenth Circuit **affirmed a $5M jury verdict for AECOM** in a breach of contract/unjust enrichment dispute over change-order work on a Colorado joint venture, following an 18-day trial. Demonstrates AECOM's willingness to litigate change orders to judgment — consistent with the claims-resolution backlog issue.

**D. Regulatory / compliance posture**
- **FCPA:** Stanford FCPA database records a historical review of AECOM's internal controls and legal compliance procedures with respect to possible FCPA violations. No active DOJ/SEC FCPA action identified in this review.
- **Environmental/EPA:** AECOM is positioned as a *beneficiary* of EPA PFAS maximum-contaminant-level rules (remediation demand) — a regulatory tailwind, not a risk.
- **Data privacy:** UK/EU GDPR and Hong Kong/India localization obligations apply across design and program-management data. No identified enforcement action.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **🚩 Contract-asset / claims accounting under SEC scrutiny** — the $400M → $680M jump in claims, a 98% cash-flow collapse, and six firms circling is the highest-severity flag. Watch for an SEC subpoena disclosure in the FY2026 10-K.
2. **🚩 Serial FCA defendant as a US government contractor** — three separate eight-figure settlements/matters (hurricane relief, nuclear cleanup, Army man-hours). This is a structural control weakness in time-and-materials billing, not isolated misconduct. Debarment/suspension risk is low-probability but severe.
3. **🚩 Middle East sanctions adjacency** — Gulf operations plus an active Iran conflict and aggressive secondary sanctions create genuine restricted-party and end-use screening exposure.
4. **⚠️ Disclosure granularity** — no country-level revenue disclosure means investors cannot size the Saudi/UAE, Hong Kong, or UK exposure precisely. This opacity itself is a governance concern given the conflict environment.
5. **⚠️ Geopolitical concentration in the growth engine** — International (the segment growing operating income) is where the conflict, sanctions and currency risk is highest.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** AECOM's *core* geopolitical exposure (77% US revenue) is genuinely low-risk and the business is winning record work (**FY2026 backlog $28.0B, +13%; record $13.4B of wins; FY2027 adj. EPS guidance $6.15–$6.35; dividend raised 10%**). The **High** rating is driven not by geography but by two concentrated, correlated legal/compliance problems layered on top of a Middle East conflict:

- The **claims/contract-asset overhang (~$680M)** has already produced a $344.6M quarterly net loss, a 12% single-day share decline, and a multi-firm securities investigation — with class-action and SEC follow-on risk still ahead.
- The **Middle East war** sits directly on the 14–15% International segment that is currently the only part of the business growing profitably.

**What would change the rating:** *Downgrade to Medium* if the FY2026 10-K shows claims resolution/cash collection and no SEC enforcement action. *Upgrade to Very High* on (a) a formal SEC subpoena or DOJ probe into claims accounting, (b) a material Gulf project cancellation or receivable impairment, or (c) any FCA matter resulting in suspension or debarment.
