## Sector Brief — Lumentum Holdings (LITE)
**Sector:** Technology / Communication Equipment — Optical & Photonic Components
**Note on scope:** LITE's true competitive set is optical/photonics vendors (COHR, FN, CIEN, AAOI), not the mega-cap "Technology" bucket the peer tool returns. I built the comparison peer set manually. **Material event flag:** on **2 Nov 2026 Marvell announced a $115B all-stock acquisition of Lumentum** — this now dominates LITE's investment case and should be treated as the primary valuation driver, not sector fundamentals.

### 1. Sector Overview
Lumentum sells the optical engines of AI infrastructure — EMLs, pump lasers, transceivers, ROADMs and amplifiers — into datacom (now dominant) and telecom. The sector is in a violent up-cycle: 800G modules have hit peak ramp and 1.6T is converting from samples to revenue, with the transceiver market forecast to roughly double by 2036. Demand is increasingly concentrated in a handful of hyperscale/GPU-platform buyers, so revenue is levered to AI capex sentiment — as the selloff on the Marvell deal announcement ("AI spending jitters") demonstrated. Supply, not demand, is the binding constraint: EML/InP laser capacity is the gating input, and firms with owned fab capacity are pricing into scarcity.

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **1.6T transition + 800G volume doubling.** Each speed node multiplies laser content per port; 1.6T units projected at 3–5M in 2025 and >$1B value, with the ramp still early.
- **Operating leverage is dramatic and visible.** LITE swung operating margin from **-26.6% (FY24) to +17.4% (FY26)** on revenue nearly doubling — one of the steepest margin recoveries in hardware.
- **Oligopoly supply of EML/InP lasers.** Capital intensity and process know-how cap new entrants, letting incumbents hold price in a shortage.

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Customer concentration and AI-capex cyclicality.** A handful of hyperscalers/GPU platforms drive datacom demand; any digestion pause hits revenue sharply (FY24 revenue fell 23%).
- **Lumpy, order-driven revenue with history of air pockets.** The FY24 collapse from $1.77B to $1.36B shows the sector's boom-bust amplitude.
- **Deal overhang.** The Marvell transaction is all-stock and drew shareholder criticism from *both* sides ("trash offer"), capping near-term upside via the exchange ratio rather than sector fundamentals.

### 5. Target's Competitive Position: **STRONG**
LITE combines (a) scarce, vertically-integrated laser/photonics IP, (b) top-of-peer growth, and (c) best-in-class margin conversion. It is the highest-quality asset in the optical component set and commands the sector's largest market cap ($62B > COHR $39B). Caveats: margin durability is unproven through a full down-cycle (FY24 evidence), and the pending Marvell combination transfers the decision to a deal exchange ratio — so "strong" applies to *operating* position, while *equity* outcome now hinges on merger mechanics.

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**
Structural AI-datacom demand, a supply-constrained oligopoly, and early 1.6T ramp support a multi-year growth runway with real pricing power. The rating carries a high-beta caveat: revenue is concentrated and cyclical, and the sector trades on AI-capex sentiment — the Marvell/Lumentum announcement and its shareholder reception is the immediate swing factor.
