## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **Medium**

**Positives (genuine leader):** Tesla's 2025 Impact Report claims ~37M tonnes CO₂e avoided; lifecycle emissions per vehicle are roughly half an equivalent ICE (US model ~52 t CO₂e avoided on average); net-zero GHG by 2040 target and 100% renewable-electricity operations goal. Climate transition risk is **Low** — Tesla *is* the transition product and faces minimal stranded-asset/carbon-tax exposure. Scope 3 occurs mostly in the supply chain, which is monitored via conflict-minerals audits (3TG) and supplier due-diligence frameworks.

**Negatives:** Water stress and hazardous discharges at Grünheide (forest clearing, drinking-water pollution allegations vs. Berlin aquifer); energy/water intensity of battery and lithium refining; growing dependence on regulation to monetize environmental output — see credits below. Physical climate risk **Low–Medium** (Texas/Nevada water, storm exposure).

**Key financial wrinkle:** Regulatory credits ran ~$1.79B in a recent period — **>10% of gross profit**, materially higher share of net income in weak quarters. As rivals electrify and US federal credits lapse (analysts cut 2025 credit revenue ~40% to ~$1.5B), this environmental headwind **fades structurally** — a margin risk, not a tailwind, going forward.

---

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **High**

- **Product safety (most material):** NHTSA opened an inquiry into 2.4M FSD-equipped vehicles (Oct 2024, reduced-visibility collisions) and a broader probe into **2.9M vehicles** (Oct 2025) with 58 reported FSD traffic-violation incidents, 14 crashes, 23 injuries — now escalated toward engineering analysis. Rolling Autopilot recalls (2M+ vehicles) and the "FSD (Supervised)" naming itself are regulatory flashpoints. This is financially material not just via recall costs but because **autonomy is the equity story**.
- **Brand/social licence:** Consumer backlash tied to CEO political alignment is real and quantified: European registrations fell ~28% in 2025 (Germany −48%), Q1-2025 global deliveries −13% YoY, FY2025 deliveries expected −8.5% — amid EV market growth. This is demand destruction driven by the social dimension, not product quality.
- **Labour:** Historic Fremont injury under-counting (722 injuries/yr; ~30% above peer rates; Cal/OSHA citations), NLRB findings (2018 CEO anti-union tweet upheld), California CRD racial-harassment litigation, and an ongoing Swedish IF Metall blockade since Oct 2023. Improvements made, but the record is stubbornly above peers (Toyota, VW).

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10**

- **Structure:** Independent chair (Robyn Denholm; Musk holds CEO only — no formal duality since 2018), nominal majority-independent board. Auditor PwC. Clean basics.
- **What drags the score:** (1) **Extreme key-person concentration** — Musk is simultaneously CEO, product architect, and brand; succession risk is unquantifiable in the current multiple. (2) **Compensation misalignment with checks and balances:** the $56B 2018 grant was voided twice by Delaware Chancery (Jan 2024; Dec 2024 rejection of ratification) before the **Delaware Supreme Court reinstated it on Dec 19, 2025** — options now worth **>$140B**, making Musk the highest-paid CEO in corporate history with historically weak independent oversight of the grant process. (3) **Related-party entanglement:** recurring transactions with xAI, SpaceX, and X; 2026 prediction markets pricing Tesla–SpaceX merger odds >50% — a related-party conflict at the equity-structure level. (4) SEC settlements over Musk's tweets; political role (DOGE) creating perceived conflicts with business interests. (5) Removed from S&P 500 ESG Index in 2022 partly on governance/codes-of-conduct criteria.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US (relaxing):** SEC proposed **full rescission of climate-disclosure rules (May 29, 2026)**; EPA has concluded CAA §202(a) does not authorize tailpipe GHG standards and is deregulating — less compliance burden, but also weakens the regulatory-credit economics and the "green premium" moat. IRA EV tax credit expected to lapse — credit revenue already being marked down.
- **EU (tightening — the binding frontier):** CSRD reporting applies to EU operations; **EU Battery Regulation** (carbon-footprint declarations, battery due diligence, recycling quotas) and **CSDDD** full-value-chain due diligence bite hardest here; indirect CBAM exposure via steel/aluminium and battery materials; EU tariffs on China-made EVs squeeze Shanghai→EU export economics.
- **NHTSA/FMVSS** is the single most consequential regulatory thread for the equity story (AV approvals and FSD oversight).
- Net: **regulatory risk is migrating from "compliance" to "safety enforcement + EV subsidy withdrawal."**

---

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **Laggard** (with an environmental asterisk)

Tesla is a best-in-class **climate-impact** company (genuine E leadership) but a **laggard on social, governance, and overall ESG index standing** — it was dropped from the S&P 500 ESG Index, is under active safety probes, faces self-inflicted demand destruction, and carries extreme key-person/related-party governance risk. On a rounded materiality basis (where safety and governance now dominate the risk register), the aggregate is **Laggard** relative to peers like Toyota on governance and labour, despite the E outperformance.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **Negative**

The ESG risk register now directly erodes the thesis rather than supporting it:
1. **Autonomy regulation** (S) is a first-order threat to the valuation's optionality — FSD probes at 2.9M vehicles can delay robotaxi monetization.
2. **Brand/social licence** (S) is measurably destroying demand in Europe at the same time Chinese competition intensifies.
3. **Governance/key-man and related-party risk** (G) — $140B+ pay package, SpaceX/xAI interlock, and merger speculation — inferiors shareholder protection versus the multiple paid ($1.4T, ~35x+ earnings).
4. **Environmental credit tailwind is fading** (E), removing a profit prop just as margins compress (Q2-2026: sharply lower operating income, negative FCF).

While the energy-transition tailwind stays positive long-term, the **near-to-mid-term ESG risk-adjusted stance is Negative** — recommend underweighting or requiring a material valuation discount to compensate for regulatory, brand, and governance tail risk.
