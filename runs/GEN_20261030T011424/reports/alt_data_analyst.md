### 1. Consumer Demand Signals — **Negative / decelerating**

**Google Trends, US, weekly (last 4 weeks vs. same 4 weeks a year ago):**

| Keyword | Oct-2026 avg | Oct-2025 avg | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Norton antivirus" | 35.8 | 54.3 | **−34%** |
| "Norton 360" (flagship SKU) | 29.5 | 43.3 | **−32%** |
| "Avast antivirus" | 43.8 | 78.3 | **−44%** |
| "McAfee antivirus" (control) | 35.3 | 36.5 | −3% |

Three readings:
- **The decline is GEN-specific, not category-wide.** McAfee is roughly flat YoY while both Norton and Avast are down a third or more. Category search (generic "antivirus software") failed on a 429 rate-limit, so the McAfee read is my only control — treat it as directional, not definitive.
- **There was a category-wide demand spike in early April 2026** (all four brands hit their 12-month peak in the weeks of 5–12 April, Norton ~100). That inflates the normalisation base for the whole window, but the YoY comparison above is apples-to-apples and still shows a wide GEN-vs-McAfee gap.
- **Interpretation, not alarm:** GEN's growth is no longer search-acquisition-led. Management disclosed partner channel revenue of $295M, **+40% YoY**, now **>20% of total revenue**, with employee-benefits/financial-wellness as the fastest lane. Fading direct-search interest is therefore a *modest* leading negative for direct-to-consumer gross adds, not for the guided revenue trajectory — but it does argue the DTC funnel is softening and that renewal/retention (not new-customer flow) is carrying the model.

**Web traffic:** similarweb public data shows **norton.com visits −4.81% MoM** (August 2026 reference). Directionally consistent with the search trend; I could not retrieve a monthly time series or visit counts. Low-quality input.

**App/store data:** not available from the toolset — gap.

- **Caveat:** the sample is dominated by 28–29 Oct post-earnings coverage, so it is largely *reporting the beat*, not predicting it. It carries near-zero forward information and a structural positivity bias on earnings days.
- The **prior 5-week news regime was sharply negative** (24 Sep: FT report of an unsolicited ~$12B approach for GoDaddy → GEN −12% in a day, −28% peak-to-trough to $20.85 on 28 Sep, on 3–4x normal volume, driven by leverage/debt concerns). The news-sentiment score is measuring the resolution of a self-inflicted overhang, not a new fundamental upcycle.

### 6. Overall Alt Data Signal: **BULLISH (low conviction)**

Supporting: 7/7 beat record, rising consensus staircase, second FY27 guidance raise, +40% partner channel growth, AI-driven margin expansion, M&A overhang removed, stock +35% off the 28 Sep low.
Offsetting: brand search interest down 32–44% YoY versus a flat competitor, norton.com traffic −4.8% MoM, and a +15.8% post-earnings gap that has likely already capitalised the near-term alt signal.

The honest framing: **fundamental momentum is real and confirmed; the forward-looking alt data I could actually measure is deteriorating.** The two reconcile only if the DTC channel keeps shrinking as a share of revenue — which is exactly what management says is happening.

### 7. Confidence Level: **MEDIUM**
Reasoning: earnings-surprise history and revision data are high quality and internally consistent (high confidence). Google Trends is high quality but is a weak proxy for a renewal-driven subscription model, and I had only one competitor control. Web traffic and job postings are single-point proxies. Patents and social sentiment are complete gaps.

**Data limitations and lag:** Google Trends lags 3–5 days and normalises to the in-window peak (April 2026 scare event compresses all other values); Similarweb public data is modelled, monthly and rough; Google Trends keyword choice may miss migration to app-based acquisition; the GoDaddy situation is unresolved in principle (talks described as early-stage, approach unsolicited) and a re-escalation would invalidate the entire bullish read. Alt data is a leading indicator, not a definitive one.

**What would flip the view:** (a) renewal of GoDaddy/targeted M&A chatter, (b) a third consecutive quarter of >30% YoY decline in Norton brand search, (c) partner-channel growth decelerating below ~20% — which would remove the only growth engine that alt data cannot see.
