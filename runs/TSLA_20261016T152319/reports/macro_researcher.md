# Macroeconomic Assessment — Tesla, Inc. (TSLA)

*Prepared as of October 16, 2026. Data: FRED, FMP, UST yield curve.*

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional, tilting risk-off**

The US sits in a delicate middle: the Fed has delivered a genuine easing cycle (effective funds rate cut from 4.33% in Jan-2025 to **3.63%** by mid-2026), growth remains respectable (~2.2% real YoY), and the labor market is stable but cooling (unemployment **4.2%** in Sep-2026, up from ~3.9% in early-2024). The 2s10s curve is **no longer inverted** (+34bp, steepening) — the classic hard-recession signal has cleared.

The problem is the **long end and the inflation tape**. CPI has re-accelerated to **~3.7% YoY** (Sep-2026) while the policy rate sits at 3.75% — a real funds rate of roughly **zero**, with no further easing room. The 10-year Treasury has backed up from ~4.4% in May-2026 to **5.0–5.2%** in early October, a sharp term-premium repricing. That is the worst possible constellation for a high-beta (1.92), long-duration growth equity like Tesla: rates have stopped falling where it matters (consumer credit, discount rates) and the USD is at cycle highs (~121.6 broad index). Stance: **risk-off for long-duration, internationally exposed cyclicals.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates / Fed policy — MIXED, net negative skew.** The 200bp+ of Fed cuts since 2024 are a genuine tailwind for vehicle affordability and Tesla's captive-financing/leasing book — meaningful for a ~$40–50k price point where payment-per-month drives demand. But the *long end* is what prices Tesla's equity: 10Y at ~5% and rising into October, with the market already re-rating the stock from the $480 December-2024 peak to ~$358 today. Tesla is a duration asset (auto + robotaxi/energy optionality priced decades out); every +50bp in the 10Y is a disproportionate haircut at beta 1.9. **Effect: Negative** (dominant, via discount rate) with a partial **Positive** offset (auto-loan affordability).

**Inflation — NEGATIVE.** Automotive margins are under simultaneous assault: sticky 3.5–3.7% CPI keeps battery-metal, steel, aluminum and freight input costs firming, while Tesla is *cutting prices* to defend share against Chinese EV competition and absorb the expiry of US EV purchase incentives (Q1-2026 deliver the weakest quarter in a year on that expiry). Q2-2026 already showed sharply lower operating income and negative free cash flow on high capex. Inflation re-acceleration with impaired pricing power = compressed gross margin. **Effect: Negative.**

**Growth / consumer — NEUTRAL-to-POSITIVE.** 2.2% growth with 4.2% unemployment is a soft-but-functional consumer backdrop, and the demand data are improving: H1-2026 deliveries of 838k were **+16.3% YoY**; Q2 (480k) beat estimates on recovering Europe/China demand; China-made EV sales +5% YoY in Sep-2026 and accelerating. A discretionary-durable franchise benefits most from a re-accelerating real-economy cycle, which we are not currently getting. **Effect: Neutral.**

**Currency & trade — NEGATIVE.** The broad USD index at 121.6 and rising hits Tesla on two fronts: translation of China/EU revenue back into USD, and a stronger dollar vs. the RMB/yuan undermines the competitiveness of Tesla's price-sensitive China position (and raises relative FX headwinds versus yen-based Toyota, its closest auto comparable). A silver lining: USD-priced commodity inputs (lithium, nickel, copper for the Megapack/energy business) get cheaper in local terms only marginally. Tariff/industrial-policy risk (US–China auto/tariff tensions, fading EV subsidies) remains live. **Effect: Negative.**

**Energy tailwind — POSITIVE.** The AI/data-center-driven demand for grid-scale storage is a genuine macro tailwind for the Energy segment — "the wrong Tesla being priced by the market" is an increasingly mainstream view — but it doesn't neutralize the rate/currency headwinds on the core auto book. **Effect: Positive (secondary).**

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

For a 1.9-beta, long-duration, internationally exposed consumer cyclical, the current configuration — **10Y at ~5% and rising, CPI re-accelerating toward 4% with a zero real policy rate, USD at cycle highs, and US EV tax credits expired** — is the worst of the "transitional" states: the Fed's cuts are done, long rates are repricing up, and the affordability gains from 2024–25 easing are being partially reversed by the stealth rise in long-term borrowing costs. Growth stability keeps this from being deeply unfavourable, but the skew is clearly negative.

## 5. Materially Surprising Scenario — **Bear: The Term-Premium Shock**

**Path:** Sticky inflation re-accelerates into Q4-2026 (CPI >4% on energy/shelter), forcing the Fed to halt — or even reverse — its cutting cycle exactly as fiscal supply balloons. The 10Y breaks **5.5%+** while a strong dollar (USD index >123) persists. 

**Transmission to TSLA:** (i) the long-duration equity re-rates violently — a 50–75bp further backup in the 10Y is worth a multiple-point compression on Tesla's forward earnings power at beta 1.9, pushing shares toward the **$250–270s**; (ii) auto loan rates re-harden just as affordability was recovering, choking the consumption-side recovery (deliveries already show the tax-credit cliff); (iii) a stronger USD hits China/EU revenue translation and price competitiveness. A positive scenario would require the mirror image: disinflation resuming to ~2.5% in 2027, Fed easing to ~3% with the 10Y back under 4.2% — that alone (plus China/EU delivery momentum) could carry TSLA back through **$450+** with no change in fundamentals. The risk asymmetry is real: valuation is more exposed to the 10Y than to any single delivery statistic, and the 10Y is currently the most misbehaved number in the macro system.
