**Critical accounting nuance — the reported leverage is misleading.** Reported total debt of $13,489M is *not* financial debt. It decomposes as:

- **Actual debt: $2,869M** ($999M current + $1,870M long-term)
- **Operating lease liabilities: $10,620M**

Against $6,230M of cash, TJX carries **net cash of ~$3,361M on a financial-debt basis**. The reported 1.32x debt/equity and 0.85x net debt/EBITDA are artifacts of lease capitalization (ASC 842). On an economic basis, **D/E is ~0.28x and net debt/EBITDA is negative (−0.39x)**. This is a conservatively financed, essentially debt-free retailer.

The Z-Score of 3.03 sits only 0.04 above the safe-zone floor — but this is a **false signal**. The tool's x4 term uses *book* equity ($10.2B) rather than market cap. Substituting the $154.3B market cap gives **Z ≈ 6.4**, deep in the safe zone. The low book equity is the product of decades of buybacks, not distress.

**Structural mitigant:** the off-price model is *countercyclical*. TJX buys closeout inventory when full-price retailers are liquidating, so gross margins typically **expand** in consumer downturns. This is the single most important risk offset in the entire profile.

2. **S&P downgrade to "A" from "A+"** — directionally negative, though from an exceptionally high base. Rationale not retrievable (S&P page returned HTTP 403); **data gap flagged**.

3. **Quick ratio of 0.59** — below 1.0, but structurally appropriate: 100% of inventory is marketable closeout goods with rapid turns ($7,297M inventory). Not a liquidity concern in practice.

4. **Working capital declining** — $2,213M → $1,983M → $1,841M over three years, while the current ratio slipped 1.21 → 1.14. Worth monitoring; driven by rising current lease liabilities, not debt.

5. **Rate environment** — 10Y at 5.13%, 30Y at 5.47%, 2s10s at +39bps (no inversion). Rising long rates pressure the ~20x+ multiple more than the debt stack, given minimal floating-rate exposure.

6. **Tariff / import cost exposure** — TJX sources heavily from Asia; cost of revenue rose 6.6% in FY2026. Gross margin expansion has so far absorbed this, but it is the primary threat to the margin trend.
