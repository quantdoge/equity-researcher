# TJX Companies (TJX) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price:** ~$140–157 (recent range; $156.66 last close in feed, $140.30 in ratio feed) · **Market cap:** ~$155–176B · **FY end:** Jan-2026 · **Shares:** 1.125B diluted

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **WIDE**

TJX is the world's dominant off-price apparel and home-fashion retailer: T.J. Maxx, Marshalls, HomeGoods, Winners, HomeSense, T.K. Maxx, Sierra (~5,000 stores, $60.4B revenue, 377,000 employees).

The moat is **not** brand loyalty or switching costs — it's a self-reinforcing **buying-scale cost advantage**:

- **$41.7B of annual merchandise purchased** from a vendor network of 20,000+ brands and manufacturers, bought opportunistically at closeout prices. TJX is one of the few buyers on earth who can absorb a 500,000-unit irregular lot in a week, in cash, without return rights. That makes it the *buyer of first call* for distressed and excess inventory.
- **The flywheel:** more vendor relationships → first look at the best closeouts → better "treasure hunt" assortment at 20–60% off department-store prices → traffic → even more scale. This is efficient-scale plus a genuine intangible (the sourcing network), and it is not replicable by capital alone.
- **Evidence the moat has widened, not narrowed:** gross margin expanded from 27.6% (FY23) to **31.0% (FY26)**, +340bps; operating margin from 9.7% to **11.9%**, +215bps. Revenue grew in every year including through the 2022 inflation shock. That margin expansion in the face of wage and freight inflation is direct proof of pricing/sourcing power.
- **ROIC 30.3%** (FY26), and 25.4% → 29.2% → 29.8% → 30.3% — rising every single year. My own check: NOPAT $5.55B ÷ invested capital $17.45B = **31.8%**, roughly 4x WACC. This is the textbook signature of a moated business.

**Honest moat limits:** no consumer switching cost, no contractual lock-in, exposure to discretionary spending and import tariffs, and the September analyst question — *"What caused the slowdown at Marmaxx?"* — signals the core US division (roughly half of sales) is maturing.

**Reverse DCF — what the price requires:** to justify an EV of ~$183B at 8.5% WACC, TJX must compound FCF at **~14.5% annually for ten years.** Its six-year revenue CAGR is 6.35%; its best three-year FCF CAGR (COVID-distorted) was 23%. Demanding 14.5% for a decade from a business growing revenue 7% and expanding margins in a mature US market is heroic.

**Why the stock is expensive — multiple expansion, not earnings:**

Roughly **two-thirds of the last two years' return came from re-rating, not from business performance.** That is precisely the kind of return that does not repeat.

**Implied forward return:** $5.45 FY27E EPS, grown 10%/yr for 10 years, at a normalized 20x exit = $283 → **~6.9% IRR + 1.3% dividend ≈ 8.2%.** Market-like, not attractive.

**Margin-of-safety test: FAILED.** I require 25–30% below intrinsic value — roughly **$75–80/share**. At $140–157, TJX trades at a **35–55% premium to base-case intrinsic value** and above even my bull case.

## 4. Management Quality — **High (with one reservation)**

- **Ernie Herrman** (CEO since 2016) has presided over a near-tripling of the share price, disciplined "no-frills" store economics, and steady global expansion. Off-price retailing is operationally brutal; TJX has executed for three decades. Succession is a live but well-managed question.
- **Honest and conservative accounting:** clean FCF conversion, minimal goodwill ($96M), no aggressive capitalisation, full lease disclosure. The absence of acquisition-driven growth is a hallmark of discipline — TJX has built its moat organically rather than buying empires.
- **Shareholder-friendly:** dividend raised every year (quarterly from $0.26 in 2022 to $0.48 now, ~85% cumulative increase); ~$2.5B/yr of buybacks; 35% payout ratio.
- **The reservation:** buyback discipline is price-insensitive. Repurchasing $2.5B of stock at 32x earnings when intrinsic value is ~$100 destroys value. A Buffett-style allocator would cut buybacks aggressively at these levels. Manageable, but it means one leg of capital allocation is currently mis-priced.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (existing owners) / **DO NOT INITIATE** (new money)

Not a Sell — this is a 30%-ROIC, wide-moat compounder with 89% cash conversion and a fortress balance sheet, and selling a business of this quality on valuation alone incurs tax friction and forfeits the compounding. **But it fails every value test I apply at this price.** Mr. Market has already paid TJX full credit for a decade of flawless execution. There is no margin of safety, and the reverse-DCF implied growth rate is materially above what the business has ever delivered.

**Buy zone: below ~$105; aggressively buy below ~$85.** That requires a 30–40% drawdown — plausible in a consumer recession or a tariff-driven margin scare, which is exactly when you want to be a buyer.

*Surrounding sentiment corroborates the valuation call:* Jefferies downgraded to Hold ($145 PT), Gordon Haskett downgraded to Accumulate, Guggenheim cut its target from $175 to $152, and recent Morgan Stanley/Evercore/Barclays targets ($173–188) sit barely above the traded price — i.e., the Street sees ~0–15% upside for substantial risk.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Downside risks (business):**
1. **Tariffs and trade policy** — the single largest threat. TJX imports heavily from China and Vietnam; tariffs raise landed cost and disrupt the closeout supply that *is* the moat. Off-price can benefit from others' excess inventory, but sustained tariffs shrink the whole pool.
2. **Marmaxx deceleration** — the core US banner is maturing. The market's expectation of 7%+ growth depends on it re-accelerating plus international carrying the load (international carries structurally lower margins).
3. **Consumer health** — TJX's core customer is lower-to-middle income. A worsening labour market hits traffic with no offset, since TJX has no ability to raise prices above its already-discounted position.
4. **Competitive leakage to Shein/Temu/Amazon** — value-seeking shoppers have more alternatives than in 2015, and TJX has no e-commerce moat (online is a small, structurally disadvantaged piece of the model).
5. **Wage and freight inflation** — 377,000 employees; a tight labour market compresses the 31% gross margin the thesis relies on.

**Downside risk (valuation) — the dominant one:** ~32x earnings on a 7% grower has no cushion. **A simple de-rating to the peer median (23.7x) on unchanged $4.88 EPS is $116 — a ~25% loss with zero fundamental deterioration.** That is the asymmetry a value investor must refuse.

**Risk to *my* cautious view (the bull case against me):** if TJX sustains its recent 12–18% EPS growth via margin expansion to 13%+ operating margin plus international scaling, intrinsic value migrates toward $140–150 and today's price becomes merely fair. That is possible — but "possible" is not "probable," and I do not pay for probables as if they were certainties.

---

**Bottom line:** Outstanding business. Wrong price. TJX is a name to own in the buy zone, not at a 35–55% premium to intrinsic value. Put it on the watchlist at $105, back up the truck at $85.
