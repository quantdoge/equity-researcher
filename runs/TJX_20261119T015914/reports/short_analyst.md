### 2. Key Red Flags Found

**① Peak margins are being flattered by a disclosed one-time item — tariff refunds.**
Q2 FY27 reported gross margin 33.4%; *excluding a 2.0pt net benefit from tariff refunds*, adjusted GM was 31.4%. On $15.18B of revenue, 2.0pp ≈ **$304M of gross profit ≈ $0.21/share** — roughly **15% of that quarter's $1.36 EPS**. Q3 FY27 GAAP EPS was $1.39 vs. **$1.34 adjusted** (again excluding tariff refunds). The headline "+19% 9M EPS growth" ($3.94 vs $3.30) is therefore materially overstated; on adjusted figures growth is closer to ~+11%.

**② Gross margin has stopped expanding — the inflection quarter.**
FY22 28.50% → FY23 27.61% → FY24 30.00% → FY25 30.60% → FY26 30.96% → TTM 32.07%.
But Q3 FY27 GM was **32.85% vs 32.96% in Q3 FY26 (−11bp)** — the first YoY decline in the series. SG&A ratio also flat (20.09% vs 20.10%). GAAP operating margin **12.76% vs 13.04% YoY (−28bp)**. The margin tailwind that produced the entire EPS outperformance has run out.

**③ Cost tailwinds are already reversing in guidance.** Q4 FY27 EPS guided **$1.42–$1.45, below the prior outlook on higher fuel/freight costs** — vs. $1.58 in Q4 FY26. FY26's merchandise margin was explicitly credited to "lower freight costs and lower inventory shrink expense." Both are cyclical, not structural.

**④ Comps decelerating into a 2–3% guide.**
YoY revenue growth: Q1 FY27 **+9.2%** → Q2 **+5.4%** → Q3 **+5.5%**; consolidated comps +4% in Q3, but **Q4 comps guided +2–3%** — the slowest plan in eight quarters.

**⑤ Inventory days rising for two consecutive years — the one genuine balance-sheet flag.**
Inventory days 57.4 → 59.9 → **63.9**. Inventory $5,965M → $6,421M → **$7,297M (+22.3% over two years)** vs revenue **+11.4%**. Inventory is growing at ~2x revenue. Bulls will (correctly) call much of this deliberate "packaway" buying; but packaway inventory bought ahead of tariffs, into decelerating comps, is exactly how markdown risk gets manufactured. Watch H1 FY28 gross margin.

**⑥ Insider selling clustered at the all-time high.** (See §5.)

### 3. Beneish / Accruals Detail

Full component read: DSRI 0.921, GMI 0.988, AQI 1.010, SGI 1.071, DEPI 0.969, SGAI 0.982, LVGI 0.972, TATA **−0.0386**. Not a single index is in a stress range; the two highest (SGI, AQI) simply reflect sales growth and capex growth. Accruals have been negative for four straight years (−3.9%, −3.9%, −5.3%, −2.1%) — TJX converts earnings to cash better than almost any retailer alive.

**One honest note on leverage:** the Altman model's weak input (X4 = 0.398) and the reported $13.5B "total debt" are misleading. Funded debt is only **$2.87B** against **$6.23B of cash** — TJX is in a net *cash* position excluding ~$10.6B of operating lease liabilities. A leverage-based bear case is not available here. The composite earnings-quality score of 55/100 is depressed only by the FCF-conversion input (capex), not by accrual quality.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

- **Ross (+12% sales) and Burlington are growing faster than TJX on the margin**, and Ross is explicitly described as adopting the TJX playbook — copycat behaviour is how off-price differentiation erodes.
- **Off-price's structural advantage is counter-cyclical**: it thrives on vendor distress and excess inventory. Tariff refunds and normalising supply chains *reduce* the closeout firehose that TJX feeds on. The refunds are a one-quarter P&L gift but a signal that the sourcing environment is normalising.
- Extreme-value e-commerce (Shein/Temu/Amazon Haul) caps the low end; TJX is nonetheless cited as the #2 most disruptive force in retail, so this is a slow bleed, not a cliff.
- TJX Canada comp +6% shows international is fine; the pressure is at the US core where the base is largest.

**Verdict: moat intact but no longer widening. Growth reverting to mid-single-digit comps is the base case, not a bear case.**
