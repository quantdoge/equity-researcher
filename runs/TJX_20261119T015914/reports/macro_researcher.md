## 1. Macro Environment Summary — *Transitional, risk-off at the long end*

The US is in an unusual and unstable regime: **solid real growth, a re-accelerating headline inflation impulse, a Fed that has resumed hiking, and a violent bear-steepening of the curve driven by term premium rather than inflation expectations.** The Fed raised the target range to 3.75–4.00% in September 2026 — the first increase in three years — then paused on Oct 28 after headline CPI moved to ~3.8%. Effective fed funds sits at 3.88% (Oct 2026), up from a 3.63% trough (May–Aug 2026).

Critically, the bond selloff is **not** an inflation-expectations story: 5-year breakevens have *fallen* to 2.23% (Nov 18) from 2.48% in June. The 10Y at 5.10–5.13% is therefore almost entirely **real-rate and fiscal-term-premium**. The 5Y real yield is ~2.7%. That is a discount-rate shock, not a growth shock — and it explains TJX's 27% peak-to-trough drawdown from $167.29 (June 12) to $122.41 (Sept 16) despite flawless operating results.

Credit conditions remain **easy** — HY OAS at 3.43% (Nov 17), having touched ~2.65% in September, among the tightest prints on record. Equities (VIX 24.6) are more nervous than credit. This divergence — 5.1% Treasuries with 3.4% high-yield spreads — is the single most fragile feature of the tape.

The consumer is **K-shaped**: real retail sales are running +6.8% YoY (Oct 2026: $655.3B vs $613.6B), unemployment is 4.1%, yet University of Michigan sentiment is 48.1 (Sept) — down 33% from 71.7 in Jan 2025 and near record lows. The gap is being bridged by **savings depletion**: the personal saving rate has fallen to 3.8% (Sept) from 5.6% (Jan 2026) and 6.2% (Apr 2025). Spending is real but increasingly fragile.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**POSITIVE — Consumer trade-down (largest driver).** Sentiment at 48 and a 3.8% saving rate describe a household under stress but not yet cutting nominal spend. That is precisely the environment in which off-price takes share. Confirmed in the numbers: Q3 FY27 (ended Oct 31) net sales **$16.0B, +6%; comps +4%**; Marmaxx improved to +2% (vs +6% LY) while **HomeGoods, TJX Canada and TJX International all led**. Consumers are trading out of home-improvement/big-ticket into affordable decor and branded apparel — a housing recession (mortgage rates tracking a 5.1% 10Y) is *bullish* for HomeGoods.

**POSITIVE — Pricing power / COGS in an inflationary tape.** Headline inflation at 3.8% is a cost headwind for a 377,000-employee retailer, but TJX's opportunistic pack-away model buys into vendor distress and cancelled orders. Margins are *expanding*: Q1 FY27 gross margin 31.3% vs 29.5%; Q2 adjusted 31.4%; Q3 pretax margin 13.0% (adjusted 12.5%, −20bp on wage/incentive investment, above plan). Very few retailers convert 3.8% CPI into margin expansion — this is structural alpha in an inflationary regime.

**POSITIVE — Trade policy reversal.** Q3 included **IEEPA tariff refunds** worth $0.05 EPS ($0.19 over 9M, i.e. FY27 EPS $3.94 reported vs $3.75 adjusted, +14%). A tariff-refund regime is a direct source-of-goods tailwind and widens the cost advantage over full-price import-dependent peers.

**NEUTRAL — Interest-rate sensitivity.** TJX is not a classic rate-sensitive business: essentially no refinancing wall, strong free cash flow, $1.5B returned in Q3 alone and a $3B buyback. The exposure is **valuation duration**, not solvency. At ~$140.30 and $154B market cap, TJX trades in the mid-20s P/E — a long-duration compounder. Each 50bp of real-yield increase compresses the multiple ~2–3x. This, not fundamentals, drove the June–September de-rating.

**NEGATIVE — USD strength.** The broad dollar is up 3.8% in nine weeks (118.21 → 122.65). TJX Canada (~10% of sales) and TJX International (~15%) were the *strongest* divisions in local currency in Q3; a persistently firmer dollar converts that outperformance into a reported-revenue and EPS translation drag in Q4 FY27/FY28. Guidance assumes a stable dollar — a move toward 128–130 is an unhedged risk to the raised outlook.

**NEGATIVE — Consumer fragility at the margin.** Savings at 3.8% cannot fund +6.8% retail sales growth indefinitely. Card delinquencies (2.85%) are improving, which argues the low-income consumer is holding up — but the *rate of change* in real disposable income is the variable to watch into 2027.

**Watch item:** management explicitly flagged **unseasonably warm US/Canada weather** holding back Q4 categories while still guiding to accelerating holiday momentum. That is a demand cushion thinner than the +4% comp headline implies.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Decomposed:
- **Demand channel: Favourable** (trade-down, +4% comps in a 48-sentiment tape)
- **Cost/margin channel: Favourable** (gross margin +180–270bp, tariff refunds)
- **Discount-rate channel: Unfavourable** (10Y 5.13%, 5Y real ~2.7%, Fed hiking)
- **FX translation channel: Mildly unfavourable** (USD +3.8% in 9 weeks)

Net neutral for the *equity*, favourable for the *business*. TJX should continue to outperform discretionary retail on a relative basis, but with the 10Y just breaking above 5.1% and the 30Y at 5.47%, multiple-led upside is capped even as earnings estimates rise. The Nov 18 +10% gap ($129.85 → $140.30) gives back roughly a third of that; the stock remains ~16% below its June high.

## 5. Material Surprise Scenario — **BEAR (primary): term premium becomes a credit event**

The market is priced for a modest hiking cycle (2Y at 4.74%, 86bp above effective fed funds) while HY spreads sit at 3.43%, near record tights. That combination is unstable. If headline CPI pushes toward 4.5% on $82.72 crude plus dollar weakness, the Fed resumes hiking to 4.50%+ by mid-2027 and the 10Y clears 5.75% (5Y real ~3.2%):

- HY spreads re-widen to 5%, consumer credit tightens, and card delinquencies — currently improving at 2.85% — reverse above 3.25%
- TJX's multiple compresses from ~25x to ~18x (roughly −28%), more than offsetting the 5% EPS growth it just delivered
- USD rallies to 128–130, stripping a further 1–2% off International and Canada reported sales
- **Estimated downside: −20% to −25%**, and the trade-down demand cushion does *not* protect the multiple

**Bull alternative (lower probability, high payoff):** the IEEPA refunds foreshadow a durable tariff rollback and energy retraces below $70. Headline CPI falls to ~2.5% by H1 2027, the Fed delivers two cuts, the 10Y returns to 4.30% and the dollar slides to 116. TJX then gets multiple re-rating *and* FX translation relief *and* further margin expansion on top of a 3.8%-savings-rate consumer still trading down — a +30% outcome. Watch the 5Y breakeven (2.23% and falling) as the leading tell that this path is being priced.
