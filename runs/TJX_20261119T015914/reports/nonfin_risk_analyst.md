### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — LOW-to-MODERATE, no active federal enforcement

- **EDGAR full-text enforcement search: zero SEC enforcement actions naming TJX.** No DOJ or SEC investigation found in feed coverage. This is the single most favourable data point in this report.
- **No active securities class action identified** (2026 securities-litigation trackers show nothing TJX-specific).
- **Historical/closed matters (pattern matters more than the dollars):**
  - **CPSC $13M civil penalty (2022)** — knowingly distributing product from 21 recalls (incl. products tied to child deaths). Largest-ever CPSC penalty at the time; establishes a product-safety compliance record.
  - **California hazardous-waste multi-county stipulated judgment — $2.05M** ($1.8M civil penalties) for improper disposal at stores.
  - **NYSDOL recovery of $674,526 in back wages for 7,577 TJX employees.**
  - **Credit-card interchange fee antitrust litigation** — settled in Q4 FY2026 (per 10-K).
  - **2007 payment-data breach** — ~45.7M+ cards; total cost estimated ~$256M; the reference case for retail cyber liability.
- **Pending/ongoing litigation themes:** false "reference price"/deceptive-discount class actions (California and Florida), a disability-access class action alleging merchandise-blocked aisles, and recurring wage-and-hour/overtime collective actions (including loss-prevention investigators).
- **Pending approvals:** none material (no FDA/FCC-type gating).
- **Live policy exposure:** IEEPA tariff litigation/refund mechanics — TJX received **$331M of tariff refunds**; the refund regime's durability is a policy risk, not a legal one.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Import/tariff and vendor-concentration risk (highest):** The off-price model depends on a deep but geographically concentrated Asian vendor base and opportunistic buying. TJX has quantified tariff sensitivity (duty-cost reduction targets, e.g. ~$180M → ~$70M) and booked $331M of IEEPA refunds — meaning **earnings currently benefit from a policy windfall that can reverse**. A refund clawback, adverse Supreme Court/congressional action, or a renewed tariff escalation would hit both margin and merchandise availability.
- **Cybersecurity and legacy technical debt:** A sprawling e-commerce estate (tjmaxx.com, marshalls.com, sierra.com, tkmaxx.com) plus in-store payment systems and the 2007 breach legacy make TJX a permanent target. **No 2026 cyber incident was disclosed** in the news/regulatory feeds reviewed — a genuine positive — but the absence of a disclosed breach is not evidence of strong controls, and a repeat event would carry outsized class-action and brand cost.
- **Labour, product-safety and environmental compliance across ~5,000 stores / 377,000 employees:** The recurring pattern (wage/hour collectives, CPSC penalty, California hazardous-waste judgment, NYSDOL back wages) indicates **distributed compliance failures in store-level execution** rather than a single systemic defect. With aggressive unit growth (new banners, international expansion), compliance infrastructure is a scaling constraint. Secondary: shrink/organized retail crime — industry data shows a 12.4% decline in shoplifting incidents in 2026, easing this risk.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — MODERATE and rising

- **Tariff-refund controversy is the live issue.** ProPublica, The Guardian, NY Post and Inc. (Nov-2026) report TJX kept **$331M in refunded IEEPA tariffs** and accrued **$112M for year-end employee incentives/bonuses** rather than passing savings to shoppers. **Senator Adam Schiff has publicly criticised companies retaining refunds.** This is a politically-charged, consumer-facing narrative that directly contradicts the "value/chynaa" brand promise of TJ Maxx and Marshalls. Escalation path: congressional letters, state AG inquiry, consumer press pile-on. Currently low financial cost, high headline risk.
- **Pricing-integrity allegations** (false reference prices / fake "compare-at" discounts) attack the core value proposition rather than a peripheral claim — the most brand-damaging category of consumer litigation for an off-price retailer.
- **Human-rights/supply-chain scrutiny:** A shareholder proposal on human-rights due diligence in the supply chain was filed; third-party benchmarks (KnowTheChain) scored TJX **4 of 26 points (2020)**. Vendor Code of Conduct prohibits forced/child/prison labour and a Modern Slavery Statement exists, but UFLPA-era diligence beyond Tier 1 is a documented gap — an import-detention (WRO/CBP) event would be both operational and reputational.
- **Accessibility/ADA litigation** is a modest, recurring brand irritant.
- **Counterweights:** Q3 FY27 comps +4%, raised FY27 outlook, $3B buyback, 22 analysts at "Moderate Buy," and no boycott or recall-driven consumer backlash in the period. Brand equity is intact.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Strong: clean SEC/DOJ record, no securities litigation, no disclosed cyber incident, credible independent-director and Lead-Director scaffolding, heavy insider ownership. Offsets: an entrenched non-independent executive chair, persistent store-level compliance dings, a highly concentrated import/tariff dependency, and an unmanaged-tone tariff-refund controversy that is only weeks old.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Tariff-refund retention + bonus accrual ($331M in / $112M to incentives) with Senate-level criticism.** Highest-velocity reputational risk. Request: any 8-K/10-Q language on refund accounting, contingency disclosure, and government-inquiry risk. Watch for congressional correspondence or state AG activity in the next 60 days.
2. **Insider selling cluster with zero open-market purchases over 12 months**, including the CEO's $10.7M June sale at ~$158 and ~$14M aggregate quarterly sales. Not a fraud indicator on its own, but a persistent one-way tape should be tracked against the pre-announcement window for Q3 FY27.
3. **Non-independent Executive Chairman (Meyrowitz, 72, ex-CEO, 20-year director) combined with CEO tenure since 2016 and a new CFO** — succession and oversight concentration. Escalate if a shareholder proposal to require an independent chair attracts meaningful support.
4. **Pricing-integrity litigation pattern** (false reference pricing) — brand-core risk; escalate on any California UCL injunction or AG action.
5. **Product-safety compliance track record** (CPSC's then-record $13M penalty for selling recalled goods) — any new recall-distribution event should be treated as a governance failure, not an operating accident.
