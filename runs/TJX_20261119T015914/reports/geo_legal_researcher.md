**Key structural observations**

- **All 10 operating countries are high-income, high-rule-of-law jurisdictions.** No operations in Russia, China, Middle East, Africa, or Latin America. Zero direct exposure to US sanctions regimes (OFAC), export controls, nationalisation, or currency-repatriation risk. This is the single strongest mitigant in the credit story.
- **~22.5% of revenue is non-USD** (Canada 9.3%, Europe ~11%, Australia ~2%) → FX translation exposure, but no repatriation restrictions in any market.
- **Distribution:** ~31M sq ft of DCs across six countries (10-K).
- **Sourcing is the real geographic exposure — not the store base.** Buying offices across the globe; merchandise sourced from **more than 100 countries** from a universe of **~21,000 vendors**, including thousands of new vendors added in FY2026 (10-K, Item 1). This diversification is the core geopolitical hedge, but it means TJX is a price-taker on US trade policy.
- **Growth vector:** first TK Maxx store in Spain opened March 2026 (Barcelona), with continued Spain openings through FY2027; Europe store potential cited at 1,225 vs. 835 today. Expansion is into stable, mature Western European markets — low political risk, but adds EU regulatory surface (GDPR, consumer protection, works councils).

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US tariff policy volatility and the IEEPA refund reversal (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)

The Supreme Court invalidated a large share of the IEEPA tariffs. TJX disclosed **$331M in aggregate tariff refunds** "for a portion of the IEEPA tariffs it previously paid" (Aug 19, 2026 press release, via Yahoo Finance/ProPublica), and accrued **$112M of incremental incentive compensation and discretionary bonuses** against that recovery (Calcbench; Forbes, Nov 16, 2026).

The 10-K's forward-looking statement list explicitly names "international trade and tariff policies" and "tariffs on imports from China and other countries" among principal risks. The refund is a one-time benefit, not a structural margin gain — Q3 FY27 results (Nov 18, 2026) were flattered by tariff refunds (net sales $16.0B, +6%; comps +4%; EPS $1.39; TJX International comps +8%, the strongest division).

**Why it matters:** the refund is reversible in economic substance. Other tariffs remain in force under different statutes (Section 301, Section 232, reciprocal baseline) — PwC notes the ruling invalidates *only* IEEPA tariffs. USTR has launched Section 301 investigations into Bangladesh, Cambodia, and China (Mar 2026) and extended 178 China Section 301 exclusions only through Jan 9, 2027 — a recurring cliff-edge. A re-imposition under alternative statutory authority would re-introduce cost pressure into FY2028.

### Risk 2 — Asia sourcing concentration vs. a fragile US–China truce (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium)

TJX's off-price model is often described as a tariff *beneficiary* — it buys close to need and can pivot to cancelled orders and packaway — but ~100-country sourcing means it cannot fully escape duty pass-through. US apparel import shares have shifted hard: China fell from ~40% of US apparel imports in 2017 to ~26% in 2023, with Vietnam, Bangladesh, Indonesia and India taking share (Rhodium Group). Nearly 79% of US importers moved some volume out of China during 2025 (EAW Logistics).

The Nov 2026 Trump–Xi–Putin summit in Shenzhen exposed how thin the truce is: new US restrictions on China's access to advanced AI chips, Chinese rare-earth export-control suspensions extended only to Nov 9, and both leaders publicly invoking "protectionism" (Reuters, Nov 18, 2026). TJX is not a technology company, but a re-escalation that hits Vietnamese, Cambodian, or Bangladeshi rates would strike its sourcing base directly — and unlike full-price retailers, off-price buyers absorb cost shocks through *availability* and *margin mix*, which is harder to model.

### Risk 3 — North American trade architecture (Canada) and European demand/sanctions friction (Probability: Medium / Impact: Low-Medium)

**Canada (9.3% of revenue):** USMCA entered its annual review cycle on July 1, 2026 and the joint review concluded without resolution — trade policy toward Canada and Mexico is now a recurring annual negotiation through 2036 (Geolegalpolitics; ICPA). A 9.3% revenue segment in a single country with renewed tariff uncertainty is a discrete, identifiable exposure. TJX Canada's 589 stores and its Canadian dollar revenue also carry translation risk.

**Europe (~11% of revenue):** EU ministers continue to tighten Russia sanctions (113 new entities, Nov 16, 2026; new OFSI guidance for UK businesses, Nov 2026). TJX has no Russia operations, so direct sanctions exposure is effectively nil — but UK/EU sanctions compliance obligations attach to its UK and European entities, and the EU–China trade confrontation (anti-dumping duties, Chinese export restrictions on supply chains) plus a soft European consumer are the operative drags. The EU–US trade agreement took effect July 1, 2026, removing duties on a range of US goods — modestly helpful.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation posture (10-K, Item 3):** "TJX is subject to certain legal proceedings, lawsuits, disputes and claims that arise from time to time in the ordinary course of its business." TJX "has accrued" for loss contingencies and "evaluates pending litigation and other contingencies at least quarterly." **No material pending proceeding is disclosed as probable-and-quantifiable.** This is a clean Item 3 — a positive.

**Active/watch items:**

| Matter | Status | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **Tariff refund consumer class action** (Migliaccio & Rathod LLP investigation, opened July 15, 2026) | Pre-suit investigation | Alleged deceptive trade practices, false advertising, unjust enrichment, unfair competition — theory is TJX raised prices for tariffs, then kept the refunds without passing savings through. Putative class period Feb 2025–Feb 2026. **This is the most likely new consumer class action against TJX.** |
| **Wage & hour** (structural, recurring) | Historical settlements | $31.5M assistant-manager settlement (2020); $8.5M California settlement (~83,000 workers); $4.75M federal wage-and-hour (2016); active FLSA collective actions involving DC workers in Pittston and Philadelphia, PA. TJX's 377,000-employee, ~86%-store-based workforce makes this a permanent, low-severity cost of doing business. |
| **NYSDOL investigation** | Resolved | ~$675K recovered for 7,500+ workers across ~185 TJX stores (spread-of-hours violations). |
| **Data privacy** | Legacy + ongoing | 2013 payment-card breach (historic); now subject to GDPR/UK GDPR across European entities, CCPA/CPRA in California, and state privacy statutes. The 10-K lists "data security and maintenance and development of information technology systems" as a named risk. |

**Regulatory surface:** FTC (deceptive pricing/advertising — the core of the tariff-refund theory), state AGs (consumer protection; California, New York most active), DOL/NYSDOL and state labor agencies, EEOC, PCI-DSS/card-network rules, GDPR/UK GDPR and EU consumer law (expanding with Spain), and CBP/trade compliance (customs valuation, duty drawback, refund claims).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Tariff refund retention — the one genuine red flag.** TJX received $331M in IEEPA refunds and, per ProPublica/Forbes/Quartz reporting (Nov 2026), directed $112M to "incentive compensation and discretionary bonuses" rather than price relief. At least nine companies have been identified doing the same. This is **not illegal**, but it is a textbook predicate for state AG inquiry and consumer class action, and it invites political scrutiny. The plaintiffs' theory writes itself: "raised prices for a cost that was later refunded, then paid bonuses with the proceeds." Watch for California, New York, and New Jersey AG activity and for the Migliaccio & Rathod investigation converting to a filed complaint.
2. **No FCPA/AML/sanctions exposure of consequence.** No operations in sanctioned jurisdictions; no reported OFAC or DOJ matters. Sanctions risk is limited to UK/EU entity-level compliance obligations following the Nov 2026 EU/UK Russia designations.
3. **Wage-and-hour is chronic, not acute.** Expect continued $5–35M settlements every few years across a 377,000-person workforce. Not thesis-changing.
4. **Customs/trade compliance.** The refund claims themselves create exposure — inaccurate IEEPA refund claims or drawback filings could draw CBP review. Also monitor whether any refund was recognized in a period where the receivable was not probable (accounting/disclosure risk, SEC comment-letter risk).

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

### **MEDIUM** (leaning low)

**Rationale for the rating:**

**Mitigants (why not High):** 100% of store operations sit in the US, Canada, UK, Ireland, Germany, Poland, Austria, Netherlands, Spain, and Australia — every one a high-income, strong-rule-of-law, no-sanctions jurisdiction. There is no Russia exposure, no China store base, no nationalisation risk, no capital controls, no currency-repatriation risk, and no FCPA/AML/sanctions matter on file. The 21,000-vendor, 100-country sourcing base is the most diversified in global apparel retail. Item 3 discloses no material litigation. Beta of 0.59 confirms the market treats it as a defensive name.

**Why not Low:** the risk is concentrated in *trade policy and consumer-protection law*, both of which are unusually volatile in 2026–27. The IEEPA refund windfall is reversing out of the base, Section 301 actions against Bangladesh/Cambodia/China and the Jan 9, 2027 China exclusion expiry create recurring cliff-edges, the USMCA now renegotiates annually, and the tariff-refund consumer action is a credible, well-telegraphed new legal front. Add chronic wage-and-hour exposure across 377,000 employees and expanding EU privacy/consumer obligations with the Spain entry.

**What would change the rating:**
- **To High:** a filed consumer class action by a major AG or firm combined with a material adverse ruling; or re-imposition of broad tariffs under Section 301/232 that TJX cannot offset through opportunistic buying; or a USMCA breakdown hitting the Canadian segment.
- **To Low:** resolution/quiet dismissal of the tariff-refund claims, sustained tariff de-escalation, and continued comps strength in Europe (Q3 FY27 International comps +8% is a good sign).
