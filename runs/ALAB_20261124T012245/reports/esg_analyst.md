## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- **Structural advantage:** fabless model. Manufacturing emissions sit with TSMC/OSATs, so ALAB's Scope 1+2 is de minimis relative to a $2B+ revenue base. Estimated intensity <5 tCO2e/$M revenue vs. ~10–20 for fab-owning peers.
- **The real exposure is Scope 3 and physical:** purchased wafers/substrates dominate the footprint, and 100% of manufacturing is concentrated in Taiwan and East Asia. Taiwan water stress and seismic/typhoon risk transmit into ALAB's delivery schedule — an environmental risk that shows up as a *revenue* risk.
- **Disclosure is the weakness:** the public "Corporate Responsibility" page contains only REACH/RoHS/conflict-minerals compliance and ISO 9001 language. No GHG inventory, no SBTi target, no CDP response, no TCFD/IFRS S2 alignment, no product carbon footprint. This is well behind NVDA/AVGO/TSM and increasingly out of step with hyperscaler supplier scorecards, which cascade Scope 3 and renewable-energy requirements down the chain.
- **Offsetting positive:** product use-phase. Scorpio/Orion fabric claims up to 89% higher tokens/sec in an 8-GPU config — efficiency enablement is a genuine climate-solutions angle, though partially offset by Jevons effects on customer compute demand.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Talent concentration is the single largest social risk.** A 756-person company valued at $38.8B relies on a small cohort of elite SerDes/fabric architects in Santa Clara and an expanding Taiwan team. With the stock ~56% off its high and beta of 3.86, equity-based retention loses its anchor precisely when competitors (Marvell, Credo, Broadcom, NVIDIA) are hiring aggressively. Attrition here is a direct revenue risk, not a reputational one.
- **Health & safety: low risk.** No manufacturing workforce of its own; no disclosed incidents, OSHA citations, or labour disputes.
- **Supply-chain labour:** indirect exposure via Asian foundry/OSAT/assembly partners. Conflict-minerals (Rule 13p-1) compliance is asserted but smelter-level traceability is not publicly evidenced.
- **Diversity & human capital data:** none disclosed — no EEO-1 summary, no turnover metrics. Not a legal exposure, but it caps any "Leader" classification.
- **Community/social licence:** benign; US-headquartered, no controversial markets or products identified.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.0 / 10**

**Positives**
- **Independent Chair (Manuel Alba, since March 2018) separate from CEO Jitendra Mohan** — no CEO/chair duality.
- **Single class of common stock** — 171,281,952 shares entitled to vote at the June 2026 meeting, one vote each. This is a genuine differentiator versus dual-class semiconductor peers and avoids permanent founder control.
- **Say-on-Pay: ~96.9% support** (111.5M for / 3.6M against), with annual say-on-frequency adopted — strong compensation alignment.
- **Audit committee fully independent** (Jack Lazar, Chair; Craig Barratt; Bethany Mayer) and PwC ratified with 99.9% support. Board's semiconductor/financial literacy is high (ex-CEO Atheros, ex-CEO Ixia, ex-CFO GoPro, CEO of Synaptics).
- **No controlling shareholder, no poison pill disclosed** in materials reviewed.
- CEO 2025 total pay of ~$2.2M is modest in cash terms for a $38.8B company — heavily equity-weighted, which aligns with long-term value.

**Negatives**
- **Classified (staggered) board** — Class II directors elected only through 2029. Combined with a small board, this blunts shareholder ability to refresh the board.
- **Director-level dissent is material:** Michael Hurlston received only **68.3%** for (36.5M withheld); co-founder Sanjay Gajendra 85.4%; Craig Barratt 83.8%. Roughly a third of voted shares opposing one nominee signals proxy-advisor recommendations against and likely overboarding/independence concerns. This is a recurring AGM flashpoint.
- **Founder-CEO and founder-President both serve as directors**, and the Chair is a VC-affiliated director (Sutter Hill lineage) — insider-influenced board.
- **Minimal ESG governance infrastructure:** no board sustainability committee, no ESG disclosure controls, no CDO/CSO identified.
- **Amazon warrant (8-K, 2/5/26):** up to 3,262,299 shares (~1.9% dilution) at $142.82, exercisable to Feb 2033, vesting on up to $6.5B of purchases, with registration rights and anti-dilution. Economically sensible customer alignment, but it is an equity-for-revenue arrangement with a related commercial counterparty and should be monitored as a governance/dilution item.
- **Insider selling:** >$40M of disclosed sales including CEO RSU tax-withholding sells (Nov 2026). Routine in substance, but heavy in aggregate.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (Governance Leader, Disclosure Laggard)

ALAB is **structurally low-impact on environmental factors** (fabless), **genuinely well-governed on the dimensions that matter most for shareholder value** (independent chair, single share class, 97% say-on-pay support, credible audit committee), but **materially under-disclosed** for a $38.8B company and carries a **classified board plus a 32% withhold vote** that keep it out of "Leader" territory. There is no controversy record and no ideological exposure. Call it: governance structure ≈ top quartile; ESG reporting ≈ bottom quartile; net = Average.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

ESG factors **do not change the investment thesis** — neither as a tailwind nor as a hidden liability. The material non-financial risks here are already embedded in the fundamental risk case, and they are the same three the equity analyst should be underwriting anyway:

1. **Taiwan/TSMC concentration** — the dominant "ESG" risk is really a supply-continuity and geopolitics risk.
2. **Talent retention in a 756-person, high-beta equity-comp model** with the stock 56% off its high.
3. **Customer/contract concentration**, now formally deepened by the Amazon warrant.

**Where ESG becomes financial:** California SB 253 Scope 3 compliance (2027) will force a reporting build-out ALAB is not visibly preparing for — a modest opex/controls cost and a supplier-data-collection burden, not a valuation event. Board classification and the director withhold votes create proxy-vote and governance-screening friction that can exclude ALAB from some sustainability-mandate funds, a marginal flow headwind rather than a fundamental one.

**Watch items:** the Hurlston withhold vote trajectory (proxy-advisor posture), any de-staggering proposal, cadence of insider sales, first GHG inventory/SBTi commitment, and whether hyperscaler supplier scorecards begin demanding product carbon footprints.

**Bottom line:** ESG-neutral. Underweight ESG as a decision input for ALAB; overweight supply-chain geography and human-capital concentration — those are the non-financial factors that would actually move the cash flows.
