# Financial Risk Assessment — Morgan Stanley (MS)

**Coverage:** FY2025 annuals (period end 2025-12-31), 3-year price history vs SPY, current yield curve. Market cap ~$300bn.

---

## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **Investment Grade (Strong)**

Morgan Stanley is a Global Systemically Important Bank (G-SIB) and a top-tier global broker-dealer. Standard credit screens must be interpreted through a bank lens — corporate-style ratios (Altman Z, D/E vs EBITDA, EBIT/interest) are **methodologically misleading for financial holding companies**, where balance-sheet leverage is the regulated business model and interest expense is embedded funding cost, not financing risk.

The binding credit indicators are all strong:
- **Regulatory capital:** Standardized CET1 of **15.0%** at 12/31/25 (15.1% at Q1-26), **~330bps above the ~11.8% minimum** requirement — a wide, accreted buffer.
- **Credit ratings:** Morningstar DBRS affirmed **AA (low), stable trend**; Moody's/S&P/Fitch all in the single-A range (A1/A+/A+).
- **Earnings power:** Record FY25 net income of **$16.9bn** (up 26% YoY), ROE ≈ **15%** — top-quartile for a G-SIB. Revenue grew **11.5%** to $115.0bn.

**Note on the Altman Z of 0.38 ("Distress"):** this is an artifact of applying a manufacturing-firm model (x4 uses book equity, assets/liabilities ~1.09, equity/assets ~8%) to a bank. It does *not* indicate bankruptcy risk; no G-SIB with triple-A-profile capital and an AA rating is a distress candidate.

## 4. Downside Scenario — Revenue −20% (market/credit crisis year)

Model anchored to FY2022–23 experience (capital-markets downturn with revenues −25% year-over-year):

| Item | FY2025 base | Stress (−20% rev) | 2023 precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $115.0bn | **$92.0bn** | $88.3bn |
| EBIT margin | 19.1% | ~13–14% (comp ratio flexes) | 13.4% |
| Net income | $16.9bn | **~$9–11bn** | $9.1bn |
| ROE | ~15% | **~8–10%** | ~9% |
| CET1 | 15.0% | **~13.5–14%** (organic accrual) | n/a |

**Verdict: Survivable without external capital.** Even at 2023-style revenue compression, the firm remains solidly profitable, retains a >13% CET1, and has $540bn cash + $95bn working capital to absorb funding stress. No covenant breach risk (banks carry no EBITDA covenants), no refinancing cliff (LT debt ~$327bn, diversified maturities; curve **not inverted** — 2s10s +34bps). Historical drawdown of −29% brackets the realistic equity pain; this is a **returns shock, not a solvency event**.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Negative/lumpy reported FCF in 2022 (−$9.5bn), 2023 (−$36.9bn), 2024 (−$2.1bn)** — the headline 15.2% FCF yield is a working-capital artifact of 2025's balance-sheet expansion, not a stable cash-generation yield. Do not underwrite on it.
2. **Rapid balance-sheet growth:** total assets +17% YoY to **$1.42tn**; short-term debt up **$72bn** to $144bn. Growing reliance on short-dated wholesale/repo funding raises sensitivity to market-wide liquidity freezes (the SLR is the binding constraint — currently comfortable, but monitor).
3. **High equity market risk:** beta ≈ 1.2–1.35, annualized vol ~30%, max drawdown −29% — expect ~1.3x market downside in a selloff.
4. **Elevated funding costs:** 10Y at **4.99%**, 30Y at **5.37%** — higher-for-longer compresses NIM on a fast-expanding funding base; benign (non-inverted) curve partially offsets.
5. **F-score drag:** F5 (rising leverage) and F9 (falling asset turnover) — consistent with the leverage-fueled asset growth; watch that book growth translates into durable ROA (~1.2%).
6. **Earnings concentration:** Institutional Securities (trading/IB) makes results cyclically sensitive — FY23 net income was nearly half of FY25's.
