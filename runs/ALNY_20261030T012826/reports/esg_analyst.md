# ALNY — ESG Analysis (Alnylam Pharmaceuticals)
**Sector:** Healthcare / Biotechnology | **HQ:** Cambridge, MA | **Mkt cap:** ~$23.3bn @ $174 | **~2,500–3,700 employees** | Data as of latest available (Q3 2026 10-Q, Oct 29 2026)

## 1. ESG Materiality Map

For an asset-light, single-technology-platform commercial biotech, most traditional ESG factors are immaterial to cash flow. Four factors are genuinely material, in this order:

| Materiality | Factor | Transmission to cash flow / cost of capital |
|---|---|---|
| **HIGH** | **Product safety & clinical risk** (S) | Label warnings, boxed warnings, REMS, litigation — a safety signal on Amvuttra is an existential revenue event (TTR franchise = ~$4.2–4.5bn guided 2026) |
| **HIGH** | **Pricing & patient access / social licence** (S) | $477k/yr US list price; IRA negotiation eligibility, HTA bodies (CADTH/NICE), payer pushback → realised net price and volume |
| **HIGH** | **Disclosure credibility & board oversight** (G) | July 2026 guidance cut + securities class action → cost of capital, litigation reserve, management credibility discount |
| **MEDIUM** | **IP / patent & legal conduct** (G) | Patent estate is the moat; litigation outcomes (Moderna, Pfizer) move terminal value |
| **LOW** | **Climate / emissions (E)** | No carbon-intensive operations; Scope 1+2 small. Buyer-side supply chain (Scope 3 API/drug product) and EU CBAM on chemical inputs are second-order |
| **LOW** | **Diversity, community** (S) | Talent/licence-to-operate in Cambridge/MA labour market only |

**Key point:** ALNY's ESG risk is 80% **S** (product safety + price) and **G** (disclosure/oversight). Environmental is near-irrelevant to valuation and should be weighted accordingly — do not let ESG scoring noise obscure the two factors that can actually impair cash flow.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW**

- **Footprint:** Asset-light. Own manufacturing at Norton, MA (expanded) plus CMOs. No Scope 1-intensive processes, no water-stress exposure of note.
- **Disclosure quality is improving and above-average for the sector:** 6th Corporate Responsibility Report (May 19, 2026); reports Scope 1, Scope 2 **and material Scope 3**; new **Scope 1+2 reduction framework through 2030 anchored by an independent third-party assessment of the GHG inventory and methodology**; CDP submission; voluntary California SB 261 (Climate-Related Financial Risk Act) disclosure; first **double materiality assessment** completed — a genuine CSRD-readiness step.
- **Gaps:** No public net-zero or Scope 3 target; no TCFD/ISSB-aligned quantitative scenario analysis disclosed; emissions intensity per revenue is rising by construction as Amvuttra volumes scale (though absolute levels are trivial).
- **Controversies:** None found. No spills, fines, or remediation liabilities surfaced.
- **Verdict:** Financially immaterial. The 2030 framework + verified inventory is good hygiene and reduces future CSRD/SB 253 data risk, but no valuation impact.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM** (the single largest ESG swing factor)

**Negative vectors**
1. **Price/access:** Amvuttra (vutrisiran) lists at roughly **$477,404/yr** in the US ($572k/yr at the Canadian submitted price). In the TTR-CM indication the eligible population is large and growing — precisely the profile that attracts payer scrutiny, HTA restriction, and political attention. Amvuttra is a **strong candidate for a future IRA Medicare Drug Price Negotiation cycle** (selection for the 2028 cycle is announced by Feb 2027; combined Part B/D spend on a $477k therapy with a broad ATTR-CM label makes inclusion probable on current spending trends). Negotiated pricing from 2028 onward is a live net-price risk not in consensus long-term models.
2. **Product safety:** **Qfitlia (fitusiran)** carries a **boxed warning for thrombotic events and gallbladder disease**, and the 80 mg dose was **not approved** because of increased serious thrombotic risk — a reminder that this platform has a real safety tail. Historical precedent: the 2016 revusiran (ENDEAVOUR) programme termination. Any thrombotic or hepatic signal in the larger ATTR-CM population would be a Critical event.
3. **Class/competitive risk that markets read as scientific:** the July 9, 2026 failure of AstraZeneca/Ionis' eplontersen raised questions about RNAi performance in patients on background therapy — a reputational-scientific risk that amplified the July 30 selloff.

**Positive offsets**
- Patient access infrastructure meaningfully expanded: **$0 out-of-pocket for the majority of US patients**, **>95% of US residents covered across payer types**, Patient Advisory Council launched, Case Management and Access & Reimbursement teams **nearly doubled**, $2m/2yr Alnylam Challengers investment in care navigators in underserved Boston communities.
- Workforce: **90% of employees affirm Alnylam supports a diverse workplace** (up YoY); Great Place to Work certification in 10 countries; 30+ workplace awards; published **Human Rights Principles**; HRC Corporate Equality Index score of 90/100 (2023–24). Low union exposure; no labour disputes found. Attrition/turnover not disclosed — a gap.

**Verdict: Medium.** Good access architecture and strong employee metrics are offset by a high-list-price, high-volume-launch model that is structurally exposed to US and ex-US pricing reform. This is a *political/regulatory* risk wearing an ESG label.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **6 / 10**

**Strengths**
- **Independent Chair, no CEO/Chair duality:** Amy W. Schulman is Chair (and Lead Independent Director); Dr. Yvonne Greenstreet is CEO. Clean separation.
- **Board independence:** 11 directors (expanded from 10 with Benjamin Cravatt, Ph.D., effective June 1, 2026); overwhelmingly independent; substantive, sector-appropriate committees including a **Science & Technology Committee** (Sigal chair) — genuinely useful oversight for a platform company.
- **Compensation alignment:** CEO FY2025 total pay **~$14.9m** (reported at the **25th percentile** of the disclosed peer group — restrained for a company of this scale); directors receive **$75k cash + $200k RSU + $200k options**, i.e. heavily equity-weighted (~$475k/yr). Say-on-pay passed (111.77m votes for vs 133.4m shares outstanding — implying roughly high-80s/low-90s % of votes cast, i.e. a solid but not emphatic mandate).
- Auditor: **PricewaterhouseCoopers** ratified for FY2026; no restatements, no material weaknesses disclosed; CEO/CFO certifications clean in the Q3 2026 10-Q.
- Dedicated **Chief Ethics & Compliance Officer** (Piyush Sharma) with a published Compliance Program and Third-Party Code of Conduct.

**Weaknesses**
- **Staggered (classified) board** with three-year terms — a structural reduction in shareholder rights versus annual elections; no evidence of proxy access.
- **Disclosure credibility damaged in 2026** (see §5) — the most important governance development, and one that directly raises cost of capital.
- **OIG civil-monetary-penalty settlement** for late pricing-data submission shows a compliance-control gap in government pricing (AMP/ASP), a recurring pharma enforcement theme.
- No disclosed ESG-linked compensation metric; no explicit board-level climate or access KPI in the committee charters reviewed.

**Score rationale: 6/10.** Structurally sound, independent, well-committed board with restrained pay (would be 7–8), marked down for the classified board and, more importantly, for a 2026 disclosure failure severe enough to trigger securities litigation.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (−) for the thesis

**ESG is not the reason to own or avoid ALNY — but two ESG factors sit directly on the thesis's fault line.**

1. **The material ESG risk is indistinguishable from the investment debate.** The July 2026 guidance cut, the class action, and the OIG pricing-data settlement all point to one thing: a company whose **disclosure and government-pricing controls have not scaled as fast as its revenue** (Q3 2026 revenue ~$1.3bn, +52% YoY). That is a legitimate **governance discount** — I would apply a modest valuation haircut / require a wider margin of safety rather than a hard exclusion. Governance score of 6/10 is consistent with "investable, not exemplary."
2. **Social/pricing risk is the under-priced item.** Amvuttra's price and ATTR-CM breadth make IRA negotiation and HTA restriction close to inevitable. This should be modelled explicitly in terminal-value net pricing — most sell-side DCFs treat US price as durable. Combined with the boxed-warning precedent in the platform (Qfitlia) and the eplontersen read-through on RNAi in background-treated patients, **S-risk is the factor most likely to convert into a genuine earnings event.**
3. **Environmental is a non-issue for valuation** and generates no incremental cost of capital; the disclosure programme is a modest positive (CSRD/SB 253 readiness) but not a thesis input.
4. **Net:** No ESG-driven exclusion or re-rating warranted. **Neutral implication, with the following monitoring triggers:**
   - Any FDA safety communication, label change, or litigation on Amvuttra/ATTR-CM → immediate re-underwrite (Critical).
   - Amvuttra appearing on the **Feb 2027 IRA selected-drug list** → downward net-price revision.
   - Securities class action surviving motion to dismiss (post-Dec 2026) → governance discount widens; watch for **CFO/Chief Legal Officer turnover** as a leading indicator.
   - A disclosed **Scope 3/net-zero target** and ESG-linked comp metric would lift the G score toward 7 and is a cheap, credible management action post-controversy.

**Bottom line:** ALNY is an **ESG Average** company whose ESG profile is a governance-and-pricing overlay on a commercial/scientific story, not an independent driver. Treat the governance controversy as a **discount-rate and credibility input**, and the access/pricing exposure as a **terminal-value input** — both modest, neither thesis-breaking.
