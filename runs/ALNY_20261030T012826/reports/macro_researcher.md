## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional / Risk-Off for Long-Duration Assets**

The regime has changed character. After an easing cycle that took the funds rate toward 3.50%, the Fed **hiked 25bp on 16 September 2026 to 3.75–4.00% — its first increase since 2023** — because core PCE has run **above 3% in every month of 2026** and CPI re-accelerated to **3.7% YoY in September** (~4.5% annualised over the last three months). Growth is not the problem: **Q3 2026 real GDP came in at 3.4% SAAR** with unemployment at **4.2%**. This is a hawkish, inflation-fighting Fed in a strong economy — the worst combination for long-duration equity multiples.

The Treasury market is the transmission channel that matters for ALNY. The **10Y is 5.05%** (28 Oct), up **~61bp from 4.44% on 30 June**, and touched **5.31% on 5 October**. The curve has bear-steepened: 2Y 4.65%, 10Y 4.99%, 30Y 5.37% (**+34bp 2s10s, no longer inverted**) — a rise in *term premium*, not growth optimism, which is precisely the move that compresses the present value of back-end-loaded cash flows. FOMC projections point to **4.00–4.25% by year-end 2026**, i.e. the market is pricing the risk of one more hike, not cuts.

Meanwhile, policy risk is idiosyncratic and *sector-specific*: the **Section 232 pharma tariff (100% default on patented drug imports) became enforceable from 31 July / 29 September 2026**, with large-cap MFN signatories exempted until ~2029 while smaller biotechs face the levies. **MFN drug-pricing** remains the structural overhang on US launch pricing.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**What ALNY is, macro-wise:** a commercial-stage rare-disease/cardio-metabolic biotech — **Q3-26 net product revenue $1.29B, +52% YoY** (AMVUTTRA $1.13B), FY26 TTR guidance reset to **$4.2–4.5B**, market cap **$23.3B at $174** (~4.9x FY26 product sales). Demand is *medically* driven, not economically sensitive: ATTR-CM and rare-disease patients do not defer therapy in a slowdown. **Revenue is largely GDP-insensitive; the equity is highly rate-sensitive.**

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL — with a negative valuation bias**

The **operating business is macro-resilient**: non-cyclical demand, US-based manufacturing aligned with the tariff regime, strong pricing power, negligible refinancing risk. On fundamentals ALNY is a macro *beneficiary* of nothing and a macro *victim* of nothing much.

The **equity**, however, is a long-duration asset in a rising-real-rate, hawkish-Fed, term-premium-widening environment. That is a persistent 10–20% multiple headwind that can swamp good operating news — as 2026 has demonstrated. My rating is therefore Neutral at the business level, Unfavourable at the security level.

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**Bull surprise (under-priced): a fast disinflationary turn forces a Fed pause and a 10Y back to ~4.20–4.40%.**
Consensus is positioned hawkish — dots imply another hike, and the long end has been bleeding for four months. But September CPI's strength was concentrated in a few categories, and shelter/rent disinflation is still working through. If Q4 prints come in at 0.1–0.2% core m/m and the Fed signals a halt at the December meeting, the **10Y could retrace 60–80bp**. For a name that has de-rated 57% while growing revenue 52%, that is the single most powerful catalyst available — a **30–45% re-rating** on multiple expansion alone, on top of any AMVUTTRA guidance stabilisation. Notably the stock's beta to the long bond has been far higher than its reported 0.27 market beta suggests.

**Bear surprise (also live): term premium blowout.** If the Fed delivers the second hike and 30Y clears 5.60% on fiscal-supply concerns, long-duration biotech de-rates another leg regardless of earnings. Probability-weighted, I would put ~30% on the bull disinflation surprise, ~25% on the bear term-premium scenario, ~45% on the current "higher-for-longer grind" base case.
