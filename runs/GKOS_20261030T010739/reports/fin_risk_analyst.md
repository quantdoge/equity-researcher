## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **Sub-Investment Grade (unrated), but with a genuinely strong solvency profile**

The headline rating and the underlying reality point in opposite directions, so both need stating plainly.

**The good news:** GKOS is a *net cash* company. Cash and short-term investments of **$278.8M** against total debt of **$139.6M** (of which only **$35.8M is long-term borrowings** — the balance is predominantly facility finance leases) produces **net cash of ~$139.1M** on a debt basis. Debt/equity is **0.21**, debt/assets **0.16**, equity/assets **0.73**. Critically, the company **exchanged its convertible notes for common stock during 2025** — interest expense fell from $13.6M (2023) → $10.0M (2024) → **$4.6M (2025)**, and long-term debt dropped from $282.8M to $35.8M. The refinancing cliff has been *removed*, not deferred. There are no near-term maturities of consequence.

**The bad news:** the company has never earned an operating profit. FY2025 operating income was **−$86.7M** on **$507.4M** of revenue (op margin **−17.1%**), net income **−$187.7M**, EBIT **−$188.4M** on the reported basis, and free cash flow **−$54.0M**. Interest coverage of **−18.7x** is an artifact of negative EBIT, not a covenant stress — but it correctly flags that the business does not yet cover its own cost of capital from operations. It is unaudited by any rating agency and carries **no public credit rating or CDS spread** (data gap).

**Verdict:** default risk over the next 24 months is **low** because liquidity is ample and debt is small. But the credit is *speculative* on earnings quality and self-funding capacity, hence Sub-Investment Grade.

### ⚠️ Two metrics are actively misleading here — flag them in the memo

- **Altman Z = 0.57 "Distress" is an artifact, not a signal.** Two of five components are structurally broken for this company: **x2 = −1.044** (retained earnings/total assets) simply encodes $933M of accumulated losses from deliberate R&D and stock compensation investment, and **x3 = −0.211** (negative EBIT). The tool also substitutes *book* equity for market cap in x4 (2.76). On the market-cap version x4 would be ~$14.3B/$237M ≈ 60x, which would mechanically produce a Z above 35 — equally useless. **The Z-score has zero diagnostic value for GKOS in either direction.** The honest reading is: real negative-EBIT stress (true), bankruptcy risk (false).
- **Piotroski F = 3 is a value-screen metric being misapplied to a growth-stage medtech.** It penalizes exactly what this company is supposed to be doing (no positive ROA, no positive OCF, no positive net income — 3 of 4 profitability points lost). What it *does* confirm is meaningful: **accruals quality is clean (F4=+1), gross margins are improving (F8=+1), and asset turnover is improving (F9=+1)** — i.e., the losses are cash-honest and operationally improving, not accounting-driven. What genuinely deserves a red mark is **F7 = 0 (dilution)**: shares went 47.4M → 48.4M → 52.8M → **57.2M**, +20.6% over three years (~6.4%/yr), with a further step-up from the convert exchange.

**Interpretation:** beta of 0.74 is a trap. Reported beta says "defensive"; realised volatility of **64.6%** — roughly 4x the market's — says the opposite. The low beta is a statistical accident of idiosyncratic, catalyst-driven moves (single-day gaps of −20% on 2025-02-21, +16% on 2025-10-30, −18% on 2025-07-31) that are uncorrelated with the index. **Do not size this position on beta.** The correct risk unit is the 3-year **−53.7% peak-to-trough drawdown**, a materially worse figure than the tool's 252-day −28.4%, precisely because the stock has since more than doubled from those lows.

**The dominant market risk is valuation, not leverage.** At ~$242/share and ~27x trailing revenue (roughly 18x the FY2026 guidance midpoint of $780M), the equity is priced as a successful, durable-growth franchise. A growth disappointment compresses the multiple *and* the terminal-value assumption simultaneously. The Sharpe of 1.64 is entirely a function of the trailing 12-month melt-up and should be treated as **non-repeatable**.

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue −20%

Assume a reimbursement shock, competitive entry, or an iDose TR / Epioxa launch stall that cuts revenue 20% from the FY2025 base ($507.4M → **$405.9M**), with gross margin held at 77.5% and **no cost action**.

| Line | Base | −20% Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $507.4M | $405.9M |
| Gross profit (77.5%) | $393.2M | $314.6M |
| SG&A | $329.3M | $329.3M |
| R&D (implied) | ~$150.6M | ~$150.6M |
| **Operating income** | **−$86.7M** | **−$165.3M** |
| **FCF** | **−$54.0M** | **≈ −$130M** |
| Cash | $278.8M | $278.8M |
| **Cash runway** | ~5.2 yrs | **~2.1 yrs** |

**Key finding:** the cost base is ~95% fixed relative to revenue. Because R&D + SG&A ($479.9M) exceed total revenue, **every 1% of lost revenue costs ~0.78 points of gross profit straight to the operating line.** A 20% revenue decline roughly *doubles* the operating loss and cuts cash runway from ~5 years to ~2 years.

**Mitigants (why this is not a solvency event):**
- Management controls ~$96M of discretionary opex (a 20% cut) plus $63M of SBC (12.5% of revenue) — the SBC line alone is a non-cash lever that would take the −20% case back to an operating loss of roughly **−$69M** and FCF of ~−$70M, i.e. ~4 years of runway.
- Capex is only $7.7M (1.5% of revenue), so there is almost no capital-intensity relief valve needed — but also almost none available.
- **No debt wall.** With $35.8M of long-term borrowings and $278.8M of cash, there is no refinancing cliff under any revenue scenario. This scenario threatens the *equity*, not the *enterprise*.
- Working capital is a modest tailwind, not a lever: receivables ($108.6M) and inventory ($63.6M) would release some cash in a downturn, but note receivables grew 79% YoY vs. 32% revenue growth — **collections are lagging growth**, worth monitoring.

**The real downside is the re-rating, not the cash burn.** At 27x revenue, a −20% revenue shock plausibly compresses the multiple to 10–12x, implying a 55–65% equity decline — consistent with, and worse than, the observed −53.7% three-year drawdown.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

**Upgrade pressure toward Low:** FY2026 guidance was raised twice (to **$775–785M** from $680–700M), Q2 2026 revenue was a record **$185.6M** (+50% YoY) with a narrowing loss, FCF has improved two consecutive years (−$78.0M → −$67.6M → −$54.0M), the convert exchange removed the only material debt maturity, and gross margins are expanding at 77.5%. On current trajectory the company approaches operating breakeven without needing external capital.

**Downgrade pressure toward High:** the business has no earnings cushion, cannot self-fund at current growth spend, is still diluting ~6%+/yr, and trades at a multiple that assumes flawless execution.

**Why not "Low":** persistent negative FCF, negative EBIT, no self-funding capacity, high SBC, and severe realised volatility.
**Why not "High":** net cash, 4.69x current ratio, no maturities, no covenant risk, and a visible, narrowing path to breakeven.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Negative EBIT and FCF in every year on record** (2022–2025). FCF has never been positive. Business is not self-funding; it is funded by equity issuance.
2. **Dilution: +20.6% shares over 3 years** (47.4M → 57.2M), with the 2025 convert-for-equity exchange adding a further step. Piotroski F7 = 0 flags this correctly.
3. **Valuation risk dominates the risk profile.** ~27x trailing revenue / ~18x forward. The −53.7% three-year drawdown is the right proxy for downside, not the −28.4% trailing figure.
4. **Receivables +79% vs. revenue +32%** — collections materially lagging growth. Watch DSO in the FY2026 10-K.
5. **SBC at 12.5% of revenue** — recast margins on a fully-loaded basis and the profitability curve flattens considerably.
6. **Negative net-debt/EBITDA and interest-coverage ratios are not meaningful** given negative EBITDA — do not present them as "strong coverage." They are undefined.
7. **No credit rating, no CDS, no debt maturity schedule disclosed in retrieved data** (data gap — a full 10-K debt footnote would confirm the lease-vs-borrowing split within the $139.6M).
8. **EBITDA inconsistency in the data feed:** reported FY2025 EBITDA of −$146.1M vs. operating income + D&A of −$44.4M. Also, income-statement net income (−$187.7M) differs from cash-flow net income (−$133.7M) by ~$54M, most plausibly the convertible-note exchange charge. **Flagged as a data-quality issue; the FCF and balance-sheet figures are internally consistent and are what I have relied on.**

## Data Gaps & Caveats
- **Quarterly statements were unavailable** — the tool returned annual rows only despite quarterly requests. The FY2026 figures above come from company disclosures (Q2 2026 results, raised FY2026 guidance of $775–785M), not from the tool.
- **Latest balance sheet is 12/31/2025.** Cash and debt have almost certainly moved in FY2026; the net-cash conclusion is directionally robust but the precise figure is stale.
- **Price history ends 2025-12-31 at $112.91**, while the current price is **$242.48** — the stock has more than doubled since. Volatility, beta, and Sharpe are computed on a trailing window that includes this melt-up; the 3-year drawdown statistic I calculated from raw price data is the more reliable downside anchor.
- No peer comparison was performed (deferred to preserve budget); a comp set on EV/revenue and FCF-margin trajectory would sharpen the valuation-risk conclusion.
