| Scope 1 intensity (tCO₂e/$m rev) | 1.83 | 1.36 | **1.05** |

**Positives**
- Total footprint ~2,818 tCO₂e is negligible in absolute terms — roughly the emissions of ~180 US homes. No carbon pricing, CBAM or stranded-asset exposure.
- Scope 1 intensity down **43% since 2023**; Scope 2 down 9.9% y/y — genuine decoupling from revenue growth.
- Completed a **TCFD-aligned climate risk assessment** in 2025 (physical + transition risk scenario analysis), ahead of most SMID-cap medtech peers.
- Obtained **external limited assurance** on GHG disclosures — a real governance differentiator at a $14bn market cap; most peers of this size do not assure.
- No environmental fines, spills, remediation liabilities or notices of violation identified in search.

**Watch items**
- **No Scope 3 inventory.** For a device maker, purchased goods (titanium, medical-grade polymers, travoprost API), contract manufacturing, EtO sterilisation and cold-chain distribution likely dwarf Scope 1+2 by 10–50x. This is the single largest disclosure gap and would block inclusion in most net-zero-aligned indices/funds.
- **No quantitative environmental targets** (no SBTi commitment, no renewable-electricity or waste-diversion goal with a date). Goals described as qualitative.
- **New Huntsville, Alabama R&D/manufacturing facility** (ground broken 2025) will add Scope 1/2 and increase fixed-cost/utilisation risk — but also shortens a currently elongated supply chain (net positive for supply-chain resilience).
- Physical risk: California HQ (wildfire/water stress) and Alabama (Gulf hurricane exposure) are both moderate-to-elevated hazard zones; mitigated by the fact that neither is a sole-source critical plant today.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Product safety — the dominant social factor**
- iDose TR (travoprost intracameral implant, 75 mcg) label carries warnings for **narrow angles/angle abnormalities**, with reported AEs in the 2–6% range including raised IOP, iritis, dry eye and visual field defects; corneal endothelial cell loss is the key long-duration safety variable for a resident implant.
- **Material 2026 positive:** FDA approved an NDA supplement (28 Jan 2026) permitting **re-administration** of iDose TR. The original 2023 approval was effectively single-use because of insufficient long-term safety data — that restriction was a genuine commercial ceiling on lifetime-patient value. Its removal is the most important safety-regulatory event of the year and de-risks the multi-dose revenue model. It also shifts the monitoring burden to cumulative endothelial safety; expect KOL scrutiny as re-dosing data accumulate.
- **Epioxa** (epi-on corneal cross-linking) approved Oct 2025 — the first FDA-approved epithelium-on CXL. Safety advantage is structural (no epithelial removal → lower infection/ulcer/haze risk than epi-off), though the label flags caution in herpetic keratitis. Favourable risk profile; supports the safety narrative.
- No Class I recalls, FDA warning letters, 483-driven import alerts or product-liability verdicts identified.

**Access and community (relative strength)**
- **>$22m in cumulative product donations**; ~14,000 keratoconus patients served via Glaukos Patient Services; ~15,000 patients educated with advocacy partners; ~870 employee volunteer hours across 61 events.
- Copay/financial-assistance programmes for iDose TR — supportive of both access and commercial ramp.
- *Counterweight:* the procedural-pharma model creates genuine affordability friction; iDose TR economics depend on coverage decisions, and any payer pushback is simultaneously an access issue and a revenue issue.

**Human capital**
- **Voluntary turnover 7.7% in 2025** (up from 6.9% in 2024 and 5.3% in 2023) — a rising trend worth monitoring, but still well below typical US medtech (~11–13%).
- Retention levers strengthened in 2025: increased 401(k) match, strong ESPP participation, new "Culture Leaders" programme.
- Published **EEO-1 report** (2024) — better-than-average workforce transparency for a company this size.

**Social gaps:** no public gender/ethnicity pay-gap or representation-by-level data; no disclosed TRIR/recordable-incident rate for manufacturing; no supplier code-of-conduct audit metrics despite a filed Form SD conflict-minerals report.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (upper half of SMID-cap medical devices)

**Why not Leader:** combined Chair/CEO; no Scope 3 inventory; no quantitative environmental targets or SBTi commitment; no absolute safety/TRIR data; no independent chair; ~8% say-on-pay dissent against a 67:1 (and rising) pay ratio.

**Why not Laggard:** seven consecutive years of sustainability reporting; **third-party assured GHG data**; TCFD-aligned climate scenario analysis; 7/8 independent board with 38% female representation and genuine clinical/policy expertise; single-class share structure; new Code of Conduct; clean controversy record with zero enforcement actions; better-than-average workforce transparency (EEO-1).

This is a well-governed, controversy-free, low-carbon business whose ESG weaknesses are almost entirely *disclosure-completeness* issues rather than *behavioural* issues — the cheapest kind to fix, and therefore low-conviction as a negative ESG signal.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**ESG is not a thesis driver for GKOS in either direction.** It neither adds nor subtracts from the investment case, and there is no plausible ESG-related de-rating catalyst on the current evidence.

**What matters and is already in the numbers**
- The three financially material ESG items — iDose TR/Epioxa **product safety**, **FDA/quality compliance**, and **reimbursement access** — are already the core analytical variables in the clinical/regulatory workstream. The Jan 2026 re-administration approval *improved* this risk profile.
- Governance is adequate-to-good but capped by Chair/CEO duality and rising, above-peer pay growth. At ~18x forward sales the stock has no tolerance for a governance shock (restatement, disclosure failure, executive departure) — but nothing in the record suggests elevated probability.

**Where I would change my view**
- **Downgrade to Negative** on: any FDA warning letter/483 escalation at Huntsville; a Class I recall or endothelial-safety signal in re-dosed iDose TR patients; an FCA/AKS subpoena; or a failed say-on-pay (<70%) at the 2027 AGM.
- **Upgrade to Positive** on: a Scope 3 inventory plus SBTi-validated targets; appointment of an independent chair; quantified waste/energy/renewable targets; and publication of TRIR and representation-by-level data. Combined, those would move GKOS to a genuine small-cap medtech ESG Leader and unlock ESG-mandate ownership.

**Positioning read:** ESG screens are not a source of incremental demand or supply for this name. The absence of controversies means no exclusion risk for ESG funds; the absence of Scope 3 and targets means no inclusion catalyst either. Treat ESG as a **monitoring function on product safety and healthcare-compliance**, not a valuation input.
