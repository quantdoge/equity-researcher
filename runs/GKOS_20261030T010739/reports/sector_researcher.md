# Sector Brief — Glaukos Corporation (GKOS)
**Sector:** Healthcare / Medical – Devices (ophthalmic pharma + device) | **Price:** $242.48 | **Mkt cap:** ~$14.3bn

## 1. Sector Overview

Glaukos competes at the intersection of two adjacent ophthalmic markets: **MIGS/glaucoma surgery** (market estimates range widely, ~$0.6–1.8bn today growing ~5–10% CAGR, depending on whether drainage devices and lasers are included) and **cataract surgery devices** (~$8.4–9.2bn, ~7% CAGR). The category is structurally attractive on demographics but **growth-poor for incumbents**: the two scaled players, Alcon (ALC) and Bausch + Lomb (BLCO), are growing revenue only ~5–6.5%, and the listed pure-plays have gone into reverse — STAAR −23.7%, RxSight −3.9%, Sight Sciences −3.1%. The sector is therefore **bifurcated**: mature, cash-generative, low-growth device franchises on one side, and a small group of drug-device convergence assets (sustained-release glaucoma implants, corneal cross-linking) where most of the incremental growth and all of the multiple is being created. Barriers to entry are high — PMA-class clinical evidence, physician training, and above all Medicare reimbursement — which concentrates returns among the few players who clear the regulatory bar.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Demographic demand with a reimbursement unlock.** Epioxa (epi-on corneal cross-linking for keratoconus) received FDA approval Oct-2025 and a **permanent J-code effective 1-Jul-2026**, converting a prior authorisation bottleneck into a clean Medicare Part B pathway. Corneal Health revenue hit **$59.0m in Q3-26, +154% y/y**. iDose TR also won FDA approval for **readministration** in a previously implanted eye in 2026, extending lifetime revenue per eye.
- **Drug-device mix is structurally margin-accretive.** Because Epioxa/iDose TR carry pharma-like economics, cost of sales "barely rose as revenue surged": GAAP gross margin reached **~85% in Q3-26** (vs 77.5% FY25 and 75.6% FY22), against a peer median of 65.5%. Operating margin inflected from **−40.9% (FY23) → −17.1% (FY25) → +0.4% in Q3-26**.
- **Scarcity value in a consolidating sector.** Alcon paid $475m upfront for Ivantis/Hydrus to buy its way into MIGS. With ALC and BLCO growing 5–6% and pure-plays de-rated, a sub-$15bn asset compounding >30% with sole-source CXL positioning is a strategic target, which underpins a floor under the multiple.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Medicare coverage risk on the largest franchise (the single biggest issue).** In Sept-2026 **five Medicare Administrative Contractors proposed LCDs** that would require patients to fail *both* selective laser trabeculoplasty and *two* topical medications before iDose TR is covered. U.S. Glaucoma is **$118.6m of Q3 sales (56%) and growing 47%** — an adverse final LCD would compress both volume and price in the core growth engine.
- **International pricing deflation and China VBP.** Successive Chinese volume-based-procurement rounds have cut IOL pricing 20%, 26% and 38%. Consistent with this, **international glaucoma sales fell ~9% y/y in Q3-26** — the mirror image of the U.S. strength.
- **Crowded MIGS arena with credible substitutes.** Alcon's Hydrus now has five-year HORIZON data and a deep installed base; AbbVie's Durysta competes directly with iDose TR; and generic topical prostaglandins plus SLT remain cheap first-line alternatives that cap pricing power. Surgical stent pricing is effectively commoditising even as the implant franchise grows.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

**Rationale for strength:** (i) only meaningful share-gainer in ophthalmology — accelerating from +11% (FY23) to +32% (FY25) to a guided +54% (FY26) against a flat-to-shrinking peer group; (ii) **sole FDA-approved epi-on CXL therapy** plus the only bio-activated epi-off agent, i.e. a near-duopoly of one in corneal cross-linking; (iii) top-quartile gross margin with visible operating leverage (op margin +64pp peak-to-trough over three years); (iv) a $1bn 2027 revenue target implying only ~28% growth off the Q4-26 run-rate of $228–238m — guidance that looks conservative rather than stretched.

**Caveats that keep this short of "dominant":** sub-scale at ~$780m versus ALC's $10.4bn, so single-payer decisions (the MAC LCDs) can move the whole thesis; SG&A intensity of 62% of sales leaves little cushion if growth slows; international is contracting ~9%; and the stock is priced for execution — at $242.48 (an all-time high, 52-wk range **$80.91–$243.71**) GKOS trades at roughly **18x FY26E and ~14x 2027E sales**, versus mid-single-digit revenue growth at ALC/BLCO. *(Peer EV/S multiples were not retrievable from the data feeds; the comparison above is market-cap and fundamentals-based.)*

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

**For the broad ophthalmic medical device sector: Neutral.** Underlying procedure demand is dependable, but revenue growth at scaled incumbents is only 3–8%, China VBP and Medicare coverage tightening are squeezing price, and the listed pure-plays are contracting — this is a **mature-to-late-cycle sector with a mid-single-digit growth ceiling**.

**For the interventional-glaucoma / CXL sub-segment GKOS occupies: Attractive.** Drug-device convergence is early-cycle (sustained-release implants and epi-on CXL are both still in the first years of commercial penetration), commands 80%+ gross margins, and is growing at 3–10x the sector rate with high regulatory barriers.

The net investment implication: **the sector backdrop is a modest tailwind at best, and GKOS's alpha is company-specific, not sector-driven.** The variant perception to test is whether the proposed MAC restrictions on iDose TR are a temporary comment-period irritant or the first real cap on U.S. Glaucoma — that single regulatory outcome matters more to the equity than any sector-level force identified here.
