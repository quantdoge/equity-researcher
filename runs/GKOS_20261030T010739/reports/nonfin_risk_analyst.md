# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Glaukos Corporation (GKOS)

**Snapshot:** $14.3B market cap · $242.48 · 1,094 employees · Medical Devices/Ophthalmics · CEO & Chairman Thomas W. Burns (founder-era, since 1998) · Beta 0.70
**Data caveats (material):** The 10-K and DEF 14A would not return through the filing feed, and SEC.gov direct fetch was 403-blocked. Compensation tables, board independence percentages, and full Item 1A risk factors are therefore **not verified**. Findings below draw on the 10-Q SOX certification, company press releases (Q4'25, Q1–Q3'26), the 2026 proxy's existence, Form 4 data, and press/analyst coverage. Treat governance findings as directional.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate** (watch item)

**Positives**
- Board appears majority-independent with a genuine mix of skills: Hoffmeister, Kliman (investor), Foley, Wen, Stapley, Weisner, Torres — plus separate Compensation and Nominating/Governance committees that grant and size equity (Form 4 notes the "Compensation, Nominating and Governance Committee… determined on March 25, 2026").
- CEO Burns holds ~1.77M shares and took **no open-market sales** in 12 months — only tax-withholding dispositions ($142.57, June 2026). Founder-CEO alignment is real and large.
- Clean SOX 302 certification (Oct 28, 2026) with no disclosed material weakness; no restatement or non-reliance 8-K in the filing history.

**Concerns**
- **Chairman/CEO duality** — Burns signs the 10-Q as "Chairman and Chief Executive Officer." No disclosed independent lead director in the material reviewed. Concentration of agenda-setting power at a company where the founder is also the public story.
- Compensation structure at a company where **Q3 GAAP EPS of $0.47 flipped positive against a $(0.12) consensus loss** but press commentary explicitly notes "profit is thinner than the adjusted figures suggest." Heavy reliance on non-GAAP (85% gross margin framing) plus an at-the-money equity program creates incentive to defend the adjusted narrative. Unverified pending proxy review.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **Moderate — reimbursement-concentrated, not enforcement-driven**

- **No active SEC/DOJ enforcement actions** surfaced in EDGAR full-text search. No securities class action identified in current coverage.
- **Litigation history is settled, not live:** Glaukos v. Ivantis (patent) settled 2021 — $60M cash plus a 10% royalty on Hydrus U.S. sales **through April 26, 2025 (now expired)**. Transcend settled 2015. IP estate is deep ("over 500 issued patents and exclusively licensed" as of Dec 31, 2025), but the Ivantis/Hydrus royalty tailwind is gone — a quiet headwind to 2026+ revenue quality.
- **The real regulatory risk is CMS, not the SEC.** Revenue now hinges on two reimbursement codes: **iDose TR (J7355)** and **Epioxa (J2789, permanent J-code effective July 1, 2026)**. A permanent J-code is a strong validation, but it also means revenue is now levered to Medicare physician-fee-schedule and commercial payor coverage decisions. Any coverage-restriction or rate revision is a direct revenue cliff, not a slow bleed.
- **Pipeline regulatory exposure emerging:** the Oct 2026 RevOpsis license (RO-104 tri-specific biologic, nAMD/DME/DR) moves GKOS into **biologic** regulation — BLA pathway, CMC, and inspection risk — a materially different regime from device PMA/510(k). iDose TR also has an FDA prior-approval supplement (readministration labeling), adding label-expansion execution risk.

Executives sold into strength well before the biggest positive catalyst. That is legal and common, but it means management's revealed preference is diversification, not conviction — worth weighing against the aggressive forward targets they are now asking the market to underwrite.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Single-source supplier dependence — company-disclosed, unresolved.** Glaukos's own risk language (repeated verbatim across the FY2025 release, Q1'25 release, Jan 2026 investor deck, and 8-K exhibits) flags "**dependence on a limited number of third-party suppliers, some of which are single-source, for components of our products**" and "the occurrence of a crippling…" disruption. With revenue guided to ~$780M and a $1B 2027 target, a single component failure in the iStent or iDose supply chain is now a much larger dollar event than it was at $300M of revenue. No disclosed second-sourcing program.
- **Drug-device manufacturing scale-up under 50–58% growth.** iDose TR is a sterile, 75µg travoprost intracameral implant requiring pharma-grade CMC and sterility assurance; Epioxa is a procedural therapy requiring a photochemical drug plus device. Ramping two drug-device platforms simultaneously at this growth rate is precisely the profile that produces 483s, recalls, and lot failures. **1,094 employees** supporting ~$780M revenue and three franchises is thin — execution and quality-systems bandwidth is the constraint, not demand.
- **Founder-CEO key-person concentration.** Burns has led since 1998, is Chairman *and* CEO, and is the external face of the iDose/Epioxa narrative. There is no disclosed succession plan or COO-to-CEO pipeline signal. Combined with a CFO and COO both actively liquidating, bench depth is a live question.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Low**

- Coverage is uniformly constructive: Reuters, Business Wire, Zacks, Benzinga all positive on three-year Epioxa durability data, positive Phase 4 iDose TR topline, and the J-code wins. No recall, no product-liability controversy, no ESG or labor controversy, no activist/SEC whistleblower activity found.
- Brand-building is being professionalized: the Sept 2026 **Stephen Curry "Could it be KC?"** keratoconus awareness campaign is a reputational *asset* but also a **concentration risk** — a celebrity ambassador program plus disease-awareness spend makes the Epioxa funnel dependent on paid DTC marketing and on Curry's own reputation. That is a low-probability, high-amplitude exposure.
- Structural reputational hazard: with two J-coded drugs now driving the story, the "medtech device company" identity is being replaced by a "pharma pricing" identity. Pharma reimbursement narratives attract far more political and payor scrutiny than device narratives. Expect the reputational surface area to grow with the revenue mix.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE** (upper end)

The company has no fraud indicators, no active enforcement, no litigation overhang, no governance scandal, and a clean control environment. Offsetting that: a Chairman/CEO duality, **unbroken 12-month insider selling with no buys**, single-source manufacturing disclosed by the company itself, a reimbursement architecture concentrated in two CMS codes, and operational stretch implied by 58% growth on a 1,094-person base. None of these is individually severe; collectively they mean the equity's current valuation assumes flawless execution on every one of them at once.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Zero insider buying across 12 months against ~$28M+ of sales, including COO ($10.8M single day) and CFO (recurring 10k/month), immediately preceding a raise to a $1B 2027 target.** Escalate to the PM: this is a management-credibility and incentive-alignment question, not a legal one. Request the DEF 14A to test whether 2026 grants were performance-based or time-based, and whether 10b5-1 plans were adopted or amended in Q2–Q3 2026.
2. **Chairman/CEO duality with no identified independent lead director.** Low urgency at current performance, high urgency if growth decelerates — this is the structural feature that makes boards slow to challenge a founder narrative.
3. **Single-source component dependence during a 2x revenue ramp.** Request the 10-K Item 1A and any supplier-concentration disclosure. A single-source failure here is now a $200M+ annualized revenue exposure.
4. **Two-J-code reimbursement concentration.** A permanent J-code is a win, but it converts commercial risk into policy risk. Any MAC or commercial payor coverage restriction on J7355/J2789 is an immediate, non-gradual revenue event.
5. **Entry into biologic regulation (RO-104/RevMod).** New CMC, BLA, milestone and royalty obligations, plus R&D spend against a GAAP P&L that press coverage already describes as "thinner than the adjusted figures suggest." Watch for dilution or incremental convert issuance — note the existing 2.75% convertible notes due 2027 were partially de-levered via a 2024 equity exchange, leaving a refinancing item on the horizon.

**Gaps to close before the memo is finalized:** full 10-K Item 1A; DEF 14A compensation tables and board independence determinations; auditor identity and any critical audit matter disclosures; cybersecurity incident history (not surfaced in available material — absence of evidence, not evidence of absence).
