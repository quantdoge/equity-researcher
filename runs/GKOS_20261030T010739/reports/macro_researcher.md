## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional / Late-Cycle Reflation with a Bear Steepener**

The US economy is in an unusual and hostile-for-duration combination: **re-accelerating growth and headline inflation, a Fed that has stopped cutting, and a long end that is repricing upward violently.** Q3 2026 real GDP grew at a ~3.4% annualised pace (Q2→Q3: $24,408B → $24,611B), unemployment has *fallen* to 4.2% from 4.5% in Nov 2025, and headline CPI has accelerated to **3.72% YoY** (Sep-2026) with a 0.65% monthly print — a 4.6% three-month annualised rate. Core CPI (2.57% YoY, 3.4% 3m annualised) says the acceleration is largely energy/supply-driven, not demand-driven, which is the worst setup for the Fed: it cannot ease into a supply shock.

Meanwhile the Fed stopped easing after April 2026 (Fed funds 3.63% → 3.75% by Sep-2026), and **the 10-year has risen ~50bp in four months, from 4.48% (Jul 1) to 5.05% (Oct 28)**. The curve is now positively sloped (2s10s +34bp) after a long inversion — the classic exit from inversion via *long-end* repricing rather than front-end cuts. That is a term-premium/fiscal-risk signal, not a growth signal.

**Stance for equity risk: risk-on in earnings, risk-off in valuation multiples.** Cyclical revenue momentum is excellent; the discount rate applied to long-duration growth equities is deteriorating. The dollar is also strengthening again (broad index 121.3 on Oct 23 vs 117.9 low on Sep 9, +2.9%), a modest global headwind.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL**

**Demand side is favourable; the discount rate is unfavourable; the balance sheet is fully insulated.** GKOS's zero net debt and pricing power make it one of the better-positioned names in a reflation regime — the company will not be squeezed by input costs or by an inability to refinance. But the valuation is doing all the work, and a 5.05% 10-year with a rising term premium is an active, measurable headwind to a 14x-forward-sales asset. Net: macro neither adds nor subtracts from the thesis, but it dominates the *volatility* of the stock.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Would Materially Surprise (Bear)

**"Term-premium capitulation" — 10Y to 5.75–6.00% by Q2 2027.** With headline CPI at 3.7% and a 4.6% three-month run rate driven by energy, if headline breaks above 4.5% the Fed's pause becomes a credibility problem, not a comfort. Add a rising fiscal deficit premium at the 30Y and the 10Y could take out 5.5% with the Fed *on hold*. In that world GKOS's fundamentals — 58% growth, a $1B 2027 target, 88% gross margin — would be **irrelevant to the share price**: a pure multiple compression of 25–35% (roughly $160–180) on unchanged estimates is the mechanical outcome, and it would likely coincide with the stock already up 190% in 12 months (a crowded, high-beta-to-rates long). The irony: the *bull* surprise is symmetric and equally non-consensus — if the energy component of CPI mean-reverts and the 10Y returns to 4.40%, GKOS re-rates 20%+ on no change to a single estimate. Because GKOS's fundamentals are idiosyncratic, macro is essentially a **discount-rate bet layered on top of an operational thesis** — size positions accordingly, and treat the 10Y at 5.05% as the single most important number on the screen next to the Q3 print.
