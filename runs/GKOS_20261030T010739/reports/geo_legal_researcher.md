## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**Risk 1 — US reimbursement & drug-pricing policy concentration (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
Glaukos is converting from a device company to a physician-administered *pharmaceutical* company. iDose TR and Epioxa both now carry permanent J-codes (J2789 for Epioxa effective 1 July 2026; iDose TR re-administration NDA supplement approved). This makes CMS coding, Medicare Part B payment rates, prior authorisation, and — critically — **Medicaid Drug Rebate Program (MDRP)** obligations the single largest driver of the P&L. The 10-Q explicitly flags MDRP rebates on pharmaceutical product sales. If Congress extends Medicare drug-price negotiation or "most-favoured-nation" pricing to Part B physician-administered drugs, iDose TR/Epioxa economics could compress. The company's own risk factors cite "changes to domestic or foreign healthcare laws or trade policies, which could impact our profitability." This is not a foreign-policy risk but it *is* the dominant sovereign/policy risk in the model.

**Risk 2 — US–China trade escalation & Section 232/301 tariffs (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Low-Medium)**
2026 medtech tariff architecture includes Section 301 China duties, a 10–12.5% baseline, and a pending Section 232 probe on medical devices. Glaukos' direct China revenue is negligible (<1%, via Gaush Medical), but device components, electronics and KXL/Avedro system hardware flow through globalised supply chains, and gross margin (85% GAAP in Q3 2026) is sensitive to landed-cost inflation. Mitigant: high gross margin and pricing power in a niche procedural category absorb modest tariff pass-through.

**Risk 3 — European market access, FX and Japan/EM pricing (Probability: Medium / Impact: Low-Medium)**
Europe (~8–10% of revenue) is a patchwork of national reimbursement regimes with downward price pressure; Japan applies biennial NHI repricing that structurally deflates device/drug prices. FY2025 international growth was 18% reported vs 16% constant currency — FX is already a ~200bp drag. LatAm distributor relationships concentrate counterparty and repatriation risk (Argentina in particular), though the absolute dollars are immaterial.

*De-prioritised:* sanctions exposure (none — no Russia/Iran/Syria operations), nationalisation/expropriation (none in material jurisdictions), and export-control risk (no dual-use technology).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active litigation**

| Matter | Status | Financial exposure |
|---|---|---|
| **Glaukos v. SpyGlass Pharma, Inc. & Long Doan** (C.D. Cal., 8:25-cv-02105, filed 17 Sept 2025) — trade-secret misappropriation, breach of contract, fraud re: employee exit documentation; Glaukos seeks injunction | Survived motions to dismiss (Bloomberg Law) | $5.9m litigation expense in Q3 2026; defensive value for the iDose franchise |
| **Regents of the University of California** — resolution agreement dated 29 Sept 2026 | Settled | **$10.0m non-refundable upfront paid + ongoing royalties; $14.1m total Q3 2026 charge.** Ongoing royalty is a permanent margin leak on the affected product(s) |
| **Ivantis/Hydrus (Sight Sciences)** — 2021 settlement: $60m + 10% royalty on US Hydrus sales | **Royalty expired 26 April 2025** | Tailwind: ~10% royalty on a competitor's sales now gone |
| **Transcend Medical** — patent suit re: iStent Supra | Settled 2015 (covenant not to sue) | Nil |

**Regulatory posture — constructive but concentrated**
- FDA: iDose TR approved Dec 2023; NDA labelling supplement approving **re-administration** granted 2026 (materially expands lifetime revenue per patient); Epioxa approved Oct 2025, commercially available Q1 2026 — first and only FDA-approved epithelium-on, oxygen-enriched CXL therapy for keratoconus.
- CMS: permanent J-code J2789 for Epioxa (eff. 1 July 2026); iDose TR reimbursement guide published 2026.
- Pipeline: positive Phase 4 topline (GLK-101-04, iDose TR + cataract surgery, Oct 2026); three-year Epioxa data; RevOpsis licensing deal (RO-104, RevMod platform) for retinal.
- **No FDA Warning Letter, Form 483 escalation, DOJ/FTC/SEC investigation, or securities class action was identified** in this review. Absence of evidence, not evidence of absence — the 10-K "Legal Proceedings" section could not be read directly.

**IP portfolio risk:** Glaukos is both serial plaintiff and potential target. As iDose TR (a drug-device combination with travoprost) scales toward $275–280m+ annually, it becomes a materially larger IP target. The UC Regents settlement implies core technology is university-licensed, which invites further royalty stack-up.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

> ### **LOW–MEDIUM** (Geopolitical: **LOW** · Regulatory/Legal: **MEDIUM**)

**Rationale.** Geographically this is close to a pure US play — ~76% domestic FY2025, drifting toward ~84% as Epioxa and iDose TR ramp, with zero exposure to the highest-risk theatres (Russia, Middle East conflict zones, China at <1% and only via distributor). No sanctions, expropriation, or export-control exposure of consequence. The residual international book is concentrated in rule-of-law jurisdictions (W. Europe, Japan, Canada, Australia), with LatAm and SE Asia immaterial in dollar terms.

The medium rating is driven by **regulatory and legal**, not geopolitical, factors: (i) the entire equity story now hinges on CMS reimbursement architecture for two J-coded drugs; (ii) a fresh $14.1m legal charge plus an ongoing royalty to the UC Regents; (iii) rising exposure to pharma-specific compliance regimes (MDRP, AKS, FCA) that the company did not face as a pure device maker; and (iv) an escalating IP war with SpyGlass Pharma in the same glaucoma segment.

**What would change the rating upward (worse):** an FDA Warning Letter or 483 escalation at the Aliso Viejo/Avedro manufacturing sites; a securities class action following any guidance reset; extension of Medicare drug-price negotiation to Part B; or a material adverse ruling in the SpyGlass matter (if Glaukos were enjoined or counterclaimed against on iDose IP).

**What would change it downward (better):** sustained >85% US revenue mix, expiry/absence of new litigation, and confirmation in the FY2026 10-K that the UC Regents royalty is low-single-digit and non-escalating.
