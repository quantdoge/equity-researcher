**Price $242.48 | Market cap $14.3B | EV ~$13.7B (net cash $340M, no debt) | As of Oct 29, 2026 close**

## 1. Growth Story

Glaukos has crossed from a MIGS device story into a **two-platform ophthalmic therapeutics company**, and the Q3 2026 print proved it: revenue +58% to a record $211M, non-GAAP gross margin ~88%, first positive GAAP operating income, and $50M of cash generated in a single quarter. The two engines are **iDose TR** (a dropless, multi-year travoprost implant redefining "interventional glaucoma" at $13,950 WAC) and **Epioxa** (the first and only FDA-approved epithelium-on corneal cross-linking therapy for keratoconus, launched Q1 2026, with Corneal Health revenue +154% YoY). Management raised FY26 guidance by $90M at the midpoint and — unusually for a company this early in a launch cycle — issued **preliminary FY27 revenue guidance of $1 billion**.

## 3. TAM Assessment

- **Glaucoma:** ~$7–8B global Rx market, with the interventional segment still in single-digit penetration. iDose TR's Medicare payment limit is $14,717 vs. a generic drop cost of pennies — the value proposition is adherence (a $3B+ adherence failure problem in glaucoma). With FDA approval of the **re-administration NDA supplement**, lifetime revenue per patient expands from one implant to a repeatable series — this is the single most underappreciated TAM multiplier.
- **Keratoconus (Epioxa):** Glaukos effectively created the interventional keratoconus category; Epioxa is the only FDA-approved epi-on CXL (faster recovery, less discomfort vs. epi-off Photrexa). US diagnosed population is in the hundreds of thousands with a global pool several times larger. Corneal Health growing 154% off a ~$23M/quarter 2025 base implies genuine category creation, not share-shifting.
- **Penetration math:** FY2027 at $1B on a combined $10B+ SAM is <10% penetrated. If Glaukos simply compounds at ~30% through 2030, revenue reaches ~$2.2B — still low-teens penetration.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4 2026 results (~Feb 25, 2027):** consensus Q4 revenue ~$227M; first formal refinement of the $1B FY27 number. Historic pattern favors a beat-and-raise.
- **iDose TR Medicare LCD finalization (near-term, binary):** five MACs proposed restricting coverage. Management explicitly said the $1B FY27 guide already contemplates "potential iDose LCD scenarios" — a favorable/neutral final determination is a clear re-rating event.
- **iDose re-administration commercial rollout** through 2027 — converts a one-time procedure into a recurring-revenue annuity.
- **Epioxa reimbursement/CPT expansion and international rollout** (Brazil regional hires already visible).
- **Pipeline readouts** in retinal disease and next-gen dropless platforms.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (not Strong Buy — entry price matters)

This is the rare medtech inflection where revenue acceleration, gross margin expansion, and cash generation are happening *simultaneously*, and where FY2027 guidance of $1B was delivered with confidence rather than hedging. The innovation moat is real: iDose has no direct dropless-implant competitor at scale, Epioxa has a first-mover FDA label, and both ride a procedure-based reimbursement model with strong per-unit economics.

**But the stock tripled in 12 months (+225% off the $74.67 low) and gapped 34% on the print.** At 18.3x FY2026E sales and 14.3x FY2027E sales (~100x non-GAAP EPS), the market is already discounting a ~$2.9B revenue business by 2030 — roughly a 38% CAGR. That is achievable (FY27 $1B + 32% CAGR gets $2.3B), but it leaves little cushion. My preference: **Buy on weakness toward $200–215** (the pre-print consolidation zone), where the risk/reward on the iDose LCD resolution becomes favorable rather than symmetric.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **iDose TR Medicare LCD (highest severity).** Five MACs proposing restricted coverage criteria is an existential-adjacent threat to the fastest-growing glaucoma product. Final LCD language — not clinical data — is the swing factor. This alone justifies not paying a premium multiple.
2. **Valuation compression risk.** 18x forward sales with no GAAP earnings buffer; a single quarter of deceleration (e.g., 58% → 35%) could halve the multiple.
3. **Operating leverage cuts both ways.** GAAP SG&A grew 58% YoY in Q3 (non-GAAP +32%); the sales force and Epioxa infrastructure are being built ahead of revenue. If a launch stumbles, the non-GAAP profitability story inverts quickly.
4. **Competition.** AbbVie/Allergan Durysta and Alcon Hydrus in glaucoma; Sight Sciences in MIGS; multiple CXL providers in keratoconus. J&J and Alcon have far larger ophthalmic sales forces.
5. **GAAP vs. non-GAAP gap and dilution.** Shares outstanding rose from 46.4M (2021) to 59.1M (Q3 2026), +27%; SBC was $63.2M in 2025 (~12% of revenue). Non-GAAP EPS of $0.47 versus GAAP $0.03 is a wide bridge. FY2025 also carried a large below-the-line charge (net loss $187.7M vs. operating loss $86.7M) — expect periodic one-offs.
6. **Reimbursement concentration.** US procedure-based pricing leaves ASPs exposed to any site-of-service or physician-fee pressure.
