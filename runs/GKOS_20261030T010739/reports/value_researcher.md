## GLAUKOS CORPORATION (GKOS) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price: $242.48 (29‑Oct‑2026 close, +34% on Q3 results) | Market cap ~$14.3B (basic) / ~$14.8B diluted | Net cash ~$60M*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW** (and widening, but not yet proven)

Glaukos is a founder-led ophthalmic drug-device company with two franchises: (a) the **iStent family** of micro-bypass stents (MIGS) implanted during cataract surgery, and (b) **iDose TR**, the first FDA-approved physician-administered, sustained-release travoprost implant for glaucoma (approved Dec‑2023). iDose is now the growth engine: ~$45M in Q3‑26 alone and ~$280–285M implied for 2026, more than double 2025.

**Sources of advantage:**
- **Intangible assets / regulatory barriers.** iDose TR is a drug-device combination requiring a PMA plus NDA-like efficacy data — a multi-year, capital-intensive barrier. iStent has a decade-plus clinical dataset and entrenched surgical workflow.
- **Pricing power visible in the P&L.** Gross margin rose from 77.5% (FY25) → 77.8% → 81.7% → **85.2%** (Q1→Q3 2026) as drug-based iDose mixes in. Very few device companies sustain 85% gross margins.
- **Moderate switching costs.** Surgeons are trained on the implant, and iDose replaces the single largest unmet need in glaucoma — topical drop non-adherence.

**Why not Wide:** the MIGS/sustained-release space is actively contested — Alcon (Hydrus), AbbVie/Allergan (Durysta), Sight Sciences (OMNI), Nova Eye — and adoption is gated by ASC/hospital outpatient reimbursement and payer coverage, not just clinical superiority. Critically, **the moat is not yet visible in returns on capital**: ROIC was −9.0%, −15.2%, −14.1%, −10.9% in 2022–2025 and remains negative on a TTM basis (operating income −$63M on $690M revenue). A moat that cannot yet earn its cost of capital is a *candidate* moat, not a proven one.

**Positives:** revenue growth re-accelerated from 11% (2023) to 22% (2024) to 32% (2025) to a guided **+54% in 2026** ($775–785M); margins inflecting sharply; first GAAP-profitable quarter in Q3‑26; balance sheet de-risked (converts retired, net cash, no material maturities).

**Negatives that keep the score at 5:** (i) historically **negative free cash flow every year 2020–2025**, cumulative ~$343M burned; (ii) **stock-based compensation of $83M/yr — more than twice reported FCF**. Reported FCF is an illusion here: economic owner earnings are negative. Share count has gone from 46.4M (2021) to 59.1M basic / 61.2M diluted — **~29% dilution in five years**; (iii) FY2025 contained a ~$106M non-operating charge (Q4‑25 net loss −$133.7M vs. operating loss of only −$27.0M), consistent with the ~$121M reduction in goodwill/intangibles — i.e., a write-down of prior acquisition value (the 2019 Avedro deal); (iv) FCF conversion has never been demonstrated through a full cycle.

### 3. Valuation Analysis — intrinsic value **~$130/share**; price 86% above it

Multiples today: **20.7x EV/TTM sales**, ~18.3x EV/2026E sales, ~13.9x EV/2027E sales, ~400x TTM reported FCF, and negative EV/EBITDA. Peer-median P/E (31x) is not meaningful against a GAAP loss. Analyst targets cluster at **$225–255** — the stock now trades *at or above* the average post-print target, a classic sign that good news is fully discounted.

**Reverse DCF — what the price demands.** At a 9% WACC / 3% terminal growth, $14.3B of enterprise value requires roughly **$1.6–2.0B of terminal free cash flow within ten years**. At a mature 25–30% FCF margin that means **$5.3–6.4B of revenue by 2036** — a **~21–23% revenue CAGR for a full decade**, with best-in-class margins, zero competitive erosion, and no pricing pressure. Only a handful of medtechs in history have compounded like that.

**Scenario DCF** (9% WACC, 3% terminal growth, 62M diluted shares, +$60M net cash, FCF measured *after* SBC):

| Scenario | 2036 revenue | Terminal FCF margin | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bear (payer pushback, share loss to Hydrus/Durysta) | $1.9B | 18% | **~$56** |
| Base (leader in a $2–3B category) | $3.1B | 23% | **~$128** |
| Bull (iDose becomes standard of care) | $4.4B | 28% | **~$213** |

Probability-weighted (25/50/25): **~$131/share**. Note the bull case — revenue quadrupling with 28% FCF margins — *still* lands below today's price at a 9% discount rate. You only reach $264 by assuming the bull case **and** an 8% WACC. There is no margin of safety; there is a negative one.

### 4. Management Quality — **Good, not exceptional (B)**

- **Thomas Burns**, founder and CEO since 1998, still leads the company — genuine owner-operator posture, deep domain expertise, long-horizon R&D orientation (built iDose over 15+ years).
- **Capital allocation is mixed.** Deleveraging the 2027 converts and reaching net cash is a real win. But: the Avedro (corneal cross-linking) acquisition appears to have been written down (~$121M goodwill/intangibles reduction in FY2025) — an empire-building mark. No dividend, no meaningful buyback (only $2.8M in Q2‑26), against $83M/yr of SBC and 29% cumulative dilution. Shareholders bear the cost of compensation while management's equity grants are the beneficiary.
- **Guidance credibility is decent but the stock is unforgiving**: FY2026 guidance implies Q4 revenue of ~$228–238M (+60% y/y) and consensus Q4 EPS of ~$0.45 — a steep hockey stick that leaves no room for error. The shares fell 20% on 21‑Feb‑2025, 11% on 1‑May‑2025 and 9% on 31‑Jul‑2025 on comparatively modest disappointments.
- *Gap noted:* I could not verify current insider ownership percentages from the available data; this should be checked in the DEF 14A before acting.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (avoid at $242)

This is a genuinely good business — first-in-class products, 85% gross margins, an inflection to GAAP profitability, a clean balance sheet and founder leadership. If it were priced at $110–130 I would be a willing buyer and would happily own it for a decade.

But Buffett's first rule of valuation is that **a wonderful business bought at a foolish price is a foolish purchase**. At $242 the market is capitalizing a near-flawless decade: ~20%+ revenue compounding through 2036, margins at device-industry best practice, and no erosion from Alcon, AbbVie or payer pressure. My conservative-but-fair intrinsic value is **~$130/share**; my generous bull case is ~$213. Even the optimists' own price targets ($225–255) are now behind the price.

Additional confirmations of over-extension: the stock has gone from $75 (Oct‑2025) to $242 (Oct‑2026), and from $181 to $242 in a single session; reported FCF of $36M against a $14.3B market cap is a 0.25% FCF yield; and *owner earnings, properly charging for $83M of stock compensation, are still negative*.

**Action:** Sell / do not initiate. Revisit below ~$150 (still ~30% above base-case value but with a plausible bull path), and become seriously interested at **$110–130**, which is where the risk/reward finally pays you for the execution risk.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Valuation/multiple risk (the dominant risk).** 20x EV/sales leaves no cushion; a shift from 20x to 10x sales is a 50% drawdown even if fundamentals beat.
2. **iDose concentration and reimbursement.** iDose is ~36% of 2026 revenue in only its third year. Coverage decisions by CMS and commercial payers, and any step-down in ASC reimbursement, would be the single fastest way to break the growth narrative. It is a drug administered in a procedure setting — exposed to both drug-pricing and procedure-pricing politics.
3. **Competition.** Alcon's Hydrus, AbbVie's Durysta, and next-generation sustained-release programs from large-cap ophthalmic players; Glaukos has a history of MIGS patent litigation, and its patents will not protect pricing forever.
4. **Dilution.** $83M/yr of SBC (12% of revenue) with no offsetting buyback. If I am wrong and FCF never converts cleanly, shareholders own a growing slice of a slower-growing pie.
5. **Execution against a hockey-stick guide.** Q4‑26 must deliver ~+60% y/y growth. Any deceleration triggers violent de-rating, as 2025 demonstrated repeatedly.
6. **Loss of the "quality" premium.** The thesis that supports the price is a *narrative* premium (category leadership), the most fragile kind. Note the business already carries a −$969M accumulated deficit and a history of acquisition write-downs — this is not yet a proven compounder of capital.

**Honest gaps:** current insider ownership and the exact composition of the Q4‑2025 ~$106M non-operating charge could not be verified from the data available; sector-peer multiple comparison was not usable (the peer set returned healthcare mega-caps with no revenue-growth match). Both should be checked before any purchase decision — though neither would change a verdict driven by a 2x gap to intrinsic value.
