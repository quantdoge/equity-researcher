# Akamai Technologies (AKAM) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $91.70 (Nov 18, 2026) | Market cap ≈ $13.5B | Net debt ≈ $8.4B | EV ≈ $21.9B**

## ⚠️ Framing: This is No Longer the Business the Multiple Describes

Akamai has fundamentally changed character over the last 15 months. It is no longer an asset‑light CDN/security compounder converting ~90% of net income to free cash flow. It is now, by choice, a **levered AI cloud‑infrastructure ("neocloud") provider**:

- **$11.6B / 7‑year agreement with Anthropic** (Sept 2026) — Akamai must commit **~$5.5B of capex** and granted Anthropic a **5% equity stake** (~$670M of value) to win it. Only **$150–300M of revenue** arrives in 2027.
- **FY2026 capex guidance raised to 40–42% of revenue** (from 25–26%). ~$1.8B on ~$4.45B of revenue, versus $820M (19.5%) in FY2025.
- **Total debt $9.45B** at 9/30/26 (vs. $4.6B at YE2024). **S&P cut AKAM to BB+ — junk.** Moody's followed. S&P forecasts **cumulative negative free operating cash flow >$1B for 2026–2029** and **~4x+ adjusted leverage in 2027**.
- Buybacks of **$616M against TTM FCF of $211M** — i.e., borrowing to repurchase stock while simultaneously borrowing to build data‑center capacity. This is the single clearest capital‑allocation red flag in the file.

Everything below is analyzed on the *new* Akamai, because that is what you are buying.

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Narrow (and narrowing)**

**Where a moat genuinely exists:**
- *Security* (~55% of revenue, $622M in Q3'26, +9% y/y / +10% cc): WAF, API security, and Guardicore segmentation carry real switching costs — security tooling is embedded in customer workflows and re‑architecting is painful. Akamai's 4,000+‑location distributed edge is a genuine, hard‑to‑replicate asset.
- *Delivery* (CDN, ~36%): Efficient scale and decades of peering relationships, but this is a **structurally eroding** franchise — S&P explicitly cites "structural pricing erosion and a deepening CDN market."

**Where the moat is absent — and where all incremental capital is going:**
- *CIS / AI cloud infrastructure* (~8% of revenue, $94.6M/quarter, +40%): competing head‑on with AWS, Microsoft, Oracle OCI, CoreWeave, and Nebius for AI compute. This is a **commodity, capital‑intensive, capacity‑rental business with no pricing power**, evidenced by the price Akamai paid to win Anthropic (a 5% equity sweetener plus $5.5B of committed capex). Single‑customer concentration of this magnitude is the antithesis of a moat.

**The decisive evidence — the moat has narrowed:**

| Metric | 2020 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | Q3'26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 64.6% | 61.7% | 60.4% | 59.4% | 54.7% | — |
| GAAP operating margin | 20.6% | 18.7% | 16.7% | 13.4% | 14.9% | **9.7%** |
| ROIC | — | 8.98% | 6.98% | 5.61% | **5.29%** | lower |
| Revenue growth | 10.5% | 4.5% | 5.4% | 4.7% | 5.4% | 6.4% |

A **~1,000bp gross‑margin decline in five years** and **ROIC falling from 9.0% to 5.3%** — below a ~9% WACC — is the textbook signature of a moat in retreat. Even excluding $3.8B of goodwill/intangibles, ROIC on tangible capital is only ~7.3%, still below cost of capital. **Akamai is currently destroying economic value on a trailing basis**, and it is voluntarily increasing its exposure to its lowest‑return asset class.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **4 / 10**

**What's still decent:**
- Revenue is highly predictable: +4.5% to +6.4% every year for six years, ~$4.45B FY2026E. Recurring, contracted, low churn.
- Gross profit dollars still growing modestly; adjusted EBITDA ~$1.74B (38.8% margin in Q3'26).
- No near-term liquidity crisis: ~$1.0B cash, and OCF remains robust (~$1.5B in FY2025).

**What is broken:**
- **FCF conversion has collapsed:** FY2025 FCF $699M vs. net income $452M; TTM FCF now **$211M** and guided **negative** through 2027–2028. Owner earnings (NI + D&A − maintenance capex) fell from $658M (2022) to **$341M (2025)**.
- **Stock‑based comp is a real, massive cost:** $459M in FY2025 = **10.9% of revenue** (up from 6.8% in 2022). True owner earnings, charging SBC as cash, are **negative**. Buybacks of $800M (2025) did not shrink the count materially — diluted shares went 155.4M → 147.0M while SBC issued ~$459M of new stock.
- **Leverage:** $9.45B debt, ~4x+ adjusted EBITDA by 2027, **BB+ (junk)**. Interest expense already $31M/quarter and rising.
- **Dilution:** the Anthropic 5% equity grant is a ~$670M hidden cost of revenue that does not appear in the P&L.
- No dividend (entirely reasonable for a growth business — but not for one borrowing to buy back stock).

## 3. Valuation Analysis — Intrinsic Value ≈ **$70–75/share** (base case)

**Conventional multiples (FY2026E):**

| Metric | AKAM | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Non‑GAAP P/E | **14.0x** ($91.70 / $6.57) | Looks cheap |
| GAAP P/E | ~41x | The honest number |
| EV/adj. EBITDA | **~12.6x** ($21.9B / $1.74B) | Not cheap for 6% growth + junk debt |
| EV/Sales | 4.9x | Rich for a hardware‑intensive business |
| FCF yield | **1.6%** ($211M / $13.5B) | And negative in 2027 |

The 14x non‑GAAP P/E is an illusion. Non‑GAAP EPS excludes **$709M of D&A and $459M of SBC** — precisely the costs that matter now that Akamai owns $3.8B of PP&E and must replace servers on a 4–5 year cycle. For a capital‑intensive business, you must value owner earnings, not adjusted EPS.

**Reverse DCF — the most decisive test:** At $91.70, EV of $21.9B requires roughly **$2.0B of sustainable owner earnings** to deliver a 9% return. Akamai produced **$341M** in FY2025 and will produce close to zero or negative in 2027–2028.

**Scenario build (FY2030 earnings power, discounted at 9%):**

- **Bear ($35–45):** CIS ramps slowly ($800M by 2030), 2027 guidance disappoints again, Security decelerates to 5%, leverage forces an equity raise. EBITDA ~$1.9B, capex 25% of revenue.
- **Base ($70–75):** CNS/CIS reaches ~$2.5B by 2030, Security $2.9B, Delivery $1.9B → revenue ~$7.3B. EBITDA margin 37% = $2.7B; capex 22% = $1.6B; EBITDA − capex $1.1B; less ~$550M interest and taxes → **FCF ~$400–450M**. Capitalized at 9% with 3% growth ≈ $7.5B, discounted back four years ≈ $5.3B, less net debt → equity **$70–75/share**.
- **Bull ($150+, roughly the Street's high targets):** CIS reaches $4B+ at 45% EBITDA margins with no pricing pressure from hyperscalers and no further equity sweeteners. Even here, the math gets you to roughly **$130–150** — i.e., the bull case earns you a market return only if executed flawlessly.

**Crucially: the base case — successful, on‑plan execution of a $14B backlog — gets you to below today's price.** You are being asked to pay full price for flawless execution of a strategy in a business with no moat, one dominant customer, junk credit, and negative free cash flow. There is no margin of safety; there is negative margin of safety.

*Analyst targets span an unusually wide $85 (RBC, UBS) to $175 (KeyBanc) — the dispersion itself is a confession of how un‑underwritable this is.*

## 4. Management Quality Assessment: **Mixed → Below Average on Capital Allocation**

**Strengths:** Founder‑CEO Dr. Tom Leighton (since 1998) is a genuine technologist of the first rank and has built a differentiated global platform; the security franchise was built organically and via disciplined early tuck‑ins; guidance is generally conservative and reliably met/beaten (Q3'26 non‑GAAP EPS beat by 5.8%).

**Serious reservations:**
1. **Capital allocation has inverted.** Borrowing to buy back $616M of stock *while* committing $5.5B to data‑center capex and $11.6B of contract obligations is the opposite of Buffett discipline. Buybacks should follow free cash flow, not precede it.
2. **Paying for revenue with equity.** Giving Anthropic a 5% stake to win a contract means the headline $11.6B overstates true economics — you sold a slice of the whole company to buy a low‑return contract.
3. **Strategic pivot outside the historical competence.** Leighton's edge is distributed content delivery and security. Competing with hyperscalers on GPU/CPU capacity rental is a different game — one where scale and cost of capital decide winners, and where Akamai is sub‑scale and now the highest‑cost borrower among peers.
4. **Credit destruction.** Management allowed a formerly near‑net‑cash‑neutral balance sheet to become a **BB+ junk credit** with 4x+ leverage. That permanently raises the cost of the next dollar of capital.
5. **Insider ownership is low (~1–2%)** and SBC at 11% of revenue transfers substantial value to employees.

Honest, transparent, and operationally excellent — but the incentive structure and debt‑funded expansion suggest the organization is optimizing for revenue growth and relevance rather than per‑share owner value.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (or, for non‑holders, **Avoid**)

**There is a real business here, and a real bull case.** Security has switching costs, the edge platform is unique, bookings are up 7.8%, the Anthropic deal could unlock a multi‑year CIS ramp with "substantial acceleration in 2027 and beyond," and $14B of contracted commitments is not nothing.

**But it fails every test I apply:**

| Buffett Criterion | AKAM | Pass? |
|---|---|---|
| Circle of competence | Now a levered AI compute landlord | ✗ |
| Durable moat | Narrow, narrowing; ROIC < WACC | ✗ |
| FCF generation | $211M TTM, negative 2027–28 | ✗ |
| Balance sheet strength | $9.45B debt, junk rated | ✗ |
| Capital allocation | Debt‑funded buybacks + 5% equity give‑away | ✗ |
| Margin of safety | Base case ≈ $70–75 < $91.70 | ✗ |

**The core argument in one sentence:** Akamai is converting a 60%‑gross‑margin, asset‑light franchise with ~9% ROIC into a 37%‑margin, capital‑intensive capacity business with sub‑WACC returns, funding the transition with junk debt and equity, and the market is still paying ~12.6x EBITDA and 4.9x sales for the *old* mental model.

**If I am wrong**, I am wrong on execution, not on price — and I can re‑enter far cheaper after the capex cycle demonstrates actual returns on invested capital. **Price to get interested: below $65** (~30% discount to base‑case intrinsic value), or once CIS shows two consecutive quarters of on‑schedule revenue conversion with FCF turning positive. Neither is true today.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (i.e., what would make the bears wrong)

1. **CIS ramp is lumpy, not broken.** Management says delays "push revenue to next year, no loss of revenue." If 70–75% of CIS revenue lands in H2 2027 as guided, my 2027–28 FCF estimates are too harsh.
2. **Contract value could exceed $20B.** Reports suggest room to expand beyond $11.6B with Anthropic; additional hyperscaler/AI customers would transform the terminal-value math.
3. **Security re‑acceleration.** API security, Guardicore, and the LayerX acquisition (AI data‑leak prevention) could push Security back to low‑double‑digit growth — the highest‑margin, highest‑moat part of the franchise.
4. **Inflection in 2028.** If margins "bottom in H1 2027" and operating leverage arrives in 2028 as the CFO states, the equity could re‑rate sharply.
5. **Strategic value / takeover.** A sub‑$14B EV edge‑security + AI‑capacity asset with an irreplaceable distributed network could attract an acquirer (though $9.45B of debt complicates this).
6. **My D&A treatment could be conservative** if Akamai's hardware life is longer than 4–5 years than I assume.

**Risks that would break the bull case (and why I weight them heavily):** further Anthropic concentration, hyperscaler price competition in inference capacity, a >4x leverage ratio colliding with a covenant or refinancing at junk spreads, continued buybacks funded by debt, and the very real possibility that FY2027 revenue guidance ($150–300M of Anthropic revenue against $5.5B of capex) disappoints a third consecutive time.

**Bottom line: a fair business at a fair-to-full price, wrapped in a capital-intensive reinvention I cannot underwrite with confidence. Pass.**
