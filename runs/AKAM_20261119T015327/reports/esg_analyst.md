# ESG ANALYSIS — AKAMAI TECHNOLOGIES (NASDAQ: AKAM)

**Sector:** Technology / Software–Infrastructure (CDN, edge computing, cybersecurity) | **FY2025 revenue:** $4,208M (+5% y/y) | **Market cap:** ~$13.2B | **Employees:** 11,832 | **Price:** $91.70 (18-Nov-26), ~45% below the May-2026 high of $165.45

> **Context that reframes this ESG analysis:** AKAM is mid-pivot from asset-light CDN to capital-heavy AI/cloud infrastructure — an $11.6B Anthropic cloud commitment, >$14B of multiyear customer commitments signed in 2026, $2.5B of 1.00% convertible notes closed 12-Nov-26, and disclosed data-center supply delays. ESG risk for AKAM is therefore **no longer a "software company" risk profile**; it is increasingly a **data-center operator** profile. That shift is the single most important ESG finding in this report.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (rising to HIGH on trajectory)

**Verified data — 2025 inventory, externally assured (auditor sign-off 10-Jun-26), GRI/SASB-aligned:**

| Metric | 2025 | 2024 | 2023 | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope 1 | 70 tCO₂e | 45 | 54 | Negligible |
| Scope 2 (location-based) | 322,800 t | 262,401 | 243,301 | **+23%** |
| Scope 2 (market-based) | 173,000 t | 121,001 | 112,701 | **+43%** |
| Scope 3 (gross) | 180,350 t | 174,340 | 226,615 | +3% |
| **Total S1+2+3** | **353,420 t** | 295,386 | 339,370 | **+20%** |
| S1+2+3 intensity | 84 tCO₂e/$MM rev | 70 | 81 | Worsening |
| Electricity | 1,073,200 MWh | 873,400 | 828,534 | **+23%** |
| **% clean energy** | **52%** | 59% | 56% | **Deteriorating** |
| **Average PUE** | **1.48** | 1.40 | 1.40 | **Deteriorating** |
| Water consumption | 669 ML | 885 | 790 | Improving (noisy) |
| E-waste diverted | 100% | 100% | 100% | Strong |

**Assessment.** Absolute carbon is small (41 tCO₂e per $MM revenue on Scope 1+2 market basis — low for the sector) and 100% of operations are ISO 14001 certified. But **every operating vector is moving the wrong way simultaneously**: electricity +23%, market-based Scope 2 +43%, PUE 1.40→1.48, and clean-energy share 59%→52% against a **"100% renewable and net-zero by 2030"** pledge. Reported progress against the 2030 target *fell* from 47% (2024) to 42% (2025). Closing the remaining 48-point renewable gap requires ~527,000 MWh of new procurement **today**, before accounting for AI-driven load growth — implying well over 1 million MWh of incremental clean supply by 2030.

Two quality-of-disclosure flags: (i) the location-vs-market Scope 2 gap of ~150,000 t rests entirely on contractual instruments (RECs/VPPAs), so a tightening of GHG Protocol market-based rules would materially restate reported emissions; (ii) water consumption (669 ML) **exceeds** reported withdrawal (49 ML), indicating the water figures are operator-estimate-based and internally inconsistent. Notably, AKAM runs its own proprietary "emissionality"/consequential accounting and argues the GHGP doesn't capture its impact — sophisticated, but it also signals the headline number flatters reality.

**The decisive issue:** the $11.6B Anthropic deal converts a modest environmental footprint into a large one. A 2026 pause in the PUE and renewable metrics is a governance red flag — capex is being committed faster than decarbonisation is being funded.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Strengths:** Product is socially positive (DDoS mitigation, infrastructure resilience, the firm publicly publishes security research). Strong disclosure culture, no evidence of forced-labour or safety controversies, supplier engagement programme with science-based alignment.

**Risks:**
- **Reliability is the core social/product issue.** 2026 shows recurring incident frequency (Edge/DNS incidents on 22-Jul, 1-Aug, 30-Sep, 2-Oct, 2-Nov, 13-Nov; uptime trackers record ~9 incidents in 2026). Each event puts SLA credits and enterprise renewals at risk and undermines the "durable internet" narrative the ESG report leans on.
- **Community licence.** AKAM's capacity growth depends on colocation and leased data centres in jurisdictions where local opposition is now a documented, bipartisan blocker (a class action in Wisconsin against a hyperscaler over noise is the template). AKAM does not appear to publish site-level community impact or water-stress data — a gap versus European peers.
- **Talent/restructuring.** 11,832 employees, revenue/employee ~$355k; the AI-infrastructure pivot requires different skills than legacy CDN operations, creating execution and turnover risk.
- **Human-rights/content intermediation.** As a traffic intermediary AKAM faces government takedown and surveillance requests; no material financial exposure identified, but policy needed.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Positives:**
- **Independent Chair** (Daniel Hesse, independent Chair since 2016) — **no CEO/Chairman duality**. CEO Tom Leighton sits on a 9-member board as the sole insider.
- **Single class of common stock** — no dual-class structure, no controlling founder votes, equal shareholder rights.
- **Board-level ESG Committee** (separate from Audit/Nominating) with explicit climate governance, plus an annual board effectiveness review led by the Chair and ESG Committee Chair.
- **Say-on-pay** in place; CDP disclosure; IFRS S2 alignment planned; ESG data third-party assured — above-median transparency for a $13B-cap tech name.
- **Recent shareholder-rights improvement:** 2026 proxy includes an amendment to let certain stockholders call a special meeting — a right AKAM previously lacked.

**Detractors:**
- **Founder-CEO concentration.** Leighton co-founded AKAM in 1998 and has been CEO since 2013 — long tenure plus board seat plus deep technical authority; meaningful key-person risk at precisely the moment of maximum strategic change.
- **Political spending disclosure still resisted** — a shareholder proposal on political spending appears on the 2026 ballot, meaning recurring investor dissatisfaction on transparency.
- **Small board (9)** with limited evidence of refresh; 2026 virtual-only AGM reduces shareholder engagement quality versus in-person/hybrid.
- **Capital allocation tension:** $800M of buybacks and $2.5B of converts raised in the same period that management cites rising infrastructure costs and margin pressure. Not fraud — but a governance question about whether shareholder returns are being prioritised over the balance-sheet flexibility needed for grid/power contracts.
- **Compensation:** no clear evidence found that decarbonisation or reliability metrics carry material weight in the incentive plan; climate-related comp linkage is referenced but not quantified in available sources.

**Score rationale:** structurally strong (independent chair, single class, ESG committee, assured data) but penalised for founder-CEO entrenchment, an unresolved political-spending fight, and a shareholder-rights package that has only just been upgraded. 7/10 = better than the median US mid-cap tech board, short of best practice.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (high-quality disclosure, deteriorating environmental trajectory, rising social risk)

AKAM is **not a Laggard**: independent chair, single-class shares, board ESG committee, externally assured emissions data, 100% ISO 14001 coverage and 100% e-waste diversion put it in the top half of US mid-cap tech on governance and transparency.

It is **not a Leader**, and cannot be called one, because the environmental commitments central to its own narrative are being missed *while capex scales toward AI infrastructure*. A company that grows electricity 23% and lets clean-energy share fall seven points while reaffirming "100% renewable by 2030" has a target-credibility problem. Classify as **Average, with negative momentum**.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL-to-NEGATIVE** for the thesis

The ESG story here is not fines or scandals — it is **cost of capital and execution risk embedded in the AI pivot**:

1. **Power is now a COGS and a permitting problem, not a PR problem.** Falling renewable share (52%) and worsening PUE (1.48) mean AKAM's incremental AI revenue is being served with dirtier, costlier power. That is consistent with management's own framing of "rising infrastructure costs squeezing margins." ESG risk and margin risk are the same risk here.
2. **Delays are already in the numbers.** Revenue slipping out of Q4-26 due to data-centre delays is the first hard evidence that grid/community constraints are a real earnings variable for AKAM, not a theoretical one. Watch PUE, clean-energy %, and capacity-delivery language each quarter — these lead the P&L.
3. **Reliability incidents compound the discount.** Nine incidents in 2026 against a stock down ~45% from its May peak creates a narrative loop: capex up, margins down, service wobbles — exactly the combination that compresses a multiple.
4. **Governance is a genuine offset, and the main reason this is not a "Negative."** Independent chair, single-class shares, assured ESG data and an ESG committee mean AKAM is unlikely to suffer an ESG-driven governance event; the board has the structure to oversee the AI/security risk it is taking on.
5. **What would move this to Positive:** a quantified, funded path back to >59% clean energy (PPAs tied to new load, not unbundled RECs), a disclosed Scope 3 Category 11/embodied-carbon plan for GPU refresh cycles, board-level reliability KPIs in the incentive plan, and political-spending disclosure. Conversely, a second consecutive year of falling renewable share with reaffirmed 2030 targets should be treated as a **credibility discount on all forward guidance**, not just ESG guidance.

**Bottom line: ESG is a binding constraint on the AI-infrastructure thesis, not a separate consideration. It does not break the thesis, but it argues for a higher cost of capital and a wider margin of safety than the headline growth story implies.**
