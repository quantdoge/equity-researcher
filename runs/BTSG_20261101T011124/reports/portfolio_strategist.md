## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT** — but a *high-quality* split

This is not noisy disagreement. It decomposes cleanly:

- **On fundamentals/direction: HIGH agreement (9 of 12 positive-to-neutral).** Nobody disputes that the business is improving — revenue +26%, adj. EBITDA +42%, ROIC 3.4%→10.7%, leverage 4.7x→1.8x, seven consecutive beats, five fresh up-revisions in a month. The forensic case is genuinely clean.
- **On price: NEGATIVE majority (Value, Macro, Quant, Sector caution, Short).** Value's base case ($48) and bull case ($62) are *both below* spot. Macro independently derives a 25–30% downside path from the discount rate alone. Risk-adjusted, five independent lenses land on "expensive."
- **The tell:** not one of the twelve agents recommends buying size at $66.89. The bull (Growth) says mid-$50s. The alt-data bull says "don't chase the print." Value says ≤$45. **The consensus action, even among bulls, is "not here."**

**Conflict resolutions (weighted by evidence quality and independent corroboration):**

1. **Script growth — the single most important conflict.** Growth cites "+29% script growth"; Short and Alt Data *independently* compute total scripts at **+2%** (Q3: 10.95M vs 10.79M; 9M flat at 32.52M). **I resolve to ~+2%** — Growth appears to have conflated specialty-segment *revenue* (+34%) with unit volume. This matters enormously: it means ~100% of earnings growth comes from revenue-per-script (+24%) and GP/script (+27–34%), i.e. **spread and mix, not demand.** Spread is precisely what IRA negotiation, 340B reform and PBM/DIR reform attack. I weight this as a material durability discount.
2. **No fraud override.** My framework says a short analyst's fraud flag pre-empts a value bull. Here there is no flag to pre-empt — the manipulation tests are clean. The bear case is *valuation and growth-quality*, not accounting, so it informs sizing rather than vetoing the position.
3. **Net debt:** $1.66B (Fin Risk, LTM Q3'26, best-sourced) over Sector's $3.1B estimate and stale $2.62B FY25 figure. Market cap $14.7B (diluted), not $13.2B.
4. **Beta:** 1.03 measured vs 1.85 profile — unresolved, 64% apart, on a 1.7-year post-IPO sample. **Underwrite to 1.5–1.85.**

**Net conviction: MEDIUM at the business level, LOW-to-NEGATIVE at this price.**

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector concentration.** Cap all reimbursement-policy-exposed healthcare services (home health, hospice, specialty pharmacy, distribution) at **4–5% of the portfolio in aggregate.** If BTSG plus existing ADUS/ENSG/OPCH/CHE/AMED exposure already exceeds 3%, size BTSG at the low end (1.5%) or fund it by exiting a lower-growth peer. BTSG is the highest-growth and highest-multiple name in that bucket — it should be the *smallest* position per unit of policy exposure, not the largest.

**Correlation — do not be fooled by the matrix.** Measured 252-day correlations are strikingly low: BTSG/ADUS 0.14, BTSG/ENSG 0.08, BTSG/OPCH 0.11, BTSG/CHE 0.09, BTSG/SPY 0.28. On its face this looks like excellent diversification. **I discount it heavily:** the sample is ~1.7 years of post-IPO history dominated by a single idiosyncratic re-rating (+79%), and the same matrix shows peers with *negative* correlation to SPY, which is clearly noise. Economically, all five names share one factor — CMS rate rulemaking, state Medicaid budgets, IRA/Part D drug economics — and will correlate toward 1.0 in exactly the scenario that hurts BTSG. **Treat as one undiversified policy bet.**

**Factor exposure — the decisive point.** BTSG currently loads on: high multiple (negative), negative 12-1 momentum (negative), high volatility (negative), high beta (negative), positive earnings revision (positive), improving quality (positive). In a Fed-hiking, bear-steepening regime with the 10Y at 4.98%, the negative loadings dominate. **Do not add this on top of other long-duration, high-multiple growth exposure. Fund it from that sleeve** — a multiple-sensitive healthcare or growth-compounder holding. Adding BTSG to a portfolio already long duration is doubling a losing factor bet.
