## SECTOR BRIEF — BTSG (BrightSpring Health Services)

**Sub-industry:** Home- & community-based health services (home health, hospice, personal care) plus specialty/oncology pharmacy and infusion — NOT the mega-cap pharma universe that the sector-peer feed returns. True comparables are ADUS, ENSG, CHE, AMED, OPCH.

---

### 1. Sector Overview
US home- and community-based care is a structurally growing, highly fragmented, government-funded services pool spanning home health (~$130bn+), hospice (~$30bn), home infusion and specialty pharmacy. Volume is driven by demographics (~10,000 Americans turn 65 daily; 65+ population rising toward ~80m by 2040) and by payers and CMS steering complex patients out of institutional settings; the average home health episode costs a fraction of a skilled-nursing stay. Pricing, however, is administered: CMS sets fee-for-service rates and states/MCOs set Medicaid rates, so operators cannot price to inflation. The sector is in a **mid-to-late consolidation phase** — scale buys drug-purchasing leverage, payer contracts and fixed-cost absorption, which is why growth is increasingly M&A- and share-driven rather than purely organic. Recent proof: **McKesson/CD&R agreed to acquire Option Care Health (OPCH) for $5.8bn EV at $32.05/share (37% premium) on 6 Oct 2026**, closing H1 2027 — financial sponsor + strategic distribution capital is now underwriting this sector.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Site-of-care migration is accelerating, not slowing.** BTSG's Q3'26 revenue grew **26.4% to $4,213m** with adjusted EBITDA **+42% to $228m** — roughly 2x the growth of any listed service peer — as payers move complex, dual-eligible populations into home/community and specialty-pharmacy channels.
- **Structural demand inelasticity.** Medicare/Medicaid-funded volumes are largely non-discretionary and tied to an aging cohort; BTSG's 100% US footprint sits directly on that demographic curve.
- **Consolidation runway + strategic bid interest.** A top-5 player was just taken private at a 37% premium. Sub-scale operators face rate compression and labour costs they cannot absorb, which feeds both organic share gains and bolt-on M&A for scaled platforms (BTSG has been integrating acquired home-health branches).

---

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Administered pricing is turning negative.** The **CY2026 home health PPS final rule cuts aggregate Medicare payments by ~1.3% (−$220m)** despite a 3.2% market-basket update (behavioural offsets), while hospice gets a modest **+2.6%** — i.e. rate updates below wage inflation for the largest segment.
- **Medicaid HCBS retrenchment.** Post-H.R. 1, **18+ states have proposed or enacted cuts to home- and community-based services**, directly hitting the personal-care/Medicaid-heavy slice of BTSG's Provider segment. This is the single largest policy tail risk in the sub-sector.
- **Labour and drug-cost pass-through.** Caregiver/nurse shortage drives wage inflation (labour ~75% of the home-health rate base); simultaneously IRA-driven Part D redesign and negotiated drug pricing compress pharmacy gross margin — visible in BTSG's gross margin slide from **17.5% (2022) to 11.8% (2025)**, even as EBITDA dollars rose.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (growth) / AVERAGE (profitability)**
- **Rationale for strong:** fastest organic-plus-M&A revenue growth in the comp set; seven consecutive quarters of beating or raising cash-profit guidance; FY26 revenue guided to **$15.9–16.1bn** and adj. EBITDA to **$850–860m (+37.6–39.3%)** with operating cash flow **≥$700m**; a differentiated dual-eligible/Medicaid + specialty-oncology pharmacy franchise that peer home-health pure-plays cannot replicate; and a 23,500-employee national platform that is a natural consolidator.
- **Why not unambiguously strong:** structurally the lowest gross and operating margin in the peer group, meaningful leverage (FY25 interest expense $157m on a ~$13bn market cap), beta 1.85, and a business model whose margin is a function of CMS/state rate decisions rather than pricing power. Porter's: **supplier power HIGH** (drug manufacturers, GPOs), **buyer power HIGH** (CMS, states, MCOs), **rivalry HIGH but consolidating**, **entry barriers MODERATE** (licensure + payer contracts + drug-purchase scale), **substitutes MODERATE** (SNF/hospital-at-home).
- **Stock-specific caution:** after +104% LTM and a 12% post-Q3 pop, the equity embeds continued 25%+ growth and margin expansion; any Medicaid HCBS cut broadening or a CY2027 home-health rate disappointment would compress a ~19x multiple toward the ~10–14x range peers and take-private precedents imply.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE** (with above-average policy risk)
Demand is among the most durable in healthcare and the site-of-care shift plus a proven take-private bid give the group a supportive tape. The offset is that 2026 reimbursement is a net cut in home health and Medicaid HCBS is under active state retrenchment — so the rating is really **"Attractive for scaled, growing operators (BTSG, ENSG, ADUS); Neutral-to-Unattractive for subscale, Medicaid-concentrated or margin-squeezed names (AMED, CHE)."** BTSG's growth justifies a premium multiple; the *degree* of that premium (~19x vs ~10x strategic precedent) is where the risk now sits.
