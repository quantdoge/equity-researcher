# BTSG — BrightSpring Health Services, Inc.
## Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company snapshot:** US-domiciled, Louisville, KY-headquartered provider of home- and community-based pharmacy (Pharmacy Solutions) and clinical provider services (Provider Services). FY2025 revenue **$12,910.6M** (+28.2% y/y); net income $104.8M. FY2026 guidance raised to **$14.45–15.0B** (Q3'26 revenue $4.2B, +26% y/y). ~465,000 patients daily, ~10,500 clinicians/pharmacists, ~175 pharmacies, 43M+ prescriptions in 2025, ~$2.6B total debt as of 12/31/25. Market cap ~$13.2B. *(Source: FY2025 10-K filed 2/27/2026, via TradingView/StockTitan summaries; Q3'26 release, GlobeNewswire 10/30/2026.)*

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

BTSG is a **pure-play US domestic business**. There is no disclosed foreign revenue, no emerging-market operations, and no cross-border currency, repatriation, expropriation, or sanctions exposure. This is the single most important structural fact for this analysis: BTSG has **no geopolitical risk in the traditional sense** — its risk is almost entirely *US policy and regulatory* risk.

| Geography | Revenue exposure | Nature of exposure | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States (all 50 states)** | **~100%** | Nationwide pharmacy + home health/hospice/rehab; reimbursement from Medicare, Medicaid, commercial payers | **Medium** (low political risk; **High regulatory intensity**) |
| **10 concentrated states** | **~53% of FY2025 revenue** | 10-K: "approximately 53% of our revenue in 10 states" with "favorable demographics and regulatory environments" | **Medium-High** (state Medicaid rate & CON/licensure risk concentrated) |
| **Pharmacy Solutions segment** | **~88%** (Q1'26: $3,171M of $3,614M) | Infusion, specialty, home/community pharmacy; drug-price and Part D exposure | **High** |
| **Provider Services segment** | **~12%** (Q1'26: $442M) | Home health, hospice, rehab, personal care, home-based primary care | **Medium-High** |
| **Non-US / emerging markets** | **0%** | None disclosed | **Negligible / N/A** |

**Implication:** sanctions (OFAC), export controls (EAR/ITAR), FCPA foreign-bribery, and Russia/China decoupling exposures are **immaterial**. Country-risk screening is effectively irrelevant; the correct lens is *payer-risk and regulator-risk*.

*(Caveat: the 10-K discloses revenue concentration by payer — Medicare A, B, C, D, Medicaid, commercial — for each segment in its XBRL, but I was unable to extract the precise percentages from the available excerpts. Treat payer-mix figures below as directional.)*

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

### Risk 1 — Reimbursement and drug-pricing policy (HIGH probability / HIGH impact)
This is the dominant risk. The 10-K explicitly flags that "changes in Medicare and Medicaid could lower pharmacy and provider solutions rates or increase our expenses," plus "potential changes in drug pricing and reimbursement under Medicare Part D and Medicaid" and "legislative interest in drug pricing practices."

Three live vectors:
- **IRA Medicare Drug Price Negotiation:** negotiated prices for the first 10 drugs took effect **1/1/2026**, with further rounds phasing in. For a business that fills 43M+ prescriptions, reduced brand-drug prices compress the spread-based economics of specialty/infusion pharmacy. Sell-side coverage is already framing this as an "ongoing IRA risk" heading into results *(Zacks, 10/29/2026; CMS fact sheet; KFF)*. Community pharmacy groups (NCPA) have publicly warned the program "threatens pharmacies" — a signal that distributor-level margins face pressure.
- **Medicaid funding pressure:** Provider Services (home health, personal care, rehab) is heavily Medicaid-linked and sensitive to state rate decisions and enrollment policy. With 53% of revenue concentrated in 10 states, a handful of state rate cuts is a material event.
- **Part D / PBM cost containment and post-payment audits:** 10-K cites "cost containment initiatives by third-party payors, including post-payment audits" and managed-care/alternative payment model transitions as risks to realized revenue.

### Risk 2 — Controlled-substance and healthcare-fraud enforcement (MEDIUM probability / MEDIUM-HIGH impact)
PharMerica, BTSG's long-term-care pharmacy subsidiary, has a **repeated** DEA/CSA and False Claims Act track record (details in §3). This is the highest-severity *legal* tail risk: DOJ/OIG enforcement in LTC pharmacy typically arrives as a multi-year qui tam investigation, then a nine-figure settlement headline. The 2015 PharMerica matter resolved at **$31.5M** for CSA/FCA violations (dispensing Schedule II drugs without valid prescriptions); a **$778,000** DEA recordkeeping settlement followed a **June 2024** DEA on-site inspection that "revealed multiple recordkeeping failures." A second recordkeeping failure at the same subsidiary after a prior CSA settlement is the kind of pattern that upgrades a civil matter into a compliance-integrity-agreement matter.

### Risk 3 — Antitrust/transaction regulatory friction (HIGH probability — already realized / MEDIUM impact)
BTSG's own portfolio reshaping has been slowed by regulators on both ends:
- **Seller side:** the **$835M Community Living divestiture** (ResCare Community Living → Sevita/National Mentor) was *delayed by an FTC antitrust complaint* and only closed in **March 2026**; the FTC required Sevita to divest **128 locations** and finalized its consent order **June 10, 2026** *(FTC case file; Home Health Care News 3/2026; Law360)*.
- **Buyer side:** BTSG is the DOJ-mandated divestiture buyer in *United States v. UnitedHealth/Amedisys*, acquiring **~160 home health/hospice branches across 19 states (~$528M annual revenue)**, closed in 2026. Critically, the 10-K/ARS discloses that **BrightSpring learned in December 2025 it must file a Certificate of Need application with the New York State Department of Health**, and that acquired agencies "must enroll as new providers with Medicare which may cause significant Medicare billing delays" *(DOJ ATR consent motion; SEC ARS PDF; Modern Healthcare; Wilson Sonsini)*. This is a concrete, disclosed revenue-timing risk from state-level licensing — the closest thing BTSG has to "country risk," but at the state level.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **🚩 Controlled-substances compliance at PharMerica (elevated).** A 2015 CSA/FCA settlement followed by a 2024 DEA inspection finding "multiple recordkeeping failures" is a documented repeat-failure pattern at a single regulated subsidiary. Highest-severity item in the file.
2. **🚩 HIPAA/cyber.** 5.8M individuals breached (2023), $5.275M class settlement, ransomware-group naming, and a newly finalized (May 2026) Security Rule raising the compliance bar. Expect rising cyber-insurance and remediation cost.
3. **⚠️ Post-payment audit exposure.** Home health/hospice/rehab is the single most audit-intensive segment of Medicare (RAC, UPIC, SMRC, TPE). The 10-K explicitly names post-payment audits as a revenue risk.
4. **⚠️ FCA/qui tam susceptibility.** Given the segment mix and history, an unsealed qui tam matter is a plausible, unquantified tail.
5. **⚠️ State-level licensing/CON dependence.** The New York CON example shows state administrative process can delay recognition of acquired revenue.
6. **✅ Not flagged:** FCPA/UK Bribery Act (no foreign ops), OFAC sanctions, export controls, AML/KYC, GDPR (US patients only, though state privacy laws apply), expropriation/nationalisation, currency controls. These are non-issues and should be excluded from the investment debate.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# MEDIUM
*(Geopolitical sub-score: **LOW** — 100% US, no foreign/sanctions/EM exposure. Regulatory & legal sub-score: **HIGH** — heavily regulated payer-dependent model with a repeat DEA/CSA and FCA history, active privacy litigation, and demonstrated antitrust friction on M&A.)*

**Why not higher:** the two largest legal overhangs are now *closed* (Community Living sale, FTC consent order; PharMerica breach settlement approved), the securities class action appears financially sub-material relative to a $13B cap, the balance sheet has de-levered with $565.6M liquidity, and 53% revenue concentration in 10 states actually reflects deliberate selection of favourable regulatory regimes.

**Why not lower:** (a) the IRA/Medicaid reimbursement squeeze is structural and intensifying, not cyclical; (b) the DEA recordkeeping recurrence at PharMerica is a genuine enforcement-escalation marker; (c) the New York CON/Medicare re-enrollment issue shows the growth-by-acquisition engine can be slowed by state administrators; and (d) the securities class action's disposition is unverified.

**What would change the rating:** a new DOJ/OIG civil investigative demand or CIA at PharMerica → upgrade to **HIGH**. Conversely, dismissal with prejudice of the IPO securities class action plus two clean DEA inspection cycles → **LOW-MEDIUM**.
