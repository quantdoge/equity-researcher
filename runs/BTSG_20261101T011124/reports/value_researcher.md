## BrightSpring Health Services (BTSG) — Value Analysis
*Price $66.89 (close 30 Oct 2026) · Market cap ~$14.7B · EV ~$16.4B · TTM revenue $15.25B*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow** (genuinely widening, but thin)

BTSG is a US home-and-community care and pharmacy platform, now essentially two businesses after the March 2026 divestiture of the low-margin Community Living unit (Q3'26 annualized run-rate):

| Segment | Revenue (ann.) | Segment EBITDA | Margin | % of seg. EBITDA |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pharmacy Solutions (specialty, infusion, LDD hub services) | ~$14.8B | ~$808M | 5.5% | 72% |
| Provider Services (home health, hospice, rehab, infusion) | ~$1.9B | ~$316M | 16.4% | 28% |

**What is actually moated:** the specialty-pharmacy/LDD franchise. Managing 162 limited-distribution drugs across dozens of manufacturers implies REMS compliance, accreditation, payer-network inclusion and manufacturer trust — real intangible-asset barriers that took decades to assemble. Provider Services benefits from local referral density and payer contracts.

**What is not moated:** pricing power. Gross margin is 11.8% and has *fallen* from 18.6% in 2021 (mix shift and pass-through drug economics). Reimbursement is set by CMS, state Medicaid agencies, PBMs and manufacturers. A 100bp change in gross margin moves EBITDA by ~15%. This is a scale-and-execution business, not a pricing-power business.

**The decisive test — ROIC vs. cost of capital:**

| | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 | TTM (Q3'26) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROIC | 4.2% | 3.4% | 4.5% | 6.4% | **~10.7%** |

The trajectory is the real story: from value-destroying to just above a ~9% WACC. But 79% of invested capital is goodwill and intangibles ($3.03B) — returns are contingent on acquisitions earning their keep, not on organic capital efficiency. That is a **Narrow** moat at best, and it was *absent* as recently as 2024.

---

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **6.0 / 10**

**Working for it:**
- Organic revenue +26% in Q3'26, +23–26% for three straight quarters; FY27 organic growth guided 15–20%
- Adjusted EBITDA +42% YoY ($228M); adjusted EPS +80% to $0.54 — margin expansion *and* growth
- FCF conversion: TTM FCF $492M vs. net income $338M (146%); capex only ~$110M (0.7% of revenue)
- Deleveraging: net debt from $3.66B (2023) to $1.66B; net leverage ~1.9x vs. ~4.7x at IPO
- Retained earnings finally positive ($139M); GAAP profitability restored

**Against it:**
- 11.8% gross margin, ~3.8% operating margin — no shock absorbers
- FCF history is unreliable: **negative** FCF in 2022 (–$75M) and 2024 (–$57M) on working-capital swings; the Q2'26 quarter alone consumed $117M of WC
- SBC $56M/yr (~16% of net income) — an economic cost the "adjusted" figures ignore
- Debt $2.38B; interest still $145M TTM (~17% of adjusted EBITDA), floating-rate exposed; beta 1.85
- Only ~2.5 years of public-company disclosure; 2021–2024 cumulative net losses of ~$213M

### 3. Valuation Analysis — the price already assumes the bull case

| Metric | BTSG | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (2025 GAAP) | 76.9x | stale base year |
| P/E (2026E adjusted ~$1.93) | **~34.7x** | vs. healthcare peer median ~30.8x |
| EV/EBITDA (2026E adj. $855M) | **~19.2x** | ~16x on 2027E |
| FCF yield (TTM FCF / EV) | **3.0%** | 2.4% after SBC |
| Owner-earnings yield (NI + D&A – maint. capex – SBC ≈ $330M) | **~2.0%** | poor for a levered distributor |
| P/B | 7.8x | on 79% goodwill |

**DCF (9% WACC, 2.5% terminal; 2026 FCF base $460M):**

| Scenario | FCF growth path | Intrinsic value/share |
|---|---|---|
| Bear (reimbursement adverse, growth 8%→3%) | 8%/3% | ~$35 |
| **Base** (2027 guide top end, then fading) | 15%/10%/5% | **~$48** |
| Bull (20% for 5 yrs, then 8%) | 20%/8% | ~$62 |
| Bull+ (sustained 20%/8%) | | ~$72 |

**At $66.89 the market is paying for roughly 20%+ FCF growth for five years *and* high-single-digit growth for a further five** — i.e., flawless execution for a decade in a business that earned below its cost of capital as recently as 2025. A reverse-DCF sanity check confirms this: the price discounts the bull case, not the base case.

You can also see the asymmetry: the entire sell-side (Goldman $71, UBS $85, BTIG $90, Morgan Stanley/KeyBanc/Raymond James $80) is clustered at or below the current price on 2027 numbers. When the most bullish analyst's target is only ~34% above spot and the median is ~13% above, you are not buying at a discount to value — you are buying at the consensus estimate *and* hoping the multiple holds.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Above average, with two reservations**

**Credit where due (CEO Jon Rousseau, CFO Jen Phipps):** they have raised FY26 guidance repeatedly, beat in four of the last four quarters, converted ~$1.2B of proceeds/FCF into net-debt reduction, and had the discipline to exit Community Living rather than defend a low-return asset. That is owner-operator behavior.

**Reservations:**
1. **Buyback discipline.** $120M repurchased in Q1–Q2 2026 at 30x+ earnings. Repurchasing stock at a 2% owner-earnings yield is not value-accretive; it is momentum-following with shareholders' money.
2. **Sponsor overhang and M&A appetite.** KKR still holds ~26.8M shares (13.7%) after selling at $58.45; the sponsor base has been a persistent supply of secondary offerings. And with $3.0B of goodwill, the reflex toward bolt-on M&A remains the main way capital is deployed — historically at returns below WACC.

No dividend. Capital allocation is improving but is not yet Buffett-grade.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD — do not initiate at $66.89**

This is a *good, rapidly improving* business that is no longer a *cheap* one. The operational turnaround (ROIC 3.4% → 10.7%, leverage 4.7x → 1.9x, margin expansion in both segments) is real and deserves respect. But my base-case intrinsic value is **~$48/share**, and even my bull case (~$62) sits *below* the current quote. At a 39% premium to base-case value and a 2.0–2.4% owner-earnings yield, there is no margin of safety — the defining requirement of the discipline.

- **Existing holders:** hold, but trim into strength toward $70+. Let the compounding continue while leverage falls and the LDD franchise widens, but size for a 1.85-beta name at 19x EBITDA.
- **New money:** pass. A 25% discount to base-case value implies an entry near **$36–40**; even a generous 30% discount to my bull case is only ~$44. I would want ≤$45 before underwriting this business.

**What would raise my moat rating and value:** two consecutive years of ROIC ≥12% with no margin erosion; net leverage <1.5x; Provider Services revenue mix rising above 30%; cadence of LDD wins maintained through generic conversions.

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **340B and drug-reimbursement reform (highest severity).** CMS proposed in July 2026 to reimburse 340B-acquired drugs at ASP–33.4%. Pharmacy Solutions is 88% of revenue; a reimbursement reset there would compress the 5.5% segment margin and invalidate the growth path my base case assumes. Site-neutral payment and PBM/direct-and-indirect-remuneration reform are additional live threats.
2. **Razor-thin margins plus high operating leverage.** 11.8% gross margin means a modest rate cut or labor-cost spike produces an outsized EBITDA hit — and the 19x EBITDA multiple would compress simultaneously (double hit).
3. **Leverage and rates.** $2.38B debt with a 1.85 beta; interest at 17% of adjusted EBITDA. A refinancing at higher rates or a covenant-sensitive downturn matters.
4. **Growth durability.** 26% growth is heavily tied to branded LDD launches and specialty script volume (+29%). Generic conversions of key drugs are deflationary to revenue and can pressure margins.
5. **Earnings-quality skeptics.** GAAP profitability is barely two years old; "adjusted" EBITDA excludes $56M of SBC. Should the market shift to valuing on GAAP/owner earnings, the multiple has a long way to fall.
6. **Sponsor supply.** KKR's residual 13.7% (and any other sponsor stake) is a recurring secondary-offering overhang that caps upside on rallies — with the stock up ~6x from its $13 IPO, tax-motivated selling is a persistent feature.
7. **Acquisition integration.** $3.0B goodwill against $2.19B of equity: any impairment materially dents book value and the ROIC story.

**Bottom line:** excellent execution, narrow and widening moat, capital structure finally repaired — but priced for perfection. A wonderful business purchased at a fair-to-full price is still a hold, not a buy.
