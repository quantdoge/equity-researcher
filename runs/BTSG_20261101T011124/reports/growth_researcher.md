# BrightSpring Health Services (BTSG) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $66.89 | Diluted mkt cap ~$14.7B | Net debt ~$1.5B | EV ~$16.2B | Data as of Q3'26 (reported Oct 30, 2026)**

---

## 1. Growth Story

BrightSpring is a **care-migration platform**: a specialty pharmacy + home-based clinical provider business built to profit as complex care shifts out of hospitals and nursing homes into the home. The disruptive engine is its **limited-distribution drug (LDD) franchise** — 162 branded LDDs across dozens of manufacturers, 19 launched YTD 2026 — where BTSG is a *designated* channel partner for launch-stage specialty therapies, capturing script growth (Q3 +29%) and gross profit per script (+27% to $28.86) as drugs scale. The second engine is a **fragmented home health/hospice/personal care roll-up** now being accelerated by the Amedisys/LHC branch acquisition (home health average daily census +58% YoY). This is not a "story stock" anymore: management has raised FY2026 guidance twice, and EBITDA is compounding ~40% against ~25% revenue growth — operating leverage is inflecting.

**The Rule of 40 problem — and why it misleads here:** the tool scores BTSG at **17.7** (14.6% growth + 3.1% FCF margin). That is the wrong lens for a 12%-gross-margin drug distributor. The correct scorecard for this business model is **EBITDA growth (+38%) + FCF conversion (>60% of EBITDA)**, which reads as a high-quality compounder. Flagging this explicitly: any screen that mechanically rejects BTSG on Rule of 40 will miss the actual inflection.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

| Market | Est. US TAM | BTSG 2026E | Implied share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Home health / hospice / personal care / rehab | ~$250B+ | ~$1.9B (Provider) | **<1%** |
| Specialty pharmacy + infusion channel | ~$350–400B | ~$14.1B (Pharmacy) | ~4% |
| Home/community & senior-living pharmacy | ~$40B | ~$2.2B | ~5% |

The asymmetry is in **Provider Services**: a $1.9B business in a $250B fragmented market where management states *"demand for services continues to outstrip our ability to provide them."* Structural drivers are demographic, not cyclical — aging population, rising chronic-disease prevalence, and payers' hard financial incentive to fund home-based care at a fraction of institutional cost. The moat is **distribution density**: pharmacy and provider operations co-located in ~40 states, 465,000 patients served daily, ~10,500 clinicians/pharmacists, 96% of legacy home health branches rated 4-star+, and manufacturer relationships that make BTSG a launch partner of choice for LDDs (19 launches YTD). That co-location is the cross-sell asset — pharmacy scripts feed provider referrals and vice versa — and it is genuinely hard to replicate.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4/FY2026 results — Feb 26, 2027:** first formal FY2027 guide. Consensus sits at $17.72B (+14%), *below* management's stated 15–20% organic framework → high probability of another raise-and-beat cycle.
- **LDD launch cadence (ongoing, quarterly):** 7 launched in Q3, 19 YTD, 162 total; the 2026 cohort is outperforming the 2025 cohort in year one. Each launch adds high-GP scripts with no incremental SG&A.
- **IRA headwind roll-off (Q4'26 → 2027):** another ~$50M Q4 impact completes the ~$200M FY2026 drag. Home & community pharmacy laps this entirely in 2027, converting a −6% segment into a growth contributor.
- **LEAD ACO Medicare program — starts January 2027:** meaningful primary-care economics from 2028, with *no downside risk* in current ACO arrangements and shared savings already being realized.
- **Capital deployment:** $120M of buybacks YTD, 1.79x leverage, and stated appetite for tuck-ins up to ~$20M EBITDA — plus Amedisys/LHC branch integration tracking to the $35M 2026 EBITDA target.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY**

**Thesis:** 15–20% organic revenue growth with EBITDA compounding nearly 2x faster, funded internally, at 18.9x forward EV/EBITDA and 0.94x sales. The market is implying ~18% revenue CAGR to 2030 ($30.9B), which is *exactly* management's framework — so you are not paying for heroic execution, you are paying for the base case and getting the LDD optionality, the LEAD ACO value-based upside, and continued bolt-on M&A for free. Estimate revisions are positive, beats are consistent and large, and the PT cascade has room to run toward the $80–90 top of the range.

**Why not Strong Buy:** (1) a ~12% gross margin structure means small pricing or mix errors move EBITDA violently; (2) 36x forward EPS and a 6x run from IPO already discount a lot of the inflection; (3) the revenue base is increasingly concentrated in branded LDD scripts that can convert to generics or lose exclusivity; (4) the Rule-of-40/quality screens that many growth mandates use will keep this name out of the buying pool. **Position sizing: core growth holding, accumulate on pullbacks toward the mid-$50s.**

## 7. Key Risks

- **Reimbursement / policy:** ~$200M of FY2026 revenue drag from the IRA and Part D redesign. Further Medicare/Medicaid pharmacy reimbursement compression — or state Medicaid rate cuts to home care — hits the thinnest part of the P&L directly.
- **LDD concentration and generic conversion:** the specialty engine depends on manufacturer partnerships. Loss of a major LDD relationship, an earlier-than-expected generic conversion, or a wave of exclusivity expirations would compress gross profit per script ($28.86 today) faster than volume can offset.
- **Thin-margin operating leverage cuts both ways:** revenue growth of 24% translated to 38% EBITDA growth — that same leverage means a 2–3% revenue miss could flatten EBITDA growth.
- **Integration and labor:** Amedisys/LHC branch consolidation, de novo home health buildout, and clinician wage inflation. Retention has improved, but the personal care/home health labor market remains tight and wage inflation is the sector's chronic risk.
- **Leverage and rate exposure:** $2.2B of debt / $1.5B net. 1.79x is comfortable today, but the 10-yr at ~4.9% makes refinancing and acquisition financing more expensive than the 2024–25 environment.
- **Accounting quality:** GAAP FY2025 net income of $191M against ~$618M of adjusted EBITDA — a wide gap from D&A, interest, and intangible amortization. Diluted shares (219.8M) exceed basic (197.9M) by 11%, with ~$70M/yr of stock comp; dilution is real and ongoing.
- **Divestiture-distorted comparability:** the Community Living sale (3/30/26) restated everything into discontinued operations. Any trailing-multiple screen on BTSG is unreliable — and so is any growth rate compared to reports published before the divestiture.
