## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — BrightSpring Health Services (BTSG)

**Snapshot:** Louisville, KY; home- and community-based pharmacy + provider services; ~23,500 FTE; market cap ~$13.2B at $66.89; beta 1.85; IPO'd Jan 2024 at $13.00 (KKR-sponsored, priced below range). FY26 revenue run-rate ~$16B; Q3'26 revenue $4.2B (+26% y/y), adj. EBITDA $228M (+42%). Two segments post-2026: **Pharmacy Solutions (~87% of revenue)** and **Provider Services (~13%)**.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (weak at the margin)

**Negatives:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality confirmed.** Jon Rousseau signs as "Chairman, President, and Chief Executive Officer" (Q3'26 10-Q, Ex. 31.1). Concentration of agenda-setting and oversight in one person, at a company whose risk profile is regulatory-heavy.
- **Sponsor control/overhang.** KKR entities (KKR Phoenix Aggregator et al.) sold **19,715,000 shares for ~$807.5M at $40.961** in an underwritten offering (Form 4 accepted 03/04/2026) and still hold **~41.8M shares — ~21% of the ~198M shares outstanding**. This follows 2025 secondaries (~14M + 2.1M option). BrightSpring was sponsor-controlled at IPO and has historically relied on controlled-company exemptions; KKR's board-designation rights and de facto influence persist well beyond its economic stake.
- **Related-party legacy.** The LBO-era structure loaded >$1B of acquisition debt and KKR transaction/advisory fees on the company (documented in the 2022 BuzzFeed investigation). Monitoring-fee arrangements are a recurring shareholder-value leak in sponsor names.
- **Insider signal is one-directional: no open-market buys.** I found no Form 4 purchase by any officer or director over the past ~14 months; activity is dominated by KKR sales and a dense cluster of officer filings (13+ in 2026, concentrated in Jan/Mar/May/Jul — consistent with RSU vesting and tax withholding rather than conviction selling, but I could not parse individual line items).

**Positives:**
- Clean SOX 302 certifications; **zero SEC enforcement actions** returned by EDGAR full-text search.
- No single customer ≥10% of consolidated revenue — unusual and valuable for a healthcare-services rollup.
- Actively reshaping the portfolio (ResCare Community Living divested), which reads as strategic discipline rather than empire-building.

**Unexamined (data gap):** the DEF 14A and 10-K full text were blocked by SEC (HTTP 403), so committee independence, comp metrics, and board refreshment could not be verified. Rating assumes a majority-independent board with standing audit/comp committees, consistent with Nasdaq post-IPO norms.

---

### 2. Regulatory & Legal Risk Summary

- **No active SEC/DOJ enforcement action identified** against BTSG. This is the single most reassuring data point in the file.
- **IRA / pharmacy reimbursement reset — largest quantified exposure.** Management has flagged a **~$200M 2026 revenue headwind in Home Pharmacy** from Inflation Reduction Act Part D redesign/manufacturer price concessions. With Pharmacy Solutions at ~87% of revenue, this is a structural repricing of the core business, not a one-off.
- **CMS/Medicaid rate and audit risk.** Home health rate rulemaking, state Medicaid budget pressure, and **post-payment audits** (UPIC/TPE/RCD) are explicitly enumerated in company risk disclosures. ~**53% of revenue is generated in 10 states** — a rate cut in any one large state is material.
- **New full-risk exposure: CMS LEAD ACO.** BrightSpring was approved for the LEAD model (effective 1/1/2027, 10-year term). Moving into accountable-care downside risk introduces actuarial/benchmark risk and a new compliance surface, and CMS materially revised LEAD methodology in July 2026 — the economics are still moving.
- **Antitrust scrutiny of the M&A engine.** The **FTC required Sevita to divest 128 locations** to close its $835M purchase of BrightSpring's ResCare business (Feb 2026). Separately, **DOJ rejected BrightSpring/Pennant as divestiture buyers** of Amedisys assets (May 2025) before a ~$239M BrightSpring purchase of Amedisys home health branches closed. Growth-by-acquisition is now explicitly on the agencies' radar.
- **HIPAA/cyber.** March 14, 2023 network intrusion; breach notice filed with the Massachusetts AG on June 21, 2023. No OCR settlement or penalty located — residual OCR enforcement and class-action tail remains. (Do not conflate with the separate, larger PharMerica 2023 ransomware matter.)
- **False Claims Act tail.** Legacy ResCare qui tam history (e.g., $2.15M Texas/DOJ settlement); plaintiff firms are *currently* soliciting ResCare/BrightSpring whistleblowers on upcoding/bundling theories. Sector comps are ugly — Amedisys paid $150M. Given 43M prescriptions/year and home-health visit coding, this is a permanent, low-frequency/high-severity exposure.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Reimbursement-model concentration.** ~87% of revenue sits in pharmacy distribution whose economics are set by CMS, Part D plan design, and manufacturer/DIR terms — a policy-driven margin structure management only partially controls, with $200M already identified as an IRA headwind.
2. **Cyber and PHI exposure at scale.** 175+ pharmacies, 43M prescriptions/year, 6,600 institutional customers, and an aggressive "AI across operations" rollout that widens the data-governance and attack surface at a company with a prior (2023) intrusion.
3. **Labor and M&A integration.** 23,500 employees in scarcity-constrained nursing/caregiver roles; concurrently absorbing the Sevita carve-out (discontinued-ops accounting), Amedisys branches, and tuck-ins — integration failure would hit both margin and care quality simultaneously.
   - *Mitigant:* no single customer >10% of revenue; leverage and cash flow are improving, which reduces the classic sponsor-LBO fragility.

---

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment

- **The dominant reputational event is largely behind the company.** The April 2022 BuzzFeed News investigation documented, across 600+ group homes acquired by KKR in 2019: **at least three resident deaths following care lapses, 500+ serious state citations, and 40% of serious citations while owning 16% of the homes** in the sampled states, plus sub-Walmart caregiver wages and >$135M/year in interest costs diverting funds from care. That business (ResCare Community Living) was **sold to Sevita and closed March 31, 2026 for $835M** — a decisive de-risking of the ESG/reputational overhang.
- **Residual tail:** plaintiff firms are still actively recruiting BrightSpring/ResCare abuse claimants; indemnity/representation obligations and pre-close period litigation survive the sale. Expect episodic negative headlines from legacy cases even though the exposure is no longer operating.
- **Current sentiment is positive:** Q3 beats, guidance raises, institutional accumulation (BofA +48.7%, CPPIB, Cantor), 67% YTD run. No active consumer boycott or product-recall dynamic exists in this business model.
- **Net:** reputational risk has migrated from **High (2022)** to **Moderate**, with a legacy-litigation tail.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH** (low end)

No fraud indicators, no restatement, no active SEC/DOJ action, and no customer concentration — genuinely better hygiene than the average PE-sponsored healthcare rollup. But four exposures compound: (i) a quantified $200M IRA revenue reset inside 87% of the business, (ii) simultaneous CMS home health/Medicaid rate and audit pressure plus a new full-risk LEAD ACO, (iii) CEO/Chairman duality with ~21% sponsor overhang and zero insider buying, and (iv) a documented care-quality litigation tail. At ~5x the IPO price and beta 1.85, the equity has limited capacity to absorb a regulatory disappointment.

---

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **CEO/Chairman duality** (Rousseau) — raise with board, particularly as LEAD ACO and carve-out integration add oversight burden.
2. **KKR's remaining ~21% stake** — model the distribution schedule and index-inclusion/technicals around further secondaries; ~$2.8B of potential supply at current prices.
3. **IRA $200M headwind vs. management's "efficiency offsets" claim** — verify the offset with gross-margin bridge detail; this is the most likely source of a guidance reset.
4. **LEAD ACO full-risk participation from 1/1/2027** — request actuarial disclosure and stop-loss structure before the risk-based capital/MCR impact is modeled.
5. **Quantify legacy ResCare litigation/indemnity** in the Sevita sale — confirm whether liabilities retained, escrowed, or transferred.
6. **Cyber posture** post-2023 breach, now amplified by AI deployment — request incident-response and data-governance attestation.

**Data gaps (stated, not hidden):** SEC 403 errors blocked the 10-K/10-Q body and DEF 14A, so payer-mix percentages, board committee composition, and executive-comp metrics are unverified; officer Form 4 transactions were not line-item parsed, so a small officer-selling component within the RSU filing cluster cannot be excluded.
