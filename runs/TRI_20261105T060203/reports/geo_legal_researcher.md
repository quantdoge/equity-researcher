### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — AI copyright / training-data liability regime (Probability: High that regulation evolves adversely; Impact: Medium)**
- TRI won a landmark appellate victory: on **29 Sept 2026** the Third Circuit (No. 25-2153) affirmed that ROSS Intelligence's use of 2,243 Westlaw headnotes to train an AI legal-research tool was **not fair use** and infringed TRI's copyright. This is the first US appeals court ruling on AI training and fair use. *(Reuters, 2026-09-29; ca3.uscourts.gov opinion 252153p.pdf; ABA Business Law Today, Sept 2026)*
- **Two-sided:** TRI is a net *beneficiary* as plaintiff and licensor (Reuters genAI content licensing drove Reuters segment revenue +13% and margin +440bp to 24.3% in Q3'26). But TRI is simultaneously a *deployer* — CoCounsel Legal/Tax and Thomson LLM ingest third-party and customer content. Adverse fair-use doctrine or a plaintiff win against TRI's own models is the symmetric downside.
- Reuters reported (Jan 2026) TRI remains in an ongoing copyright dispute with another party over alleged misuse of its material; AI copyright litigation is described as entering a "pivotal year."

**Risk 2 — EU AI Act + GDPR compliance cost across the European book (Probability: Certain; Impact: Low–Medium)**
- The EU AI Act's risk-based obligations (GPAI transparency, high-risk system requirements) phase in through 2026–2027, layered on GDPR. Penalties reach **€35m or 7% of worldwide turnover**. *(Reuters Legal, 2026-07-17; Berkeley BCLT, 2026-06-30)*
- TRI sells *into* regulated workflows (legal, tax, compliance, HR-adjacent corporate tools) — meaning its products are frequently classified by customers as regulated systems, raising documentation, audit-trail and model-governance obligations. This is a cost/complexity risk, not an existential one, and partly a **tailwind**: compliance complexity is the core demand driver for the Corporates and Tax segments.

**Risk 3 — US–Canada trade rupture (Probability: Realised; Impact: Low)**
- Since **29 September 2026**, the US has prohibited certain Canadian goods and imposed an **additional 50% duty** on others. *(Reuters/Practical Law, "Doing business under US Canada tariffs and bans," 2026-10-27)*
- TRI is a Canadian-domiciled issuer with a US-majority revenue base and a controlling shareholder (The Woodbridge Company / Thomson family) in Canada. Direct COGS exposure is minimal (software/content, not goods), but second-order exposure exists via: (a) cross-border data-transfer friction, (b) CAD/USD translation on the C$1.0bn notes issued Sept 2026, (c) Canadian government-customer budgets, and (d) retaliatory procurement measures.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation (favourable posture)**
- *Thomson Reuters Enterprise Centre GmbH v. ROSS Intelligence* — **won on appeal, Sept 2026.** Affirmed copyrightability of headnotes and rejection of fair use. Establishes a licensing market for AI training data — strategically valuable IP precedent for Westlaw/Reuters content.
- No material adverse judgments, DOJ/SEC enforcement actions, or securities class actions surfaced in search. Company discloses only "various legal proceedings, claims, audits and investigations… in the ordinary course" (6-K, Aug 2026).

**Antitrust**
- Historical EU matter: Commission closed its probe into Reuters Instrument Codes (RICs) after binding commitments (abuse-of-dominance theory). **Resolved, no ongoing EU competition investigation identified.**
- Structural note: TRI's 51% sale of Global Print to KKR (closed 1 Oct 2026, ~$500m gross) and the earlier Refinitiv/LSEG disposal have progressively reduced horizontal overlap — antitrust surface is shrinking, not growing.

**Regulatory tailwinds (important for the thesis)**
- Global **e-invoicing / continuous transaction control (CTC) mandates** are expanding across Europe and LatAm. TRI's Pagero and Indirect Tax assets are explicitly cited as growth drivers (Corporates transactions revenue +32%, +23% organic in Q3'26). TRI was named a Leader in IDC MarketScape for compliant e-invoicing (Sept 2026). **Regulatory change is a revenue driver here, not a cost.**
- Reuters News benefits from a contractual price increase in its news agreement with LSEG's Data & Analytics business.

**Governance / structural**
- Controlled by The Woodbridge Company Limited (Thomson family). Minority-shareholder influence is limited; this is a standing governance discount factor, not a new risk.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

| Area | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Sanctions / export controls** | **Low.** No disclosed Russia/Iran/sanctioned-market revenue. Pagero's global trade-compliance module is a *seller* of sanctions tooling, not an exposure. |
| **FCPA / anti-bribery** | **Low–Medium.** Material government-customer base (Legal Professionals serves "governmental bodies"; Global Print served government). Third-party distributor network in LatAm/APAC warrants routine monitoring. No disclosed investigation. |
| **Data privacy** | **Medium.** CLEAR aggregates public-records/risk data — FCRA/DPPA-adjacent exposure in the US and GDPR exposure in Europe. This is the single most plausible source of a future regulatory action. |
| **AI training data / copyright** | **Medium.** Both sword and shield — see Risk 1. Watch for TRI being named *defendant* in a content-owner suit over CoCounsel/Thomson LLM training. |
| **AML/KYC** | **Low.** Not a regulated financial institution post-Refinitiv. |

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **LOW**

**Rationale:** ~88% recurring revenue, developed-market concentration, USD-denominated, no sanctions exposure, no active adverse litigation, and a landmark IP win that *monetises* rather than threatens its content moat. Regulatory change (e-invoicing, tax digitisation) is a net demand tailwind.

**What would move this to Medium:** (1) TRI named as a defendant in a material AI-training copyright suit; (2) a CLEAR data-privacy enforcement action; (3) escalation of US–Canada measures into cross-border data or services restrictions; (4) EU AI Act classification of CoCounsel as high-risk, triggering conformity-assessment delays in a ~20% revenue region.
