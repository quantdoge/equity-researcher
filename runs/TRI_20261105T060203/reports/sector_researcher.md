## SECTOR BRIEF — Thomson Reuters (TRI)

**Classification note:** FMP files TRI under *Industrials / Specialty Business Services*, alongside 27% of the S&P Industrials by cap (SpaceX, GE, CAT, RTX). That aggregate (21 companies, FY25 revenue $797bn, **31.8% gross margin**) is an economically meaningless benchmark for a 76%-gross-margin subscription information business. I have therefore benchmarked TRI against its true competitive set — information services & analytics (RELX, SPGI, MSCI, VRSK, FDS, MORN, CLVT) — and flagged where data was unavailable.

---

### 1. Sector Overview

TRI competes in **professional information services & decision-support analytics** — legal research, tax & accounting workflow, regulatory/compliance content — a roughly $60–80bn global pool growing mid-to-high single digits, of which legal AI is only ~$3.3bn in 2026 (Precedence Research) but compounding at 20%+. Structurally it is a **duopoly-plus oligopoly**: legal research is effectively TRI versus LexisNexis (RELX), which together hold an estimated 70–80% share (HHI est. ~2,500–3,000, highly concentrated), with vLex/Fastcase and Wolters Kluwer at the fringe. Barriers are high on proprietary content, citation/verification trust, and workflow embedding (multi-year subscriptions, high switching costs), but 2024–26 has introduced a **new and dangerous entry vector**: AI-native platforms (Harvey ~$190m ARR at end-2025, Legora, Luminance, Robin AI) that bypass the content-licensing moat by orchestrating foundation models. The sector is **mature/late-cycle in its core research business but in early-stage re-acceleration** as generative AI adds ARPU and regulation (e-invoicing) forces non-discretionary spend — a barbell between a defensive base and a highly contested growth layer.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Regulation as a demand engine (non-discretionary).** E-invoicing is now active policy in **60+ markets** with mandates phasing through 2026–27 (EU ViDA, Germany, France, Poland, Malaysia), pulling compliance and reporting spend forward. This directly feeds TRI's Corporates and Tax/Audit/Accounting segments and is largely immune to macro conditions — the classic reason these models carry beta of ~0.17.
- **Gen-AI monetisation is now measurable, not speculative.** Generative AI products reached **34% of annualised contract value** in Q3-26, with total organic revenue +10% and the "Big 3" segments +10% organic for a **second consecutive quarter** — evidence of genuine ARPU uplift rather than cannibalisation so far.
- **Portfolio simplification toward higher-quality revenue.** The 51% Global Print sale to KKR (~$500m, closed 1 Oct 2026) exits structurally declining, low-margin print, **adding 60–70bps to organic growth from Q4-26** and lifting group margin. Combined with a completed $600m buyback and $2.0bn debt raise, capital allocation now skews to AI/content M&A.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Foundation-model supplier power is the central structural risk.** OpenAI/Anthropic/Google are simultaneously partners and potential captors of the value chain. The cost of serving AI shows up immediately in margins: TRI's D&A rose from $725m (2023) to $930m (2025), and its **operating margin has fallen 770bps** (34.3% → 26.6%) while revenue grew only 10%.
- **AI-native entrants attack the seat-based pricing model.** Harvey's token consumption went from ~1tn to 12tn per month in a few months (July 2026); LexisNexis+ Protégé, vLex/Vincent and Legora are all viable. If AI compresses billable research hours and seats, the incumbent revenue model — not just market share — is at risk.
- **Fading pricing power/FX in the reported numbers.** FY25 reported growth was just **3.0%** (vs 6.8% in FY24) against mid-single-digit organic, i.e. FX/net divestiture drag, and Q3-26 still missed consensus EPS ($0.81 vs $0.89), driving a **9.9% single-day drawdown** — the market is no longer paying indiscriminately for the franchise.

### 5. TRI's Competitive Position: **STRONG** (franchise) — with a *deteriorating-momentum flag*

*Rationale for Strong:* #1/#2 position in legal research with a duopoly structural share; #1 in tax & accounting professional workflow; 75.8% gross margin confirms pricing power and content scarcity that AI-native entrants must license or replicate; ~80%+ recurring subscription revenue and 0.17 beta give best-in-class revenue visibility; FY26 guidance of ~8.5–9.0% organic growth would put it **above the peer median** for the first time since 2022 — the AI strategy is working at the top line.

*Why not unambiguously strong:* it is the only large-cap in the set with **negative operating-margin trajectory** (−7.7pp), it **trails SPGI/MSCI/MORN/VRSK on revenue growth over 4 years**, and its 2026 re-acceleration depends on defending seat-based pricing against Harvey and LexisNexis simultaneously. Position: **Strong franchise, mid-pack economics, negative momentum — i.e., a defensive compounder that must prove AI is accretive to margin, not just to revenue.**

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

Non-discretionary, regulation-underwritten demand, ~90% recurring revenue, 60–76% gross margins and a genuine step-up in AI-driven ARPU make this one of the highest-quality business models available. The rating is capped below "Highly Attractive" by two facts: the sector is **structurally more contested than at any point in 20 years** (AI-native entrants with venture funding and foundation-model leverage), and **incumbent margins are compressing sector-wide** as AI compute and software amortisation are absorbed (TRI −7.7pp, FDS −3.6pp). Within the sector, **prefer the ARPU-monetisers that also expand margin (RELX, SPGI, VRSK) over the content-holders absorbing AI cost** — TRI sits on the wrong side of that trade until operating margin inflects, which is the single metric to monitor at Q4-26.
