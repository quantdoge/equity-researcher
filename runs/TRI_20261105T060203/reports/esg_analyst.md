## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW**

- **Carbon intensity:** Scope 1 ≈4.6 kt CO₂e; Scope 2 (location-based) ≈22 kt CO₂e (third-party estimate). Against ~$7.6B revenue that is ≈3–4 tCO₂e per $1M revenue — order-of-magnitude below any Industrials peer and below most software peers. **Physical climate risk** is limited to offices/data-centre siting and employee safety; no stranded-asset exposure.
- **Targets:** SBTi-joined (2020) — 50% cut in Scope 1+2 by 2030 vs 2018; Scope 3 (fuel/energy, business travel, commuting) −25% by 2025 vs 2018; net zero by 2050; carbon-neutral today largely **via offsets** (a quality weakness: offsets do the work, not abatement). A 2025 TCFD-aligned summary confirms scenario/risk assessment work is progressing, with the company stating it is "evaluating opportunities to align future targets" — i.e., **the current net-zero architecture is under review, not locked in**.
- **Portfolio de-carbonisation:** sale of a 51% stake in **Global Print to KKR (closed 1 Oct 2026)** removes the most carbon- and paper-intensive legacy line and structurally lowers Scope 3 (paper/purchased goods).
- **Watch item:** AI compute. Legal/tax AI at 34% of annualised contract value is the growth engine; rented hyperscaler compute means the emissions land in **Scope 3 category 1**, exactly where disclosure is weakest. Expect rising Scope 3 and offset dependence.

**Verdict: Low.** Environmental is not a valuation factor for TRI; it is a disclosure-quality and offsets-credibility question.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM (with high-tail data-privacy risk)**

**Product/data (highest severity).** CLEAR and Westlaw PeopleMap aggregate public records, including SSNs, into investigative products sold partly to law enforcement. Consequences are already financial: a **$27.5M settlement** of California claims that TRI "secretly collected and sold detailed personal data about millions of Californians"; a **new Michigan class action (May 2026)** over display of Social Security data on Westlaw PeopleMap/CLEAR; and reported claims of a **March 2026 breach** of court case-management software exposing names, SSNs, licence and medical data (breach claim from a class-action aggregator — **unverified**, treat as a risk flag, not fact). This is the single ESG factor most capable of impairing cash flows: it threatens a product line in the Corporates segment, invites state AG action, and could force data-sourcing changes.

**Licence to operate — DHS/ICE conflict.** Thomson Reuters holds multi-million-dollar DHS/ICE contracts while owning Reuters News, whose independence is governed by the **Trust Principles**. In 2026, 150+ employees joined internal chats questioning whether these contracts compromise "independence and freedom from bias"; a contractor was investigated for alleged leaking and his contract was not renewed. The **NewsGuild filed a third unfair-labour-practice charge in twelve months** (retaliation, plus "bylining" disputes over editors taking union members' work). Immigration advocates allege the tools help locate raid targets; the company denies they are surveillance tools. A **2021 shareholder resolution** on human-rights review of ICE work drew elevated support — the issue is durable, not episodic, and a 2026 shareholder proposal still drew only 3.68% (see §4).

**Workforce.** ~500 engineering roles cut (≈1.8% of workforce) in July 2026, alongside aggressive AI-product reskilling. Turnover/engagement data exists in the 2025 Social Impact datasheet but was not machine-readable in this window. Journalist safety (Reuters staff in conflict zones) remains a standing operational risk.

**Verdict: Medium.** Financial impact so far is contained (~$30–40M known legal cost), but the combination of privacy litigation and the ICE/editorial-independence conflict attacks the two assets TRI monetises: trusted content and trusted data.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **6 / 10**

**Positives**
- **Board independence:** 100% independent directors apart from CEO Steve Hasker; a **Lead Independent Director** role exists with a defined mandate to work with the Chairman and CEO; all-independent committees; annual election of directors; 97% of director pay in equity.
- **Shareholder feedback:** director elections carried with ~**99.85%** support (2026 AGM); say-on-pay approved; auditor ratified. No poison pill, no super-voting *economic* structure beyond the family block.
- **Structural safeguard:** the **Founders Share Company** holds a special share requiring consent for changes that would breach the Trust Principles — a genuine, enforceable commitment to editorial independence that most media-adjacent issuers lack.
- **Litigation posture:** TRI is a **net IP plaintiff, not defendant** — the Third Circuit affirmed its win against ROSS Intelligence (29 Sept 2026), the first federal appellate fair-use ruling on AI training. That is a governance-positive: the board has defended the content moat rather than diluting it.

**Negatives (why not higher)**
- **Controlled company:** Woodbridge/Thomson family holds **~67.9–70.6%** of common shares. Minority holders cannot influence any vote; the 2026 shareholder proposal received **3.68% for / 95.41% against**. Engagement is cosmetic in outcome terms.
- **Chairman = controlling shareholder principal:** David Thomson is Chairman of both TRI and Woodbridge. The Lead Independent Director mitigates, but the chair is not independent.
- **Trust Principles under commercial strain:** the board has permitted the DHS/ICE data business to expand faster than the internal-governance answer to the conflict; staff concerns were met with an investigation and a non-renewal. Principle-vs-P&L tension is a live board-quality test.
- **Compensation:** pay design is TSR/EPS-led (appropriate), but I could not retrieve Hasker's 2026 pay figure or the precise weightings — **gap**. No ESG metrics verified in the incentive plan.

**Score: 6/10** — clean board mechanics and a real independence charter, capped hard by family control and a chair who answers to the same family.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD/ESRS:** TRI has substantial EU operations; EU subsidiaries fall in scope on size thresholds (Phase 2/3), with group-level value-chain data expected as non-EU parent rules phase in from FY2028. Readiness appears partial (voluntary TCFD summary + ESG datasheet, not assured ESRS reporting).
- **EU AI Act:** TRI is a **provider** of general-purpose and professional AI (CoCounsel, Westlaw AI). Transparency/documentation duties apply now; **Annex III high-risk classification is live where products are used by courts, prosecutors and public defenders** (a real TRI customer segment). This is the most under-priced regulatory item in the file.
- **Data protection:** GDPR (new customer Data Processing Addendum effective **15 Oct 2026**), UK GDPR, CCPA/CPRA and a widening set of US state privacy laws — the direct legal basis for controversies #1.
- **SEC climate rule:** adopted but stayed/not in force; no current US federal climate-disclosure obligation.
- **Canada:** CSSB/CSDS climate standards voluntary; no mandatory Canadian climate disclosure yet.
- **CBAM / carbon taxes:** not applicable (no covered goods); minimal direct carbon cost.
- **Not applicable:** ESG-linked debt covenants — TRI's Sept-2026 $2.0B note issuance appears conventionally priced; no sustainability-linked margin ratchets identified.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Third-party ESG risk score is genuinely good — Sustainalytics-style **~13.6 ("Low Risk")** per aggregator data (unverified; not a primary source). That score is right on **E** (negligible intensity) and on operational **G** hygiene, and wrong-footed on the two things that actually matter here: **data-privacy liability** and **minority-shareholder disenfranchisement**. TRI is best characterised as a low-footprint, well-boarded, family-controlled information monopolist whose ESG risk sits almost entirely in the product (data) and the cap table.

- Environmental: Low → near-Leader
- Social: Medium → Average, with a High-risk data-privacy tail
- Governance: 6/10 → Average, structurally capped by control

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

ESG is **not** a thesis driver for TRI in either direction, and that is itself the conclusion.

**Why neutral, not negative:** the environmental profile is effectively de-risked (and improved by the Global Print/KKR exit); known controversy costs (~$30–40M) are immaterial against $50B of market cap and ~39% adjusted EBITDA margins; and the Third Circuit ruling materially *strengthens* the IP moat that underpins long-duration pricing power.

**Why not positive:** the ~68–70% family control means ESG engagement has no voting consequence; the DHS/ICE conflict is a slow-burn reputational liability for the Reuters asset that management is resolving in favour of revenue; and CLEAR-type data monetisation is precisely the business model that US state AGs and plaintiffs' firms are now targeting.

**What would move this to Negative:** (a) a state AG enforcement action or injunction against CLEAR/PeopleMap data sourcing; (b) a confirmed material breach of court case-management data; (c) a Reuters editorial-independence rupture (Trust Principles challenge or high-profile resignation) that damages the news brand's pricing; (d) EU AI Act Annex III high-risk classification forcing product remediation in the government/courts channel.

**What would move it to Positive:** credible ESRS-grade disclosure, retirement of offsets in favour of real abatement with an AI-compute Scope 3 target, and a human-rights/data-use review with board-level accountability for the law-enforcement portfolio.

**Bottom line for the memo:** own TRI for AI-driven Legal/Tax compounding and the content moat — *not* for ESG. The ESG work here is a monitoring checklist, not a valuation input.
