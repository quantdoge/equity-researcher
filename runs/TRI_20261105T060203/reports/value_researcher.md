### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: WIDE (with a genuine durability question)

**What it is:** ~$8.0B of 2026E revenue, **88% recurring**, split across Legal Professionals (Westlaw/KeyCite — the crown jewel), Tax & Accounting (Checkpoint, UltraTax), Corporates (compliance/regulatory), and Reuters News. Global Print has been divested, completing a decade-long pivot from a conglomerate to a focused, subscription-based information utility.

**Evidence the moat is real:**
- **Intangible assets + switching costs:** Westlaw's value rests on a curated case-law/citator graph (KeyCite) that is a professional-liability requirement in legal practice, embedded in daily workflow. Rip-and-replace risk at a law firm is high (retraining, precedent-verification risk, matter-file integration).
- **ROIC ≈ 14–16% every year (2022–2025) vs. WACC ≈ 7.5–8%.** Consistent excess returns on capital is the single best moat fingerprint. Pre-goodwill returns on tangible capital are far higher.
- **Recurring revenue at 88%, retention consistently high-80s/low-90s, and contractual 3–5% escalators** — pricing power is visible.
- **Organic growth accelerated:** company guidance is **8.5–9.0% organic for 2026** (raised from ~8% at Q3), up from ~3% *total* growth in 2025 (FX/divestiture-dampened). A business being disrupted does not usually post accelerating organic growth with stable retention.

**The honest counter-argument (why this is not a clean "Wide"):** TRI sells seats and content into legal, tax, and compliance workflows. LLMs compress the value of "retrieve and summarize the law," and AI-native entrants (Harvey, Legora) plus law-firm associate-headcount reduction directly attack **seats**, the unit of pricing. Management's response — Casetext (2023), the "fiduciary-grade" Thomson AI LLM, and content-licensing deals — is credible and, so far, empirically successful. But the moat has **not yet been proven through a full AI-native competitive cycle**. I would call the moat Wide on current evidence and Narrow if growth decelerates below ~5% organic for two consecutive years with declining net revenue retention.

**Moat trend, 2021→2026: widened in quality, narrowed in certainty.** Portfolio focus improved materially; the terminal value is now more contested.

**Deductions:** (1) **Four-year FCF stagnation** — 2021 FCF $2.26B vs. 2025 $2.05B, while revenue rose 18%. Earnings growth is being reinvested, and M&A mix is diluting margins (GAAP operating margin down ~780bp since 2023, partly D&A from acquired intangibles). (2) Goodwill + intangibles are **71% of total assets**. (3) 2026 adjusted EPS tracks ~$4.24, only ~5% growth on 9% revenue growth — the incremental margin is not dropping to the bottom line. (4) Interest and tax are rising.

**Manual DCF** (base FCF $2.3B, 10 years, 8% WACC, 3% terminal):
- 5% growth → **$124/sh**; 6% → **$135/sh**; 7% → **$145/sh**
- **Intrinsic value ≈ $135 (range $124–145).**
- **Bear case** (2% growth, 2% terminal, 8.5% WACC) → **≈$78/sh** — precisely where the stock bottomed in April 2026. The market has already fully priced the bear case once this year.
- **Bull case** (8%+ growth held) → **≈$160/sh.**

**What the price implies:** at $117, the market is discounting roughly **4–5% perpetual FCF growth**. That is a modestly pessimistic assumption for a business with 88% recurring revenue, ~9% organic growth, and a 0.5x-levered balance sheet — but it is not an absurd assumption given the AI question. Probability-weighted (25% bear / 50% base / 25% bull) → **~$127**, i.e. the stock is ~8% below fair value. **That is not a margin of safety.**

Context: the stock is **−40% from its $196 July-2025 peak**, −9% YTD, and +44% off the April 2026 low of $78. The multiple, not the business, has de-rated. Analyst targets are unusually dispersed ($85 Wells Fargo to $135 Scotiabank; Jefferies initiated Buy with $130 on Oct 20), which itself signals terminal-value uncertainty rather than an information edge.

### 4. Management Quality — **7.5 / 10**

- **Steve Hasker (CEO since 2020):** disciplined focuser. Sold the Refinitiv/LSEG stake and Global Print, redeployed into Legal/Tax/Corporates and AI. The **Casetext purchase ($650M, 2023)** looks prescient — an in-house AI team bought before the panic.
- **Woodbridge / Thomson family controls ~65–70%.** This is owner-operator capitalism in the best sense (multi-decade horizon, ignores quarterly noise) but also means minority holders have little voice and the float is thin. High insider alignment; low governance recourse.
- **Capital allocation:** dividend raised 10% in Feb 2026 (11 consecutive years of growth), ~$1B/yr buybacks at sensible prices (heavier in 2023 at higher prices — a modest misstep), bolt-on M&A (Pagero, SafeSend, Materia, Casetext). Discipline is good, but **serial M&A has diluted margins and built a large goodwill balance** — the returns on those deals are not yet proven.
- No red flags on accounting: FCF exceeds net income, working capital is clean, no aggressive capitalization beyond normal software development.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (accumulate below ~$95–100)

This is a high-quality compounder with a real moat, fortress balance sheet, improving organic growth and an owner-operator anchor shareholder. It is **not** a bargain at 27.6x adjusted earnings, 22x FCF and ~15.6x EV/EBITDA when adjusted EPS grows ~5% and FCF has been flat for four years.

Applying my own discipline: intrinsic value ≈ $135, so a 25% margin of safety requires entry at **≈$101** (a 30% discount → ~$95). At $117 I am paying ~87% of intrinsic value for a business whose terminal value is genuinely contested. A fairly-priced wonderful business is not a buy.

**What would move me to Buy:** (a) price < $100, or (b) two more quarters of 8%+ organic growth with stable/expanding adjusted EBITDA margin and net revenue retention holding — which would validate the moat through the AI cycle and justify a higher intrinsic value.

**What would make me Sell/Avoid:** organic growth < 5%, disclosed retention deterioration, or seat-count/ARR pressure in Legal Professionals.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI disintermediation (primary, existential-ish):** if law firms substitute AI assistants for associate research hours and seats, TRI's pricing unit shrinks. Two consecutive quarters of decelerating organic growth would be decisive evidence.
2. **Seat-based pricing model under pressure** — law-firm headcount per matter is falling; content/usage-based repricing must offset it.
3. **Margin math:** AI compute costs plus lower-margin acquired revenue can keep adjusted EPS growth near 5% even with 9% revenue growth — a "growth without operating leverage" outcome that caps the multiple at ~20–25x.
4. **Terminal-value sensitivity:** ~65% of my DCF value is terminal value; a 1pt change in perpetual growth moves fair value ~$10–12/share. Small assumption errors are amplified.
5. **Concentration/governance:** Woodbridge control limits shareholder influence and float; index/ESG-driven flows can distort the price in both directions.
6. **Reuters News** is structurally low-margin and carries litigation/reputational tail risk for little economic value.
7. **Currency (CAD/USD) and M&A integration risk** on the goodwill-heavy balance sheet.

---
**Bottom line:** A wonderful business at a fair-to-slightly-cheap price with an unproven long-term moat. Patient investors
