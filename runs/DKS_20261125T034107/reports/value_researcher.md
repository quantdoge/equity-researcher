# DICK'S Sporting Goods (DKS) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $153.17 | Market cap ≈ $13.4B (88.9M shares) | Net financial debt ≈ $1.0–1.5B | EV ≈ $14.5B**
*Analysis date: late November 2026. Fiscal year ends late January.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW (and narrowing)**

**What it is.** DKS is the last remaining national big-box sporting goods retailer in the US, operating ~850 Dick's banners plus Golf Galaxy, Public Lands, Going Going Gone!, House of Sport/Fieldhouse experiential formats, and the GameChanger youth-sports app. Since the Sept-2025 close of the ~$2.4B Foot Locker acquisition (at $24/share, an 86% premium), it also owns Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker, Champs Sports, WSS and atmos — roughly 2,400 additional mall-based doors.

**Sources of advantage:**
- *Scale/vendor leverage*: As the largest remaining specialty athletic goods buyer in the US, DKS gets allocation priority on scarce Nike/Adidas/On/Hoka product. This is real and has widened as Sports Authority, Modell's, Bob's and Hibbett consolidated away.
- *Intangibles*: The Dick's brand plus exclusive/private-label tiers (DSG, VRST, CALIA) support gross margin; the House of Sport concept is genuinely differentiated retail.
- *Network effect (small but real)*: GameChanger's youth-sports scheduling/streaming platform creates a sticky, data-rich funnel into team-sports equipment — a legitimately underappreciated asset.
- *Efficient scale*: Big-box sporting goods is a business most competitors cannot profitably enter at national scale.

**Why the moat is narrow, not wide.** Athletic retail is a discretionary, low-margin, price-transparent category with Amazon and brand DTC as permanent threats. The moat has **narrowed** measurably over five years:

| Metric | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Gross margin | 38.33% | 34.64% | 34.92% | 35.90% | 32.92% |
| Operating margin | 16.55% | 11.83% | 9.88% | 10.96% | 7.72% |
| ROIC | — | — | — | 19.18% | **10.0%** |
| ROE | — | — | — | 36.44% | 15.33% |

Gross margin is down 540bps and operating margin down 880bps from FY2022's stimulus-era peak. The **ROIC collapse from 19.2% to 10.0%** is the single most important fact in this file: DKS now earns roughly its cost of capital (~9%) and no more. That is the opposite of the "ROIC > WACC consistently" hallmark of a moated business. The proximate cause is structural — Foot Locker's lower gross margin, mall-based traffic decline, and excess inventory — but the trend was already negative before the deal.

**Conclusion:** A good, well-run operator with a real but narrow moat, now operating in a structurally challenged channel it just bought into at a premium. This is not a "wonderful company at a fair price." It is a fair company at a fair price.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **5.5 / 10**

**Positives**
- *Revenue growth*: $12.37B (FY2022) → $22.15B (FY2026E), 12.4% CAGR — but the last leg is acquisition, not organic. FY2026 organic core comps have been ~+2–6%, which is genuinely good.
- *Balance sheet*: Financial debt is modest. Total debt of $7.75B at FY2025 year-end includes ~$5.84B of operating-lease liabilities; long-term debt is only $1.91B against $1.35B cash → **net financial debt ~$0.55B**, ~0.25x EBITDA. Lease-adjusted leverage is ~3.8x, but rent is already in SG&A, so the financial leverage is not the problem.
- *Dividend discipline*: 13 consecutive years of increases; quarterly dividend from $0.4375 (2021) to $1.25 today, +186% in five years. 3.24% yield.
- *Inventory*: $4.91B, up 46% YoY — a flag, but much of it is Foot Locker's reset inventory. Watch Q4 markdowns.

**Negatives**
- *Earnings quality*: Net income fell from $1.52B (FY2021) to $849M (FY2025); diluted EPS from $14.05 to $9.97. Revenue +28% while net income −27% is the definition of a dilutive acquisition.
- *Cash conversion is poor*: FCF of $1.31B → $558M → $940M → $509M → **$400M**. FCF/net income in FY2025 was **47%** — well below the 80% Buffett threshold. Capex ballooned from $308M to $1.14B while D&A rose only to $489M.
- *Owner earnings collapsed*: $1.045B (FY2023) → $853M → $763M → **$201M** (FY2025). Owner-earnings yield on today's market cap is ~1.5%. This is a growth-capex distortion rather than a permanent impairment, but it is real cash being spent.
- *Returning more than it earns*: FY2025 dividends ($414M) + buybacks ($347M) = $761M against $400M of FCF — a ~$360M shortfall funded with debt. Payout is ~89% of FCF. That is not sustainable if FCF stays at $400M.
- *Goodwill*: $1.63B of goodwill and intangibles, 29% of equity. Tangible book is ~$3.91B → P/TBV ~3.4x. An FL impairment is a live possibility.

**Score rationale:** A 5.5 reflects a fundamentally sound balance sheet and a real franchise, offset by deteriorating returns on capital, weak cash conversion, and shareholder returns running ahead of free cash flow. Not a 7+ until ROIC is back above 12% and FCF conversion above 80%.

*Note: the reported sector-median P/E of 24.9x is not a meaningful comparison — the "Consumer Cyclical" peer set includes AMZN and TSLA. Academy Sports is the correct yardstick, and DKS trades at a **premium** to ASO on P/E (15.4x vs 13.0x) despite lower returns on capital and heavier integration risk. That is a warning, not a bargain signal.*

**Normalized earnings power (my build, not the tool's)**

FY2026E revenue ≈ $22.15B (Q1 $5.16B + Q2 $5.59B + Q3 $5.13B + Q4E ~$6.27B). Guidance implies operating income of $1.45–1.55B (~6.9% margin) and EPS of $11.00–12.00. Q1–Q3 operating income summed to $1.20B; Q4 needs only $250–350M, i.e. a 4.0–5.6% margin — a low bar versus Q4 FY2025's 3.45%.

- Normalized EBIT: ~$1.50B → NOPAT ~$1.14B at a 24% tax rate
- Add D&A ~$640M; subtract maintenance capex ~$750M (D&A plus a remodel allowance; total capex is running ~$1.0–1.1B, with ~$350M growth capex for House of Sport and FL remodels)
- **Normalized FCF ≈ $870M**, i.e. a ~6.5% owner-earnings yield on $13.4B

**DCF scenarios** (10-year explicit, WACC 9%, terminal 2.5%; net financial debt $1.2B deducted)

| Scenario | FCF base | Growth | Equity value | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear — FL stays broken, margins stuck at 6.5% | $700M | 2.0% | ~$10.2B | **~$115** |
| Base — guidance achieved, modest synergy | $870M | 4.0% | ~$13.9B | **~$160** |
| Bull — FL fixed, margin recovers to 9%, $100M+ synergies | $1.35B | 5.0% | ~$19.5B | **~$225** |

**Intrinsic value: ~$160/share base case (range $115–$225). Current price of $153.17 is a ~4% discount to base — well short of the 20–30% margin of safety I require.**

**What growth is implied by the price?** Reverse-DCF at $153.17 with normalized FCF of $870M and 9% WACC implies roughly **4% perpetual FCF growth**. That is not a demanding assumption — it is also not a conservative one, given Foot Locker comps of −3.6% and a 19% guidance cut in a single quarter. The market is paying a fair price for a genuinely uncertain integration outcome. Note the sell-side dispersion confirms this: targets range from $99 (BNP Paribas) to $205 (D.A. Davidson), with Wolfe initiating Outperform at $181 and BMO initiating Underperform at $110 — a 2x spread on the same facts.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Mixed / B-minus**

**The good:**
- Founder-family culture. Executive Chairman Ed Stack is a ~10% owner (per Form 4 disclosures); CEO Lauren Hobart owns ~0.5–1%. Insider alignment is meaningful and there has been net insider buying (~$5.6M recently) — modest but directionally positive.
- Operating execution in the core Dick's banner has been strong: comps up ~6% in Q1, House of Sport and Fieldhouse stores performing, digital and GameChanger building.
- Dividend raised 14% in 2026; long record of increases. Management returned ~$760M to shareholders in FY2025.
- Candor: Stack publicly called Foot Locker's problems "straying from retail 101" and moved quickly to clear inventory and close stores. That is the right diagnosis, stated plainly.

**The bad:**
- **Capital allocation.** Paying an 86% premium ($24/share) for a structurally declining, mall-based, lower-margin retailer is the classic empire-building error. It converted a 19% ROIC business into a 10% ROIC business and added $1.6B of goodwill. Buffett's test — does the deal widen the moat or just add revenue? — fails here.
- **Guidance credibility.** Cutting full-year operating income from $1.68–1.81B to $1.45–1.55B and EPS from $13.50–14.50 to $11.00–12.00 within one quarter is a ~19% downward revision. The market's 31% one-day repricing was rational. Management has lost the benefit of the doubt for at least two or three quarters.
- **Funding buybacks and dividends with debt** while FCF was $400M is a mild capital-allocation discipline failure.
