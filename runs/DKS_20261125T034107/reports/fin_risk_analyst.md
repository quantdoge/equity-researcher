## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: INVESTMENT GRADE — low BBB (Moody's Baa2 stable; S&P 'BBB' issue-level; S&P 'A-2' commercial paper).** Deteriorating *trend* within the IG band.

The IG rating is deserved on coverage but is being carried by a still-modest *financial* debt load, not by earnings quality. Decomposing total debt is essential here:

| **Total debt (as reported)** | **8,969.8** |
| Less cash | (464.4) |
| **Net debt (lease-inclusive)** | **8,505.4** |
| **Net *financial* debt (ex-lease)** | **3,419.1** |

Lease-inclusive net debt/EBITDA is **4.15x**; net *financial* debt/EBITDA is only **1.67x**. Most screen-based leverage reads on DKS are inflated ~2.5x by lease accounting. That said, the ex-lease figure has deteriorated sharply — DKS was in a **net cash position** (−$0.2B) as recently as FY2024.

**Interest coverage is not the problem:** TTM EBIT $1,358.6M / TTM interest $77.2M = **17.6x** (FY2025: 20.7x). Even at a 50% EBIT decline, coverage stays above 8x. The risk is *leverage and liquidity*, not solvency from the income statement.

**Altman Z-Score: 2.22 (Grey zone)** on the tool's book-equity basis. Rebasing x4 to market cap ($13.6B / $13.74B liabilities = 0.99) lifts Z to **~2.7 — still grey, not safe**, and below the pre-acquisition profile. On the latest quarter's balance sheet I compute Z ≈ **2.34 book / 2.72 market-adjusted**. Momentum is adverse.

**Piotroski F-Score: 5/9 (Moderate).** Failed factors are diagnostic: no ROA improvement, no gross-margin improvement, no asset-turnover improvement, and share dilution. DKS is passing on cash-flow and leverage mechanics while failing on every operating-efficiency test.

**Liquidity is the tightest link.** Cash has fallen 73% to **$464M** while short-term debt re-emerged at **$988M** — **negative net short-term liquidity of −$524M**. Quick ratio of **0.26** means 82% of current assets is inventory, the least liquid and most price-fragile asset in a downturn. Inventory is up **+25% YoY** on only +23% revenue growth, in a tariff-inflated cost environment. Mitigants: positive operating cash flow, a revolving facility (with LC sublimit), and the newly assigned A-2 commercial paper program for liquidity flexibility — but CP access is rating-contingent and would evaporate in a downgrade.

**FCF sustainability is questionable.** Capex has climbed from 2.9% of revenue (FY22) to **6.6%** (FY25, $1,137M) on House of Sport rollout and FL integration. FY2025 FCF of $400M was **less than half** the $761M returned via dividends ($414M) + buybacks ($347M) — a **190% payout of FCF**, funded off the balance sheet. That is no longer available as a buffer.
