## DKS — Dick's Sporting Goods
### Growth Investment Analysis | Price $153.17 · Mkt Cap $13.6B · EV ~$19.4B

---

## 1. Growth Story

DKS has re-made itself from a $12B mall sporting goods chain into a **$22B omni-channel platform** through the ~$2.4B acquisition of Foot Locker (closed Sept 2025) plus two genuine innovation assets: **DICK'S House of Sport** (experiential, 75–100 stores targeted by 2027) and **GameChanger** (~$150M revenue, high-margin youth-sports streaming/scoring platform with real network effects, ~9M games annually). The growth math, however, has shifted: the +28% top-line prints are **inorganic annualization**, consensus models only **3–4% organic growth** from FY27 onward, and gross margin has **contracted ~300bps to 32.9%** as lower-margin, promotional footwear retail is absorbed. This is a self-help/turnaround with a digital option, not a disruptive-growth compounder.

The tape tells the story: the stock fell from ~$225 (June 2026) to ~$124 after an **Aug 25 Q2 miss and guidance cut — a −30.7% single-day drop, the worst since IPO** — then rallied +18% on Nov 24 when Q3 adj. EPS of $1.94 crushed the $1.41 consensus and guidance was raised.

## 3. TAM and Penetration

- **U.S. sporting goods TAM ~$140–160B**, growing only **~3–4%** — a mature, fragmented market. DKS + Foot Locker at $22B holds **~14–15% share with no path to dominant penetration**; the ceiling is share-shift, not category creation.
- **Experiential retail sub-TAM**: House of Sport units are the highest-productivity boxes in the fleet; 16 opened in FY25, ~14 more in 2026, targeting 75–100 by 2027 — a **real 3–5 year unit-growth runway** that supports low-single-digit comp uplift.
- **Youth sports digital (GameChanger)** is the only venture-scale TAM here: youth sports is a ~$30B+ ecosystem with almost no digital infrastructure. GameChanger at ~$150M revenue = **0.7% of consolidated sales** — too small to move the multiple today, but it's the highest-margin, most defensible asset DKS owns, and is now embedded in comps.
- **Foot Locker is a negative-TAM asset**: mall-based athletic footwear is structurally challenged; management guides FY26 FL comps **−2% to 0%** and a **$40–80M operating loss**. Q3 pro-forma FL comp **+0.5%** (vs. −4.7% LY) and U.S. Foot Locker banner **+4%** is the first genuine sign the bleed is stabilizing.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4 FY26 report (~March 11, 2027)** — first quarter with FL in comps for the full period; consensus EPS **$3.53** on $6.27B. Holiday read-through + FY27 initial guide is the single biggest re-rating event.
- **Foot Locker loss inflection** — FY26 guided to a $40–80M op loss; a path to breakeven by mid-FY27 would remove the single largest bear argument (BMO's $110, BNP's $99 targets).
- **House of Sport openings (FY27: ~14)** — each new box comps at a step-change vs. legacy; watch the 2027 "75–100 stores" milestone credibility.
- **GameChanger monetization** — subscription price increases, competitive-soccer vertical expansion; disclosure of standalone revenue/margins would force a sum-of-the-parts re-rating.
- **Buyback cadence** — 6.9% of shares retired in one quarter at ~$135; continued repurchase at 12–13x EPS mechanically compounds EPS growth.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

**Not a Strong Buy under a disruptive-innovation lens; a legitimate HOLD (GARP/turnaround) with a favorable risk/reward.**

The bear case is arithmetic: organic growth of 3–4%, gross margin down 530bps in four years, Rule of 40 at 30, FCF margin 2.3%, and $6.4B of new debt against a structurally declining Foot Locker. The innovation moat — GameChanger, House of Sport — is real but only ~0.7–1% of revenue, too small to change the growth algorithm by 2027.

The bull case is also arithmetic: at **12.9x FY26E EPS ($11.87 mid) and 11.8x FY27E ($12.93)** — a discount to Academy Sports (13.0x P/E, 10.0x EV/EBITDA) despite growing while ASO shrinks −2.8% — with **~12.8% consensus EPS CAGR to $19.20 by FY30**, a **3.24% dividend**, and a management retiring 7% of the float per quarter. That's a plausible 12–15% annualized return with a re-rating kicker. It is not a 5x-in-5-years story.

**Upgrade to Buy** if: FL returns to operating profit by mid-FY27, consolidated comps sustain >3% with gross margin stabilizing above 33%, or management quantifies GameChanger separately.
**Downgrade to Sell** if: FY27 guide implies further gross-margin erosion, FL losses exceed $80M, or net leverage pushes past ~3.5x EBITDA.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Foot Locker integration/structural risk (highest).** A promotional, brand-driven footwear market drove a Q2 operating loss and the FY26 guidance cut. Mall-based footwear traffic may be in permanent decline regardless of execution — DKS paid ~$2.4B for an asset now generating negative operating income.
2. **Gross-margin mix trap.** Consolidated GM fell to 32.9% from 35.9%; the FL mix drag is structural, not cyclical, and limits operating leverage even if revenue grows.
3. **Consumer/tariff exposure.** Discretionary hardlines and apparel are tariff-sensitive and trade-down vulnerable; a weak holiday would hit the March 2027 guide.
4. **Leverage and capital allocation.** ~$6.4B net debt plus heavy capex ($1.14B) and a large buyback leaves little cushion; funding the dividend and repurchases with new 2036/2056 notes at 13x earnings is accretive but reduces flexibility.
5. **Estimate dispersion / low conviction.** Price targets span $99–$205 — one of the widest spreads in consumer retail. Only 4 analysts model FY30; the far-forward "upgrade" signal rests on a thin sample, and the near-term FY27 consensus looks stale relative to the Q3 beat and raised guide.
6. **Competitive threats.** Academy, Bass Pro, and Amazon in hardlines; Nike's DTC push and on/off-price channels in footwear; Dick's remains dependent on third-party brands (Nike, adidas, HOKA, On) rather than proprietary product.

**Data gaps:** net debt bridge derived from EV less market cap (deal-related debt not line-item verified); FY27 consensus EBITDA ($1.99B) appears stale vs. FY26 guidance (~$2.2B), so I used ~8.9x EV/EBITDA on FY26 guidance rather than the consensus figure.
