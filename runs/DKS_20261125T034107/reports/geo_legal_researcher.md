# DICK'S Sporting Goods (DKS) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

DKS was a near-pure US retailer until the **September 8, 2025 acquisition of Foot Locker (~$2.5B)**, which created two reporting segments (DICK'S, Foot Locker) and imported meaningful international exposure. Foot Locker's last standalone fiscal year (ended Feb 1, 2025) generated **$7.99B** revenue with the following split: US $5.33B (66.7%), Canada ~$0.44B (5.5%), EMEA $1.74B (21.8%), Asia Pacific $0.465B (5.8%); Foot Locker operated ~1,469 stores across 25 countries (677 US, 84 Canada, 601 Europe) ([stockanalysis/S&P Global](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/fl/revenue/), [FL Annual Report](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/850209/000143774925011673/floc_ars.pdf)).

Consolidated Q3 FY2026 (ended Oct 31, 2026) net sales were **$5,133M**; 39-week YTD **$15,885M**, implying FY2026 revenue of roughly **$21.5–22B** ([Q3 FY26 release, Nov 24 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/dicks-sporting-goods-inc-reports-third-quarter-results-302948936.html)).

| Geography | Est. % of consolidated revenue | Basis | Geopolitical risk | Key exposures |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | **~85%** | DICK'S segment ~$13.5B (100% US) + FL US ~$5.0B | **Low** (stable institutions) / **Medium regulatory** | Tariff policy, state AGs, firearms patchwork, CFPB, CPSC |
| **EMEA (Europe, ~20 countries)** | **~7–8%** | FL Europe ~$1.6–1.7B | **Medium** | Weak EU consumer, EUR/GBP translation, CSRD/CSDDD/ESPR compliance, works councils, Middle East conflict spillover |
| **Canada** | **~2%** | FL Canada ~$0.4B | **Low** | USMCA 2026 review, CAD FX |
| **Asia Pacific** | **~2%** | FL APAC ~$0.44B | **Medium** | Australia/Japan/NZ consumer, sourcing-country nexus (China, Vietnam) |
| **Latin America / franchise-licensing** | **<1%** | FL licensing ~$17M | **Medium** | Distributor/partner risk |
| **Sourcing countries (China, Vietnam, Indonesia, Cambodia, India)** | ~100% of COGS, no revenue | Vendor/agent relationships | **Medium-High** | Tariffs, UFLPA forced labour, shipping disruption |

**Key structural read:** ~88% of revenue is US; there is **no Russia, China, or sanctioned-market revenue exposure**, so classic sanctions/confiscation/currency-control risk is negligible. The real geographic vector is **cost-side — the Asian import supply chain** — not revenue-side.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US tariff policy whiplash and reversal of the IEEPA windfall (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
The trade regime has been unusually unstable. The **Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs on February 20, 2026** (*Learning Resources, Inc. v. Trump*, No. 24-1287) ([SCOTUS opinion](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-1287_4gcj.pdf)). The administration's 10% **Section 122 replacement was held unlawful by the Court of International Trade on May 7, 2026** and **expired July 24, 2026** ([Sheppard Mullin](https://www.sheppard.com/insights/blogs/ieepa-tariffs-struck-down-replacement-tariffs-expired-what-importers-need-to-know-now), [ABA](https://www.americanbar.org/groups/public_contract_law/resources/procurement-lawyer/2026-fall/ieepa-tariffs-struck-down-replacement-tariffs-expired/)). Section 301 tariffs on China remain live.
- DKS is a material importer and a direct beneficiary: it recovered **$61.1M in IEEPA refunds**, comprising **$59.0M received in the 13 weeks ended Aug 1, 2026 (booked as a reduction to COGS) plus $2.1M interest** ([ProPublica tariff-refund tracker, data as of Oct 7 2026](https://projects.propublica.org/trump-tariff-refund-ieepa/companies/1089063-dick-s-sporting-goods-inc/)).
- **Investment implication:** this is a *non-recurring, low-quality* margin benefit. Q3 FY26 non-GAAP operating margin still fell 95bps to 4.9% *despite* the refund tailwind — underlying merchandise margin pressure is worse than headline. Do not capitalise the refund.
- Residual risk: new tariffs pursued under alternative authorities (expanded Section 232/301), plus refund-claim audit risk.

**Risk 2 — Foot Locker's EMEA/APAC turnaround under a weakening European consumer (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium)**
Roughly 10% of consolidated revenue sits in Europe/APAC, precisely where Foot Locker is weakest. Management is already retrenching: FY2026 closures include **44 WSS stores, 16 Foot Locker Europe stores, 2 Asia Pacific stores, 2 Atmos stores** ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91596030/foot-locker-closing-stores-2026-see-locations-where-dicks-shuttering)), later partially walked back as pilots succeeded ([Retail Dive](https://www.retaildive.com/news/dicks-pulls-back-foot-locker-store-closures-pilot-success/814655/)). The international banners are the drag: US Foot Locker comped **+4%** in Q3 FY26 while total Foot Locker comp was only **+0.5%** — implying negative EMEA/APAC comps ([Q3 FY26 release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/dicks-sporting-goods-inc-reports-third-quarter-results-302948936.html)). Store closures in 20+ European jurisdictions trigger **works-council consultation, TUPE-style transfer obligations, and lease-exit costs** — a slow, litigious process.

**Risk 3 — Asian sourcing concentration and forced-labour/tariff enforcement (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium)**
Footwear and apparel are overwhelmingly sourced from **China and Vietnam**, with Vietnam alone carrying a **$134B US trade surplus in sporting goods in 2025** ([SGI Europe](https://www.sgieurope.com/deep-dive-vietnams-134bn-us-surplus-driven-by-sports/118830.article)). DKS does not control its vendors' sourcing — brand partners (Nike, Hoka, adidas) bear the primary exposure, but DKS absorbs it through COGS and availability. Secondary risk: **UFLPA forced-labour detentions** and EU **CSDDD** supply-chain due-diligence liability.

*(Honourable mention — Political/ESG polarisation: DKS's post-Parkland firearms restrictions and its DEI posture make it a target from both directions. See §4.)*

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Securities class action (material, active).** A consolidated putative class action is pending against DKS and three executives — **Edward Stack (Exec. Chairman), Lauren Hobart (CEO), Navdeep Gupta (CFO)**. Class period **Sept 8, 2025 – Aug 24, 2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline **Nov 3, 2026** (now passed); lead plaintiff is the **Plumbers & Pipefitters Local Union #295 Pension Fund**. The complaint alleges DKS misrepresented that Foot Locker's inventory/promotional problems were fixed when Foot Locker remained dependent on unproductive legacy footwear. On **Aug 25, 2026** DKS fell **$55.02 (~30%) to $124.31** on the Q2 FY26 miss and guidance cut ([Levi & Korsinsky](https://zlk.com/cases/dick-s-sporting-goods-inc-class-action-lawsuit-dks); other firms soliciting: [Kaplan Fox](https://www.kaplanfox.com/case/dicks-sporting-goods-inc-class-action-lawsuit-learn-more-now/), [Rosen](https://rosenlegal.com/cases/dicks-sporting-goods), [BFA](https://www.bfalaw.com/cases/dick-sporting-class-action-lawsuit)). No accrual has been publicly quantified; expect 2–4 years to resolution and a probable settlement in the tens of millions. **This is the single largest identifiable legal contingency.**

**State AG enforcement — firearms.** New Jersey AG (Platkin) and the Division of Consumer Affairs reached a **settlement resolving allegations DKS unlawfully sold large-capacity magazines into New Jersey**, in violation of the Consumer Fraud Act and Hazardous Products regulations ([NJ OAG](https://www.njoag.gov/ag-platkin-division-of-consumer-affairs-reach-settlement-with-dicks-sporting-goods-resolving-allegations-that-retail-chain-unlawfully-sold-large-capacity-magazines-into-new-jersey/)). Low dollar cost, but establishes a template for other states and signals ongoing state-level compliance scrutiny of DKS's hunt/firearms category.

**Employment/DEI charge.** **America First Legal filed a federal civil-rights complaint** requesting an EEOC investigation of DKS for alleged Title VII / PWFA violations via "illegal employment quotas" ([AFL](https://aflegal.org/press-release/afl-files-federal-civil-rights-complaint-against-dicks-sporting-goods-for-discriminating-against-moms-using-illegal-employment-quotas-2/)). Politically motivated and low probability of material outcome, but part of a broader anti-DEI enforcement wave against retailers.

**Merger review — cleared.** The **FTC cleared the Foot Locker acquisition without a second request** ([Econic Partners](https://econicpartners.com/newsroom/cases/ftc-clears-2-5-billion-dicks-sporting-goods-foot-locker-merger-without-issuing-a-second-request/)). No live antitrust overhang.

**Customs/tariff litigation.** A nationwide class of all importers of record who paid IEEPA duties was **certified October 8, 2026** (*Freestyle World*) ([Lieff Cabraser](https://www.lieffcabraser.com/tariffs/)). DKS has recovered via the CBP administrative process rather than litigation, which is the lower-risk path.

**Legacy labour citations.** Violation Tracker records a history of NLRB/OSHA/wage-type violations across the parent ([Violation Tracker](https://violationtracker.goodjobsfirst.org/parent/dicks-sporting-goods)) — individually immaterial, indicative of a large, union-sensitive hourly workforce (105,200 employees).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Customs & trade compliance (moderate-high).** A $59M refund claim requires accurate duty valuation and importer-of-record documentation. Misdeclaration creates False Claims Act and 19 U.S.C. penalty exposure. Also first-sale/transfer-pricing scrutiny on Asian-sourced goods.
2. **Youth data privacy — GameChanger (underappreciated).** GameChanger (FY26 revenue approaching **$200M**) is a youth-sports app collecting data on **minors**. This implicates **COPPA** and state child-privacy/AADC statutes. A youth-data enforcement action would be reputationally severe and is not a risk most DKS models capture.
3. **Firearms/ammunition compliance patchwork.** State-by-state magazine, ammunition, and background-check rules; the NJ settlement is a precedent. Ongoing CPSC product-recall exposure across hardlines.
4. **Supply-chain transparency.** UFLPA (China/Xinjiang) and EU CSDDD due-diligence obligations for the Foot Locker Europe business.
5. **Disclosure controls over the Foot Locker integration.** The securities class action is, in substance, an allegation of failed integration disclosure — a governance flag on the largest transaction in company history.
6. **Consumer credit.** DKS co-brands a consumer credit card; retail-credit marketing and "buy now, pay later" practices remain a CFPB/state focus.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** The revenue base is ~88% US with **zero exposure to Russia, China, or sanctioned jurisdictions** — no sanctions, export-control, expropriation, or currency-repatriation exposure of consequence. That anchors the rating at Medium rather than High. Offsetting items: (a) the **Foot Locker securities class action** is a live, name-the-executives claim with a documented 30% share-price drop; (b) the tariff regime remains **judicially and politically unstable**, and DKS's current margin is being flattered by a **non-recurring $61M refund**; (c) the newly acquired **EMEA/APAC footprint** adds European consumer, FX, employment-consultation, and CSDDD exposure where the business is already underperforming; and (d) DKS is a **politically salient brand** (firearms and DEI), inviting state AG and advocacy-driven enforcement.

**Analyst note for the memo:** the market is pricing DKS as a US turnaround story. The legal/geopolitical risks that matter are *not* the ones that usually dominate this framework (sanctions, nationalisation) but rather **US trade-policy instability, disclosure litigation risk on Foot Locker, and non-US retail execution** — three items that are correlated with each other through the Foot Locker integration.
