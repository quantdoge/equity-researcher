### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW–MEDIUM**

- **Carbon profile is structurally light.** No manufacturing; footprint is stores, distribution centres and purchased goods. Scope 1+2 are small relative to ~$20bn+ revenue; **Scope 3 (merchandise) is >90% of footprint** and largely outside direct control.
- **Targets are modest and behind where peers have moved:** ~**30% Scope 1&2 reduction by 2030 vs. a 2016 baseline**. Third-party tracking shows Scope 1 emissions actually *rose ~8.6%* in one recent year — target progress is not linear.
- **Stranded-asset risk: negligible.** No carbon-intensive fixed assets, no fossil reserves, no Scope 1-heavy plants.
- **Physical climate risk: moderate but diffuse** — ~850+ DICK'S banners plus ~2,300 Foot Locker-family doors, with heavy concentration in hurricane-exposed Southeast/Gulf and hail-prone Midwest. Risk is episodic store closures and insurance cost inflation rather than capex impairment.
- **Chemical content is the real environmental exposure**, not carbon: PFAS/"forever chemicals" in own-brand apparel and hardgoods. DKS has committed to **banning PFAS in own-brand clothing** and settled a California PFOA matter in bicycle saddle bags ($9,200 — de minimis) agreeing to cease added fluorinated compounds. This is a live, expanding regulatory front (see §6).

**Verdict:** Not a valuation driver on its own, but a governance-of-disclosure test. DKS has not published a sustainability report since ~2022 — a transparency lag that matters more than the emissions themselves.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Workforce (largest exposure).** Post-Foot Locker headcount jumped to **105,200**, overwhelmingly hourly retail. Retail's economics make labour the swing cost line, so wage inflation and scheduling regulation are directly P&L-relevant.

- **Adverse litigation record is persistent, not one-off.** Violation Tracker aggregates **~$40.5M across 11 wage-and-hour actions** and **$3.58M across 2 employment-discrimination actions**. Active FLSA/state class actions allege assistant store managers were misclassified as exempt to avoid overtime — a recurring retail pattern that invites repeat claims.
- **Safety:** OSHA citations exist but are individually trivial (e.g., one 2023 penalty of $11,252). OSHA's warehousing/distribution-centre enforcement directive (extended July 2026) raises inspection frequency for exactly DKS's DC format.

**Product safety.** Recalls have touched DKS as a retailer of record — notably **Cipton kids' bike helmets** sold at DICK'S (Apr-2024 to May-2026) over head-injury/death risk, and a Thule child bike-seat chemical-ingestion recall. As a *retailer*, liability is usually contractual/indemnified, but reputational damage attaches to the banner.

**Data privacy.** A **2024 data breach** (publicised July 2024) produced a class settlement with a claim deadline of March 2026. As e-commerce and the GameChanger youth-sports app scale, this is a growing, recurring category.

**Community / social licence — a genuine positive.** Youth-sports investment (Sports Matter, The DICK'S Foundation, GameChanger access programmes) is authentic, CEO-sponsored and core to brand. This is real social capital and partially offsets labour negatives.

**Firearms.** DKS famously restricted gun sales post-Parkland (raised purchase age to 21, ended assault-style rifle sales, later removed firearms from ~125 stores), accepting an estimated **~$250M revenue hit**. The reputational cost now cuts both ways: gun-rights boycotts vs. ESG-screening exclusions for firearms exposure. Revenue impact today is modest (<5% of sales by most estimates).

**DEI.** A **2025 anti-DEI shareholder proposal (Item 5, National Center/FEP)** requesting a report on affirmative-action and DEI risks was **rejected by shareholders** — DKS joined ~24 large US companies whose investors voted down such proposals in 2025. DKS retains ERGs and inclusion programmes. Net: reputational noise, not acute financial risk.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10** (Weak)

**The single most important ESG fact about DKS is its control structure.**

- **Controlled company.** As of March 20, 2026: **65,474,242 common shares and 23,570,633 Class B shares**. Class B carries supervoting rights (10:1), implying Class B holders command **~78% of total voting power on ~26% of the equity**. DKS is a NYSE "controlled company," exempt from majority-independent board and independent-compensation-committee requirements. The family's voting share has drifted down over time (68% in 2005 → 61% in 2019 → 58% in 2020 → 55.97% in 2021) but remains an absolute block.
- **Founder + family in operating seats.** **Edward Stack** has been Chairman since 1984 and is today Executive Chairman after serving as CEO; **Michael Stack** is President (disclosed FY compensation: $442,973 salary, $226,878 bonus, $1,250,212 equity); **William Colombo** is Vice Chairman and trustee of family trusts holding Class B shares. This is dynastic governance, not meritocratic succession.
- **Board.** There is meaningful independence on paper and a credible independent-leadership signal in **CEO Lauren Hobart** (first female CEO, appointed 2021) — but the Chairman is the controlling shareholder's patriarch, which neuters board challenge.
- **Disclosure quality is the live problem.** On **August 25, 2026**, DKS fell **$55.02 (−30.68%) to $124.31** after Q2 FY2026 results revealed **excess Foot Locker inventory, weak footwear demand and heavy discounting**, with a guidance cut. A **securities class action covers September 8, 2025 – August 24, 2026** — precisely the period from deal close to the day before the write-down became public. Whatever the merits, the *pattern* (close a transformative acquisition, then disclose inventory distress a year later) is a governance and disclosure-controls red flag, and it is the dominant ESG driver of the equity story.
- **Third-party validation of weak governance:** **S&P Global CSA score of 26/100 (ESG Score 31)** — bottom-quartile for the sector, driven heavily by governance/transparency gaps.
- **Positives:** no poison pill, annual director elections de facto, say-on-pay, shareholder proposals are permitted and voted (the anti-DEI proposal reached the ballot), and capital returns are shareholder-friendly ($900M+ buyback in Q3 FY2026 alone).

**Why 3, not lower:** real board independence, a credible non-family CEO, active shareholder-return policy. **Why not higher:** a ~78% voting bloc on 26% of the economics with the founder as Executive Chairman and his son as President is the definition of a structurally unaccountable board.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US — minimal direct carbon exposure.** No meaningful EU CBAM exposure (retail, not importer of record for covered goods at scale); SEC climate-disclosure rule remains stayed. DKS's exposure is **indirect and supply-chain based**.
- **EU CSRD — newly applicable via Foot Locker.** Foot Locker operates European banners (Foot Locker Europe, atmos). That acquisition converts a purely US domestic reporter into a group with EU-facing operations, pulling DKS toward CSRD-style Scope 1/2/3 and due-diligence expectations — expensive to build, and DKS's disclosure infrastructure (no report since ~2022) is not ready.
- **State chemical/PFAS regimes — the sharpest near-term rule.** California's textile PFAS ban (AB 1817) and parallel laws in NY, ME, MN and others target exactly DKS's own-brand apparel and hardgoods assortments, with Prop 65 warning/settlement mechanics already generating claims.
- **Supply-chain due diligence:** Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA) import detentions are the key product-availability risk for apparel/footwear sourced from China; state-level forced-labour disclosure laws add reporting burden.
- **Labour & competition:** state pay-transparency and predictive-scheduling laws across ~15+ states; FTC review of the Foot Locker deal cleared, but combined share in athletic footwear invites ongoing antitrust attention.
- **Anti-ESG political risk:** DKS is named in the National Center's anti-DEI proposal campaign and in conservative boycott lists. Governance-related reputational volatility is a two-sided, recurring cost.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD**

DKS is not an ESG *risk* case in the environmental or product sense — its operations are low-carbon, its community programmes are genuine, and its controversies are retail-typical rather than systemic. But it is a clear **laggard** on the two dimensions that actually matter for valuation: **governance accountability** (78% of votes on 26% of equity, founder as Executive Chairman, son as President, NYSE controlled-company exemptions) and **disclosure quality** (no recent sustainability report; a 30.68% single-day collapse and ensuing securities class action on acquisition-related inventory). **S&P Global's CSA score of 26/100 and ESG score of 31** corroborate the laggard classification, and that scorecard is driven by governance and transparency, not carbon.

**Direction of travel: stable-to-improving on S, deteriorating on G.** The DEI proposal's rejection shows functioning shareholder voice, and Q3 FY2026 (revenue +23.2% to $5.13bn, EPS $1.94 vs. $1.41 est., Foot Locker comps turning positive, 134 underperforming stores closed, guidance raised to $11.45–12.30) shows the operating fix is underway. But the governance architecture is unchanged and the litigation overhang persists.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (with a governance-driven discount)

**Not a negative screen for most ESG mandates** — DKS would pass environmental screens comfortably and would fail only firearms-exclusion and possibly labour-standards screens at the margin. There is no stranded-asset, carbon-transition or supply-chain-collapse thesis here.

**But ESG does not support the thesis; it argues for a haircut on multiples.** Three transmission channels:

1. **Cost of equity.** A ~78%-controlled board with a founder Chairman and family President warrants a persistent governance discount. Minority investors cannot force change on capital allocation (e.g., a $2.4bn acquisition that destroyed ~$3.6bn of value in a day), succession, or disclosure. Expect the market to keep applying a conglomerate-style discount to the Foot Locker segment.
2. **Earnings quality / litigation drag.** The securities class action's class period maps almost exactly onto the post-close year, and wage-and-hour claims (~$40.5M cumulatively) are a recurring, non-trivial opex leak at retail margins. Model modest ongoing legal/settlement expense.
3. **Disclosure catch-up capex.** CSRD exposure via Foot Locker's European banners plus escalating state PFAS/chemical regimes will require real spend on product-compliance and emissions data systems — probably low tens of millions annually, not thesis-changing, but a drag on the "self-help margin story."

**Where ESG is a genuine positive:** the youth-sports/community franchise (GameChanger, Sports Matter, House of Sport community anchoring) is authentic social capital that supports traffic, brand and landlord relationships; and the firearms exit — taken at a ~$250M revenue cost — is a rare case of a retailer trading revenue for conviction without permanent franchise damage.

**Bottom line for the memo:** treat DKS as **ESG-neutral / governance-constrained**. Do not underwrite an ESG re-rating. Any long thesis must be an operational Foot Locker turnaround + core-compound thesis, and it should carry an explicit **governance discount of roughly 50–100bp on cost of equity** plus a litigation reserve line. Watch three things: (i) whether a current sustainability/CSRD-readiness report is published; (ii) resolution direction of the securities class action; (iii) any move to collapse the dual-class structure — the single highest-value governance catalyst available, and historically the least likely.
