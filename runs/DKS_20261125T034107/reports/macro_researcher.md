# Macroeconomic Assessment — DICK'S Sporting Goods (DKS)

**As of 25 November 2026** | Price $153.17 | Market cap $13.62B | Beta 1.10 | 100% US revenue

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — "Late-Cycle Re-acceleration with a Hawkish Fed"

**Stance: Transitional, tilting risk-off for rate-sensitive consumer discretionary.**

This is not a conventional late cycle. The US economy is *re-accelerating*, not slowing: Q3 2026 real GDP came in at **+3.4% SAAR** (Q2 +2.2%, Q1 +2.5%), unemployment has fallen to **4.1%** from 4.5% a year ago, and nominal retail sales are running **+6.8% YoY**. But inflation has turned back up — **CPI 3.82% YoY in October**, and on a 3-month annualised basis running at roughly **5.7%** — and the Fed has been forced to reverse course. After cutting from 5.33% to 3.63% through mid-2026, the FOMC **hiked at its 15–16 September meeting**, its first increase in three years, taking the effective funds rate to **3.88%** in October. Futures price roughly **70% odds of another hike at the 9 December meeting**.

The critical feature for equity valuation is *where* the tightening is showing up. The **10Y yield has risen ~93bp since late June** (4.38% → 5.31% on 5 Oct, now ~4.92–5.13%) and the **30Y sits at 5.47%**. Yet **5-year breakevens have fallen to 2.11%** from 2.49% in late October. Long-run inflation expectations remain anchored; the sell-off is being driven by **real rates (~2.8%) and term premium**, not inflation expectations. That is the most hostile possible configuration for long-duration consumer equity multiples.

Critically, **real Fed funds is approximately zero** (3.88% nominal vs 3.82% CPI). Policy is barely restrictive in real terms — which is precisely why the Fed is still hiking.

The consumer is the paradox. **University of Michigan sentiment is 51.0 (Nov), versus 71.7 in January 2025** — a level historically associated with recession. Yet spending is robust. This "vibecession" divergence is the single most important macro risk to DKS: spending is currently funded by a strong labour market and positive real income growth, but a sentiment level this depressed is fragile if the labour market cracks.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

DKS is a **domestically-sourced-revenue, import-heavy-cost, moderately levered retailer**. Its macro transmission channels are therefore: (a) real disposable income → discretionary units, (b) import cost/tariffs → gross margin, (c) interest rates → interest expense and multiple, (d) USD → landed cost.

| Macro Variable | Direction | Impact on DKS | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nominal retail sales +6.8%** | ▲ | **Positive** | Q3 FY26 DKS comps +2.6%, average ticket +3.7% (transactions −1.1%). DKS is capturing price, not units — inflation is flattering the top line. |
| **Low unemployment 4.1%** | ▲ | **Positive** | Management explicitly stated spending grew across *all* income groups with "no trade-down from best to better or better to good." |
| **CPI 3.82% / sticky** | ▼ | **Negative** | Input and wage cost pressure into a promotional athletic-footwear market. Q3 gross margin held only via price increases — a narrowing runway. |
| **Fed hiking to 3.88%** | ▼ | **Negative** | Direct: interest expense. Adjusted EPS **fell 6% YoY in Q3 despite +3% adjusted operating income**, driven by a higher tax rate and **higher interest costs** after the $1B bond sale. |
| **10Y at 4.92–5.13%, 30Y 5.47%** | ▼ | **Negative** | Valuation. At the FY26 adjusted EPS guide midpoint of **$11.88**, DKS trades at **12.9x** — a 7.75% earnings yield, only **~+283bp over the 10Y**. Thin for a levered discretionary retailer. |
| **Strong USD (+4.6% since Sept)** | ▲ | **Positive (mild)** | ~100% US revenue → **no translation drag**. Conversely, a stronger dollar *lowers landed cost* of Asian-sourced footwear/apparel, partially offsetting tariffs. This is a genuine, under-appreciated tailwind. |

### The rate-sensitivity question, answered directly

DKS is **not** a classic rate-sensitive business (no REIT/bank/utility dynamics), but it is **more rate-sensitive than its beta of 1.10 implies**, for three reasons:

1. **Balance sheet:** The $1B bond issued in Q3 FY26 was raised at a time when the 10Y was 4.9–5.3%. That is materially more expensive than the debt it replaced or supplemented. Interest expense is now a visible EPS drag and a refinancing/reinvestment constraint.
2. **Valuation duration:** A 12.9x multiple is short-duration — this actually *protects* DKS relative to high-multiple consumer names. The discount rate effect is muted because the multiple is already compressed.
3. **The consumer credit channel:** 30Y at 5.47% and a hawkish Fed tighten household credit conditions. DKS's average ticket is $100–150 — big-ticket hardlines (fitness equipment, golf) are the most credit-sensitive category and would weaken first.

**The stock has already traded the macro.** DKS fell **−35% from its June peak of $236.92 to $121.15 on 18 September**, including a **−30.7% single-day collapse on 25 August** when it cut guidance citing a "dynamic macroeconomic environment," and has since rebounded **+26%** to $153.17 after the 24 November Q3 beat and guidance raise (shares +14.6% that day). Much of the rate and tariff damage is in the price.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL**

*(Neutral at current valuation; would be Unfavourable at 15x+)*

**Why not Unfavourable:** The demand side of the macro equation is genuinely strong — 3.4% GDP, 4.1% unemployment, +2.9% real retail sales, broad-based spending across income cohorts, and a World Cup catalyst. A strengthening dollar is reducing landed costs. And at **12.9x forward earnings with a 7.75% earnings yield**, DKS is priced for a macro environment considerably worse than the one currently prevailing.

**Why not Favourable:** Every *valuation* and *margin* variable is pointed the wrong way. The Fed is hiking, not cutting. Real rates near 2.8% cap multiple expansion. Tariff escalation is live and unresolved as of this week. Real Fed funds at ~0% means the tightening cycle has further to run. Consumer sentiment at 51 is a genuine fragility.

**Net:** The macro is supportive of DKS's *earnings* but hostile to its *multiple*. With the multiple already de-rated by a third, these roughly offset.

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

### Bull surprise: **The Fed pauses on 9 December and declares the hiking cycle complete** (market-implied odds of a hike: ~70%)

The non-consensus read is hiding in the breakevens. **5Y breakevens have fallen from 2.49% to 2.11% in a month** while headline CPI sits at 3.82%. The market is telling you it believes current inflation is **tariff- and supply-driven, not demand-driven** — a one-time price-level shift, not a persistent inflation process. If the FOMC concludes the same and holds at 3.75–4.00%, the entire front-end repricing inverts.

**Transmission to DKS:**
- 2Y falls from 4.74% toward 4.20%; 10Y from 4.92% to **~4.30–4.40%**; 30Y from 5.47% to ~5.0%
- DKS's ~12.9x forward multiple re-rates toward **14.5–15.0x** on the same $11.88 EPS → **$172–178**
- Add EPS upside: lower interest expense on the $1B bond plus a weaker dollar-driven… no — a weaker USD raises landed cost, so the FX offset partially reverses. Net EPS benefit is modest, perhaps **+$0.30–0.50**
- Combined: **~$175–185, or +15–20% from $153**, without any change to the operating story

**What would make this more than a trade:** If the Fed pause coincides with a negotiated resolution of the newly-announced tariff round, gross margin guidance for FY27 becomes conservative rather than optimistic. That is the combination that would force a genuine re-rating rather than a bounce.

**The mirror-image bear case (equal magnitude):** A December hike *plus* implementation of the China/EU/Mexico/Japan tariff round would push the 10Y through 5.50%, compress DKS to ~11x, and — combined with sentiment already at 51 — trigger the discretionary trade-down management currently insists is absent. That is a **$120–130** scenario, i.e. a retest of the September low.
