# CBRS — Cerebras Systems Inc.
## Non-Financial Risk Assessment

**Snapshot:** IPO 14-May-2026; stock $96.36 (2-Nov-26) vs. $386.34 intraday IPO high — **−75% from peak**, −69% from first-day close. Market cap ~$21.8B. 708 employees. Nasdaq: CBRS.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

| Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Controlled-company structure** — all Class B stock carries ~**99.2% of total voting power**, held by pre-IPO insiders. Public Class A holders control well under 1% of votes. | Negative. Public shareholders have no practical voice. Nasdaq controlled-company exemption permits a board *without* majority independence. |
| **Founder economic stake vs. voting stake mismatch** — CEO Andrew Feldman ~5.5% economic, co-founder Lie ~3%, yet insiders control ~99% of votes. | Classic super-voting alignment gap: insiders bear ~8% of economic downside but ~100% of the votes. |
| **No proxy statement on file** — first-year filer; no DEF 14A available, so board independence, comp design, and related-party committee process are **unverifiable**. | Information gap, itself a governance concern at a $22B company. |
| **Related-party density (high)** — G42 (an existing large customer) agreed to buy **22M non-voting shares**; OpenAI holds **warrants on 33.4M non-voting shares at $0.00001** plus a **$1.0B working-capital loan** to the company. | Material. Customers are simultaneously equity holders, lenders, and revenue counterparties. Arm's-length pricing is unproven. |
| **Comp structure** — $377M of Q2 stock-based comp in a single quarter, "much of it triggered by the IPO and large executive awards," inside a *$450M quarterly net loss*. | Negative. Windfall vesting at IPO decouples pay from post-IPO shareholder outcome — precisely the shareholders now down 75%. |

**Verdict:** Control is entrenched, economic alignment is thin, and related-party exposure is unusually large. This is a founder-controlled company in substance and in form.

---

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **ELEVATED AND RISING**

**No formal SEC enforcement action** found in EDGAR full-text search. However:

- **Securities-fraud investigations (multiple, unfiled):** Block & Leviton, Johnson Fistel, Levi & Korsinsky, Kaplan Fox, Bronstein Gewirtz & Grossman, and SueWallSt all opened investigations after Culper Research's 28-Oct-2026 report. All are **pre-complaint solicitations** — no class action has been filed, and these firms file routinely after any sharp decline. Treat as a *leading indicator*, not yet a legal liability.
- **Active litigation disclosed in the 10-Q:** a **copyright class action** is being defended (AI training-data exposure) — a live, unquantified liability common to foundation-model-adjacent infrastructure.
- **Export controls (two-sided):** BIS's July-2026 easing of UAE restrictions and express authorization of advanced AI semiconductor exports to **G42** is a direct enabler of the core business. This is a structural dependency on an **administratively reversible** approval channel. A policy reversal would hit the majority of revenue.
- **The allegation with real regulatory teeth** is the reported **change in assumed useful life of data-center equipment from 1.5–3 years to 3–5 years between draft S-1 and the final registration statement** — alleged to have inflated first-half gross margin. If substantiated, this is an accounting-policy question for the auditor and potentially the SEC's Division of Corporation Finance, not merely a short-seller talking point.

### 3. Insider Activity Analysis: **NET SELLING (no offsetting open-market buys identified)**

- **30+ Form 4 filings** between 20-Aug-2026 and 28-Oct-2026 — an unusually dense cadence for a company four months past IPO.
- The **staggered lockup** released tranches of up to ~14.6M shares at intervals from September through **9-Nov-2026**; unsold supply remains an overhang past the assessment date.
- Reported transactions are predominantly **Class B → Class A conversions followed by "sell-to-cover" tax-withholding sales** (e.g., CEO Feldman converted 93,497 Class B and sold 26,644 Class A, described as non-discretionary). This is a **material mitigating factor** — mechanical, plan-driven selling is far less informative than discretionary selling.
- **No insider open-market purchase was identified.** At a price down 75% from the high, the complete absence of insider buying is itself a signal.

*Data gap:* the filings feed returned metadata only (accession numbers/dates), not transaction-level share counts or prices, so a precise net-share and net-dollar calculation could not be constructed.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Extreme customer concentration — sovereign-linked.** ~**86–87% of 2025 revenue** came from two Abu Dhabi-linked entities (G42 ~24%, MBZUAI ~62%). Post-IPO the concentration has *shifted* rather than diversified, now toward **OpenAI**, which drives the bulk of **$25.4B remaining performance obligations**. Two consecutive single-counterparty dependencies in a row.
2. **Revenue quality / circular financing.** OpenAI is simultaneously the largest backlog counterparty, a **$1.0B lender** to Cerebras, and holder of near-free warrants; ~**$1.1B of customer warrants will reduce reported revenue over time**. Revenue backed by vendor financing and equity sweeteners warrants a quality discount.
3. **Technology-durability and supply-chain fragility.** Culper alleges wafer field lifespan of **1.5–2.5 years vs. the 3–5 years** disclosed, that a connector material described by the CEO as invented in-house is actually **off-the-shelf**, and that wafers are run at near-peak power to avoid thermal fracture. Independently, wafer-scale engines depend on leading-edge foundry capacity with **no second source**, and the business is entering **large multi-year data-center leases** that convert to fixed obligations if demand disappoints. Key-person dependency on Feldman is acute.

---

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH / DETERIORATING**

- A named short-seller (Culper) has published a technical report alleging **product reliability misrepresentation and an accounting-motivated depreciation change** — the most damaging category of allegation for a hardware company because it attacks the product *and* the numbers simultaneously.
- Stock at an **all-time low**, down ~75% from the IPO high, four months after a $6.2B IPO. The retail and growth-fund cohort that anchored the deal is deeply underwater, which sustains plaintiff-firm solicitations and negative press flow.
- Countervailing: genuine commercial traction — **Oracle Cloud, AWS, and Gimlet** partnerships announced in the last six weeks, revenue nearly doubling to $373.5M in H1-26, and **$8.6B of liquidity**. The franchise is not impaired; the credibility is.
- **Short interest is elevated but reported inconsistently** (8.4% of float per MarketBeat vs. 15.4% as of 30-Sep per Chartmill) — either way, a crowded short base implies continued volatility and headline sensitivity.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not *Critical*, because there is no formal enforcement action, no restatement, no auditor resignation, no covenant breach, and liquidity of $8.6B removes near-term solvency pressure. But this is a **first-year public company with 99% insider voting control, 86%+ customer concentration, a circular OpenAI relationship, alleged product-life misrepresentation, six live plaintiff investigations, and a lockup expiring this week.** The non-financial risk profile is materially worse than the average semiconductor peer and is not yet reflected in consensus treatment of the name as a straightforward AI-infrastructure growth story.

---

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🚩 Accounting integrity (highest severity):** the 1.5–3yr → 3–5yr useful-life extension between draft and final IPO filings. *Action: request depreciation sensitivity and gross-margin bridge excluding the change; ask the audit committee directly whether it was auditor-reviewed; monitor for SEC comment letters or a restatement.*
2. **🚩 86% sovereign-linked revenue concentration** plus dependence on a reversible BIS export authorization for G42/UAE.
3. **🚩 Circular related-party web:** OpenAI as customer + lender + warrant holder; G42 as customer + 22M-share buyer. *Action: demand disclosure of arm's-length terms and any side letters; scrutinize revenue recognition on prepaid/warranted capacity.*
4. **🚩 Controlled-company governance:** 99.2% insider voting power, unverifiable board independence pending first DEF 14A (expected 2027).
5. **🚩 Product-durability claim conflict:** management's 3–5 year life vs. alleged 1.5–2.5 year field life. *Action: seek field telemetry/return-rate data; this is the single variable that determines whether the depreciation schedule — and therefore gross margin — is honest.*
6. **🚩 Lockup expiry 9-Nov-2026** with ~8–15% short interest: near-term supply/volatility event independent of fundamentals.

*Gaps acknowledged: no 10-K, 10-Q, or DEF 14A retrievable through the filing-text tool (a $6.2B IPO filer should have one on file — likely a feed limitation); insider data was metadata-only; short interest figures conflicted across sources.*
