## 2. Consensus Assessment: **MEDIUM agreement, SPLIT on direction**

**Universal agreement (high confidence, all 11 agents):** SE is a genuinely high-quality operating business with an accelerating top line, a fortress net-cash balance sheet, and no fraud. Not one agent — including the short — disputes the fundamentals or alleges accounting manipulation. That is a strong, cross-validated floor.

**The split is narrow and specific:** Growth says buy the growth at 1.2x sales; Value says the growth is not yet worth the price; Short says the *credit book* will force the margin question sooner than consensus expects. Macro says the discount rate, not the business, is setting the price.

**Conflict resolution:**
1. **No fraud override applies.** The framework's "fraud risk takes precedence" rule was tested and does *not* trigger — Beneish −2.79, accruals −11.8%, DSRI 1.01, clean SEC record. The Short's case is therefore a **weighted fundamental risk**, not a veto.
2. **I resolve the ROIC/cash-flow conflict against the bulls.** Sector cited 19.2% ROIC and ~10x EV/FCF; Value and Short independently derived 11.7% ROIC and ~4% owner-earnings yield. The difference is the fintech loan book, which consumes shareholder capital without appearing in FCF. The conservative figures govern: **ROIC ≈ WACC, owner-earnings yield ≈ 3.2–4%.** This alone caps sizing.
3. **I discount the sell-side.** Median target is still ~$110–133, but the *direction* is unambiguous — Barclays $156→(cut), Morgan Stanley $153→$121→, BofA →$110, New Street downgraded to Neutral at **$70**. Price-target momentum is a contrarian indicator here; do not treat $110 as fair value.
4. **Alt data resolves the "demand vs margin" question in favour of margin.** Revenue is fine (indeed beating); the de-rating is a multiple/margin event. That supports a *small* position, not a large one — because margin is the variable we cannot observe and the Short's provision trend hits exactly there.

**Net aggregated conviction: MEDIUM-LOW, positive expected value, wide dispersion.** This is not a 3–5% position under any framework.

## 6. Risk Management Rules

**Primary exit is fundamental, not price** — a 53%-vol name generates ±15% monthly 2σ moves, so a tight price stop is guaranteed to be noise-triggered.

**Thesis invalidation — exit on any TWO:**
1. Provisions grow faster than the loan book for three more consecutive quarters (six total), or NPL90+ exceeds **2.0%**.
2. Shopee adj. EBITDA below **$100M** for two consecutive quarters (Growth's own sell trigger).
3. Garena bookings growth turns negative, or QAU declines >5% QoQ.
4. FY2027 guidance implies group EBITDA margin below FY2026 with the $1B Shopee target abandoned.
5. Sea Cloud AI annual capex commitment >$1.5B with no disclosed contracted customer revenue.
6. **Statutory** (not voluntary) Indonesian merchant commission cap, any adverse KPPU ruling, or capital-control/repatriation measures.
7. Any independent director resignation (the single highest-signal governance event at a 43%-independent board).

**Price stop (backstop only):** weekly close below **$57.50** (−22%). Set beyond the 2σ monthly band *and* at the top of the bear case ($50–58), so it fires only if the fundamental case has already broken.

**Trim triggers:** sell ⅓ at **$100** (~1.2x central IV), ⅓ at **$115** (floor of the bull case), let the residual run behind a 20% trailing stop. Mechanical rebalance if the position exceeds **3.0%** of portfolio through appreciation.

**Portfolio-level overlays:** (a) halve the position ahead of the 9-Dec FOMC if the book's aggregate duration is already high; (b) no additional EM-fintech credit exposure while SE is held; (c) monitor USD/IDR (18,230), USD/BRL (5.49), and the Monee provision line as the three leading indicators — each is a direct input to the exit rules above.
