## Sandisk Corporation (SNDK) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company snapshot:** NAND flash/SSD designer spun out of Western Digital on 21 Feb 2025; ~11,100 employees; HQ Milpitas, CA. FY2026 (ended 3 Jul 2026) revenue **$20.25bn (+175% YoY)**, gross margin 71.5%, net income $11.43bn. Fabless in the literal sense — it owns **no wafer fabs**; 100% of wafers come from Flash Ventures, three JVs with Kioxia (49.9% owned) operating eight fabs in Japan (six Yokkaichi, two Kitakami). Market cap ~$83.8bn; 8-for-1 split effective 19 Nov 2026. *(Sources: company profile; sec-api.io FY2026 10-K analysis; Kioxia/Sandisk JV release 29 Jan 2026.)*

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Tariff architecture on Japan-origin NAND (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
A Section 232 action on semiconductors took effect 20 Jan 2026 (Federal Register 2026-01052; HTS 9903.79.01 at up to 25%), and a separate Section 301 "excess capacity" investigation has produced proposed duties on 16 trading partners including **China, the EU, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan**, with reported ceilings of 15% (EU/Japan/Korea) and 20% (China) — i.e., these are *replacement* rather than additive duties per White House clarification. Sandisk's problem is structural: every wafer is Japanese, so a 15% duty on Japan-origin goods plus Section 232 duties on derivative products (SSDs) hits landed U.S. cost on ~18% of revenue and on any product assembled abroad from Japanese wafers. Tariffs are simultaneously a cost item and a demand shock — memory stocks fell 6–11% on the 25 Nov 2026 tariff headlines alone. Management already flags Sections 232 and 301 in the FY26 10-K as factors that "may impact tariff rates for Sandisk products." Mitigants are limited by the JV structure (Sandisk cannot unilaterally relocate Flash Ventures capacity).

**Risk 2 — China revenue compression and PRC retaliation (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
Two vectors are converging. First, demand: TrendForce cut its outlook on 25 Nov 2026, citing **Chinese cloud service providers reducing 2027 memory orders**, and separately warned NAND is entering a downcycle as 2027 supply expands — this is the proximate cause of the ~53–75% drawdown in SNDK. Second, policy: PRC authorities have an established playbook of market-access and procurement pressure on U.S. memory suppliers (the 2023 CAC cybersecurity review of Micron is the template), and Beijing is directing domestic substitution toward CXMT/YMTC. Conversely, a U.S. decision to *permit* Apple to source NAND from CXMT — reported in Nov 2026 — knocked SNDK 11% in a session, because it would legitimise a subsidised competitor. A Senator Cotton push to add CXMT to the Entity List cuts the other way. Either outcome is a headline risk measured in double-digit market cap.

**Risk 3 — Japan concentration: single geography, single partner (Probability: Medium / Impact: Very High)**
All supply runs through eight Kioxia-operated fabs. Sandisk guarantees **half of Flash Ventures' Japanese equipment lease obligations — maximum exposure $923m (¥149bn)** at 3 Jul 2026 — and funds 49.9–50% of JV capex when JV cash flow falls short. The JV was extended to 2034 on 29 Jan 2026 and a **$31bn Japanese capacity expansion** was announced 27 Aug 2026, deepening the commitment just as NAND pricing turned. Add Nankai Trough seismic risk, a 50/50 governance structure with a Japanese partner, and the fact that Tokyo is now itself a tariff counterparty. An adverse event here is unhedgeable: there is no second source.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation (active)**
- **In re Sandisk SSDs Litigation**, No. 3:23-cv-04152-RFL (N.D. Cal., Judge Rita F. Lin) — consolidated consumer class action alleging data loss/failures in Extreme/Extreme Pro SSDs; named defendants SanDisk LLC and Western Digital. Plaintiffs have proposed California, New York and Florida subclasses; docket still live as of 3 Nov 2026 and **no settlement fund or claims process exists** as of Nov 2026. Exposure is modest relative to $20bn revenue but the reputational and warranty overlay is real.
- **PNY Technologies antitrust** — direct-purchaser claims alleging SanDisk fraudulently procured two flash-memory patents (Walker Process); claims were dismissed with leave to amend and standing was affirmed on appeal. A live, if low-probability, treble-damages tail.
- **In re SanDisk Technologies / Western Digital**, Fed. Cir. No. 25-152 — mandamus petition arising from third-party patent litigation (Polaris PowerLED). Legacy IP docket inherited from the WDC separation; a reminder that the spin-off carries pre-spin liabilities.
- 10-K language concedes that "an adverse outcome in any litigation, investigation or governmental proceeding" could require "substantial damages, fines or [penalties]."

**Regulatory posture**
- **BIS/Commerce:** export controls on advanced AI chips were tightened again in Nov 2026 (remote-access rule). Sandisk's enterprise SSD line sells directly into AI data centres — the product most likely to be reclassified or licence-restricted. Watch ECCN classification of high-capacity eSSD and any end-use/end-user licensing on China-bound AI storage.
- **Trade:** Section 232 (Jan 2026) and Section 301 excess-capacity action (Nov 2026) as above; both are executive-branch instruments with no legislative ceiling and minimal notice.
- **Antitrust:** the DRAM price-fixing suit against Samsung/SK hynix/Micron survived dismissal in Nov 2026. Sandisk is not a named defendant, but NAND pricing discipline is under the same scrutiny and the "New Business Model" long-term contracts (10 contracts covering >50% of FY27 bits at ~80% gross margin, backed by $5.0bn of third-party collateral, plus two post-year-end contracts at a $31.3bn aggregate transaction price) are precisely the kind of multi-year offtake structure that attracts price-fixing and disclosure questions.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Hong Kong/China distribution and diversion risk.** 25% of revenue booked through Hong Kong, into a jurisdiction subject to layered U.S. export controls. Distributor and reseller screening for Entity List/MEU exposure is the single highest-frequency FCPA/BIS enforcement vector in this profile. No disclosed investigation — but the structural exposure is elevated.
2. **Country-of-origin and tariff classification.** Wafers from Japan, back-end assembly/test in Asia, sold into the U.S. Getting HTS classification and origin wrong under Section 232/301 creates duty underpayment and penalty exposure (19 U.S.C. §1592) across an $20bn revenue base. The 10-K's own tariff language suggests this is still being worked.
3. **Prepayment/offtake accounting.** "New Business Model" contracts with $5.0bn of third-party collateral and $31.3bn of post-year-end transaction value imply large customer prepayments or financing. Revenue recognition on prepaid multi-year offtake, plus counterparty concentration in a handful of hyperscalers, is a disclosure-quality and SEC-comment risk.
4. **JV related-party accounting.** The 49.9% Flash Ventures structure, cost-plus wafer transfers, and $923m of lease guarantees invite scrutiny of off-balance-sheet commitments and VIEs.
5. **Insider activity.** Sixteen Form 4s filed 17–24 Nov 2026 around the split and the drawdown — worth checking for 10b5-1 timing optics.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** ~47% of revenue sits in the China/Hong Kong bloc, 100% of wafers come from one allied but now-tariffed geography (Japan), and the company is simultaneously inside a Section 232 semiconductor action, a Section 301 excess-capacity action, and an escalating U.S.–China export-control regime. Legal exposure is moderate in dollar terms (consumer class action, legacy IP) but the regulatory surface is wide and expanding. The offsetting strengths are real: U.S. and Japanese rule of law, no sanctions-designated counterparties disclosed, no bribery/AML matters, and a JV locked to 2034.

**Downgrade to Medium if:** a U.S.–Japan tariff carve-out for semiconductors is signed; China revenue stabilises; and CXMT stays off the Entity List-free path (i.e., Apple does not qualify it).
**Upgrade to Very High if:** PRC procurement restrictions or a SAMR/CAC action target U.S. NAND suppliers; BIS extends licensing to high-capacity eSSD; or a NAND price-fixing complaint names Sandisk alongside the DRAM defendants.
