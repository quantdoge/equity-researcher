## Sector Brief: Sandisk Corporation (SNDK)

### 1. Sector Overview

Sandisk is a pure-play **NAND flash** manufacturer (sector: Technology; industry: Computer Hardware) — SSDs, embedded/eMMC-UFS, removable cards, and wafers. NAND is a **commodity memory oligopoly**: the top six suppliers (Samsung ~25–28%, SK Hynix ~19%, YMTC ~13–14%, Micron ~13%, Kioxia ~11%, Sandisk ~11–12%) control essentially all bit supply, giving an HHI of roughly **1,800–1,900** — concentrated, but with a rising Chinese insurgent (YMTC) that is structurally eroding the incumbents' pricing discipline. The market is extraordinarily large and violent: global NAND revenue hit **$76.8bn in Q2 2026, +73.1% QoQ**, after a 2023–24 bust that drove Sandisk's own gross margin to ~7%. Critically, **the sector is now past peak**: TrendForce called the turn on 19 Nov 2026, forecasting NAND contract prices to decline from **1Q27 (−0–5% QoQ) with drops widening through the year**, as **2027 bit supply grows 25–30%** on CapEx of **$29.9bn (+26.7% YoY)**.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **AI data-centre storage intensity.** AI training/inference racks require multi-terabyte SSD tiers; enterprise SSD is the tightest part of the NAND complex, and North American hyperscaler demand is still growing even as Chinese CSP orders are cut.
- **Industry supply discipline has been replaced by contract lock-in.** Sandisk has pre-sold much of its forward output under multiyear agreements with **minimum-pricing clauses**, which should cushion the slope of the 2027 ASP decline versus prior cycles.
- **Capital return at trough valuations.** Management has deployed **$5bn of buybacks with $10.5bn more authorised** — at ~8x earnings this is mechanically EPS-accretive and provides a valuation floor that did not exist in prior downcycles.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Pricing has already rolled over.** NAND spot is **down ~20% since mid-October 2026**; 512Gb wafer spot has fallen **32% QoQ to $14.30**. Contract prices, which lag spot, are expected to inflect negative in 1Q27.
- **Demand air-pocket from Chinese CSPs.** Enterprise SSD order cuts by Chinese cloud providers, plus soft consumer PC/smartphone NAND (BofA expects consumer contract prices **−5–10%**), are hitting simultaneously with new supply.
- **Capacity additions plus YMTC.** 25–30% bit-supply growth in 2027 arrives just as YMTC pushes past Micron and Kioxia toward **25%+ global share by late 2027** — a permanent deflationary structural force.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position — **Average**

- **Credible but sub-scale.** Sandisk is roughly the **#5–6 NAND supplier (~11–12% share)** and has slid in the rankings as YMTC and Micron advanced; Kioxia falling out of the top four weakens Sandisk's own position given its fab-light model sources wafers through **Kioxia JV fabs (Yokkaichi/Kitakami)** — a cost and technology-sharing advantage, but one where its partner's fortunes matter as much as its own.
- **Highest purity, highest beta.** No DRAM/HBM hedge means Sandisk gets 100% of the upcycle *and* 100% of the downcycle. FY2023 gross margin was 7.1% and operating margin **−33.4%**; FY2026 was 71.5% / 61.2%. That ~64pt swing in three years is the defining risk.
- **Balance-sheet and contract pier.** Net-cash position, $10.5bn remaining buyback authorisation, and minimum-pricing LTAs are genuine structural improvements versus the 2023 trough — this is the strongest argument that the next downturn is shallower than the last.

### 6. Sector Rating: **Unattractive** (cycle position), with a value caveat

The NAND sector is in **late-cycle peak-to-contraction**: record margins, record CapEx, an inflection in spot pricing already visible, and a demand-side air-pocket from Chinese CSPs. Both SNDK and MU trade at ~**8x trailing peak EPS** — the market is discounting a normalisation, not missing it. On mid-cycle EPS of roughly $3–4/share, SNDK at $73.61 is **~20–25x**, i.e. not the "cheap tech" the screen implies. The stock is already **−75% from its 25 June 2026 peak of $291.88** (split-adjusted post 8-for-1), so much bad news is priced — but cycle turns in commodity memory historically overshoot, and there is no evidence yet of a fundamental bottom in ASPs.

**Recommendation to the investment team:** treat this as a **cycle-timing call, not a quality call**. The sector is unattractive on a 12-month view; the entry point becomes attractive only when NAND contract prices stop falling (watch 2Q–3Q27 contract prints and YMTC supply), not merely because the multiple looks low.
