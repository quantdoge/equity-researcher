# ESG ANALYSIS — SANDISK CORPORATION (NASDAQ: SNDK)
**Sector:** Technology / Computer Hardware (NAND flash) | **HQ:** Milpitas, CA | **Employees:** 11,100 | **Mkt cap:** $83.8B | **Price:** $73.61 (post 8-for-1 split, Nov 19 2026) | **FY26 revenue:** $20.25B (+175% YoY), zero net debt at FYE

*Note on evidence base: SNDK is a Feb-2025 spin-off from Western Digital; no 10-K was retrievable through the filing tool, so this analysis relies on the FY2026 proxy (filed 10/6/26), the inaugural FY2025 Sustainability Report disclosures, court records, and trade press. Peer-multiple and ESG-score data were not reliably available — gaps flagged.*

## 1. ESG Materiality Map — NAND Flash / Storage Hardware

| Factor | Materiality | Why it hits cash flow |
|---|---|---|
| **Scope 3 / supply-chain carbon** | **High** | Fabs are held in unconsolidated JVs (Kioxia, Yokkaichi Japan); real manufacturing emissions sit in purchased NAND, not Scope 1/2 |
| **Product quality & safety** | **High** | Consumer SSD firmware failures → class action, refunds, brand damage in a brand-driven category |

| **Cyber / IP security** | **Critical** | Predecessor suffered a major intrusion; IP is the entire asset base |
| **Water & chemical management (fab)** | Medium | Fab-level, indirect to SNDK; escalating disclosure pressure via customers |

| **Circularity, e-waste, packaging** | Low–Medium | EU ESPR / digital product passport phased in later this decade |
| **Community relations, DEI disclosure** | Low | Immaterial to valuation; limited peer-leading disclosure |

**Core insight:** SNDK is a *structurally Scope-3-heavy* company. Its direct operational footprint is assembly/test and HQ; the energy- and water-intensive wafer fabs sit inside JV structures. Reported Scope 1+2 of ~83k tCO2e therefore **materially understates economic exposure** — the Company's true carbon liability is the ~2.3Mt in Scope 3 plus the JV fabs it part-owns but does not consolidate.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (tail: High)

**Reported FY2025 performance:**
- Scope 1: **1,265 tCO2e** | Scope 2: **82,058 tCO2e** | Scope 3: **2,309,126 tCO2e**
- Scope 3 = **27.7×** Scope 1+2 — an unusually wide ratio for a manufacturer, reflecting the JV fab model
- Targets (first set as independent company): **net-zero Scope 1+2 by 2040**, **100% carbon-free electricity by 2035**, **50% water recycle rate**

**Assessment**
- **Carbon intensity:** Scope 1+2 ≈ 11 tCO2e per $M revenue (FY25) — trivially low. Including Scope 3, ~314 tCO2e per $M in FY25, falling to roughly **~115 tCO2e per $M in FY26** purely because revenue tripled against a flat operational base. The AI/data-centre revenue surge is mechanically *de-carbonising* the revenue denominator — a genuine but low-quality intensity improvement.
- **Transition risk is indirect, not regulatory:** there is no meaningful CBAM exposure on semiconductors. The transmission mechanism is **customer-driven** — hyperscalers and Apple-tier OEMs with Scope 3 science-based targets push supplier requirements down the chain. At a shadow price of $100/t, Scope 3 exposure is **~$231M** (≈1.1% of FY26 revenue) versus just **$8M** for Scope 1+2 combined. That asymmetry is the whole story.
- **Target quality:** Net-zero 2040 for Scope 1+2 with 2035 CFE is **mid-pack to lagging** versus semiconductor peers who have pulled renewable targets to 2030. Scope 3 — the only number that matters here — carries **no disclosed reduction target**. This is the single clearest gap in SNDK's environmental programme.
- **Japan GX-ETS:** The Yokkaichi JV operates inside Japan's emissions-trading phase-in; carbon costs pass through the JV to SNDK's cost of wafers. Not modelled by the Company as a discrete risk.
- **Physical risk:** Japan fab concentration (earthquake/water) is a real supply-continuity exposure, though it is an inherited JV structural risk rather than a disclosure item.

**Verdict:** Low direct operational risk, **High transition/supply-chain risk, inadequately disclosed.**

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Product quality & consumer protection (the live issue):** *In re SanDisk SSDs Litigation* (N.D. Cal., 3:23-cv-04152) over firmware-induced data loss on Extreme/Extreme Pro portable SSDs. Class certification was **granted in part and denied in part in May 2026** (Judge Rita F. Lin), notably with reluctance to certify a nationwide class — which materially caps exposure. **No settlement, fund, or claims process existed as of November 2026.** Estimated financial impact: **$20–60M** in refunds/defence costs, immaterial to an $83.8B cap but a persistent brand liability in a category where "your photos are safe" is the value proposition.
- **Workforce:** 11,100 employees; **restructuring announced 24 August 2026** (dozens of roles in Israel, multiple functions) layered on post-spin duplication removal. Modest scale, but signals the volatility of a cyclical employer and elevates attrition risk among NAND engineers.
- **Supply-chain labour:** Assembly/test in Malaysia and China, plus Chinese component inputs. Reliance on third-party audits; **no disclosed supplier human-rights audit coverage metric or forced-labour remediation data** in the public materials reviewed. This is a disclosure gap, not a demonstrated violation.
- **Safety:** No public TRIR or recordable-incident trend disclosed in accessible sources — unable to score. Treat as an unverified gap rather than a clean bill of health.

**Verdict:** No systemic labour or community failure. Risk is concentrated in **product liability and disclosure immaturity** at a newly independent company.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Positives**
- **Single class of common stock**, one vote per share — no dual-class overhang, no controlled-company status. All 8 directors elected **annually**.
- Say-on-pay passed with **~96% support** (88.8M for / 3.8M against) — no meaningful compensation revolt.
- **Compensation design is genuinely long-term aligned:** FY27 PSUs run a three-year performance period with annual measurement, 0–200% payout range, and a **Relative TSR modifier** — the right structure for a hyper-cyclical memory business where absolute targets can be gamed by the cycle.
- **Balance-sheet discipline:** reached zero debt at FY26 year-end ahead of plan, with a buyback authorised — capital allocation credibility established early.
- Audit Committee explicitly oversees **enterprise risk management and Ethics & Compliance**, a sensible post-spin consolidation of oversight.

**Negatives**
- **Chairman/CEO duality: David V. Goeckeler is both Chair and CEO.** Mitigated by a **Lead Independent Director (Richard B. Cassidy II)** and a majority-independent board, but at a two-year-old issuer the counterweight is thin. This is the main reason the score is not 8–9.
- **Short governance track record** — the board was assembled for the spin; only Alexander Bradley (Dec 2025) has been added since. Limited evidence of independent challenge through a full memory down-cycle.
- **No ESG-linked executive metrics** disclosed in incentive design — pay is financially and TSR-driven only. Defensible, but means sustainability targets carry no compensation weight.
- **Virtual-only annual meeting** — reduces shareholder access; standard for peers but still a mild rights negative.
- No shareholder ESG proposals on the 2026 ballot — absence of conflict, but also evidence the board has not been stress-tested on climate or human-rights oversight.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD** — In scope once the non-EU threshold (€150M EU turnover) is triggered; first reporting now realistically FY2029 under the Omnibus "stop-the-clock" delays. Practical near-term exposure is **value-chain data requests from EU-based customers**, which SNDK must service regardless of its own filing obligation. The FY25 **first double-materiality assessment** is a genuine readiness signal.
- **California SB 253 / SB 254** — **Highest-probability mandatory regime for SNDK**: California-domiciled, revenue far above $1B. Scope 1/2 and eventually Scope 3 disclosure required; CARB timelines have slipped but are not dead. The Company's Scope 3 = 99.7% of footprint means SB 253's Scope 3 requirement is the binding constraint on readiness.
- **Japan** — GX-ETS phase-in and METI transition policy apply to the Yokkaichi JV fabs; carbon cost flows to SNDK through wafer pricing.
- **EU product regulation** — ESPR digital product passport, WEEE, RoHS, REACH, and EU Conflict Minerals Regulation (2017/821) apply to SSDs and the tantalum/tin/tungsten/gold and cobalt supply chain. Consumer-facing compliance cost, low single-digit $M annually.
- **US export controls & China's dual-use ban on Japan (Jan 2026)** — the **most material regulatory item in this report**, and it is misfiled as "ESG." China's blanket restriction on dual-use goods exports to Japan directly threatens **critical inputs to the Yokkaichi JV**, alongside rare-earth and magnet controls. Add China's precedent of banning Micron memory sales (2023) as evidence that NAND suppliers can lose a market on non-commercial grounds. *This is a licence-to-operate risk with multi-hundred-million-dollar revenue and cost implications — far larger than every other ESG item here combined.*
- **SEC climate disclosure rule** — rescinded/stayed; **de minimis** US federal exposure.
- **CBAM** — negligible (no covered goods).
- **ESG-linked debt covenants** — **none**; SNDK is debt-free. This is a clean structural positive: no sustainability-linked pricing step-up risk.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

SNDK is a **credible first-year reporter with real governance strengths and shallow environmental depth.** It gets full credit for: single-class shares, annual elections, ~96% say-on-pay support, a Relative-TSR-modifier PSU structure, a zero-debt balance sheet, a first double-materiality assessment, and disclosure of all three scopes in its inaugural year. It loses ground on: **combined Chair/CEO**, an unproven two-year board, **no Scope 3 reduction target** (the only target that matters for a fab-light NAND company), no compensation link to ESG metrics, unverified safety and supply-chain-audit metrics, and disclosure that has never been tested through a full down-cycle.

Direction of travel: **improving, from a low base.** The 2025 inaugural report and 2035/2040 targets are a floor, not a ceiling — peers have held renewable and net-zero targets for years.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (with one material non-financial flag)

**ESG factors are not the driver of the SNDK thesis — the NAND/AI-memory cycle is.** In that sense the headline is genuinely neutral: no stranded assets, no CBAM, no ESG-linked debt, no methane or spill exposure, no dual-class control, and a litigation tail that is real but small relative to an $83.8B market cap.

Three things do matter to a cash-flow model:

1. **Customer-driven Scope 3 pressure is a slow margin tax, not a fine.** At $100/t, SNDK's Scope 3 is ~$231M (~1.1% of revenue) and rising in relevance as hyperscaler procurement gates on supplier emissions. Because the Company has **no Scope 3 target**, it is a price-taker on that dialogue. Watch for supplier mandates from the cloud customers now signing multi-year take-or-pay agreements — that is where disclosure failures convert into pricing outcomes.
2. **Governance score of 7/10 is appropriate but not a moat.** Combined Chair/CEO at a company this young, in a business this cyclical, with a 75% drawdown from the June 2026 high, is a real question about board challenge capacity. The Relative-TSR PSU modifier is a meaningful offset — management is paid on relative performance, which is the correct incentive in memory.
3. **The dominant non-financial risk is geopolitical, not environmental.** China's dual-use export restrictions on Japan put JV fab inputs in the blast radius. Investors screening SNDK on environmental metrics will completely miss a supply-continuity exposure capable of moving a year of earnings.

**Recommendation to the investment committee:** Treat SNDK ESG as **monitor-only, not a valuation input.** Do not apply an ESG discount — the balance sheet, share structure, and pay alignment don't warrant one. Do, however, add **Japan/China export-control supply continuity** to the core risk register as a top-three item, and flag to management that a **Scope 3 target** is the cheapest available ESG credibility upgrade ahead of California SB 253.
