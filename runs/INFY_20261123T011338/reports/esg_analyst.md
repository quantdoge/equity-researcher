## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- **Carbon:** Carbon neutral across Scope 1, 2 **and 3** for the seventh consecutive year (FY26); FY26 reductions of **71.2% in Scope 1+2** and **45.1% in absolute Scope 3** versus baseline. Target: **climate positive by 2030**; **Net Zero 2040 with SBTi-validated targets**; **CDP Climate A-** (2024). *(All company-reported; not independently audited here.)*
- **Transition risk:** Near-zero carbon-tax exposure. No CBAM-liable goods. Energy cost is a modest opex line, largely offset by rooftop solar and RE procurement.
- **Physical risk:** Asset-light; dispersed delivery centres in India, Eastern Europe, LatAm and US — moderate business-continuity exposure to heat/water stress in Indian campuses, not a valuation item.
- **The real channel is second-order:** EU clients under CSRD push Scope 3 supplier data requirements and sustainability clauses into MSAs. Infosys is a *supplier of* decarbonisation services, not a carbon liability — this is net commercial opportunity (sustainability/ESG-tech practices).
- **Watch item:** AI-driven data-centre and GPU compute growth (AI now 10.5% of revenue) will raise Scope 2/3 intensity; the climate-positive-by-2030 claim becomes harder to defend. Greenwashing/claims risk is the tail risk, not carbon cost.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Strengths**
- **Women 39.5% of workforce** (129,625 women) — best-in-class for Indian IT; 155 nationalities; 900+ women re-hired through Restart.
- **Attrition normalised to 12.6%** (Q4 FY26), down from the 20%+ post-COVID peak — a major de-risking of the wage-inflation thesis.
- 15mn+ people digitally skilled; 262,000+ employee volunteering hours; strong employer-brand recognition.

**Risks**
- **Workforce contraction:** FY26 headcount fell by ~25,994 (Q4 alone −8,440) with ~2% restructuring in 2025, alongside plans to onboard ~20,000 freshers. Net effect: **experience-mix dilution** — 166,636 employees are aged ≤30. This pressures utilisation, quality and client escalations, and creates social/reputational friction in India's politically sensitive IT labour market (state labour-authority scrutiny of layoffs is a live theme).
- **Immigration dependency — the single largest social/regulatory risk.** A US **DOJ investigation into H-1B employee classification** was disclosed 23-Oct-2026; Infosys says it cannot predict whether the outcome will be materially adverse. This is a **repeat issue**: the company paid a record **$34M** in 2013 to settle B-1/H-1B misuse allegations with independent-audit undertakings.
- **Product responsibility:** the **Infosys McCamish ransomware breach (Nov-2023)** exposed ~6.5mn individuals' names, SSNs and driver's licences, resolved via a **$17.5M class settlement (2025)**. Direct cost is immaterial; the reputational cost inside the US insurance/annuity vertical is not.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Positives**
- Separate Chair/CEO; non-executive co-founder Chairman (Nilekani takes **no salary**); majority-independent board (~9–10 directors, 2 independent women directors ≈ 22–30%).
- **One share, one vote** — no dual-class structure; ADS = 1 equity share; robust pre-emptive rights, postal-ballot and e-voting requirements.
- Disclosure is genuinely superior: 20-F + IFRS + SEBI BRSR + GRI/SASB-aligned ESG report + separate governance filings quarterly.
- Founder/promoter holding ~14.6% aligns founders with long-term value; no controlling-shareholder extraction.
- Board backed Nilekani's third term as Chairman alongside the FY26 CEO pay increase — continuity, though it also signals limited board challenge.

**Deductions**
- **CEO pay ratio 742x median** (₹82.6 crore vs ₹11.13 lakh median, FY26, +2.5% YoY) — one of the highest in Indian IT; alternative disclosures put the ratio at ~289x, which itself suggests reporting-methodology inconsistency. Say-on-pay is only advisory.
- **Compliance track record is the weak link:** 2013 ICE/DOJ visa settlement ($34M) → 2019 whistleblower allegations on revenue recognition and the Panaya acquisition → SEC resolution (2021, reported ~$3M civil penalty) → 2017 founder-vs-management crisis (Sikka resignation) → 2026 DOJ H-1B probe. Each is individually manageable; the **pattern** is a governance-quality signal.
- **Tax controversy overhang:** the ₹32,403 crore DGGI GST pre-show-cause notice (Jul-2024) was **closed in June 2025 with no demand** — a clean outcome, but smaller GST matters persist (₹415 crore show-cause notice under challenge at Karnataka HC; ₹13.60 crore CGST penalty order).
- Disclosure-credibility noise: securities-investigation notices (Oct-2026) following the Q2 FY27 guidance cut and a 6.5% ADR drawdown.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **India:** SEBI **BRSR** (mandatory, with BRSR Core reasonable-assurance phasing); Companies Act **2% CSR mandate** (~₹500–600 crore p.a. on FY26 net profit of ₹29,440 crore); new **DPDP Act** data-fiduciary duties; Companies Act "Government Investigation" disclosure obligations (triggered the DOJ disclosure).
- **United States:** **H-1B/B-1 immigration rules** (highest-severity exposure); SEC climate-disclosure rule stayed — not currently binding; California SB 253/261 arguably applicable given US revenue scale; OFAC/export-control and AI-usage governance for regulated clients.
- **EU:** **CSRD** — Infosys' EU subsidiaries/clients make it a value-chain reporter (and EU is a major revenue region), so Scope 3 supplier data demands arrive regardless of its own filing obligations; **CSDDD** value-chain due diligence via contracts; **EU AI Act** is the fastest-rising item given AI at 10.5% of revenue (high-risk system classification, documentation and client indemnities); **GDPR** as processor for EU clients.
- **CBAM:** No direct obligation (no CBAM goods); indirect pass-through only via EU manufacturing clients.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** Not a feature of the capital structure (net-cash balance sheet, negligible leverage) — **no covenant risk**.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (E: Leader · S: Average · G: Average)

Infosys has **Leader-grade environmental and disclosure credentials** — seven years carbon-neutral across all three scopes, SBTi-validated Net Zero 2040, CDP A-, 39.5% women, BRSR/20-F dual disclosure — but the composite is held at **Average** because the two *most financially material* factors for an IT-services model are exactly where the record is weakest: **immigration compliance (live DOJ probe, $34M 2013 precedent)** and **data privacy (major 2023 breach)**. High CEO pay ratio and recurring disclosure/litigation noise compound the governance discount. This is a well-governed company by Indian standards and a mediocre one by the compliance-record standard that matters for a US-listed, visa-dependent, client-data-handling business.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

- **Nothing here re-rates the equity.** There are no stranded assets, no carbon-cost pass-through, no ESG-linked debt, and no evidence of a governance discount in the multiple. ESG is not the thesis.
- **Two items are genuinely thesis-relevant and belong in the bear case, not the ESG appendix:**
 1. **US immigration policy/DOJ probe** — a $100k H-1B fee (now struck down) would have cost 5–12% of EBIT. Any re-imposition, or a consent decree limiting onsite deployment, is a margin event, not a reputational one. Manageable but live.
 2. **AI transition on the S side** — headcount down ~26,000 in FY26 while hiring 20,000 freshers and pushing AI-led delivery is a *pricing and pyramid* issue; social friction (labour scrutiny, reskilling obligations, EU AI Act indemnities) is a slow cost leak.
- **Offsetting positives:** best-in-class attrition (12.6%), 39.5% female workforce, SBTi-validated climate targets, and clean resolution of the ₹32,403 crore GST matter all reduce tail risk and support the "high-quality compounder" framing.
- **Actionable for the model:** no change to valuation. Flag the **DOJ outcome** and **H-1B fee litigation** as monitored catalysts with a **$10–50M / $200–500M** sensitivity range respectively; treat the McCamish and GST items as fully de-risked.
