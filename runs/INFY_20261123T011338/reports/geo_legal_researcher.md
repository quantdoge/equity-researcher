### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US immigration regime tightening (DOJ probe + PERM suspension + H-1B fee)**
*Probability: High (already materialising) | Impact: Moderate (10–100bps margin; tail risk of a larger settlement)*
- Infosys **disclosed a US DOJ investigation** into how it classified certain H-1B holders in immigration documents, in its Sept-2026 quarter legal proceedings ("Government Investigation"). "The Company is engaged in discussions with the DOJ regarding its ongoing investigation" and cannot predict whether the outcome would be material. [American Bazaar, 24-Oct-2026; Times of India citing SEC filing]
- Precedent is unhelpful: Infosys paid a **record $34M** civil settlement to settle "systemic visa fraud" allegations over B-1 visa misuse and I-9 verification in 2013. [ICE press release] A repeat penalty of similar or larger scale is plausible; the market has precedent for pricing this in.
- On **8-Oct-2026**, DOL announced a **PERM processing suspension** covering Infosys and seven other employers. Management downplayed it, citing the shift to local US hiring and noting most US employees do not require visas. [VisaHQ; staffingindustry.com, 3-Nov-2026]
- The **~$100,000/$103,265 H-1B fee** has been **blocked twice by federal judges** — so it is delayed, not dead. Sell-side estimates of the margin hit range from 10–20bps (Crisil) to 50–150bps, with 30–70% assumed pass-through to clients. [Financial Express/Crisil; Kotak Neo]

**Risk 2 — US-India geopolitical friction (Russia-oil secondary tariffs)**
*Probability: Medium (30–40% of renewed tariff escalation) | Impact: Low-Moderate, indirect*
- US legislation authorising tariffs of **up to 100%** on major buyers of Russian crude, India included, has passed; the administration has effectively **paused** its application, and India has said it will take "all necessary measures." [Indian Express; Hürriyet; InsideTrade]
- Direct INFY revenue exposure is minimal (IT services are not the tariffed goods), but second-order effects matter: Indian client sentiment, INR volatility (a weaker INR is a **tailwind** to Infosys margins given USD revenue / INR cost base), and US client willingness to award large offshore contracts during trade friction. Note India's continued Russian oil purchases have already drawn Western criticism. [S&P Global; CFR]

**Risk 3 — Securities/disclosure litigation after the guidance cut**
*Probability: Medium | Impact: Low*
- Following the **23-Oct-2026 Q2 FY27 print** (revenue $5,145M, +1.5% YoY cc; EPS $0.20 vs $0.21 est.; FY27 cc growth guidance cut to **1–2% from 1.5–3%**), plaintiff firm SueWallSt issued an "investigation" notice on 26-Oct-2026 into potential securities-law violations. [PRNewswire, 26-Oct-2026; Barron's; Proactive]
- Caveat: this is a law-firm solicitation, **not a confirmed filed complaint**, and the alleged "miss" was a penny of EPS on a soft-guidance day. Infosys has a history of these suits (the 2019 whistleblower-driven class action), which have historically been nuisance-value. Low financial materiality.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **McCamish data breach (resolved):** $17.5M class settlement resolving claims from ~3M insurance customers over the 2023 breach at the US subsidiary; **final court approval received** (reported December 2025). [classaction.org; HIPAA Journal; Business Standard] This is the single largest quantified legal liability and is now closed — but it established an adverse data-security track record that raises future breach exposure in a business handling BFSI client data.
- **BFSI client concentration:** The largest vertical is financial services, which imports heavy regulators (OCC, Fed, FCA, ECB, BaFin) and third-party risk-management obligations onto Infosys as a vendor — an ongoing but well-managed compliance cost.
- **Data privacy / AI:** GDPR and the **EU AI Act's** phased obligations from summer 2026 land directly on a company now generating **10.5% of revenue from AI services** (Q2 FY27). India's **DPDP Act** rules are in implementation (compliance timeline running through 2026–27). Infosys markets GDPR/CCPA/EU AI Act compliance as a service line, meaning it is simultaneously a regulated party and a compliance vendor — a reputational lever if it self-fails.
- **Sanctions / FCPA / AML:** No OFAC, FCPA, or AML enforcement action against Infosys surfaced in this review; no material Russia or Iran-linked operations were identified. Compliance red flag rating: **no active flags**, though the absence of a Russia disclosure in reviewed material is a gap rather than a clean bill of health.

---

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Immigration compliance is a demonstrated control weakness, not a one-off.** Two government actions in 13 years (2013 $34M settlement; 2026 DOJ probe) plus a 2026 PERM suspension. This is the firm's most persistent compliance theme and warrants explicit hair-cut treatment in any legal-contingency model.
2. **Data-security controls at the McCamish subsidiary** — a $17.5M, 3M-person settlement is a leading indicator of systemic third-party risk-management gaps.
3. **Disclosure discipline** — the FY27 guidance reset and accompanying plaintiff-firm activity invites repeat securities claims. Watch for a formal complaint filing within 60 days.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Revenue is heavily concentrated in the single highest-scrutiny jurisdiction (US, 56%) at a moment of maximum immigration-policy hostility, and there is a live DOJ investigation with an unquantified tail. Offsetting this: no sanctions exposure, no goods-tariff vulnerability, a strong net-cash balance sheet capable of absorbing a $34M-plus settlement, a resolved data-breach liability, and litigation risk that is nuisance-grade. Europe (32%) adds regulatory complexity (AI Act, GDPR) but no acute legal exposure. India (2.9% of revenue) is a low-risk domestic market.

**Key monitorables:** (i) DOJ resolution size/timing; (ii) whether the H-1B fee survives appellate review; (iii) PERM suspension duration; (iv) whether a formal securities class action is filed; (v) Russia-oil tariff legislation activation.
