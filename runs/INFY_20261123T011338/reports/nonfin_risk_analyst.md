## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (with specific control weaknesses)**

**Positives — genuine, not box-ticking:**
- **Chair/CEO separation is real.** Nandan Nilekani (co-founder, non-executive Chairman) vs. Salil Parekh (MD & CEO). Nilekani voluntarily draws **no remuneration** — a meaningful alignment signal rare among global peers.
- **No controlling shareholder.** Founder-promoter block ~648M shares (~15.6%); professional board, dispersed institutional ownership. No promoter pledging disclosed in the Mar-2026 shareholding pattern.
- **Audit committee has a track record of standing up to management.** In the 2019 whistleblower episode (allegations that the CEO/CFO bypassed board and auditor review, ~$2bn of "no-op" invoices, improper revenue recognition on large deals), the committee concluded the allegations were "substantially without merit"; the **SEC closed its investigation with no further action** and the US securities class action was **dismissed**. That is the correct outcome and evidence the process functions.
- **₹18,000 crore (~$2B) buyback** — largest in a decade — executed with founders abstaining but retaining holdings.

**Weaknesses — these are what drive the rating down from "Strong":**
- **SEBI penalty touching the CEO personally.** Salil Parekh paid a **₹25 lakh penalty to settle SEBI charges relating to weak internal controls around insider-trading compliance.** Small in dollars; serious in kind — it is a finding against the *insider-trading control framework* and it names the CEO. No admitted wrongdoing assumed, but this is a control-integrity flag, not a parking ticket.
- **~14% shareholder votes against Chairman Nilekani's reappointment** (86% support). For a founder-chairman at a company with this ownership base, double-digit opposition is a loud signal of governance fatigue. Nilekani has since stated no chairman transition is planned.
- **Chairman's political/outside commitments.** In July 2026 the Prime Minister appointed Nilekani to lead a High-Powered Task Force on Examination Reforms. Not a conflict of interest, but it is bandwidth diversion for the single most important non-executive figure during the company's most consequential strategic transition.
- **Disclosure architecture is thinner for US holders.** As an FPI, Infosys files no DEF 14A (confirmed: none available) and insiders are **not subject to Section 16**. US investors therefore get no proxy-season compensation rationale and no Form 4 substance — governance visibility is structurally inferior to a domestic US issuer.
- **History of founder–board friction:** the 2017 Sikka resignation and the 2019 whistleblower saga are not ancient history; they establish a pattern of episodic board-management rupture.

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis — Net **SELLING / NO BUYING** over 12 months

Caveat: as an FPI, insiders file under **SEBI PIT regulations**, not Section 16. The SEC Form 4 index (8 filings in six months) returned filing links without transaction detail — no substantive Form 4 tape exists to analyse. Signal is drawn from Indian exchange disclosures.

- **CEO Salil Parekh sold 95,800 shares at ₹1,177.86 on 15 May 2026 (~₹113 crore, ~$13M).** (Note: some syndicated headlines reported "$112M" — a unit-scaling error; treat the rupee figure as accurate.) Mechanically their percentage rises as shares are extinguished, which is not bearish per se, but there is **zero insider conviction buying** at a 6-year-low share price. That absence is itself informative.
- **No insider purchases identified in the trailing 12 months.**

**Verdict:** Net selling, no buys, into a −25% tape. Not evidence of malfeasance; a genuine dearth of insider confidence.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **AI-driven deflation of the core time-and-materials model — the existential one.** Q2 FY27 revenue +1.5% y/y in constant currency, margin flat at 21.0%, large-deal TCV $5.2B (62% net new), AI revenue now **10.5% of sales and +29% q/q**. The tension: record deal wins are *not* converting into revenue growth. AI is compressing price per unit of work faster than Infosys can sell new work — which is why **FY27 guidance was cut three times in seven months (1.5–3.5% → 1.5–3% → 1–2%)**. Guidance cut on 23 Oct came with a same-day −6.6% move and JPMorgan's downgrade to Neutral. Consensus is now "Reduce" (1 sell, 10 hold, 0 buys).
2. **Workforce and labour relations.** Headcount fell **~21,000 in FY26**, including **8,440 in a single quarter**, with **950+ terminations since January 2026** while management publicly maintained there were no mass layoffs. Job postings down 22%. In the Indian market this "silent layoffs" narrative carries real reputational and political cost, and the French labour-authority fine shows the model is now exposed to European labour enforcement too.
3. **Talent mobility / immigration dependence.** The PERM suspension and the $100,000 H-1B fee together attack the offshore-onsite arbitrage that underpins North America delivery. A company already growing at 1% cannot easily absorb a 2–3% onsite cost step-up; and PERM suspension impairs retention of the senior onsite bench precisely when client-facing AI work demands it. Secondary: **third-party/vendor risk** — the ESOP Direct breach was a *vendor* compromise one year after a $17.5M principal-side settlement, indicating vendor security governance has not been fixed. Client-concentration data (top-10/top-25 %) could not be retrieved — infosys.com returns 403 — **flagging as a data gap**, historically a material 20%+ exposure.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **ELEVATED**

**Negative:** the "silent layoffs" story is running in Indian business media and social channels; Infosys is a named defendant-in-waiting in the politically charged H-1B/PERM crackdown (Reuters framed the DOL action as the administration "freezing green cards… [and] probes"); the AI-disruption narrative has reclassified the stock from quality compounder to structurally challenged; and plaintiff-firm "investigation notice" press releases (SueWallSt, Levi & Korsinsky) now surface in the news flow on a near-weekly basis, which is a persistent low-grade brand irritant. Sell-side tone is hostile (average rating "Reduce").

**Positive offsets:** strong client proof-points (Truist GCC expansion, Syngenta SAP S/4HANA go-live across 80 countries, Qorus-Finacle awards, UNI Financial Cooperation); top-200 client AI engagement breadth (90%); a genuinely well-regarded ESG/governance disclosure franchise; no product-safety or consumer-backlash exposure (B2B model). This is a **credibility** problem, not a **character** problem — reputational damage is contained to the investor and talent markets, not to the customer base.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical — there is no fraud evidence, no restatement, no going-concern or liquidity issue, no CEO/chairman duality, and no active SEC enforcement. But **High** is warranted on the convergence of five independent, mutually reinforcing risks: (i) a control-integrity finding against the CEO over insider-trading controls; (ii) three guidance reductions in seven months producing securities litigation and a credibility discount; (iii) a policy shock (PERM/H-1B) aimed directly at the delivery model; (iv) a structural AI deflation threat that management has not yet demonstrated it can outgrow; and (v) repeated data incidents including a vendor compromise. Each is individually manageable; together they explain a 39% de-rating and they are *correlated* — a growth-challenged company has less financial slack to absorb compliance, immigration and cyber costs.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Insider-trading control failure + CEO sale timing.** SEBI settlement (₹25 lakh) over weak insider-trading internal controls, followed by a ~₹113 crore CEO share sale in May 2026 and a guidance cut five months later. *No inference of wrongdoing is made* — but the sequencing requires an explicit review of trade-window compliance and pre-clearance records before we underwrite management credibility.
2. **Three guidance cuts in seven months (1.5–3.5% → 1.5–3% → 1–2%).** This is the single most important escalation. Repeated downward revision, each with a sharp price reaction, invites SEC/SEBI scrutiny of the forecasting and disclosure process — not just a class action.
3. **Immigration policy shock is live and unresolved.** PERM suspension (Oct 2026) plus the $100k H-1B fee directly impair onsite delivery and senior-staff retention. Model the revenue/margin sensitivity; treat as an active, not tail, risk.
4. **Vendor cyber governance.** ESOP Direct (Oct 2025) one year after a $17.5M McCamish settlement implies third-party risk management is a recurring, unfixed weakness — with GDPR/CCPA tail exposure for a firm holding client data across US insurers and banks.
5. **Founder/chairman alignment signals.** ~14% votes against the Chairman's reappointment, zero insider buying at a six-year low, and founders abstaining from the flagship buyback. Watch for further chairman-transition ambiguity.
6. **Employment practices under scrutiny.** 21,000 headcount reduction against a public "no layoffs" stance, plus a French labour-authority fine — rising probability of Indian labour-department or works-council attention.

**Data gaps (disclosed, not papered over):** full 20-F Item 3.D risk factors not retrievable (the filing tool returned Exhibit 2.1 — Description of Securities); no DEF 14A exists (FPI exemption); infosys.com blocks automated fetch (403), so board independence counts, committee composition, and top-10 client concentration percentages were not verified.
