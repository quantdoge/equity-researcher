## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Late-cycle reflation with a term-premium shock — UNFAVOURABLE for long-duration IT services.**

Three forces define the backdrop. First, US growth is *not* rolling over: real GDP grew **+3.4% in Q3 2026** (Q2 +2.2%, Q1 +2.5%), a sharp acceleration from the 0.1%/0.2% stall in 2025. Second, inflation has **re-accelerated to 3.82% CPI YoY (Oct 2026)** from ~3.0% a year earlier, forcing the Fed to stop cutting and resume tightening (effective funds rate **3.63% → 3.88%** between August and October). Third, and most important for Infosys, the **long end has sold off hard**: the 10Y is **5.13%**, up ~65bp from 4.48% at end-June, and the 2s10s spread is **+39bp** — a *bear steepener*, not a recession signal.

The critical detail: **10Y breakevens are only 2.15%**, down from 2.38% in late October. The market is *not* pricing structurally higher inflation — it is pricing **real rates of ~3.0%** (5.13% nominal less 2.15% breakeven). That is a term-premium/fiscal-supply shock, amplified by debt-financed AI datacentre capex. The Fed's real policy rate, by contrast, is roughly **zero** (3.88% vs 3.82% CPI). So the curve is tightening far ahead of the Fed, and the 2Y at **4.74%** sits 86bp *above* the effective funds rate — the market is priced for more hikes.

For Infosys this is the worst combination: **client budgets deflating while the discount rate inflates.** Discretionary IT spend is the first line item cut when CFOs face 5%+ long bond yields and expensive refinancing — and IT services budgets are forecast to grow only **~2% in 2026 vs 3.7% for total IT spend**, precisely because AI is crowding out traditional services.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Negative**

- **Real long rates (~3.0%)** — The single largest driver. Infosys derived ~55–60% of revenue from North America, heavily BFSI (~30%). At 5.13% 10Y, bank clients face mark-to-market losses on securities portfolios and tighter capital, while every client's CFO raises hurdle rates on multi-year transformation programmes. This is visible in the numbers: **Q2 FY27 revenue $5.145bn, just +1.5% YoY in constant currency**, with **guidance cut** and explicit "cautious client spending and heightened pricing competition."
- **India cost inflation + RBI tightening** — Repo hiked to 5.50% with CPI projected at 5.2%. Infosys' true input is Indian wage inflation, and it is absorbing this **without pricing power** (pricing competition flagged by management). Classic margin squeeze: input costs rising, output prices falling.
- **INR appreciation** — USD/INR moved from 96.78 (8-Oct) to 94.24 (13-Nov), a ~2.6% INR rally in five weeks. With ~70% of costs in INR against USD revenue, each 1% INR move is worth roughly **20–30bp of EBIT margin**; this swing is a ~50–75bp headwind if sustained. Critically, the **FX tailwind that Indian IT relied on for a decade has stalled** — INR is roughly flat over six months.
- **AI budget cannibalisation** — AI reaches **~20% of business tech spend in 2027** (Forrester) while traditional IT services budgets grow only ~2%. This deflates the headcount-based pricing model. Infosys' AI revenue at **10.5% of total** is progress, but not yet enough to offset the deflation in the legacy base.
- **Trade policy overhang** — No signed US–India trade deal as of 3-Nov-2026; the 18% framework rate was never applied and a 10% Section 301 duty persists. IT *services* are not directly tariffed, but the uncertainty suppresses Indian asset sentiment and FII flows.
- **Immigration policy** — The **$100,000 H-1B fee** is in effect, with litigation ongoing (a US court has reportedly struck it down, creating uncertainty). Brokerages see limited P&L impact via localisation and offshoring, but it is a structural cost escalator. Infosys also disclosed an **H-1B usage probe** on 29-Oct — idiosyncratic, but macro-policy-adjacent.

**Positive**

- **Solid US growth (3.4%)** and **4.1% unemployment** — Client balance sheets and cash flows are healthy. This is not a demand collapse; it is deferred demand. That distinction matters for the recovery trade.
- **Record deal TCV** — **$5.2bn in Q2 FY27 large deals** confirms clients are still committing to multi-year cost-takeout and AI transformation. The order book, not the P&L, is where the recovery will first appear.
- **Bear-steepening helps BFSI clients' net interest margins** — a partial offset to their capital-markets pain, supporting eventual IT budgets.
- **Mild USD strength** — Translation-positive for USD-reported revenue, though it squeezes clients' own budgets.

**Neutral**

- **Rate sensitivity of Infosys' own balance sheet** — effectively nil. Near-zero net debt means no refinancing risk, no interest-rate exposure on the liability side. The rate sensitivity here is **entirely valuation-driven (duration) and demand-driven (client capex)**, not balance-sheet-driven. This is important: INFY is *not* a rate-sensitive business in the REIT/utility sense; it is a long-duration equity whose multiple compresses with real yields.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The macro regime is working against Infosys on three axes simultaneously: **demand** (discretionary budgets deferred at 5%+ long yields), **cost** (Indian wage inflation with no pricing power), and **FX** (INR strength reversing a structural tailwind). Growth is too firm to force Fed cuts that would rescue the multiple, and inflation is too sticky to let the long end rally on its own. Inflation is *not* the problem for Infosys — **real rates are**.

This is already discounted: the ADR at **$12.03** (20-Nov) is **down ~28% from $16.73 a year ago**, and IT services peers are down up to 31% YTD. The macro risk is largely *in* the price, which is precisely why the rating is Unfavourable on fundamentals rather than a sell signal on valuation.

---

## 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise: **BULL — a rates-led violent re-rating**

The market is positioned for higher-for-longer (2Y 4.74%, 86bp above the effective funds rate, implying ~3 more hikes). But breakevens at **2.15%** — and falling — say inflation expectations are anchored, and the real 10Y near **3.0%** is historically restrictive.

**The surprise: an AI-capex-related growth scare in H1 2027 that breaks the long end.** If AI datacentre ROI disappoints and credit spreads widen, the 10Y falls from 5.13% toward **4.00–4.25%** and the front end prices cuts back to ~3.25%. Infosys would re-rate on two engines at once: (a) discount-rate compression on a long-duration equity that has already de-rated 28%, and (b) release of the deferred discretionary pipeline — the $5.2bn quarterly TCV converts into revenue. Historically, Indian IT re-rates 25–40% in the two quarters following a peak in US real yields. A 15–20% move off $12 is plausible and would be driven by **macro, not company news** — which is exactly why it is under-priced.

**Bear tail (secondary):** if CPI pushes through 4.5% and the Fed takes policy to 4.75%, with the 10Y at 6.0%, the deferred-demand thesis breaks and guidance cuts extend into FY28 — another 20% downside from here. Watch the 30Y (5.47%) as the tell; a break above 5.75% would be the signal.

**Key monitorables:** monthly CPI (is 3.82% the peak?), the 10Y breakeven (2.15% — any break above 2.50% changes the regime reading), the 2Y-vs-EFFR gap (86bp of priced hikes), RBI's December MPC, USD/INR at the 94 level, and any resolution of the US–India trade framework.
