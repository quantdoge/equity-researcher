# Amazon.com, Inc. (AMZN) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price $262.45 | Market cap ~$2.84T | EV ~$2.87T | Beta 1.46**
*Fiscal 2025 (Dec-2025) audited; TTM through Q2-2026 (reported 30-Jul-2026). Q3-2026 reports 29-Oct-2026 (consensus EPS $1.96, revenue $202.2B).*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: WIDE (and widening over the past five years)**

| Engine | Scale (latest) | Growth | Moat source |
|---|---|---|---|
| AWS | $42.2B/qtr (~$169B annualized), **21% of revenue but ~60% of operating income** | **+37% YoY** | Switching costs (data gravity, IAM/VPC integration), efficient scale, custom silicon (Graviton/Trainium) |
| Advertising | ~$19.8B/qtr (~$79B annualized) | **+26% YoY** | Intangible + network: purchase-intent data no rival can replicate |
| Marketplace/retail | ~$600B+ GMV | High single digit | Two-sided network effects, Prime switching costs, logistics efficient scale |

The single most important fact in this file is the structural mix shift. Gross margin has gone from **39.6% (2020) → 50.3% (2025)**, and operating margin from **5.9% → 11.2%** (2.4% trough in 2022). That is not cyclical recovery; it is the high-margin AWS + ads + third-party-services mix displacing low-margin first-party retail. A moat that shows up as 1,070bps of gross-margin expansion over five years is a real moat.

Evidence on returns: ROIC has climbed **4.3% (2022) → 10.9% (2023) → 16.5% (2024) → 14.2% (2025)** against a ~9% WACC. Consistently above cost of capital, but the *gap is thin* for a "wide moat" name — and the 2025 decline reflects a capital base inflating faster than profits (equity $286B → $411B).

**Candid caveat:** the moat is wide in *cloud and advertising*, narrow-to-none in *first-party retail*, which is where most of the 1.58M employees and the capital sit. I can underwrite AWS and ads with confidence; I cannot underwrite AI datacenter demand through 2030.

\*TTM net income includes roughly $40B+ of non-operating investment revaluation gains booked in Q2-2026 — **core earnings are ~$88–90B, not $135B.** Anyone quoting a 21x P/E on this stock is using the flattered number. Core P/E is ~32x.

**Strengths:** fortress balance sheet — cash & investments $123.0B vs net debt $66.2B (~0.4x EBITDA), interest coverage ~28x, equity $411B. Revenue is highly predictable. SBC is falling as a share of revenue (4.2% → 2.7%) — genuine improvement in earnings quality.

**The disqualifying weakness:** FCF conversion is **~10% of net income**. Buffett's threshold is >80%. Amazon fails by an order of magnitude, and this is a deliberate capital-allocation decision, not distress — 2026 capex guidance was raised twice in 2026, from $200B to **$220B**. Owner earnings (NI + D&A – capex) have fallen for two straight years to $11.6B. Reported profits and cash profits have decoupled. That caps the score regardless of how good the P&L looks.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**The measurement problem:** P/FCF is 369x on trailing figures and meaningless. EV/EBITDA (17.4x) and core P/E (~32x) are the only usable multiples.

**Peer positioning — the right comps are hyperscalers, not the "Specialty Retail" sector (peer median P/E 21.8x, where AMZN looks +68% expensive against Home Depot and McDonald's; that comparison is invalid):**

| | P/E | EV/EBITDA | P/FCF |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AMZN** | 36.6x (core ~32x) | **17.4x** | n.m. (neg.) |
| MSFT | 29.7x | 19.4x | 59.4x |
| GOOGL | 31.8x | 22.9x | 57.5x |
| META | 30.0x | 17.4x | 39.4x |

AMZN is the **cheapest on EV/EBITDA** and mid-pack on P/E. On relative value it is not stretched. This is the strongest argument against a Sell.

**Intrinsic value — owner-earnings DCF (maintenance capex ≈ D&A, so growth capex is valued as a separate investment rather than expensed):**

| Scenario | OE base | Yr 1–5 growth | WACC | Terminal | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear (AI capex over-builds; ROIC compresses) | $75B | 8% fading | 10.0% | 3.0% | **~$103** |
| Base (AWS 25%+ for 3 yrs, ads 20%, retail HSD) | $85B | 12% → 8% | 9.5% | 4.0% | **~$196** |
| Bull (AWS 30%+ sustained, margins keep expanding) | $95B | 16% fading | 8.5% | 3.5% | **~$326** |

Probability-weighted (~25/50/25): **~$205–240**.

**Verdict on price:** at $262.45 the stock sits **at or modestly above** my probability-weighted intrinsic value. To earn my required 25% margin of safety I would need to buy near **$165–180**. Note the built-in DCF tool returned garbage (it read my 9.0% WACC as 900% and compounded a capex-depressed $7.7B FCF base), so the above is hand-built — treat it as my model, not vendor output.

**What the price implies:** roughly 12–14% core EPS growth for a decade *plus* a successful conversion of $220B/yr of capex into high-return AWS capacity. That is a reasonable expectation — not a discounted one.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

- **Andy Jassy (CEO, since 2021), Bezos as Executive Chair.** Founder-anchored, long-horizon, and management has been directionally honest: the 2022–23 cost reset (headcount, fulfillment footprint) was decisive and produced the margin expansion now visible. *(I did not pull the DEF 14A this pass, so I am not quoting precise insider ownership or compensation ratios.)*
- **Capital allocation — the crux of the whole thesis.** The historical record is excellent: AWS was built counter-cyclically into a ~$169B annualized, ~39%-operating-margin business. But the current commitment of **$220B in a single year** — roughly 8% of market cap, and an amount that exceeds the entire market cap of most S&P 500 companies — is the largest single capital-allocation bet in the company's history, made against demand visibility management itself says "won't meet demand through 2027." That is either the greatest second act in corporate history or a classic late-cycle capex bubble. I cannot tell you which, and neither can they.
- **Shareholder returns: none.** No dividend, no buybacks since 2022. Share count has drifted from 10.0B (2020) to 10.83B diluted — ~1.6%/yr dilution. Tolerable, but shareholders are being asked to fund the capex program *and* absorb dilution while receiving zero cash return.
- **Grade: B+.** High integrity, strong operating execution, but capital discipline is currently unproven at this scale. Watch the 2027–28 depreciation roll-forward: D&A should climb from ~$80B toward $150B+. If operating income doesn't outgrow that, the whole model breaks.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

*Buy zone: below ~$185. Do not initiate at $262.*

This is a genuinely great business — wide moat, honest management, expanding margins, fortress balance sheet, the best cloud franchise on earth — trading at **full price with zero margin of safety**, at the exact moment it has stopped converting earnings into cash.

The discipline that matters here is not "is Amazon a good company" (obviously yes) but "what am I being paid for the risk?" At $262 the answer is: nothing. The sell-side median target of ~$350 assumes AWS acceleration to 38%+ continues into 2027 and the capex wave converts at high returns. My base case says that outcome gets you to roughly $326; the bear case says $103. When the bull case offers ~24% upside and the bear case offers ~61% downside, the asymmetry is not in a value investor's favor.

I would be a **buyer** on either (a) a pullback into the $165–185 range, which would price in the bear case nearly in full, or (b) evidence that the capex is converting — specifically, FCF turning positive for four consecutive quarters while AWS holds 30%+ growth.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **The capex/depreciation whipsaw (highest severity).** $173B of TTM capex against $75B of D&A means a depreciation wave of $70–90B *per year* is coming by 2028. AI accelerators depreciate over 5–6 years and face rapid obsolescence. If AWS growth decelerates to 15% while D&A doubles, operating margins compress violently from the current 11–12%.
2. **Negative FCF is now actual, not theoretical.** TTM FCF –$11.6B, and $220B of 2026 guidance implies deeply negative FCF for the full year. Any financing-market tightening or AI-capex sentiment reversal hits this stock hardest, because there is no cash return to anchor the price.
3. **Concentration risk in the profit pool.** ~60% of operating income now comes from AWS. A single segment carries the valuation. Regulatory action — the FTC Prime settlement and ongoing antitrust matters (the UBS note in the target data flagged a $200 target in that context) — or a cloud price war with MSFT/GOOGL would hit the crown jewel directly.
4. **Earnings quality optics.** The Q2-2026 headline EPS of $5.75 vs $1.82 consensus was roughly 70% non-operating investment gains. Expect the reported P/E to look deceptively low for several quarters; normalize before valuing.
5. **AI demand concentration.** A large and growing share of AWS growth is AI workloads from a small number of model developers — many of whom Amazon has itself financed via equity stakes (the same stakes generating those revaluation gains). That is circular revenue with correlated risk: if AI capex at the customer layer freezes, both the AWS revenue *and* the investment gains unwind together.
6. **Consumer cyclicality.** Beta 1.46; retail segment is exposed to a consumer slowdown, and tariffs/higher memory costs (the stated reason for the $20B capex increase) squeeze both COGS and capex simultaneously.

---

**Bottom line:** Wide-moat compounder, 7/10 financial quality, B+ management, fair-to-full valuation. **Hold** — and honestly not a value investor's purchase at this price. The 20–30% discount to intrinsic value that justifies committing capital simply isn't there.
