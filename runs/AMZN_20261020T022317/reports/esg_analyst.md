### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

**Carbon performance — deteriorating, not improving.** FY2025 sustainability report (published 1 Jul 2026): absolute emissions **+16% YoY**, with Scope 1 +2%, Scope 2 (purchased electricity) **+34%**, Scope 3 **+20%**. Split: S1 19% / S2 5% / S3 76%. Carbon intensity +3% but still 38% below the 2019 baseline against 156% revenue growth. Amazon added **more data-centre capacity in 2025 than any company globally** (>1.2 GW in Q4 alone) and guides AI/cloud to keep growing.

- **Net-zero-by-2040 target is now structurally off-track.** Absolute emissions are rising for a third year while the pledge is reaffirmed — this is the core greenwashing/credibility exposure, and it is why the 2026 data-centre-climate reporting proposal drew **18.4% support**, the highest of any Big Tech proposal per Glass Lewis.
- **Scope 2 quality caveat.** "100% renewable matched" for the third year, but matching is annual and global, not hourly/regionally matched — so reported Scope 2 emissions rise despite the claim. Expect regulatory scrutiny of market-based accounting (EU CSRD ESRS E1 requires location-based disclosure as well).
- **Mitigants (genuine).** 1.14 global PUE (improved from 1.15); X-energy/AEP 960MW SMR project in Indiana with a 5 GW Xe-100 ambition and Amazon as anchor offtaker; earlier Talen/Susquehanna nuclear PPA; 33 of 35 materials/ag sourcing goals met; carbon per unit shipped −7%.
- **Physical risk: Medium.** Fulfilment and logistics network exposed to flood/heat/storm; data centres concentrate water stress in specific basins (75% of the water-replenishment goal achieved, i.e. a shortfall).

**Estimated carbon-cost exposure:** ~80 MtCO₂e at a $50–100/t shadow price ⇒ **$4–8bn/yr** of theoretical transition cost, currently largely unmonetised. Not near-term cash, but it is the vector by which ESG becomes a P&L item for AWS.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Worker safety.** Senate HELP Committee investigation found Amazon warehouse injuries at **nearly twice the rate** of comparable warehouses (2023 data), with 34,000+ serious injuries in a year; OSHA ergonomic citations followed. Independent analysis puts Amazon's 2024 total injury rate ~80% above its own 2025 target. This is a recurring, quantifiable cost (self-insured workers' comp, absenteeism, wage premium) plus regulatory tail risk (OSHA egregious-case referrals).
- **Labour relations — escalating in 2026.** Teamsters are organising DSP delivery drivers and pressing NYC's Delivery Protection Act; NLRB charges of retaliation via DSP contract cancellations are live. In **October 2026 Amazon publicly threatened to cut thousands of NYC jobs if a Mamdani-backed labour bill passes** — an unusually aggressive stance that raises reputational and political risk and invites retaliatory legislation in other cities. Directly or indirectly ~12,000 NYC hourly workers.
- **Consumer protection.** FTC's **$2.5bn Prime settlement (Sep-2025)** — $1bn civil penalty + up to $1.5bn in consumer redress, with additional payouts announced Sep-2026 — establishes Amazon as the reference defendant for dark-pattern/negative-option enforcement (US click-to-cancel, EU Digital Fairness Act).
- **Third-party marketplace product safety / counterfeit** and **privacy (Ring, Alexa)** remain live but lower-velocity issues.
- **Positives:** scale of entry-level job creation, $15+ minimum wage since 2018, upskilling spend, and a workforce that is the largest single private employer in the US — politically durable but politically exposed.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**Supports the score:**
- **Single class of shares** — no dual-class structure; one share, one vote. Genuinely better than Alphabet/Meta and rare among founder-controlled mega-caps.
- Majority-independent board with a designated **lead independent director** and standing Audit, Leadership Development & Compensation, and Nominating/Governance committees.
- Clean audit relationship (EY ratified), no auditor controversy, robust disclosure (proxy runs 84 pages; separate sustainability report with methodology).

**Detracts:**
- **Founder as Executive Chairman.** Bezos remains Executive Chairman with a large personal stake and outsized informal influence over a CEO he appointed — a de facto CEO/Chairman concentration even though the formal roles are split. A **mandatory independent chair** proposal was on the 2026 ballot (rejected).
- **78% say-on-pay support** in 2025 is materially below the ~90% market norm — a real signal of investor discomfort with Jassy's equity-heavy package set against a period of heavy AI capex and negative free cash flow.
- **All four 2026 shareholder proposals were rejected decisively** (some by >99% of votes cast), and the highest-supported climate proposal reached only 18.4%; ~78% support for the board's slate indicates investors are currently deferring to management on ESG. That is a governance *choice*, not an absence of risk.
- **Antitrust/regulatory posture** counts against governance quality: the FTC case survived partial dismissal, and the EU has Amazon designated a DMA gatekeeper with an open anti-discounting/price-parity investigation. Repeated regulatory conflict is a governance-quality indicator.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD/ESRS** — in scope via large EU subsidiaries (Amazon EU S.à r.l. et al.); note the 2025 Omnibus "stop-the-clock"/threshold changes delay and narrow Wave 2/3, so timing is uncertain. Highest-quality disclosure obligation Amazon faces, and the one most likely to force location-based Scope 2 and value-chain data.
- **US SEC climate rule** — adopted 2024, stayed, and effectively abandoned by the SEC in 2025. **Near-term US federal disclosure risk is now low**; this is a genuine reduction in the regulatory burden versus 2024 expectations.
- **California SB 253/261** — in scope (>$1bn revenue doing business in CA); Scope 1/2 and Scope 3 reporting obligations begin 2026–2027. The binding US climate-disclosure regime now lives at state level.
- **EU Digital Markets Act** (gatekeeper: Amazon Marketplace & Ads) and **Digital Services Act** — the most financially consequential regulatory regimes for AMZN, arguably more than any climate rule.
- **EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR)** — operator obligations on palm oil, leather, cocoa, coffee, rubber, wood in private brands and Whole Foods; 94% certified palm oil in NA private brands (not 100%) leaves a compliance gap.
- **EU Packaging & Packaging Waste Regulation / WEEE / battery rules** — EPR fees scaling with packaging volume; partially offset by per-unit packaging improvements.
- **CBAM** — immaterial direct exposure.
- **US FTC negative-option / dark-pattern enforcement** — case-by-case post-vacatur of the click-to-cancel rule; Amazon remains the named precedent.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants** — no evidence of sustainability-linked bond KPIs in the capital structure; Amazon funds via straight debt. **Low exposure.**

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

**Justification.** Structurally, Amazon is a governance *positive outlier* on share class and disclosure, and its renewable procurement and nuclear offtake are best-in-class in ambition. But the two factors that actually move cash flows — **antitrust/license to operate** and **absolute emissions from AI-driven data-centre buildout** — are both deteriorating, and its **social/labour record is Laggard-calibre** on objective third-party injury data. Third-party raters typically place Amazon in the *high ESG-risk* band (driven by governance/antitrust and labour), which is consistent with this assessment. It is not an ESG "Leader": absolute emissions rising three years running while reaffirming a 2040 net-zero pledge is incompatible with that label.

**Direction of travel: Neutral-to-deteriorating.** Environmental credibility is the weakest link in 2026; governance is stable; social risk is rising because of the NYC confrontation and Teamsters momentum.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (slightly negative for ESG-mandated capital, neutral-to-positive for the generalist thesis)

- **For the core investment thesis, ESG is not the swing factor.** The material ESG risks here — antitrust and power/capex for AI — are already fully inside the bull/bear debate as idiosyncratic risks (Morgan Stanley's $350 target, AWS re-acceleration to 37%). ESG analysis does not add a new negative; it reframes two known risks.
- **Concentration risk to monitor:** ~$2.5bn of realised regulatory cost in 12 months plus an open monopoly case is the single largest ESG-derived threat to terminal value. Any adverse remedies ruling (2027 window) is a step-change event, not a gradual one.
- **Cost-of-capital channel is real but narrow.** ESG-constrained mandates (Article 8/9 funds, Nordic/European pension capital) will underweight on the labour and emissions record. At $2.83T market cap and index weight, exclusion-driven de-rating is not a credible mechanism; marginal demand effects only.
- **Watch items that would flip this to Negative:** (1) EU DMA penalty or mandated marketplace divestiture; (2) a Scope 2 restatement or regulatory challenge to Amazon's market-based renewable accounting; (3) a national warehouse-safety standard or sustained Teamsters contract wins at DSP scale; (4) emissions growth continuing past 2027 with no credible path to the 2040 pledge, inviting an ESG-labelled fund outflow narrative.
- **Watch items that would flip this to Positive:** hourly-matched carbon-free energy procurement at scale (the SMR programme is the candidate), a first DSP collective bargaining agreement that de-risks the labour overhang, or an independent-chair adoption signalling genuine governance maturation.

**Bottom line:** Amazon is an ESG-*average* company with ESG-*high* risk concentrated in two places — antitrust and energy/emissions — both of which are capex- and regulation-driven rather than ideology-driven. Own it on fundamentals; do not own it as an ESG holding, and do not treat its ESG profile as a reason to avoid it.
