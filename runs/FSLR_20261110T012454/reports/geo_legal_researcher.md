## FSLR — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company:** First Solar, Inc. (Tempe, AZ; ~7,900 employees; mkt cap ~$20.6B; beta 1.71). CdTe thin-film module manufacturer, not a silicon/polysilicon player — a structurally important distinction for trade and sanctions exposure. Latest filing in hand: 10-Q for period ended 9/30/2026 (filed 10/29/2026).

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

FY2025 net sales were **$5.2B**, of which **96% was generated in the United States**. FY2026 guidance was cut to $4.6–4.9B (from $4.9–5.2B) at Q3, implying *further* US concentration.

| Country/Region | Revenue exposure | Asset/operational exposure | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | ~96% of net sales | OH, AL, LA manufacturing; ~17 GW nameplate by 2027 | **High** (policy-concentrated, not instability) |
| **India** | ~$196M FY25 (~3.8%); 2027 output guided 2.2–2.6 GW | Tamil Nadu plant | **Medium-High** |
| **Malaysia** | De minimis/third-party | 1.8 GW plant — management flagged a "binary decision" on its future (Q3 call) | **Medium-High** (stranded-asset risk) |
| **Vietnam** | De minimis | Underutilized; production being relocated to US | **Medium** |
| **Japan / France / Canada / Australia** | <1% combined | Legacy project/EPC tail | **Low** |
| **China** | ~0% revenue | None directly; primary competitor bloc and litigation counterparty | **Medium** (indirect) |

**Key point:** this is not a conventional EM/geopolitical story. FSLR is a **single-jurisdiction policy arbitrage**: earnings derive from US federal manufacturing credits (45X) plus tariff-protected pricing. Geographic *diversification risk* is low; geographic *concentration risk* is extreme.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US trade-policy execution (Section 232 / AD-CVD). Probability: High. Impact: High.**
The August 2026 Section 232 proclamation imposes a **15% ad valorem tariff on polysilicon and derivatives with minimum import prices ($21/kg polysilicon; ~$0.22/W modules), effective December 4, 2026**. FSLR is a *net beneficiary* (non-silicon, domestic capacity) and has publicly pursued higher US module prices post-232. But the transition is already hurting: Q3 net sales fell 23% y/y to $1.23B and FY26 volume guidance was cut to 16–17 GW because **customers deferred decisions pending tariff clarity**. A tariff regime that is good for FSLR's *price* is currently bad for its *volume* — and the Dec 4 start date is a live catalyst in both directions. Political backdrop shifted materially: Democrats swept both chambers on Nov 3, 2026, sparking a 9%+ one-day rally — but Section 232 is executive-branch action already signed, so legislative change does not unwind it quickly.

**Risk 2 — Subsidy dependence and FEOC/OBBBA durability. Probability: Medium-High. Impact: Very High.**
Section 45X credits contribute roughly **$0.17/W** and, per sell-side work, constitute **most of current profit** — gross margins in the ~58% range (Q3 2026) would compress toward high-single/low-double digits without them. OBBBA preserved 45X with phase-down beginning 2030, but imposed **foreign-entity-of-concern material-assistance thresholds of 50% in 2026 rising to 85% by 2030** (IRS Notice 2026-15, January 2026). Rising domestic-content thresholds create a ratchet: each step requires supply-chain re-tracing (glass, frames, junction boxes, trace elements) and carries **credit recapture/disallowance risk**. Any adverse IRS guidance or a future fiscal-consolidation push is the single largest value risk in the name.

**Risk 3 — Southeast Asia footprint rationalization and India trade friction. Probability: High. Impact: Medium.**
FSLR idled/underutilized Malaysian and Vietnamese capacity while relocating to the US; Malaysia's 1.8 GW plant faces a pending "binary decision" (retain/divest/close). Separately, **Commerce imposed steep tariffs on solar imports from India in February 2026** (part of the India/Indonesia/Laos AD-CVD actions), which constrains the export optionality of the Tamil Nadu plant and leaves it dependent on Indian domestic-content-rule demand. This is the exact fact pattern alleged in the securities litigation below — management's handling of this transition is now a legal, not just operational, issue.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action (active, material).** Class period **Feb 26, 2025 – Feb 24, 2026**; defendants include CEO Mark Widmar and CFO Alexander Bradley; lead-plaintiff deadline Aug 24, 2026 (now expired, case proceeding). Allegations: FSLR **overstated its ability to manage US tariff policy** and **understated the 2026 earnings impact of underutilizing Malaysia/Vietnam facilities** while relocating production to the US. Triggering disclosures: **−10.29% on Jan 7, 2026** (Jefferies downgrade on international facility underutilization) and **−13.61% on Feb 25, 2026** on weak FY26 guidance. *Assessment:* the theory is unusually well-corroborated by public guidance cuts; settlement probability is meaningful, but quantum is small relative to a $20B market cap.
- **Swift Current Energy warranty litigation (new, 2026).** Developer suit over module warranty/remediation. Widmar: "We will stand by and we will support under the parameters of our limited liability warranty… Where people are asking for remedies that are outside the parameters of the warranty," the company will contest. *Assessment:* watch for warranty-reserve build and precedent for other developers; low near-term dollars, moderate reputational/contractual read-across.
- **TOPCon patent enforcement — FSLR as plaintiff.** Section 337 investigation instituted **March 30, 2026** at the ITC on FSLR's complaint; **voluntarily terminated ~September 2026** in favor of direct district-court actions. On **October 1–5, 2026**, FSLR sued **JA Solar (and four affiliates) and American Panel Solutions (a Corning subsidiary)** in D. Del. for infringement of **US Patent 9,130,074** (TOPCon tunnel-oxide passivation; acquired via TetraSun in 2013, protection through 2030+), seeking damages and permanent injunctive relief. Prior campaigns targeted **JinkoSolar, Canadian Solar, Trina Solar, and T1 Energy**. *Assessment:* offensive IP is a moat-defense lever over Chinese silicon imports; counter-risk is PTAB invalidity challenges and retaliation.
- **Regulatory posture.** No DOJ/SEC/FCPA enforcement action or government investigation is publicly known. **Gap noted:** the latest 10-K's Item 3 (Legal Proceedings) and geographic revenue note could not be retrieved directly — the filing tool returned only the 10-Q Exhibit 31.1 certification, so the 96%/India split is sourced from the FY2025 10-K as indexed by SEC search results and secondary aggregation, not read in full text.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **45X credit substantiation and FEOC tracing** — the highest-probability compliance issue. Credits are the profit pool; documentation of cost-ratio compliance across a global supply chain is auditable and recapturable.
2. **UFLPA / forced-labor traceability** — a *relative* strength (CdTe, non-Chinese polysilicon chain), but Malaysian/Vietnamese assembly and any Chinese-sourced BoS components (glass, frames, junction boxes) remain detention-exposed at the border.
3. **Transferable tax credit sales** — FSLR has monetized IRA credits via transfer (e.g., $857M in Feb 2025); buyer indemnity/repurchase exposure on disallowed credits is a contingent liability to monitor.
4. **FCPA/anti-corruption** — India and Southeast Asian operations, utility-scale counterparties; no disclosed issues, standard risk tier.
5. **Data privacy** — immaterial to the business model.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Legal risk alone is **Medium** (one mid-sized securities class action, warranty suit, and offensive IP campaigns — none threatening solvency). The **High** overall rating is driven by geopolitical/regulatory concentration: **~96% of revenue and essentially all economic profit sit inside a single policy regime** whose two支柱 — Section 232 tariff rents and 45X manufacturing credits — are both subject to administrative reinterpretation, with a December 4, 2026 implementation cliff and FEOC thresholds ratcheting to 85% by 2030. The company's own guidance cut and the pending Malaysia "binary decision" demonstrate the risk is already crystallizing, not hypothetical. Mitigants: no China revenue, a non-silicon supply chain that is insulated from polysilicon sanctions, no nationalization/currency-control exposure, and no disclosed FCPA or sanctions violations.
