# First Solar (FSLR) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $191.61 | Market Cap ~$20.6B | Nov 2026**

## 1. Growth Story

First Solar is the only fully-integrated utility-scale solar manufacturer in the world with zero dependency on the Chinese crystalline-silicon supply chain — it deposits cadmium-telluride (CdTe) semiconductor films on glass, a fundamentally different process from the polysilicon incumbents. Section 232, effective **December 4, 2026**, imposes a **$0.38/W minimum import price + 15% tariff** on imported modules and a $21/kg tariff on polysilicon — meaning FSLR's nearest legal import competitor must charge roughly **$0.44/W** while FSLR books U.S. volume at **$0.35–0.36/W**. That is an 18–20% structural cost advantage handed to them by policy, converting a commodity module business into a protected-margin platform play. The second, longer-dated leg is a genuine technology wedge: CuRe (up to 8% more lifetime energy yield vs. TOPCon) and a perovskite-tandem pilot line in the Series 6 form factor — the industry's most credible path to a step-function efficiency jump on non-silicon substrates.

The tension: revenue is *contracting* in 2026 (−8%) while EPS grows 24%. This is a margin-mix story in a volume-shrink year, not yet a re-accelerating growth story.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

- **U.S. utility-scale module demand:** ~50–60 GW/year, growing with datacenter/AI load growth. At $0.38–0.44/W effective protected pricing, that is a **$19–26B/year** U.S. SAM — and FSLR should be able to defend 30%+ share of it as the only tariff-advantaged domestic integrated producer.
- **Current penetration:** 17.0 GW 2026 volume guidance against a >650 GW global module market — under 3% globally, but ~30% of the U.S. utility segment where the economics actually work for them.
- **Backlog:** **44.0 GW** contracted, **~$14.4B** value, extending through 2030. Critically, **41 of 45.1 GW** (Q2) require domestic content — under 45X/OBBBA domestic-content rules, FSLR is one of very few suppliers who can satisfy that. This is a regulatory moat, not just a cost moat.
- **Adjacent TAM expansion:** hyperscaler/datacenter procurement (e.g., Steel River) is a genuinely new, price-insensitive demand pocket.
- **U.S. nameplate:** ~25 GW across Perrysburg/Lake Township OH, Decatur AL, New Iberia LA, and Gaffney SC (Phase 1 online H2'26, Phase 2 mid-2027 with CuRe) — plus ~6 GW SE Asia, of which 1.8 GW is deliberately furloughed ($30M/qtr drag).

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Dec 4, 2026 — Section 232 takes effect.** FSLR's ASP ($0.35–0.36/W) sits *below* the $0.38/W import floor. Immediate ASP-expansion headroom plus a 1.8 GW idled-capacity restart decision. Highest-impact, days away.
- **Late Feb 2027 — Q4'26 + 2027 guidance.** The single most important datapoint: does backlog stabilize? Trajectory is 54.5 → 50.1 → 47.9 → 45.1 → **44.0 GW** — four straight declines, but the *rate* of decline decelerated sharply (−2.8 GW, then −1.1 GW). ~2 GW under conditions precedent plus ~2 GW "highly likely" to close by year-end.
- **H1 2027 — Perovskite-tandem pilot line** begins operation in Series 6 form factor. Binary technology re-rating event.
- **Mid-2027 — Gaffney SC Phase 2** with CuRe integration; CuRe price adjusters reaching customers.
- **Ongoing — $500M buyback execution** into a stock 40% off its June 2026 high ($318).

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (high conviction)

Buy, not Strong Buy, because the one thing my framework weights heaviest — durable *revenue* growth — is negative in 2026 and the backlog leading indicator is still pointing down.

Everything else screams mispricing. A company compounding EPS at 24% with a 57% gross margin quarter, a $1.2B FCF run-rate, $1.5B net cash, a first-ever buyback, and a policy-mandated price umbrella is trading at **8.3x 2027E earnings and roughly 6.1x 2029E**. A PEG of 0.45 for a business with 41+ GW of domestic-content-qualified backlog is not a valuation — it's a policy-fear discount. The market is implying essentially no long-term growth; my TAM work says 14%+ revenue CAGR and 24% EPS CAGR through 2029 is achievable *without* assuming any perovskite success and *without* ASP expansion above $0.38/W.

The asymmetric part: the $0.38/W import floor went into force days ago and the sell-side PTs have not been marked to it. If Q4 bookings confirm backlog stabilization, this re-rates toward the $253–259 median (≈+33%) quickly; a perovskite proof-point in H1'27 could take it past the $328 high. Downside is cushioned by net cash, positive FCF, and 2.16x book.

**Upgrade trigger to Strong Buy:** Q4'26 backlog ≥44 GW with U.S. ASPs at or above $0.36/W.
**Downgrade trigger to Hold:** another quarter of backlog declines *combined with* sub-$0.34/W bookings, or 45X phase-down language worse than current statute.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Policy dependency is existential, not incidental.** Section 45X manufacturing credits are the single largest driver of profitability — the Q1'26 deck cited a $330–400M range, and gross margin swings from 2.7% (2022) to 57% (2026) are overwhelmingly credit-driven, not operational. OBBBA already restructured these credits; any accelerated phase-down, FEOC/PFE reinterpretation, or a legal challenge could compress earnings by more than half. FSLR is effectively a levered bet on U.S. industrial policy.
2. **Backlog erosion.** 54.5 GW → 44.0 GW over four quarters. Management calls it "deliberate selectivity" — plausible given unsettled policy, but a growth investor must treat a shrinking contracted book as a leading negative until disproven.
3. **Section 232 cuts both ways.** It protects output pricing but raises FSLR's *input* costs — aluminum, steel, copper, and electricity all face new tariff pressure, plus $21/kg polysilicon. Q2's 57% gross margin included an **$89M one-time IEEPA tariff refund** that will not recur.
4. **Competitive threat.** Global c-Si overcapacity remains enormous. If the tariff regime softens or exclusions broaden, FSLR's CdTe cost position is not competitive on a $/W basis globally — its entire moat is geographic. Domestic c-Si rivals (Qcells, Silfab, Heliene) are also scaling U.S. capacity.
5. **Concentration and input scarcity.** Tellurium supply limits CdTe scaling; revenue is concentrated in a single market and a single technology. Perovskite success is unproven at commercial volume.
6. **Extreme volatility.** Beta 1.71. Drawdowns of −31% (June 2025), −20% (Jan 2026), −33% (June–Sept 2026) — all headline-driven. Position sizing matters as much as direction.
