## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE (on metrics); effectively net-cash, unrated**

The balance sheet is the strongest part of this story and is essentially immune to the operating cycle. FSLR carries **virtually no debt**: total debt of $325.0M against $10.88B of equity and $1.62B of cash plus short-term investments. Net debt is **negative $1.25B** — the company is in a net cash position of roughly 6% of market cap.

Debt has been retired aggressively: total debt fell from $718.8M (FY24) → $498.6M (FY25) → $325.0M (Q3'26), with **long-term debt now at zero**. There is no refinancing cliff and no covenant risk of consequence. Interest expense has collapsed to $2.4M in Q3'26 (~$27M LTM), against EBIT of $1.96B — **interest coverage of ~73x**.

Critically, **no public credit rating or CDS spread was available in the data** (data gap). On fundamentals alone the profile warrants investment-grade treatment. The correct framing for FSLR is that **credit risk is not the risk** — the debt is a rounding error. The risk sits in the equity and in the policy-linked earnings stream (Section 4).

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue −20%

**Modelling assumptions:** COGS TTM $2,544.6M, of which D&A is fixed at $583.9M → variable COGS = **39.1% of revenue**. SG&A/other held broadly fixed at ~$512M. Interest $27M.

| Line item | Base (LTM) | **Rev −20%** | Rev −40% | Rev −50% (severe) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $5,014M | $4,011M | $3,008M | $2,507M |
| Gross margin | 49.3% | 46.3% | 44.5% | 43.6% |
| **EBIT** | $1,958M | **$1,347M** | $736M | $431M |
| EBIT margin | 39.1% | 33.6% | 24.5% | 17.2% |
| **EBITDA** | $2,542M | **$1,931M** | $1,320M | $1,015M |
| **Interest coverage** | 72.9x | **50.1x** | 27.4x | 16.0x |
| Net debt / EBITDA | net cash | **net cash** | net cash | net cash |

**Findings:**
- A 20% revenue decline cuts EBIT by **31%** (to $1.35B) but leaves interest coverage at **50x**. The company remains solidly profitable and net-cash.
- Even a **50% revenue collapse** leaves $1.0B of EBITDA and 16x coverage. **Solvency is not remotely at risk in any realistic operating scenario.**
- The downside hits *equity value*, not *credit*. At 7.6x EV/EBITDA and 11x LTM P/E, a 20% revenue shock with margin compression would plausibly take 35–45% off the equity — consistent with the observed 53% max drawdown.

**⚠️ This scenario is partially LIVE, not hypothetical.** Q3'26 revenue of $1,231M was **−22.8% YoY** vs Q3'25's $1,595M. LTM revenue ($5,014M) is already *below* FY2025 ($5,219M). Management has flagged "underutilization costs of $110–120M" and contracting caution around Section 232/301.

**The 45X stress (the scenario that actually matters):** In Q3'26, **net income ($571.1M) exceeded EBIT ($568.4M)** — the tax line was a *net benefit*. FY2025's effective tax rate was **1.6%** vs a 21% statutory rate. Estimated value of Section 45X credits flowing through the tax line: roughly **$300–570M annually, or 20–30% of net income**. Strip this out and normalized net income is ~$1.2–1.3B rather than $1.86B — meaning the "cheap" 11x P/E is closer to **15–17x on normalized earnings**. This is the single largest unquantified risk in the model, and its magnitude is a **flagged estimate** — the company does not break out the credit split in the data available to me.

**Verdict:** The balance sheet scores as a fortress; the income statement scores as a leveraged bet on US industrial policy. A prolonged demand downturn is survivable with room to spare — the company could fund itself out of net cash for years. What is *not* survivable is a hostile policy reset (45X repeal/phase-down without grandfathering, adverse Section 232 or AD/CVD outcomes), which would compress both earnings and the multiple simultaneously. **The company passes the survival test decisively; it does not pass the earnings-durability test.**

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Cash burn inflection.** Cash + ST investments fell **$2,855M → $1,624M (−43%)** in nine months. 9M26 operating cash flow was **−$403M** and FCF **−$837M**, driven by a **$2.2B working-capital drain** (Q2'26 alone: −$933M) while inventory rose only modestly and receivables were flat — pointing to contract-asset/45X-receivable build. Simple runway at the current ~$280M/quarter burn is ~5.8 quarters, but the burn is WC-driven and reversible (FY25 produced +$1.19B FCF). **Watch item, not a distress signal.**
2. **Revenue already declining.** Q3'26 −22.8% YoY; LTM revenue now below FY2025. This is a shrink in the top line while margins hit records — historically an unsustainable mix.
3. **Earnings quality / credit dependency.** Net income exceeding EBIT in Q3'26 and a 1.6% effective tax rate confirm that reported profitability is materially subsidised. Normalized P/E ~15–17x, not 11x.
4. **Binary policy exposure.** Section 232, Section 301, AD/CVD and FEOC rules are single-headline events capable of −10% to −14% single-day moves (observed twice in 2026). Guidance is explicitly contingent on unresolved determinations.
5. **Customer concentration + $1.88B receivables.** Backlog is ~44–45 GW (~$13.5B) contracted through 2030 — supportive, but concentrated in a handful of utility-scale developers whose own credit is rate-sensitive.
6. **Terrible risk-adjusted returns.** Sharpe 0.125 on 57% vol; a 53% max drawdown means position sizing, not fundamentals, will drive most investors' outcomes.
7. **FCF yield of 5.76% is stale.** It is computed on FY2025's $1,187M FCF. LTM FCF is now negative. Do not underwrite this stock on that metric.
