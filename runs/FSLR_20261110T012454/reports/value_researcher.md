## First Solar, Inc. (FSLR) — Value Analysis
**Price $191.61 (close 11/09/26) | Mkt cap $20.6B | Shares 107.5M | Net cash ~$2.5–3.0B | EV ~$17.6B**

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW (and it is a *policy* moat, not a commercial one)**

First Solar is the largest US thin-film (CdTe) module maker: 22.1 GW nameplate, ~98% US utilization, vertically integrated with no dependence on Chinese crystalline-silicon supply chains. That is a genuine cost and supply-security advantage. But the profit pool is not primarily earned in a competitive market — it is granted by statute:

| Evidence | Figure |
|---|---|
| FY2026 guidance revenue / EBITDA | $4.6–4.9B / $2.55–2.75B |
| FY2026 assumed Section 45X credits | **~$2.1–2.2B** |
| Q4 2024 disclosure: government grants reducing COGS | **$396M** (COGS fell from $1.38B → $984M; grants = 70% of that quarter's gross profit) |
| Q3 2026 58% gross margin, attributed by mgmt to | "more favorable mix of US-made modules **eligible for 45X**" |

Strip out 45X and FY2026 EBITDA is roughly **$0.4–0.6B**, not $2.65B. The Q4'24 datapoint — an ~9% ex-credit gross margin — is the clearest proof that the underlying module business is a commodity with thin economics and no pricing power of its own. The *real* moat is the tariff wall: Section 232 polysilicon import restrictions and a 15% minimum import price effective 12/4/26, plus AD/CVD, which lift the landed cost of Chinese c-Si modules and let FSLR book US ASPs of ~$0.36/W against costs near $0.20/W.

**Has the moat widened over five years?** Revenue compounded 14% (FY20–FY25) and ROIC went from ~negative to 15.9% — on the surface, yes. But the path included a **2.7% gross margin and a net loss in 2022**, which is exactly what a *non*-moated commodity business looks like in a down-cycle. 45X for solar steps down to 75%/50%/25% for components sold in 2030/2031/2032 and **reaches zero in 2033**. The moat is being legislated away on a known schedule.

### 3. Valuation Analysis — intrinsic value ≈ **$135–145; the price already discounts the bull case**

The stock is optically cheap — 11.1x TTM EPS ($17.28), 6.6x FY26E EBITDA, 1.9x book, 13.5x FY25 EPS, vs. a sector-peer median P/E of 21x. **This cheapness is correct, not a mispricing.** The earnings being capitalized expire by statute. A 45X annuity must be valued as a **declining finite stream**, not a perpetuity at a market multiple.

- **Base case** (terminal ex-credit FCF $0.8B on Section 232-protected economics): **~$135/share**
- **Bull case** (25 GW output, new US bookings hold ≥$0.36/W, credit fully monetized, $1.2B terminal FCF): **~$192/share**

**What the price implies:** ~$155/share of value must come from *post-2032, post-subsidy* cash flows — i.e., the market is assuming First Solar permanently earns tariff-protected, 2033-and-beyond margins that it has never yet demonstrated without a government check.

### 4. Management Quality — **Good, not great**

- **CEO Mark Widmar** (since 2016) has executed a genuine technology/manufacturing strategy: Series 6 → Series 7, CuRe, perovskite R&D (~$120M/yr), CubicPV patent license. Building 22 GW of non-China capacity was strategically correct.
- **Capital allocation is the weak spot.** Share count has been frozen at ~107M for years — no dilution (creditable) — but essentially **zero buybacks until the $500M authorization in October 2026** (2.4% of cap) despite sitting on $2.3B+ of net cash for years. The buyback is well-timed (after a 33% drawdown from the December 2025 high of $284.59) but immaterial. **No dividend.**
- **Communication:** reducing FY26 shipments to 16–17 GW and revenue to $4.6–4.9B *while* raising profit expectations is honest, mix-driven guidance — a positive signal.
- **Insider ownership is low** (founder-era stake long gone); this is a professional-manager company, not an owner-operator one.
- **M&A discipline:** clean — no empire building, no goodwill ($82M of intangibles on a $13.3B balance sheet).
