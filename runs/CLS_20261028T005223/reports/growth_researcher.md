# Celestica Inc. (CLS) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $276.54 (27-Oct-2026 close) · Market cap ≈$34.8B · EV ≈$35.1B · Net cash-ish · Beta 1.46*

---

## 1. Growth Story

Celestica has stopped being an electronics manufacturing service (EMS) contractor and has become the merchant **AI infrastructure engine** of the hyperscaler buildout — designing and building the 800G/1.6T Ethernet switches, custom AI compute platforms, and rack-scale systems that sit between the GPU and the cluster. Connectivity & Cloud Solutions (CCS) is now **84% of revenue and grew 102% YoY** in Q3 2026, and management has guided revenue from $12.4B (2025) to **$21.0B (2026) to $40.0B (2027)** — a ~80% two-year CAGR that is, remarkably, accelerating. The stock is nonetheless **~41% below its June 2026 peak ($472)** because the market is repositioning from "AI growth" to "AI capital intensity" — exactly the kind of fundamentals-versus-price dislocation a growth investor should be buying.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

- **TAM:** Hyperscaler + neocloud capex on AI data centers is running in the **$600B+ range annually in 2026** and is guided higher for 2027 by every major spender. The addressable slice for a merchant ODM — high-speed switching/routing fabrics, custom accelerator boards and systems, storage and rack integration — is the fastest-growing cut of that spend as clusters scale from tens of thousands to hundreds of thousands of GPUs.
- **Penetration:** Celestica's $40B 2027 revenue is roughly **5–6% of total industry capex** — low share-of-wallet given CCS is the #1/#2 merchant AI networking supplier. Two structural tailwinds extend the runway: (1) the **800G → 1.6T** speed transition, which forces another full platform refresh cycle with higher content per port; (2) hyperscalers' shift toward **custom silicon (in-house accelerators)**, which favors merchant design-and-build partners over incumbent OEM brands.
- **Differentiation:** Celestica is neither a chip vendor nor a box-shifter — it co-designs programs with hyperscalers and owns the manufacturing NPI-to-volume ramp. That combination (design services + 23,800 employees + global footprint + supply chain leverage) is a narrow but real moat. It is a *moat of execution*, not of IP, which caps how much of the value it can retain.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Customer concentration (the big one).** ~84% of revenue is CCS, and that segment is a handful of hyperscalers. A single program loss, re-sourcing decision, or in-house insourcing move would be catastrophic to a company with 12% gross margins. Concentration is not disclosed in the data I could pull — *this is the gap I would close before sizing up*.
2. **Capital intensity / cash conversion.** 2027 capex of $1.5–2.0B against $460M of 2026 FCF. If the revenue ramp slips even one quarter, Celestica is carrying idle capacity and likely raises debt or equity — the fastest way for this multiple to stay compressed.
3. **Margin ceiling.** 9.0% adjusted operating margin is the 2027 target — +50bps. Hyperscalers with $600B+ of buying power should be assumed to capture most of the scale benefit. Revenue also contains pass-through component content (optics, memory), which inflates the top line without commensurate profit — so "90% growth" overstates the economic growth.
4. **AI capex digestion risk.** The $40B 2027 guide rests on customer forecasts, not contracts. Management says 2028 forecasts are "meaningfully higher," but if hyperscaler capex plateaus in 2028, this becomes a 1x-sales business with falling estimates simultaneously — a brutal double.
5. **Execution at 3x scale.** Tripling revenue in two years requires hiring, qualifying new US lines, and securing constrained components (optics, HBM, power). Any bottleneck delays revenue recognition rather than deferring it — but the market will price it as a miss.
6. **Peer read-through.** FLEX and JBL are chasing the same sockets with more balance sheet. Share gains are not permanently locked in.

**Bottom line:** the fundamental engine (69% → 90% growth, expanding margins, conservative guidance, 23% upward revisions) is running far ahead of the share price, which has de-rated 41% on capital-intensity fears rather than demand fears. That is the definition of a growth dislocation. **Strong Buy**, with the concentration and cash-conversion questions as the monitoring items.
