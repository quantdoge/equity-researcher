## CELESTICA INC. (NYSE/TSX: CLS) — ESG ANALYSIS

**Company context:** Toronto-headquartered EMS/ODM (Tech Hardware). FY2024 revenue ~$9.65B → Q3'26 revenue $5.83B (+83% y/y) with a FY2027 outlook near $40B; market cap ~$34.8B; beta 1.46; ~27,800 employees. Two segments: Connectivity & Cloud Solutions (hyperscaler AI switches/servers) and Advanced Technology Solutions (aero/defense, industrial, healthtech). *This is a low-margin, labour-intensive assembler whose ESG risk profile is driven almost entirely by its supply chain and customer concentration — not by its own smokestacks.*

---

### 1. ESG Materiality Map — where ESG actually hits cash flow

| Rank | Factor | Materiality | Why it is financial |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Scope 3 / purchased goods carbon** | **High** | ~93% of footprint; hyperscaler customers impose Scope 3 reduction requirements as a condition of award |
| 2 | **Supply-chain forced labour** (Malaysia, Thailand, Mexico, China) | **High (tail)** | UFLPA/CBP withhold-release + customer code-of-conduct termination risk on concentrated revenue |
| 3 | **Export controls / AI regulatory perimeter** | **High** | Cited in FY2025 10-K risk factors; restricts addressable revenue and adds compliance cost |
| 4 | **Human capital & wage structure** | **Medium** | 27,800 staff, median pay $13,855; rapid headcount scaling outruns audit/training coverage |
| 5 | **Product regulation (EU CRA, Ecodesign for servers)** | **Medium** | Redesign + conformity-assessment cost against an 8.5% operating margin |
| 6 | **Board/compensation governance** | **Medium** | CEO assumed Chair in 2026 during a period of aggressive capital deployment |
| 7 | **Direct carbon cost (Scope 1+2 = 23.5 kt)** | **Low** | At $100/t = ~$2.4M. Genuinely immaterial — do not over-weight |
| 8 | **Water, waste, biodiversity** | **Low** | 88% waste diversion; EMAS-certified Valencia site |

**Key structural insight:** Scope 3 is ~1.4 Mt vs Scope 1+2 of just 23.5 kt. Celestica's environmental risk is a *procurement and customer-requirement* problem, not an operations problem. Consequently, a carbon tax does almost nothing to this P&L; a customer's supplier-emissions mandate does a great deal.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

| Metric (FY2024, third-party verified) | Value |
|---|---|
| Scope 1 | 3.9 kt CO₂e |
| Scope 2 (market-based) | 19.6 kt CO₂e |
| Scope 3 | ~1,400 kt CO₂e |
| **Total** | **~1,500 kt CO₂e** |

**Absolute emissions trajectory — the core finding: 725 kt (2020) → 657 → 798 → 1,200 (2023) → 1,500 (2024). Roughly a doubling in two years, and on my estimate ~3–4x by FY2027 if the $40B revenue outlook is achieved at constant intensity (~155 tCO₂e per $1M revenue).**

- **SBTi-validated Scope 3 target will be missed badly.** The commitment was a 10% absolute cut by 2025 vs 2018 across purchased goods & services, fuel/energy-related and upstream transport. Independent trackers classify it **"Behind target."** Scope 3 has instead roughly doubled. The 30% Scope 1+2 cut by 2025 vs 2018 is reported on track (and is achievable precisely because the base is trivially small).
- **Credibility/greenwashing exposure:** the company still markets itself as "carbon neutral since 2017" — an offset-based Scope 1+2 claim. **EU Directive (EU) 2024/825 bans generic "carbon neutral" claims resting on offsetting from 27 September 2026.** A Canadian issuer using this language in EU-facing marketing/customer materials carries a real compliance and NGO-attack vector — inexpensive to fix, expensive to be caught on.
- **Genuine strengths:** CDP climate score **A- in 2024 (up from B)**; GHG statements independently verified 2021–2024; GRI Core reporting; disclosure since 2009; 88% waste diversion; IBM Envizi-based measurement. *Disclosure quality is top-quartile for EMS; absolute performance is not.*
- **Peer reality check:** third-party climate scoring places Celestica at ~41 vs an industry average of 30, but well below Jabil at 77 and Compal at 90 — i.e., above-average disclosure, below-average decarbonisation among large EMS/ODM peers.
- **Physical climate risk (Medium):** dense manufacturing in coastal China/Thailand/Malaysia plus Taiwan-concentrated semiconductor inputs; water-stress exposure at certain sites (company reports on water security).
- **No environmental fines or remediation liabilities identified** in searches (see §5 gap note).

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (with a High-severity tail)

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Workforce safety | Adequate — TRIR and NMFR tracked and disclosed; no serious incidents surfaced |
| Labour standards / forced labour | **Structural exposure.** Migrant-worker-heavy footprint across Malaysia, Thailand, Mexico, China; UN experts again flagged "widespread and systematic" migrant exploitation in Malaysian manufacturing (Nov 2025) |
| Supply chain diligence | Real programmes exist: RBA Code supporter, supplier assessments and verification visits, ethical sourcing/conflict minerals, annual Business Conduct training with certification, modern slavery statement (FY2025), global ethics hotline |
| Diversity | Board and inclusion programmes disclosed; no granular pay-gap data located |
| Product safety | Low — enterprise hardware |
| Data security | Medium — manufactures hyperscaler IP; CIO reports security posture to the Board |

**The material asymmetry:** 96%+ of Celestica's revenue comes from a handful of large customers who each run their own supplier responsibility audits. A Tier-1 forced-labour finding — in a Celestica site *or* in its component supply chain — triggers the customer's code-of-conduct enforcement machinery, not merely adverse publicity. Because revenue is concentrated in a few hyperscaler relationships, a single disqualification is a multi-billion-dollar revenue event, not a rounding error. Mitigant: the RBA/MSR infrastructure is genuinely in place, which lowers probability even as severity stays high.

**Wage-structure issue:** CEO-to-median-employee pay ratio of **1,257:1** on median pay of **$13,855**. For an investor this is less an equity about fairness than about (a) wage-inflation and turnover sensitivity in a labour-intensive model, and (b) reputational ammunition in labour-dispute or tariff-driven-restructuring scenarios.

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Single class of shares — no dual-class structure or super-voting rights | **CEO/Chair combination newly created in 2026** — Mionis moved from President & CEO into Chair immediately before the 2026 AGM; prior chair retired |
| Majority voting policy for directors; advance-notice bylaw (no poison pill) | Mitigant for the above is a Lead Independent Director (Koellner), but combining the roles at the start of a bold capital-deployment phase weakens the check on management |
| All three committees (Audit, HR & Comp, Nom & Gov) constituted to exchange requirements | **Koellner received 92.60% support** — the lowest of the elected slate and a visible dissent signal; overboarding questions are fair given other board seats |
| **96.2% say-on-pay support** — strong shareholder endorsement | **1,257:1 pay ratio**; $17.4M CEO package (+16% y/y) vs $13,855 median |
| 92% of CEO target pay at-risk, 71% in equity — genuine pay-for-performance | **No ESG/sustainability modifier identified** in the incentive design reviewed — sustainability targets sit outside the compensation contract |
| Board expanded to 9 with a semiconductor executive added — relevant industry expertise | 2026 is a governance *regression* year, not an improvement year, on the dimension (independence of chair) that matters most |

**Rationale for 6.0:** Structurally clean (single class, majority voting, independent committees, high say-on-pay) but the deliberate re-combination of CEO and Chair — at the exact moment the company is committing to a ~2x revenue expansion — offsets the structural strengths. Legacy litigation (In re Celestica Securities Litigation, ~$30M settlement; earlier SEC/OSC inquiries, all resolved) is historical and not a current governance discount.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

| Jurisdiction | Rule | Exposure | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **EU** | **CSRD/ESRS** | EU manufacturing (Ireland, Romania, Spain — EMAS-certified Valencia) likely puts subsidiaries in scope; even where the parent is not, EU hyperscaler customers will demand ESRS-grade data | **High — the single most demanding obligation**, and the one Celestica's rising Scope 3 makes hardest to satisfy |
| **EU** | **Ecodesign for servers & data storage (Reg. 2024/1364)** | Energy-efficiency, reparability, recycled-content and information requirements for the exact products Celestica builds | **Medium-High** — direct redesign cost |
| **EU** | **Cyber Resilience Act** (obligations from 2027) | Cybersecurity-by-design, conformity assessment, vulnerability reporting for hardware placed on the EU market | **Medium** — engineering + documentation burden |
| **EU** | **Empowering Consumers Directive (2024/825)** | Bans offset-based "carbon neutral" claims from 27 Sep 2026 | **Medium, easy to fix** |
| **EU** | CBAM | Aluminium/steel in racks, chassis, enclosures | **Low** |
| **US** | SEC climate rule | Stayed | Low |
| **US** | **California SB 253 / SB 261** | Celestica almost certainly exceeds the $1B "doing business in California" threshold → Scope 1–3 disclosure and climate financial-risk reporting | **Medium-High**, timing in flux |
| **US** | **UFLPA** | Forced-labour import rebuttable presumption across the whole supply chain | **High severity, low probability** |
| **US** | **BIS AI export controls** | Explicitly flagged in the FY2025 10-K as a risk that could restrict customers or raise their costs | **High** — revenue-perimeter risk |
| **Canada** | CSA climate rule | **Paused April 2025** — no near-term mandatory Canadian climate rule | Reduces near-term home-jurisdiction burden |
| **Debt** | ESG-linked facilities | None identified | Neutral |

**Net:** Celestica's regulatory exposure is **EU-weighted and product-weighted**, not carbon-tax-weighted. The compliance bill is manageable in dollars (tens of millions against $20B+ revenue) but lands on an 8.5% operating margin and on a product roadmap that must be re-engineered for energy and reparability criteria.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (disclosure Leader, performance Laggard)

The tension is the whole story. On *process and transparency* Celestica is better than most EMS peers: CDP A-, four years of independently verified GHG statements, GRI Core, RBA participation, modern slavery statement, single-class shares, 96% say-on-pay. On *outcomes* it is going backwards: absolute emissions have roughly doubled in two years, the SBTi-validated Scope 3 target will be missed, and a "carbon neutral" claim sits on the marketing page while the footprint triples from here.

Not a Laggard — there is real machinery and real disclosure. Not close to Leader — the trajectory is wrong and the governance trend in 2026 is negative. **Average, with deteriorating momentum.**

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (with an asymmetric tail)

**Why neutral rather than negative:** none of the material ESG factors currently touch the cash flow statement. Direct carbon cost is ~$2M. Compliance spend is immaterial to a $35B market cap. There is no stranded-asset risk, no environmental liability, no active controversy. An ESG screen would neither force nor forbid ownership — and the market is not pricing any of this.

**Why not positive:** the two things that *could* bite are both tail events with high severity and no balance-sheet cushion:
1. **A forced-labour finding** in a Celestica site or its component chain → customer code-of-conduct enforcement against a concentrated hyperscaler base → plausibly **$1–3B+ of revenue at risk**, which against Q3'26's 8.5% adjusted operating margin is a thesis-breaking, not thesis-trimming, event.
2. **Export-control tightening** on AI hardware shipments — already disclosed as a 10-K risk to the customer base that is generating the entire growth story.

**The subtle risk the market is missing:** Celestica's valuation re-rate rests entirely on hyperscaler relationships — and hyperscalers are precisely the counterparties with the most aggressive supplier-emissions and human-rights procurement gating. Celestica's Scope 3 has doubled while its SBTi target says it should have fallen 10%. That gap is now a *commercial* liability, not a *reputational* one: procurement scorecards in the AI supply chain increasingly reward verified Scope 3 reduction, and Celestica's disclosure — excellent as it is — documents failure rather than progress. A company that reports beautifully on a badly deteriorating trajectory is in a more exposed position than one that reports badly, because the evidence is on the record.

**Recommendation to the investment committee:** treat ESG as **non-differentiating for the long thesis, but a monitorable tail**. Two specific catalysts to track: (i) the 2025 Sustainability Report's treatment of the missed Scope 3 target (re-basing it quietly would be a governance tell; retiring the "carbon neutral" claim would be a positive tell), and (ii) any customer- or regulator-driven supply-chain audit disclosure. Neither changes the base case today. Both would change it quickly if they broke.
