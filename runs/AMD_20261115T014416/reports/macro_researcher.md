# AMD — Macroeconomic Environment Assessment
**As of 15 November 2026 | Data through 13 Nov 2026 | Sources: FRED, FOMC, S&P Global, Goldman Sachs, company filings**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional, tilting risk-off at the duration end**

The US economy is in a **late-cycle nominal boom with a re-accelerating inflation problem** — the most hostile possible policy mix for a $876bn, 2.49-beta, long-duration semiconductor equity.

Three forces define the regime:

- **Growth is not decelerating.** Q3 2026 real GDP +3.4% (Q2 +2.2%, Q1 +2.5%). Unemployment has *fallen* to 4.1% from 4.5% a year ago. Nominal PCE is running +6.3% y/y — the economy is running hot, not cooling.
- **The Fed has restarted hiking.** After cutting through 2025, the FOMC raised the target range 25bp to 3.75–4.00% on 16 Sept 2026 (effective FF 3.88%). It skipped October. **Nearly all economists polled by Reuters expect another hike in December**, Governor Waller has said a December hike is likely and opened the door to 50bp, and 16 of 18 committee members see at least one more hike with rates held elevated until 2028.
- **The bond market is bear-steepening on real yields, not inflation expectations.** 10Y nominal is 5.13% and 30Y 5.47%. Since 1 June, the 10Y has risen 69bp — but the **10Y TIPS real yield has risen 79bp (2.07% → 2.86%) while 10Y breakevens have *fallen* 16bp (2.40% → 2.24%)**. This is a term-premium / fiscal-risk / real-rate shock. That is the single most damaging type of rate move for equities whose value sits in the terminal value.

**The offset:** credit markets show no stress at all. HY OAS is **3.20%** — among the tightest prints on record. VIX 18.7 after a transient spike to 28.2 on 5 Nov. There is no recession signal anywhere in credit or labour. This is not a cycle-end; it is a **discount-rate problem inside a demand boom**.

**Stance: transitional — risk-on in credit and earnings, risk-off in duration.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**POSITIVE**

- **Growth & nominal spending.** AMD's PC (Ryzen) and embedded/industrial franchises are levered to a 3.4%-GDP, 4.1%-unemployment economy. Enterprise server refresh and commercial PC demand are cyclical positives; a "no landing" economy is the best case for AMD's non-AI segments.
- **AI capex supercycle.** Goldman sees hyperscaler capex +~50% to $1.2tn in 2027; S&P Global >$1.3tn by 2027 at ~3% of GDP versus 1.4% in 2025. This is the dominant driver of AMD's Instinct/EPYC data-centre ramp and swamps every other variable in magnitude. Recent Google Cloud host-infrastructure wins are evidence the spend is broadening beyond Nvidia.
- **Credit conditions remain wide open.** At 3.20% HY OAS, the debt financing that increasingly funds hyperscaler capex (S&P already flags negative free operating cash flow across the hyperscalers) is still cheap and available. Tight spreads are the *enabler* of AMD's revenue line.
- **Section 232 / Chips Act domestic build-out** is a modest structural positive for supply-chain resilience and pricing, even as the tariff itself is a cost.

**NEGATIVE**

- **Real yields at 2.86% are the core threat — not to earnings, to the multiple.** AMD is the purest long-duration asset in large-cap tech: $876bn market cap, most of its value in 2029+ cash flows. Its **2.49 beta** and realised volatility make it the index's de-rating proxy. A 50bp real-yield shock typically costs 8–12% on names priced at 40x+. AMD's all-time high of $658.52 on **6 October** coincided almost exactly with the 10Y's cycle peak of 5.31% on **5 October** — then fell 38.6% to $402 by 5 November. Macro did not cause that fall, but it set the ceiling and the amplification.
- **December hike risk.** A hike — particularly a 50bp move — would lift the front end above 4.0% while the 30Y presses 5.5%+, tightening the vice on duration.
- **Rising cost of financing the AI capex boom.** This is the critical nexus. The 2027–28 capex ramp was underwritten in a 4.4% 10Y world; it now faces 5.1%. If HY OAS widens from 3.2% toward 4.5%, hyperscalers re-underwrite capex plans and AMD's data-centre revenue trajectory takes a direct hit. The tight spread is the load-bearing assumption — watch it weekly.
- **Strong dollar.** The broad USD is up 3.4% in two months. AMD bills a majority of revenue outside the US through global ODMs and distributors — a translation headwind that compounds with the tariff regime.
- **25% Section 232 tariff on imported advanced logic.** AMD is fabless; all leading-edge wafers come from TSMC in Taiwan, so substantially all US-bound product is an import subject to the duty. This is a structural gross-margin cost and a supply-chain friction, partially offset by pricing power in a supply-constrained AI market.
- **Input-cost inflation.** At 6% 3-mo inflation, HBM, CoWoS advanced packaging and substrate costs are rising. AMD's pricing power in data centre offsets much of this, but consumer GPU/PC segments have thinner cover.
- **Policy-driven China ceiling.** Export controls on MI308 produced an **$800m inventory charge in Q2 2026** and a ~$360m Q4'25 benefit from licensing changes — a policy variable, not a macro one, but it caps a large addressable market and adds binary headline risk.

**NEUTRAL**

- **Yield-curve shape.** At +39bp the curve is positively sloped — no inversion, no classic recession signal. This is a bear steepener (term premium), which hurts long-duration equities without the growth warning that historically justified owning them.
- **VIX at 18.7** — the 5 November spike to 28.2 was AI-complex idiosyncratic (Nvidia leadership turmoil, AI-trade rethink), not macro. Index-level vol is contained.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL — with a distinctly unfavourable skew**

**Rationale for not going to outright Unfavourable:** AMD's *earnings* are exposed to the strongest part of the macro picture — 3.4% GDP, a tight labour market, capex growing ~50% into 2027, and the tightest credit spreads on record. On fundamentals the macro is a tailwind, and that tailwind is why the stock is up **116% in twelve months** despite a 69bp rise in the 10Y.

**Rationale for the unfavourable skew:** AMD's *valuation* is exposed to the worst part. A Fed restarting hikes into 6% momentum inflation, real yields at 2.86%, a bear-steepening long end, a 3.4% dollar rally in two months and a 25% import tariff is the maximum-headwind combination for a high-multiple, high-beta, mostly-non-US-revenue, fabless-dependency semiconductor. There is no valuation cushion: the multiple cannot absorb another leg higher in real yields. The risk is asymmetric — upside is capped by the discount rate, downside is not.

## 5. The Material Surprise Scenario (Bear)

**A December 50bp hike that cracks the AI-capex financing model.**

Waller has explicitly opened the door to a half-point move. Imagine the December FOMC delivers 50bp on the back of a fifth consecutive hot CPI print, the 30Y breaks 6% on deficit/term-premium concerns, and the 10Y real yield clears 3.25%. The immediate effect is a mechanical de-rating of duration — painful but survivable.

The second-round effect is what matters. The 2027 AI capex plan is ~$1.2–1.3tn and, per S&P Global, is being funded with negative free operating cash flow — i.e. it is **increasingly debt-financed at the long end of the curve**. In that world HY OAS does not stay at 3.20%; it moves to 4.5–5.0%. Hyperscalers then re-underwrite 2027–28 orders, AMD's data-centre backlog slips a year, and the equity takes a **double hit: lower forward revenue *and* a lower multiple**. From $534, a de-rate to a low-30s forward multiple on trimmed estimates is a **35–45% drawdown** — roughly $290–350. The November 5 intraday move to $402 (−13.6%, a 38.6% drawdown from the October high inside a month) was a preview of this mechanics at one-quarter scale.

**Triggers to monitor weekly, in priority order:** (1) HY OAS — a break above 3.75% is the early warning; (2) 10Y TIPS real yield above 3.10%; (3) the December FOMC size; (4) hyperscaler capex guidance revisions on Q4 calls; (5) broad USD above 125.

**Bull counter-scenario (lower probability, ~30%):** the Oct 2026 CPI is the peak, December delivers a hawkish 25bp and a pause, real yields retrace to 2.3%, and AMD re-rates to $700+ on 2028 capex guidance of $1.5tn. This is the consensus-adjacent path and therefore the one with less surprise value — but it is the live upside risk, and the tightness of credit spreads says the market assigns it meaningful odds today.
