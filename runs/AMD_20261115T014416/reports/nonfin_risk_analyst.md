# AMD — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**As of 14 Nov 2026 | Price $534.06 | Mkt cap ~$876B | Beta 2.49 | 31,000 employees**

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE** (with structural blemishes)

**Concentration of power — flagged.** Dr. Lisa Su is simultaneously **Chair, President and CEO** (confirmed in the Q3'26 Section 302 certification signed "Chair, President and Chief Executive Officer"). AMD has offsetting balance: **Nora Denzel serves as independent Lead Director** — a genuine mitigant, and the board was refreshed via an 19 Aug 2026 8-K announcement of new appointments.

**But oversight separation is thinning:** in August 2026 Denzel, *while serving as Lead Independent Director*, was also placed on the **Audit and Finance Committee**. The lead independent director sitting on audit — the committee responsible for policing management's financial reporting — blurs the independence firewall. Governance purists should score this against AMD.

**Customer equity warrants — the most underappreciated governance item.** Per AMD's own 8-K and OpenAI's release, AMD issued OpenAI a warrant for **up to 160 million shares at $0.01**, vesting in tranches tied to GPU purchase milestones and (per secondary reports) stock-price thresholds near $600. Secondary sources report a **similar 160M-share warrant to a second hyperscaler**, implying up to ~20% combined potential dilution *(this second warrant is reported but unconfirmed — treat as a lead to verify, not a fact)*. Shareholders never voted on transferring a low-single-digit-to-high-single-digit percentage of the company to customers. This is functionally a **revenue-for-equity swap** and raises a legitimate revenue-quality question: how much of AMD's 97% Data Center growth is being *purchased* with stock?

**Compensation/alignment:** Executive pay is heavily equity-weighted and Su retains ~2.87M shares (~$1.5B) after sales — real alignment. No related-party transactions, no restatements, no material weakness disclosed; SOX certifications are clean and unqualified.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **SEC/DOJ enforcement: none identified.** EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned **zero** actions for AMD. No active SEC investigation, no securities class action surfaced. This is the single strongest data point in the file.
- **Export controls — the dominant regulatory risk.** AMD's April 2025 disclosure flagged up to **$800M of charges** tied to MI308 China restrictions; MI308 licensing resumed and generated ~**$390M** of China revenue in Q4 2025. Critically, AMD agreed in **August 2025 to remit 15% of China AI-chip revenue to the U.S. government** in exchange for licenses. This is a *pay-to-export* arrangement — legally unusual and politically radioactive.
- **Contagion risk is now acute.** NVIDIA's CEO resigned 12 Nov 2026 amid allegations of a spy-linked relationship and **illegal diversion of high-performance AI chips to China**; NVIDIA's board opened an independent investigation, and **Congress and the Senate (Warren) have launched inquiries** touching Commerce Secretary Lutnick's role in approving AI-chip sales. Every U.S. AI-chip vendor's China distribution channel is now under de facto scrutiny. AMD's 15% revenue-share deal sits directly in the blast radius — expect hearings, possible renegotiation, and heightened BIS enforcement attention on AMD's China channel.
- **Antitrust/IP:** no live action found. (Historical: the ZT Systems acquisition required a manufacturing divestiture — resolved, not current.)
- **Pending approvals:** none material identified. **Data privacy:** low direct consumer exposure; diluted by ZT Systems, Silo AI and ROCm software expansion.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Single-point foundry and packaging dependency.** Leading-edge N2 wafer supply and CoWoS advanced packaging are concentrated at TSMC, with acute Taiwan geopolitical exposure. AMD must compete with a larger rival for allocation *while* simultaneously ramping MI450/MI455X and "Venice" — guidance explicitly depends on "scale Helios and 'Venice' production" in Q4. An allocation shortfall is not a margin event; it is a revenue-existence event.
2. **Systems-integration execution risk + customer concentration.** AMD is converting from chip vendor to rack-scale systems supplier (Helios), adding manufacturing, test and logistics complexity (ZT Systems). Demand is now concentrated in a handful of named counterparties — OpenAI (up to 6 GW), Anthropic (up to 2 GW), Meta, Microsoft, Oracle, Google. Losing or delaying any single one, or a counterparty financing shock, is a step-function revenue risk. Reports of swelling inventory and a thin shipping record warrant close monitoring of **inventory and receivables growth vs. revenue** for channel-stuffing signals.
3. **Key-person dependency on Lisa Su.** Strategy, the marquee hyperscaler relationships and the equity-warrant architecture are personally identified with Su — in the same quarter she was named CEO of the Year and received the National Medal of Science. Post-NVIDIA, the U.S. AI-chip CEO role is the most politically exposed job in the market. There is no disclosed succession plan and no credible internal bench.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Paradoxically strong, but fragile**

AMD is currently the **beneficiary** of the sector's reputational crisis — the "clean second source" as NVIDIA reels. It posted its best five-day relative performance vs. NVIDIA since 1999 (+19% in the week to 13 Nov), Google selected EPYC for TPU host infrastructure, and sell-side ratings remain 33 Buy / 10 Hold / 1 Sell (avg PT $640).

The fragility is in the tape: **−17% on 5 Nov** (from $488.78 to $404.40) despite a genuine record quarter ($13.4B revenue, +45% y/y, Data Center +97%, GM 54%) and **−8.75% on 13 Nov**. A stock up 116% in twelve months with beta 2.49 and a $649 peak now carries embedded expectation risk that no operational metric justifies on its own. Three specific reputational tripwires: **(a)** any leak placing AMD's China channel inside the diversion probe would instantly invert the "clean" narrative; **(b)** the 15%-of-China-revenue-to-the-government arrangement is the kind of structure that attracts hostile press and NGO attention; **(c)** the customer-warrant scheme invites a "buying revenue with stock" critique that could taint reported growth.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Upgraded from Low by five factors: Chair/CEO duality, $239M+ of CEO selling with zero insider buying, extreme hyperscaler customer concentration, a single-source TSMC dependency, and the political contagion now radiating from NVIDIA. It does **not** reach High because there is **no enforcement action, no investigation of AMD, no restatement, no material weakness, no litigation overhang**, and the board retains a functional independent lead director. Exposure is to *sentiment and policy shocks*, not to demonstrated misconduct.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **⚠️ HIGH — Customer equity warrants.** Up to 160M shares at $0.01 to OpenAI (8-K confirmed), plus a reported second hyperscaler warrant. Potential ~8–20% dilution, no shareholder vote, milestones tied to both purchases *and* stock price. **Escalate to accounting-quality and governance review:** test whether revenue growth is equity-subsidised and how the milestone structure could incentivise aggressive revenue recognition.
2. **⚠️ HIGH — Export-control / political contagion.** AMD's 15%-of-China-AI-revenue-to-U.S.-government arrangement now sits inside a live Congressional and Senate investigation of AI-chip China policy. Model a scenario where this arrangement is voided, retroactively challenged, or made a public hearing exhibit.
3. **⚠️ MEDIUM — Insider selling at the high.** ~$239M by the CEO plus a Lead Independent Director sale at $565 with no offsetting purchases. **Request 10b5-1 plan adoption and modification dates** — plan adoption shortly before favourable news is the tell worth checking.
4. **⚠️ MEDIUM — Board oversight architecture.** Chair/CEO duality plus the Lead Independent Director sitting on the Audit and Finance Committee. Raise in any proxy-season engagement.
5. **⚠️ WATCH — Revenue quality.** Monitor inventory and receivables vs. the +45% revenue trajectory; the systems-business conversion makes working-capital signals noisier and easier to mask.
