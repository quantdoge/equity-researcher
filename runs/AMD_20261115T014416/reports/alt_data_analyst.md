## 3. Earnings Preview Signal: **IN-LINE** (revenue), with asymmetric downside risk

**Tracking in-line — not above — and the alt data cannot corroborate the step-function.**

Supporting above-consensus: 100% beat rate (7/7), **+5.18% avg surprise**, revenue beats of +6.2%, +3.6%, +2.0%, +2.1% in the last four quarters; guide of $17B vs. consensus $17.09B leaves the usual conservative-guide cushion; AI-infrastructure hiring and a new EPYC cloud win.

Arguing against upside: the Q4 guide requires **+27% QoQ** — a demand inflection roughly 3x the sequential rate AMD has been delivering — and the only product-level demand data we have (EPYC, Ryzen) is at 12-month lows with negative YoY. **The entire incremental $3.7B is the Helios ramp, and alt data is structurally blind to it.** Analyst revisions are *mechanically* upward (post-guide), not independently confirming.

**Net:** the 100% beat rate and Helios ramp plausibly clear the guide, but with a stock at ~16x forward sales, an in-line print is a disappointment. Risk is skewed to the guide, not above it.

## 4. News Sentiment: **+0.556 (Positive)** — 20 articles, 7 positive / 2 negative

Aggregate score is positive but **lower quality than it looks**. The bullish coverage is overwhelmingly *relative* — "AMD Just Beat Nvidia by the Widest Margin Since 1999," "Harvard Cut Nvidia by a Third and Bought AMD," "AI confidence shock" rotation pieces. AMD is the beneficiary of a **competitor-specific shock (Nvidia's CEO scandal/Senate spotlight), not of its own demand inflecting.** That kind of bid is high-beta and reversible the moment Nvidia's situation stabilizes.

Negative threads worth flagging: **insider selling** (stock −8.8% Friday on the disclosure), **Michael Burry's bearish chip bets**, a widely circulated "chip stocks peak every two years — another top" cycle call, and valuation pushback ("20x revenue, P/E 150").

## 6. Overall Alt Data Signal: **NEUTRAL (bearish risk/reward tilt)**

The defining feature of this dataset is a **maximal divergence between attention and demand**: investor attention (brand search, retail buzz, news sentiment) sits at a 12-month high, while observable product-demand proxies (Ryzen, EPYC) sit at 12-month lows, and the one thing that actually matters (Helios) is unmeasurable. Add a stock at ~16x sales, an insider sale, and a bid built on a rival's scandal — and the setup is **fragile into a Feb 2 print that requires a 27% QoQ step-up.** The business signal is intact; the *entry point* is not attractive.

## 7. Confidence Level: **LOW**

Genuine gaps, stated plainly: **patent trend unavailable** (API failure ×2), **web traffic unavailable** (proxy returned wrong domains), **job postings qualitative only** (no historical baseline → direction yes, magnitude no), **social sentiment non-quantitative** (web-search proxy, not API), **MI450 search below reliability threshold**. Product-name Google Trends are also heavily distorted by an April–May 2026 event spike, making YoY comparisons noisier than the raw deltas suggest. Direction of the attention-vs-demand divergence is reasonably robust; **any magnitude estimate would be false precision.**

**Data lag note:** Google Trends is weekly and revised; news/sentiment covers trailing 14 days. All alt data is leading-indicator, not definitive — and here the highest-conviction element (Helios rack-scale demand) is precisely the one no alt source can see.
