# AMD — Full ESG Analysis
**Advanced Micro Devices, Inc. (NASDAQ: AMD) | Technology / Semiconductors (fabless) | US | Mkt cap ~$876B | FY2025 revenue $34.6B**

---

## 1. ESG Materiality Map

AMD is **fabless** — it owns no fabs. That single fact inverts the conventional semiconductor ESG map: direct operational emissions are immaterial relative to revenue, while supply-chain (Scope 3), product-use energy, human capital, and geopolitics/compliance dominate.

| Tier | Factor | Financial transmission channel | Materiality |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Critical** | **Export controls / geopolitics (China, Taiwan)** | Forced revenue loss, inventory write-downs, license conditions, 15% revenue-share levy | Critical |
| **1 — Critical** | **Scope 3 upstream (TSMC wafer, OSAT packaging)** | Supplier carbon costs, customer Scope 3 mandates (hyperscalers), CBAM-adjacent | High |
| **1 — Critical** | **Product energy efficiency (scope 3 use-phase)** | TCO/perf-per-watt is the core purchase criterion for hyperscalers — ESG *is* the product here | High (positive) |
| **2 — High** | **Human capital / talent retention** | AI-chip design talent scarcity; stock-based comp dilution | High |
| **2 — High** | **Responsible minerals / forced-labour due diligence** | UFLPA & EU CSDDD exposure; customer audit requirements | Medium-High |
| **3 — Moderate** | **Own operations energy/emissions (Scope 1–2)** | Small absolute footprint; rising fugitive emissions and Singapore grid exposure | Medium |
| **3 — Moderate** | **Governance: board leadership, comp, shareholder rights** | Cost of capital; proxy-advisor risk; equity dilution | Medium |
| **4 — Low** | Product safety, direct water stress, community relations | Minimal (fabless), limited to office/R&D sites | Low |

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Operational footprint (FY2025, fiscal year basis; selected data ISAE 3000 limited assurance):**

| Metric | 2020 | 2024 | 2025 | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total energy (GWh) | — | 264 | 299 | +13% |
| Energy / revenue (MWh per $M) | — | 10.3 | **8.6** | Improving |
| Electricity from renewables | — | 50% | **58%** | Improving |
| Scope 1 direct (mtCO₂e) | 6,412 | 12,419 | **13,586** | ⚠️ **+112% vs 2020** |
| Scope 2 market-based (mtCO₂e) | 55,342 | 31,771 | **29,386** | Improving |
| **Scope 1+2 total** | **61,754** | 44,190 | **42,972** | **−30% vs 2020** |

**Carbon intensity:** ~1.24 tCO₂e per $M revenue (vs ~6.3 in 2020) — a strong ~80% decline, driven by both renewables procurement and explosive revenue growth. Absolute emissions fell only 30% against a **50%-by-2030 target**, so AMD must roughly double its historical pace of reduction over the next five years.

**Two red flags inside the good headline:**
1. **Scope 1 is going the wrong way** (+112% since 2020). Fugitive emissions of 7,297 mtCO₂e are now **54% of all Scope 1** — i.e., refrigerant/PFAS-type leakage, not combustion. This is an engineering-fixable but under-managed category.
2. **Singapore concentration.** Singapore 1 site alone is 2,182 mtCO₂e Scope 1 (up from 261 in 2023) and 10,183 mtCO₂e Scope 2 — roughly a third of group Scope 2 — on a gas-heavy grid with no renewable sourcing recorded. Any incremental Singapore capacity expansion (test/packaging) is high-carbon-intensity growth.

**Supply chain:** 82% of manufacturing suppliers have public GHG-reduction goals vs a 100% (2020–25) target — **missed**; 80% renewable-energy sourcing — **met**; supply-chain carbon intensity down 8% vs a 25% (2024–30) goal — behind pace. AMD collects primary supplier data covering ~85% of spend and >70% of Scope 3 Cat 1, with spend-based estimation for the residual — better-than-peer transparency.

**Product (the offsetting positive):** 38x AI-training/HPC energy-efficiency improvement 2020–25 vs a 30x target — **exceeded**. This is AMD's single most valuable environmental attribute: perf-per-watt is a procurement criterion for hyperscalers and directly underwrites the data-center thesis.

**Physical climate risk:** Low-to-moderate and almost entirely *inherited* — concentrated in TSMC (Taiwan water stress/seismic) and Asian OSATs rather than AMD-owned assets.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Workforce ~31,000 FTE** but ESG reporting scope covers **>45,800** including temporary workers and contractors — implying a contingent workforce of roughly **30%+ of the reporting population**. That is a real labour-standards and flexibility-cost issue, and it also flatters per-employee metrics.
- **Attrition:** female voluntary turnover 4.3–4.8% (2025), which is low in absolute terms; women ~21% of the reported workforce population in disclosed tables. Representation has improved over five years but remains below software-peer levels — typical for semis, not a thesis-breaker.
- **Talent is the binding constraint, not cost.** AMD competes for AI silicon design talent against Nvidia and hyperscaler in-house teams; equity compensation is the retention tool, which links Social directly to the dilution/governance issue in §4 (the 2026 request for +65M shares).
- **Supply-chain labour:** AMD publishes a UK Modern Slavery Act statement and runs OECD-aligned minerals due diligence; the exposure is structural (Taiwan/SE Asia OSATs, Chinese substrate suppliers) rather than incident-specific. UFLPA and EU CSDDD raise the cost of weak traceability. No forced-labour enforcement action against AMD surfaced in this review.
- **Community/social licence:** Low salience — AMD is not a large landowner or water user.
- **Product safety/security:** Silicon-level vulnerabilities (e.g., the 2024 "SinkClose" CPU flaw) are a recurring but low-financial-impact category; PSIRT and patch cadence appear adequate.

**No material S controversies identified in this review.** The absence of a major labour or product-safety incident is a genuine positive relative to industrial semis peers.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.5 / 10 — Above average, with three specific deductions**

**Positives**
- **Single class of stock, one vote per share** (1.63B shares outstanding; no dual-class structure). This is a meaningful differentiator versus much of US tech and removes the largest structural governance discount.
- Annual, majority-vote director elections; **8 director nominees**, board majority independent with fully independent Audit, Compensation and Nominating/Governance committees.
- Board refreshed with semiconductor, software and finance expertise; dedicated risk-oversight disclosure.
- Say-on-Pay received **~85% support in 2025** — a pass, but below the ~90%+ that signals clean alignment.

**Deductions**
1. **Combined Chair/CEO.** Dr. Lisa Su serves simultaneously as **Chair, President and CEO**. For a company of this strategic and geopolitical complexity, the absence of an independent chair inverts best practice and is a standard ISS/Glass Lewis negative.
2. **Compensation scale and dilution.** Say-on-Pay at 85% (not 95%) reflects concern over magnitude of equity awards at a $500+ share price; the 2026 proxy seeks a **+65M share increase** to the 2023 Equity Incentive Plan (~4% of shares out) — material incremental dilution without a correspondingly explicit link to returns on capital.
3. **Shareholder rights.** Stockholders must hold **20%** of shares for one year to call a special meeting — well above the 10% market best practice. Management recommended **against** a 2026 stockholder proposal to lower the threshold to 10%, and the proposal failed. The annual meeting is **virtual-only**, reducing engagement quality.

**Unverified:** ISS Governance QualityScore, director-level over-boarding detail, and the 2026 Say-on-Pay outcome were not confirmed within this session's budget. Third-party ESG ratings (MSCI, Sustainalytics, CDP) could not be retrieved from primary sources — treat any figure quoted elsewhere in the memo as unconfirmed.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

AMD is a **structurally clean environmental story wrapped in an elevated geopolitical and governance risk profile**. Its Scope 1–2 footprint is trivial for a $34.6B-revenue manufacturer (~43k tCO₂e — roughly what a single mid-sized factory emits), it has no dual-class shares, no major labour or safety incident, and its product-energy-efficiency leadership (38x vs 30x target) is a genuine competitive asset rather than a marketing claim. Against that: Scope 1 has doubled since 2020, the 50%-by-2030 goal now requires a step-change, two supply-chain goals were missed, and governance carries three structural deductions (combined Chair/CEO, sub-90% Say-on-Pay, above-market special-meeting threshold). It is not a Laggard and does not warrant an ESG Risk flag — but neither is it a Leader. **Average, leaning solidly toward the better half of semis peers.**

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (with one material negative overlay already priced as geopolitical risk)

**Why neutral, not negative:**
- The E and S factors are **not cash-flow-relevant** to the thesis. No carbon cost, no remediation liability, no labour disruption, no product-safety recall. No ESG-driven increase in cost of capital is identifiable.
- Product energy efficiency is an **upside** factor misclassified as ESG: perf-per-watt is a hyperscaler procurement criterion, and the 38x achievement directly supports the Helios/MI-series data-center ramp. AMD's environmental story is a *sales* argument, not a compliance cost.

**The one place ESG becomes financial — and it is large:**
The China export-control regime and the 15% revenue-share levy are scored here under "regulatory ESG" but are, in practice, **the dominant idiosyncratic risk in the AMD thesis** (China ~15–27% of revenue; MI308 China alone ~$390M in Q4 2025; 2025 inventory charges reported at ~$800M). A framework that separates "ESG" from "geopolitics" would understate this. **Investors should treat AMD's China compliance exposure as a first-order valuation variable, not a footnote.**

**Governance/watch items for the next 12 months:**
1. **May 2026 Say-on-Pay result** and the size of the +65M share equity-plan dilution.
2. Whether AMD ever separates the **Chair and CEO** roles (would be a re-rating signal for governance-focused holders).
3. **Scope 1 trajectory** — fugitive emissions and Singapore Scope 2 need to bend, or the 2030 goal credibility erodes.
4. Any **shareholder litigation** arising from the revenue-share arrangement or AI-chip disclosure.

**Bottom line:** ESG is a **neutral, non-driver** for AMD's valuation. It neither adds a premium nor imposes a discount. The stock should be underwritten on AI compute share gains and the China/policy regime — and any ESG screen that excludes AMD would be discarding a company whose environmental profile is materially better than the average semiconductor manufacturer.
