# AMD — Advanced Micro Devices, Inc.
**Value Investment Analysis | Price $534.06 | Market Cap ~$876B | Date: mid-November 2026**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat Rating: NARROW** (and the *widening* part of the story is unproven)

AMD is two businesses wearing one ticker, and they deserve very different moat scores:

| Business | Moat | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **x86 CPUs (Ryzen / EPYC)** | **Narrow-to-Wide** | Genuine duopoly with Intel, protected by the x86 cross-license, ~$5B+ of annual R&D scale, and real switching costs once EPYC is designed into a hyperscaler rack. EPYC took meaningful server share from Intel on superior performance-per-watt. This is a real moat. |
| **AI accelerators (Instinct MI450)** | **None today** | NVIDIA's CUDA ecosystem is the moat — and AMD does not own it. ROCm remains a distant second. AMD is buying its way in: the OpenAI agreement includes **warrants for up to 160 million shares**, and a similar 6 GW structure with Meta. |
| **Gaming / semi-custom** | **Narrow** | Console sockets (Sony, Microsoft) are sticky but low-margin and cyclical. |

**The tell on pricing power:** a business with a durable moat does not hand a customer ~10% of its equity to win an order. AMD's AI GPU position is currently a **share-shifting commodity race funded with balance-sheet equity**, which is the opposite of Buffett-style pricing power. Gross margin has improved (49.4% FY25 → **53.9% in Q3 FY26**), but NVIDIA operates near 75%. That gap *is* the moat gap.

**ROIC vs. WACC — the disqualifying fact:** ROIC was **5.5% in FY2025, 3.2% in FY2024, 0.7% in FY2023**, against a WACC of roughly 10%. A moated business earns above its cost of capital *consistently*. AMD has not done so in any of the last four years. Excluding the $41.8B of Xilinx goodwill and intangibles, the operating business earns ~25% on tangible capital — but shareholders paid for that goodwill, so it counts.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**What's genuinely good:**
- **Revenue CAGR of 28.8%** over four years; FY2025 +34.3%, and FY2026 tracking to ~$52B (Q1 $10.25B → Q2 $11.54B → Q3 $13.38B → Q4E $17.1B). Accelerating, not decelerating.
- **Operating leverage is real:** operating margin 1.8% (FY23) → 7.4% (FY24) → 10.7% (FY25) → **21.2% in Q3 FY26**.
- **Balance sheet is pristine:** $10.55B cash vs. $4.47B debt = **net cash ~$1.07B**; current ratio 2.85x.
- FCF positive and rising: $1.12B (FY23) → $2.41B (FY24) → **$6.74B (FY25)**.

**What keeps this from being a great business:**
- **FCF yield of 0.77%** on trailing FCF — you are paid almost nothing for waiting.
- **Stock-based compensation of $1.64B** (38% of net income). Buybacks of $1.32B do *not* cover it — net share count is flat-to-rising. This is dilution dressed as capital return.
- **Inventory up 38% to $7.9B** and receivables at $6.3B — working capital consumed $2.4B of cash in FY25. That's a build-ahead bet on the AI ramp; if demand slips, it becomes write-downs.
- **$41.8B goodwill/intangibles = 66% of equity.** Tangible book value is ~$21B against an $876B market cap — **41x tangible book**.
- No dividend, ever.

| FCF Yield | **0.8%** | — | — |
| P/Tangible Book | **41x** | — | — |

**My DCF work** (I used my own model; the house DCF tool returned a corrupted discount rate):

- **Base case** — $10B FY26 FCF, +25%/yr for 5 years, +12%/yr years 6–10, 3% terminal, 10% WACC → **≈ $294/share**
- **Bull case** — $10B FCF, +35%/yr for 5 years, +15% for 5, 3.5% terminal, 9% WACC → **≈ $604/share**

**Cross-check from the top down:** assume AMD reaches **$100B revenue by FY2029** (roughly double today's run rate) at a superb **28% operating margin** → ~$28B EBIT → ~$22B FCF. Capitalize at 35x and discount back three years at 10% → **≈ $580B equity value, or ~$355/share.** Even the "AMD becomes a second NVIDIA" scenario does not clear today's price.

**What the price implies:** the market is paying **~88% of my bull case**. You are being asked to underwrite 35% FCF growth for a decade, flawless 6 GW+ AI execution, sustained ~54%+ gross margins, and no cyclical digestion — with no room for error. That is not an investment; it is an option premium.

**No margin of safety — negative margin of safety.** At $534 the stock is roughly **80% above my base-case intrinsic value** and only ~13% below my most optimistic case.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment: **Strong (A-)**

**Lisa Su is arguably the finest semiconductor CEO of her generation.** She inherited a company near insolvency and executed one of the great turnarounds in tech history: Zen architecture, EPYC server share gains, the disciplined Xilinx acquisition (which brought the $41.8B of goodwill — expensive, but strategically sound), and now the OpenAI/Meta anchor deals. Capital allocation has been shareholder-friendly in direction (buybacks, minimal dilution historically, net cash preserved).

**Three reservations:**
1. **The warrant structures are economically a transfer of equity to customers.** Strategically rational — you must buy your way into a two-vendor world — but it reveals AMD is the price-taker in AI accelerators, and it dilutes existing owners by potentially 10%+.
2. **Buybacks at 100x+ earnings are value-destructive.** $1.3B repurchased in FY25 at a fraction of intrinsic value is capital misallocation, however small in absolute terms.
3. **Insider ownership and alignment are adequate, not exceptional** for a founder-less, professionally managed company.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

**This is a good business at an extraordinary price — which is precisely the situation Buffett warns about.** "A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy"; this one trades at 2.7x my base-case intrinsic value.

The verdict is not a judgment on AMD's products or Lisa Su's execution — both are excellent, and revenue growth is genuinely accelerating. It is a judgment on **price paid versus value received**:

- **ROIC of 5.5% is below WACC.** The single most important Buffett test fails.
- **The AI moat is NVIDIA's, not AMD's.** AMD is a fast, well-run second source paying for share with equity.
- **81x forward earnings, 17x sales, 0.8% FCF yield, 41x tangible book.** Every metric demands perfection.
- **The stock is up ~165% in nine months** ($200 in Feb 2026 → $649 peak in Oct 2026) and fell **38% in a single month** (to $404 on Nov 5). Beta 2.49. This is a momentum instrument, not a compounding asset.

**For existing holders:** trim aggressively into strength. **For value investors:** do not initiate. Revisit below **$300** (my base-case intrinsic value), and only with a 20–30% margin of safety — i.e., **$210–240**, which is roughly where the stock traded in February 2026. A better entry may never come; that is an acceptable outcome.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks that would make it cheaper (i.e., support the Sell):**
1. **AI capex digestion.** Hyperscaler spending is cyclical. As the #2 vendor with the weakest ecosystem lock-in, AMD loses the most revenue and all of its pricing power in a pause. This is the dominant risk.
2. **CUDA/ROCm gap persists.** If ROCm does not close the software gap, AMD remains structurally a discount vendor — margin ceiling capped well below NVIDIA's.
3. **Customer concentration.** OpenAI, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle are the entire AI thesis. Any single deferral is a double-digit percentage hit to forward estimates.
4. **Valuation compression.** At 81x forward, a shift to 40x (still premium) is a **50% drawdown**. The market already demonstrated this in November.
5. **Warrant dilution** of up to 160M OpenAI shares plus Meta warrants — 10%+ of the share count, arriving exactly when earnings must compound.

**Risks to my Sell call (i.e., where I could be wrong):**
6. **Genuine operating leverage surprise.** Q3 FY26 gross margin of 53.9% and 21.2% operating margin came in ahead of expectations. If AI revenue scales to $100B+ by 2029 *at* NVIDIA-like margins, my bull case proves too conservative.
7. **Intel's continued stumble** hands AMD more CPU share at better prices, funding the AI build.
8. **A large, unexpected anchor customer** (e.g., a sovereign AI program) could reset the growth trajectory upward again.

**Structural risks:** TSMC/Taiwan concentration (single-point-of-failure for all leading-edge supply); inventory write-down risk on the $7.9B build; and the possibility that the AI accelerator market consolidates to one winner, as GPU markets historically have.

---

### Bottom Line
AMD is a **high-quality, well-managed, narrow-moat semiconductor company trading at a wide-moat-plus-flawless-execution price.** The business deserves respect; the valuation deserves refusal. **Sell / Avoid.** Fair value ≈ **$295/share**; only a price near **$210–240** would offer the margin of safety this framework requires.
