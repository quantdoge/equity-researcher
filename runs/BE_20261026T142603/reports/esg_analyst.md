## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

**Carbon intensity.** Bloom's own technical note discloses 679–833 lbs CO₂/MWh (0.308–0.378 tCO₂/MWh) for natural-gas Energy Servers. Against a US grid average of roughly 0.36 tCO₂/MWh, **a gas Bloom cell is approximately at parity with the US grid** — better than coal, roughly comparable to a gas CCGT, and *worse* than the grid in hydro/nuclear-heavy jurisdictions. The marketing claim ("no combustion," "99% less smog-forming pollutants") is factually accurate on NOx/SOx/PM but is routinely read by the market as a carbon claim it does not support. This is the central greenwashing vector.

**Scale-up math is the material risk.** Verified 2022 Scope 1 was **1,758,296 tCO₂e** (Scope 2: 7,267 t) on ~$1.2B revenue. Per GW running at ~90% capacity factor, the fleet emits **~2.4–3.0 Mt CO₂ per year**. A 5 GW annual build rate therefore implies **~12–15 Mt/yr of new on-site CO₂** — a roughly 7x increase over the 2022 verified footprint. Bloom has a net-zero target and a 40%-by-2030 reduction goal, but these are absolute-target commitments that a gas-fuel-cell volume ramp structurally contradicts unless biogas/hydrogen blending or carbon capture scales faster than the base business. Carbon capture remains pre-commercial at scale.

**Permitting and enforcement record.**
- **Hilliard, Ohio (Feb 2026):** the city sued to block AEP's 228-cell, 73 MW array for an Amazon campus — described as the largest fuel-cell project in North America. Hilliard challenged the Ohio EPA air permit (allowing **1.45 million lbs CO₂/day, ~66,000 cars**) at the Environmental Review Appeals Commission after its zoning block failed. Norwich Township trustees cited "fire and explosion hazards" on the 8-inch gas pipeline. This establishes a **replicable template for local obstruction**.
- **Oracle "Project Jupiter," New Mexico:** reported to lack an approved air permit *and* a gas pipeline; an environmental group is litigating. First power estimates have slipped from 2027 toward 2029+.
- **Delaware (2019):** DNREC fined Bloom **$40,000 plus ~$5,455 cost recovery** for operating new fuel cells before permits were issued — evidence that "energise first, permit later" is a historical pattern.

**Positives:** water use is genuinely low (a real advantage vs. cooling-intensive alternatives); a Green Bond Financing Framework, EMS manual, and environmental policy are published; ~1 Mt of *avoided* emissions claimed, though this is customer-scope accounting that flatters Bloom's own inventory.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Community relations / social licence — the weak point.** The Delaware experience is the template: ratepayers were legally obligated to buy Bloom power for two decades at costs that reportedly ran **>5x original projections, for 600 fewer jobs than promised**. Hunterbrook documents a repeat pattern (SK ecoplant granted three extra years when Korean demand stalled). Bloom is now the physical embodiment of the data-centre backlash — residents in Hilliard describe discovering that "putting in a data centre meant putting in a mini gas power plant." Ironically, management has publicly framed NIMBY opposition as a *demand tailwind* (Bloomberg, Jun-2026); the same opposition becomes Bloom's own permitting problem once its name is on the array.

**Safety.** Pressurised gas delivery to dense residential-adjacent arrays is an unresolved concern raised by elected trustees, not just activists. No serious injury event was found in this review, but hydrogen blending raises the hazard profile. **Workforce:** 2,214 FTEs; CEO-to-median pay ratio of only **36:1** (median $98,317) — a genuine positive versus US large-cap norms.

**Supply-chain labour standards — elevated and under-tested.** Bloom publishes a Conflict Minerals Report, Responsible Sourcing Policy, and California Supply Chain Disclosure, which is better than nothing. But four China-linked scandium routes allegedly flow through Thailand, Japan, and South Korea intermediaries — precisely the structure that defeats traceability and creates UFLPA/forced-labour exposure, and specifically **obscures origin rather than verifying it**.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10** (Weak)

**Credits:** dual-class structure **sunset July 2023** — all shareholders now have one vote per share (material improvement); independent Lead Director; Deloitte as auditor; standard independent committee structure.

**Debits, in descending severity:**
1. **Related-party revenue concentration.** 74% of Q4-2025 revenue ($574M of $778M) reportedly came from joint ventures Bloom part-owns with Brookfield, where Bloom's own footnotes concede the counterparty "may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user." A financier booking revenue on equipment it is financing is a circularity problem, not a customer-adoption proof point.
2. **Backlog quality.** $20B unaudited "backlog" versus **$492.6M of RPO** (31-Mar-2026) — a ~40x gap, versus ~2x for the widest of ten peers analysed. Backlog includes non-obligatory service revenue and ungranted tax credits. This is an ESG-relevant disclosure-quality issue, not merely an accounting one.
3. **CFO churn.** **Four finance chiefs since start of 2024**, including nearly a year with no permanent CFO — the same year the Brookfield structure was built. A CAO currently serves as Acting Principal Financial Officer. Audit-committee oversight has not been demonstrated to be adequate against this.
4. **CEO/Chairman duality.** Founder K.R. Sridhar holds Chairman *and* CEO — mitigated but not cured by a Lead Independent Director.
5. **Say-on-pay support of only 62%** — well below the ~70% threshold that typically signals investor discontent; the company acknowledges "extensive shareholder outreach."
6. **Officer exculpation charter amendment** (2026 ballot) — reduces personal accountability for officers, moving against market trend.
7. **Active securities class action** alleging the CEO's repeated "no China supply chain / not dependent on China for scandium" statements were false.

Compensation design is defensible (PSUs on multi-year product revenue growth and non-GAAP product gross margin; max payout raised to 200%), but the 2025 CEO package was **71% non-equity incentive with no 2025 equity grant**, weakening long-horizon alignment in the year the equity story mattered most.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **California SB 253 / SB 261 (Material, near-term):** Bloom is headquartered in San Jose and FY2025 revenue of $2.02B exceeds the $1B threshold — it is in scope for Scope 1/2/3 GHG disclosure and climate-risk reporting. *This is the most concrete mandatory disclosure obligation.* Bloomberg has minimal non-US ESG disclosure infrastructure relative to this requirement, and the 5 GW gas ramp will make Scope 1 a public, growing number.
- **US SEC climate rule:** adopted but stayed; the Commission has since moved to abandon defence. Effectively **not binding** — do not underwrite disclosure-cost risk from it.
- **State air-quality regimes (Critical):** Ohio EPA, New Mexico EDAC, Utah DAQ, Delaware DNREC, and historically Bay Area AQMD. Permits are now the **binding constraint on revenue recognition**, and they are being contested administratively, not just politically.
- **China export controls on scandium (Critical):** Beijing requires an export licence for essentially every scandium shipment. This is the single largest regulatory exposure and it is **not** a conventional E/S/G rule — it is industrial policy arriving through a supply-chain channel. Bloom's competitive position depends on being *inside* the Western ex-China supply build-out, which requires capital it has not demonstrated.
- **EU CSRD / CBAM:** limited direct revenue exposure; indirect exposure via European customers (including the announced, apparently unfulfilled, Brookfield European AI-factory site) demanding value-chain emissions data. CBAM does not cover fuel cells.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** **none identified** — the $2.2B zero-coupon convertible and $600M senior secured revolver are conventional. However, Bloom maintains a **Green Bond Financing Framework**, which invites use-of-proceeds scrutiny and creates a potential "green default" reputational event if labelled proceeds are contested.

---

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **ESG RISK**

Not "Laggard" in the conventional sense — the technology has real environmental merit (no combustion, low NOx/SOx/PM/water, high efficiency, hydrogen/biogas optionality) and one governance improvement is genuine (dual-class sunset). But "Laggard" implies *backwardness*, whereas the specific problem here is **active controversy plus a disclosure regime that the market has just lost confidence in**. A company whose central ESG-adjacent claim ("no China supply chain") is under securities litigation, whose revenue is materially related-party, and whose flagship projects are blocked on air permits belongs in the controversy-driven, high-risk bucket.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE** (for the thesis)

The ESG work does not rebut the growth story — it **relocates the risk from regulatory cost to revenue timing and cost of capital**, which is worse for a $59B-cap, 3.8-beta, 118x-P/E stock.

Three transmission channels:

1. **Scandium is a hard physical ceiling, not a soft ESG score.** 220t needed for 5 GW against ~240t of global supply — with Lockheed already contracted and essentially no Western processing capability — means the ramp is supply-constrained. This is the rare ESG factor that can *directly falsify the revenue model* rather than merely raise its cost. It also hands Beijing a strategic lever over US AI infrastructure, which raises the probability of US government intervention (positive for a domestic alternative, negative for Bloom if it is deemed the exposed party).
2. **Permitting is now a revenue-recognition gate.** Hilliard and Project Jupiter show that each 200+ cell array must survive administrative litigation. Bloom's own AEP order has already slipped two years. Delays hit the FY2026–27 numbers the current multiple capitalises.
3. **Disclosure quality raises cost of capital structurally.** Four CFOs in three years, a 40x backlog/RPO gap, 74% JV-sourced quarterly revenue, 62% say-on-pay, and an active securities class action are the profile that expands the risk premium permanently. The 53% drawdown from June 2026 is the market beginning to price it.

**Watch items that would change the call to Neutral:** a permanent CFO with a clean control opinion; reconciliation of the $20B backlog to RPO with disclosed contractual obligor; an audited, named ex-China scandium supply source; receipt of an uncontested air permit for one flagship data-centre array; and disclosure of Scope 1/3 under SB 253 that ties emissions to revenue rather than to "avoided emissions."

**Bottom line:** the cleanest long-term ESG read on Bloom Energy is that it is a **gas-fired distributed generation company with an excellent NOx profile and a challenged disclosure culture** — priced by the market as a decarbonisation pure-play. That gap is the thesis risk.
