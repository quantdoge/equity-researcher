## NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — Bloom Energy Corp. (NYSE: BE)

**Snapshot:** $59.2B market cap; $200.41/share; beta **3.78**; 2,214 employees; Industrials–Electrical Equipment. 52-week range $75.70–$351.28 (≈4.6x high-low spread). Data as of ~Oct 26, 2026.

*Data caveat: the 10-K and DEF 14A retrieval tools returned errors (no 10-K found / SEC 403). Findings below are built from SEC Form 4 data, an 8-K rebuttal, EDGAR full-text search, and secondary reporting. Items I could not independently verify are flagged.*

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

Headline optics are good — **9 of 10 directors independent (90%)**, Audit Committee stocked with credible names (Mary K. Bush, Michael Boskin, Cynthia Warner, Eddy Zervigon); Boardroom Alpha grades the board **B-**. But structure and behaviour say otherwise:

- **CEO/Chairman duality:** K.R. Sridhar holds both roles (confirmed in Form 4 officer titles: "Chairman & CEO") — no independent chair.
- **Dual-class control:** Class A = 1 vote, Class B = **10 votes**. Founder and early VC holders retain outsized voting power; minority shareholders cannot discipline the board.
- **Vote concentration:** Holder Voting Agreements grant Sridhar the right to **vote other stockholders' shares** — a rare and aggressive control mechanism that further insulates management.
- **Related-party revenue is the central issue**, not a footnote. FY2025 disclosed related-party revenue of roughly **$865–892M**, including **$574.2M in Q4'25 alone** — about **74% of that quarter's revenue** — from joint ventures Bloom part-owns with **Brookfield**. SK ecoplant (~2.5% of Class A, JV partner) is a second related-party channel with a 500MW/$4.5B commitment. Bloom's own footnotes concede these counterparties "may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user." Growth partly reflects a financing partner buying from a company it co-owns, not broad customer adoption.
- **Notable:** three customers were 43%/13%/12% of FY2025 revenue, and the 43% customer is a related party.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **Active securities class action** — *Nevins v. Bloom Energy Corp.*, No. 3:26-cv-07944 (N.D. Cal., Judge Rita F. Lin). Class period **Feb 27, 2025 – Jul 8, 2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline was **Sept 28, 2026**. Allegations: Bloom sourced scandium through intermediaries from China while management repeatedly stated it had "no China supply chain." Multiple firms (Rosen, Kessler Topaz, ZLK, Federman) are competing for lead counsel — a crowded bar signals expected size. Company filed an **8-K on July 9, 2026 "categorically rejecting"** the Hunterbrook report as false and misleading.
- **SEC/DOJ:** EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned **no SEC enforcement actions** against BE. This is the single most important mitigant — but a class action alleging misstatements about a disclosed risk factor is the classic precursor to a formal inquiry. **Treat as a watch item, not a cleared item.**
- **Tax-credit / FEOC exposure (highest-magnitude regulatory risk):** 2026-vintage **Foreign Entity of Concern** rules can disqualify §48E/§45Y credits if a China/Russia/Iran/N.Korea link exists anywhere in the supply chain. Bloom's credit-dependent economics plus alleged Chinese scandium/ceramic routing is a direct collision course. Beijing also now requires an **export licence for every scandium shipment** — a regulatory off-switch held by a strategic adversary.
- **Permitting:** Oracle's **Project Jupiter** (New Mexico) reportedly lacks an approved air permit and a gas pipeline; Oracle issued a **force majeure notice** (Sept 2026) tied to pipeline regulatory delay. An environmental group is litigating to stop it. Oracle reaffirmed the **2.4GW** fuel-cell award, which lifted the stock 8% — but schedule risk is real (SemiAnalysis reportedly pushed first power from 2027 to 2029).
- **Tariffs:** management states tariff impact on gross margin is currently immaterial and not expected to be material in FY2026. Low near-term risk.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Scandium single-point-of-failure.** Hunterbrook traced four China-linked routes (oxide shipped directly to the Delaware plant; scandium-bearing ceramics/powders via Thailand, Japan, South Korea). Hunan Oriental Scandium — >50% of global fuel-cell-grade supply — is alleged to be Bloom's largest supplier, and was featured at Bloom's own May supplier conference. Global supply is ~240t against ~310t demand; Bloom alone would need ~**220t** to hit 5GW. Management now claims visibility to **25GW/year** of production. Either the constraint is solved or the growth model is not physically buildable — the gap is too large to hand-wave.
- **Related-party revenue concentration.** 74% of Q4'25 revenue from Brookfield JVs; 43% of FY2025 from one related party. If that financing structure slows, revenue does not degrade gracefully.
- **Key-person + scale-up execution.** A founder-CEO-Chairman with supervoting control is simultaneously the chief technical visionary and the chief capital allocator. Ramp from ~1GW to 5GW (headcount only 2,214) coincides with **negative free cash flow (−$181M in Q3'26)** against a $2.5B cash buffer. Nine consecutive quarters of guidance raises do not eliminate the funding-gap question that drove the 11.6% single-day selloff on Oct 23.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Elevated / deteriorating**

- The Hunterbrook report ("Bloom's Big Lie," July 8, 2026) is a durable, well-documented short thesis with named reporters, trade-data and satellite sourcing — not an anonymous message board hit. It now has a **pending class action attached to it**, which guarantees the allegations resurface in every news cycle for 2–3 years.
- **Short interest ~7.3% of float** (20.98M shares, Sept 30, 2026) — elevated, and an activist short seller with a disclosed position has every incentive to keep publishing.
- **AI-infrastructure sentiment is the dominant stock driver**: the Oct 23–26 drawdown was attributed to "deteriorating AI infrastructure sentiment" and debt concerns across the AI complex, not to Bloom-specific news. BE's 3.78 beta means reputational and sector-sentiment shocks are amplified ~4x.
- **ESG/greenwashing:** marketing leans on hydrogen-readiness while current fleet runs on natural gas; combined with the China supply-chain allegation, the "clean domestic power" narrative is the company's most exposed intangible asset.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: an **active securities fraud class action** tied to a **CEO-repeated factual claim** (no China supply chain) that a short seller has substantiated with trade data; **related-party revenue at 74% of a quarter**; a **physically constrained critical input** under adversary export control; **unanimous insider selling with zero buys**; and combined CEO/Chairman plus 10:1 supervoting control. The mitigants — 90% independent board, a competent audit committee, no SEC enforcement action on file, $2.5B of cash, and a formal 8-K rebuttal — are real but are not sufficient to offset the aggregate.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Unaudited $20B "backlog" vs $492.6M RPO** (as of 3/31/26) — a ~**40x** ratio against a worst-in-class peer gap of ~2x. The backlog reportedly includes non-obligatory service revenue and **tax credits the government has not granted**. This is the highest-severity accounting/disclosure indicator in the file.
2. **Circularity allegation:** Brookfield funds → buys Bloom equipment → Radiant (Brookfield's own captive cloud company, reportedly no named CEO or disclosed customers) is anchor tenant → Nvidia anchors the fund. Revenue recognised *before deployment* is alleged. If any of this is validated, restatement risk is non-trivial.
3. **Prior ICFR failure precedent:** Bloom has a documented history of a **material weakness in internal control over financial reporting** and restatement/remediation (2020–21 proxy disclosures). A company with a prior control failure that is now growing 138% and booking three-quarters of revenue from related parties deserves auditor-level scrutiny. *(Current-year ICFR status and current auditor unverified — the 10-K was not retrievable; recommend confirming the auditor's opinion and any critical audit matter on related-party revenue before publishing.)*
4. **Director-level selling into strength** — Chambers ~$23.2M and Immelt ~$7.2M. Independent directors liquidating at $240–300 while the CEO publicly asserts the supply chain is clean is a governance signal that should be surfaced to the IC explicitly.
5. **Scandium export-licence chokepoint** — a single Chinese regulatory decision can impair delivery on the Oracle 2.4GW and AEP orders. This is uninsurable and unhedgeable.

**Recommendation:** Do not treat the Hunterbrook matter as resolved by the 8-K. Require (i) the auditor's FY2025 opinion and any related-party critical audit matter, (ii) a backlog-to-RPO reconciliation, and (iii) disclosure of the ultimate end-users behind Brookfield-affiliated revenue, before relying on the growth narrative in the investment memo.
