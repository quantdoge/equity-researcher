## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — China's scandium export-licence regime ("the off-switch"). Probability: High. Impact: High.**
China added scandium to its national-security export-licensing list in April 2025, meaning every shipment leaves at Beijing's discretion. Scandium stabilises the ceramic electrolyte in every Bloom stack, and Bloom is likely the world's largest consumer. Hunterbrook Media (8 Jul 2026) documented **four China-linked routes** into Bloom's supply chain — scandium oxide shipped directly to the Delaware plant plus scandium-bearing ceramics/powders routed through **Thailand, Japan and South Korea** intermediaries — and quoted Hunan Oriental Scandium (claims >50% of fuel-cell-grade Sc₂O₃ market) as saying "We are also BE's largest supplier." Bloom's COO's rebuttal blog conceded scandium plays "an important role" and that Bloom is the largest global consumer, while dropping the CEO's earlier unequivocal "no China supply chain" language. Hunterbrook's supply model implies ~220 t Sc₂O₃ needed for a 5 GW ramp versus ~240 t global supply against ~310 t demand (incl. Lockheed F-35 offtake) — i.e., a physical shortage by ~2028 even absent an embargo. *Impact channel: unit-cost inflation, delivery slippage on data-center contracts, and a credible Chinese leverage point over U.S. AI infrastructure.*

**Risk 2 — U.S. clean-energy tax-credit architecture, incl. FEOC "material assistance" rules. Probability: Medium. Impact: High.**
Fuel cells were folded into **§48E** (30% ITC) for projects beginning construction after 2025 (Sidley, RSM on OBBBA), and JPMorgan cited restored fuel-cell 48E eligibility (effective 2026) as a pricing-power driver. Two exposures: (a) the credit still carries commence-construction deadlines and prevailing-wage/apprenticeship conditions; (b) OBBBA's **prohibited-foreign-entity / "material assistance cost ratio"** tests can disqualify a project from §48E if Chinese-linked content is embedded. A scandium supply chain routed through Chinese processors is therefore **not just a disclosure problem — it is potentially a tax-credit-eligibility problem for Bloom's customers**, which is the single most underappreciated legal linkage in this name. Bloom's own 10-K flags reliance on the continuation of these credits as a risk factor.

**Risk 3 — U.S. project-level permitting and local political resistance (the real "country risk" is domestic). Probability: Medium-High. Impact: Medium-High.**
Oracle's **Project Jupiter** (New Mexico) — a flagship Bloom fuel-cell/gas installation — has no approved air permit and no gas pipeline; NMED proceedings have faced repeated delays and public hearings, Oracle publicly lobbied NMED for approval (Jul 2026), and Oracle cancelled a separate NM gas plant amid local pushback (May 2026). Separately, the AEP $2.65bn / up-to-1 GW order slipped from end-2028 to "no later than 2030" per AEP's own deck. Bloom has won at least one permitting fight (Santa Clara, CA — court affirmed fuel cells displace combustion power), but county/municipal air-district discretion is a recurring, non-diversifiable gate on revenue recognition.

*Secondary:* South Korea policy pivot — the Lee Jae-myung administration's **KRW ~1,000tn ($747bn) energy transition plan to 2035** targets 100 GW of renewables by 2030 (Reuters, 7 Oct 2026), and the **Hydrogen Act 2026 amendment (in force 18 Sep 2026)** moved hydrogen/fuel-cell provisions out of the renewables law and adds a **clean-hydrogen sale/use obligation from 31 May 2027 with penalties**. A natural-gas SOFC fleet in Korea faces both a support re-weighting toward wind/solar and new compliance obligations — a structural headwind to the ~19% international slice.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action (active):** *Filed 30 Jul 2026, N.D. Cal.*, class period **27 Feb 2025 – 8 Jul 2026**, alleging §§10(b)/20(a)/Rule 10b-5 violations — that Bloom sourced scandium via intermediaries from China, understated China reliance, and that positive statements were misleading. Lead-plaintiff deadline was **28 Sep 2026**; multiple firms (Kessler Topaz, Rosen, Levi & Korsinsky, Robbins, ZLK) are soliciting. Stock fell 5.7% on the Hunterbrook report (D&O Diary, 2 Aug 2026). This is a *geopolitical-disclosure* case, not a disruption case — a template now being used against other issuers (e.g., First Solar, June 2026).
- **Delaware Chancery §220 books-and-records demand** — *Jacob v. Bloom Energy Corp.*; Chancery ordered Bloom to respond to a stockholder inspection demand. Governance-hygiene signal that precedes derivative/board-oversight claims.
- **Related-party revenue concentration:** the largest FY2025 customer (**43% of revenue**) is a **related party via the Brookfield joint ventures**; three customers = 43%/13%/12%; **one customer = ~73% of Q2 2026 revenue** (10-Q/A). Brookfield partnership expanded to **$25bn**. This is the highest-probability source of future SEC/auditor scrutiny (backlog ~$20bn vs. audited RPO of ~$442m at 30 Jun 2026).
- **Financial-reporting churn:** four CFOs since start-2024, ~1 year without a permanent CFO; a 10-Q/A on file for Q2 2026; $98.6m debt-exchange/conversion-inducement charge in FY2025 and a Nov-2025 note exchange (42m shares). Combined with the above, this raises ICFR/disclosure-controls risk.
- **Prior settled class action:** Bloom paid **$3.0m** to resolve an earlier securities class action (KTMC case page) — track record of recurring securities exposure.
- **Environmental/permitting:** New Mexico (Project Jupiter) air permit contested; local-opposition litigation risk; California air-district precedent favourable.
- **No evidence found** of DOJ/SEC enforcement action, FCPA matter, ITC patent case, or Russia/OFAC sanctions exposure. Peer/patent litigation search returned nothing material.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Transshipment / export-control optics (HIGH):** Chinese scandium routed via Thailand, Japan and Korea intermediaries is precisely the pattern that draws BIS scrutiny and, post-OBBBA, FEOC disqualification of customers' §48E credits. Public CEO statements ("no China supply chain," repeated ≥5 times since Feb 2025) now sit uneasily against trade-data evidence — the classic predicate for both SEC and DOJ-fraud-section interest.
2. **Disclosure-controls quality (MEDIUM-HIGH):** related-party-dominant revenue, backlog/RPO mismatch (>40x vs. peer ~2x per Hunterbrook), CFO turnover, 10-Q/A.
3. **Sanctions/AML/FCPA (LOW):** no Russia, Iran, or high-risk-jurisdiction operations identified; Korea/Taiwan/Japan customer base is well-regulated. Korea's own new export/import restriction procedures (Oct 2026) bear monitoring but are not targeted at Bloom.
4. **Data privacy (LOW):** equipment vendor to data centers; no material consumer data.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

*Sub-ratings:* Geopolitical **High** (China critical-mineral chokepoint on a single-sourced rare earth, with no Western processing alternative at scale); Legal & Regulatory **High** (live securities class action + §220 demand + FEOC/tax-credit dependency + permitting gating); Compliance **Medium-High**.
