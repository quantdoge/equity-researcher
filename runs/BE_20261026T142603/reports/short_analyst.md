## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **MEDIUM**

The important finding: **the classic accrual and manipulation models do not flag Bloom.** That is unusual for a forensic short and I will not manufacture a fraud case the data doesn't support. The problems here are about the *composition and durability of revenue*, which accrual models are structurally blind to.

| Test | Reading | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Beneish M-Score | **−2.52** | "Low risk." Well inside safe territory |
| Accruals ratio (25, 24, 23) | **−0.046 / −0.046 / +0.029** | Negative accruals = conservative, cash-backed |
| DSRI (receivables/revenue index) | **0.834** | Receivables *lagging* revenue — no channel-stuffing signal |
| GMI | **0.946** | Gross margin improving — no pricing-power erosion |
| DEPI | **1.01** | Depreciation rate unchanged — no useful-life extension trick |
| DSO (annual) | 99d → 119d → 163d* | Improved sharply; Q3'26 DSO ≈ **47 days** |
| Inventory days | 185.9 → 163.4 → ~103d | Falling — no demand-weakness signal |
| Receivables growth vs revenue | AR +14.5% vs rev +37.3% | **No red flag** |

\*FY23 elevated; trend since has been decisively better.

On cash conversion the FY2025 print (OCF/NI = −1.29, FCF/ni = −0.65, composite score 0/100) is **mechanically broken** by a negative net-income denominator, not evidence of poor conversion. OCF was +$114M against a −$88M net loss. In 2026 the Q2 receivables drawdown ($892M → $635M) confirms real collections.

**The genuine quality problem — Q4 2025.** This is the quarter that "made" the FY2025 numbers and it is my strongest original finding:

- Record revenue **$777.7M**, gross margin 30.9%, operating income **$87.5M**…
- …but net income of **$1.09M**. A ~$86M wedge between operating income and net income against only $10.6M of interest expense.
- Hunterbrook alleges **74% of Q4'25 revenue ($574M of $778M) came from joint ventures Bloom part-owns with Brookfield**, and that Bloom's footnotes concede these customers "may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user."
- Independently corroborated direction: FY2025 had three customers at **43% / 13% / 12%** of revenue, **with the 43% customer a related party**.

A consolidated JV books 100% of revenue at the top line while the economics are shared with the partner — so reported revenue growth overstates growth in value accruing to BE shareholders. The microscopic net income on a record revenue quarter is precisely the fingerprint you'd expect.

**Second quality problem — backlog vs RPO.** Bloom markets an unaudited **~$20B backlog**. Remaining performance obligations — the only figure the auditor touches — were **$492.6M** at 31 Mar 2026, roughly **2.5%** of backlog. Hunterbrook's cross-section of ten peers found a maximum backlog/RPO gap of ~2x; Bloom's is ~40x, partly because the backlog embeds servicing revenue customers aren't obligated to pay and tax credits not granted. Wall Street's models are anchored on a number no auditor has vouched for.

**Rating: Medium — NOT Fraudulent Risk.** Accruals, receivables, inventory and depreciation tests are all clean. I would be misrepresenting the evidence to call this an accounting fraud.

## 2. Key Red Flags

1. **Related-party/captive-financing revenue concentration** (43% related-party customer FY25; alleged 74% of Q4'25 revenue from Brookfield JVs). Not illegal — but it is *vendor-financed* growth, and it is not end-market adoption.
2. **40x backlog-to-RPO gap** on an unaudited $20B backlog.
3. **Scandium supply-chain misstatement risk.** CEO has stated at least five times since Feb 2025 that Bloom has "no China supply chain" and is "not dependent on China for scandium." Trade data, Chinese corporate filings and supplier interviews (Hunan Oriental Scandium, which claims >50% of the global fuel-cell-grade scandium oxide market, reportedly says it is "BE's largest supplier") contradict this. Beijing now licenses every scandium export. The COO's own blog post published the day before the report reportedly *confirmed several* of these findings.
4. **Physics constraint on the bull case.** Hunterbrook models ~220t of Sc₂O₃ needed for 5GW against ~240t of global supply and ~310t of total demand (including Lockheed's F-35 needs). A shortage by 2028 is implied. Bloom's 8-K counters with "visibility" to 25GW/yr — an assertion, not third-party verified.
5. **Flagship projects slipping.** Oracle "Project Jupiter" (NM): no approved air permit, no gas pipeline; SemiAnalysis reportedly pushed first power from 2027 to 2029. AEP's $2.65B order reportedly slipped from end-2028 to "no later than 2030," with a hyperscaler backstop that can walk for costs +10%.
6. **Depreciation intensity is very low for the obligations assumed.** D&A of ~$54M TTM on $3.8B revenue (**1.4%**) and $640M net PP&E, against a service obligation on Bloom's installed base of 10–15 years. If D&A ran at even 6% of revenue, TTM operating income of $567M would fall to ~$392M (−31%). DEPI of 1.01 says this is *not* a manipulation trend — but it is a level risk that flatters GAAP profitability, and it is directly linked to stack degradation (Bloom's own patents reportedly identify low electrolyte scandium as a cause of accelerated aging).
7. **Interest expense collapse without deleveraging.** $14.4M/qtr through 2025 → $8.9M (Q1'26) → $7.5M (Q3'26) while total debt stayed ~$2.8B (≈1.0% effective rate). Almost certainly low-coupon convertibles — which flatters EPS today and dilutes tomorrow.
8. **Dilution.** Basic shares 227.4M (FY24) → 295.8M (Q3'26), **+30%**; diluted 324.3M. Fully diluted equity at $200 is ~$65.0B, not $59.3B. Retained earnings still **−$3.47B**.
9. **Unreconciled data anomalies (flagged, unverified):** goodwill/intangible $0 → **$215.5M** (Q2'26) → **$0** (Q3'26), implying either an acquisition written off within one quarter or a data inconsistency; and FY2025 quarterly operating income sums to $110.6M versus $72.8M reported annually (~$38M unexplained). Both need checking against the filings before they can be relied on.
10. **SBC is not in the cash-flow data for 2025** (reported as 0 vs $82–112M in prior years) — a data gap, not an assertion that SBC ceased.

## 3. Beneish & Accruals Detail

M-score **−2.523** vs thresholds of −1.78 (possible manipulation) and −2.22 (softer warning). The single largest *adverse* contributor is **SGI = 1.373** — i.e., the score is being pushed up by high growth, which is exactly the benign reason to be skeptical of a mechanical reading. GMI < 1 and DSRI < 1 are both *favourable*. TATA of −0.046 is a conservative accrual position.

**Conclusion: no accrual manipulation signal.** The revenue-quality questions above sit in related-party and off-balance-sheet disclosure territory, where Beneish has zero detection power. A clean M-score here is not exculpatory.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, and it is unusually specific.** It is *not* a fraud/solvency short; it is a **circular-financing and physics short at a perfection-priced multiple.**

The mechanism: Bloom's record quarters are substantially produced by counterparties who are also Bloom's capital partners. Delaware ratepayers → SK ecoplant (given three extra years when Korean demand stalled) → now Brookfield. Each time, in Hunterbrook's framing, "the financier's commitment — not a customer's consumption — makes the numbers." Layer on a $20B unaudited backlog that is 40x RPO, two flagship projects (Oracle Jupiter, AEP) slipping *after* guidance was raised, a rare-earth supply chain with a Beijing off-switch, and 1.4%-of-revenue depreciation against 10–15-year service obligations — and you have a stock whose multiple discounts flawless execution of a plan that may be physically supply-constrained.

Valuation makes the asymmetry concrete (at $200.41, EV $59.6B):

- **EV / TTM revenue $3.83B = 15.6x**; EV / FY26E revenue ($4.5B guide) ≈ **13.2x** (14.4x fully diluted)
- **P / TTM net income $518M ≈ 114x**; P/E on FY26E ≈ ~72x
- **P / 2027E EPS $5.29 = 37.9x** — consensus already assumes revenue tripling to $7.16B by 2027 and $15.6B by 2030
- Analyst 2028 revenue range: **$6.7B to $15.4B** (2.3x dispersion). That is not a forecast, it is a coin toss.
- Altman Z **0.148** ("distress", book-equity basis, FY25); Piotroski F **4** ("weak"); liabilities 2.3x equity

**The other side (and it is strong).** GAAP profitability has genuinely arrived: $250M of net income and a 23% operating margin in Q3'26, revenue +138%, gross margin at a record 33.8%, receivables and inventory trends clean, accruals negative, M-score clean, and **net debt of only $295M against $2.49B of cash**. If the AI power shortage persists and Bloom executes the 5GW ramp, the 2027–2028 estimates are achievable and 38x forward earnings for a 50%+ grower is not obviously wrong. Bears have already been run over: this stock went from $9 to $350. A 40% backlog/RPO gap could also simply reflect a company that markets non-binding framework agreements aggressively — promotional, not fraudulent.

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **SHORT** (valuation/narrative fragility — not fraud/solvency)

**Not "Strong Short."** I cannot underwrite a strong short when the accrual tests, receivables, inventory, gross-margin trajectory and net-cash position all point the right way. The fraud thesis is not supported.

What I *will* underwrite: **reported growth quality is materially inferior to headline growth**, the backlog underpinning consensus is unaudited and 40x RPO, two flagship projects are slipping, and the supply chain has a single-point foreign failure mode. At 13x forward sales, the margin for error is nil.

**Position mechanics matter more than the thesis.** Beta 3.78; the stock fell 48% in three weeks (Nov 2025) and 29% in a month (June 2026) — it will do the same upward. Size at ≤1/3 of a normal short, use defined-risk structures (puts/put spreads) rather than naked stock, and expect borrow to be expensive.

**What would make me wrong / kill the short:** (1) Brookfield JV revenue mix falling below ~25% as named hyperscalers contract directly; (2) audited RPO converging toward the backlog figure; (3) Oracle Jupiter securing air permit and pipeline; (4) verifiable non-China scandium offtake at scale; (5) a permanent CFO with two clean 10-K cycles.

**Catalysts:** Oracle/AEP permit and FID milestones; Q4'26 guidance against a $4.4–4.6B bar; any Chinese export-licence action on scandium; backlog-to-RPO disclosure under pressure; convert conversion/dilution events.

**Data gaps acknowledged:** short interest and borrow cost not retrieved; auditor identity/changes not checked; peer EV/sales multiples not computed (the sector-peer tool returned only mega-caps, and I did not pull their financials) — the "single-digit capital-goods multiples" comparison is indicative, not calculated.
