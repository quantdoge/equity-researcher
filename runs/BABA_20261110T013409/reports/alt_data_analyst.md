## 6. Overall Alt Data Signal: **NEUTRAL** (with a clear internal divergence)

The alt data is telling **two different stories about two different halves of Alibaba**, and they point in opposite directions:

**Constructive — the AI/Cloud half:** AI-cloud hiring surge, Qwen search holding at a structurally high level, Apple/Qwen partnership, triple-digit AI revenue growth, strongest cloud growth in 22 quarters. This is a genuinely inflecting, high-quality growth engine, and it is the part the market is now paying for.

**Negative — the earnings-quality read:** four straight EPS misses averaging −21%, driven by margin compression from the very capex that powers the bullish AI narrative. The two stories are the same story viewed from different lines of the P&L.

**Net: Neutral.** I cannot get to Bullish on a name with four consecutive double-digit EPS misses and softening international commerce data eight days before print, and I cannot get to Bearish against a +0.556 sentiment score, an Apple/Qwen catalyst, and stabilising core Taobao search. Price action supports "neutral with a bid": BABA has recovered **+7.5% off the Oct 30 low ($98.04 → $105.38)** on rising volume, but remains ~19% below the May high of $144.51 and range-bound $98–115 since September.

**Positioning implication:** this is a **revenue/cloud-narrative long versus an EPS short**. The likely outcome on Nov 19 is a revenue beat with an EPS miss — a combination that has historically been resolved by the market focusing on the AI story (the stock rose 6% on the Aug 20 revenue beat before giving it all back the next day). Expect high post-print volatility in both directions.

## 7. Confidence Level: **MEDIUM**

**Why Medium, not High:**
- **Google Trends geo problem:** the series are US-geo. Alibaba derives the majority of revenue from Chinese consumers, so US search interest is measuring overseas brand awareness and cross-border shopping intent — **not** Chinese domestic consumption. The Taobao/AliExpress reads are therefore directional proxies at best.
- **Web traffic is scraped from SimilarWeb preview blurbs**, not the API — I have one-month MoM changes with no absolute visit levels and no multi-month history, so I cannot distinguish trend from noise.
- **Hiring data is a level, not a series** — I can show composition (AI/cloud-heavy) but not acceleration.
- **Patents: unavailable** (tool error, 2 attempts). **Social: no quantitative score.** **Satellite/geospatial: unavailable.**
- **Tmall's collapse is almost certainly a low-volume artefact** and should not be weighted.

**Why not Low:** the signs are *mutually corroborating* where it matters. AliExpress search decline and AliExpress traffic decline point the same way independently. AI-cloud hiring composition, Qwen search durability, the Apple partnership, and reported cloud revenue acceleration all point the same way independently. And the earnings-surprise history is hard, complete, and unambiguous.

**Lag note:** Google Trends is weekly and lagged ~1–3 days; SimilarWeb MoM is a trailing 30-day window; LinkedIn role counts are stale and duplicate-inflated. Hiring and capex signals lead revenue by 2–4 quarters — the AI-cloud build-out will not fully show up in the Nov 19 print. **This is a leading indicator, not a definitive one.**

### Bottom line for the memo
Alt data does not overturn the consensus narrative — it *sharpens* it. Consensus is already long the AI story and already expects margin pain. The differentiated read is that **core Taobao consumer demand is holding up better than the collapsing headline search data suggests**, while **International Commerce (AliExpress) is deteriorating faster than the AI narrative distracts from**, and the **EPS miss streak is a structural margin feature of the capex cycle, not a one-off.** Watch the Nov 19 print for the ratio of cloud revenue growth to capex — that is where this alt-data divergence resolves.
