### 2. Key Red Flags Found

**RF-1 — Operating profit halved while EPS beat by 37%.** FY2026 operating income −57.6% YoY; reported EPS 45.6 vs consensus 33.36. The beat was manufactured by investment gains/associate income, not operations. Classic low-quality beat.

**RF-2 — Free cash flow negative for three consecutive quarters and widening.** −18.8bn → −21.4bn → −18.1bn → **−44.7bn** (Q1 FY27). FCF has swung from +165bn (FY23) to −51bn (FY26): a **RMB 216bn deterioration in three years**.

**RF-3 — Capex is outrunning the P&L's ability to absorb it.** Capex/Revenue went 3.9% (FY24) → 8.7% (FY25) → **12.4% (FY26)** → 25.2% (Q1 FY27). Capex is now **2.7× D&A**. Q1 FY27 D&A already +63% YoY (12.5bn vs 7.7bn). On the RMB 380bn/3-year AI pledge, annual depreciation trends toward RMB 120–150bn — *against current operating income of 59.7bn*. This is a lagging charge on money already spent, and it will consume essentially all present operating profit.

**RF-4 — The balance sheet is funding the income statement.** Cash + short-term investments fell from **602.2bn (FY24) → 464.8bn (FY25) → 357.6bn (FY26)**: −RMB 244.6bn (−41%) in two years, while reporting RMB 233.7bn of net income over the same span. Net debt swung from **−73.5bn net cash to +86.1bn net debt** (~RMB 160bn swing). Total debt 205.7 → 259.0bn.

**RF-5 — The EPS support mechanism was switched off.** Buybacks: 89.4bn (FY24) → 87.4bn (FY25) → **7.6bn (FY26)**, a 91% cut. Meanwhile RMB 33.7bn of dividends were paid in FY26 *with negative FCF* — balance-sheet-funded. Diluted shares have turned back up: Q1 FY27 2,415.5m vs 2,394.1m (+0.9% YoY). **Dilution has resumed precisely as the buyback stopped** — the opposite of the bull narrative.

**RF-6 — SG&A up 48% against 2.7% revenue growth.** SG&A/revenue 18.9% → 27.2%. This is the instant-commerce subsidy line. HSBC estimates Alibaba lost up to **RMB 87bn in instant retail over the trailing 12 months** — roughly the entire incremental SG&A.

**RF-7 — Accruals quality deteriorating, though not yet alarming.** Accruals ratio: −0.0725 (FY23) → −0.0589 (FY24) → −0.0192 (FY25) → **+0.0144 (FY26)**. An 8.7pp swing in three years. Still "ok" in absolute terms, but the direction is unambiguous.

**RF-8 — Extreme analyst dispersion, and consensus models its own rollover.** FY2029 EPS estimates range **46.9 to 162.4** (3.5× spread). The consensus path is EPS 44.0 (FY27) → 61.4 (FY28) → 78.7 (FY29) → **73.5 (FY30) — i.e. the sell side itself has EPS peaking in FY2029 and declining thereafter.** When consensus cannot bracket its own terminal year, the "cheap multiple" argument is unfalsifiable.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment — **Moat erosion is real, and the company is paying to hide it**

- **Core China commerce growth has stalled at low single digits.** Four of the last five quarters printed below 5% YoY (1.8%, 4.8%, 1.7%, 2.9%). The Q1 FY27 reacceleration to 8.3% was bought with subsidies, not won on product.
- **Instant commerce: share purchased, not earned.** Taobao Instant Commerce reached **45.7% share vs JD's 7.7%** — Alibaba "won." But the prize cost ~RMB 87bn of losses in 12 months, and RMB 50bn of consumer/merchant subsidies from July 2025. **A business whose unit economics require perpetual subsidy has no moat** — Meituan and JD can match any cheque, and Meituan consolidated further by acquiring Dingdong's China operations (Feb 2026, ~$717m). This is a three-way war of attrition in a commoditised 30-minute delivery market with zero switching costs.
- **Gross margin is holding (39.8%), which is the bull's best card** — pricing power at the platform/advertising layer has not collapsed. But gross profit is being consumed *below* the line by subsidies and will next be consumed by depreciation.
- **Cloud/AI is the one genuine franchise.** Mid-20s% growth with triple-digit AI-services growth is real. The question is whether it earns a return above its RMB 380bn cost of capital — currently unproven, and the D&A math in RF-3 is the test.
- **Regulatory/geopolitical overhang** is structural and permanent for a PRC-domiciled, VIE-structured ADR. Not a timing catalyst, but a permanent multiple ceiling.

### 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, but it is a quality-of-earnings and capital-cycle short, not a fraud short.**

The thesis in one paragraph: Alibaba is spending ~RMB 250bn+/yr on AI infrastructure and ~RMB 90bn/yr on instant-commerce subsidies to defend a core franchise growing 2.7%. Neither spend has demonstrated a return above cost of capital. The result is that **reported profit and cash generation have decoupled**: net income of 103.6bn against operating income of 59.7bn and FCF of −50.7bn. The buyback that masked this — RMB 176bn over FY24–FY25 — has been cut 91%, removing the marginal buyer and letting dilution resume. Depreciation on capex already sunk will rise ~2.5–3× and consume the entirety of current operating profit. Strip the non-operating gains and the core business trades at roughly **28× EV/operating earnings** (EV ≈ $232bn net of ~$14bn net cash, vs $8.4bn FY26 operating income) for a low-growth, cash-negative enterprise. **That is not a value stock; it is a leveraged bet on AI payback.**

**The other side — and it is strong:**

1. **Price has already done a lot of work.** $186 (Oct-2025) → $96 low (Jun-2026) → $105 now. **−44% YoY, −49% from peak.** Shorting here is late, not early.
2. **Headline multiple is undemanding.** 16.8× FY26 reported EPS; net cash of ~RMB 99bn; unlisted stakes (Ant, Cainiao) carried well below any plausible fair value. Sum-of-parts can be argued far above the current EV.
3. **The subsidy war may be de-escalating.** Reuters (Sep-2026) reports Chinese e-commerce "moving to the next phase after the delivery price war," with JD's segment losses narrowing significantly. If instant-commerce losses normalise, operating income snaps back toward RMB 100bn+ and the stock re-rates violently. **This is the primary squeeze risk and it is not small** — Alibaba holds 45.7% share of the segment.
4. **Cloud/AI is a genuine option.** Mid-20s% growth, triple-digit AI revenue growth, and a domestic AI-champion status that Western peers cannot replicate in China.
5. **Management has historically defended the stock** and retains the balance sheet (RMB 357.6bn cash + STI) to restart the buyback at any time.

**Where I land:** the bear case is *structurally correct but tactically late*. The clean short window was $150–190 in late 2025. At $105 with a de-escalation catalyst live and 45.7% segment share, the risk/reward has narrowed materially. This is a short to express with **defined risk and small size**, or better, as a **pair** (long the AI/cloud optionality via a peer, short BABA's cash-burn) rather than an outright.

**Invalidation triggers — cover immediately on:** (a) two consecutive quarters of positive FCF; (b) instant-commerce segment reaching breakeven or management disclosing a path to it; (c) buyback restarted at scale (>RMB 30bn/qtr); (d) cloud revenue growth >35% with stable margins; (e) operating margin recovering above 12%.

**Confirmation triggers — add on:** (a) a second consecutive operating loss quarter; (b) capex guided above RMB 300bn for FY2027 with no cloud-margin disclosure; (c) dividend cut or further buyback suspension; (d) net debt turning positive on a gross basis (cash + STI below total debt); (e) verified net insider selling from the Sep/Oct-2026 Form 4 cluster.
