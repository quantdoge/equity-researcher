## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT — but not along the axis you'd expect**

This is the most important structural finding of the synthesis: **there is near-unanimous agreement on demand and near-total disagreement on financing. Nobody is bearish on AI compute.**

- **High agreement (10 of 11 agents):** the backlog is real ($38.7B RPO), GPU pricing is rising, prepayments are genuine cash, and demand is not the constraint. Even the Short Analyst concedes the order book and prepayments are "the strongest possible customer-commitment signal."
- **High agreement (9 of 11):** the binding constraint is *delivered capacity + cost of capital*, not orders. New Jersey slipped, Vineland has stop-work orders, Birmingham is in litigation.
- **High agreement (8 of 11):** Q3 quality was poor — **$325M of $980.8M (33%) was company-flagged non-recurring**, ex which underlying growth decelerated to ~+13% QoQ from +46%.
- **Total split on the only question that sets the price:** intrinsic value ranges from **$0 to $171** across agents; leverage readings range from 2.6x to 13.9x purely on EBITDA-definition.

**Conflict resolution (per mandate):** Value vs Growth is the core clash. I resolve it in Value's favour on *method*, and against Growth on *anchor*. Growth's "5x FY28E EBITDA" is not cheap: with D&A at 36% of revenue, 5x EBITDA on a GPU fleet is roughly 14x EBIT *before* replacement capex on assets that must be entirely re-bought every 3–6 years. EBITDA is not a valid anchor for a depreciating-asset landlord. **The Short Analyst — not the Value Researcher — supplies the decisive analytical point**, and critically, he *exonerates* the name on fraud: accruals are clean, goodwill is trivial, prepayments are real. So there is **no fraud override** here. What remains is a leverage, duration and dilution problem.

**Net synthesis:** every bull case on this name is *conditioned on financing*. That makes NBIS a **capital-markets derivative wearing an AI-growth costume.** That is the single fact that should drive sizing.
