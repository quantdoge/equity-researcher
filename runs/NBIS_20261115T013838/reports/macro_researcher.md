## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional, tilting risk-off for long-duration equities**

As of 15 November 2026, the US is in a **reflation / "no-landing" regime with a Fed that has resumed tightening**. Real GDP grew 3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026 with unemployment at just 4.1% — the economy is not slowing. But CPI inflation has **re-accelerated to 3.82% YoY** (October 2026), up ~117bp from 2.65% in December 2025. The FOMC **hiked 25bp on 16 September 2026** to 3.75–4.00%, held in October, its September SEP median dots point to **4.00–4.25% for both end-2026 and 2027**, and Governor Barr has publicly said further hikes are likely. Market pricing implies a December hike plus two more.

The critical feature is the **shape of the curve, not the level of the funds rate**. Effective fed funds is 3.88% against 3.82% CPI — a **real policy rate of roughly zero**. The Fed is behind the curve, and the long end is repricing term premium accordingly: the 10Y has moved from ~4.45% in June 2026 to a peak of 5.31% on 5 October, settling near **5.10%**. This is a **bear steepening** (+39bp 2s10s), the signature of fiscal-dominance / inflation-risk-premium pricing rather than growth optimism.

Unusually, **credit is wide open**: HY OAS is 3.20% (12 Nov), up ~55bp from the 2.65% September low but still near cycle tights. Equity volatility in AI names coexists with complacent credit spreads — a divergence that matters enormously for a capital-hungry issuer like Nebius.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Nebius is an **AI neocloud**: Q3 2026 revenue $980.8M (+571% YoY, though $325M non-recurring), FY26 guidance cut to the **low end of $3.0–3.4B**, FY27 target **$12–13B** (~4x). Market cap ~$27.8B, beta 1.56, trading ~2.8x forward revenue. It is a **multi-year, capital-intensive, back-end-loaded cash flow story** — i.e., the single most rate-sensitive archetype in the market.

| Macro Variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **10Y / term premium at 5.1–5.5%** | **Strongly Negative** | Cash flows are weighted 2028–2032. Duration of this equity is ~15–20 years. The ~65bp rise in the 10Y since June plausibly removes **10–15%** of fair value on discount rate alone — before any earnings change. |
| **Fed hiking cycle / dots at 4.00–4.25%** | **Negative** | Kills the "falling-rate tailwind" that supported neocloud multiples in 2025. Also raises marginal cost on the debt/convert portion of a $10B+ buildout. |
| **Bear steepening (+39bp 2s10s)** | **Negative** | Data centre assets are financed long. The 30Y at 5.47% is the relevant hurdle rate for 15-year GPU-hosting contracts — the worst possible curve shape for this business model. |
| **CPI 3.82% / GPU pricing +20% QoQ** | **Net Positive** | Rare genuine pricing power: GPU-hour rental rates rose >20% sequentially. Nebius is passing inflation through rather than absorbing it. This is the strongest macro offset. |
| **Electricity CPI +4.4% YoY** | **Negative** | Power is the dominant non-GPU opex and the binding constraint (the New Jersey site delay is a power/interconnect issue, not a demand issue). Grid constraints are a structural margin headwind. |
| **GDP 3.4% + AI capex supercycle** | **Strongly Positive** | Hyperscaler capex ~$697B in 2026 (JPM); AI DC capex ~$3.2tn over three years. Demand is not the risk. |
| **USD +3.4% since September** | **Mildly Negative** | Revenues are USD-denominated, but the EUR/ILS cost base translates cheaper (small positive). The net negative is liquidity: a strong dollar plus 5% US yields tightens offshore dollar funding — precisely the channel that finances Nebius's AI-startup customer base. Netherlands-domiciled, EUR-functional exposure is modest. |
| **HY OAS 3.20%** | **Strongly Positive** | The **largest single offset**. Capital markets are open — CoreWeave closed $8.5B of investment-grade GPU-backed financing in Q2 2026. Nebius also has customer prepayments covering **>100% of associated capex**, structurally reducing reliance on the bond market. |
| **Tariffs / Section 232 on semis** | **Negative (risk)** | GPUs are USD-priced and imported; semiconductor tariffs would inflate capex per MW directly. EU–US trade friction adds a second layer given the Dutch domicile and Finnish data centres. |

**Rate-sensitivity estimate:** each 100bp on the long end is worth roughly **-15% to -20%** on NBIS's equity value via duration alone, versus maybe -7% for the S&P. This explains the tape: NBIS is **-33% over three months** and fell **~17% on 12 November** despite beating revenue and posting a fourth consecutive positive adjusted EBITDA quarter. The selloff was a guidance trim plus capex bill — but the amplification is macro (discount rates), not operational.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

But with an important decomposition: the **operating macro is favourable** (demand insatiable, pricing power +20% QoQ, EBITDA positive), while the **financial macro is hostile** (real policy rate ~0%, 10Y at 5.1%, 30Y at 5.5%, term premium expanding, Fed hiking). For a 2.8x-forward-revenue, capex-heavy, long-duration equity, the discount rate dominates near-term returns. The tight HY spread is the one meaningful mitigant and the reason this is *Unfavourable* rather than *Severe*.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario — Bear (funding-market accident)

**Consensus assumes the Fed stops near 4.25% and the 10Y stabilises near 5%.** The non-consensus path: with real policy at zero, 3.4% growth and CPI at 3.8%, the Fed is forced to **4.75–5.00% by mid-2027**, and fiscal supply plus a loss of confidence in inflation targeting drives the **10Y through 6%**. The 2s10s steepens to 100bp+.

Transmission to Nebius is brutal and specific: (a) the equity de-rates ~25–30% on duration alone; (b) the **30Y at 6.25%** reprices the entire data centre buildout, and project-finance lenders — already flagged by Moody's on grid constraints — pull back; (c) HY spreads normalize from 3.20% toward 5%, shutting the GPU-backed ABS market that funds the sector; (d) offshore dollar tightening cracks the AI-startup customer base, converting demand risk from theoretical to actual. The prepayment cushion (>100% of associated capex) protects existing capacity but not the FY27 ramp to $12–13B, which requires incremental capital.

The mirror-image bull case: inflation rolls over to ~3% by Q1 2027, the Fed signals a pause, the 10Y retraces to 4.25%, and NBIS re-rates 30–40% on top of 4x revenue growth. **Note the asymmetry: this stock is now far more levered to the 10Y than to GPU demand.** Position sizing should reflect that the macro variable that matters is the long bond, not the AI narrative.
