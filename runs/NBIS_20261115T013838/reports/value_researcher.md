# Nebius Group N.V. (NBIS) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price $115.88 (13 Nov 2026) | Market cap ≈ $34.8B | Net debt $8.5B | EV ≈ $43.3B**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NONE**

Nebius is a "neocloud": it buys Nvidia GPUs, houses them in data centers, and rents compute by the hour to AI developers and hyperscalers. Revenue is ~95% AI cloud; the remainder is edtech (TripleTen), data services (Toloka) and autonomous driving (Avride) — all subscale.

Testing the five moat sources:

| Moat source | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Intangible assets** | None durable. No proprietary chip, no exclusive IP. Nvidia sells the same silicon to CoreWeave, Lambda, Crusoe, IREN and the hyperscalers themselves. |
| **Switching costs** | Low. AI training workloads are increasingly portable across clouds; customers deliberately multi-source to avoid lock-in. |
| **Network effects** | None. Renting a GPU to customer A does not make the platform more valuable to customer B. |
| **Cost advantage** | None demonstrated. Nebius pays market price for GPUs and power. Scale is *smaller* than AWS/Azure/GCP, so it lacks purchasing leverage. |
| **Efficient scale** | Not applicable — this is a land-grab market with dozens of funded entrants. |

The clearest evidence of no moat is the financial record: **ROIC has been negative in every one of the last four years** (−2.7%, −7.4%, −12.1%, −6.4%). A moated business earns returns above its cost of capital through a cycle. Nebius has never done so.

The one genuine asset is **contracted demand**: RPO of **$38.7B** at 30 Sep 2026, anchored by a multi-year Microsoft agreement and a five-year, up-to-$27B Meta deal. That is real and valuable — but it is a *backlog*, not a moat. It converts to revenue only if Nebius spends $40–45B on capex in 2027 alone. The contracts are effectively a promise to deploy capital at whatever return the market allows.

**Moat trend: narrowing.** Gross margin fell from 68.6% (FY25) to 41.4% in Q3'26 as third-party colocation capacity was layered in. Management "raised prices" in Q3 — that is scarcity pricing at a cyclical peak, not pricing power.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **2 / 10**

| Metric | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| TTM revenue | $2.19B | Q3'26 alone $980.8M, +571% YoY, +68% QoQ |
| 2026E revenue | ~$3.0B (low end of $3.0–3.4B) | Guidance trimmed on capacity delays |
| 2027E revenue | $12–13B | Management outlook; requires flawless execution |
| Q3'26 adj. EBITDA | $465.8M (47.5% margin) | Genuine inflection — first real operating leverage |
| TTM EBITDA | ~$0.6B | Q4'25 and Q1'26 were near zero/negative |
| **TTM free cash flow** | **−$12.4B** | Q3'26 alone: −$7.6B |
| Capex | $4.1B (FY25) → ~$27B (2026E) → $40–45B (2027E) | |
| Net debt | $8.5B (from net *cash* of $2.4B at YE24) | Total debt $16.0B |
| Shareholders' equity | $12.3B | |
| ROIC | −6.4% | Negative four years running |
| FCF conversion | Negative | Owner earnings −$3.57B (FY25) |

**The core problem:** this business consumes capital faster than any business I can value. In Q3'26 Nebius spent $7.3B of capex to generate $466M of EBITDA — a 6% quarterly cash return on deployed capital before depreciation. PP&E is $21.5B and rising; D&A is already $420M/quarter ($1.7B annualized) and will compound as the 2026–27 build depreciates. GPU useful lives are 5–6 years, so the depreciation is real economic cost, not accounting noise.

**Financing gap:** 2027 capex of $40–45B against $7.4B of cash and equity of $12.3B. Even assuming $8–10B of 2027 operating cash flow, Nebius must raise **$25–30B+** externally. At today's price, funding that with equity implies roughly 60–80% dilution. Debt is already $16B and interest expense is running $112M/quarter and climbing.

**Data quality caveat:** the reported statements are internally inconsistent (Q3'26 operating income of +$50.4M cannot be reconciled with $406M gross profit less $237M SG&A less $420M D&A; FY25 net income differs between the income statement and cash flow statement; Q2'26 gross margin of 77% versus Q3'26's 41% implies a reclassification). I have relied on revenue, adj. EBITDA, capex and balance-sheet figures, which are corroborated by the earnings release. Investors should not treat the GAAP line items as clean.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

Traditional multiples are close to meaningless here, so I anchor on EV and on scenario-weighted outcomes.

**Current multiples**
- EV / TTM revenue: 19.8x
- EV / 2026E revenue ($3.0B): 14.4x
- EV / 2027E revenue ($12.5B): **3.5x**
- EV / TTM EBITDA: ~71x
- EV / annualized Q3'26 EBITDA ($1.86B): 23x
- EV / 2027E EBITDA (45% margin → $5.6B): **7.7x**
- P/B: 2.8x | P/E: meaningless (351x on inflated FY25 GAAP earnings)

The bull case is entirely contained in that 7.7x 2027E EV/EBITDA. If Nebius delivers $12.5B of revenue at 45% margins, the stock is cheap. That is a very large "if."

**Scenario-weighted intrinsic value (2028 horizon, ~380M diluted shares)**

| Scenario | Prob. | 2028 revenue | EBITDA margin | EV/EBITDA | Net debt | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** — capacity lands, AI capex persists | 25% | $25B | 30% | 12x | $25B | **~$160** |
| **Base** — growth but margin compression, dilution | 50% | $18B | 25% | 10x | $25B | **~$53** |
| **Bear** — AI capex digestion, GPU oversupply, equity raise at low price | 25% | $10B | 15% | 8x | $25B | **~$0** |

**Probability-weighted value ≈ $67/share**, roughly **42% below** the current $115.88.

Note the asymmetry: the bear case is not a modest loss, it is a near-total loss of equity, because $25B+ of net debt sits ahead of shareholders in a business whose assets (GPUs) depreciate 20%+ per year and have a thin secondhand market. This is a levered bet on the durability of the AI capex cycle.

**What the current price implies:** ~$43B EV requires roughly $4–5B of sustainable EBITDA at a 10x multiple. That is achievable in 2027 *if* guidance is met — but it leaves zero margin of safety for the $40B+ of dilution required to get there.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Mixed, trending negative on capital allocation**

**Positives:** Founder-led. Arkady Volozh built Yandex into a genuine technology franchise and retains meaningful ownership; the team has real infrastructure engineering depth and moved with extraordinary speed (revenue up 571% YoY). Securing Microsoft and Meta as anchor tenants is a strong commercial achievement. Nvidia's equity stake is a useful signal and may confer allocation priority.

**Negatives:**
- **Capital allocation is the whole story, and it is aggressive.** Committing $40–45B of 2027 capex against $12.3B of equity, in a business that has never earned a positive ROIC, is the definition of empire-building at a cyclical peak. Buffett's test — "does a dollar retained create more than a dollar of value?" — cannot be answered affirmatively here.
- **Guidance credibility is impaired.** 2026 revenue was trimmed to the low end on third-party capacity slippage; the >$7B ARR target slipped from December to February. Two misses in one quarter.
- **No capital returned** — no dividend, no buyback, and none possible for years.
- **Dilution is the likely path.** Share count has already risen from 247.7M (FY25 diluted) to 298.0M (Q3'26 diluted), +20% in nine months, before the large raise that 2027 capex necessitates.

Honest and technically capable, but the incentive structure rewards scale over returns on capital.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

This is a well-run, fast-growing company that fails every test I apply.

- **Circle of competence:** I cannot forecast the economics of a GPU-rental business ten years out. The asset base turns over every 5–6 years, pricing is set by a supply race, and the demand curve depends on whether hyperscaler AI capex persists. There is no predictable owner-earnings stream to discount.
- **Moat:** None. Negative ROIC in all four years of available history.
- **Financial quality:** TTM FCF of −$12.4B; a $25–30B external financing requirement in 2027; net debt already $8.5B.
- **Margin of safety:** Absent. Even a generous probability-weighted intrinsic value of ~$67 sits 42% *below* the current price. The stock is down 61% from its June 2026 high of $299 and still is not cheap on my numbers.

The stock is a **levered, dilutive call option on the AI infrastructure cycle**, not an equity ownership stake in a moated business. That can be a legitimate trade. It is not a value investment.

**If you already own it:** the position sizing question matters more than the price. Treat it as a venture-style holding, not a core compounder.

**What would change my mind:** (1) two consecutive quarters of positive free cash flow after maintenance capex; (2) capex guidance cut to a level fundable from operating cash flow; (3) ROIC crossing above WACC; (4) evidence of genuine multi-year customer lock-in (multi-year take-or-pay with pricing escalators, not just RPO). None of these are close.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to the bear case (i.e., what could make me wrong):**
1. **RPO conversion.** $38.7B of contracted backlog against a $43B EV is genuinely cheap if delivered at even modest margins. Microsoft and Meta are creditworthy counterparties.
2. **Operating leverage is real.** EBITDA margin went from negative to 47.5% in three quarters. If that holds at $12B+ of revenue, the equity is worth multiples of today's price.
3. **Scarcity rents.** If GPU capacity stays tight through 2027–28, pricing power persists longer than a cyclical view implies.
4. **Strategic value.** Nvidia's stake and hyperscaler dependence could make NBIS an acquisition target at a premium.

**Risks to the bull case (the ones I weight most heavily):**
1. **Financing/dilution risk** — the dominant risk. $25–30B+ to raise against a $34.8B market cap, with the stock already down 61%. A distressed raise could permanently impair per-share value.
2. **Customer concentration** — Microsoft and Meta likely represent the majority of RPO. Either renegotiating or in-sourcing capacity is catastrophic.
3. **GPU obsolescence** — Nvidia's annual cadence (Hopper → Blackwell → Rubin) can strand capacity. A $21.5B PP&E base with 5–6 year lives is exposed to technological writedowns.
4. **Margin structure** — Q3'26 gross margin of 41.4% (from 68.6%) suggests third-party capacity is being resold at thin spreads. If the model is "buy capacity at 40% gross margin and depreciate it," the terminal economics are poor.
5. **Cycle risk** — if hyperscaler AI capex decelerates in 2028, Nebius will be holding $40B+ of depreciating assets and $25B+ of debt into a spot-pricing collapse. This is the bear case, and it is not remote.
6. **Governance/jurisdiction** — Dutch-domiciled, Yandex legacy, complex multi-entity structure (Avride, Toloka, TripleTen), limited disclosure granularity.

---

**Bottom line:** Superb growth, no moat, negative returns on capital, and a capital program that requires the market's continued cooperation. At $115.88 the market is paying for the bull case and getting no protection from the bear case. **Sell.**
