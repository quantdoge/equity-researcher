# ESG Analysis — Nebius Group N.V. (NASDAQ: NBIS)

**Sector:** Technology / AI Cloud Infrastructure ("neocloud") | **Domicile:** Netherlands (Amsterdam HQ) | **Market cap:** ~$27.8bn | **Employees:** ~1,543
**Business:** AI cloud (GPU clusters, PUE-optimised data centres), Toloka (data solutions/crowdwork), TripleTen (edtech), Avride (autonomous driving). Formerly Yandex N.V.; rebranded Aug 2024 after divesting Russian assets.

---

## 1. ESG Materiality Map

For a neocloud, ESG is unusually *financially* material because the binding constraints on the growth model are physical and social, not just reputational.

| Factor | Materiality | Transmission to cash flow / cost of capital |
|---|---|---|
| **E — Energy & carbon intensity** | **Critical** | Power is the #1 input cost and the #1 gating factor to gigawatt-scale buildout. Grid carbon intensity and PPA availability determine both opex and access to capital. |
| **E — Scope 3 / embodied carbon (GPUs, construction)** | **High** | Likely the dominant share of true footprint; undisclosed. Exposes the company to CSRD assurance challenges and EU Taxonomy alignment claims. |
| **E — Water & cooling** | Medium | Liquid cooling is a genuine mitigant; water stress affects site selection in US Southwest/South. |
| **E — Physical climate risk (flooding, heat)** | **High** | Vineland, NJ site already litigating stormwater management. Flood/heat exposure at 7+ sites is a capex and downtime risk. |
| **E — E-waste / GPU circularity** | Medium | ~3-year GPU refresh cycles create a growing disposal/recovery liability and a write-down vector. |
| **S — Community social licence & permitting** | **Critical** | Directly gates capacity delivery. Already realised as a >$1m fine and active federal litigation at the flagship US site. |
| **S — Data privacy & cybersecurity** | **High** | EU processor obligations (GDPR, NIS2, DORA); enterprise/Sovereign AI contracts require SOC 2, ISO 27001, HIPAA. A breach is a licence-to-operate event. |
| **S — Labour standards in supply chain (Toloka)** | **High** | Gig crowdwork model carries human-rights exposure; prior Russia surveillance-training allegations are a live reputational overhang. |
| **S — Workforce safety (construction)** | Medium | Rapid buildout at 7+ sites; contractor safety incidents are an operational and reputational risk. |
| **G — Controlled company / dual-class** | **Critical** | ~52% voting control by founder-CEO; Nasdaq controlled-company status. Subordinates all other governance protections. |
| **G — Capital allocation & dilution** | **High** | $40–45bn capex plan vs. ~$28bn market cap; serial equity issuance. Governance of dilution is a first-order shareholder-value question. |
| **G — Sanctions legacy / geopolitical** | **High** | Founder's prior EU sanctions listing and Yandex lineage remain a screening and counterparty-diligence issue. |

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

**Genuine strengths (better than sector):**
- **PUE 1.25** portfolio-wide in 2025 vs. Uptime Institute global average of **1.54**; best monthly PUE of **1.15** at an owned facility. In-house server design claimed to cut energy use ~20% vs. off-the-shelf.
- **80% of data-centre energy from low-carbon sources, 62% renewable** (location-based); **100% renewable electricity in Iceland, France and UK**; Finland transitioned from 95% low-carbon to 100% renewable.
- **Water usage effectiveness of 0.018 L/kWh** at Finland-1 via closed-loop liquid cooling, vs. ~0.45–0.49 L/kWh projected US average — a ~25x advantage.
- **19.5 GWh of waste heat** exported to Mäntsälä district heating, cutting local heat emissions ~65% (~4,000 tCO₂e avoided).
- Discloses under ESRS as voluntary reference, with a **formal double materiality and climate risk assessment** (2025) — ahead of most neocloud peers.

**Why the rating is still High:**
- **Scope 1+2 (market-based) rose 32x to 65,001 tCO₂e in 2025 from 2,036 tCO₂e in 2024**, driven by expansion to seven active sites; 99% from data-centre operations. This is a *trajectory* problem, not a level problem — absolute emissions are trivial today but scale linearly with gigawatts.
- **Scope 3 is not disclosed at all.** The company states its reporting boundaries and supplier data "are still being developed" and available data "would not support a representative inventory." For a business whose footprint is overwhelmingly embodied carbon in Nvidia GPUs and steel/concrete construction, this is the single largest disclosure gap.
- **No quantified emissions-reduction or resource-use targets.** A 32x increase with no stated pathway is uninvestable for climate-aligned mandates.
- **Behind-the-meter gas:** the US strategy relies on **Bloom Energy fuel cells and 62 gas-powered generators** at Vineland — the source of the current regulatory action (see §5). This converts the "renewable AI cloud" narrative into a fossil-fuelled one in its largest growth market.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Community social licence is the binding constraint.** Organised opposition is now on record at multiple sites: Vineland NJ (Sustain SJ, Earthjustice), plus contested-site trackers and local opposition around the Lille, France project. The Vineland Planning Board approved the site plan *over* residents' and expert testimony — a decision now being litigated.
- **Regulatory enforcement already crystallised:** the **New Jersey DEP issued an Administrative Order and civil penalty assessment of over $1 million** against DataOne for operating 62 unpermitted generators. This is a realised, quantified social/environmental cost.
- **Toloka human-rights overhang:** a 2024 Bureau of Investigative Journalism investigation found Russian gig workers on the Toloka platform unwittingly trained facial-recognition tools used to detain Moscow activists. Toloka states the engagement ran through a Russian subsidiary now divested. Residual risk: continued crowdwork-model labour scrutiny, unpaid rejected work, sub-$20/hr task pay, and EU platform-work directive exposure.
- **Mitigants:** community programmes are substantive rather than purely cosmetic — Nebius Academy partnerships with **16 universities**, Rowan University (NJ) pathways, skilled-trades apprenticeships, first-responder support, and local tax-base contributions. A dedicated Head of Sustainability sits in management.
- **Positive on data trust:** broad certification stack (SOC 2/3, ISO 27001 family, HIPAA, NIS2, DORA) and EU-processor GDPR positioning — this is a commercial asset for Sovereign/regulated-workload AI sales, not just compliance.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **4.5 / 10**

**Positives:**
- **Independent non-executive Chairman** (John Boynton) — CEO/Chair roles are separated.
- **5 of 8 directors independent** (Boynton, Grimme, Radinsky, Ryan, Weigand) = 62.5%, with a diverse matrix and credible international tech/finance profiles.
- **Clawback policy adopted** (filed as Exhibit 97.1 to the 20-F/A) — a genuine, if table-stakes, alignment mechanism.
- Voluntary ESRS-aligned reporting with third-party-referenced methodology and a public governance document set.
- Clean structural separation from Russian assets, with the EU lifting sanctions on Volozh in **March 2024** after he publicly condemned the war and committed to full divestment.

**Why only 4.5:**
- **Controlled company.** Arkady Volozh holds **~52% of voting control** (as of 31 March 2026) and Nebius **qualifies as a Nasdaq "controlled company."** It is therefore exempt from majority-independent board and independent compensation/nominating committee requirements. Minority holders cannot effect change on any matter.
- **Dual-class structure:** each **Class B share carries 10 votes** and converts into one Class A plus one Class C share — a permanent entrenchment mechanism, not a sunset.
- **Founder-CEO with concentrated voting power** in a capital-intensive, rapidly-diluting business is the weakest possible alignment configuration. The $40–45bn capex plan against a ~$28bn market cap implies heavy future issuance, and the controlling holder's interests may diverge from minority holders on dilution pace and price.
- **Related-party complexity:** four businesses (Toloka, TripleTen, Avride, Nebius) under one holding, with affiliate transactions and deconsolidation events (the group ceased to hold majority voting power in a subsidiary during Q3 2026) that warrant close related-party scrutiny.
- **Short public-company ESG track record** — three reporting cycles only, all post-restructuring.
- **Sanctions lineage** remains a live diligence flag for sovereign, defence-adjacent and some European institutional counterparties.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU CSRD / ESRS** — Nebius is a Dutch N.V. with >250 employees and >€50m turnover, so it is in scope as a **large EU undertaking irrespective of its Nasdaq listing**. It already reports ESRS as voluntary reference, which de-risks the transition, but **limited assurance on Scope 1+2 and eventual Scope 3** will be the pressure point. Note the EU Omnibus simplification may delay wave 2/3 timelines — a tailwind to near-term compliance cost.
- **EU Energy Efficiency Directive — data centre reporting:** facilities above 500 kW must report energy performance annually. Nebius's EU sites (Finland, France, Iceland, UK-adjacent) are in scope; its PUE/WUE disclosure already exceeds the requirement.
- **EU Taxonomy** — data centre activities are eligible; alignment requires climate-risk assessment and DNSH compliance. Absent Scope 3 and quantified targets, taxonomy-aligned revenue share is hard to substantiate — a constraint on green-bond/loan marketing.
- **NIS2, DORA, GDPR** — Nebius operates as an EU data processor serving financial-services and healthcare clients; management-system certifications are in place. **EU AI Act** applies indirectly (Avride autonomous systems; customer model deployment) with penalties up to €35m/7% of turnover.
- **US:** SEC climate disclosure rule remains stayed — no near-term federal requirement. **Clean Air Act** and **NJ stormwater/zoning law** are the live, binding regimes. California SB 253/261 would apply if the revenue threshold is met in CA.
- **CBAM:** negligible direct exposure (not a steel/cement/aluminium importer), though embedded carbon in EU construction is an indirect cost channel.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** debt is currently conventional — a **$775m first secured facility (Jul 2026)** and a **€250m raise for the Lille data centre**. No sustainability-linked pricing ratchets identified, so no near-term covenant step-up risk, but also no pricing incentive to decarbonise. *Gap: covenant schedules not independently verified.*

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE — with negative momentum; "ESG Risk" for sustainability-mandated capital**

The components diverge sharply and should not be averaged away:
- **Operational environmental engineering: Leader.** PUE 1.25, WUE 0.018, heat recovery, low-carbon siting — genuinely best-in-class for a neocloud.
- **Environmental disclosure & trajectory: Laggard.** 32x emissions growth, no Scope 3, no targets.
- **Governance: Laggard.** Controlled company, 10:1 dual class, founder-CEO with ~52% of votes.
- **Social: High risk.** Active federal environmental litigation and community opposition at the flagship US site.

A generalist investor can own this as an **Average** ESG name. An Article 8/9 fund, or any mandate with a controlled-company or clean-air screen, will treat it as **ESG Risk / excluded**.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL to NEGATIVE (a risk modifier, not the thesis driver)**

ESG is not the primary valuation driver for NBIS — the AI-capacity growth story is. But two ESG factors are **already transmitting into cash flows** and should be underwritten explicitly:

1. **Permitting and social licence is the real bottleneck, and it has already bitten.** The Vineland Clean Air Act notice, the >$1m NJDEP penalty, and the zoning/stormwater suit together represent a credible path to **construction stoppage or generator shutdown at the highest-value US site**. This is arguably the most concrete, near-dated downside risk in the entire thesis — and it is an *ESG* risk, not a market risk. Management has guided to $12–13bn of 2027 revenue; that number is only as good as the permits behind it.
2. **The gas-generator reliance contradicts the "low-carbon AI cloud" narrative** precisely where growth is fastest (US). This raises the cost of future capital — green/sustainability-linked financing is unavailable without Scope 3 and targets, so the $40–45bn capex programme will be funded with conventional debt and dilutive equity.
3. **Governance caps the re-rating.** At ~52% voting control with a 10:1 dual class and a controlled-company exemption, minority shareholders have no recourse on capital allocation or dilution pace. For a company that will plausibly issue equity repeatedly, this is a permanent valuation discount factor, not a temporary one.

**Where ESG is a positive:** best-in-class PUE/WUE and heat recovery are a **real cost advantage** (energy is the largest opex line) and a genuine commercial differentiator for EU Sovereign-AI and regulated-workload contracts. If management publishes Scope 3, sets a quantified net-zero pathway, and resolves Vineland permitting, the ESG overhang compresses quickly and the "responsible AI infrastructure" positioning becomes an asset rather than a liability.

**Recommendation to the investment committee:** Do not treat ESG as neutral boilerplate here. **Escalate the Vineland litigation to the core risk register**, and request (a) the FY2025 20-F related-party note, (b) a Scope 3 roadmap with a date, and (c) disclosure of unpermitted/behind-the-meter generation across the US portfolio before sizing the position.
