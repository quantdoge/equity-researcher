# Nebius Group N.V. (NBIS) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Price $115.88 (13 Nov 2026) · ~298M shares → ~$34.5B mkt cap · −61% from June 2026 high of $286.69**
*Note: vendor market cap of $28.7B uses a stale 248M share count. Diluted shares rose 251.8M → 298.0M between Q4'25 and Q3'26 (+18% in three quarters). All ratios below use ~$34.5B.*

## 1. Growth Story

Nebius is the purest listed expression of the **AI compute shortage**: a full-stack "neocloud" that builds GPU data centers and rents them on multi-year contracts to the two largest buyers of AI capacity on earth. In under two years it has gone from $9.8M of revenue (FY2023) to a **$980.8M quarter growing 571% YoY**, backed by **>$40B of contracted backlog** (Microsoft up to ~$50B, Meta $27B over five years) and **>3GW of contracted power**. The thesis is that AI infrastructure demand is running years ahead of supply, and Nebius — with NVIDIA Exemplar Cloud status, a $2B NVIDIA warrant investment, and EU data-sovereignty positioning — can convert contracted power and backlog into a $12–13B revenue business in 2027. The entire debate is no longer about demand; it is about **who pays for the buildout and on what terms**.

**Growth quality read:**
- **Organic and demand-pulled, not acquired.** Software tuck-ins (Tavily, Eigen, Clarifai) are immaterial to revenue.
- **Revenue acceleration is real:** Q1'26 $399M → Q2 $582M (+46% QoQ) → Q3 $981M (+68% QoQ).
- **The Q4 problem:** guiding FY26 to the *low end* of $3.0–3.4B implies Q4 of only ~$1.04B, i.e. **+6% QoQ after +68%** — a hard deceleration management attributed to capacity delivery delays. This, not the beat, is what broke the stock.
- **Gross margin compressed 1,850bp in one quarter** (77.1% → 58.6%) as new capacity's depreciation and power costs hit the P&L ahead of utilization. Watch whether this is a timing trough or a structural reset. The offsetting positive: **Q3 was the first GAAP operating profit (+$50.4M)** despite $420M of quarterly D&A.
- **Rule of 40 is analytically useless here** (vendor computes −215.8 on FY25; FCF-margin basis is −772% in Q3). The honest framing: 571% growth + positive operating margin = far above 40 on an operating basis, but **capex intensity of 9x revenue (FY26) and 3.3x (FY27) makes FCF negative by design**. This is a build-out, not a cash-generating business — yet.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

- **TAM:** Global AI data-center capex is running into the hundreds of billions annually and is still supply-constrained. The *rentable third-party GPU cloud* (neocloud SAM) is the relevant slice — plausibly $150–250B+ annually by 2028–30 as hyperscalers outsource overflow and model labs diversify off AWS/Azure/GCP.
- **Penetration:** Nebius at $3.0B (2026) is capturing low-single-digit percent of that SAM. Capacity, not demand, is the binding constraint — the company has **>3GW contracted power today targeting >4GW by end-2026**, and at industry norms of roughly $10B of annual revenue per GW, 4GW underwrites a $40B revenue ceiling. Consensus' $57B for 2030 is *consistent* with that footprint, which is the bull case in one line.
- **Differentiation:** (1) NVIDIA Exemplar Cloud status on GB300/Blackwell NVL72 = privileged allocation in a supply-constrained GPU market; (2) EU data-sovereignty — on-continent capacity that US hyperscalers cannot easily replicate for regulated workloads; (3) behind-the-meter power via a $2.6B Bloom Energy fuel-cell deal, attacking the real bottleneck; (4) software layer (Token Factory inference, Aether) to lift blended margins off pure GPU rent.
- **Penetration risk:** the moat is **capital and power access, not proprietary technology**. CoreWeave, IREN, Applied Digital and hyperscaler self-build all compete for the same GPUs, the same electrons, and the same two anchor customers.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4/FY2026 results & 2027 capex pacing — ~11 Feb 2027.** Must show Q4 re-acceleration (consensus $1.26B) and a credible path to 4GW. Highest-impact near-term event.
- **The financing package (ongoing, next 1–2 quarters).** $40–45B of 2027 capex against a $34.5B market cap cannot be self-funded. Watch for: additional debt (they raised $5.75B in Q3), SPV/ABS structures, further **customer prepayments** (guided $9B+), vendor financing, and — the dilution risk — equity issuance. Cost of capital is the whole investment case.
- **Meta/Microsoft contract expansions and a third anchor customer (2027).** Backlog moving from ">$40B" to "$60B+" would de-risk the capex mathematically.
- **Capacity deliveries:** Independence MO, Finland and France data centers; GB300/Blackwell Ultra ramp; Bloom fuel-cell energization.
- **Policy:** US ballot initiatives restricting data-center development and shifting political control are a live permitting risk; EU sovereignty demand is the offsetting tailwind.
- **NVIDIA overhang:** Jensen Huang's November 2026 resignation injects headline risk into the entire neocloud complex — NBIS is a high-beta proxy on NVIDIA's ecosystem.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (not Strong Buy)

On growth-company math the stock is cheap *if the plan is financed*. At ~$34.5B market cap you are paying **~2.8x FY2027E revenue (~$12.5B), ~1.4x FY2028E revenue ($25.3B), ~19x FY2027E EBITDA ($2.0B) and ~5x FY2028E EBITDA ($7.2B)** for a business compounding >100% with an ended backlog exceeding $40B. That is not a premium multiple for this growth rate — it's a discount that prices in real financing doubt. The company guided 2027 revenue *above* consensus ($12–13B vs. $11.98B), turned GAAP operating-profit positive, and holds NVIDIA's balance-sheet endorsement ($2B warrants; $329M of shares at 30 June).

But I will not pay up for it: **$40–45B of 2027 capex against a $34.5B market cap, −$7.6B of quarterly FCF, 18% share-count growth in three quarters, interest expense up 77% QoQ, and 1,850bp of gross-margin compression** mean the equity is effectively a levered bet on continuous capital-markets access. "Demand is real, financing is just a task" is the CEO's framing; for equity holders, the task *is* the risk. **Buy, sized as a high-volatility position** — accumulate on financing clarity, and treat a large equity raise at a depressed price as the thesis-invalidating event.

## 7. Key Risks

- **Financing/dilution (highest severity).** Cumulative 2026–27 capex of ~$67–72B is ~2x the market cap. If debt markets stay unsettled (CoreWeave bond yields >16%), the marginal dollar arrives as equity — potentially at 2x+ your cost basis.
- **Customer concentration.** Meta and Microsoft are the backbone of a ">$40B" backlog whose values are largely "up to" ceilings with no take-or-pay guarantee. Two counterparties hold all the negotiating leverage — and each has its own incentive to self-build.
- **Unit-economics risk.** Gross margin falling from 77% to 59% in one quarter shows depreciation and power hitting before utilization. If per-GW revenue economics settle below plan, the 2028–30 EBITDA estimates collapse.
- **Execution/delivery risk.** The Q4 guide cut was explicitly a capacity-delay problem. Power interconnection, permitting (US ballot initiatives), and GB300 allocation are all outside management's control.
- **Hardware obsolescence.** 5–6 year GPU depreciable lives against an 18–24 month architectural cadence (Rubin, then beyond) is an asset-liability mismatch that the P&L will eventually adjudicate.
- **Competitive commoditization.** GPU-hours are converging on a commodity; differentiation rests on power access and NVIDIA allocation, neither of which is a durable IP moat.
- **Governance/legacy structure.** Dutch-domiciled, ex-Yandex, with non-core holdings (ClickHouse stake, Toloka, Avride, TripleTen) that have repeatedly injected non-operating gains into headline EPS (Q2'25 +$584M, Q1'26 +$621M net income on operating *losses*). Use adjusted figures; ignore reported EPS.
