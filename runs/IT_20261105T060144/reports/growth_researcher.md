# Gartner, Inc. (NYSE: IT) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $207.19 | Market cap ~$13.1B (64M sh.) | EV ~$14.6B | Net debt ~$1.4B**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Gartner is the closest thing enterprise IT has to a standard-setting institution: its Magic Quadrants and benchmark data anchor how CIOs allocate hundreds of billions in technology spend, sold via subscription Contract Value (CV) at ~68% gross margins and ~34% ROIC. But the growth thesis has inverted — this is no longer a compounder being priced for durability; it is an **incumbent whose core product is the single most obvious AI-disintermediation target in business information** (LLMs can summarize vendor landscapes, which is Gartner's syndicated research product). The stock has de-rated from ~$548 (Feb-2025) to $207, and the debate is now binary: is $5.3B of CV a melting ice cube, or a moat that AI makes *more* valuable because enterprises need trusted, primary-source judgment when AI ROI is failing 40% of the time?

Importantly, the tape has begun to disagree with the bear case: CV growth has accelerated for three consecutive quarters (+2.5% YoY, +2.8% ex-US federal), Q3-26 adjusted EPS beat by 26%, and guidance was raised — yet the stock fell 13% on the print. That gap between operating reality and sentiment is where the investment question lives.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **TAM:** Gartner's own forecast puts global IT spend at ~$5.6T in 2026 heading toward ~$7.3T by 2027, with AI agent software alone going from $86.4B (2025) to $206.5B (2026) and $376.3B (2027). The relevant **SAM — enterprise research & advisory plus decision-support consulting — is roughly $250–300B**, and adjacent business-function advisory (GBS, $1.3B CV) is largely unpenetrated.
- **Penetration:** $5.3B CV against that SAM is **~2%** — mathematically enormous runway. **Penetration is not the binding constraint.** The binding constraints are (a) seat-based pricing exposed to enterprise headcount cuts, and (b) AI commoditizing the *synthesis* layer of the product.
- **Segment mix (Q3-26):** GTS CV $4.0B (+2%, retention 98%, **new business -4%**); GBS CV $1.3B (+3.7%, retention 99%, **new business -3%**, core subscriptions +7%); Insights revenue +2% with 78% contribution margin; Conferences +8% same-conference; Consulting +2%, backlog +9%; **Contract Optimization $150M LTM at ~13% 2-yr CAGR** — the clearest AI-era green shoot (vendors' AI-driven contract sprawl needs negotiation help).
- **Moat verdict:** brand/benchmark standard + proprietary benchmark data + executive peer networks + 98–99% wallet retention = a real moat. But high retention with **shrinking new business is the classic melting-ice-cube signature**: the installed base renews while the funnel narrows.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4-26 / FY26 results & FY27 guide (~Feb 2, 2027)** — the decisive print: the CV exit rate, FY27 CV framework, and whether Q4 EPS lands near the implied $3.37 or the $3.88 consensus.
- **CV re-acceleration to 4%+** — three quarters of sequential improvement is the bull's strongest evidence; a fourth would force a re-rate toward the $230–250 zone (BMO $231, Truist $235 prior).
- **AI product monetization** — Gartner AI/AskGartner and AI-native benchmarking bundled into existing subscriptions; success would convert a threat narrative into an ARPU story.
- **Contract Optimization scaling** — $150M LTM at ~13% CAGR; a genuine new-business engine if it crosses ~$250M.
- **Capital return cadence** — $1.7B LTM repurchases (~13% of market cap) with gross debt/EBITDA <2x and $1.6B cash; a fresh authorization would underpin ~9–10% annual share shrink. At 9x EBITDA, 34% ROIC and no controlling family, activist or sponsor interest is a live possibility.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

**As a growth investor, I cannot underwrite this as a compounder.** Organic growth of ~2–3%, a Rule of 40 of **21.8**, gross margins drifting down 180bp since 2021, and new business shrinking at both GTS and GBS mean the durable-growth criterion is not met. AI is a structural threat to the syndicated-research layer, not a tailwind this management team has yet proven it can monetize.

**But the valuation has already priced a severe outcome, and that is why this is not a Sell:** ~**2.3x EV/sales, ~9.0x EV/EBITDA, ~14.1x FY26E EPS, ~10.7x P/FCF**, versus 34% ROIC, 98–99% wallet retention, and a 9–13% buyback yield. At 10x cash flow, an incumbent with this moat doesn't need growth to compound per-share value — it needs CV to stop decelerating. The asymmetry favors owning a starter position, not full size.

**Action framing:**
- **Buy trigger:** CV exit rate prints ≥4%, or new business returns to positive — or price <$170 (~12x forward, ~14% EV/FCF), where even a melting ice cube pays you back fast.
- **Trim/Sell trigger:** CV growth falls below ~2% for two quarters, wallet retention breaks below 97%, or the buyback is cut (which would expose the EPS-growth illusion).
- **What I'd need for Strong Buy:** evidence that AI-enhanced subscriptions lift ARPU *and* new-logo counts simultaneously — i.e. the moat converting from defensible to expandable.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **AI disintermediation of core research (highest severity).** If LLMs erode willingness-to-pay for syndicated vendor analysis, CV growth goes negative and the 68% gross margin becomes a cost structure to shrink, not a moat.
2. **New-business contraction masked by price.** CV growth of 2.5% is being carried by renewal escalation; new business -4% (GTS) and -3% (GBS) with 98–99% retention leaves essentially no retention headroom to offset.
3. **Buyback-funded EPS optics.** $1.99B (FY25) and $1.7B (LTM) of repurchases against $1.2–1.3B FCF; share count already -15% YoY. This engine is decelerating by arithmetic, not choice — investors will re-underwrite EPS quality quickly if FCF dips.
4. **Seat/headcount exposure.** Per-seat subscriptions tie revenue to enterprise hiring; continued tech-sector layoffs and longer sales cycles are the stated cause of soft Q4 guidance.
5. **Customer concentration within segments.** GTS is ~75% of CV; US federal is now a drag (CV +2.8% ex-federal vs +2.5% reported).
6. **Value-trap de-rating.** A 30x-multiple stock that became a 14x stock can become a 10x stock; if growth is structurally ~2%, the terminal multiple is single-digit EBITDA and today's price embeds no further compression.
7. **Execution risk on the AI pivot** plus Conference cyclicality (travel budgets, AI-substituted events) and $3B gross debt carrying $112M annual interest.
