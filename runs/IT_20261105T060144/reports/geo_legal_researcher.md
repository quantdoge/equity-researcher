## GARTNER, INC. (NYSE: IT) — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment
*Prepared for the investment memo | Data as of Q3 2026 filings (10-Q filed 2026-11-03) and FY2025 10-K*

**Company:** Gartner, Inc. — Stamford, CT; ~20,244 employees; operates in ~90 countries and territories; FY2025 revenue ~$6.50B; market cap ~$13.1B.

**Key exposure notes:**
- **~38% of revenue is non-US** — meaningful FX translation exposure (EUR/GBP) and a growing EMEA concentration as the US/Canada share erodes ~4pts in two years.
- **US federal government is a discrete, identified exposure:** revenue from US federal agencies **declined ~$58M y/y in 2025** on reduced discretionary spending, with reporting that DOGE oversight terminated 11 Gartner federal contracts (Lusher Advisory; Gartner FY2025 10-K).
- **India** hosts major delivery/research capacity (Gurgaon — Gartner India Research & Advisory Services), a cost-center concentration risk rather than a revenue risk.
- **Russia:** Gartner is characterized as *reducing* (not fully exiting) business operations in Russia (leave-russia.org) — residual exposure appears de minimis but is the primary sanctions touchpoint.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk #1 — US federal demand contraction / politicized procurement**
- **Probability: High (already materialized) | Impact: Medium (~1–2% of revenue)**
- $58M federal revenue decline in 2025; 11 contracts cut under DOGE review. Gartner's Consulting segment and public-sector Research seats are directly levered to federal IT budgets.
- *Watch item:* Reuters (2026-11-04) reports a **Democratic sweep of the 2026 US midterms**, which Scope Ratings calls positive for the US credit outlook. Divided/opposition control of Congress plausibly slows further executive-branch contract-cutting, a modest positive — but budget standoffs and continuing-resolution risk remain.

**Risk #2 — EMEA regulatory divergence and sanctions-compliance burden**
- **Probability: Medium-High | Impact: Medium**
- EMEA is now 26% of revenue and the only region growing at double digits (+12% in 2025). That growth raises exposure to EU **AI Act** transparency/deployer obligations, **GDPR** enforcement, and the EU's 21st Russia sanctions package (July 2026) with tighter dual-use export restrictions.
- Gartner both *publishes on* and *deploys* AI (research, events, client-facing tools) — it is a compliance subject, not just an advisor. Cost of compliance and potential content/access restrictions are the transmission channel.

**Risk #3 — Emerging-market data sovereignty and cross-border data flows**
- **Probability: Medium | Impact: Low-Medium**
- China PIPL, India DPDP Act, Brazil LGPD affect Gartner's global delivery model (research production in India, client data globally) and its ability to move attendee/client data across borders for Conferences ($645M 2025 revenue, +11%).
- Travel/visa restrictions and Middle East event risk (Dubai symposium) are secondary conference-level exposures.

*Note: the dominant threat to this business — generative AI compressing subscription demand — is a **commercial** risk whose financial-reporting consequences are now being litigated (below), rather than a geopolitical one.*

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**A. Securities class action — the single largest legal contingency**
- *In re Gartner, Inc. Securities Litigation*, No. **3:26-cv-00394** (D. Conn.). Judge Omar A. Williams appointed **Cohen Milstein co-lead counsel on June 16, 2026**; Arkansas Public Employees' Retirement System is lead plaintiff.
- **Class period: July 30, 2024 – February 2, 2026.** Defendants: CEO Eugene Hall and CFO Craig Safian.
- Allegations: repeated false statements/omissions about AI's competitive threat to Gartner's subscription model; **insiders allegedly sold >$68M of stock** during the period. Stock fell 27% (Aug 5, 2025), 7% (Nov 2025), and 20.9% (Feb 3, 2026) as CV growth fell 4.9% → 3.0% → **0.8%**.
- **Amended consolidated complaint filed July 27, 2026.** A motion to dismiss is the next catalyst; the insider-sale allegations materially raise settlement/trial risk versus a pure "fraud-by-hindsight" case.

**B. Derivative exposure (pre-suit)**
- Shareholder derivative investigations opened by Bronstein, Gewirtz & Grossman and Bernstein Liebhard (public alert 2026-09-22) alleging **breach of fiduciary duty** by officers/directors. Derivative suits typically follow a surviving class action; low standalone severity, but extends the overhang and D&O cost.

**C. Trade secrets / employee mobility — Gartner as plaintiff**
- ***Gartner, Inc. et al v. Virk et al*, No. 3:26-cv-01450 (D. Conn., filed Sept 8, 2026)** against former employees Anmol Virk and Tyler Rudolph **and International Data Corp. (IDC)**, a direct competitor, for breach of contract/restrictive covenants. Signals both aggressive IP defense and the competitive threat from rival research providers.

**D. FCPA precedent (settled)**
- **May 26, 2023:** SEC settled FCPA charges against Gartner over its **South Africa** subsidiary's improper payments to obtain government contracts. Gartner paid **$856,764 disgorgement + prejudgment interest and a $1.6M civil penalty (~$2.5M total)** and consented to a cease-and-desist order (SEC Admin. Proc. 34-97609).

**E. Routine/regulatory posture**
- 10-Q (filed 2026-11-03) discloses only "legal proceedings, claims and compliance matters arising in the ordinary course of business." CEO/CFO certifications are **clean — no disclosed material weakness** in ICFR.
- **No active FTC/DOJ/EU antitrust investigation identified.** Gartner publishes its own antitrust guidelines for peer interactions; a legacy 2003 *Expert Choice* antitrust matter is long dormant.

| **Data privacy (GDPR/UK GDPR/CCPA/India DPDP/China PIPL)** | Moderate | Global client and attendee data; AI-generated inferences now a stated privacy risk vector. No enforcement action identified to date. |

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Gartner is geographically diversified but US-weighted (62%), operates in no materially sanctioned or expropriation-prone jurisdictions, holds minimal hard assets abroad, and faces no active antitrust or national-security scrutiny. Geopolitical risk is therefore *moderate and identifiable* (federal procurement, EMEA regulation, data sovereignty). The rating is held at **Medium rather than Low** principally by **legal risk**: a live, well-organized securities class action with insider-sale allegations and a 2026 amended complaint, derivative follow-on exposure, and a 2023 FCPA settlement establishing a compliance precedent. Watch catalysts: the MTD ruling in 3:26-cv-00394, Q4 CV growth versus the "4% by year-end" guidepost, and any renewed FCPA or sanctions touchpoint in EMEA/emerging markets.
