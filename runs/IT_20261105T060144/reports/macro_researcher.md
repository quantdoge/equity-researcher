# Macroeconomic Assessment — Gartner, Inc. (NYSE: IT)

**As of 5 November 2026 | Price $207.19 | Market cap $13.08B | Sector: Technology / IT Services**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Risk-off / re-tightening — a "bear steepener" regime.**

The US economy is *not* slowing into recession — it is re-accelerating in nominal terms while the bond market reprices the entire curve higher. Real GDP grew **+3.4% annualized in Q3 2026** (24,611 vs 24,408 chained $B) and **+2.1% YoY**, with unemployment at **4.2%**. But CPI has inflected decisively upward: **+3.72% YoY in September 2026**, versus a **2.39% trough in January 2026**, with three-month momentum running at **~4.6% annualized**.

The Fed responded by ending its 2025 easing cycle (effective funds rate fell 4.33% → 3.63% through mid-2026) and **resuming hikes in H2 2026** — 3.63% (Jul) → 3.75% (Aug) → **3.88% (Oct)**. Real fed funds is barely positive at **+0.16%**, i.e. the Fed is *behind* the curve. That is precisely why the long end has blown out: the **10Y is at 5.13%**, up **+75bp from 4.38% on 26 June 2026**, and the 30Y sits at **5.47%**. The curve is upward-sloping (2s10s **+39bp**) but driven by term premium and inflation expectations, not growth optimism — the most hostile shape for long-duration equities.

This is the single most important macro fact for Gartner: a subscription business with near-perpetual cash flows is a long-duration asset, and its discount rate has repriced violently in four months.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**a) Interest rates — NEGATIVE (the dominant variable).**
Gartner generates ~**$1.0B of free cash flow** against a **$13.1B market cap — roughly 13x FCF**. The multiple has already compressed from ~35x in 2024 to ~13x today, so the *first-order* damage is largely done. But the sensitivity remains material: on a simple FCF/(r−g) frame with 3% growth, **each 50bp move in the discount rate is worth roughly ±6–15% of equity value**. A 10Y at 5.13% and rising is a persistent headwind to any re-rating, and it caps the payoff to Gartner's aggressive buyback program (repurchasing shares at 13x FCF is accretive, but the opportunity cost versus debt paydown rises with every basis point).

**b) Refinancing risk — NEUTRAL / manageable.**
Total debt is only ~**$1.0B** (down from $1.2B), comprising **$800M senior notes due 2028**, **$600M due 2029**, and **$350M issued November 2025**. Leverage is low (<1x gross debt/EBITDA). The 2028 maturity will refinance at ~5.5–6% versus an original ~3.5% coupon — a ~$16–20M annual interest headwind, immaterial against $1B of FCF. **Gartner is not a credit story; it is a duration story.**

**c) Inflation & wages — NEGATIVE.**
Gartner is a people business: ~20,244 employees, with the bulk of its cost base in research and sales headcount. With unemployment at 4.2% and CPI at 3.7%, **cost per associate is rising faster than contract value per client** — management explicitly flagged "rising costs" alongside Q3 results. This is the classic margin-squeeze mechanism: Gartner's pricing power is value-based and discretionary, so it cannot fully pass through wage inflation when clients are extending sales cycles. Sticky services inflation is therefore a direct gross-margin tax.

**d) Growth / enterprise IT budgets — NEUTRAL to slightly NEGATIVE.**
GDP at +2–3% and 4.2% unemployment argue against a demand collapse, and Gartner's Q3 2026 showed **revenue +2% YoY, contract value +2.5% (third consecutive quarter of acceleration), and improved retention**. But management's caution that a 4% CV pace by year-end is "unlikely," plus **longer sales cycles**, is a textbook late-cycle signature: budgets are intact but approval friction is rising. Note the stock fell **13% on 3 November 2026** despite a guidance raise — the market is pricing the *second derivative*, not the level.

**e) Currency — NEGATIVE.**
Roughly **40% of Gartner's revenue is outside the US** (EMEA and other international). The broad dollar has strengthened **+3.1% in seven weeks** (118.1 → 121.7) and is up from ~119 in June. That is a ~1.2% translation headwind to reported revenue, and it is already visible: Q2 2026 contract value grew **1.7% FX-neutral** but only "slightly" on a reported basis. A stronger dollar mechanically widens the gap between Gartner's FX-neutral operating narrative and its reported numbers — an unhelpful optics problem for a stock already being sold on growth deceleration.

**f) AI capex supercycle — POSITIVE (offsetting).**
The one genuinely favourable macro current: enterprise AI spending is surging, and management's thesis is that companies that "handed everyone AI tools are finding little payoff and coming to Gartner for help." Gartner's Magic Quadrant franchise is a structural beneficiary of vendor proliferation and buyer confusion. This is a secular tailwind that partially insulates the top line from the macro.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Four of six macro channels are working against the stock simultaneously: a 75bp long-end repricing in four months, re-accelerating inflation squeezing a people-heavy cost base, a 3% dollar rally hitting 40% of revenue, and lengthening enterprise sales cycles. The offsetting forces — solid GDP, low leverage, and an AI-driven demand tailwind — are real but insufficient to flip the rating.

**Important nuance:** the rating is *unfavourable*, not *dire*. At ~13x FCF the equity has already absorbed an enormous amount of rate damage (down **62%** from $548.96 in November 2024). The macro headwind from here is incremental, not existential — which is why the asymmetry in the surprise scenario below runs to the upside.

---

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise: A Disinflation-Driven Re-rating (BULL)

**The setup:** CPI momentum is running at 4.6% annualized, the Fed is hiking, and the 10Y is at 5.13%. Consensus is positioned for higher-for-longer. But the inflation impulse is heavily shelter- and services-driven, and the Fed is already at a *positive real* rate with GDP decelerating from a 3.4% Q3 print.

**The surprise:** If shelter and services inflation roll over in H1 2027 — the same way they did in the 2023–24 disinflation — the Fed's two 2026 hikes get reversed, the 10Y falls from 5.13% back toward **4.25%**, and the term premium compresses. Gartner, at 13x FCF with contract value already accelerating for three straight quarters, would re-rate toward **17–18x FCF** — roughly **$270–290 per share, +30–40%** — *on essentially unchanged earnings estimates*. The entire move would be multiple expansion, not fundamentals.

**Why it's a genuine surprise:** the current data flow points the opposite way, and the stock's 13% drop on a guidance *raise* shows how little optimism is embedded. That is the definition of a setup where the macro, not the company, does the work.

**The bear mirror-image to watch:** if the Fed is forced to 4.5%+ and the 10Y breaks 5.75–6.0%, Gartner compresses to ~10x FCF (~$155–160, another −25%). Given the low leverage and $1B FCF, this is a valuation event rather than a solvency one — but it is the live risk if September's 3.7% CPI print is the start of a trend rather than a bump.

---

### Bottom Line for the Investment Memo
Gartner's problem is **not** the business cycle — GDP is growing, unemployment is 4.2%, and contract value is accelerating. The problem is that **the discount rate has repriced 75bp in four months while inflation eats into a people-based cost structure and a stronger dollar shaves reported revenue.** The company is a long-duration asset in a bear-steepening tape. At ~13x FCF the macro damage is substantially priced, which makes the risk/reward hinge almost entirely on the direction of the 10Y rather than on Gartner's execution. **Watch the 10Y and the monthly CPI print more closely than the quarterly contract value metric.**
