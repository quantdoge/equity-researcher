## NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — GARTNER, INC. (NYSE: IT)

**Snapshot:** Technology / IT Services, Stamford CT · ~20,244 FTE · market cap ~$13.1B · price $207.19 · beta 0.95 · CEO **Eugene A. Hall** (since Aug 2004) · CFO **Craig Safian** (since Jun 2014). All data as of the Nov 4, 2026 close.

**Data caveat up front:** the filing API returned no 10-K and no DEF 14A for IT, and the 10-Q pull surfaced only the Exhibit 31.1 §302 certification (clean, no restatement, no ICFR weakness disclosed). Legal-proceedings language, board independence ratios and D&O reserves could not be read directly. Conclusions below are triangulated from the price tape, SEC filing metadata, Form 4 metadata and third-party trackers; gaps are marked.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK** (borderline)

Evidence:

- **Combined Chairman/CEO — deliberately re-created.** The Board *recombined* the Chair and CEO roles effective **July 1, 2024**, appointing Gene Hall as Chairman, succeeding James C. Smith (an independent chair). This is an affirmative step *away* from good governance, not an inherited defect. Karen Dykstra serves as **Lead Independent Director** — a partial offset.
- **Extreme executive entrenchment.** Hall: CEO for **22 years** and now Chairman. Safian: CFO for **12 years**. No successor or transition disclosure surfaced. Tenure of this length in a business whose core product (syndicated IT research) is being structurally disrupted by AI is a live board-refresh problem.
- **The alleged disclosure failure is a board-oversight issue.** The consolidated securities action (*Schmidt v. Gartner*) covers **Feb 4, 2025 – Feb 2, 2026** and alleges materially false statements about **contract-value (CV) growth** resilience and 2025 consulting guidance. Note the precise governance flaw: **CV is a non-GAAP/operating KPI that no auditor opines on** — the metric that anchors the entire equity story is unaudited by design, and that is exactly where the five-quarter misstatement is alleged. A **parallel derivative action** (shareholder-on-behalf-of-company) has been filed, which typically names directors for breach of fiduciary duty/Lack of oversight.
- **Mixed insider alignment.** Insider ownership only **3.38%** of shares; Hall's 1.19M shares are **~54% of all insider holdings** — i.e., alignment is one man. Positively, **director Stephen Pagliuca (Bain Capital) bought ~$9.9M** of stock in the open market on Dec 10, 2025 — the largest open-market insider transaction of the year.
- **No SEC enforcement, no accounting fraud alleged, no restatement, clean certifications.** These are genuine mitigants and keep this out of "Critical."

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **HIGH (litigation-driven; no enforcement action found)**

- **EDGAR full-text enforcement search returned zero SEC actions** naming IT. No DOJ, FTC, GDPR/CCPA, or state-AG action identified in searches. No product-liability or antitrust docket surfaced.
- **Active federal securities class action, unresolved.** Amended Consolidated Complaint filed **July 27, 2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline was **May 18, 2026**; multiple firms (Pomerantz, Rosen, Levi & Korsinsky, Bernstein Liebhard, Bronstein Gewirtz, Cohen Milstein) are soliciting. Class certification, fact discovery and summary judgment still lie ahead — this is a **2027–2028 overhang**, not a near-term resolution. Defense costs are D&O-absorbed but the *reputational* cost is being paid now.
- **Parallel derivative suit** (Bragar Eagel & Squire) — demanding shareholder-held shares; these settlements frequently extract governance/compensation reforms even when the securities case fails.
- **Two documented "liability events"** in the tape: **Aug 5, 2025 −27.6%** ($336.71 → $243.93) and **Feb 3, 2026 −20.9%** ($202.40 → $160.16, intraday −31%); plus **Nov 3, 2026 −13.2%** ($235.26 → $204.22) despite a guidance *raise*. Peak-to-trough (Aug 4, 2025 $337.29 → Jun 22, 2026 $124.25) is **−63%**; the stock is still −39% below the Aug 2025 high.
- **Political/fiscal channel risk:** management explicitly attributed guidance pressure to client cost-cutting tied to US federal **"DOGE"** efficiency initiatives and tariff-driven CEO caution — direct government-demand and government-adjacent-contractor exposure to purchasing freezes.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **AI disintermediation of syndicated research — the existential risk.** Gartner monetizes proprietary insight and benchmark data by subscription. Generative/agentic AI now synthesizes comparable answers inside client workflows, pressuring seat counts and pricing power. Management's own thesis ("companies that handed everyone AI tools found little payoff, so they come to us") is a *defence*, not yet proof. CV growth of **2.5%** and management conceding a 4% run-rate is unlikely by year-end shows the strain.
2. **Single-KPI dependency + lengthening sales cycles.** Retention and contract value are the whole story; longer sales cycles and rising costs mean revenue visibility deteriorates with a one-to-three-quarter lag on any demand shock. Renewal friction from client cost programmes (DOGE, tariffs) hits directly.
3. **Key-person and bench concentration.** A 22-year CEO who also chairs the board, a 12-year CFO, no disclosed succession, and an insider ownership base where one individual holds >half. Any unplanned Hall departure would be a material event with no publicly telegraphed successor — and would likely crystallize the derivative claims.
4. **Modest, not acute:** conference-segment venue/travel exposure; sales-force productivity/turnover; no evidence of any cyber incident, breach, or outage affecting the platform (a clean record vs. peers).

---

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **ELEVATED**

- **Structural conflict in the franchise asset.** Gartner earns research revenue from the same technology vendors its Magic Quadrant ranks — and those rankings gate enterprise procurement. "Pay-to-play" allegations recur (notably revived in 2026 procurement-forum commentary); a 2014 US court ruling in the NetScout matter did confirm Gartner does not operate a pay-to-play model, which is a durable legal shield but has not retired the perception. Beyond perception, this fee/evaluation duality is the kind of structure that attracts antitrust and unfair-competition attention over time.
- **The securities litigation is now the dominant reputational narrative** — a five-quarter alleged misstatement about growth, followed by two double-digit single-day collapses and a third −13% reaction to a *good* quarter. The market has stopped paying for guidance, which is itself a reputational asset impairment.
- **Litigation-solicitation amplification:** heavy plaintiff-firm press volume (NatLawReview, GlobeNewswire, PRNewswire placements) keeps the alleged fraud in search results for prospective clients and recruits — a hiring and sales-cycle friction.
- **Absent:** product recalls, consumer backlash, criminal exposure, ESG/environmental controversy, workplace-safety findings. Brand equity in the Magic Quadrant remains genuinely strong (weekly 2026 MQ announcements from vendors are free advertising).

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical — there is no fraud finding, no restatement, no material weakness, no regulatory enforcement, no data breach, and a director bought $9.9M of stock with his own money. But HIGH is justified by: (a) an unresolved federal securities class action plus derivative action targeting five quarters of the company's *headline* KPI; (b) a board that recombined Chair/CEO and is anchored by a 22-year CEO and 12-year CFO with no visible succession; (c) a business model facing AI-driven disintermediation with decelerating contract value; and (d) a demonstrable market-wide loss of confidence in management's disclosure (three double-digit drawdowns in 15 months).

**Where this becomes Critical:** an adverse class-certification ruling, a missed CV guide-down, a credible whistleblower/SEC informal inquiry, or an unplanned CEO exit.

---

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Disclosure-integrity flag (highest priority).** Unaudited CV KPI is the locus of an alleged five-quarter misstatement; two −20%+ single-day gaps bracket the class period. Verify whether the Audit Committee commissioned an independent review and whether D&O reserves/insurance limits are disclosed — **10-K and DEF 14A were not retrievable, so this must be pulled manually.**
2. **Derivative suit = director-oversight exposure.** Follow it for demanded governance reforms (independent chair, CEO succession disclosure) — those are the leading indicators of real change.
3. **Combined Chair/CEO reinstated July 2024, reversing an independent-chair structure.** Governance regression in the exact window of the alleged misstatements. Escalate as a board-quality issue, not a formality.
4. **Key-person cliff:** 22-year CEO/Chair, no disclosed successor, and insider ownership concentrated 54% in him.
5. **Zero open-market buying by any operating executive through a 63% peak-to-trough decline** — the lone large buyer is a financial-sponsor director whose incentives differ from public holders'.
6. **Vendor-funded research conflict** — monitor for antitrust/unfair-competition interest and for large-vendor revenue concentration disclosure.
7. **DOGE/federal budget channel** — unquantified government and government-contractor renewal exposure; request the client-mix breakdown.
8. **Data gaps to remediate before committee:** FY2025 10-K risk factors and legal proceedings, FY2026 proxy (board independence, comp metrics, related-party transactions), 10b5-1 plan status, and any 2026 8-K (Oct 8, 2026 filing unexamined).
