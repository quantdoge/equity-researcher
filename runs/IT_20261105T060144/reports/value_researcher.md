# Gartner, Inc. (NYSE: IT) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price $207.19 (Nov 4, 2026) | Market cap ~$13.1B | Net debt ~$1.9B | EV ~$15.0B**

*Data note: the built-in DCF tool returned malformed output (WACC mis-parsed as 900%), so I built the valuation manually. FY2025 10-K figures, Q3 2026 results, and FY2026 guidance are the anchors.*

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow** (narrowed from Wide)

Gartner sells syndicated IT research and advisory subscriptions. FY2026E revenue ~$6.4B splits into **Insights** (research, ~$5.1B), **Conferences** (~$330M), **Consulting** (~$490M).

**Evidence of a real moat:**
- **Intangible assets/standard-setting**: The Magic Quadrant is an industry reference point. Vendors must engage Gartner to be positioned; buyers use it for vendor selection. That is a two-sided dynamic few research firms replicate.
- **Gross margin of 67–68%, unchanged for six straight years** (67.2% in 2020 → 67.7% in 2025) despite revenue nearly doubling. This is exceptional pricing stability and the clearest single piece of evidence of pricing power.
- **Insights contribution margin of 78%** (up 90bps YoY in Q3'26) — software-like economics on a services-like revenue base.
- **Wallet retention: 98% GTS, 99% GBS.** Extremely sticky revenue.
- **Annual price increases of 3–4%** for decades (the Nov 2026 increase was ~3%, at the low end of the historical range).
- **Scale/efficient scale**: 20,244 employees, dominant share of the IT research category, proprietary benchmark datasets that get better with more clients.

**Evidence the moat has narrowed — this is the crux of the thesis:**
- **Contract value growth collapsed**: double-digit CV growth in 2021–22 → +7.9% revenue in 2023 → +3.7% in 2025 → **CV of +1% in Q4'25**. It has since re-accelerated for three consecutive quarters to **+2.5% (Q3'26)**, or +2.8% ex-US-federal.
- **Operating margin fell from 20.9% (2023) to 15.8% (2025)** — partly restructuring/investment, but real.
- The open question is whether AI substitutes for syndicated inquiry and syndicated research. Management argues the opposite is happening: several clients bought large AI agreements, saw "cost but not benefits," and are returning for help building business cases. AI is now the most-requested topic across all client roles. That is plausible and the CV re-acceleration supports it — but two quarters of stabilization is not disproof of a secular threat.

**Verdict on moat:** Still narrow-to-moderate, not gone. ROIC of 26% vs. WACC ~8% is a genuine moated-business signature. But I cannot underwrite a *durable* 10-year moat with high confidence, and I will not pay as if I could.

The de-rating is ~60–65% on every measure. This is the market pricing structural decline, not a cyclical wobble.

**Manual DCF** (base $1.135B normalized owner earnings = LTM FCF $1.3B less $165M SBC; net debt $1.9B; ~63M shares):

| Scenario | Growth assumptions | WACC / terminal | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear** (AI erodes seats) | −1% yrs 1–5, 0% yrs 6–10 | 9.0% / −2.0% | **~$149** |
| **Base** (CV holds ~3–4%, pricing persists) | +4% yrs 1–5, +3% yrs 6–10 | 8.5% / +2.5% | **~$307** |
| **Bull** (AI fear fades, CV back to 5–7%) | +7% yrs 1–10 | 8.0% / +3.0% | **~$478** |

Probability-weighted (30/45/25): **~$302** intrinsic value. At $207, the stock trades at roughly **a 31% discount to base-case intrinsic value** and is ~39% above my bear case.

**What the current price implies:** ~10.6x FCF and ~14x adjusted EPS implies the market expects roughly zero real long-term growth or mild decline. Given CV is *accelerating* (three consecutive quarters), 98–99% wallet retention, and a 3% price increase already enacted, that expectation looks too pessimistic — while not so absurd that this is a fat-pitch bargain.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Positive:**
- Gene Hall (CEO since 2004) built Gartner from a mid-size advisory firm into the category standard; EPS compounded at a high-teens rate over his tenure. The operating discipline shows in the 78% Insights contribution margin.
- **Capital allocation is shareholder-oriented**: no dividend, essentially all FCF into buybacks. Share count fell from ~90M (2020) to ~63M — a ~30% reduction in six years. This is exactly the right use of cash for a capital-light, non-capital-intensive franchise.
- Guidance has been raised repeatedly through FY2026 (revenue, EBITDA, adj. EPS, and FCF all lifted at Q3), and Q3 beat across every line. Management is not lowballing.
- Leverage kept low (~1.2x) — they do not empire-build. Acquisitions have been modest and bolt-on, not dilutive mega-deals.

**Negative:**
- **Buyback timing has been poor.** They repurchased $1.99B in 2025 at an average around $350 — and repurchased heavily at $500+ in 2021–22. Gartner's buybacks have historically been largest at peak confidence/peak price. This is a real capital-allocation flaw, though at $130–210 in 2026 the same program is highly accretive (~$1.1B authorization remains as of Sept 30, 2026, against a $13B market cap).
- **Key-man/succession risk** on a long-tenured CEO whose strategy is the franchise.
- Insider ownership and exact comp details I could not verify within budget — a gap, not a red flag.

**Net: above-average, disciplined, honest operators — not elite capital allocators on timing.**

## 5. Investment Verdict: **BUY** (moderate position, scaled for uncertainty)

Not a Strong Buy. This is a good business at a genuinely attractive price with an unresolved existential question attached.

**Why Buy:**
- 26% ROIC vs. ~8% WACC, 78% segment contribution margins, 98–99% wallet retention, 3% pricing power — these are moat markers that have not disappeared.
- 9.4% FCF yield plus ~3% growth is a ~12%+ expected annual return if the franchise merely holds.
- Contract value has accelerated three quarters running (+1% → +2.5%), guidance is rising, ex-federal growth is 2.8%, and the stock fell 13% anyway. Expectations are washed out.
- 31% discount to base-case intrinsic value clears my 20–30% margin-of-safety bar.

**Why not more aggressive:** My bear case is $149 — only 28% below the current price — and it is a plausible scenario, not a tail risk. A 30% weight on that outcome caps position size. I would add materially only on evidence that CV growth re-establishes above 4% for two-plus quarters, or below ~$150.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI disintermediation (the dominant risk).** If LLMs and AI-native research tools compress seat counts and substitute for syndicated inquiry, revenue goes into structural decline and $149 — not $307 — is the right anchor. This is unproven in either direction.
2. **Tech-vendor client concentration.** Tech vendors are a large slice of GTS; their CV growth is low-single-digit and they are laying off staff. Gartner's revenue is levered to tech-sector headcount and marketing budgets.
3. **Price-increase dependence.** 3% annual pricing against 2.5% CV growth means volume/seat growth may be *negative*. A 3% increase is already at the low end of the 3–4% historical range — if pricing power slips below ~2%, the model breaks.
4. **Sales-cycle elongation and macro.** Management flagged the Middle East conflict, tech-vendor layoffs, and longer sales cycles; ex-federal CV is unlikely to reach 4% by year-end.
5. **Guidance credibility / deceleration relapse.** The Aug 2025 (−27%) and Feb 2026 (−21%) gaps show how violently this stock reprices on CV disappointments. Volatility will remain extreme.
6. **Thin book equity and a shrinking share base.** Net debt ~$1.9B against $320M of equity leaves less flexibility if FCF disappoints; a large remaining buyback authorization could also be spent poorly if the stock rallies.
7. **CEO succession** at a company whose strategy is closely identified with a two-decade CEO.

**Monitoring triggers:** quarterly contract value growth (need >4% ex-federal to confirm the bull case), GTS wallet retention (watch for a break below 95%), and the pace of the annual price increase (a drop below 2% would be a serious tell).

---

*Bottom line: Gartner is a genuine 26%-ROIC franchise with a 40-year brand, exceptional retention, and a management team returning all cash to shareholders — trading at ~10.6x FCF because the market fears AI eats the research business. At $207 I am paid roughly 12% a year if the franchise merely persists, and I keep ~28% of downside protection to the bear case. That is a Buy, not a table-pounder, because the durability of the moat over the next decade is exactly the thing I cannot compute.*
