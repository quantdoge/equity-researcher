### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- **Footprint:** Office- and travel-based; no manufacturing, no Scope 1-heavy assets, no water stress, no CBAM exposure (no goods trade). Carbon intensity is effectively negligible (~single-digit tCO2e per $m of ~$6.5bn revenue — estimate; absolute tonnage not retrievable from available tools).
- **Targets (SBTi-aligned, company-disclosed):** 53% absolute cut in Scope 1+2 by 2029 vs. 2019 base; 52% cut in Scope 3 from **business travel and employee commuting** by 2029. These are credible because the abatement levers (office consolidation, travel policy) are directly within management control.
- **Weakness:** No long-term net-zero commitment on file — a **near-term-only** target set. That is behind large-cap tech peers and is increasingly a screening flag for EU-domiciled ESG funds.
- **Physical/transition risk:** Low. The one real channel is **travel-disruption risk to the Conferences segment** (FY2025 revenue $645m, +11%) — pandemic/weather/geopolitical disruption to physical events is a revenue risk, not a carbon risk.
- **Verdict:** Not thesis-relevant. Do not pay for environmental alpha here; do note the missing net-zero pledge as a marginal exclusion risk.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Human capital is the product.** ~20,200 employees; the value proposition rests on named analysts and their research credibility. Analyst attrition or comp compression is a *direct revenue risk*, not an ESG nicety.
- **Restructuring strain:** Multiple 2025–26 employee reports of "significant rounds of layoffs," including senior research staff and India-based design/research roles, alongside Glassdoor commentary on eroding culture and "expectations changing weekly." Severance charges plus execution risk in the research engine sit alongside the growth deceleration that triggered the securities litigation. **This is the social factor most tightly coupled to the thesis.**
- **Compensation:** Median employee pay $127,275 (2025) — high-wage, professional workforce; low wage-hour/pay-floor risk. EU Pay Transparency Directive and UK/Japan gender pay-gap reporting obligations apply to subsidiaries; Gartner discloses Japan gender pay gaps. Board/management gender representation appears reasonable for a US mid-cap but exact percentages were not verifiable (investor site 403).
- **Product/consumer safety:** Not applicable in the traditional sense. **Vendor-relations trust is the analogue:** the "pay-for-play" criticism of Magic Quadrant placement (see §5) is a persistent social-license irritant with its largest clients (technology vendors).
- **Positive:** High-skill workforce, low safety risk (no industrial operations), no evidence of supply-chain labour exposure (services-only value chain).
- **Verdict:** Medium — headline ESG scorers will rate this low-risk; the real exposure is talent retention and culture during a cost-cutting cycle, which shows up in renewal rates and CV growth before it shows up in any ESG score.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **5.5 / 10**

**Positives**
- **Single-class share structure.** The legacy dual-class regime — under which Class B elected 80% of the board — has been collapsed by Board-approved combination of Class A and Class B into one class, one vote. This is a genuine, material improvement in shareholder rights (timing ~2021; exact completion date unverified from available filings).
- **Board mechanics:** 13 nominees (2026 proxy); "independent director nominees"; **committees composed solely of independent directors**; majority-vote standard for director elections; annual say-on-pay; Lead Independent Director required when the Chairman is not independent.
- **Pay alignment:** CEO 2025 total comp $19.23m, **~95% equity/performance-linked** (only ~5% salary) — cash-flow-aligned with long-term TSR, and Gartner's heavy buyback programme has compounded per-share value.

**Negative — and these drive the score down**
- **CEO/Chairman duality with 22-year tenure** (Hall since Aug 2004, also Chairman). Succession risk is a live, unaddressed governance item; a single individual has anchored guidance credibility through the entire modern era. Chairman is not independent → relies on a Lead Independent Director as a mitigant only.
- **Disclosure-controls failure alleged in 2025–26.** The class action alleges that management promoted 12–16% "normal environment" CV growth and Consulting targets while, per the complaint, internal projections showed a shortfall — with the truth disclosed only on 3 Feb 2026. Whether or not the claim survives, the *pattern* (aggressive public framing vs. internal visibility) is exactly what governance analysts should penalise. The Q3 FY2026 10-Q certifications assert controls are effective; no restatement or material weakness has been disclosed.
- **Pay ratio 151:1** — high in absolute terms but unremarkable for a $13bn-cap US company with a 95% equity-weighted CEO package.
- **Board refreshment/tenure:** several long-tenured directors (e.g., Peter Bisson, since 2015; Richard Bressler, Raul Cesan) — stability, but limited evidence of aggressive refreshment.

**Score rationale:** Structurally above-median governance (single class, independent committees, majority voting, equity-heavy pay) marked down materially for combined Chair/CEO, extreme CEO tenure with no disclosed succession plan, and a 2026 disclosure episode that has already cost shareholders ~$3bn in a single session. **5.5/10 = "adequate but with a live credibility deficit."**

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure: **MODERATE (disclosure, not operational)**

- **EU CSRD:** Gartner has substantial EU/UK turnover via European subsidiaries, so it is likely in scope notwithstanding the 2025 Omnibus threshold increase; double-materiality reporting would require value-chain and Scope 3 data it does not fully publish today. **Compliance cost: low single-digit $m/yr; disclosure-gap risk: moderate.**
- **EU Pay Transparency Directive (transposition by June 2026, first reporting 2027):** applies to EU entities; requires gender pay-gap reporting and joint pay assessments. Gartner already reports Japan and UK gap data — partial readiness.
- **California SB 253 / SB 261:** revenue >$1bn and doing business in California → Scope 1/2 (and eventually Scope 3) disclosure plus TCFD-style climate risk report. Implementation has been repeatedly delayed; Gartner's Scope 3 travel data is already tracked under SBTi, so readiness is high.
- **SEC climate disclosure rule:** effectively withdrawn/stayed at federal level (2025) — **removes near-term US federal reporting burden**, leaving California and EU as the binding regimes.
- **EU AI Act:** Gartner both deploys AI in research tooling and publishes AI guidance — limited provider obligations, mainly transparency and GPAI-usage governance. Low direct cost.
- **CBAM:** **not applicable** (no goods).
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified; balance sheet is modestly levered with conventional facilities.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (leaning "Average with a governance risk flag")

- **E: Low risk, but a laggard on ambition** (near-term-only SBTi targets, no net-zero pledge).
- **S: Medium risk** — high-quality, high-wage workforce, but a visible cost-cutting/attrition cycle in the exact function (research) that generates revenue.
- **G: Structurally sound, behaviourally questioned** — single-class shares, independent committees, equity-heavy pay, offset by Chairman/CEO duality, 22-year tenure, no disclosed succession, and a 2026 securities-fraud action alleging an internal-visibility gap on guidance.

**Why not "Leader":** the disclosure episode is disqualifying for a leadership rating regardless of the environmental profile. **Why not "Laggard":** no environmental liabilities, no labour-rights or corruption issues, clean shareholder-rights structure, and genuinely aligned executive pay.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (ESG is not the thesis; it is a risk overlay)

- **Not a positive driver.** There is no ESG alpha case: no green-revenue story, no ESG-fund inclusion catalyst, and the environmental profile is immaterial to valuation. Index/SFDR Article 8 flows are unlikely to move the stock.
- **The one ESG factor that is priced and still live: governance/disclosure.** The 3 Feb 2026 miss plus the ensuing class and derivative actions mean the *credibility discount* on management guidance is the relevant cost-of-capital input. Until a settlement or a clean multi-quarter re-establishment of CV-growth guidance occurs, expect a governance-related multiple discount versus information-services peers. Watch for: (i) **CEO succession/Chair separation announcements** (would be a genuine re-rating positive for the G score), (ii) derivative-suit-driven governance reforms, (iii) research-analyst attrition metrics in the next 10-K human-capital disclosure.
- **Monitor, don't underwrite:** talent retention in the research organisation is the leading indicator for renewal rates and thus for the CV-growth thesis. Social factors here are a *business-model* risk dressed as an ESG risk.
- **Net call:** **Neutral for the thesis — ESG neither supports nor undermines the investment case, but the governance/disclosure controversy should be sized explicitly as a $30–100m contingent liability and a persistent discount-rate premium.**
