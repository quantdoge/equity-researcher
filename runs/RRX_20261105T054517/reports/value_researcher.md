# Regal Rexnord Corporation (RRX) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price: $173.38 | Market cap: $11.55B | Net debt: $4.53B | EV: $16.08B | 66.6M shares**

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Narrow**

RRX is a diversified industrial manufacturer of motors, electronic controls, air-moving products, bearings, couplings, gearing, and power transmission components, organized into four segments (Commercial Systems, Industrial Systems, Climate Solutions, Motion Control / Automation & Motion Control). It sells to OEMs and end-users through distributors and direct reps across HVAC, general industrial, metals, energy, aerospace, and — increasingly — data centers.

**Sources of advantage:**
- **Intangible assets / brands:** A genuine portfolio of specified industrial brands (Marathon, Leeson, Rexnord, Falk, Warner Electric, Boston Gear, Thomson, Coupling Corp). These are recognized, but they are component brands, not consumer franchises with pricing power.
- **Switching costs (moderate):** Once a motor, gearbox, or bearing is designed into an OEM's product or a plant's maintenance schedule, requalification and redesign costs create real stickiness. Replacement/aftermarket demand is recurring.
- **Cost advantages:** None durable. Manufacturing is capital-intensive and globally competitive.

**The decisive test — does the moat get monetized?** No. ROIC has run **5.75% (2025), 5.76% (2024), 4.66% (2023), 8.4% (2022)** against a WACC of roughly 8.5%. **RRX has not earned its cost of capital in any of the last three years.** That is the single most important fact in this analysis. A wide-moat industrial earns 12–20% ROIC through cycles; RRX earns half its cost of capital.

**Moat trend: stable-to-slightly-widening on gross margin, but not on returns.** Gross margin has climbed from 28.2% (2021) to 37.4% (2025) — genuinely impressive, driven by Altra mix, price/cost discipline, and portfolio pruning. But operating margin is only 11.5%, and the gap between gross and operating margin is consumed by $500M of D&A (largely acquired-intangible amortization) and $349M of interest. The business is more profitable at the gross line than it is at the capital-return line.

**Competitive reality:** RRX competes with ABB, Siemens, Nidec, SKF, Timken, Parker Hannifin, Eaton, and Danfoss — all larger or better-capitalized. In a fragmented, cyclical, specification-driven market, RRX is a solid #2/#3 player, not a pricing-setter. **Narrow moat, and one that has not yet been converted into excess returns.**

**Weaknesses:**
- **Returns on capital are poor.** ROE 4.1%, ROIC 5.8%. The $10.0B goodwill + intangibles (72% of total assets) generate almost no return. **Tangible book value is negative ~$3.2B.**
- **Leverage is still high.** $5.06B total debt, 2.0x EBIT/interest coverage. This is not a fortress balance sheet; it is a leveraged cyclical.

- **Cyclicality.** Revenue fell 1.7% in 2025 and 3.5% in 2024. Short-cycle industrial and residential HVAC exposure is unforgiving.
- **Impairment risk is live:** a $119M goodwill writedown on the autonomous mobile solutions business in Q3 2026 already demonstrates that the $10B intangible balance is not sacrosanct.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Adequate, with a blemish**

**Positives:**
- **Deleveraging discipline is exemplary.** Prioritizing debt paydown over buybacks (zero repurchases in 2025) after the Altra deal is exactly the right call. Net debt down $1.44B in two years.
- **Portfolio rationalization.** The strategic review and potential sale of the autonomous mobile solutions business signals a focus on core, higher-return assets rather than empire-building.
- **Conservative dividend.** $1.40/share (~13% of adjusted EPS) is well covered and not a distraction.
- **Operational execution.** Margin expansion through a volume decline, and 13% order growth ex-data-center, indicate real commercial momentum under CEO Aamir Paul.

**The blemish:**
- **The Altra acquisition (2023, ~$5B) was poorly timed and value-destructive on a returns basis.** It was closed near the peak of the industrial cycle, doubled debt to $6.5B, and diluted ROIC to ~5%. This is the classic serial-acquirer pattern: growth in scale, erosion in returns. The $119M impairment in 2026 is a small admission of that.
- Insider ownership is modest; management is professional rather than owner-operator.

**Verdict:** Competent operators, improving capital discipline, but with a demonstrated willingness to overpay for scale. Watch closely whether the deleveraging completes before the next acquisition.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

RRX is a **good business at a fair price — which, by my standards, is not a buy.** The company has real assets: a narrow moat built on specification-driven switching costs, a genuine data-center growth vector (sales +307%, A&MC segment +24.5%), improving margins, strong free cash flow, and a management team that is doing the right thing with the balance sheet.

But three things keep me on the sidelines:

1. **Returns below cost of capital.** ROIC of 5.8% vs. ~8.5% WACC means the business is not creating economic value on its invested capital. Until ROIC sustainably exceeds WACC, this is a "show-me" story, not a compounder.
2. **No margin of safety.** Intrinsic value ~$172 vs. price $173.38. I require a 25–30% discount. **My buy price is ~$125–135** — which the stock touched as recently as September 2026.
3. **Leverage and cyclicality.** 3.4x net debt/adjusted EBITDA and 2.0x interest coverage in a cyclical industrial is a fragile combination if the industrial economy rolls over.

**If you own it:** hold. The data-center inflection and deleveraging path are real, and a sale of the AMS business would sharpen the story. **If you don't own it:** do not initiate at $173. Wait for either (a) a price below ~$135, or (b) evidence of two consecutive years of ROIC above 9% with net leverage below 2.5x — which would justify re-rating the moat from Narrow toward Wide.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Data-center concentration risk.** The growth narrative now rests heavily on a single end market growing triple digits. If hyperscaler AI capex decelerates, both earnings and the multiple compress simultaneously. This is the largest swing factor in the bull case.
2. **Sub-WACC returns persist.** If ROIC stays at ~6%, the business destroys value even while growing. Resumed M&A before deleveraging completes would compound this.
3. **Balance-sheet fragility.** $5.06B debt, 2.0x interest coverage, negative tangible equity. A cyclical downturn plus elevated rates would pressure both earnings and refinancing.
4. **Goodwill impairment.** $10.0B of goodwill/intangibles (72% of assets) with a $119M writedown already taken. Further impairments are plausible and would hit book value and sentiment.
5. **Tariff and trade policy.** FY2026 guidance includes $0.58/share of one-time IEEPA tariff refunds. The underlying tariff cost trajectory is a live margin risk, and the "clean" EPS is ~$10.17, not $10.75.
6. **Adjusted-earnings illusion.** Investors anchoring on $10.75 adjusted EPS may be underappreciating that GAAP EPS is ~$3.50. The $6+ gap is amortization and interest — the true cost of the acquisition strategy.
7. **Cyclical end markets.** Residential HVAC, general industrial, and metals remain soft; the recovery is not yet broad-based.

---

**Bottom line:** A narrow-moat, cash-generative industrial in the early innings of a data-center-driven recovery, run by disciplined stewards of the balance sheet — but earning below its cost of capital and priced at intrinsic value. **Hold; buy below ~$135.**
