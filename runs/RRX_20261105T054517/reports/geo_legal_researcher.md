### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① Tariff regime on metals inputs — Section 232 (highest, ongoing)**
- IEEPA tariffs were struck down by the Supreme Court on **Feb 20, 2026**; CBP has been running refunds. Section 232 (steel/aluminum, raised to **50%**) and Section 301 remain in force; **from Apr 6, 2026, Section 232 derivative duties apply to full customs value** of covered steel/aluminum/copper derivative products — directly relevant to a motor/bearing/gearing manufacturer.
- RRX booked **$14.7M of net IEEPA refunds in Q3 2026** (+$0.18 EPS), had guided ~$8M/qtr, and **assumes zero further refunds in Q4** (Q3'26 call, CFO Rehard). Ex-refunds, Q3 gross margin fell **80bp YoY** on "price cost pressure."
- Mgmt guided (Q1'26) to **dollar-cost neutrality by mid-2026** and margin neutrality by year-end.
- *Probability: high (in effect now). Impact: 50–100bp of gross margin, partially price-recovered.*

**② US–China decoupling — 5.3% of sales plus sourcing and IP**
- China revenue has been shrinking (6.5%→5.4%→5.3%), suggesting active de-risking, but Chinese manufacturing remains in the network.
- New Chinese countermeasures raise operational risk: MOFCOM draft **Special Provisions on Protection of Chinese Trade Secrets** (released Oct 26, 2026) and exit-ban powers effective **Sept 15, 2026** for export-control/technology violations (Steptoe sanctions update, Nov 2, 2026). For an engineering-heavy business (Kollmorgen, Thomson, Warner Electric), this is a personnel-mobility and IP-protection risk, not just a tariff one.
- *Probability: medium. Impact: medium (low revenue at risk, non-trivial supply/IP tail).*

**③ North American trade architecture — USMCA 2026 joint review + Mexico footprint**
- USMCA's joint review is due in 2026, with bilateral side deals expected to ease some Section 232 duties (Capstone DC 2026 trade preview). A negative outcome would hit RRX's Mexican production and cross-border flows that support its 63% US revenue base.
- *Probability: medium-high (renegotiation is scheduled, not hypothetical). Impact: low–medium.*

**Watch item (indirect):** Data-center sales **+307% YoY** (switchgear tracking to ~$200M in 2026, ePods $25M/qtr) tie RRX increasingly to US AI-infrastructure capex and, indirectly, to the AI export-control regime — a demand-concentration risk rather than a legal one.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Asbestos and product liability (legacy, structural).** The 10-K risk factors state: *"We are subject to litigation, including product liability, asbestos and warranty claims that may adversely affect our financial condition or results of operations."* The Rexnord PMC merger also carried *"certain contingent liabilities of the Rexnord PMC business, including certain pre-closing"* obligations, plus environmental remediation matters. **Gap: I could not retrieve the reserve quantum** — `fetch_filing_text` returned "No 10-K found for RRX" and the annual-report PDF returned unparseable binary. Peers with similar legacy books (Honeywell ~$1.4B asbestos liabilities at 6/30/25) show this can be billions of gross exposure net of insurance; **treat RRX's asbestos reserve as unquantified in this note.**
- **Product class action (live):** *TSC900 Transfer Switch Controller* defect class action (Thomson Power Systems) filed Aug 2023 — the sole RRX matter catalogued by classaction.org, with no dismissal/settlement disclosed. Low dollar materiality, some reputational/recall tail.
- **Regulatory/safety:** OSHA citations at Regal Rexnord Wausau, WI (2022, $6,642) and Rexnord Industries (2019) per Violation Tracker — de minimis, immaterial.
- **Government investigations:** No DOJ, SEC, FTC or EU investigation into RRX identified in searches through Nov 2026. **Absence of evidence, not verified clearance.**
- **Export controls/ITAR:** Aerospace & defense business (Kollmorgen / Regal Rexnord Aerospace & Defense Solutions, exhibiting at AUSA and Xponential 2026) carries ITAR/EAR licensing obligations; a mis-licensing event would be a reportable compliance matter. No violation disclosed.
- **Structural/event risk:** $119M non-cash **goodwill impairment** on the autonomous mobile solutions (AMS) business, with AMS now being explored for sale as part of a full portfolio review under new CEO Aamir Paul (4 months in). Divestitures imply regulatory approval and potential successor-liability negotiations on legacy asbestos/environmental liabilities — a common source of value leakage.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Earnings quality / disclosure.** GAAP EPS guided to **$3.76–$4.06** versus **adjusted $10.60–$10.90** — a >$6.80 gap built from amortization, restructuring, the $119M impairment, and now **IEEPA tariff refunds**. Q3 swung to a **$12M GAAP net loss** from $80M income, largely on the impairment. Non-recurring government refunds embedded in "adjusted" EBITDA is an aggressive construction.
2. **Unquantified asbestos/environmental tail** — a legacy-tort liability carried by a levered (2.9x net debt/EBITDA) industrial. Not a red flag per se, but an unreported-in-this-note exposure.
3. **Tariff cost recovery depends on pricing power** — margin neutrality is a management assertion, not a contractual hedge.
4. **No FCPA/AML/sanctions violations identified.** Customer base is industrial OEMs/distributors with no identified high-risk-jurisdiction concentration beyond the above.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** ~63% US revenue and no sanctioned-market exposure keep geopolitical risk fundamentally contained; China is only 5.3% of sales and declining. The rating is held at Medium rather than Low by (a) a live and escalating US metals-tariff regime that directly hits input costs and has already shown up as an 80bp ex-refund gross-margin headwind, (b) the scheduled USMCA review overhanging a Mexican footprint that supports the US business, and (c) an unquantified legacy asbestos/product-liability book on a 2.9x-levered balance sheet. No active government investigation, no material litigation identified.
