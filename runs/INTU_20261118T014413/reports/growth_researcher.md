# Intuit Inc. (INTU) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Price $320.79 | Market Cap ~$85.7B | EV ~$90B | Sector: Technology / Application Software**
*Data as of Nov 17, 2026. FQ1-27 earnings: Nov 19, 2026 (2 days out).*

---

## 1. Growth Story

Intuit is the dominant financial operating system for US consumers and small businesses — an ~80% gross-margin, 40% FCF-margin franchise with an installed base of ~39M US TurboTax filers and a QuickBooks/Online Ecosystem generating $9.9B at +19%. But the growth thesis that supported a $800 stock has broken: management guided FY2027 revenue to **+9–10% from +14%**, with **TurboTax units guided to only +2–3%**, as agentic AI (OpenAI, Anthropic, native tax agents) raises the question of whether Intuit's DIY tax and bookkeeping toll booths get disintermediated. Intuit's answer is a pivot from software to "AI-driven expert platform" — TurboTax Live (+37%, now 53% of TurboTax) and a multi-year Anthropic partnership embedding Claude across TurboTax, QuickBooks, Credit Karma and Mailchimp — but the market is currently pricing the disruption, not the pivot. **At ~9x FY27E free cash flow, this is now a debate about terminal value, not near-term growth.**

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

Intuit frames its TAM at roughly **$300B+** across three pillars (SMB accounting/payroll/payments/marketing, consumer tax prep, and consumer fintech/marketplace). On $21.4B of revenue that implies **~7% penetration** — the bull case rests on that runway.

My assessment is more cautious than the company's, for three reasons:

- **The SMB platform TAM (~$150B+) is real and underpenetrated**, and Intuit's ex-Mailchimp Online Ecosystem at +23% with QuickBooks Online Accounting +23% shows genuine pricing power and attach-rate expansion (payments, payroll, money, capital). This is the durable core.
- **The consumer tax TAM (~$30B+) is the problem child.** It is not growing, it is being *automated* — and critically, the moat was never the software, it was the regulatory plumbing (e-file rails), the forms library, and the trust/brand. Those are exactly the assets an AI agent can rent rather than replicate. TurboTax units shrinking –2% while revenue grows +7% means Intuit is monetizing price/assisted mix, not volume — a strategy with a finite runway.
- **Credit Karma (+20%)** is the most underappreciated asset: a two-sided marketplace with personal-loan, auto-insurance and credit-card strength, benefiting from AI-driven matchmaking rather than being threatened by it.

**Differentiation vs. peers:** versus H&R Block, Intuit owns the DIY + assisted digital funnel; versus Xero/Sage, Intuit has the US accountant channel and the payments/payroll attach; versus pure AI startups, Intuit has the data (proprietary transaction graphs across ~10M businesses and ~100M consumers) and the distribution. That data + distribution is the only credible defense — and the Anthropic and OpenAI (reported >$100M) partnerships are Intuit choosing to rent frontier models rather than build them.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **FQ1-27 results — Nov 19, 2026 (2 days).** Consensus $4.30B revenue (+10.8%) / $2.46 EPS. Guide is $4.294–4.313B. The first print against the reset bar; a beat-and-raise on the *new* 9–10% baseline would be a genuine re-rating event. High near-term volatility either way.
- **Tax season 2027 (Jan–Apr 2027) unit data.** The single most important data point in the thesis. FY2027 guidance assumes TurboTax units return to **+2–3%** after –2% in FY2026. If agentic-AI DIY defection shows up in Feb–Apr 2027 unit counts, the "contained disruption" case fails outright.
- **Mailchimp separation (segment reporting began Aug 1, 2026).** Now a standalone reportable segment guided to *declining* revenue. Separation is a classic precursor to divestiture — a sale/exit would remove a ~$1.3B drag, crystallize a public admission of the failed $12B deal, and let the core multiple re-rate.
- **Anthropic/Claude product launches** across TurboTax, QuickBooks and Credit Karma (partnership announced Feb 2026). Concrete agentic SKUs with attach rates would convert the "co-opting the disruptor" narrative into numbers.
- **FY2027 margin delivery.** Management guided operating income growth of up to ~27% on 9–10% revenue — i.e., ~200bp+ of margin expansion alongside the growth deceleration. Delivering this converts INTU from "broken growth" to "compounding cash machine," which is what the 9x FCF multiple is mispricing.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

**Not a Buy on growth merits — but a Hold because the valuation floor is now genuinely compelling and the disruption is unproven rather than proven.**

- **The growth thesis is broken, and that is what I am paid to assess.** A 5-year 18.7% CAGR has become 9–10%, and consensus models ~6.5% EPS CAGR through FY2030 with EPS *declining* in the final year. TurboTax unit volume is already negative. Mailchimp — a $12B acquisition — is now a declining, separately-segmented business being groomed for exit. This is the profile of a maturing incumbent defending a franchise, not a company at the frontier of disruptive innovation. On a pure Cathie Wood-style growth framework, INTU has exited the universe.
- **But the market has over-discounted the terminal value.** At $320.79 the stock trades at **~3.7x FY27E revenue, ~9x FY27E FCF (≈11% FCF yield), 14x FY27E non-GAAP EPS, 12.6x EV/EBITDA** — for a business with 80% gross margins, 40% FCF margins, a Rule of 40 of 54, a 100% earnings beat rate, a near-monopoly in US digital tax, and a $7.9B (≈9% of cap) buyback authorization. The market is pricing permanent structural decline; the actual guide is high-single-digit growth with *expanding* margins. **If Intuit merely holds 8–9% growth and 40% FCF margins, 9x FCF is materially too cheap.**
- **The Anthropic/OpenAI partnerships are a defensible strategic response.** Co-opting frontier model providers and pairing them with proprietary consumer/business data and the assisted-expert model (TurboTax Live +37% and now a majority of TurboTax revenue) is the correct strategic play. It may work. It is simply not yet evidenced in units or bookings.
- **Risk/reward is roughly balanced, with an upward bias on valuation and a downside skew on evidence.** Mean analyst target ~$353 (+10%). I would become a buyer on either (a) tax-season 2027 unit data confirming the +2–3% guide, or (b) a Mailchimp divestiture announcement — and I would turn seller if TurboTax units go negative again in FY2027.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Agentic AI disintermediation of DIY tax (the existential risk).** The moat was regulatory plumbing + brand + forms library, all of which a well-tooled AI agent can rent or replicate. TurboTax units at –2% and desktop –7% in FY2026 is the leading indicator; if FY2027 units miss the +2–3% guide, the franchise is in structural decline and the 9x FCF multiple is not cheap — it is a value trap. Pricing power has been masking volume loss, and that runway is finite.
2. **Growth deceleration is now structural, not cyclical.** +32% → +25% → +13% → +13% → +14% → +9–10%. Consensus flat-to-down EPS in FY2027 and a declining FY2030 EPS estimate mean the compounding engine is stalling. A growth multiple cannot be defended by margin expansion alone forever.
3. **Mailchimp is a capital-allocation black mark.** A ~$12B deal now guided to declining revenue and carved out as a separate segment. Continued drag on consolidated growth, plus impairment/exit risk.
4. **Artificial margin expansion is doing the heavy lifting.** FY2027 guidance leans on up to ~27% operating income growth against 9–10% revenue growth. Cost discipline of that magnitude at this scale is difficult to sustain without starving the AI investment the thesis depends on — and the SBC-in-non-GAAP change (Aug 1, 2026) reduces comparability and optically depresses forward EPS.
5. **Credit Karma cyclicality.** At +20% it is the best-performing segment and a discretionary-ad marketplace highly levered to personal-loan and insurance demand. A consumer credit downturn would remove the one genuinely reaccelerating growth engine.
6. **Competitive intensity on both flanks.** H&R Block and free-file alternatives pressure consumer pricing; Xero, Sage and AI-native bookkeeping startups pressure QuickBooks in the SMB ledger; and the frontier labs Intuit now depends on (OpenAI, Anthropic) are also its most likely future competitors.
7. **Balance sheet now levered for the first time.** $7.7B debt vs $7.2B cash, with $1.75B of notes issued in June 2026 to address FY2027 maturities. Modest, but it constrains the buyback that is currently supporting EPS.
