# Macroeconomic Assessment: Intuit Inc. (INTU)
**As of 18 November 2026** | Data: FRED, company filings, market data

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-Off / Late-Cycle Tightening**

The US economy is in an unusual and hostile mix for long-duration software: **growth is solid, the labor market is tight, and inflation is re-accelerating — forcing the Fed back into a hiking cycle.**

- **Real GDP** grew **2.07% y/y in Q3 2026** (GDPC1: $24,611B vs $24,113B), with QoQ SAAR of ~3.4% — growth has *accelerated* through 2026 (Q2 +2.2%, Q1 +2.5%).
- **CPI inflation has roughly doubled in 2026**: from **2.65% y/y in Dec 2025** (326.031) to **~3.8% in Oct 2026** (337.476). Three-month momentum (Jul→Oct) is running at **~5.6% annualized** — inflation is not sticky-and-flat, it is *rising*.
- **The Fed pivoted from cutting to hiking.** Effective fed funds bottomed at **3.63%** (Jun–Aug 2026) and is now **3.88%** (Oct). Fed speakers (Daly, Barkin) publicly flag inflation "moving in the wrong direction"; markets price **>80% odds of a December hike plus one more in 2027**.
- **Crucially, policy is barely restrictive**: with CPI at 3.8%, the *ex-post real* fed funds rate is **~+0.1%**. That is the mechanical reason the hiking cycle has resumed.
- **The long end has blown out**: 10Y UST **5.13%**, up **+69bp since 30 June (4.44%)**; 30Y at **5.47%**. The 2s10s curve is now **+39bp (bear steepening)** — a term-premium/fiscal-risk signal, not a recession signal. **30Y mortgage: 7.11%**, up from 6.43% in early July.
- Labor market is tightening, not loosening: **unemployment 4.1%**, down from 4.5% in Nov 2025.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Intuit's macro profile:** ~95% US revenue, negligible tariff/FX exposure, low leverage — but three cyclical gears: **SMB health** (QuickBooks/Mailchimp, ~60% of revenue), **consumer credit volumes** (Credit Karma), and **tax/employment-driven filing units** (TurboTax). It is also a **long-duration equity**, so the 10Y is a direct valuation input.

| Macro Variable | Impact | Transmission Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **10Y at 5.13% (+69bp)** | **Negative** | Pure duration effect. INTU trades at ~13.9x FY27 non-GAAP EPS — but that is *after* a 60% de-rating; the multiple compression from ~30x has already been inflicted largely by this move. |
| **Fed hiking to 3.88%+** | **Mixed → Negative** | *Positive:* interest income on customer funds held for payroll/tax remittance, and higher float yields. *Negative:* raises QuickBooks Capital funding costs and SMB borrowing costs; Credit Karma's marketplace (cards, personal loans, auto, mortgage) sees lower origination volumes and lower lender marketing budgets. |
| **Mortgage 7.11%** | **Negative** | Freezes the Credit Karma mortgage and home-equity verticals; 7%+ also suppresses consumer refi/auto activity, throttling the highest-RPM marketplace categories. |
| **CPI 3.8% / sticky input costs** | **Mixed** | *Positive:* strong pricing power — revenue per Online Ecosystem customer grew ~15% vs only ~3% customer growth, i.e. Intuit is monetizing price, not seats. *Negative:* that same price/mix dynamic signals **SMB seat formation is stalling**, and tariff-driven input inflation squeezes the small-business customer base that funds Intuit's ARPU. |
| **GDP +2.1%, unemployment 4.1%** | **Positive** | Supports new-business formation, 1099/self-employment (TurboTax Self-Employed, QuickBooks Solopreneur) and filing volumes — a genuine buffer against the AI-disruption narrative. |
| **Card delinquencies 2.85% (falling)** | **Positive** | Intuit bears credit risk via QuickBooks Capital; improving consumer credit quality limits charge-offs even as rates rise. |
| **USD +3.4% since Sept** | **Neutral/Slightly negative** | Only ~5% non-US revenue; Mailchimp and QBO UK/CA/AU translation drag is immaterial. No direct tariff exposure (software/digital delivery). |

**What the tape says:** INTU's decline is **~80% idiosyncratic, ~20% macro**. The stock is **-59.6% from its 29 July 2025 peak ($793.68)** and **-50.9% YTD** (from $653.21), with discrete breaks on: **3 Feb 2026 (-10.9%)**, **21 May 2026 (-20.0%)**, and **17 Nov 2026 (-7.2%)** into the 19 Nov FQ1'27 print. The trigger was company guidance — **FY2027 revenue growth cut to 9–10% from 14%**, TurboTax units guided +2–3% — plus AI-disruption fears. But macro is the **amplifier**: rising discount rates removed any tolerance for a growth deceleration, and the 3% Online Ecosystem customer growth number is itself a macro read on SMB formation.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The macro configuration is a genuine headwind on every revenue gear simultaneously: **higher long rates compress the multiple, a 7.11% mortgage throttles Credit Karma, sticky inflation with tariffs squeezes the SMB customer base, and a resumed Fed hiking cycle keeps the pressure on through 2027.** The offsetting relief is that **valuation has already reset hard** — at **$320.79 / $85.7B market cap**, INTU trades at **~13.9x the FY27 non-GAAP EPS midpoint of $23.00** and **~3.7x EV/sales** on $23.4B revenue — leaving far less multiple risk than a year ago, plus a low-leverage balance sheet that is not exposed to a refinancing wall.

**Net:** unfavourable macro, but a substantially de-risked starting valuation. Macro is now more likely to hurt **fundamentals** (SMB seats, Credit Karma volumes) than to compress the multiple further.

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**Bear (underpriced): a 1994-style tightening cycle.** The market prices one hike after December. If inflation momentum holds near 5% annualized and the Fed is forced to **4.75–5.00% by mid-2027**, the 10Y goes to **5.75–6.00%** and mortgages to **7.75%+**. That is the regime where the **SMB and consumer-credit gears break together**: QuickBooks Online Ecosystem customer growth (already 3%) turns negative, QuickBooks Capital charge-offs inflect from today's benign 2.85% delinquency base, and Credit Karma originations fall >20%. A 10x forward multiple on $23 EPS implies **~$230 (-28%)** — and unlike the 2026 de-rating, this one would be earnings-driven, not sentiment-driven.

**Bull (equally underpriced): disinflation arrives fast in H1 2027.** If tariff pass-through fades and growth cools to ~1.5%, CPI snaps back toward 2.5%, the Fed reverses to **3.25%** by late 2027, and the 10Y falls to **4.30%**. INTU is a 9–10% grower with ~26% operating margins trading at 14x into its **Jan–Apr 2027 tax season** — the re-rating to 18x FY28 EPS alone is **~$420–460 (+35–45%)**. Watch the December FOMC and the Nov 19 FQ1'27 print as the two nearest resolution points.
