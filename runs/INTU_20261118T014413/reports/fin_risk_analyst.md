## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE**

Intuit's balance sheet is not the problem. FY2026 (ended 31-Jul-2026) closed with gross debt of $8.42B against $7.20B of cash and short-term investments (net debt ~$1.22B, or 0.17x EBITDA), interest coverage of ~23x, and $8.66B of free cash flow. A company generating 40% FCF margins and covering interest 23 times is structurally investment-grade regardless of what the equity is doing.

The one genuine credit signal is the *direction*: gross debt rose 27% YoY ($6.64B → $8.42B), short-term debt reappeared ($0 → $1.25B), and book equity fell $0.72B — the first leverage inflection in three years. The Piotroski F-Score registers this (F5 leverage = 0, F6 equity change = 0). This is consistent with buybacks funded partly with debt, not distress.

**Note on Altman Z-Score:** the tool's 2.68 ("grey") uses *book* equity. Substituting market cap ($85.7B vs book $19.0B, x4 moves 1.07 → 4.51) lifts the Z-Score to roughly **4.7 — safe zone**. The grey-zone reading is an artifact of accumulated goodwill ($18.6B, 51% of assets) suppressing book equity, not of balance-sheet weakness.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **LOW (credit/liquidity) — but MODERATE overall, because market and franchise risk dominate**

- **Credit risk: Investment Grade.** Net leverage 0.17x, coverage 23x, Z-Score safe on a market-equity basis.
- **Liquidity risk: Low.** Cash ratio 0.71x, $7.2B cash, positive FCF every year of the sample, no meaningful near-term maturities.
- **Market risk: High.** −68% drawdown, −50% in twelve months, ~45% realized vol, negative Sharpe and Sortino.
- **FCF sustainability: Strong but cyclical in composition.** FCF margin has risen 29% → 40% in two years; 23% of operating cash flow is SBC add-back, so economic FCF is roughly $6.6B (~7.7% yield) rather than $8.66B.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Franchise disruption, not financial distress — this is the whole thesis.** FY2027 guidance cut to 9–10% revenue growth (from 14%), with TurboTax unit growth of only **2–3%**, explicitly framed around AI-disruption fears spreading from tax prep to accounting. The equity is pricing structural impairment of the core tax franchise.
2. **Restructuring execution risk.** May-2026 announcement cut **17% of the workforce (~3,000 staff)** alongside a "bigger AI bet." Restructuring charges plus attrition risk during a platform transition is the classic recipe for a growth air-pocket.
3. **Receivables tripled: $1.98B → $7.26B (+266%).** This is not a normal working-capital swing and looks like a shift of lending/credit-card balances onto the balance sheet (QuickBooks Checking / Credit Karma). **Provision coverage and credit quality of that book are not disclosed in the data I could retrieve — this is the single largest unassessed exposure.** It also inflates the "quick ratio 1.51x" reading: strip the receivables and liquidity is materially thinner.
4. **Buyback capital destruction.** $5.41B repurchased in FY2026 while the stock traded at an average well above today's $321 — and it was partly debt-funded. Payout is 78% of FCF with the stock in free-fall; the buyback is not a valuation signal, it's a mechanical policy.
5. **Leverage inflection.** Debt +27% YoY, equity −$0.72B, F-Score leverage point lost. Small in absolute terms, but it reverses three years of deleveraging.
6. **Refinancing at higher coupons.** ~3.0% average cost of debt on $8.42B → ~5%+ on rollover roughly doubles interest expense. Trivial to coverage; a real EPS drag if margins compress simultaneously.
7. **Momentum/flow risk.** −20% single-day gap on guidance (21-May-2026) demonstrates extreme sensitivity to forward-looking commentary; 22M shares traded that day (~8% of the float).

**Explicit data gaps:** (i) volatility/beta/drawdown/Sharpe computed by me from raw prices — the dedicated tool is unavailable on this subscription; (ii) no credit rating or CDS spread data; (iii) no covenant terms or detailed maturity ladder — interest expense is inferred from the income statement; (iv) segment-level margins and the credit quality/provisioning of the receivables book were not retrievable; (v) the vendor's net-debt figures are internally inconsistent across tools ($1.22B in the leverage module vs $3.72B on the balance sheet) — I used gross debt less cash and short-term investments.

**Bottom line:** Intuit is financially robust and would survive a severe revenue shock with liquidity and coverage intact. The risk in INTU is not whether it can pay its bills — it can, comfortably. The risk is whether its tax and accounting franchises hold value in an AI-native world, and the market has already rendered a 60% negative verdict on that question. Underwrite this as a franchise-risk position, not a credit-risk one.
