## Intuit Inc. (INTU) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price $320.79 | Market cap $85.7B | EV ≈ $86.4B | 277M diluted shares | 52-wk range $252.84–$682.27**

Intuit is the worst-performing large cap in the S&P 500 this year, down ~53% from its high, and the sell-off is not a multiple accident — it is the market repricing the durability of the moat. My job is to determine whether the business has been permanently impaired or merely de-rated. The answer, on the evidence, is mostly the latter — but with a genuine crack in one part of the franchise.

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW** (downgraded from Wide)

**What still works (the durable half):**
- **Global Business Solutions, $12.9B, +16%.** Online Ecosystem +19%, QuickBooks Online Accounting +23% on *higher effective prices* — genuine pricing power, still intact. Ex-Mailchimp, GBS grew 18%, so Mailchimp is roughly flat/declining inside that.
- **Mid-market is compounding fast:** Mid-market revenue +39% in Q4; QBO Advanced + Intuit Enterprise Suite +38%; IES annualized revenue >$145M, 4x year over year, with ~75% of customer adds from desktop migrations. This is high-switching-cost, accountant-embedded software. The accountant network (a distribution moat of roughly a million-plus practitioners trained on Intuit's ledgers) is the single most underappreciated asset here.
- **Assisted tax:** TurboTax Live +37%, now 53% of TurboTax revenue. Human accountability — someone who signs the return — is precisely what an AI agent cannot easily replicate in a regulated, liability-bearing workflow.
- **Credit Karma +20% to $2.6B**, though this is an advertising marketplace tied to consumer lending volumes; economically cyclical and not moated in the Buffett sense.
- **ROIC 21.5%** (FY26), up from 13.1% in FY23, consistently above a ~9% WACC. ROE 24%. That is the quantitative signature of a moat.

**What has broken (the vulnerable half):**
- **DIY tax has lost pricing power.** Management's own words: *"We lost quality DIY customers to low-cost providers... Price is now the number one reason customers leave TurboTax."* TurboTax units fell 2% (39.0M), online units −2%, desktop −7%. When a business must cut price to hold volume, the moat has narrowed — full stop.
- **The funnel is leaking at the low end.** Total online paying customers 8.9M, +3%, decelerating from +5%. TurboTax growth guided to 2–3%; Consumer segment to 4–6%.
- The company is launching **QuickBooks Free and QuickBooks Lite** (20,000+ customers in month one) — a defensive move against AI-native entrants, and an admission that the low end of bookkeeping is being commoditized.
- AI agents are the threat vector: if a general-purpose model can prepare a simple 1040 or categorize a small business's transactions, the simple-return and solopreneur segments lose their toll booth.

**Net:** A bifurcated franchise. Roughly 25% of revenue (DIY tax) plus the low end of SMB is being commoditized; the assisted, mid-market, and platform/payments/payroll layers retain real switching costs. That is a **Narrow moat with a stable-to-slightly-narrowing trend** — not the Wide moat that justified 40x earnings, but far from none. The 5-year trend: widened through FY24 on price, narrowed sharply in FY25–FY26.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **9 / 10**

| Metric | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 12.73 | 14.37 | 16.29 | 18.83 | 21.45 |
| Growth | +32% | +12.9% | +13.3% | +15.6% | +13.9% |
| Gross margin | 81.1% | 78.1% | 78.7% | 79.6% | **80.2%** |
| Operating margin (GAAP) | 20.2% | 21.9% | 22.3% | 26.2% | **27.4%** |
| ROIC | — | 13.1% | 14.5% | 18.7% | **21.5%** |
| Owner earnings ($B) | — | 2.96 | 3.59 | 4.56 | **5.24** |

- **Revenue CAGR 5yr: 17.4%**, remarkably consistent, with 80% gross margins *expanding* while growing double digits. Margin expansion of 720bps since FY22 alongside a 68% revenue increase is the mark of an operating-leveraged franchise, not a melting ice cube.
- **Cash generation:** FY26 operating cash flow $8.84B, capex only $175M (0.8% of revenue — asset-light), reported FCF **$8.66B**. Owner earnings (NI + D&A − capex) **$5.24B**, i.e. $18.90/share.
- **Important adjustment:** SBC is $2.056B, or **9.6% of revenue** — high, real, and dilutive. Reported FCF flatters itself by adding SBC back. On an SBC-charged basis, FCF is **$6.6B** (7.7% yield), and FCF conversion vs. net income is still ~145%. Excellent either way.
- **Balance sheet:** Cash $7.2B vs. debt $7.7B → net debt ~$0.5B, under 0.2x EBITDA. Interest coverage ~24x. $1.75B of notes issued to prefund FY27 maturities. No solvency question whatsoever.
- **Capital returns:** $5.5B of buybacks (+96%) plus $1.35B of dividends = $6.85B returned against $8.66B FCF; diluted shares −2% despite SBC; $7.9B buyback authorization remaining; dividend raised 15% to $1.38/qtr (1.7% yield, ~29% of FCF).
- **Deductions:** $18.6B of goodwill/intangibles vs. $19.0B of equity (Mailchimp overhang); consumer-lending SPVs on balance sheet; one data caveat — receivables jumped from $2.0B to $7.3B in FY26 with total current assets up only $1.2B, implying reclassification (likely lending/funds receivable), which I could not fully reconcile and which I have not credited in valuation.

**Score 9/10** — only the SBC load and the acquisition-heavy balance sheet keep it from a 10.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples:** 19.5x trailing GAAP EPS ($16.46); **~13.9x FY27 guided non-GAAP EPS ($22.88–23.12)**; 12.6x EV/EBITDA; 9.75% reported FCF yield (7.7% SBC-charged); 6.1% owner-earnings yield. Peer software median P/E is 31.7x — a **38.6% discount**, and Morningstar's normalized P/E of 14.05 corroborates the forward multiple. For context, Intuit has spent most of the last decade at 30–50x.

**What the price implies.** A reverse-DCF at $320.79 with a 9% WACC requires roughly 4% perpetual FCF growth — i.e. the market is pricing permanent mid-single-digit growth with no margin expansion. That is a *deeply* pessimistic assumption for a business guiding 9–10% revenue growth with expanding margins and 21% ROIC. Note the $5.5B of FY26 buybacks at an average price far above today's will be substantially accretive if executed at 14x.

**Scenario DCF** (base: SBC-charged FCF of $6.6B; terminal values on year-10 FCF):

| Scenario | Growth path | WACC / TG | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bear** — AI hollows out DIY + low-end SMB | 3% → 1%, $5.8B base | 10% / 1.5% | **~$258** |
| **Mid** — moat narrows but holds; ~6% FCF growth | 6% → 4%, $6.4B base | 9.5% / 2.5% | **~$400** |
| **Base** — FY27 guide achieved, then fade | 9% → 5%, $6.6B base | 9% / 3.0% | **~$560** |

Probability-weighted (30/40/30): **≈ $407 intrinsic value**, ~27% above the current price. A simple cross-check: FY27 non-GAAP EPS of $23.00 at a still-humbled 18x = $414; at 20x = $460.

**Margin of safety:** ~21% to weighted intrinsic value, ~43% to base case — but the bear case sits ~20% *below* today's price. So the downside is bounded and modest, while the upside is large; that is a favorable asymmetry, though not the 40–50% discount Buffett demands on a business with an intact Wide moat.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **B / A−**

**Strengths:**
- **Unusual candor.** Goodarzi: *"I am personally dissatisfied and hold myself accountable for the lack of performance, which is DIY tax, and on the low end in the business group."* He also conceded the old pricing-led model *"worked for many years, but the market has changed."* CEOs rarely admit losing on price; it is the precondition for fixing it.
- **Accounting integrity, revealed.** Effective FY27, Intuit *stopped excluding SBC* from non-GAAP — guiding $22.88–23.12 on a newly SBC-charged basis (restated FY26: $18.56 vs. the old $24.27). Management voluntarily made its headline metric *worse*. That is the opposite of most software companies, and it materially raises my trust in the numbers. It is also why the headline "+23–24% EPS growth" looks deceptively strong.
- **Owner-operator capital returns:** 15% dividend raise, $5.5B buyback, 2% net share reduction, and decisive cost action (17% workforce reduction to fund AI reinvestment).

**Weaknesses:**
- **M&A record is mixed:** Credit Karma ($7.1B, 2020) has worked (+20% growth). Mailchimp ($12B, 2021) has not — GBS ex-Mailchimp grew 18% vs. 16% reported, meaning Mailchimp is shrinking. Breaking it out as a separate segment from FY27 reads like pre-divestiture housekeeping; doing so would be the right call.
- **Buyback timing was poor:** $5.5B repurchased in FY26, up 96%, at prices well above today's. Value-destructive ex post, though disciplined going forward at 14x.
- **The strategic reset is a bet, not a certainty:** deliberately cutting DIY ARPC to buy back customers whose stated reason for leaving was price. If the AI-native entrants keep pricing at zero, this buys a customer base that must be monetized elsewhere (Credit Karma cross-sell, Live upgrades). The logic is coherent — dual-product users generate ~2x revenue per customer, and >75% of TurboTax Live adds came from DIY upgrades — but unproven.
- **Insider ownership data was unavailable** in my data feeds — a genuine gap in my assessment of alignment. I do not infer it.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **BUY** (scaled entry, not Strong Buy)

**The thesis in one paragraph:** Intuit is an 80%-gross-margin, 21%-ROIC, asset-light franchise with 9–10% guided revenue growth, expanding operating margins, a pristine balance sheet, and a management team that just chose to make its own reporting more honest — trading at 14x forward SBC-inclusive earnings because the market has decided AI will commoditize tax prep and bookkeeping. The market is pricing ~4% perpetual growth against a business actually guiding high-single-digit growth. The moat has unquestionably narrowed at the low end, which is why this is not a Wide-moat compounder at any price. But at 14x, with the bear case only ~20% below the current quote, the risk/reward is roughly 1:3 in the buyer's favor.

**Position sizing:** Initiate at 1/2 to 1/3 of a full position at $320, adding toward the $255–275 region (52-week low territory), where the bear case itself would be fully discounted and the FCF yield would exceed 10%. Intended holding period 5+ years.

**What would make me a Strong Buyer:** Consumer segment growth stabilizing in the 4–6% guided range with DIY unit declines flattening by the FY27 tax season; QuickBooks paying-customer growth reaccelerating above 3%; and Mailchimp either divested or returned to growth.

**What would make me sell:** TurboTax units falling mid-single digits *despite* the price reset (proving price was not the real cause — AI substitution was); Consumer revenue turning negative; or FY27 non-GAAP EPS guidance being cut below $21.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI commoditization at the low end (highest severity).** If general-purpose agents can file a simple return or do basic bookkeeping at near-zero marginal cost, the DIY funnel — the acquisition engine for the entire consumer franchise — erodes structurally, and my $258 bear case becomes optimistic.
2. **Price-led competition becomes a spiral.** Management has conceded price is the #1 churn reason. A price war with low-cost filers (and potentially free AI filing) could compress Consumer margins faster than volume recovers, permanently lowering the terminal margin assumption.
3. **Consumer segment at stall speed.** Guided to 4–6% from +11%. Another leg down puts the whole "9–10% total growth" guide at risk, and this management team has now established a pattern of resetting expectations downward.
4. **Credit Karma cyclicality and on-balance-sheet lending.** +20% last year masks exposure to consumer credit conditions; the 10-K subsidiary list confirms multiple lending SPVs (IFI Borrower SPVs, Intuit Financing, Credit Karma Mortgage) — real credit risk inside a "software" multiple.
5. **Goodwill impairment / Mailchimp.** $18.6B of goodwill and intangibles against $19.0B of equity. A Mailchimp writedown would hit book value, though not cash earnings.
6. **SBC dilution.** At 9.6% of revenue, SBC is a real economic cost. Buybacks currently offset it, but that consumes roughly $1.5–2.0B of annual FCF that would otherwise be distributable.
7. **Data-quality caveat:** the FY26 receivables jump from $2.0B to $7.3B is unexplained by the feeds I could access and may signal a growing lending/bookings book. I have not given it valuation credit, but it warrants verification in the 10-K's credit-quality disclosures.

**Bottom line:** A good business at a genuinely cheap price, with a narrower moat than its history implied. That combination argues for ownership — sized with respect for the possibility that the moat is narrowing faster than the numbers yet show.
