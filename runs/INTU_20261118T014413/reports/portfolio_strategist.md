## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT, with a strong underlying core**

There are two clean camps, and they are not actually in conflict — they are answering different questions.

**Consensus (high agreement, 8 of 11 agents):**
- The business is **financially sound and high quality**: 80% gross margin, 21.5% ROIC rising monotonically, 40% FCF margin, 0.17x net leverage, negative accruals, 1.9x cash conversion. Fin Risk, Short, Quant, Value, Growth all confirm independently.
- **No accounting fraud.** Short Analyst formally rejects the forensic case; the Beneish red flag is a false positive proven by the cash-flow statement. This resolves the one conflict that would have overridden everything.
- The **valuation is genuinely cheap** on absolute and peer-relative bases: cheapest or 2nd-cheapest of the software comp set on P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/FCF, P/B.

**Contested (the real disagreement):**
- **Is the moat narrowing cyclically or structurally?** Value says narrowing-at-the-edges with a bounded bear case ($258). Growth says the growth mandate is *over* (6.5% EPS CAGR, FY30 EPS below FY29). Sector flags the terminal-value renegotiation as unresolved. **I weight Growth + Sector here** — the burden of proof sits with the bull, because the revenue mix is price-led (ARPU +15%, customers +3%) and that is precisely the mix AI disintermediation attacks first.
- **Is the price already discounting it?** Value/Quant say yes (4% perpetual growth implied). Non-Fin Risk and Alt Data say the *evidence* hasn't arrived yet — no insider buying, no unit stabilization, no customer-add reacceleration.

**Conflict resolution applied:** Growth's "thesis broken" and Value's "cheap" are both true and are resolved by *sizing and timing*, not by picking a winner. Non-Fin Risk's HIGH rating and the absence of any insider bid cap the ceiling on position size regardless of how cheap the multiple is. I do **not** let the fraud flag override (it was disproven), but I do let the governance/credibility flag haircut conviction.

**Net conviction: MEDIUM — enough to own, not enough to own aggressively, and not before tomorrow's print.**

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector / sub-industry concentration — the binding constraint.**
INTU is *application software with a financial-workflow wrapper*, not a diversifier within tech. Measured correlations (252d) are the key portfolio fact:

| Pair | Correlation |
|---|---|
| INTU–ADBE | **0.72** |
| INTU–PAYX | **0.66** |
| INTU–CRM | **0.62** |
| INTU–NOW | 0.59 |
| INTU–MSFT | 0.32 |
| **INTU–SPY** | **−0.02** |

**Implication:** INTU is **not** a beta diversifier even though its correlation to the index is zero. It is a **concentrated bet on the application-software value/quality cohort** and on the specific factor "cheap software that AI might eat." If the portfolio already holds ADBE/CRM/PAYX/NOW, this is effectively adding to the same factor at ~0.6–0.7 correlation — treat INTU's 1–3% as an add to a software-value sleeve and cap the *sleeve* at ~8–10%, not the name.

**Factor exposure:**
- **Adds:** Value (88/100) and Quality (80/100) — the two factors most portfolios are structurally short of after a multi-year growth/momentum run. This is a genuine factor diversification benefit.
- **Reduces:** Momentum (−52% 12m alpha) and Low-Vol (8/100). In a momentum- or low-vol-screened mandate, INTU is an *anti-fit* and should be carried in a separate value sleeve or excluded.
- **Neutral on size and on market beta** — regression beta is statistically meaningless (R² ≈ 0.0003); use the vendor's 0.92 for risk reporting but ~50% realized vol for risk budgeting.

**Correlation risk is idiosyncratic, not systemic.** With near-zero SPY correlation, INTU will not amplify a broad market drawdown much; it will, however, deliver tail events on its own (three −7% to −20% single days in 2026). Size it as an idiosyncratic-risk position, which is exactly what a 1–3% weight does.

**Macro overlay:** UNFAVOURABLE. 10Y at 5.13% with the Fed hiking and CPI re-accelerating to 3.8% is a hostile regime for any long-duration equity. This further argues for the low end of the range and for leaving room to add if the 10Y pushes toward 6%.

## 7. Final Portfolio Action

**What would make me exit entirely:** a mid-single-digit TurboTax unit decline in FY2027 despite the price reset — at which point the 14x multiple is not cheap, it is a value trap, and the bear case was optimistic.
