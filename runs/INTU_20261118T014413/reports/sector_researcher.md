## SECTOR BRIEF — INTUIT INC. (INTU)
**Sector:** Technology / Application Software — SMB financial, accounting & tax technology
**Price $320.79 | Mkt cap $85.7B | FY26 (Jul-26) revenue $21.45B**

### 1. Sector Overview

Intuit competes in two linked pools: **SMB financial/accounting software** (QuickBooks, Mailchimp, Intuit Enterprise Suite) and **consumer + professional tax preparation** (TurboTax, Credit Karma, Lacerte/ProSeries). Gartner now pegs 2026 global software spend at **~$1.44T, +15.1%** — demand did not decelerate — yet application-software multiples have compressed roughly **50% below their five-year average** as investors price the risk that AI agents disintermediate seat-based workflow software. The sector is **late-cycle for classic per-seat SaaS and early-cycle for agentic AI**: unit/customer growth is slowing industry-wide while monetisation shifts to consumption, AI add-ons and price. Barriers remain high (double-entry data gravity, tax-code compliance, accountant-channel lock-in, brand trust in filing), so incumbents enter the AI era with distribution advantages but face a structural re-rating of the terminal growth rate.

**Note on peer set:** the feed's "Technology sector" peer list is semiconductor/mega-cap dominated and not informative; I built a purpose-made comparable set of application-software and financial-workflow names.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Software budgets are intact and rising.** 2026 software spend +15.1% to $1.44T (Gartner, revised *up* from 14.7%), which caps the downside case for a franchise with 3% SMB customer growth — the sector is not in a demand recession, it is in a multiple recession.
- **AI monetisation is real for Intuit specifically.** "Big Bets" grew **+34%** and are now **30% of revenue** (FY26); TurboTax Live **+37%**; QuickBooks Online Accounting **+23%**; Online Ecosystem ex-Mailchimp **+23%**. Intuit is distributing AI (8 named QuickBooks agents) through an installed base rather than buying growth, and has partnered with **OpenAI (>$100M multiyear model access)** and **Anthropic** for agent infrastructure.
- **Compliance complexity is a permanent moat-widener.** Tax-code and payroll regulation generate mandatory, non-discretionary spend; Intuit's mid-market push (Intuit Enterprise Suite, conversational AI for CFOs/accounting firms) opens a larger revenue-per-customer pool with the same accountant channel.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **AI-native substitution is now the sector's core debate — and it is contested at Intuit's exact centre.** Intuit faces a **shareholder lawsuit alleging tax filers are migrating to AI tools**, and free/cheap LLM-assisted filing plus DIY-adjacent entrants erode the TurboTax funnel. TurboTax growth has already decelerated to **+7%** vs Consumer +11% and total +14%.
- **Growth is price-led, not unit-led.** Per third-party analysis of FY26, QuickBooks Online accounting revenue grew 23% but **Online Ecosystem customer growth was only ~3%**, with ~15% revenue per customer — a mix that is powerful in a benign economy and fragile if SMB formation or pricing tolerance weakens. Consolidated growth is decelerating: **+15.6% (FY25) → +13.9% (FY26) → Q1 FY27 guided $4.29–4.31B, ~+10–11%**.
- **Input-cost and tax-rate normalisation.** Inference costs (>$100M to OpenAI alone) migrate into cost of revenue, and FY26 free cash flow benefited from unusually low cash taxes; higher FY27 taxes and stock-based compensation compress the quality of the earnings base. Sector-wide, gross margins are being squeezed by AI compute substituting for near-zero-marginal-cost hosting.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (with a widening crack)**

**Rationale for strong:** Intuit holds the two most defensible chokepoints in US consumer/SMB finance — the double-entry ledger of ~10M+ small businesses and the tax-filing moment. Switching costs are structural (historical transaction data, accountant workflows, payroll/tax integration), the accountant channel is a captive distribution moat, and data gravity feeds Credit Karma. It converts that position into **80%+ gross margins and 41.5% non-GAAP operating margin** — best-in-class versus a 16.9% peer median — and it is the only peer growing double digits with expanding margins. Porter's Five Forces read: **supplier power high and rising** (model providers), **buyer power low** (SMB), **substitutes rising** (the one force moving against it), **new entrants rising but from a low base**, **rivalry moderate** (Xero/Sage internationally, HRB in tax).

**The crack:** the franchise is currently monetising price and AI attach into a **flat-ish customer base (Online Ecosystem customers ~+3%)**. If AI agents compress the willingness to pay for the "form-filling" layer of tax and bookkeeping before Intuit's agentic platform replaces that revenue itself, growth migrates from mid-teens to high-single-digit and the current 13x non-GAAP multiple is a value trap rather than a bargain.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Demand is healthy (software spend +15.1%) and Intuit's own execution is above peer median on every margin and growth line — but the sector's **terminal-value assumption is under active renegotiation**, evidenced by ~50% multiple compression across application software, a shareholder suit against the category leader, and unit-growth deceleration beneath Intuit's headline numbers. I would not underwrite a sector re-rating; the case for INTU here rests on **company-specific operating leverage and a heavily de-rated multiple**, not on sector tailwinds.
