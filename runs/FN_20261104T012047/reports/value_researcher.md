# Fabrinet (FN) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $363.33 (Nov 3, 2026) | Market cap ~$13.0B (36.2M diluted sh) | FY ends June; latest reported = Q1 FY27 (quarter ended 9/25/26)*

**The setup:** The stock peaked near $732 in June 2026 and has halved to $363 despite beating estimates every quarter. Revenue is compounding at 35–50%. The entire question for a value investor is whether this is a durable franchise on sale, or peak-cycle contract manufacturing earnings at a premium multiple.

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW**

Fabrinet is the world's premier outsourced precision optical packaging manufacturer: it builds 800G/1.6T transceivers, ROADMs, amplifiers and tunable lasers for optical OEMs, plus industrial lasers and sensors, from a large campus in Thailand (21,500 employees). Its genuine advantages:

- **Process know-how and vertically integrated optics** (in-house crystal growth, thin-film coating, precision alignment). Optical packaging yields are tacit knowledge built over 25 years — hard to replicate.
- **Cost advantage:** Thailand manufacturing base, ~$4.6B revenue on just $619M net PP&E. This is real efficient-scale economics.
- **Switching costs:** customer qualification cycles run 12–24 months; re-sourcing a qualified optical line is painful and risky.
- **Capital returns confirm it:** ROIC 16.0% → 15.8% → 16.3% → **18.8%** (FY23–FY26), consistently ~700–800bps above a ~10–11% WACC. ROE 19.3%. Incremental ROIC on FY26's invested capital was ~23%.

**But the moat is narrow, and the income statement proves it.** Gross margin over six years: 11.78% → 12.32% → 12.71% → 12.35% → 12.09% → **11.99%**. It has *narrowed* slightly. Operating margin has sat in a 9.0–10.0% band the entire time. A business that doubles revenue ($2.26B FY22 → $4.64B FY26) without expanding margins by even 100bps does not have pricing power — it has a volume business with a fixed mark-up. Absolute gross profit is compounding nicely (+25% CAGR two years), but that is throughput, not rent extraction.

**Customer concentration is severe and getting more so:** FY26 had **four** customers each above 10% of revenue (largest 16.3%), versus two in FY25. Worse, per B. Riley, revenue from NVIDIA — the largest customer — *fell 21% to $742M in FY26*. The company grew 36% anyway (broadening is good), but this proves the demand is customer-concentrated, lumpy, and not under Fabrinet's control.

Verdict: a good, well-run manufacturer with a **narrow moat** in optical packaging, no economic moat in the classic pricing-power sense.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **6 / 10**

| FCF / Net income | 61% | 124% | 62% | **0.6%** | negative |

**The good:** Net cash (FY26: $875M cash vs $4M debt), no dividend obligation, SBC only 0.7% of revenue (genuinely shareholder-friendly), 5-yr revenue CAGR 18.9%, and an unbroken record of beating guidance. Diluted shares fell from 37.4M to 36.2M over four years.

**The bad — and it is material:**
- **FCF conversion has collapsed.** FY26 FCF of $3M. Capex exploded to $254M (5.5% of revenue vs 1.6% in FY24) and working capital consumed another $260M.
- **Q1 FY27 is worse:** operating cash flow $21M against capex $159M. Capex is now running ~11% of revenue annualized.
- **Inventory +76% YoY to $1.02B** (~91 days vs 70 days). Receivables $1.02B (DSO stable at 80 days — that part is fine). Some of this is justified pre-build for the ramp, but it is also obsolescence risk in a market transitioning 800G→1.6T.
- **First debt in years:** $74.8M long-term borrowing drawn in Q1 FY27. Also a $21.5M loss on non-marketable equity securities — a small sign of capital-allocation drift.
- Only $104M of GAAP net income on $1.47B of revenue (7.0% net margin) in Q1 FY27, versus $157M non-GAAP. Tax rate jumped to ~29% from ~6% (Swiss/Cayman mix).

A business that converts 0.6% of net income into free cash flow in its best growth year is not yet a free-cash-flow compounding machine. It is a capex-hungry one.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **Premium to intrinsic value**

Current multiples: **27.2x TTM GAAP EPS ($13.36)**, **23.4x TTM non-GAAP EPS ($15.55)**, 19.7x EV/EBITDA, 5.4x book. No dividend. Peer median P/E 37x (mega-cap semi/tech) — so FN screens 25% "cheap" *against inflated AI peers*, which is not the right yardstick.

**Forward:** Q2 FY27 guidance is $1.60–1.65B revenue and GAAP EPS $3.81–3.96 (non-GAAP $4.77–4.92). Annualizing the trajectory, FY27E revenue ~$6.6B (+42%), GAAP EPS ~$15.5 (~23x), non-GAAP EPS ~$19.4 (~19x). On the surface, not demanding for a 40% grower.

**My DCF (10% WACC — beta 1.345 fully justifies it; 3% terminal growth), modeling the capex/WC reality rather than trailing FCF:**

| | FY27E | FY28E | FY29E | FY30E | FY31E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 6.6 | 7.3 | 7.9 | 8.2 | 8.4 |
| Net income ($M) | 570 | 679 | 750 | 779 | 781 |
| FCF ($M) | ~40 | ~425 | ~533 | ~603 | ~617 |

PV of FCFs ~$1.58B + PV of terminal value ~$5.64B + net cash → **intrinsic value ≈ $7.4B, or ~$205/share.**

**Cross-check on normalized earnings power:** mid-cycle revenue ~$6B at a 9.3–9.5% net margin = ~$560M; at 17–20x = **$265–310/share**.

**Intrinsic value range: $205–310, base case ~$265.** The stock at $363 trades at a **~35% premium** to my base case. To buy with a 25% margin of safety I would need ~**$200**.

**What the current price implies:** ~$363 requires revenue near $9–10B by FY31 (roughly doubling again from here) *and* net margins expanding to 10.5%+ — a margin level Fabrinet has never once achieved in its public history. That is a lot to ask of a 12%-gross-margin pass-through manufacturer.

## 4. Management Quality: **Good (B+)**

- CEO Seamus Grady (since 2018) has executed flawlessly on operations — 20+ consecutive guidance beats, disciplined Thailand capacity build, zero customer-losing quality failures at scale.
- **Capital allocation is restrained and rational:** minimal SBC, steady modest buybacks (share count down 3% in four years), no empire-building M&A (goodwill is a trivial $2.5M), no dividend. Buyback authorization now $637.3M (~5% of cap), $29.1M used in Q1 — sensible but small, and not aggressive enough to be a catalyst.
- **Deductions:** (a) the new $75M borrowing and the $21.5M equity-securities loss hint at a loosening of the historically conservative balance sheet; (b) buybacks are being struck at 25–30x earnings, which is not value-accretive; (c) founder-family control (Mitchell as non-exec chair) with limited recent insider buying that I could verify — a data gap.
- Nothing here is disqualifying. This is honest, competent, operationally excellent management — not exceptional capital allocators.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (avoid new purchase; trim existing positions)

This is a **valuation call, not a business-quality condemnation**. Fabrinet is a genuinely good narrow-moat manufacturer with a fortress balance sheet, an excellent operator at the helm, and a real secular tailwind in AI optical interconnect.

But every Buffett test fails on price:
1. **No margin of safety** — ~35% *premium* to base-case intrinsic value of ~$265, not the required 20–30% discount.
2. **Earnings are cyclically peak-like.** AI datacom capex is at an unprecedented high; FY27 at +42% growth with 10.8% non-GAAP operating margins is arguably an unsustainable combination.
3. **Free cash flow is not corroborating the earnings.** $3M of FY26 FCF and negative Q1 FY27 FCF mean the "owner earnings" are being reinvested faster than they are generated. Buffett's owner-earnings test is not passed today.
4. **The price already discounts the bull case.** You are paying for margin expansion this company has never delivered.

**What would change my mind:** price below ~$210–240 (20–25% discount to base case), OR evidence of structural gross-margin expansion to 13.5%+ (i.e., real pricing power emerging from silicon photonics/co-packaged optics), OR two consecutive quarters
