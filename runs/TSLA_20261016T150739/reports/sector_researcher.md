# Sector Brief: Global Auto Manufacturing / Electric Vehicles — Tesla, Inc. (TSLA)

## 1. Sector Overview

Tesla operates in global auto manufacturing (Consumer Cyclical), the epicentre of the EV transition. The sector is a mature, cyclical volume business (~$2–3T global revenue among the largest OEMs; TSLA's peers Toyota, GM, Ford, Stellantis all print $150–335B annual revenue) currently undergoing structural disruption: global EV sales still grew in 2025 but at sharply decelerating single-digit rates, triggering a brutal price war — most acute in China — that has crushed margins across the board. Aggregate sector-level data from the feed was unusable (garbled period labels/company counts), so benchmarking here is company-level.

## 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Global EV penetration still rising**: IEA's 2025 Global EV Outlook shows continued market expansion even as incumbent leaders stall; Tesla still owns ~54–60% of US EV sales while legacy rivals (GM, Ford, Stellantis) retreat from EV commitments — a US share cushion.
- **Energy storage optionality**: Tesla's Energy Generation & Storage segment is growing far faster than autos and is a genuine second growth engine at higher margins.
- **Structural scale assets**: Supercharger network, direct-sales model, FSD/robotaxi program and ~$1.4T market cap give funding/technology advantages no legacy OEM can match.

## 3. Key Headwinds
- **Demand/regulatory shock**: US federal EV tax credit expired in 2025 and Tesla deliveries fell ~9% YoY to ~1.64M units (Reuters); FY2025 revenue declined -2.9%.
- **Share loss to BYD**: BYD overtook Tesla as the world's largest EV manufacturer in 2025; Tesla's global EV share fell from ~60% (2020) to ~38% (2024) as ~110 new models entered the market (IEA).
- **Margin-destroying price competition & brand damage**: Chinese/European price wars plus polarising brand perception pressured Europe sales; margins have collapsed toward sector norms.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Weak-to-Average (leaning Weak on growth)**

Tesla is the *worst* revenue grower among profitable OEMs (-2.9% vs peer median ~+1.2%) — a growth company that has stopped growing in a market that is still expanding, i.e. structural share loss. Profitability is a relative bright spot: 18.0% gross margin remains the highest among mass-market peers and above Toyota's 16.7%, but Tesla's 4.6% operating margin now sits *below* Toyota's 7.4% and is a fraction of its own 2022 peak (16.8%), confirming price war damage. Net income fell from $15.0B (2023) to $3.8B (2025). Tesla still holds the US EV lead and an enormous balance-sheet/technology advantage, but on the two metrics that matter in this cycle — growth and margin durability — it is underperforming the sector median.

## 6. Sector Rating: **Neutral**

The EV transition is real but maturation is here: volume growth has fallen to single digits, pricing power is negative sector-wide, and legacy peers are either retreating (supporting incumbents) or dumping inventory (suppressing prices). Tesla combines genuine tailwinds (energy storage, US consolidation, robotaxi optionality) with an unusually adverse near-term mix — tax-credit expiry, brand damage, BYD-led share loss, and 3 straight years of margin compression. Balanced fundamentals and a still-rich valuation argue for Neutral, not Attractive.
