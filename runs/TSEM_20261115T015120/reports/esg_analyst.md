## TOWER SEMICONDUCTOR (TSEM) — ESG ANALYSIS

**Company:** Tower Semiconductor Ltd. | Israeli-domiciled analog/mixed-signal foundry | Mkt cap ~$16.7B, 5,613 FTEs | Fabs: Migdal Haemek (Israel, 150/200mm ×2), Newport Beach CA, San Antonio TX, TPSCo/Hokuriku Japan (51% JV, 300mm), Agrate Italy (300mm, STMicro JV)
**Evidence note:** TSEM is a foreign private issuer; SEC EDGAR and ir.towersemi.com returned 403s on the 20-F, so risk-factor language below is drawn from search-indexed excerpts of the FY2025 20-F, the company's own ESG disclosures, and its 2026 proxy (6-K). Third-party ESG ratings are below; no assurance opinion was located.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (High on water, chemicals, disclosure quality)

Evidence:
- **Emissions:** FY2024 total GHG **−11% vs 2022 baseline**. Target is **−15% absolute Scope 1+2 by 2030** and **net zero by 2050** — a conspicuously weak interim target (15% over eight years) versus peers (TSMC, STMicro, Microchip at 40–80% renewable electricity and steeper absolute cuts). ~**20% renewable electricity**.
- **Scope gap — the biggest single disclosure red flag:** the sustainability report explicitly **excludes TPSCo (Japan) and Agrate (Italy)**, i.e. a material share of group capacity, "not fully owned." Reported environmental metrics therefore do **not** represent the consolidated footprint, and TPSCo sits in the 51%-JV with Nuvoton where Tower's operational control is partial. No Scope 3 inventory disclosed.
- **Water:** ~**856 thousand m³/yr recycled (2021–2023)** — meaningful but small relative to fab draw; Israel operations depend on a desalination-heavy national grid and a politically allocated water budget. Water-stress exposure is structural, not cyclical.
- **Chemicals:** Robust policy architecture (permits, hazardous-substance management, wastewater/air-emissions control, Prop 65/REACH/RoHS/Montreal Protocol compliance, annual SGS Taiwan product audits). This is process discipline, not a forward climate strategy.
- **No environmental fines, spills or remediation liabilities surfaced** in controversy screening. Treat as a data gap rather than a clean bill of health, since Israeli EPA enforcement is not well covered in English-language feeds.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

- **Geopolitical/labour concentration is the dominant social factor.** ~1,296 Israel-based employees (FY2025) across HQ and two fabs; sustained reserve duty (miluim) since Oct 2023 creates absenteeism and productivity drag, and 20-F risk language flags "hostilities and instability in neighbouring states" with potential loss of income, reputation and customers. Customers in automotive and medical increasingly require geographic diversification — a structural, not transient, risk to a single-region foundry.
- **Product safety/quality:** Serving automotive, medical device and aerospace/defense end markets, where a quality escape is a licence-to-operate event. This is the social factor most likely to become a cash-flow event.
- **Supply chain labour:** Responsible Business Alliance member with a responsible supply-chain programme; RBA/conflict-minerals alignment is a positive but is policy-level, unverified by third-party audit.
- **Diversity & human capital:** Board has at least two female directors (Iris Avner, plus others) and a published "Board Diversity and Skills Matrix" — better than typical Israeli small-cap. But **no public disclosure of employee turnover, gender pay ratio, or TRIR/recordable incident rate**, which is below semiconductor-peer disclosure norms (TSMC, Intel, onsemi all publish these).
- **Community:** Migdal Haemek is a development-town location where Tower is the anchor employer — high social licence value, but also single-employer concentration risk for the community.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.0 / 10**

**Positives:**
- **Chairman/CEO separation** — Amir Elstein (Chair, since Jan 2009) and Russell Ellwanger (CEO) are distinct; no CEO duality.
- **No dual-class structure**; one share, one vote — genuinely shareholder-friendly relative to Israeli tech peers.
- **"Special Majority" approval** required for comp and related-party items under the Israeli Companies Law, forcing minority-holder consent — a real protection, and unusual by US standards.
- Active audit committee with two designated **audit committee financial experts** (Chelouche, Avner); Big-4-affiliated auditor (Brightman Almagor Zohar/Deloitte); **clawback policy** adopted per SEC/Nasdaq rules; performance share units alongside time-based RSUs.
- Board skills matrix published; directors drawn from Intel, Applied Materials, KLA — sector competence is high.

**Negatives:**
- **Entrenchment:** Chairman 17 years, CEO ~21 years, one director since 2005, another since 2016. Low refresh limits independence of mind and succession planning; key-man risk is material in a relationship-driven foundry business.
- **Compensation magnitude signals weak alignment discipline:** CEO base salary raised to **$1,081,859** (+5%) in a year when equity markets re-rated the stock sharply (mkt cap $19.7B → $29.8B May 2026); a large share of LTI remains **time-vesting RSUs** ($200K/yr per non-exec director; up to $14.06M policy equity ceiling), diluting pay-for-performance. Chairman receives **$600K** — high for a non-executive role at this size.
- **Related-party governance:** The Agrate 300mm JV is with **STMicroelectronics** — simultaneously a major customer and a competitor. TPSCo (Nuvoton) is a 51% consolidated JV with its own board. These structures create recurring related-party and capital-allocation conflicts that minority holders cannot easily monitor, and they are precisely what gets *excluded* from ESG reporting.
- **ESG oversight architecture undisclosed:** no evidence of a board-level sustainability committee or ESG-linked compensation metric. Sustainability report is consultant-prepared (Good Vision/Grant Thornton) with **internal review only — no limited assurance**.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (lower-half)

Corroborating third-party evidence: **S&P Global CSA ESG Score 32, CSA Score 27** — bottom-half of the semiconductors cohort (compare onsemi's Sustainalytics 19.9 "Low Risk" profile; TSMC and STMicro sit structurally ahead on decarbonisation and water). Tower is **not** an ESG laggard on governance mechanics (no dual class, minority special-majority protection, clawback, financial experts) but is a **disclosure laggard** (JV-excluded metrics, no Scope 3, no assurance, no safety/turnover data, weak 2030 target) and carries an **unhedgeable geographic concentration**.

**Leader / Average / Laggard / ESG Risk → Average, with a distinct "ESG Risk" overlay on physical/geopolitical exposure.**

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

- **ESG is not the thesis driver, and it is not a valuation headwind.** Tower's carbon intensity and controversy record are benign; there is no stranded-asset, greenwashing or governance-scandal risk that should move a DCF.
- **The one ESG factor that is genuinely priced is geography.** The Israel concentration (HQ, two fabs, ~23% of employees) is a real discount-rate and customer-concentration input — it belongs in the risk section of the memo, not the ESG appendix. Any escalation, or a major customer mandating non-Israel second sourcing, is the identifiable ESG-to-cash-flow transmission channel.
- **Governance is adequate but complacent.** A 17-year chair and 21-year CEO with rising cash compensation and time-vesting-heavy equity is a mild negative for long-term alignment; the Israeli special-majority regime and single share class offset much of it.
- **Watch items ranking (highest first):** (1) Israel security/reserve-duty escalation; (2) USITC §337 outcome touching TPSCo capacity; (3) PFAS/process-gas regulation capex; (4) any broadening of sustainability reporting to include TPSCo/Agrate (would likely *worsen* reported metrics — model for a headline regression); (5) Agrate/STMicro related-party capital commitments.
- **Positioning:** Neutral — do not pay for ESG quality here, and do not underwrite an ESG discount either. If the mandate screens on disclosure completeness or JV-transparent emissions data, TSEM fails on data quality rather than on performance; classify as **unrated/unscorable** rather than exclude.
