# Nutanix, Inc. (NASDAQ: NTNX) — ESG Assessment
**Sector:** Technology / Software–Infrastructure · **HQ:** San Jose, CA · **Market cap:** ~$12.6B · **Employees:** ~8,350 · **Revenue (FY25):** ~$2.54B

---

## 1. ESG Materiality Map

Nutanix is an **asset-light software licensor**. It does not fabricate hardware for sale at scale (its NX appliances are built by a manufacturing partner; most deployments run on customers' Dell/Lenovo/HPE servers). This dramatically narrows the standard E-footprint questions and shifts materiality toward **governance, cyber/product security, and supply-chain/social leverage**.

| Factor | Materiality | Rationale (financial transmission channel) |
|---|---|---|
| **G — Accounting integrity & disclosure controls** | **High** | Documented history of an internal-control failure (below). Drives restatement risk, class-action exposure, cost of capital. |
| **G — Board independence & shareholder rights** | Medium-High | Clean structure; underpins take-private/M&A and capital-allocation credibility. |
| **G/S — Cybersecurity & data privacy (product)** | **High** | Acropolis/AHV sit under customers' most sensitive workloads. A breach = warranty, churn, and NIS2/regulatory liability. Board has a dedicated Security & Privacy Committee. |
| **S — Human capital (restructuring, CEO transition)** | Medium-High | Two simultaneous shocks: CEO succession + 5% RIF. Retention of R&D/sales talent is the core asset. |
| **S — Supply-chain labour (hardware OEM partners)** | Medium | Indirect but real; Dell/HPE/Lenovo server supply chain carries forced-labour and conflict-minerals tail risk. |
| **E — Customer energy/climate enablement** | Medium (as **revenue driver**) | The "50% energy reduction / 66% smaller footprint" claim is a *commercial* differentiator vs. VMware, not a cost line. |
| **E — Own emissions & climate targets** | Low-Medium | ~6,561 tCO₂e Scope 1+2 — immaterial financially; rising Scope 3 and absence of targets create *disclosure* risk under CA SB 253. |
| **E — Physical climate risk** | Low | Leased offices/datacenters; no owned plant, no water-intensive operations. |

**Not material:** stranded assets, CBAM, water stress, biodiversity, product safety (consumer), EHS injury rates.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW**

Third-party-limited-assured inventory (LRQA, 4th consecutive year) — genuinely better disclosure quality than most $2.5B-cap software peers.

| Metric (MTCO₂e) | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | Δ vs. base |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Scope 1 | 398 | 331 | 265 | **−33%** |
| Scope 2 (market-based) | 7,566 | 7,243 | 6,296 | −17% |
| Scope 2 (location-based) | 16,871 | 18,626 | 17,728 | +5% |
| Scope 3 — Purchased goods & services | 28,829 | 28,665 | 34,349 | +19% |
| Scope 3 — **Business travel** | 10,007 | 18,441 | **30,062** | **+200%** |
| Scope 3 — Capital goods | 10,507 | 10,135 | 8,295 | −21% |
| **Total (market-based)** | **66,971** | **74,060** | **88,639** | **+32%** |

**Read-through:** Scope 1+2 intensity is 2.58 tCO₂e per $M revenue — trivial. The story is threefold:
- **Absolute emissions are rising +32%** against a base year while revenue grew ~15% p.a. — decoupling has *not* occurred. Business travel tripling post-COVID is the single largest driver.
- **Scope 3 is 92.6% of the footprint**, and only "selected categories" are assured. That is precisely the gap California SB 253 Scope 3 reporting will expose.
- **The 65% "renewable" rate is REC-based** (market-based accounting) and sits alongside a location-based Scope 2 that is *flat-to-up*. Real grid draw has not fallen — an important greenwashing-adjacent nuance an anti-ESG campaign or activist could seize on.

**Material gap — no climate targets.** FY25 language is explicit: Nutanix "continue[s] to evaluate the feasibility of setting climate targets." **No SBTi commitment, no net-zero date, no interim reduction goal.** For a company whose datacenters are 80% of energy use, this is the weakest element of the E profile and increasingly anomalous versus enterprise-software peers. Mitigants: HQ consolidation (440k → 216k sq ft, −51%), 7 LEED-certified facilities, circular-economy hardware repurposing, ENERGY STAR/EPEAT/ROHS/REACH compliance.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **LOW-MEDIUM**

**Positives:** Strong stated culture infrastructure (Nutanix Voices listening programme, seven global employee-led L.I.F.E. groups incl. Women of Nutanix, Pride Alliance, Mi Gente, Project Victory for veterans), high-trust benefits, and a "Hungry/Humble/Honest/With Heart" values framework that is embedded in performance language rather than being purely decorative.

**Watch items:**
- **Restructuring:** A **5% global workforce reduction (~420 roles of 8,350)**, expected complete by end-October 2026, framed as realignment for AI and long-term growth. A second round following the ~270-role (4%) cut in 2022. Two reductions in four years raises execution and employer-brand risk, and attrition of quota-carrying sellers is the highest-cost outcome in enterprise software.
- **CEO transition:** Rajiv Ramaswami announced he is stepping down after six years, with an external/internal search underway; the stock fell sharply on the news and at least one bulge-bracket downgrade explicitly flagged **succession uncertainty**. Key-person and strategy-continuity risk (the Broadcom/VMware displacement window is time-limited).
- **Disclosure thinness:** Nutanix publishes **no quantified EEO-1-style pay-gap or workforce-representation tables** in its Impact Report, and its FY25 report has dropped the explicit "3 of 9 directors are diverse" headline used in 2023. Narrative-heavy S disclosure limits benchmarkability.
- **Positive:** Community and education giving, e-waste donation partnerships, and no consumer product-safety exposure.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **8 / 10**

**Structural strengths (genuinely above market for a 2016-vintage tech IPO):**
- **8 of 9 directors independent**; **independent Chair (Virginia Gambale) separated from the CEO role** — no duality.
- **Single class of common stock, one share one vote** — the dual-class structure was fully collapsed. This is a *real* shareholder-rights upgrade and rare among founder-led enterprise-software names.
- **Board fully declassified** (all directors on one-year terms), **majority voting** with irrevocable resignations, **no supermajority provisions**, **no poison pill**.
- All four committees (Audit, Compensation, Nom/Gov, **Security & Privacy**) fully independent.
- **Clawback policy** on incentive comp, stock ownership guidelines, annual auditor ratification, regular executive sessions, annual board/committee self-assessments.
- Recent refresh: Eric K. Brandt (May 2025), Sohaib Abbasi, Greg Lavender — a board that is actively turning over.

**Why not 9–10:**
1. **The 2021 software-misuse episode is the single largest governance stain.** An Audit Committee internal investigation found that "certain employees engaged in **intentional misconduct to conceal use of**" third-party software, forcing the company to miss its 10-Q filing deadline and recognise ~**$11M** of cost. Intentional concealment by employees is a **tone-at-the-middle controls failure**, not a technical error — it cuts directly at the reliability of the control environment. Remediation appears complete and the security class action (including the related "misused software / paid extra" claim) was **dismissed in November 2023 pursuant to settlement** (~$71M reported). Dated, but the base rate of repeat control events at software companies warrants a haircut.
2. **Compensation disclosure not verifiable** here (the DEF 14A primary document was not retrievable by our filing tool); I could not independently test CEO pay-versus-performance alignment or the weight of relative-TSR metrics. Scored on structure only.
3. **Board tenure/independence optics:** with the CEO stepping down and the Chair an independent director, the succession process will be the first live test of the governance framework's quality.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**ESG factors are not a valuation driver for NTNX in either direction.** The E and S exposures are financially immaterial at ~$12.6B market cap: 6,561 tCO₂e Scope 1+2 and no owned manufacturing mean no carbon-tax, stranded-asset, or remediation liability. There is **no plausible ESG-driven re-rating or de-rating path** here. The thesis will be decided by HCI share gains from Broadcom's VMware disruption, AI-era workload demand, and NRR — not by ESG.

**Where ESG does touch the investment case, in order of importance:**

1. **Product energy efficiency is a genuine competitive weapon, not a cost.** The ~50% energy-reduction / 66% footprint-reduction claim is an increasingly quantified line item in enterprise RFPs, and the carbon/power estimator tool plus X-ray/Prism power monitoring make it auditable. As customers face their own SB 253 and CSRD obligations, "consolidate onto Nutanix" becomes a *procurement* argument. This supports win rates and is underappreciated as an ESG-positive revenue angle.
2. **Cyber-resilience and NIS2/CRA readiness are a licensing gate.** The Security & Privacy Committee and existing privacy programmes are the right structure. A product-security failure would be an existential, not incremental, risk to a vendor running customers' core workloads. Monitor closely.
3. **Governance execution risk is the live issue — succession, not structure.** The framework is strong; the 2026 test is the CEO succession and whether the 5% RIF damages enterprise sales capacity. The 2021 "intentional misconduct to conceal" episode is the reason to demand audit-committee-focused diligence rather than to reject the name.
4. **Disclosure-quality catch-up is coming regardless of US federal politics.** CA SB 253/261 is the binding constraint, and Nutanix is underprepared on Scope 3 and on targets. Expect modest (~$1–3M) incremental assurance/consulting/systems spend and, more importantly, *narrative* exposure — this is a company that will be forced to publish a climate position it currently does not have, in a policy environment where both anti-ESG activists and EU customers will litigate the wording.

**Bottom line:** ESG does not constrain the position. Nothing in the E/S/G record argues for a position-size haircut. The only ESG-adjacent item I would escalate to a portfolio level is the **CEO succession process**, and that is a governance/strategy question rather than an ESG one.

Full Scope 3 category-level assurance status and third-party anti-corruption due-diligence programme details were likewise not verifiable from public sources.
