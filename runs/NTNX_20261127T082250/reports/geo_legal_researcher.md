# Nutanix, Inc. (NTNX) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Snapshot:** Software-Infrastructure; FY2026 (ended 7/31/26) revenue **$2.85B** (+12.4% y/y); ~8,350 employees; HQ San Jose, CA. Q1 FY2027 (10/31/26): revenue **$772.4M** (+15.2%, beat $762.9M consensus), adj. EPS $0.73 vs $0.61 est., but FY27 guidance of **$3.18–3.23B** came in below consensus and **CEO Rajiv Ramaswami announced he will exit by April 30, 2027** — stock fell **15.2% to $46.30** on 11/25/26.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① Middle East escalation inside the 29% EMEA segment — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium**
Nutanix's fastest-growing region is disproportionately Gulf-driven: direct offices (Manama, Bahrain); an MoU with **Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Environment, Water & Agriculture (MEWA)** signed at LEAP 2026; named partner for the **AWS KSA Region**; GITEX 2026 focus on "sovereign AI." Regional escalation (Iran/Israel, Hormuz/Red Sea) or Gulf fiscal retrenchment would hit the one segment carrying incremental growth. Sovereign/state-linked counterparties also carry elevated FCPA/UK Bribery Act exposure.

**② US–China tech decoupling, export controls & AI restrictions — Probability: Medium-Low | Impact: Medium**
The FY2026 10-K explicitly flags "**Changes in export controls, sanctions, and related regulations, including emerging restrictions targeting AI**," and references China among jurisdictions of operation. Nutanix maintains **Nutanix (Beijing) Co., Ltd.** and sells through China-headquartered OEMs (e.g., Lenovo) — a Section 889-type prohibition on Chinese hardware in U.S. federal networks would complicate the appliance channel. Its flagship AHV/agentic-AI push raises dual-use scrutiny.

**③ Tariffs and hardware refresh economics (core-count linkage) — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium**
Nutanix licenses on **physical CPU core count**, so revenue is mechanically tied to server shipments and refresh cycles. Tariffs or supply-chain disruption that raise server ASPs defer refresh cycles and slow core growth — BofA downgraded to Neutral citing exactly this linkage plus the CEO transition. This is a trade-policy transmission channel more than a direct tariff cost.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action (active).** Class period **Aug 27, 2025 – Nov 25, 2025** (*in re Nutanix*). Core allegation: after the 11/25/25 Q1 FY2026 print landed at the bottom of guidance, management blamed a late-quarter "revenue shift from Q1 to future periods" (flexible start dates, OEM/channel growth) and cut FY26 revenue guidance from **$2.90–2.94B to $2.82–2.86B**; stock fell **17.7% to $48.34** on 11/26/25. Related claims assert Nutanix "materially overstated its customer" metrics. Pomerantz has **re-opened the lead-plaintiff process**; Rosen, Schall, Bragar Eagel & Squire, Kaplan Fox and Bronstein Gewirtz are all soliciting. Prior 2019-era action was **dismissed 11/20/2023**. *Modest financial materiality; elevated because it overlaps the CEO transition.*
- **Tessell, Inc. et al v. Nutanix, Inc., Ramaswami & Tyler Wall** — N.D. Cal. No. **4:2026-cv-02306**, filed **3/17/2026**, nature of suit **libel & slander / defamation** (competitor in the DBaaS space). Small quantum but a conduct/commercial-disparagement signal.
- **Restructuring.** 8-K filed **8/4/2026**: ~**5% global workforce cut (~390 roles)**, **$33–43M pre-tax charges**, substantially complete by end-October 2026 — wrongful-termination/WARN and non-U.S. works-council exposure.
- **Antitrust backdrop — a two-sided regulatory risk.** The EU is escalating scrutiny of **Broadcom's VMware licensing changes** (information demands to European cloud providers; *Broadcom & VMware International v. Commission*, action lodged 5/6/2026). Nutanix's growth thesis rests substantially on VMware displacement — if Brussels forces Broadcom to soften licensing, the tailwind weakens on regulatory action rather than competitive merit.
- **Privacy/regulatory stack.** GDPR/UK GDPR, the **EU AI Act**, **NIS2**, **DORA** (for financial-services customers), Gulf and Indian data-localization rules, and **FedRAMP** for federal. Compliance obligations flow down contractually from regulated customers.
- **Sanctions: no disclosed material violations, OFAC designations, or DOJ/SEC enforcement actions found.** Russia/Ukraine exposure appears immaterial (no Russian entity in the current global office list).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Indirect channel model across ~100+ countries.** Growth is concentrated in EMEA (+21%) and Other Americas (+47%) — regions where third-party resellers/distributors are the highest FCPA/UKBA/AML risk vector. This is the single largest compliance exposure.
2. **Gulf sovereign counterparties** (Saudi MEWA MoU, sovereign-cloud programs) — state-official interaction and sanctions-screening discipline required.
3. **Beijing subsidiary + AI-capable portfolio** — export-control classification and deemed-export controls over support delivered to China-based customers.
4. **Data-sovereignty commitments** sold as a product feature create contractual liability if residency guarantees are breached (aggregate customer concentration: **$323.5M/quarter** of revenue tied to a disclosed concentration item in FY26 interim filings).
5. **Disclosure-control weakness implied** by the "revenue shift to future periods" restatement of guidance and the subsequent class action.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

Mitigants: 54% U.S. revenue, dollar-denominated software with 87% gross margin, no sanctions exposure, no manufacturing footprint. Aggravants: ~46% of revenue outside the U.S., Gulf-led growth, China and India operational concentration (India is the **primary R&D hub** — a geopolitical and talent-continuity single point of failure), a live securities class action overlapping a CEO vacuum (successor due by 4/30/27), and a regulatory-driven competitive threat in Brussels. **This sits at the high end of Medium.**
