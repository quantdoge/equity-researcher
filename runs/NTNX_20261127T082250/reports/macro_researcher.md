# Macroeconomic Assessment — Nutanix (NTNX)

**Data as of 27 November 2026. All macro figures from FRED unless noted.**

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **RISK-OFF / tightening-into-a-boom**

The US is in an unusual and hostile regime for long-duration software: **re-accelerating inflation, a Fed that has resumed hiking, and a bear-steepening curve — all while growth is accelerating.** This is not a growth scare; it is a discount-rate shock.

The sequence matters. The Fed cut to a 3.63% effective rate by May 2026 and held through August. Inflation then re-heated, and policymakers responded: **two 25bp hikes in September and October** took the effective rate to **3.88%**. The market expects a third hike at the **8–9 December FOMC**, with January live. The 2Y at 4.74% sits ~86bp above the effective rate — the forward curve is pricing a full additional tightening cycle, not a pause.

Inflation is the trigger. **Headline CPI is 3.82% y/y (Oct-26), but the momentum is far worse: +4.4% annualized over six months and +5.7% annualized over three.** The monthly prints confirm acceleration (+0.65% in September, +0.40% in August). Real GDP grew **4.3% annualized in Q3-26** (from 0.2% in Q4-25), and unemployment has fallen to **4.1%** from 4.5% a year ago. There is no slack forcing disinflation — hence the hikes.

Notably, **credit is not yet signalling recession**: HY OAS at 3.72% is ~106bp wider than its 21-Sep low of 2.66% but still historically very tight. Equities are de-rating on rates, not on default risk.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Negative — the discount rate (the dominant variable).** NTNX is a long-duration asset: ~$12.6B cap, $46.30/share, ~4x CY2028 sales after BofA's cut (from 6x CY2027). The empirical proof of rate sensitivity is brutal: **the stock fell from $74.63 (9-Oct) to $46.30 (25-Nov), −38%, in seven weeks — while the company beat on revenue (+15.2% y/y to $772.4M) and raised FY27 non-GAAP operating margin guidance to 24.5–25.5%.** Fundamentals improved; the multiple collapsed. Note the reported **beta of 0.638 badly understates the true rate sensitivity** — this is a valuation-duration problem, not a cyclical-beta problem.

**Negative — memory/"memflation" as an input-cost and demand tax.** DRAM rose **~172% y/y by Q3-2025** and Gartner reports memory prices **more than doubled in the past 12 months**, with high costs and lead times "through at least 2028." Nutanix's ~88% adjusted gross margin (software-heavy) shields its own P&L, but the transmission is via the *customer's* budget: richer node costs raise total deal size, defer appliance refreshes, and — per Nutanix's own research — **AI-driven hardware supply constraints are "denting CIO plans."** Expect bookings lumpiness and backlog drawdowns (already flagged in Q1) to persist into FY28.

**Negative — USD strength.** The broad dollar is up 4.7% since early September. With roughly 40% of revenue outside the US (EMEA and APJ), this is a translation and competitiveness headwind of the order of **1.5–2.0 points of revenue growth** if the move holds — enough to matter against a 15% growth algorithm.

**Negative/uncertain — tariffs.** Reported escalation of tariffs on China, India and others raises the landed cost of Asian-manufactured appliances sold through Dell, Lenovo, HPE and Cisco, functioning as a second, policy-driven price tax on the same hardware layer.

**Mixed — growth and IT budgets.** Gartner sees **worldwide IT spending +15% in 2027 to $7.35T**. But a large share of that is *price*, not units — memory inflation inflating the same budgets. For NTNX, nominal budget growth is real support, but the mix is skewed toward AI infrastructure and away from mature HCI/virtualization refresh — precisely Nutanix's core.

**Neutral/positive — balance sheet.** NTNX is net-cash with minimal debt; refinancing risk is negligible and ~3.9% front-end rates generate modest interest income. The rate damage is entirely to the *equity* discount rate, not to solvency.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Three of the four variables that matter most to NTNX — the 10Y discount rate, memory input costs, and the dollar — are all moving against it simultaneously, and the forward calendar (December and January hikes) is skewed negative. Strong GDP is not a sufficient offset because this company's share price is currently trading on the multiple, not on the growth. The tight HY spread (3.72%) is the one genuinely supportive data point and argues this is a de-rating rather than a demand collapse — which limits downside but does not create a catalyst.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario — **BULL, and it is not priced**

The consensus (and BofA's downgrade to Neutral, PT $62) assumes higher-for-longer. **The under-priced scenario is a fast disinflation in H1-2027 driven by the very forces now inflating prices.**

The Fed's own staff expects inflation "to step down sharply next year as energy prices decelerated," reaching ~2% by 2028, while policymakers remain "at odds over the causes of high inflation" (Reuters, 25-Nov). Much of the current impulse is a **supply shock in memory and energy, not excess demand** — and memory is the most mean-reverting input in technology once new DRAM capacity ramps. If November/December CPI rolls over and the Fed pauses in January rather than hiking, the 10Y falls from 5.13% toward 4.40%.

For a name at 4x CY2028 sales with 15% growth, 24–25% operating margins, raised FCF guidance and a net-cash balance sheet, a 70bp fall in the long end plus a memory-price relief rally in customer budgets supports a re-rating to **6x sales, or $65–75** — a 40–60% move. The bear case (10Y >5.50%, 3x sales, memory margins guide-down, CEO vacancy unresolved) points to the **low-to-mid $30s**, ~30% downside. The asymmetry is roughly symmetric on today's numbers, which is why the macro *level* of rates — not NTNX's execution — is the variable to trade. **A December hike is largely priced; a January pause is not.**
