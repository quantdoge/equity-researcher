## 1. Consumer Demand Signals

**Google Trends — "Google" (US, 12m): NEUTRAL, fading vs. year-ago**
Weekly interest has recovered from its summer trough (69 in late July) to 86–95 in September and 86–87 in mid-October. But the YoY comparison is unfavourable: the same weeks a year ago (Oct–Nov 2025) printed 94–100. Current levels run roughly **8–12% below prior-year**, i.e. flat-to-soft underlying brand-search demand. Notably, the Sept 2026 spike (95) coincided with product/IO announcements — attention is event-driven, not organic.

**Google Trends — "Gemini AI" (US, 12m): POSITIVE, genuine recovery**
89 in mid-October vs. 76–84 a year earlier — up ~8% YoY. More importantly, the series bottomed at 61–64 in July–August 2026 (a real demand scare) and has since recovered ~40% to 82–89. This is the single best consumer-side datapoint: Gemini interest is re-accelerating, and the recovery leads into the Q4 print rather than lagging it.

**Google Trends — "Waymo" (US, 12m): POSITIVE but immaterial**
83 now vs. 61–67 a year ago, with a rising trend since August. Expansion into overseas markets is visible in search attention. But Other Bets is 0.3% of revenue ($372M of $127.6B) against >$2B quarterly operating losses — real momentum, no P&L relevance.

**Google Trends — "Google Cloud" (US, 12m): SEVERELY NEGATIVE, and I do not believe it**
This series **collapsed from 100 (June-2026) to 23–24 (Sept–Oct)** — down ~77%. I flag this as the most striking chart in the dataset and also the most likely artefact: Cloud revenue *accelerated* to +103% YoY in Q3 over the same window. Either (a) the June spike was a one-off event (I/O, an outage, an earnings news cycle) inflating the base, or (b) enterprise buyer intent genuinely evaporated, which would conflict with a $514B+ backlog. **I weight this signal low** — but if you want a reason to be nervous, this is where a bear would point.

**Web traffic (SimilarWeb proxy): NEGATIVE competitive read**
Per SimilarWeb, **chatgpt.com had more total visits than gemini.google.com in September 2026** on a 3-month basis. Low confidence — this came via search-result snippets, not the API, and no absolute visit numbers were returned. Treat as directional colour, not evidence.

## 3. Earnings Preview Signal: **TRACKING BELOW consensus** (near-term)

The Q3 print settles the debate on quality:

| Metric | Q3'26 actual | Consensus | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $127.58B | $127.97B | **Miss** (–0.3%) |
| EPS | $2.67 | $3.01 | **Miss** (–11.3%) |
| Search & other | $63.4B, **+12%** | — | Decel from +17% in Q2 |
| Google Cloud | ~$28.9B, **+103%** | — | Massive beat |
| Other Bets op. loss | >$2.0B | — | Wider |

**Two things are true simultaneously.** The EPS miss is *not* operational — it was driven by an **~$11B paper loss on the SpaceX stake**, which cut net income 25% YoY to $26.17B. Strip that out and operating growth was genuinely strong, with a 13th consecutive quarter of double-digit revenue growth and a half-trillion-dollar annualised run-rate.

**But the Search number is the tell, and alt data explains why.** Search revenue decelerated to +12% even as **query growth hit a five-year high**. That is the single most important intermediate finding in this report: *attention is at record levels while monetisation per query is falling.* AI Overviews are converting fewer queries into clicks and dollars. That is a structural margin-and-growth headwind, not a one-quarter wobble — and it directly contradicts the "AI is not hurting search" narrative.

**Q4'26 consensus is aggressive:** $142.7B revenue (+11.9% QoQ, requiring a very strong seasonal ramp on top of a decelerating Search) and $3.36 EPS. Against a decelerating core, an $84.75B share count increase, and $91–93B of capex, **I expect negative estimate revision risk into February.** The 86% eight-quarter beat rate (avg +53%) is now broken, and the two prior "beats" (+217%, +91%) were almost entirely non-operating equity marks — the underlying beat quality was always weaker than the headline.
