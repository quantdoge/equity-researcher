### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — EU DMA enforcement escalation and the transatlantic retaliation loop (Probability: High / Impact: High)**
- On **23 Jul 2026** the Commission fined Google **€890M** under the DMA (€460M for self-preferencing in Search, €430M for Google Play anti-steering), bringing cumulative EU antitrust penalties to **~€10.38B** (European Commission DMA portal; NYT, Al Jazeera, The Verge).
- On **2 Jul 2026** the EU's top court **upheld the ~€4.1B Android fine** — appeals avenue exhausted (CNBC).
- Google is now reportedly **degrading European search results** to avoid further DMA liability — management itself warns of "lower quality," a tacit admission of product compromise in its second-largest region (Reuters, 8 Sep 2026).
- **Second-order geopolitical channel:** the July 2026 fine triggered **US threats of additional tariffs on the EU**, a **US trade probe into EU big-tech enforcement**, and Commerce Secretary criticism — with US lawmakers explicitly lobbying for retaliation (FT, Euronews, Reuters). Google is now a *pawn in a tariff negotiation*: US pressure may moderate future EU fines (upside), but any tariff escalation raises device/hardware costs and macro demand risk (downside).
- Headline ceiling: DMA fines can reach **10% of global turnover (~$40B+)** — The Verge. Tail risk is asymmetric.

**Risk 2 — US antitrust remedy hardening on appeal (Probability: Medium / Impact: High)**
- *Search case:* Judge Mehta's **2 Sep 2025** remedies order (230 pages) rejected Chrome divestiture but barred exclusive default-distribution deals and imposed a 5-member Technical Committee; DOJ has **appealed to the DC Circuit seeking tougher remedies** (Courthouse News; CRS LSB11362).
- *Ad tech case:* Judge Brinkema's **Sep-2026** remedies opinion **rejected the AdX divestiture** but imposed behavioral remedies — auction-rule relaxation, Prebid interoperability, bid-data sharing, and an **internal antitrust compliance officer/monitor** (Reuters 16-Sep-2026; AdExchanger; Progress Chamber).
- Net: the *breakup* tail has receded materially (a genuine de-risking versus 2024-25 consensus), but **distribution economics remain in flux** and both cases sit with appellate courts. The compliance-officer regime also creates a new source of recurring friction and disclosure.

**Risk 3 — US–China tech fragmentation (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium-High)**
- China's **SAMR opened an Android antitrust probe in Feb-2025** as explicit retaliation for US tariffs; it has reportedly been **dropped during US–China trade talks** — meaning it can be *reopened at any time as a bargaining chip* (FT via BRICS Competition; The Hill).
- US advanced-chip **export controls** cut both ways for Google: they constrain TPU/cloud addressable markets abroad but advantage Google's in-house silicon; China is reportedly considering **tightening its own export controls on AI models and chips** (Reuters, 21-Jul-2026).
- APAC (16.8% of revenue) carries concentrated hardware supply-chain and Taiwan-strait exposure not visible in the revenue line.

**Additional watch item — AI product-execution litigation:** *Stewart v. Alphabet*, N.D. Cal. No. 3:26-cv-11290 (Judge Pitts), class period 19-May-2026 to 16-Jul-2026, alleges Alphabet misrepresented **Gemini 3.5 Pro** training results and concealed a launch delay; Bloomberg's 16-Jul-2026 report of a delay "months behind schedule" drove a >4% share drop. Lead-plaintiff deadline 1-Dec-2026 (KTMC; Rosen; BFA). Modest direct damages, but it converts AI-roadmap slippage into a recurring securities-litigation vector.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Repeat-offender DMA status** — the €890M order is the first *non-compliance* (not merely abuse-of-dominance) DMA fine against Google. Escalating penalty tiers up to 10% of global turnover, plus possible periodic penalty payments, make this the highest-severity compliance flag.
2. **Court-appointed monitors** — a DOJ-selected Technical Committee (search case) and an internal antitrust compliance officer (ad tech) create dual external oversight of core product decisions. Any breach becomes a contempt/enforcement event, not just a fine.
3. **Privacy/GDPR** — €403M Irish DPC penalty confirms data-processing controls remain a live weakness; ad-tech and AI-training data flows are the next likely enforcement frontier.
4. **Export controls / sanctions** — no material OFAC or FCPA exposure identified, but TPU/advanced-AI distribution and cloud access by restricted entities is a plausible future BIS/OFAC issue given the chip-control regime. No adverse finding to date; flag as *watch item*, not a current breach.
5. **Product-degradation disclosure risk** — publicly stating EU search results are being made worse to satisfy a regulator is an unusual, litigation-attractive disclosure.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Roughly **52% of revenue is earned outside the US**, with the highest-risk region (EMEA, 29%) policed by a regulator with a decade-long adversarial record, a fresh €890M DMA non-compliance finding, a final €4.1B judgment, and a statutory fine ceiling above $40B. Two US monopolisation findings remain under appeal, and Google now operates under court-appointed monitors in its home market. Simultaneously, Google has become a **direct instrument of US–EU trade brinkmanship** and a latent **US–China retaliation target**, adding sovereign-political optionality to its risk profile that is not captured by historical fine sizes.

**Offsetting factors** (why not Very High): the two worst structural outcomes — a Chrome/Android breakup and an AdX divestiture — were **both rejected by courts in 2025–26**; the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations were **deferred** under the Digital Omnibus; and China's SAMR probe was **shelved**. These are meaningful de-risking events that materially shrink the tail.

**What would change the rating:** (a) DC Circuit ordering structural remedies or reopening the Chrome divestiture → Very High; (b) a DMA periodic penalty payment or forced product separation in Europe; (c) reopening of the SAMR probe alongside a tariff escalation; (d) US tariffs on the EU implemented in a way that raises device costs or invites EU counter-measures against US digital services.
