## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **HIGH**

- Emissions are **+51% vs 2019 baseline** (2024 report); the 2026 report shows **"ambition-based" emissions +18% in 2025** and electricity consumption **>250% above 2019** (Bloomberg/Google 2026 Environmental Report).
- Positives: data-centre emissions **−12% in 2024**, **66% carbon-free energy**, ~**8 GW** new clean PPAs signed, **2.5 GW** brought online, 100% plastic-free hardware packaging, 84% data-centre waste diverted.
- **Red flag:** Alphabet reclassified its 2030 net-zero goal from a target to an **"ambition-based moonshot"** — a softening of commitment that raises greenwashing/credibility risk and weakens credibility of climate-linked bond metrics.
- Scope 3 **+22%** (supply chain, hardware) offsets Scope 1/2 gains. AI capex structurally conflicts with decarbonisation → rising energy cost line and reputational exposure.

**Verdict:** Rising trajectory, credible disclosure but deteriorating absolute metrics. High.

---

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM-HIGH**

- **Privacy:** **$1.375B Texas AG settlement (May 2025)** — largest state-level privacy settlement in US history (location/biometric data). Signals an ongoing regulatory tap on the ad-data model.
- **AI/copyright:** Publishers and authors filed **class action (July 2026)** alleging Gemini was trained on millions of copyrighted works "behind paywalls." Follows the Anthropic $1.5B benchmark settlement — establishes a plausible damages template.
- **Labour:** NLRB ruled Google a **joint employer** of Accenture/Cognizant contract workers and ordered it to bargain with the Alphabet Workers Union; Google appealing. Contractor team cut after strike; union alleges **WARN notice violation** on Mountain View layoffs (2025). Historically subject to discrimination litigation and the 2018 walkout legacy.
- **Product safety/AI:** Board-level AI-oversight shareholder proposal filed citing AI lawsuits (e.g., a ~$68M settlement referenced in the 2026 proxy).

**Verdict:** Strong workforce brand but persistent contractor-labour friction and escalating privacy/AI liability. Medium-High.

---

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **5 / 10**

**Strengths:** CEO/Chair roles **separated** (Pichai CEO; independent Chair); majority-independent board; strong audit committee; high disclosure (annual Sustainability Report, TCFD-style); transparent proxy.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Dual-class structure:** Founders control via Class B supervoting (10:1). A **majority of independent shareholders voted to eliminate dual-class / adopt one-share-one-vote in the 2026 proxy — the company ignored it.** Structural minority-shareholder disenfranchisement.
- **AI Board Oversight proposal overwhelmingly rejected** (461M for vs 11.9B against) despite active AI litigation — governance lagging the risk.
- **Compensation:** Large CEO equity awards tied heavily to TSR; limited ESG-linked metrics; founder control insulates management from accountability.
- **Antitrust conduct** (below) is itself a governance failure — court found illegal monopoly maintenance via exclusive default payments.

**Score 5/10:** Disclosure is best-in-class, but control structure and shareholder-rights record cap the score.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **EU:** DMA gatekeeper obligations & non-compliance investigations (fines up to 10–20% global turnover); **EU AI Act** GPAI obligations (in force Aug 2025); **CSRD** — in scope (large EU presence) → mandatory assurance of emissions data; EU deforestation/microplastic rules on hardware.
- **US:** SEC climate-disclosure rule (stayed/litigated — limited near-term); FTC/state AG privacy enforcement (Texas, others); **DOJ antitrust appeal**.
- **UK:** CMA / DMCC digital-markets regime; UK-Google ad-tech commitments.
- **CBAM:** Indirect — hardware/component supply chains (low-moderate).
- **ESG-linked debt:** Minimal — Alphabet has negligible ESG-linked bond exposure (investment-grade balance sheet, not covenant-driven).

---

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Best-in-class **disclosure and decarbonisation effort** (66% CFE, 8 GW PPAs) are offset by **deteriorating absolute emissions from AI**, **softened 2030 targets**, **unresolved dual-class governance**, and — decisively — **the most severe antitrust/competition exposure in mega-cap tech.** This is a "high-quality disclosure, high-controversy, governance-constrained" profile: not a Laggard (transparency is genuinely strong), not a Leader (structural G and rising E issues block that).

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL (mildly negative tilt at the margin)**

- **E:** Rising emissions are a **cost and reputational headwind**, not yet a valuation driver — grid/PPA costs are manageable vs. AI revenue upside. Watch target credibility.
- **S:** Privacy and AI-copyright liabilities are **real but largely one-off/litigable**; Texas settlement shows willingness to pay and move on.
- **G:** **The binding constraint.** Antitrust remedies (default-deal ban, data-sharing, ad-tech conduct) directly damage the advertising moat; the DOJ appeal keeps structural risk alive. Dual-class control means shareholders cannot force change. This is where ESG risk most overlaps with the investment thesis.

**Bottom line for the memo:** ESG factors do not create a *standalone* negative thesis — Alphabet's disclosure and financial capacity to absorb fines are strong, and the breakup worst-case was avoided. But the **conjunction of (a) softening climate targets amid AI-driven emissions growth and (b) governance insulation protecting a business model a court found to be an illegal monopoly** means ESG risk is **directionally negative and concentrated in G/antitrust.** Treat antitrust remedies and any DOJ appeal escalation as the key ESG-linked event risk to monitor; treat data-centre energy costs as a secondary margin variable.
