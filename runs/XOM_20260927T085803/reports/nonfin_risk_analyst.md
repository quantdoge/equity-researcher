## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate** (near-Strong, evidence truncated by data gaps)

**Positive signals:**
- **Independent board chair.** The 2026 proxy statement references "our independent Board Chair" engaging with shareholders — indicating CEO/Chair roles are split, removing the classic concentration-of-power red flag for a company of this size.
- **Unanimous board recommendation** on the redomestication vote (New Jersey → Texas, definitive proxy filed May 12, 2026) — cohesive board alignment on strategy.
- Scale and tenure of leadership (Woods, CEO since 2017) suggest stable, institutionalized management rather than founder-dominated key-person risk.

**Watch items:**
- **Redomestication friction.** The move to Texas was opposed by proxy advisors Glass Lewis and ISS; Exxon publicly attacked their analysis (partly over litigation on Texas's proxy-advisor law). The move is defensible (Texas is Exxon's operational home), but it also shifts the litigation forum away from NJ shareholder-friendly precedent — an indirect erosion of shareholder remedies. Disclosed "definitive additional proxy materials" suggest a contested, high-attention vote.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary
No active SEC enforcement actions or DOJ investigations surfaced via EDGAR search. However, three material regulatory themes are live:

- **PENDING — Diesel/renewable export restrictions (HIGH materiality).** Diesel prices hit all-time highs (≈$6.31/gal, +83% YTD 2026). President Trump has voiced support and Senate Majority Leader Thune has floated a **temporary diesel export ban** — an intervention never before applied to refined products. Exxon operates one of the largest US refining networks; a binding export ban would directly compress margins on ~1M+ bpd of US refining capacity and distort Gulf Coast product markets. Status: proposed, not enacted — but this is the single most financially consequential non-financial policy item on the board's radar.
- **Venezuela re-entry (sanctions/OFAC exposure).** Exxon is nearing a return to Venezuela — the country that **seized its assets twice** and forced it out in 2007 — following claims of a "65-billion-barrel" US-Venezuela agreement. Re-entry requires careful OFAC licensing posture; past asset seizures raise title, expropriation, and corruption-transaction risks, plus renewed human-rights scrutiny of revenue flows to the Maduro regime.
- **Climate litigation legacy (chronic tail risk).** Historical exposure includes the Massachusetts AG "ExxonKnew" document fight and investor/deceptive-practices class actions; the SEC's related investigation was dropped — favorable but the tort category remains an open-ended, multi-jurisdiction tail risk for the sector's largest integrated player.
- **Redomestication vote** pending shareholder resolution (NJ → TX) — a governance-legal forum shift (see above).

## 4. Top Operational Risks
- **Weather/physical-asset resilience — LIVE EVENT.** Floodwater overwhelmed a pump and knocked the **275,000 bpd Joliet, IL refinery offline** (Sept 17, 2026) — described as an 11M-gal/day facility "going dark" at the worst possible moment, stressing Midwest gasoline/diesel supply during record pump prices. Single-flood-event asset loss of this scale points to a demonstrable climate-resilience gap in flood-prone refining assets; restart timeline is the immediate watch item.
- **Geopolitical/supply-chain concentration (Hormuz).** Crude breached $100 on tanker-attack escalation; the Strait of Hormuz is described as "closer to closed than open." With substantial international upstream and a new Venezuela entry pending, Exxon carries concentrated exposure to transit-chokepoint and sanctions-adjacent disruption.
- **Key-person & execution concentration.** Long-tenured CEO Woods concentrates succession risk (no visible bench detail), while delivery of the 2030 growth agenda — doubling LNG to ~50 Mt, CCS scaling toward 100 Mtpa storage — is a multi-year execution program with regulatory-permitting dependencies (notably LNG export permits in a politically charged trade environment).

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment
**Elevated but manageable.** Headwinds:
- **Venezuela deal** will attract governance/human-rights condemnation regardless of OFAC posture — a predictable, repeated-activist-event.
- **Joliet outage + record diesel prices** create a "Big Oil price gouging" narrative window, with politicians already reaching for export bans — reputational and policy risk are compounding, not independent.
- **Legacy #ExxonKnew / climate-tort** brand damage remains the sector's standard ESG controversy load.

Counterweights: dividend-aristocrat standing, trusted-scale positioning, favorable SEC outcome on the climate probe, and market sentiment that is currently constructive (analyst consensus "Hold/Buy," ~$167 avg target). Net: moderate negative press pressure, no consumer-brand product recall exposure.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **Moderate** (elevated toward High if the diesel-export ban or Venezuela deal matures)
Rationale: no active enforcement, no fraud indicators, adequate governance with an independent chair, and no accounting irregularities. But three converging, non-financial vectors — **lumpy operational weather risk (Joliet), pending punitive refined-product policy (diesel ban), and sanctions-sensitive Venezuela re-entry** — sit above the oil-major baseline and justify active monitoring rather than comfort.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation
1. **Venezuela re-entry without visible OFAC license posture** — proceed only with confirmed licensing/legal-title structure; escalation if deal closes without public compliance disclosures.
2. **Diesel export ban progression** — tie legislative tracking (Thune/Trump statements) to refining-margin sensitivity; treat as High-risk scenario if a bill advances.
3. **Joliet restart timeline & flood-resilience review** — require management assurance that no other flood-exposed sites (Gulf Coast, Joliet secondary systems) share the same single-point-pump failure class; extended outage → Midwest fuel stress → policy backlash loop.
