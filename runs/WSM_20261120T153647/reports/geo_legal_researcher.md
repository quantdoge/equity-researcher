# WSM — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Williams-Sonoma, Inc. | Consumer Cyclical / Specialty Retail | US-domiciled, CA-incorporated | ~$25.8B mkt cap*

**Data note:** SEC.gov direct fetches returned 403 and the 10-K risk-factor excerpt was unavailable; `fetch_filing_text` for the 8/28/26 10-Q returned only an exhibit. Geographic percentages below are **estimates** triangulated from store counts, filing footnotes and press releases, and are labeled as such.

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

Company-owned footprint: 502 US (41 states + DC + Puerto Rico), 20 Canada, 19 Australia, 3 UK, plus 139 franchised stores and international e-commerce (Middle East, Philippines, Mexico, South Korea, India).

**Bottom line on geography:** revenue diversification is minimal (~13–15% non-US), but the *sourcing* geography is the real exposure — the company imports the overwhelming majority of goods, so trade policy transmits to gross margin far more powerfully than any single foreign market does.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US tariff regime volatility — Section 232 / 301 / 122 stacking (Probability: ~100% ongoing | Impact: High)**
- Q2 FY26 non-GAAP gross margin fell **160bps**, driven by **-230bps of merchandise margin "primarily driven by tariff costs"** — only partly offset by supply-chain efficiencies and occupancy leverage (Q2 FY26 release, 8/26/26).
- FY26 guidance explicitly assumes Section 232 tariffs, existing Section 301, **new Section 301 tariffs announced July 23, 2026** (USTR: 10–12.5% on 60 economies, including Canada, India, Vietnam-adjacent Asia), and Canada–US tariffs all remain in place.
- Q2 FY26 transcript cites "additional 30% China tariffs, 50% India tariff, 20% Vietnam tariff, an average 18% tariff on the rest of the world."
- **Partial offset / one-time windfall:** after the Supreme Court struck down IEEPA tariffs (Feb 20, 2026), WSM booked **$167.8M COGS reduction + $6.3M interest**, claimed $197.8M, and collected **$200.2M** — but had to provision **$47.5M** to reimburse vendors for prior tariff concessions, plus $10M in employee 401(k) costs. New Section 122 authority (Feb 24–Jul 24, 2026) and Section 301 replacement tariffs mean the relief is **not** a durable margin recovery. Guidance assumes **no** further refund benefit → potential upside optionality, but also refund-clawback/audit risk.

**② Canada–US trade war (Probability: High | Impact: Low–Medium, ~3% of revenue)**
US–Canada relations have deteriorated materially through 2026: Canada imposed ~C$20B in retaliatory tariffs, and new US Section 301 duties of 10% hit Canadian goods from July 23, 2026; additional 50% US tariffs on select Canadian products took effect Sept 15, 2026. WSM has 20 Canadian stores and CAD-denominated revenue — direct margin/FX drag is small, but brand-level boycott risk for a US "lifestyle" retailer in Canada is real and hard to hedge.

**③ Sourcing concentration + shipping/logistics shock (Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium–High)**
China ~25% of sourcing (down from ~50%), remainder spread across Vietnam, India, Europe and elsewhere in Asia. Guidance assumes "oil prices will remain elevated," and Q3 FY26 flagged "shipping delays and higher oil prices" as holiday-season challenges. Exposure to South China Sea/Red Sea/Panama disruption and to any *new* Vietnam or India action translates ~1:1 into gross margin. Mitigant: WSM's **direct-sourcing model** (a stated design advantage) gives it more pricing and vendor-concession leverage than wholesale-dependent peers, and it has historically used tariffs as a share-gain opportunity.

**FCPA / sanctions / AML:** No meaningful direct exposure. US-centric operations, no disclosed DOJ/SEC/OFAC inquiries. The franchised Middle East and Asia partners are the only theoretical third-party-intermediary channel, and franchise royalties are immaterial (~1–2% of revenue).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags
1. **Repeat FTC order violator** on Made-in-USA labeling — the single clearest enforcement tail risk (record penalty already imposed once; per-violation statutory penalties are meaningful even at WSM's scale).
2. **Tariff refund accounting** — $197.8M claimed / $200.2M collected, $29.3M deferred into inventory, $47.5M vendor provision. Aggressive-but-disclosed non-GAAP treatment invites SEC comment and audit scrutiny; guidance excludes all refund benefit, which mitigates but does not eliminate restatement-style risk.
3. **Vendor tariff-concession reimbursement** — arrangements with merchandise vendors to share tariff economics create transfer-pricing/customs-valuation questions (first-sale vs. transaction value) worth watching.
4. **Employment-departure pattern** at brand-president level (Bhargava) with plaintiff-counsel payment.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

Rationale: near-total revenue concentration in the US is a *policy* exposure rather than a sovereign one — no expropriation, currency-control, or sanctions risk anywhere in the footprint, and Canada/Australia/UK are high-rule-of-law jurisdictions. The rating is pulled to Medium, not Low, by (a) an import-dependent cost structure exposed to a fast-moving, legally unsettled US tariff architecture (IEEPA struck down, Section 122 bridge, new Section 301 in force), and (b) a genuine repeat-offender FTC compliance record with a record penalty already on the books. Legal contingencies in aggregate are small relative to a ~$25.8B market cap and ~$9B+ revenue base.

**What would change the rating:** adverse — a second FTC Made-in-USA penalty, a Canada retaliatory tariff covering US consumer goods, or new Section 301 rates on Vietnam/India/Mexico; favorable — legislative tariff codification that stabilizes the rate schedule.
