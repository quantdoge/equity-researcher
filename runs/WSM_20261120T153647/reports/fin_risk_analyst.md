### 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: INVESTMENT GRADE (effectively AAA-equivalent balance sheet)**

The single most important finding: **WSM carries no funded debt.** The $1,456.9M "total debt" on the balance sheet has **zero** interest expense attached — long-term and short-term *financial* debt are both $0. The entire amount is lease liabilities (operating + finance). FY2025 income statement shows interest expense of $0 (vs. $1.9M in FY2021). Interest coverage is therefore **not meaningful** — there is no coupon to cover, no maturity wall, and no refinancing cliff. Any credit-risk model that reads "Debt/Equity 0.70x" as leverage is misreading this company.

Net debt of $437.1M against EBITDA of $1,684.0M gives **net debt/EBITDA of 0.26x** — roughly two years of "net leverage" equalling 90 days of EBITDA. This is fortress-grade. Altman Z-Score of 3.26 sits comfortably in the Safe zone, and that figure is *understated*: the tool's x4 uses book equity ($2.08B) rather than market cap. Substituting market cap ($25.8B / $3.33B liabilities = 7.75x) lifts the Z-Score to **~7.5** — deep in the safe zone.

The **Piotroski F-Score of 4/9 ("Weak")** is the apparent contradiction and requires interpretation. The failures are F3 (ROA softened 21.2% → 20.1%), F5 (leverage rose), F6 (book equity declined), F8 (gross margin edged down 31bps), F9 (asset turnover flat). Four of those five are **artifacts of shareholder returns, not distress**: WSM repurchased $854M of stock and paid $316M of dividends in FY2025, shrinking share count from 138.2M (FY2021) to 123.2M — a 10.8% reduction — which mechanically depresses book equity, raises D/E, and blocks F6. The F-Score is penalizing WSM for *returning too much capital*, not for deteriorating solvency. Directionally it is nonetheless a genuine signal that returns on capital and margins have plateaued.

**Verdict:** Bankruptcy risk is effectively negligible. WSM could fund operations entirely from its own balance sheet.

### 4. Downside Scenario Analysis: Revenue -20%

**Base:** FY2025 Revenue $7,806.8M, Gross Margin 46.15%, SG&A $2,187.3M (28.0% of sales), EBIT $1,415.7M, D&A $231.4M, EBITDA $1,684.0M.

Revenue falls to **$6,245.4M** (roughly a return to 2019-2020 levels). *Assumptions below are mine; lease expense is not broken out in the feed.*

| Scenario | Gross Margin | SG&A | EBIT | EBITDA | Net Debt/EBITDA | FCF | Dividend ($316M) |

| **Severe** (300bps GM compression, SG&A rigid) | 44.0% | $2,187.3M | $560.7M | $792.1M | **0.55x** | ~$450M | Covered 1.4x |
| **Combined shock** (+ $200M inventory write-down) | 41.4% | $2,187.3M | $398.4M | $629.8M | **0.69x** | ~$280M | **Barely covered** |

**Even in the combined shock, net leverage stays below 0.7x — a level most retailers would call underlevered in a normal year.** There is no covenant, no maturity wall, and no interest burden. The only casualty in the severe case is the **capital-return program**: at 111% of FCF already, buybacks would halt immediately and the dividend would come under review in the combined-shock case. WSM would emerge with cash still above $700M.

**Cash runway:** with $1,020M cash, no debt maturities, and a still-positive FCF in every scenario, the runway is effectively **indefinite**. The company could absorb a 20% revenue decline for 3+ years without external financing.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **LOW**

Weighting the four pillars: credit risk (negligible — no funded debt, 0.26x net leverage, Z-Score 3.26 safe) and liquidity risk (negligible — positive WC, $1.0B cash, no maturities) dominate. Market risk is elevated (43% vol, 1.30 beta) but is a *shareholder*-level risk, not a solvency risk. The mild negatives — FCF margin erosion from 19.2% → 13.5%, inventory growth outpacing sales, cash declining 19% over two years, and shareholder returns exceeding FCF — are **capital-allocation and quality-of-earnings concerns, not survival concerns.**

**Overall: LOW financial risk.** The company's ability to survive a prolonged downturn is not in question; the question is whether the equity is priced for the margin/FCF plateau.

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Shareholder returns exceed free cash flow (111% payout).** $1,170M returned vs. $1,056M FCF in FY2025 — a $115M shortfall funded by drawing cash from $1,213M to $1,020M. Not dangerous with this balance sheet, but **unsustainable** if FCF keeps sliding; the buyback is the marginal buyer of the stock.
2. **FCF deterioration.** FCF down 29% from the FY2023 peak ($1,491.8M → $1,055.5M); FCF margin down 570bps. Operating cash flow fell 22% in two years while capex rose 38%.
3. **Inventory/sales divergence.** Inventory +9.8% vs. revenue +1.2%; inventory days +9.2 days to 127.0. Markdown and obsolescence risk if demand weakens — likely tariff-related pre-buying, which is a *timing* benefit at best.
4. **Gross margin has peaked and reversed.** 46.15% (FY2025) vs. 46.46% (FY2024); the prior-year expansion from 42.6% is unlikely to repeat, removing a major source of the earnings beat.
5. **Piotroski F-Score of 4/9.** Largely buyback-distorted, but it does confirm the direction of ROA, leverage, and margin trends is negative.
6. **High market risk.** 43.4% volatility, 1.30 beta, -36.8% max drawdown in 3 years. A 20% market drawdown implies ~26% downside for WSM.
