## WILLIAMS-SONOMA, INC. (WSM) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price:** $218.42 (Nov 20, 2026) | **Market cap:** ~$26.0B (118.9M diluted sh.) | **EV:** ~$26.4B | **FY ends:** late Jan/Feb

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — **Moat: NARROW**

Williams-Sonoma runs a portfolio of design-led, vertically integrated home brands (Williams Sonoma, Pottery Barn, Pottery Barn Kids/Teen, West Elm, Rejuvenation, Mark & Graham) through ~544 company-owned stores, 139 franchised units, and e-commerce at roughly two-thirds of revenue — the highest direct-to-consumer mix in home furnishings. It designs and sources its own product, which is the source of both its differentiation and its problem: it owns the inventory and the style risk.

**Evidence for a moat:**
- **Returns on capital are exceptional and persistent.** ROIC of ~40%, ROE ~52%, with capex a modest ~3.3% of revenue. Capital intensity this low with margins this high is the fingerprint of an intangible-asset (brand + design) moat, not a scale moat.
- **Gross margin expanded ~730bp over five years** (38.9% FY2020 → 46.2% FY2025) on essentially flat revenue. That is pricing/mix/sourcing power, not a cyclical accident — though part of it reflects an abnormally promotion-free environment that will not last forever.
- **Switching costs and network effects: essentially none.** Home décor is a discretionary, style-driven, replaceable purchase. Amazon, Wayfair, RH and a thousand DTC brands compete for the same wallet and often use the same factories.

**Verdict:** A genuinely good business with real brand equity and best-in-class returns on capital, but the moat is **Narrow**. Revenue has been flat-to-down for four years (5-yr CAGR 2.85%, and that includes the COVID pull-forward); FY2023 revenue fell 10.7% and has not recovered. This is a high-quality cyclical, not a compounder that grows through cycles.

### 2. Financial Quality Score — **8/10**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 8.67 | 7.75 | 7.71 | 7.81 | 8.11 |
| Gross margin | 42.4% | 42.6% | 46.5% | 46.2% | ~48% |
| Operating margin | 17.3% | 16.1% | 18.6% | 18.1% | 19.7%* |
| Net income ($M) | 1,128 | 950 | 1,125 | 1,088 | 1,221 |
| FCF ($M) | 699 | 1,492 | 1,139 | 1,055 | ~1.15B |
| Diluted EPS | 8.16 | 7.28 | 8.79 | 8.84 | 10.19 |

*TTM margins are flattered by one-time tariff-related gross-margin benefits (Q2 FY26 gross margin of 51.6% was explicitly driven by tariff items, and Q3's GAAP $2.38 included non-recurring gains vs. $2.19 adjusted). **Do not capitalize the TTM number.**

**Strengths:**
- **FCF conversion ~97% of net income** (FY2025: $1.055B FCF / $1.088B NI). Owner earnings $1.06B — essentially identical to reported FCF, because D&A ($231M) ≈ capex ($259M). This is one of the cleanest earnings-to-cash bridges in retail.
- **Fortress balance sheet:** $1.02B cash against $1.46B of "debt" that is entirely lease liabilities. Ex-leases the company is in a net cash position. No financial leverage.
- **Inventory and payables fund the business** — working capital is a modest drag, not a funding need.

**Weaknesses:** Zero revenue growth per share is doing the heavy lifting for earnings growth; margin gains are the entire story, and the FY2026 guide (revenue +4.7–7.2%, non-GAAP operating margin held at ~17.5–18.5%) implies **margin expansion is over**. Earnings growth from here is ~5–7% revenue plus buyback.

### 3. Valuation Analysis — **~30% above intrinsic value**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| P/E on FY2026E non-GAAP EPS (~$9.50) | **23.0x** |
| P/E on TTM GAAP EPS ($10.19) | 21.4x |
| EV/EBITDA (normalized) | ~15.5x |
| FCF yield (normalized $1.05B) | **4.0%** |
| Dividend yield | 1.4% ($3.04 annualized; ~32% payout) |

**My DCF (normalized owner earnings $1.0B, net leases deducted, 118.9M shares):**

| Scenario | FCF growth | WACC | Terminal | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | 2.5% | 10.0% | 2.0% | **~$106** |
| **Base** | **5.5%** | **9.0%** | **2.5%** | **~$159** |
| Bull | 8.0% | 8.5% | 2.5% | **~$209** |

**Fair value: ~$165 (range $150–185).** The current price of $218 requires *roughly 9–10% annual FCF growth for a decade* — i.e., the market is capitalizing WSM as a structural grower. Recent history and management's own guide say low-to-mid single digits. Cross-check: 17–18x normalized EPS of $9.50–$10.00 gives $162–$180, consistent with the DCF.

The forward total-return math is not compelling: ~4% FCF yield + ~5.5% growth + buyback = high-single-digit return, less multiple compression if 23x normalizes toward 18x. The 14% one-day drop on Nov 19 (an in-line quarter with *reiterated* guidance) is exactly what a de-rating high-multiple stock looks like.

*A sell-side note:* nine published targets from $240–$286 (median ~$252) all sit above the price. That unanimity is itself worth discounting — those targets assume 25x+ multiples persist.

### 4. Management Quality — **Strong (A-)**

- **Laura Alber**, CEO since 2010 — a long-tenured operator who executed the DTC/digital transition and the margin-repair program. Culture is design- and product-led, not promotional.
- **Capital allocation has been genuinely excellent on the buyback:** diluted shares fell from 158.1M (FY2020) to 118.9M (Q3 FY2026), a **25% reduction**. That is the single largest driver of per-share value creation over five years and it was done aggressively at low-teens multiples.
- **Dividend** grown from a split-adjusted $0.355 to $0.76/quarter (~14% CAGR), comfortably covered at ~32% of earnings.
- **M&A discipline: outstanding** — goodwill of only $77M against $5.4B of assets. They build brands, they don't buy them.
- **Two blemishes:** (1) FY2025 capital returns of $1.17B (buybacks $854M + dividends $316M) exceeded FCF of $1.055B, a ~110% payout funded from cash. (2) At today's 23x, the buyback is meaningfully less value-accretive — the same program at $218 buys 1% of the company per $260M versus 2.7% at $97. **Watch whether they slow the pace rather than buy aggressively at elevated multiples; that's the capital-allocation test for the next two years.**
- Insider ownership data unavailable in this data feed — a gap in my assessment.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate; trim into strength)

This is the *kind* of business I want to own: no leverage, 40% returns on capital, ~100% cash conversion, a 25-year brand franchise, and a management team that shrinks the share count rather than the balance sheet. The moat is real, if narrow.

But **price is everything, and the price is wrong.** At $218 you are paying ~23x forward earnings and ~25x normalized free cash flow for a business that has not grown revenue in four years, is guided to flat operating margins, and faces a consumer under pressure from tariffs and a soft housing market. My intrinsic value of ~$165 leaves the stock ~30% expensive; there is no margin of safety — there is a margin of *erosion*.

- **Existing holders:** Hold the position. The compounding engine (buybacks + brand) is intact and selling a 40% ROIC franchise on a valuation call is usually a mistake, but trim to target weight.
- **New money:** Do not buy. **Buy zone: $130–150** (~20–25% discount to fair value).
- **What would make me a buyer at higher prices:** sustained mid-single-digit comps *with* gross margin holding above 45%, i.e., proof the margin gain is structural rather than a promotional cycle.

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Multiple compression (the dominant risk).** At 23x with flat margins and high-single-digit EPS growth, a de-rating to 17–18x costs ~20–25% of the share price regardless of operating performance. The Nov 19 reaction shows this is already underway.
2. **Margin mean-reversion.** The 730bp gross-margin gain depended on a low-promotion environment. Any return to industry-normal markdowns, or unrecovered China/Asia tariffs on imported home goods, hits the earnings base directly — and the one-time tariff *refunds* currently flattering Q2/Q3 will not repeat.
3. **Housing/consumer cyclicality.** Beta 1.49; home furnishings is a high-ticket, housing-turnover derivative. FY2023's -10.7% revenue decline was not a one-off anomaly — it is the shape of this business in a downturn.
4. **No structural growth.** 544 stores in a category being disintermediated by Amazon and DTC-native brands; international (139 franchised units) has been a decade-long promise.
5. **Buyback diminishing returns.** Repurchasing at 23x rather than 11x is a materially worse use of $850M/yr — the per-share compounding that drove the last five years mechanically slows.
6. **Style/inventory risk.** Vertically integrated, design-led retail means owning inventory against fashion risk ($1.46B, up 10% YoY). One misjudged season can cost a year of margin.

---

**Bottom line:** Great business, narrow moat, excellent balance sheet, first-rate capital allocation — **fairly-to-fully priced at best and ~30% overvalued at $218. Hold, don't chase.** Patience is the only thing that gets you this one at the right price.
