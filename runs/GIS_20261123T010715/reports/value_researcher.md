# General Mills, Inc. (NYSE: GIS) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $35.74 (20 Nov 2026) · Market cap $19.2B · Net debt $13.1B · EV $32.3B · FY ends late May*

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW, and narrowing**

General Mills owns genuine brand assets: Cheerios, Pillsbury, Betty Crocker, Old El Paso, Progresso, Häagen-Dazs, Nature Valley, Totino's, Blue Buffalo. These are #1/#2 share positions in categories with habitual repurchase, sold through a distribution system of real scale. That is a moat — but it is a **narrow** one, and the last five years show it eroding:

| Evidence | Reading |
|---|---|
| Revenue: $20.09B (FY23) → $18.42B (FY26) | −8% over three years; organic −2% in FY26 |
| Organic sales: −2% FY26, flat Q4 FY26, flat Q1 FY27 | Volume declines; price/mix no longer offsets |
| Pet segment organic sales −3% FY26 | Blue Buffalo, the 2018 growth engine, is now a laggard |
| Gross margin 33.6–35.6% over six years | Stable — cost discipline is real, but it's not pricing power |
| ROIC: 15.1% → 14.9% → 13.5% (FY23–25) | Above WACC (~7%), but the trend is down |
| $1.75–1.8B goodwill/brand impairment in Q4 FY26 | Book value of acquired brands exceeded fair value |

The honest interpretation: General Mills is a **price-taker in a decelerating category**. Private label has permanently improved, GLP-1 adoption is a structural headwind to packaged/comfort food volumes, and consolidated retailers (Walmart, Costco, Kroger) hold the pricing leverage. Management itself concedes "price investment work" is needed to defend share — that is the language of a company without pricing power.

**Moat verdict: Narrow. It has not widened in five years; it has measurably narrowed.** Still a business I can understand and underwrite — squarely inside the circle of competence.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **6 / 10**

| Metric | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue (FY26) | $18.42B, organic −2% | Predictable, not growing |
| Adj. operating margin | ~15.2% (FY26) | Down from ~17.3% (FY21) |
| Adj. EPS | $3.55 (FY26), ~$3.15e (FY27) | −16%, then −13% |
| FCF | $1.63B FY26; $2.26B 5-yr avg | ~100% conversion vs. adj. net income, historically excellent |
| Capex / D&A | $540M / $555M | Maintenance-level; low capital intensity |
| Net debt / adj. EBITDA | **~3.9x** | The core problem |
| Tangible book equity | **−$13.5B** | $20.8B of $30.0B assets is goodwill/intangibles |
| Interest expense | $538M (FY26) vs. $382M (FY23) | Refinancing at higher rates bites |

Cash generation is the genuinely good part of this business: capex ≈ D&A, conversion ~100%, and a 5-year average FCF of $2.26B. But the balance sheet is levered, and the FY26 GAAP result (net loss $88M, EPS −$0.16) reflects **$3.0B of charges**: a $1.75B non-cash impairment (driven, per management, mainly by higher discount rates) and a $1.0B non-cash valuation loss on the planned Brazil divestiture. I treat these as non-recurring for owner-earnings purposes — but not as meaningless. They are a confession of capital-allocation error in prior M&A, and they shrink book equity to $7.4B.

**Owner earnings (my estimate):** Adjusted net income ~$1.9B; add D&A $555M less maintenance capex ~$540M ≈ **$1.9–2.0B normalised**, vs. $1.63B reported FY26 (depressed by a $478M working-capital outflow). The screening tool's "−$72M owner earnings" figure is a mechanical artifact of the GAAP loss and should be discarded.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Where it trades:** 11.3x FY27E adjusted EPS ($3.15), 10.8x FY27E EV/EBITDA (~$3.0B), 10.4% normalised FCF yield, 6.8% dividend yield.

**Peer context** (packaged food is uniformly cheap and uniformly challenged): KHC 8.2x P/FCF and 6.3% yield; SJM 12.3x P/FCF; HRL 15.0x EV/EBITDA; MDLZ 26.6x P/FCF. GIS is priced in line with **Kraft Heinz** — i.e. the market already assigns it a value-trap discount. The peer median P/E of 25.6x is meaningless here (most peers carry impairment-driven GAAP losses).

**Intrinsic value (levered FCF discounted at 9.5% cost of equity, since FCF is already post-interest):**

| Scenario | Assumptions | Value/share |
|---|---|---|
| Bear | FCF $1.6B, −4%/yr for 5 yrs, −1% terminal | ~$26 |
| Base | FCF $1.8B flat 5 yrs, +1.5% terminal | ~$40 |
| Bull | FCF $1.9B, +2%/yr, growth resumes FY29 | ~$48 |

**Fair value ≈ $38–42. At $35.74, the stock trades at roughly a 10–13% discount — not the 20–30% margin of safety I require.**

The useful cross-check: a zero-growth perpetuity of $1.75B in FCF at 9.5% = ~$36/share. **Today's price assumes literally no growth, ever, and no credit for the $3 billion cost-savings programme.** That is not irrational for this business, but it is also not a gift.

Critically, the $750M of FY27 cost savings will **not** drop to the bottom line: management guides adjusted operating profit *down* 8–13% despite them. The savings are funding price reinvestment simply to hold organic sales flat. That is the single most important line in the guidance.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Mixed / C**

**Positives.** Jeff Harmening (CEO since 2017) is candid and operationally credible. Portfolio pruning has been genuinely good: the U.S. yogurt divestiture (~$1B gain), Brazil exit, frozen dough — shedding low-return assets. The $2.44 dividend has been paid without interruption (127 consecutive years of increases claimed, though effectively frozen since mid-2025). Share count is down from 614M (FY21) to 538M.

**Negatives — and they are serious.**
- **Blue Buffalo ($8B, 2018, ~20x EBITDA)** is now the source of much of the impairment and is losing share. Tyson pet-treats ($1.2B, 2021) was likewise written down.
- **Buybacks were value-destructive**: ~$4.6B repurchased in FY24–FY26 at $60–90/share against a $35.74 price today.
- **The dividend has been frozen at $2.44 for seven quarters** — a quiet signal that free cash flow is prioritised for deleveraging. Payout is ~77% of FY27E adjusted EPS, coverage only ~1.35x FCF. A cut is a live risk, not a tail risk.
- No evidence of meaningful insider buying at these levels.

Disclosure and transparency are good. Capital allocation discipline has not been.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (accumulate below ~$30)

This is a decent, cash-generative business with real brands, priced at roughly fair value — and "fair value" is not a buy. The 6.8% yield and 11x forward earnings provide valuation support and make a *Strong Sell* inappropriate, but the quality of that support is weaker than it looks.

I would require **$28–30** to commit new capital — the level that prices in continued organic decline and offers a true 25%+ margin of safety with the dividend still covered.

**What would make me buy:**
1. Two consecutive quarters of **positive organic sales** with volume, not price, leading.
2. Net debt/EBITDA below **3.0x**.
3. Evidence the $3B savings programme is reaching the P&L (adjusted operating margin expanding, not just holding).
4. Pet segment returning to growth; insider buying.

**What would make me sell:** a dividend cut, leverage above 4.5x, or organic sales resuming a mid-single-digit decline.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Dividend sustainability (highest-probability risk).** 6.8% yield, frozen for seven quarters, 77% payout, 3.9x leverage. A cut would likely take 20–30% off the equity in a week and would signal that my FCF normalisation is too generous.
2. **Leverage in a declining business.** $13.1B net debt, interest expense up 41% since FY23, negative tangible equity. Refinancing risk if EBITDA keeps shrinking 10%+ per year.
3. **Structural volume erosion.** GLP-1 adoption, private-label permanence, and health-driven reformulation are not cyclical. Management is spending the cost savings defending a shrinking perimeter.
4. **Further impairments.** $20.8B of goodwill/intangibles still sits on the books at 2.8x book equity. If discount rates stay elevated, more write-downs follow — non-cash, but they mark down earning power in investors' eyes.
5. **Pet/Blue Buffalo.** Fresh and premium pet food competitors are taking share structurally; this was the thesis for the largest acquisition in company history.
6. **Input-cost inflation** (dairy, cocoa, grains, packaging) against retailers unwilling to accept further price.
7. **Value-trap reflexivity.** At 11x with a 6.8% yield, the stock screens cheap to income or naive value screens; each failed "recovery" quarter can drive another de-rating, as it has from $70 (2023) to $31 (Oct 2026).

**Summary:** A narrow-moat, adequately-managed, cash-generative packaged-food company carrying too much debt into a decelerating category, priced at roughly intrinsic value. The impairment charges are genuinely non-cash and the organic trend is stabilising at flat — but patience, not price, is what this situation demands. **Hold; buy at $30 with a 25% margin of safety.**
