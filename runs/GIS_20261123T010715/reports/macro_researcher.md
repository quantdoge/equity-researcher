# Macroeconomic Assessment — General Mills (GIS)

**As-of: 20 November 2026 | Price $35.74 | Mkt cap $19.1bn | Fwd P/E ~11.5x on FY27 EPS guide $3.00–3.20**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Reflationary / bear-steepening — "risk-off in duration, not yet in credit."**

The regime has flipped hawkish over the last quarter. Real GDP re-accelerated (~3.3% SAAR in Q3 2026) with unemployment down to 4.1%, and headline CPI has stopped falling at ~3.8% y/y. The Fed cut to a 3.63% trough (May–Aug 2026) and has since **re-tightened to 3.88% (Oct 2026)**, with the 2Y at 4.74% pricing a further ~85bp of hikes. The 10Y has backed up ~68bp since late June (4.45% → 5.13%), the 30Y sits at 5.47%, and 5Y breakevens have *fallen* to 2.18% — so the long-end selloff is **real-rate and term-premium driven, not inflation-expectation driven**. Credit is still wide open (HY OAS 3.52%, near cycle tights), but equities are wobbling (Nasdaq -700pts on Nov 20, negative YTD) and the household sector is visibly strained: University of Michigan sentiment **48.0** (vs 71.7 in Jan 2025 — near record lows) and the savings rate **3.8%** (from 6.9% in Feb 2024).

For a packaged-foods "bond proxy" this is the most difficult combination available: **input-cost inflation at the high end of 4–5% of COGS (stepping to ~6% in FQ4), a consumer trading down, and a risk-free rate at 5.1% competing directly with a 6.8% dividend yield.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Negative — long real rates (the largest single channel).** GIS carries **$12.4bn long-term debt** against a $19.1bn market cap (net debt/EBITDA ≈ 3.3x on my estimate). Management explicitly flagged **higher net interest expense** as an FQ1 FY27 EPS drag alongside lower operating profit. With ~$2bn/yr of maturities rolling from legacy 3–4% coupons into 5%+ money, interest expense is a structural ~$50–80M/yr headwind for the next two years. Separately, at a 5.13% 10Y, GIS's 6.83% dividend yield offers only ~170bp of spread to risk-free versus a 250–300bp historical norm — the "bond-proxy bid" that usually supports staples in a slowdown is impaired.

**Negative — input cost inflation.** Management guides input cost inflation to the **high end of 4–5% of COGS, stepping up to ~6% in FQ4 FY27**. FQ1 FY27 adjusted gross margin fell 90bp to 33.3% on higher input costs. Food CPI accelerating +0.41% m/m in October is the tell: the pricing tailwind that sheltered staples in 2022–23 is now a cost headwind without matching elasticities. $750M of Holistic Margin Management savings is the only offset.

**Negative — consumer stress / volume.** Sentiment at 48 and a 3.8% savings rate are consistent with the reported volume profile: NA Retail organic volume **-2 pts**, NA Pet organic volume **-6 pts**, total organic volume -1 pt. Private-label trade-down risk is highest precisely when real incomes are squeezed by 5%+ mortgage/auto/card rates. A defensive sector does not mean defensive volumes when the consumer is drawing down savings.

**Negative-to-neutral — USD.** Broad USD +3.9% since September. International is ~20% of sales and delivered +4% organic growth in FQ1 partly on a **+1pt FX tailwind** and, notably, **-3 pts of price/mix** (FX-driven price deflation in EM). A sustained firmer dollar reverses that tailwind, and the completed Brazil divestiture (Sept 2, 2026) reduces the EM growth engine anyway.

**Neutral — tariffs/packaging.** Not quantifiable from the data I pulled, but the FQ4 step-up to ~6% input inflation is consistent with metals/packaging and imported-ingredient (cocoa, oils) tariff pass-through. One-sided cost risk.

**Positive — defensive rotation / relative valuation.** GIS is **+17% off its Oct 1 low ($30.60)** to $35.74 while the Nasdaq has gone negative YTD, and it now trades at an 8.7% earnings yield versus a 5.13% 10Y. The multiple (11.5x forward) already discounts a lot of stagnation; the FY27 guide (organic -1.5% to +0.5%, adj op profit -13%) has been reset. If growth rolls over, staples are the only sector with visible EPS.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Rationale: three of the four live macro channels — real rates, input costs, and consumer elasticity — are working against GIS simultaneously, and the fourth (USD) has turned. The one support (defensive rotation) is a *relative* not absolute driver and is already partly reflected in the November rally. The single mitigating factor is that valuation, not the discount rate, is now the swing variable: at 11.5x the multiple has limited further de-rating room, so macro damage transmits through **FY27/FY28 EPS revisions**, where the direction of travel is down.

## 5. Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (Bear)

**The hawkish overshoot.** If the Fed validates the 2Y at 4.74% and takes policy to 4.50–4.75% on a 4.5%+ CPI print (tariff pass-through plus a 5%-annualized food CPI run-rate), the 10Y tests **5.50–5.75%** and the 30Y 5.75%+. In that world the dividend-yield spread to risk-free disappears, the "safe haven" bid for staples fails, and GIS de-rates to **9x** on a cut FY27 EPS of $3.00 → **~$27 (-25%)**, with the 79% payout ratio inviting the dividend-growth freeze debate. Trigger to watch: 10Y through 5.31% (Oct 5 high) alongside a core CPI above 4%.

*Counter-scenario (bull, ~equal probability):* if unemployment breaks from 4.1% to 4.8%+ and the Fed is forced into rapid cuts, the 10Y falls back to ~4.25%, staples re-rate as the lone earnings-visible sector, and input-cost inflation deflates into a gross-margin recovery — GIS to **~$45 (+26%)**. The asymmetry here is that the market is already positioned for the first scenario.
