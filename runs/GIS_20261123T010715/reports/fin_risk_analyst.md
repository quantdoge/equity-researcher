## GENERAL MILLS, INC. (GIS) — FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT
*Data as of FY2026 (ended 31-May-2026, filed 1-Jul-2026); market data to 30-Sep-2026 close ($31.56)*

---

### 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE, LOWER TIER (BBB/Baa2), NEGATIVE MOMENTUM**

| Item | Reading |
|---|---|
| S&P ICR | **BBB**, outlook Stable (affirmed 5-Feb-2026); proposed subordinated notes rated **BB+** (8-Apr-2026) |
| Moody's | **Baa2 / P-2**, outlook Stable |
| Altman Z-Score | **1.99 — Grey zone** (2.25–2.30 using market cap instead of book equity; still grey) |

The company is a legitimate investment-grade credit — it retains full access to unsecured and subordinated markets (a subordinated note issue was rated as recently as April 2026), and it *reduced* gross debt by $1.76B in FY2026. But the rating sits at the bottom of the IG ladder and the direction of travel is negative.

**Critical reconciliation:** the GAAP interest coverage of **1.64x** looks distressed, but ~$1.92B of the FY2026 operating-income decline is **non-cash** (≈$1.8B goodwill/brand-intangible impairment plus a non-cash Brazil divestiture valuation loss). On adjusted EBIT ($2,807M) coverage is **5.2x**; on EBITDA, **6.2x**. Credit quality rests entirely on this add-back being genuinely non-recurring.

**The uncomfortable read:** that impairment is the market telling you the acquired-growth strategy (Blue Buffalo, $8B in 2018) no longer supports its carrying value. Goodwill + intangibles are still **$20.8B = 69% of total assets**, implying **tangible book equity of roughly –$13.5B**. Asset coverage for unsecured lenders is effectively nil; they are lending against cash flow, not collateral.

That is a refinancing-risk channel, not a solvency problem — until it isn't.

The beta of –0.09 is the most misleading number in this file. GIS did **not** protect investors in a downturn — its 53% drawdown was almost entirely **idiosyncratic** (brand impairment, organic sales decline, guidance cuts). Low beta here reflects sector classification and a prior multi-decade re-rating, not genuine defensive behaviour. A –0.93 Sharpe means three years of negative risk-adjusted return; volatility of 26.7% for a packaged-foods staple is roughly double a typical peer and confirms the market is pricing an impaired franchise. The stock rallied 33% off the June low to $40.89 (28-Aug) and gave it **all back** in five weeks — this is now a high-beta-to-its-own-fundamentals name.

### 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue –20%

Base FY2026: revenue $18,425M, gross margin 33.6%, SG&A $3,389M, adjusted EBIT $2,807M, interest $539M, D&A $555M.

| | Base | **–10% rev** (GM –100bp, SG&A –2.5%) | **–20% rev** (GM –200bp, SG&A –5%) | **–30% + GM –300bp** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 18,425 | 16,583 | **14,740** | 12,898 |
| Gross profit | 6,196 | 5,321 | **4,658** | 3,946 |
| EBIT (pre-impair) | 2,807 | 2,017 | **1,439** | 722 |
| **Interest coverage** | 5.2x | 3.7x | **2.7x** | **1.3x** |
| EBITDA | 3,362 | 2,572 | **1,994** | 1,277 |
| **Net debt / EBITDA** | 3.9x | 5.1x | **6.6x** | **10.2x** |
| FCF (est.) | 1,626 | ~1,200 | **~850** | ~250 |
| **FCF / dividend (1,315)** | 1.24x | 0.91x | **0.65x** | **0.19x** |
| Altman Z | 1.99 | ~1.75 | **~1.45** | ~1.1 |

**Findings:**
- **A 20% revenue decline is a dividend-breaking event.** FCF coverage falls to ~0.65x; the board would need to cut the payout by one-third or more. Given a 7.6% yield, the market is already partially pricing this.
- At –20%, net leverage of 6.6x and coverage of 2.7x put GIS **squarely in BB (sub-investment-grade) territory** — a two-notch downgrade. Critically, this scenario does *not* require a catastrophic outcome; it is roughly the cumulative organic decline the company has already been running at, sustained for a few years.
- At –30%, leverage exceeds 10x, coverage falls to 1.3x, and the Z-score enters the distress zone. Solvency is not threatened (no maturity wall, positive FCF), but the equity would be severely impaired and the dividend eliminated.
- **Best mitigant:** the FY2027 plan targets **$3B of cost savings by FY2030** and management states price investment is "behind us." If delivered, the –10% case is absorbable. **Offsetting risk:** cost savings are the classic refuge of a company with no volume growth, and 3 years is a long time in a category losing share to private label.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Scoring credit alone, GIS is **Moderate** (IG-rated, no maturity cliff, $1.6B FCF, actively tendering debt). Scoring the *distribution of outcomes* an equity holder faces, it is **High**: the equity has lost 55%, the impairment revealed a broken acquisition thesis, organic sales are negative, three straight years of gross-margin decline, and dividend coverage has compressed to 1.24x. There is a real, non-tail path to a dividend cut and a downgrade to BB. It is **not Critical** — there is no going-concern, covenant, or refinancing cliff visible.

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **~$1.9B non-cash charges incl. ~$1.8B goodwill/brand-intangible impairment** — the Blue Buffalo/brand portfolio cannot support its carrying value. **$20.8B goodwill + intangibles = 69% of assets; tangible equity ≈ –$13.5B.**
2. **Earnings erosion is not just accounting.** Organic net sales **–2%**, revenue **–5%**, adjusted operating profit **–16% cc**, adjusted EPS **–16% cc**.
3. **GAAP interest coverage 1.64x** — investment-grade status depends on a non-cash add-back being believed.
4. **Piotroski 4/9**, with leverage *increasing* and ROA deteriorating. Gross margin down three consecutive years (34.9% → 34.6% → 33.6%).
5. **FCF down 41% since FY2022**; FY2026 operating cash flow –26% YoY on a $478M working-capital drag.
6. **Dividend coverage 1.24x FCF and falling**; $1.82B of shareholder returns exceeded $1.63B of FCF — buybacks continued into an earnings collapse.
7. **Liquidity dependence:** current ratio 0.68, cash ratio 0.07, negative working capital of –$2.16B. Requires uninterrupted revolver/CP access.
8. **Leverage up three years running:** net debt/EBITDA 3.16x → 3.81x → 3.89x; D/E 1.38x → 1.83x.
9. **Negative Sharpe (–0.93), 53% drawdown, 26.7% vol** — the "defensive" label is not supported by realized outcomes.
