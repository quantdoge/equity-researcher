## General Mills (GIS) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Research cut-off: ~Nov 22, 2026. Fiscal year ends May 31; FY2026 = year ended 5/31/2026, FY2027 Q1 = quarter ended 8/30/2026.*

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

GIS is overwhelmingly a North American business. FY2026 net sales were **$18.4B, down 5%** (organic −2%), of which the International segment was roughly **$3.0–3.1B (~17%)** and North America ~83%. FY2027 Q1 (ended 8/30/26) net sales were **$4.4B**, with International at **$794M (~18%)**.

|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** (North America Retail, Pet, Foodservice) | ~72–75% | F27Q1: NAR $2.4B, Pet $613M, Foodservice $523M of $4.4B total | **Low** |
| **Canada** (inside NAR "Big G Cereal & Canada" unit) | ~4–5% | Segment disclosure; Canada yogurt divested FY25 | **Low** |

**Notes on the map:** Country-level splits inside International are not disclosed granularly in the materials I could access (SEC.gov returned 403 and the filing tool had no 10-K/10-Q for GIS), so sub-segment percentages are estimates derived from segment disclosures and should be treated as directional. The **Brazil exit is the single biggest geographic change**: it removes the company's largest direct emerging-market manufacturing footprint (plants in Pouso Alegre and Campo Novo do Parecis) and cut ~$350M of sales, but cost a **$1.0B non-cash pre-tax valuation loss** in FY2026 Q4 — a ~$700M+ realized destruction versus the ~$1B paid for Yoki in 2012.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US tariff and input-cost pass-through (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
GIS is a domestic manufacturer but a global buyer: cocoa, sugar, edible oils, packaging, tinplate/aluminum, and imported ingredients. FY2026 gross margin fell 100bp to 33.6% on **"higher input costs,"** and FY2027 Q1 adjusted gross margin fell another 90bp on the same driver. Management repeatedly cites a **"volatile environment"** and is leaning on $3B of cumulative cost savings by FY2030 ($750M targeted in FY2027) to offset it. Because GIS's price investment program is largely "behind" it, further tariff-driven cost inflation has limited remaining pricing runway — this is a margin risk more than a solvency risk.

**Risk 2 — US–China / US–India trade friction hitting the only growing segment (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium)**
International is the sole segment posting organic growth (+4% in F27Q1, +3% FY26) and its growth is explicitly attributed to **China, India, and distributor markets**. That makes GIS's growth story disproportionately levered to exactly the geographies most exposed to tariff escalation, Chinese consumer nationalism toward US brands, and Indian regulatory/labeling action. At ~17% of sales the dollar impact is bounded, but it is the swing factor in the "return to growth" narrative.

**Risk 3 — USMCA / Canada–Mexico supply-chain and labeling friction (Probability: Medium / Impact: Low–Medium)**
Canada sits inside the North America Retail reporting unit and Mexico is a core Old El Paso market. Cross-border ingredient flows (dairy, grains, sugar) and any USMCA renegotiation noise create tariff and rules-of-origin risk on a ~10% combined revenue base. Low severity given the company's largely local-for-local North American production.

*Not a risk: sanctions, expropriation, currency controls, or capital repatriation. GIS has no Russia/Iran/etc. exposure, and the Brazil exit removes the one jurisdiction where currency and political risk were material.*

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Emerging mass-tort exposure — ultra-processed foods (UPF).** This is the most underappreciated legal item. GIS has been defending underlying litigation alleging harm from ultra-processed foods, and was simultaneously fighting its own insurers (units of **Liberty Mutual and Chubb**) over coverage. On **October 6, 2026**, GIS and the insurers **ended the coverage litigation** — implying the underlying UPF claims are being handled without broad insurer indemnification, or that a resolution structure was reached. UPF is the food industry's analogue to tobacco/sugar litigation; watch for reserve disclosures.

**State AG enforcement — active and multi-front.**
- **Texas (AG Ken Paxton):** GIS agreed to **remove artificial dyes** from its products following a Texas investigation and threatened legal action.
- **Florida (AG James Uthmeier):** issued a **civil subpoena** to GIS as part of an investigation into **potassium bromate** in bread (Pillsbury exposure).
- This is a coordinated, politically salient state-AG playbook (dyes, bromate, "generally recognized as safe" ingredients) that is cheap for states and expensive for GIS in reformulation cost and brand risk.

**Federal regulatory shift — FDA/HHS synthetic dye phase-out.** HHS/FDA moved to phase out petroleum-based synthetic dyes; FDA tracks company pledges publicly. **GIS pre-emptively announced removal of certified colors from all US cereals and all K-12 school foods by summer 2026, and has completed the phase-out for all US cereals.** This was well-managed — the FDA's voluntary timeline reportedly slipped ~a year — but the reformulation carried cost and taste/consumer-acceptance risk, and school-food channel exposure adds reputational sensitivity.

**GIS as antitrust *plaintiff* — sugar price-fixing.** On **September 4, 2026**, GIS, **Mars, Kellanova and McKee** filed suit in the **Northern District of Illinois** (Case 1:26-cv-10777) against sugar producers, alleging they **fixed, raised and maintained granulated sugar prices from January 2017 through at least October 2024** by sharing confidential competitive information. This is a *potential recovery*, not a liability — and a direct read-through on the "higher input costs" drag. Separately, GIS appears as a **remaining plaintiff** alongside Kraft Food Global and Nestlé USA in a separate protein-input antitrust matter (per Cal-Maine's Sept 30, 2026 10-Q), where several co-plaintiffs have settled.

**Consumer class action.** A class action filed **October 26, 2026** challenges "0g added sugar" labeling on **Yoplait Enjoy Zero**, alleging the claim conceals allulose content. Note the complication: the **US/Canada yogurt business (Yoplait, Liberté, Go-Gurt, Oui, :Ratio) was sold to Lactalis and Sodiaal for $2.1B in 2025** — GIS may retain indemnity/liability for pre-closing labeling. Small dollar value, but a recurring false-advertising pattern across the portfolio.

**Structural/restructuring.** A "new operating model" and "global transformation initiative" are underway alongside the $3B savings program — expect associated restructuring charges and possible non-US severance/consultation obligations. FY2026 also carried **$1.8B of non-cash goodwill and brand intangible impairments** on rising discount rates, concentrated in the segments most exposed to volume loss.

| Flag | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **Sanctions / export controls (OFAC, BIS)** | **None identified.** No Russia, Iran, Cuba, or dual-use exposure. Food is broadly exempt from most sanctions regimes. |
| **FCPA / UK Bribery Act** | **Low.** Large distributor footprint in Asia, Middle East and Africa is the classic FCPA surface, but no disclosed investigations. Standard third-party distributor diligence is the control point. |

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale.** Geopolitically this is one of the cleaner large-cap profiles in the consumer staples universe: ~83% North America, no Russia/Ukraine exposure, no sanctions surface, and a completed exit from its highest-risk emerging market (Brazil). The Brazil divestiture is strategically de-risking even though it was financially painful.

The rating is **Medium rather than Low** because the *legal and regulatory* axis is deteriorating faster than the geopolitical one:
1. **UPF litigation is an unquantified, industry-wide liability** with the tell-tale sign of insurer coverage disputes (now resolved/terminated Oct 2026) — i.e., the company may be bearing this risk more on its own balance sheet.
2. **Coordinated state-AG enforcement** (Texas, Florida) is creating a fragmented compliance burden with real reformulation costs.
3. **Tariff-driven input inflation** is compressing gross margins with limited remaining pricing power, which is a P&L risk that masquerades as a macro risk.

**Key gaps in this report, stated plainly:** (a) I could not access the FY2026 10-K Item 3/Item 1A or the geographic revenue footnote — SEC.gov returned HTTP 403 and the filing tool had no 10-K/10-Q for GIS, so country-level revenue splits and any environmental/PFAS or tax contingencies are unverified; (b) exact FY2026 International segment dollars and sub-region mix are estimated from quarterly growth disclosures rather than read from the segment footnote; (c) no reserve amounts for the UPF or consumer class actions are available. These should be closed out against the 10-K before this feeds the memo.

**What would change the rating:** a material UPF reserve or a multi-state AG coalition action would move this to **High**. A Brazil-style exit from another emerging market, or a large China/India disruption, would not — those are already sub-scale.
