## SECTOR BRIEF — General Mills (GIS)
**Sector:** Consumer Defensive › Packaged Foods | Price $35.74, Mkt cap $19.1bn | FY ends May

---

### 1. Sector Overview
U.S. packaged foods is a mature, low-growth, high-cash-return industry (~$1tn+ global; GIS competes in a ~$300bn U.S. centre-store/frozen/pet pool) now in a **late-cycle, post-inflation reset**. The 2022–24 pricing-driven recovery has fully lapped: pricing is fading (USDA forecasts food-at-home inflation slowing from 2.8% in 2026 to 1.7% in 2027, from ~6%+ peaks), so growth must come from volume — and volumes are running **below 1%** industry-wide (McKinsey). Growth is further capped by GLP-1 adoption (roughly 1 in 5 U.S. households per PwC's 2026 survey; users buy less and trade toward protein/fresh), resilient private label, and highly concentrated retail buyers. The result is zero-sum share competition, margin defence through cost programmes, and a sector-wide de-rating to ~10–13x earnings with 5–7% dividend yields.

**Structural read (Porter):** Buyer power **high** (Walmart/Costco/Kroger concentration, shelf-space gatekeeping); Rivalry **high** (share defence, promotional intensity); Substitutes **high** (private label, fresh/perishable, foodservice, GLP-1-driven smaller baskets); New entrants **low** (brands, scale, distribution, capital); Supplier power **moderate** (largely commodity inputs — cocoa/dairy/grain — but cocoa and packaging remain volatile). Barriers are brand and scale, not technology; consolidation (Mars/Kellanova, and GIS's own U.S. yogurt divestiture) is the main structural response.

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **Input-cost and inflation normalisation:** food inflation decelerating to ~1.7% by 2027 (USDA) gives visibility on COGS; GIS's Holistic Margin Management delivered "industry-leading" savings and held Q1 FY27 gross margin flat at 33.9%.
- **Defensive earnings + discount valuations:** the group now trades at ~10x forward earnings with 6–7% yields (GIS ~10.1x fwd, $2.44 DPS = **6.8% yield**, 128 consecutive years of dividends). Rate cuts would re-rate these bond-proxies; GIS's beta is ~0.0 (−0.05).
- **Portfolio pruning and consolidation:** divesting low-growth assets (U.S. yogurt) and reallocating to higher-margin Pet/International, plus sector M&A (Mars–Kellanova) creating a bid floor for brand portfolios; innovation aimed at GLP-1/protein and "value" tiers can recapture lapsed volumes.

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Volume deleverage:** pricing no longer covers volume losses; every peer is fighting for flat organics. GIS organic sales were flat in Q1 FY27 after a **−5.5%** sales year — a share problem, not just a category problem.
- **GLP-1 + private label mix shift:** structurally lower consumption for carb-heavy centre-store categories (cereal, baking mixes, snacks) and permanent trade-down to store brands, compressing both volume and price/mix.
- **Input/geopolitical and tariff volatility:** cocoa at record highs has gutted chocolate margins (HSY gross margin −1,377bp YoY; MDLZ −1,074bp), packaging/logistics costs are re-accelerating, and tariff pass-through is still working through a 12–18 month lag; retailer pushback limits pricing power.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Average**
- **Strengths:** #1/#2 brands across cereal, Mexican (Old El Paso), soup, baking, snacks and pet (Blue Buffalo); structurally superior gross margin and a proven HMM/Transformation cost engine; genuinely defensive demand (beta ≈ 0); 6.8% dividend yield covered by FCF (~11.9x trailing FCF per press coverage).
- **Weaknesses:** topline is deteriorating faster than any large peer (−5.5% FY26; organic flat only in Q1 FY27) — the "Accelerate" strategy has not yet stabilised dollar share; Pet (Blue Buffalo) has been the source of the $1.8bn impairment and is slowing; FY27 guidance is for **adjusted operating profit down 8–13% cc** and EPS of $3.00–3.20, i.e. another year of earnings contraction; leverage from the Blue Buffalo deal limits buyback flexibility.
- **Net:** a high-quality franchise with a shrinking revenue base — strong relative margins, weak relative momentum. Hence Average, not Strong.

### 6. Sector Rating: **Neutral**
Cash generation and dividend support are genuinely attractive, and at ~10x earnings / ~7% yields the group already discounts a lot of GLP-1 and private-label pessimism — so an outright "Unattractive" overstates the downside. But with industry volumes <1%, pricing tailwinds exhausted, buyer power rising and GIS-specific share losses unresolved, there is no sector-level catalyst for re-rating; returns will be income-driven, not growth-driven. **Sector: Neutral; GIS positioned as a yield/value holding, not a growth compounder.**
