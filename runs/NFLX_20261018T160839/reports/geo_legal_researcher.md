### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1. US content-policy and film-tariff intervention — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium-High**
Trump has repeatedly threatened a 100% tariff on films made outside the US (Reuters, Sep 2025; renewed 2026), which would hit Netflix harder than peers because it produces more content abroad (Yahoo Finance/CNBC, May 2025 — studio stocks including NFLX fell on the initial proposal). Netflix is also a visible target for political pressure on content and for merger politics. Geographic concentration (44% UCAN) means US policy is the single largest swing factor.

**2. European content-quota and digital-tax regime — Probability: High (in force) | Impact: Medium**
EMEA is 32% of revenue, and EU/UK/Canada/Australia rules require local-content investment quotas and prominence obligations, plus unilateral DSTs (France, Italy, Spain, UK, Turkey, Austria). This is a permanent gross-margin and compliance-cost drag plus a lever foreign governments can tighten. Low expropriation risk, high friction.

**3. Emerging-market regulatory/fiscal squeeze in LATAM & APAC — Probability: Medium-High | Impact: Low-Medium**
~24% of revenue sits in jurisdictions with weaker rule of law: Brazilian municipal/state tax assessments (CIDE-type levies), Indian content-removal orders and tax claims, Korean network-fee litigation, Vietnamese content bans, and Argentine FX controls affecting repatriation. Individually small, collectively persistent.

**Resolved/distinct item — the WBD antitrust episode:** Netflix's ~$83B bid for Warner Bros. Discovery triggered a DOJ antitrust review and intense EU scrutiny (FT; Reuters, Dec 2025); commentators judged Netflix faced materially higher antitrust barriers than Paramount (ProMarket, Mar 2026). Netflix **walked away on 26 Feb 2026** after WBD's board called Paramount's revised ~$108–110B offer superior — with Paramount reportedly assuming the $2.8B breakup fee owed to Netflix (CNBC/Reuters/Guardian, 26 Feb 2026). DOJ approved Paramount on 12 Jun 2026; the EC cleared with conditions (Case M.12278, decision 22 Jul 2026). **This removes a Very High tail risk** (a contested multi-year merger fight) but leaves Netflix facing a larger, better-capitalised rival in Paramount-WBD.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights
- **Patent litigation largely won:** DivX streaming-patent campaign — Federal Circuit ruling on validity (13 Feb 2026, *Netflix v. DivX*) and a complete defense verdict at trial in April 2026 finding no infringement of the four asserted patents, against a claim of "> $[100M]" (WilmerHale, 8 Apr 2026). Contained.
- **Data privacy:** Dutch DPA fined Netflix **€4.75M** for GDPR transparency failures covering 2018–2020 data practices (EDPB / Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens, Nov 2024). Larger GDPR/consumer-privacy class exposure remains a standing risk given the ad tier's data intensity.
- **Tax:** consistent multi-jurisdiction examination exposure (Brazil, India, Italy, UK, Japan historically). *Unverified in this pass* — 10-K text was unavailable, so quantify from the next filing.
- **Other live categories:** defamation/IP suits attached to specific titles (e.g., *Baby Reindeer*), music-licensing and VPPA/consumer-privacy class actions, and prospective EU DSA/AI Act obligations on recommender and ad systems.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags
- **Sanctions/export controls: Low.** No Russia/Iran business; geo-blocking controls in place. Sanctions-evasion scrutiny of streaming/payment flows is a recurring EU theme but no NFLX-specific action found.
- **FCPA/AML: Low.** Subscription-payment model, limited third-party intermediary risk.
- **Watch item:** content-removal demands from authoritarian-adjacent markets create free-speech/legal-conflict exposure and occasional advertiser and NGO backlash — reputational rather than financial.
- **Data privacy:** the one area with a documented enforcement record (above).

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**
Rationale: 56% of revenue is non-US, but the overwhelming majority sits in high-rule-of-law jurisdictions (EU, UK, Canada, Japan, Australia, Korea) with no nationalisation, currency-inconvertibility or sanctions exposure at scale. The single largest tail risk — a contested WBD acquisition and its antitrust war — was eliminated in Feb 2026 at modest cost. The residual risk profile is *regulatory and fiscal friction plus US political intervention*, not legal catastrophe. Key monitoring triggers: enactment of a film-production tariff, EU quota tightening or new DSTs, adverse Indian/Brazilian tax rulings, and the newly enlarged Paramount-WBD's regulatory treatment.
