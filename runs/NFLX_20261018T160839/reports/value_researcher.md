## Netflix (NFLX) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price basis: $70.53 (latest snapshot, Oct 2026); 4.317B diluted shares; market cap ≈$304B; net debt $5.4B; EV ≈$310B. Note: the automated DCF tool returned a corrupted result (it extrapolated a 30% growth rate off the depressed 2021–22 FCF base and garbled the discount-rate units), so all intrinsic-value work below is hand-built from reported financials.*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow (at the high end, and widening)**

Netflix has done what few scaled consumer businesses manage: grown revenue double digits while expanding margins for four straight years (gross margin 39.4% → 48.5%; operating margin 17.8% → 29.5%, FY22–FY25). That is pricing power, plainly demonstrated.

Sources of advantage:
- **Scale economics in content**: ~$18B/yr of content cash spend amortized across ~325M households in 190 countries. Per-subscriber content cost falls as the base grows — a genuine cost advantage no sub-scale streamer can match.
- **Intangible brand + data**: recommendation/production data compounds with viewing hours; the brand is the default verb for streaming.
- **Demonstrated price increases with acceptable churn**, plus two new monetization layers (ad tier, paid sharing) layered onto a fixed cost base — high-incremental-margin revenue.

What caps the rating at Narrow:
- **Switching costs are near zero.** A household can cancel in two clicks; churn is managed, not structurally suppressed.
- **The moat is defended by contestable capital.** Amazon, Apple, YouTube and Disney can outspend Netflix on content. Attention is the real competitive arena, and YouTube/TikTok are winning hours at the margin — precisely the 2026 worry that has taken the stock down ~35–42% from its high.
- **Content is a treadmill, not a toll bridge.** Unlike See's or Coke, the asset does not endure; it must be re-bought every year at rising prices (sports rights especially).

Verdict on moat direction: **quantitatively widening** (ROIC 16.0% → 32.4% over three years vs. ~9–10% WACC; the moat test is passed decisively) but **qualitatively contested**. This is a wonderful business, not an impregnable one.

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **8 / 10**

| Metric | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 31.6 | 33.7 | 39.0 | 45.2 |
| Growth | 6.5% | 6.7% | 15.7% | 15.9% |
| Gross margin | 39.4% | 41.5% | 46.1% | 48.5% |
| Operating margin | 17.8% | 20.6% | 26.7% | 29.5% |
| FCF ($B) | 1.62 | 6.93 | 6.92 | 9.46 |
| ROIC | 16.0% | 18.5% | 24.4% | 32.4% |

- **FCF conversion**: $9.46B FCF / $10.98B net income = **86%** — excellent, and slightly *understated* if anything because content spend is running modestly above amortization.
- **Balance sheet**: net debt only $5.4B (down from $12.1B in 2021), ~0.2x EBITDA. Cash $9.1B. Fortress.
- **Deducted for**: no dividend (fine — reinvestment is better), and the fact that EBITDA ($30.3B) is a **meaningless number here** because ~$16B of content amortization sits inside "D&A." Value this on FCF/EBIT, never EV/EBITDA.
- **Important correction**: the owner-earnings tool reports $27.0B (NI + D&A − capex). That is a false positive. Content is a consumable input, not a depreciating fixed asset; the real maintenance outlay is replenishing the library (~the amortization level). True owner earnings ≈ FCF ≈ **$9.5B (FY25), ~$11.5B (FY26E)**. I would not capitalize the $27B figure.

### 3. Valuation — price ≈ intrinsic value; no margin of safety

Multiples on today's $70.53:

| Multiple | Value | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (FY25 EPS $2.53) | 27.9x | trailing |
| P/E (FY26E ~$3.30 underlying) | ~21x | Q1'26 EPS of $1.23 contains a likely one-off |
| P/FCF (FY26E ~$11.5B) | ~26x | FCF yield ~3.9% |
| EV/EBIT (FY26E ~$15.8B) | ~20x | the honest multiple |
| P/B | 11.4x | book is not meaningful (content written off as amortization) |
| **vs. sector-peer median P/E (16.4x)** | **+70% premium** | peers are telcos/legacy media; SPOT/META are the relevant set |

**Intrinsic value (hand-built DCF)**: base FY26E FCF $11.5B, growth tapering 15% → 8% over five years, 3.5% terminal, 9.5% WACC → EV ≈$276B, equity ≈$271B = **~$63/share**. Sensitivity: 9.0% WACC → **$69**; 8.5% WACC → **$76**. Bear case (engagement erosion, content cost inflation, growth stalls at 6% and margin peaks) → **~$45**. Bull case (ad tier scales to $8–10B, margins to 35%) → **~$90**.

**Fair value range: $60–75, midpoint ~$65.** Current price is at or slightly above that: **roughly zero to −8% discount**. The reverse DCF says the market is paying for ~14–15% FCF CAGR for five years — i.e., near-perfect continuation of the last two years, with no allowance for the engagement/competition risk that has already shown up in 2026 guidance.

Buffett's rule: a wonderful business at a fair price is worth owning, but "fair" is not "a buy." My required entry is a **25% discount to $65 → below ~$49**.

### 4. Management Quality: **B / B+**

- **Pros**: founder-built, owner-operator culture; disciplined on M&A (walked away from value-destroying scale deals); debt paid down aggressively; share count down from 4.44B (2022) to 4.32B diluted (2025) — ~2.7%.
- **Cons**: **buyback timing has been poor** — $21.4B repurchased over 2023–25 at roughly 30–40x earnings ($9.1B in 2025 alone near the stock's high). That is value transfer away from continuing owners, and exactly the behavior a disciplined allocator avoids. Guidance credibility also slipped in 2026 (soft Q2/Q3 guide, stock −8% on the July print). Reed Hastings' departure removes the founder anchor from the boardroom; the Sarandos/Peters co-CEO structure is competent but unproven through a downturn.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (not a Buy at $70.53)

This is a genuinely good business — high, rising returns on capital, expanding margins, a fortress balance sheet, and a management team that has built an enormous global franchise. It passes the "would I want to own this for ten years" test. It fails the **price** test, which is the test that determines returns.

At ~$65 intrinsic value and $70.53 market price, you are accepting all of the competitive and execution risk for zero compensation. **If you already own it, hold** (nothing in the fundamentals justifies selling a 32%-ROIC compounder at 20x forward EBIT). **If you don't own it, do nothing.** My buy discipline: accumulate below **~$52** (25% margin of safety) and add aggressively below **$45**, where the ad-tier and pricing optionality becomes free.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Engagement decay / attention competition** — YouTube and TikTok taking share of viewing hours is the single largest threat; it feeds churn, pricing power, and ad inventory simultaneously.
2. **Content cost inflation, especially sports** — live rights are lower-margin, longer-duration, higher-fixed-cost capital with worse ROIC than scripted library content. A shift toward sports structurally lowers the returns shown above.
3. **AI-driven content substitution** — could commoditize scripted production (bullish for cost) or flood the market with substitute attention (bearish for pricing). Directionally ambiguous, but a real re-rating risk.
4. **Ad-tier execution and guidance credibility** — 2026's repeated guidance resets suggest visibility is worse than the 2023–25 record implied.
5. **Key-person/governance transition** — Hastings' departure removes the capital-allocation conscience of the franchise.
6. **Valuation compression** — at 26x FCF, a de-rating to 18–20x (still premium) is a 25–30% loss even with the business performing.
7. **Accounting gap between amortization and cash content spend** — if cash spend must run above amortization to defend engagement, reported margins overstate economic margins.

**Bottom line:** excellent franchise, fair-to-full price, no margin of safety. Hold and wait for the price to come to us.
