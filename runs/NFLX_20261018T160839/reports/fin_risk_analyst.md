## NFLX — Financial Risk Assessment

*Data as of FY2025 (10-K, period end 2025-12-31), with quarterly data through Q2 2026 and market data through 2026-10-18. Netflix executed a 10-for-1 split in Nov 2025; price-derived metrics are split-adjusted.*

### 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE (A-tier)**

Netflix is a high-quality investment-grade credit and, notably, a **deleveraging** one. Total debt fell from $17.99B (FY24) to $14.46B (FY25) while cash stayed ~flat at $9.06B, cutting net debt from $10.19B to $5.43B. Net debt/EBITDA of **0.18x** and interest coverage of **17.2x** are consistent with a mid-single-A profile. Cross-checking external ratings: S&P assigned an **'A' issue-level rating** to new senior unsecured notes (Jul 2026) and Moody's upgraded senior unsecured to **A3** — corroborating the tool-derived metrics.

| Credit signal | Reading | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Debt/Equity | 0.54x | Conservative |
| Net debt/EBITDA | 0.18x | Very strong |
| EBIT/Interest | 17.2x | Very strong |
| Altman Z-Score | 3.27 (Safe zone) | No distress signal |
| Piotroski F-Score | 8 / 9 (Strong) | Improving fundamentals |
| Leverage trend | Debt −20% YoY, net debt −47% YoY | Deleveraging |

**Maturity/refinancing cliff: none identified.** Short-term debt is only $0.999B against $9.06B cash; long-term debt is $13.46B, laddered with visible long-dated paper (4.90% notes due 2034, 5.40% notes due 2054). There is no near-term wall. At today's curve (10Y 4.99%, 30Y 5.37%, 2s10s +34bp, no inversion), refinancing would be roughly cost-neutral to slightly accretive versus legacy coupons — a benign backdrop.

**The central market-risk paradox:** this is a *low credit risk / high equity risk* security. Volatility of 34% with a ~50% peak-to-trough drawdown and a Sharpe of only 0.21 means equity holders have been paid poorly for the ride. The beta readings disagree sharply (0.73 measured vs 1.61 vendor 5Y) — I would treat **~1.1–1.3** as the honest planning beta and explicitly flag the discrepancy rather than rely on either print. Note also that a −50% drawdown occurred *during a period of excellent fundamental performance*, underscoring that NFLX's equity risk is multiple/expectations-driven, not solvency-driven.

**A severe recession does not threaten this balance sheet.** Even a brutal −35% revenue scenario (subscriber attrition *plus* an advertising downturn, with content costs mostly inflexible) leaves operating income near $3.4B, coverage ≈4.3x, and FCF marginally positive — still investment grade with no covenant stress. The genuine downside is **equity de-rating**, not default: an FCF yield compressing toward 2% at a 30x+ multiple implies meaningful multiple compression risk if growth stalls.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **LOW**

Solvency risk is negligible (Z 3.27, F 8/9, net leverage 0.18x, coverage 17x, A/A3 ratings, no refinancing cliff). Liquidity is adequate and self-funding. The risk that matters here is **valuation and equity-volatility risk**, not financial distress.

### 6. Key Red Flags & Watch Items

1. **Negative tangible book equity.** Goodwill + intangibles of $32.78B exceed total equity of $26.62B → tangible equity ≈ **−$6.2B**. The equity cushion is bookkeeping, not asset-backed (partly defensible, as the intangible base includes the content library).
2. **Buybacks consume ~96% of FCF** ($9.13B repurchased vs $9.46B FCF in FY25), with no dividend. Capital return is running at the limit of internally generated cash, leaving thin incremental buffer against content commitments.
3. **Accruals quality is the sole F-Score miss (F4 = 0).** FY25 net income ($10.98B) exceeded operating cash flow ($10.15B) — cash content spend is outrunning amortization, so GAAP earnings are running *ahead* of cash. This is the single most important earnings-quality item to monitor.
4. **Reported EBITDA flatters leverage.** D&A of $16.76B is 37% of revenue and consists largely of content amortization — an economic cost of staying in business. Stripping it out, leverage is still low (~0.40x) but 2x the headline 0.18x.
5. **Q1 2026 net income ($5.28B) materially exceeded operating income ($3.96B)** — a ~$1.3–1.8B non-operating/tax item inflated the print. Do not extrapolate that quarter's EPS or ROE.
6. **Off-balance-sheet content obligations** (committed licensed/live-rights spend) are the largest true claim on the business and are **not observable in the provided data** — a genuine analytical gap. Escalating live-sports rights (fixed-cost, low-margin) could structurally raise the fixed-cost base and reduce the flexibility that makes the downside scenario above so benign.
7. **Beta measurement conflict (0.73 vs 1.61)** and a ~50% three-year max drawdown — position sizing should assume high equity tail risk despite the fortress balance sheet.
