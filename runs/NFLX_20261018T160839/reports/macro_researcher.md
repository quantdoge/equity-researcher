## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional / Late-Cycle Hawkish Pivot**

The regime has changed decisively in the past six weeks. On **16 September 2026 the FOMC raised the fed funds target to 3.75–4.00%** — unanimously, the first hike since 2023 — after cutting from 5.33% (2023 peak) to 3.63% by early 2026. Sixteen of eighteen participants signalled further hikes. The long end has repriced violently: the **10Y is 5.10% (15 Oct) after touching ~5.3% intraday, its highest since 2002**, and the 30Y sits at 5.37%.

Critically, **this is a real-rate shock, not an inflation-expectations shock**. The 10Y breakeven is *lower* than in May (2.35% vs 2.50%) while the **10Y TIPS real yield has risen 87bp from 1.91% (1 May) to 2.78% (15 Oct), peaking at 2.95% on 5 Oct**. That is the single most important variable for a 1.61-beta, long-duration equity: valuation is being set by the real discount rate, not by inflation psychology.

Growth is decelerating but not stalling (Q2 real GDP +2.2% q/q saar, +2.19% y/y vs +2.64% in Q1), labour is firm (4.2% unemployment, down from 4.5% in Nov 2025), and nominal consumer spending is running +6.1% y/y. Credit is still benign — HY OAS 3.23% — but has **widened 58bp in a month (2.65% on 11 Sep)**, coinciding with a ~$260bn AI-software selloff on 16 Oct driven by Goldman's warnings on debt-financed AI capex. So: risk-on in labour and credit levels, risk-off in duration and high-multiple equity. **Stance: transitional, tilting risk-off for long-duration growth.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates / duration — STRONG NEGATIVE.** NFLX is not a leveraged business (net debt/EBITDA <1x, IG-rated, long-dated fixed coupons), so this is *purely* a discount-rate effect on the multiple. The evidence is unambiguous: the stock is **$70.53 (16 Oct), −47% from its $133.91 close on 30 Jun 2025 and −41% y/y**, while EPS has compounded at ~25–30%. Over two years the price is essentially flat ($67.53 on 3 Sep 2024), i.e. **the entire earnings growth has been absorbed by multiple compression** — from roughly 40x to ~21–22x my FY26E EPS (~$3.2–3.4 post-split). A 100bp move in the discount rate on a 15–20 year cash-flow stream is worth ~13–17% of fair value; the 87bp real-rate rise mechanically explains most of the de-rating. P/S is now ~5.7x on FY26 revenue guidance of ~$51B.

**Currency — NEGATIVE and worsening.** Roughly half of revenue is non-US (EMEA, LATAM, APAC), billed in local currency. The broad dollar is **+3.0% since 11 May and +3.2% in the last month alone** as the Fed hikes while most DM central banks hold. A 3% USD appreciation is worth ~1.5% of total reported revenue at the margin — a direct translation headwind into the **20 October Q3 print and Q4 guidance**, and a margin drag in EM where local-currency ARPU buys less.

**Inflation / pricing power — MIXED, net modest POSITIVE.** CPI at 3.7% gives NFLX cover to raise prices without a real-terms outcry, supporting nominal ARPU growth — its primary revenue lever now that subscriber growth in UCAN/EMEA is saturating. But the real-income squeeze caps how far it can push before churn rises in price-sensitive LATAM/APAC, and content cost inflation (services/labour) is running at or above the 3% core rate.

**Consumer / labour — POSITIVE but decelerating.** 4.2% unemployment and +6.1% nominal consumer spending underpin low churn on a ~$10–20/month discretionary item. Subscription video is among the stickiest discretionary categories. Watch item: the 6% staff layoffs and slowing engagement reported in October are company-specific, not macro.

**Advertising cycle — NEGATIVE at the margin.** The ad tier is NFLX's second growth engine and is the most cyclical part of the P&L. Nominal GDP ~5.5% supports ad budgets, but real growth has slowed from 2.64% to 2.19%, and the 16 Oct AI/software valuation reversal signals advertisers and investors turning more cautious on growth spending.

**Fiscal / term premium — NEGATIVE (structural).** 30Y at 5.37% with ~$1.05trn net interest cost is a supply-and-term-premium story, not a growth story. It keeps the long end elevated even if the Fed stops, permanently raising the equity hurdle rate for long-duration names and compressing the multiples of exactly the cohort NFLX trades in.

**Refinancing risk — NEUTRAL.** Immaterial. Long-dated fixed IG debt against ~$12–13bn EBITDA and a $294bn cap. Do not conflate NFLX with rate-sensitive balance-sheet businesses.

**Tariffs / trade — NEUTRAL directly, NEGATIVE indirectly.** Digital content faces no tariffs; exposure runs only through tariff-driven CPI feeding the Fed's hiking bias, plus EU digital-levy/regulation risk.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating — **UNFAVOURABLE**

Four of the five dominant macro variables (real yields, policy direction, dollar, term premium) are moving against this stock simultaneously, and the fifth (nominal consumer spending) is merely supportive. The offsetting positives — tight-ish credit, 4.2% unemployment, inflation providing pricing cover, and a de-rated multiple — are real but secondary. The macro headwind is **valuation-led, not fundamental**: earnings are fine, the discount rate is not.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **A "One-and-Done" Fed Unwind (Bull, ~35% probability, +25–30%)**

The market has ~78–90bp of *additional* hikes priced into the 2Y at 4.65% versus a 3.87% post-hike funds rate — an aggressively hawkish baseline. But the Fed hiked into *decelerating* core momentum: core PCE is running ~2.0–2.1% annualised over three months, and the 10Y breakeven has *fallen* to 2.35% through the whole episode. If Q3 GDP prints below 1.5% and unemployment ticks to 4.4–4.5%, September's hike is retrospectively read as a policy error, the 2027 hike premium unwinds, the 10Y falls to ~4.5% and the 10Y real yield to ~2.2%. On that path NFLX re-rates from ~21x to ~27–28x FY26E **with no change to a single fundamental assumption** — a 25–30% move driven purely by the discount rate, compounded by a weaker dollar adding ~1.5% to reported revenue.

**Bear tail (~20%): term-premium accident.** If the 30Y breaks 6% on fiscal supply and AI-capex debt fears push HY OAS from 3.23% toward 4.5%, the high-beta end of the market de-rates again and NFLX tests the $60s irrespective of the 20 Oct result.

**Triggers to watch:** NFLX Q3 results **20 October** (options pricing a move toward a two-year low; the stock has already gapped −8% on the July guide and −5% on 18 Sep); the late-October FOMC; and the next CPI/core PCE prints for any confirmation of the disinflation that would validate the bull case.
