## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Correlation — the strongest argument for owning it.** Verified 252-day correlations: SPY 0.27, PNR 0.17, IEX 0.28, PH 0.27, FLS 0.22. XYL is **almost uncorrelated with both the market and its own water peers.** It is genuinely additive to a correlated book.

⚠️ **But read that correctly:** low correlation + R² 0.07 means ~93% of XYL's variance is **idiosyncratic**. This is not a "low-beta defensive" — it delivered a 34.5% drawdown in a still-expanding economy and had a −38% YTD. **Size it on its 31% vol as a single-name risk position, not on its 0.64 beta.**

**Factor exposure:**
- **Negative momentum tilt** — bottom-quintile 12–1 (−16%). If the book already holds de-rated cyclicals, XYL compounds that tilt.
- **Adds long-duration / rate-sensitive capex exposure.** XYL's customers finance 30-year assets with munis at a 5.37% 30Y. If the portfolio holds utilities, REITs, or other rate-sensitive infrastructure, **XYL is a fourth expression of the same factor.** Check this before adding.
- **Positive quality/FCF tilt** — 96% FCF conversion, IG balance sheet. Consistent with a quality sleeve.
- **Small negative earnings-revision tilt** (−0.39% fwd EPS).

**Sector concentration:** Industrials / Industrial Machinery. If the sector is already at or above benchmark, XYL's low peer correlation (0.17–0.28 vs PNR/IEX/PH) means it adds **diversification within** the sector rather than concentration — a rare case where adding to an overweight sector is defensible, provided the *rate-duration* factor overlap is checked separately.

**Portfolio role:** **Optionality/special-situation holding** — a stored backlog ($5.5bn, +13% YoY, book-to-bill >1.0) whose conversion is gated on a macro variable (long-end yields), not on execution. It should sit in a "catalyst-driven, defined-risk" bucket, **not** in the core-compounder bucket, until ROIC clears 11%.
