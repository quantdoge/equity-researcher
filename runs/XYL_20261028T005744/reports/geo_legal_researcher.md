This **reduces non-US revenue exposure** over time — a modest de-risking of the geographic profile, but it also removes a growth buffer against soft Western Europe.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US–China trade escalation and China's new trade-secret/investment retaliation regime
- **Probability: High (75%). Impact: Medium.**
- Tariffs are already a live P&L item: Q3-2026 adjusted EPS excluded **"the impact of IEEPA tariff refunds,"** signalling Xylem is actively managing tariff cost/refund flows (Q3-2026 release). The 10-K flags "tariffs" among manufacturing-cost risks and "nationalism, protectionism and geopolitical tensions."
- **New escalation vector:** In late Oct-2026 China's MOFCOM proposed rules allowing it to restrict or ban foreign entities from new China investment, imports/exports, and trade in goods/technologies in retaliation for alleged trade-secret infringement (Reuters, 26-Oct-2026; SCMP; Dow Jones Risk Journal). This is a novel, discretionary lever that could impair a foreign industrial manufacturer's China operations with little due process.
- Watch item: any re-escalation of tariff rates (average statutory US tariff rate has risen from ~2.3% in Jan-2025 to ~6.7% by Jul-2026 — Penn Wharton) pressures Xylem's ~$9B cost base.

### Risk 2 — China–Taiwan / semiconductor and component supply-chain disruption
- **Probability: Low–Medium (20–25%). Impact: High.**
- The 10-K **explicitly names China–Taiwan tensions** as a risk that "could disrupt the global supply chain for semiconductors and other components critical to Xylem's products." Xylem's smart-metering and Xylem Vue digital/connected-product lines are the most semiconductor-dependent (Measurement & Control Solutions segment).
- This is the single largest *tail* risk: a blockade or quarantine scenario would hit the highest-margin, fastest-growing part of the portfolio (data centers, microelectronics, smart metering) — precisely where management is redirecting capital.

### Risk 3 — Western European demand and regulatory drag
- **Probability: High (already occurring). Impact: Medium.**
- Western Europe (20% of revenue) is the identified weak spot: 2025 Water Infrastructure organic growth came from the US and emerging markets **"despite lower demand in Western Europe."** Municipal water capex in Europe is ratepayer- and fiscally-constrained.
- Compounding regulatory load: EU environmental/water-quality rules, GDPR and the Cyber Resilience Act applying to connected products, plus the EU's evolving stance on foreign subsidies and public procurement. Cumulative compliance cost is rising faster than European revenue.

**Honourable mention:** Middle East conflict/shipping-lane risk and LatAm currency controls (notably Argentina) inside the 14% emerging-markets bucket; and FX translation exposure to EUR, SEK and CAD, which the 10-K names explicitly.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Environmental remediation (the largest quantified legal contingency).** Xylem states it is "responsible, or alleged to be responsible, for ongoing environmental investigation and remediation of sites in **various countries**," and is "currently engaged in site investigations and remediation activities to address environmental cleanup from past operations" (2025 10-K; 2024 proxy). These are legacy, largely inherited from ITT/Exoqua-era manufacturing. This is a chronic, multi-decade liability — typically well-reserved and slow-moving, but it carries the theoretical tail of a **materially under-reserved site**. Require the specific ARO/accrual figure from the 10-K contingencies note before relying on it.

**Securities litigation / shareholder investigation.** Following the 2026 guidance reductions (FY2026 revenue now ~$8.95B, down ~1% reported vs. prior +2%; adjusted EPS cut to $5.40–5.45 from $5.55–5.70), **Rosen Law Firm** and **Levi & Korsinsky** have each opened investigations into whether Xylem adequately disclosed project-delay and cautious-customer-spending risks ahead of the guidance cut. At this stage these are *investigations*, not filed class actions — but this is a live, watchable escalation path. Class-period exposure would likely centre on the H1-2026 guidance window.

**Trade/tariff litigation.** The mention of **IEEPA tariff refunds** in adjusted EPS indicates Xylem is a participant in (or beneficiary of) the ongoing litigation over IEEPA-based tariffs. This is a two-way risk: refunds are upside, but an adverse ruling on the underlying tariff authority could restore full tariff costs.

**FCPA / anti-corruption — historical, resolved.** A prior SEC/DOJ review of **Xylem South Korea** practices (broadened to include FCPA compliance by the subsidiary and its employees) was closed with the **SEC declining enforcement action (disclosed Oct-2015; Stanford FCPA database)**. No current FCPA/DOJ matter is disclosed. The legacy fact of a Korean subsidiary FCPA review, combined with a 14% emerging-markets revenue base, argues for continued elevated internal-controls scrutiny in MEA, LatAm and Asia-Pacific municipal contracting (a sector historically prone to intermediary and tender-related corruption risk).

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

## **MEDIUM — at the low end of the band**

**Rationale for the rating:**
- **Mitigants:** 58% US revenue in the world's deepest, most legally predictable water-infrastructure market; no sanctioned-country exposure; no active DOJ/SEC enforcement action; environmental liabilities appear legacy and managed; and the announced Applied Water International divestiture *further* reduces non-US exposure.
- **Aggravants:** 20% Western Europe into a soft demand and rising-compliance backdrop; 14% emerging markets carrying FX, political and tender-integrity risk; an explicit 10-K acknowledgment of **China–Taiwan semiconductor supply-chain** vulnerability in the highest-growth product lines; live tariff/IEEPA cost-and-litigation dynamics; and newly opened securities investigations following the 2026 guidance cut.

**Positioning in context:** For an industrial with ~42% international revenue, XYL screens as a **relatively clean geopolitical story** — cleaner than diversified multi-industrials with heavy China manufacturing or sanctioned-market exposure. The risk is not existential; it is a margin-and-multiple risk (tariffs, European softness) plus one genuine tail (Taiwan/semiconductor). The securities-investigation thread is the item most likely to generate negative headlines in the next 2–4 quarters.

**What would change the rating:**
- *Upgrade to Low:* resolution of securities investigations without filings + evidence of China sourcing diversification + tariff refunds crystallising.
- *Downgrade to High:* a Taiwan Strait disruption event, adoption of China's trade-secret retaliation rules applied to a US industrial, or an FCPA/DOJ inquiry into an emerging-markets subsidiary.
