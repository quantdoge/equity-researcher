### 1. ESG Materiality Map — what actually moves SITM's cash flows

| Factor | Materiality | Why it hits the P&L / WACC |
|---|---|---|
| **Supply-chain & Scope 3 (foundry dependency)** | **High** | Fabless: wafers from Bosch and TSMC. Q3'26 call flagged **wafer constraints persisting through FY2027**. This is simultaneously an E (Scope 3 accounting, foundry water/energy) and operational risk. Both foundries are comparatively strong on carbon (Bosch Scope 1&2 carbon-neutral since 2020; TSMC targeting 60% renewables by 2030, net zero 2050) — which caps environmental risk but transfers **disclosure** risk to SiTime. |

| **Human capital & key-person concentration** | **High (S)** | 441 FTEs pre-deal; founder-CEO with 20+ yr tenure; scarce MEMS/analog engineering talent in a tight market. Post-Renesas TPD integration roughly doubles the workforce (integration details not disclosed — gap). |
| **Product quality & safety (S)** | **High (S)** | Auto (AEC-Q100), medical, and aerospace/defence end markets. A field failure or recall is disproportionately damaging in auto qualification. |
| **Governance: founder control & capital allocation** | **High (G)** | Combined Chair/CEO, classified board, and a transformational ~$3B+ Renesas timing acquisition executed at a cyclical peak. |
| **Geopolitical / export controls** | **Medium-High** | 10-K carries Entity List / export-licence language; distributor customers each >10% of revenue; Taiwan/HK operational footprint. |
| **Conflict minerals & supplier labour** | **Medium** | Form SD filed; Conflict Minerals Policy, Supplier Code of Conduct, Global Human Rights Policy in place. |
| **Own-site emissions, water, community** | **Low** | Asset-light, no fabs owned at scale, negligible local community footprint. |

**Key insight:** for SITM the *financially* material ESG items are **S and G, not E**. The environmental profile is genuinely benign; the money risk sits in human capital and in a governance structure that concentrates decision rights in a founder during a high-beta expansion.

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- Fabless model means Scope 1/2 are immaterial relative to a $12.5B market cap; no manufacturing pollution, spill, or remediation exposure identified.
- **Disclosure gap:** the 2025 Sustainability Report highlights foundry partners' targets and "expanding our environmental reporting" but **publishes no quantified Scope 1/2/3 inventory and no SBTi-validated reduction target**. For a company crossing a $1B+ revenue run-rate, this is below best practice and creates customer-driven (Big-3 hyperscaler) and soon regulatory reporting pressure.
- Physical climate risk is low at SiTime's own sites but **concentrated in the Taiwan foundry/OSAT cluster** (typhoon, seismic, water-stress) — a supply-continuity issue that management already acknowledges via wafer constraints.
- No environmental fines, spills, or remediation liabilities identified in the review period.

**Verdict:** Low environmental risk, medium *disclosure* immaturity. Remediable at low cost.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Key-person risk (elevated):** founder-CEO Rajesh Vashist serves as both Chair and CEO of a ~440-person (pre-deal) organisation. Succession depth is the single largest un-hedged social risk.
- **Integration risk (elevated, new):** first full quarter of Renesas TPD contributions drove Q3'26 revenue to $298.7M. Workforce/culture integration of an acquired division across Japan and other geographies is a live retention risk in a competitive engineering labour market — and is **not yet evidenced** in published ESG reporting.
- **Product safety:** automotive/medical/aerospace qualification raises the cost of failure. No recalls or safety incidents identified.
- **Supply-chain labour:** Asian foundry/OSAT exposure; mitigated by Bosch/TSMC standards and company human-rights and supplier codes. No forced-labour or UFLPA-type allegations found.
- **Positive:** people-centric culture messaging, EHS policy, no union/labour disputes, no discrimination or harassment controversies surfaced.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.5 / 10** — adequate, structurally founder-weighted

**Positives**
- **Majority-independent board** with a designated **lead independent director (Raman K. Chitkara, since Nov-2019)**.
- **Single share class** — 26,396,828 shares outstanding, one vote per share, **no dual-class structure, no poison pill identified**. Genuinely shareholder-friendly on this axis.
- Active committees (Audit 7 meetings, Compensation & Talent 5, Nominating/Governance 6 in 2025); Deloitte & Touche as auditor; **compensation clawback policy**; stock-ownership guidelines; insider-trading restrictions; documented Corporate Governance Guidelines and Code of Conduct.
- Recent board refresh with credible independent technologists/operators (e.g., Ganesh Moorthy, ex-Microchip CEO; Akira Takata; Torsten Kreindl).

**Negatives**
- **Combined Chair/CEO** — the founder holds both roles; the lead-independent-director counterweight is structurally weaker than an independent chair.
- **Classified (staggered) board** — only three Class I directors stand in 2026, each serving to 2029. This entrenches management and delays shareholder ability to reshape the board.
- **Virtual-only annual meeting** — damps shareholder engagement and question quality.
- **Capital allocation / compensation alignment:** a large, debt-and-equity-funded acquisition at peak-cycle valuations, plus heavy equity compensation in a 2.9-beta stock, creates dilution and "growth-at-any-cost" incentive skew. Say-on-pay outcome for the 2026 meeting was not retrievable (gap); the proxy is at least responsive to prior stockholder feedback on pay.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US:** SEC climate-disclosure rule (adoption status contested/stayed — monitor); **California SB 253/261** is the live exposure — a $1B+ run-rate company doing business in California likely crosses the SB 253 revenue threshold, pulling it into **Scope 1/2/3 assurance-grade reporting**. Given no published inventory today, this is SITM's single most concrete regulatory ESG gap. **Conflict Minerals Rule (Form SD)** — currently compliant.
- **Export controls:** EAR Entity-List licensing language in the 10-K; China revenue and outbound-investment rules are a compliance cost and a demand-risk channel.
- **EU:** RoHS/REACH and Ecodesign/ESPR product rules apply; **CSRD** applicability depends on EU revenue (likely below the €150M EU-turnover trigger at present — to be confirmed). **CBAM: not applicable** (no carbon-intensive goods imports).
- **Sector/product regimes:** IATF 16949, AEC-Q100, ISO 26262 functional safety, AS9100 for aerospace/defence — higher compliance burden but well-managed.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified in the materials reviewed (balance sheet described as debt-light pre-acquisition; acquisition financing terms not verified — gap).

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Structurally low-impact business model, no controversy record, and standard US large-cap governance hygiene. Held back from "Leader" by **quantified-disclosure absence** (no GHG inventory, no climate target), a **founder-concentrated, staggered board**, and **unreported post-acquisition human-capital metrics**.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** for the thesis

- **ESG is not a valuation driver here, in either direction.** No exclusion-list problem, no controversy discount, no green premium of consequence.
- **Where ESG *does* bite is through risk to the AI-growth narrative** — and the framing matters. The two most underappreciated non-financial risks are (i) **supply-chain concentration risk through FY2027** (Taiwan foundry/OSAT, wafer allocation) and (ii) **key-person and integration risk** following the Renesas TPD acquisition. Both are classified as "S" and "operational," both are real, and neither is priced in a 33x forward multiple.
- **Governance watch item for the IC:** the combined Chair/CEO plus staggered board means shareholders have limited near-term levers if capital allocation on the acquisition disappoints. That deserves a **valuation-haircut / position-sizing consideration**, not an ESG exclusion.
- **Actionable engagement agenda (if we are a holder):** (1) publish a Scope 1/2/3 inventory with third-party assurance ahead of California SB 253; (2) set a science-based target covering foundry-attributed Scope 3; (3) separate Chair and CEO as Vashist's successor planning matures; (4) de-stagger the board; (5) disclose post-integration workforce retention and safety metrics.

**Bottom line:** SITM screens as an **ESG-neutral holding** — clean on controversy, benign on environmental intensity, adequate but founder-weighted on governance. Treat ESG as a **risk-monitoring overlay on supply chain and human capital**, not as a thesis driver or a disqualifier.
