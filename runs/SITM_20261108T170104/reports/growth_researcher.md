# SiTime Corporation (SITM) — Growth Investment Analysis

*Pricing as of Nov 6, 2026 close ($416.92). Note: a **3-for-1 stock split is effective Dec 7, 2026** — all per-share figures below are pre-split; divide by 3 for post-split equivalent (≈$139).*

---

## 1. Growth Story

SiTime is executing the most under-appreciated platform shift in semis: replacing cut quartz crystals with silicon MEMS resonators in the clock/timing chain. Quartz is analog — each frequency needs its own fragile cut, and it degrades under heat, shock and vibration. MEMS timing is programmable, shrinks with process nodes, and now sells at a *price premium* as AI clusters demand sync-grade timing across accelerators, 200G/400G optics, switches and active electrical cables. The $1.5B acquisition of Renesas' timing business (TPD, ~$300M revenue at ~70% GM, 550+ clocking products) converts SiTime from a niche challenger into a full-line timing vendor at exactly the moment AI infrastructure content per system is inflecting. Q3 2026 revenue of $298.7M (+257% Y/Y) put the company above a **$1B annualized run-rate** — the first time SiTime has operated at this scale.

## 3. TAM and Penetration

- Timing market: **$20–28B by 2030** (Diligence Stack/Bajarin base case), with **AI data center = 40–50%** of it. That compares to the legacy ~$10B analog timing market SiTime sells into today.
- SiTime at ~$1.35B run-rate holds **~5–7% of 2030's TAM** — an unusually long runway for a company already at $1B+ revenue.
- Content math is the real driver: 2025–30 base case sees accelerators grow ~4x, optical link ends ~7x, switch capacity ~16x, and AECs ~15x — i.e. **timing content grows faster than compute**. Sync-grade parts sell at several *times* the price of a standard oscillator, and co-packaged optics migrates timing from the module into the switch at several times today's content.
- Post-TPD, SiTime is the only vendor with MEMS IP *and* a complete oscillator/clock-IC/TCXO/OCXO line — the moat is process + mixed-signal design + 15+ years of MEMS reliability data, hard to replicate.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (high conviction, size for volatility)

The setup is compelling: 70%+ non-GAAP gross margin, 43% operating margin, >$1B run-rate, 100% beat rate, upward revisions, a $20–28B 2030 TAM with <7% penetration, and a multiple (25x FY27 EPS, 8.9x forward sales) at a ~50% discount to AI-connectivity peers growing slower. Consensus FY2027/FY2028 numbers embed a sharp deceleration that the Q4 guide does not support — that gap is the upside. I stop short of Strong Buy because the multiple still capitalizes years of perfect execution, GAAP earnings are thin, and beta of 2.9 means every AI-capex headline is a ±20% move.

## 7. Key Risks

- **Cyclicality is proven, not theoretical:** revenue fell 49% in 2023 as consumer/mobile inventory corrected. The TPD deal adds mature, lower-growth legacy revenue (industrial/auto/comms) that can mask core momentum.
- **AI capex concentration:** with timing content tied to switch/optics/AEC scaling, a single hyperscaler digestion pause could compress growth from 55% to low-double-digits quickly.
- **Integration & balance sheet:** $4.16B of goodwill + intangibles against ~$340M new debt; diluted shares up ~18% (26.3M → 31.2M). Write-down risk if TPD's legacy business erodes.
- **Margin mix optics:** the 70.7% non-GAAP GM benefits from AI-datacenter mix; blending in $300M of TPD revenue plus rising SBC (~13% of revenue) could stall the expansion narrative even as revenue grows.
- **Working capital / FCF:** $126M Q3 WC build and negative FCF leave little cushion if demand normalizes — inventory risk is real.
- **Competition:** Microchip is the incumbent volume leader; Epson, TXC, Kyocera/NDK and Rakon defend quartz sockets on price and qualification inertia. Design-win cycles are long; converting analog design engineers is a sales-motion bet, not a given.
- **Data caveats:** FMP diluted share count is stale (24.97M vs actual ~31.2M), so the vendor-reported P/E and P/B are unreliable; goodwill/intangible detail is from the Q3 release, not the feed. Peer multiples are single-source.
