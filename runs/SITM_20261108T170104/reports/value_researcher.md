# SiTime Corporation (SITM) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price $416.92 (Nov 6, 2026) | Market cap ~$12.5B basic / $13.7B diluted | EV ~$13.5–14.7B | Net debt $995M**

*A note on scale: SiTime reported Q3'26 on Nov 4, 2026 and announced a 3-for-1 split (record Dec 3, 2026). All figures below are pre-split. The company closed the ~$4B acquisition of Renesas' Timing Products Division (TPD) in Q3'26, so FY26 reported figures mix one quarter of TPD with a much smaller organic base. FY2025 and earlier are pre-deal.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow** (and widening, but not yet proven durable)

SiTime designs silicon MEMS timing devices (oscillators, clock generators, jitter cleaners) that displace quartz crystals. The value proposition is real and I understand it: better stability, smaller size, higher reliability, programmable, and — critically — more timing content per system as optical speeds rise (800G → 1.6T: XPO/NPO/CPO architectures drive up to 2–3x more oscillator content per module).

**Evidence for the moat:**
- Non-GAAP gross margin of **70.7%** in Q3'26 (up 1,190bps YoY) — pricing power consistent with a differentiated franchise, not a commodity.
- Share in 800G optical modules reportedly quadrupled; management expects further share gains at 1.6T.
- A reported **$10B total sales funnel** ($4B semiconductor funnel, $900M A&D, $1B auto, $1.4B industrial). Even heavily discounted, this implies a large runway against a ~$1.34B annualized run-rate.
- TPD brings the incumbent "clocking" brand (Renesas/IDT heritage), converting SiTime from an oscillator niche player into a full-suite timing vendor — a genuinely stronger competitive position.

**Evidence against a wide moat:**
- **Top-3 distributors = 59% of FY25 revenue**, and in Q3'26 a single large consumer customer contributed **$42.7M, or 14% of revenue**. That is a lumpy, concentrated customer base, not a subscription-like franchise.
- Revenue fell **-49% in 2023**. A wide-moat business does not lose half its revenue in a normal inventory correction.
- Quartz incumbents (Epson, Kyocera, TXC, Microchip, Renesas itself) are large, well-capitalized and can price defensively. SiTime's gross margin improved on *mix*, not price (management said so explicitly) — that cuts both ways when mix reverses.
- Four-year average ROIC is **-8.6%**, with no single year above 2.3%. The single most reliable hallmark of a moated business — sustained ROIC above cost of capital — is absent here. The technology moat is real; the *economic* moat is not yet demonstrated through a full cycle.

**Verdict: Narrow moat, directionally widening.** The business is better today than in 2023, but I would not underwrite a 20-year competitive advantage.

**What is excellent:** the *gross* margin structure (70%+ non-GAAP) and the operating leverage — non-GAAP operating margin went from 18% to 43% in a single year on 3x revenue. There is genuine incremental profitability here.

**What is poor, and it matters:**
- **Cumulative 2022–2025 FCF was just $21.6M against $330M of SBC.** Over four years this business consumed capital while compensating itself in stock. FCF/NI conversion is effectively meaningless because GAAP earnings have been negative.
- **SBC is ~15% of revenue** (~$140M/yr run-rate) — real, recurring dilution that non-GAAP EPS cheerfully ignores. At 32.8M diluted shares, that is roughly **$4.30/share per year** of economic cost.
- **2026 cash conversion has deteriorated badly.** 9M'26 operating cash flow was $64.4M against ~$300M of non-GAAP net income. Q3 alone was **-$6.5M OCF** driven by a $126M working-capital drag, inventory up to $112M, and DSO up 10 days to 61. Some of this is deliberate (supply constraints through 2027 → pre-building inventory). But until FCF converts, earnings quality is unproven.
- **Balance sheet has flipped from fortress to levered.** Cash fell from $1.92B (Jun'26) to $324M; total debt is now **$1.32B** against $995M net debt. Goodwill and intangibles are **$4.16B, or 83% of total assets**; tangible book value is negative. Equity of $3.49B is almost entirely accounting intangibles.

A 70%-gross-margin business earning negative ROIC and burning cash is a 5/10, not a 8/10 — however exciting the top line.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples (price $416.92):**
- ~33x FY2026E non-GAAP EPS (~$12.55, using Q4 guide of $4.76)
- ~23–24x FY2027E non-GAAP EPS (~$17.50–18.00 on my estimates, vs. street $14.93 which looks stale given a $4.76 Q4 run-rate)
- ~32x FY2027E **owner earnings** (i.e., after deducting SBC) of ~$450M
- EV/2027E revenue ~9x; FCF yield ~3.3% on after-SBC owner earnings
- GAAP TTM P/E is meaningless (~470x)

**Intrinsic value (base case, WACC 11%, terminal growth 3.5%):** I model 2027 owner earnings of ~$450M growing 25%/yr for five years, fading to 12%, and discount. That yields roughly **$480–560/share**. A DCF using the company's own tool defaults (30% growth, 10 years) is not usable — but note it produced a similar direction. My bear case (AI digestion + a 2023-style inventory correction, 10% growth, lower terminal) produces **$190–280**. Bull case, with the $4B semiconductor funnel converting: **$850–950**.

> **Intrinsic value: ~$520 base case (range $250–$900). Price at $416.92 is ~20% below base-case intrinsic value — inside the zone where I'd start paying attention, but only barely, and the dispersion of outcomes is enormous.**

Two honest caveats: (1) a DCF on a company whose revenue halved in 2023 and tripled in 2026 is a guess dressed in arithmetic — the terminal value assumption drives ~70% of the answer; (2) peer multiples were not useful here — the tool's "sector peers" are $500B+ mega-caps (NVDA, AVGO, TSM) with a median P/E of 38x, which tells me nothing about the right multiple for a $13B cyclical semiconductor with a 14%-of-revenue customer.

**What the price implies:** you are paying today for ~20–25% annual owner-earnings growth for a decade. That is plausible given the funnel, but it is not a discount — it is the base case, purchased at full price.
