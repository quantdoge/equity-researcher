### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NONE to NARROW**

HPE is two businesses stitched together, and only one of them has anything resembling a moat.

| Segment (Q4 FY26) | Revenue | Growth | Moat character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Server / Cloud & AI | $8.8bn | +94.7% | **None.** Commodity assembly, Nvidia-dependent, zero pricing power, working-capital sink |
| Networking (Aruba + Juniper) | $3.1bn | +12.3% | **Narrow.** Enterprise switching/Wi-Fi switching costs, installed base, but Cisco & Arista set the ceiling |

Evidence against a durable moat:
- **ROIC has been below WACC for three of four years**: 6.95% (FY23), 5.45% (FY24), 3.37% (FY25), 9.86% (FY26). WACC is ~9–10% given beta 1.47 and $18.6bn of debt. A four-year average ROIC of ~6.4% against a ~9.5% WACC is the signature of a business that has been destroying, not creating, capital.
- **Revenue growth was ~5.3% CAGR organically FY21–FY25** ($27.9bn → $34.3bn) — the FY26 36% jump is Juniper consolidation (closed July 2025) plus an AI server shortage, not a competitive franchise widening.
- **Operating margins have swung 2.7% → 8.3% → 4.8% → 9.8%** over five years. Great businesses do not do this.

The moat has **narrowed** in real terms: HPE has traded a services-led, annuity-heavy mix (post-DXC spinoff) for a leveraged, cyclical hardware assembly model where Nvidia captures the economics and ODM/white-box vendors cap the spread. What management calls "a fundamentally different company" is, economically, a bigger, lower-quality one.

*FY26 margins flattered by full-year Juniper software mix and shortage-driven pricing.

**What is genuinely good:** FY26 FCF/net income of 87% clears the 80% bar; interest coverage is ~22x on EBIT; management is targeting ≥75% of FY27 FCF returned to shareholders; the dividend was just raised 19% to $0.68 annualized.

**What is bad:**
- **FCF is volatile and historically thin.** Five-year FCF totals $9.5bn — an average of $1.9bn, a 3.6% average yield on today's market cap. FY25 FCF was $0.63bn.
- **Negative tangible book value**: $27.6bn equity minus $29.2bn goodwill/intangibles = **–$1.6bn**. 106% of book is acquisition accounting.
- **Working capital is the business model.** Inventory $14.1bn (+122% YoY), receivables $10.9bn, FY26 working-capital drag of –$2.3bn. Growing revenue 30% in FY27 will consume a further $2–3bn before it produces a dollar of cash.
- **Current ratio 1.05**; net debt $12.9bn (1.5x EBITDA).
- **Dilution despite buybacks**: diluted shares 1.322bn (FY22) → 1.445bn (FY26), +9.3%, while spending $2.5bn on repurchases. Buybacks are SBC offset, not compounding.
