## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — AI export-control tightening gates the growth engine. (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High)**
Commerce issued an **interim final rule (16-Nov-2026)** requiring licences for exports/reexports of advanced AI items where a data centre anywhere will supply **>12,000,000 aggregate TPP** to China-linked users, closing the "remote access" loophole (Commerce; Reuters 16-Nov-2026; Katten; TechCrunch). HPE's record AI backlog and its "sovereign AI factory" positioning depend on deploying NVIDIA-based systems into third-country data centres. Every such deal now carries end-user/end-use licensing risk and elongated sales cycles — a risk HPE itself newly flags ("Long sales and implementation cycles… large AI-system sales to a historically small customer base," new FY2025 risk factor).

**Risk 2 — Tariff stacking on a Mexico-dependent hardware supply chain. (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
A **25% Section 232 tariff on certain semiconductors and derivative products took effect 15-Jan-2026** (Presidential Proclamation, 14-Jan-2026; CBP guidance). Simultaneously, the administration is preparing a fresh tariff round against **China, the EU, Mexico and Japan** (WSJ/NY Post, 24-Nov-2026). HPE's server assembly concentration in Mexico means the tariff channel runs through both inputs (chips) and finished goods (servers). This is consistent with FY2027 guidance for Server segment operating margin falling from **18.4% to ~14%**, and with management's own language on "uncertainty and volatility" (CRN, HPE COO Mark Bakker).

**Risk 3 — Component chokepoints and the fragility of the US-China truce. (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium-High)**
China extended its suspension of export bans on **gallium, germanium and antimony** only until January (Reuters/WSJ, 24-Nov-2026), while a US Section 301 "excess capacity" probe could add new duties (Barron's, 24-Nov-2026). HPE is already inventory-loading against shortages — **$11.8B of inventory at end-FYQ3 2026** (Supply Chain Dive) — and cited "persistent component shortages" alongside its raised FY2027 outlook (Zacks, 24-Nov-2026). A truce breakdown would hit both bill-of-materials cost and revenue timing simultaneously.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **HPE/Juniper antitrust — the dominant legal contingency (still live).** DOJ sued to block the $14B deal on 30-Jan-2025; a settlement was filed **27-Jun-2025** requiring divestiture of HPE's **Instant On** WLAN business and licensing of **Juniper Mist AI** software to rivals (DOJ press release; Reuters, 28-Jun-2025). **13 state AGs moved to intervene**, joined by California AG Bonta and others alleging a politically corrupted approval process (Duane Morris; oag.ca.gov; Oregon DOJ). Judge P. Casey Pitts **denied the states' request to enjoin** the merger (Gibson Dunn), but House Judiciary Democrats are pressing for court review — meaning **ongoing appellate, monitorship and political-oversight risk** around a deal central to HPE's networking franchise. Fourteen foreign authorities (incl. EC and UK CMA) cleared the deal.
- **New "legal proceedings" risk factor added in FY2025.** The FY2025 10-K (33 risk factors, 4 new) newly warns that "legal claims and government investigations across areas such as **antitrust, privacy, intellectual property, and trade**" could result in substantial costs (Gloom, FY2025 10-K analysis). Trade is now explicitly named — a shift from FY2024.
- **IP litigation is chronic but individually immaterial.** Settled: Oracle copyright (post-$30M verdict, Jan-2023); Network-1 ($17M); a Chinese NPE (Beijing IP Finding Management) global settlement. Ongoing NPE activity includes Valtrus (patents acquired from HPE) and networking-patent suits. HPE appears on patent-defendant leaderboards with a ~94.5% favourable-outcome rate (Patexia 2026), which mitigates severity.
- **No disclosed DOJ/SEC/FCPA investigation in the FY2025 10-K.** The **2022 10-K Item 3** language is generic ("we are involved in various claims, suits, investigations"). **Gap:** I could not retrieve the full Item 3/Note litigation text (SEC HTML snippet only; the 10-K PDF would not parse), so I cannot rule out disclosed-but-unquantified matters.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM** (trending up)

**Rationale.** HPE's *political* risk is genuinely low — ~73% of revenue sits in the US, Western Europe and Japan, all high rule-of-law jurisdictions, with no nationalisation or currency-repatriation exposure of consequence. What elevates the rating is **policy risk, not polity risk**: HPE is a US-headquartered, Mexico-manufactured, NVIDIA-dependent AI hardware vendor selling into licensed end markets. Three policy vectors — export controls, tariffs, and critical-minerals access — all point the same direction and all hit FY2027 guidance, which is precisely why the FY2025 10-K added "trade tensions, tariffs, quotas, and import or export restrictions" to its business-disruption risk factor. The Juniper consent decree adds a second, slower-burn legal overhang with unusual political visibility.

**Watch items for the next 12 months:** (1) any BIS enforcement action or licence denials touching HPE AI backlog conversion; (2) the January expiry of China's critical-minerals suspension; (3) the appellate/monitorship track of *US v. HPE/Juniper* and whether state AGs obtain review; (4) the scope of the pending tariff round on Mexico and the EU.
