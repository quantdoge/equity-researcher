# Sector Brief: Semiconductors — AVGO (Broadcom Inc.)

## 1. Sector Overview

Broadcom operates at the intersection of semiconductors and infrastructure software, sitting squarely in the heart of the AI compute build-out. The semiconductor sector (~$700B+ global revenue, growing mid-to-high teens) is in an early-stage AI supercycle: hyperscaler capex on accelerators, networking, and memory is running far above historical trend, with NVIDIA, TSMC, Broadcom, AMD, Micron, and SK hynix as the key beneficiaries. The market is a segment-level oligopoly — TSMC controls >55% of leading-edge foundry, NVIDIA >80% of AI accelerators, and the big-three (Samsung/SK hynix/Micron) dominate memory — with enormous barriers to entry in capital intensity, R&D scale, and fab/ecosystem lock-in. Broadcom sits as the clear #2 in AI accelerators via custom ASICs and owns the Ethernet-switch franchise that stitches AI clusters together, plus a VMware-driven software annuity stream.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Custom AI accelerator demand (the "second AI vendor").** Hyperscalers (Google TPU, Meta, OpenAI, Apple) are routing billions in capex to Broadcom-designed custom ASICs. BofA estimates Broadcom's share of AI accelerator deployments rising from <10% in 2025 to ~15%+ in 2026–27 — a revenue line that already exceeded $1B/quarter and is growing ~50% QoQ at points.
- **AI networking content growth.** Every large-scale AI cluster needs 800G/1.6T Ethernet switching, where Broadcom is the dominant supplier — a direct tag-along to rival NVIDIA's GPU deployments.
- **High-margin recurring software (VMware).** The VMware acquisition converted Broadcom into a hybrid semis/software model, adding annuity revenue (now ~$14B+ annualized) that lifts overall margin durability and de-risks pure semis cyclicality.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Customer concentration & in-house design threat.** AI ASIC orders are concentrated in a handful of hyperscalers, and several (Google, Amazon, Meta) are actively building in-house silicon — a structural risk to the custom-ASIC franchise.
- **Geopolitics / export controls.** US–China restrictions on advanced AI chips and China demand softness constrain a sizable market; any escalation hits the wireless/RF and datacenter print.
- **Competitive squeeze and cost inflation.** NVIDIA is mobilizing against custom-silicon share, while advanced packaging/wafer costs (TSMC CoWoS) and the heavy VMware-related amortization pressure reported margins (FY25 GAAP GM 67.8% vs 69.0% in FY23).

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Strong**

Broadcom is the best-diversified compounder in the group. On growth it sits mid-pack (+23.9% YoY vs peer range of −0.5% to +65.5%) — but that is from a far larger base than AMD/ASML, and its 3-yr revenue CAGR (~24%) compounds on top of the enormous VMware re-rating. Profitability is elite: 67.8% gross and 39.9% operating margins are second only to NVIDIA among peers, and ahead of TSMC on gross margin. It is the only major whose model blends semis with ~20%+ software annuity revenue, giving it margin stability others lack. Its main vulnerabilities — hyperscaler concentration and exposure to NVDA's ecosystem dominance — are real but are partially offset by switching-cost lock-in in switching silicon. It is gaining share within AI accelerators (10%→15%+) while retaining #1 positions in Ethernet switching and broadband.

## 6. Sector Rating: **Attractive**

The sector is in the early-growth phase of an AI-driven multi-year capex cycle, with pricing power intact (fab utilisation high, memory upturn, NVDA/TSM/AVGO all expanding operating leverage). Risk is cyclicality and concentration, but names with structural AI exposure and pricing power — Broadcom among them — warrant a positive stance. Broadcom merits an Outperform profile within an Attractive sector.
