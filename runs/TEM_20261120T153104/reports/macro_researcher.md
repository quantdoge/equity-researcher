## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — a re-acceleration / "higher-for-longer" regime that is hostile to long-duration assets.** As of 20 Nov 2026, the US economy is running hot (Q3 real GDP +3.4% SAAR) with inflation stuck well above target (CPI +3.82% YoY, core PCE +3.12% YoY) and unemployment low at 4.1%. The Fed cut to 3.63% through August 2026, then **hiked 25bp on 17 Sep 2026 to 3.88%** (target 3.75–4.00%), held in October, and October minutes point to another increase. The long end has done the tightening: the 10Y went from 4.44% (30 Jun) to a 5.31% peak (5 Oct), now 5.13%, with the 30Y at 5.47% and the 2s10s at +39bp (bear steepening, not inversion). Credit is the one unambiguously risk-on signal — HY OAS at 3.52% is tight in absolute terms even after widening ~87bp from the 2.65% September tights. Net: **high nominal growth + sticky inflation + rising long rates + a firm dollar.**

This is close to the worst possible macro mix for a pre-profit, high-multiple, AI-story growth stock — which is exactly what Tempus AI is.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **Unfavourable**

The macro regime is working directly against TEM on the variable that matters most (long real rates / discount rate) and only mildly in its favour on volumes. The stock is a pure duration play: it de-rated ~35% from its 25 Sep high of $85.01 to $55.45 largely as the 10Y moved from 4.9% to 5.1–5.3%, despite *improving* fundamentals (Q3 revenue +23%, first real inflection after four quarters of deceleration). That divergence is the whole macro story — operating momentum is being overridden by the discount rate. Note the offsetting positive: risk appetite in credit is intact, and TEM is still +30% off its July low of $40.77, so this is de-rating, not distress.

Operationally TEM is largely insulated from FX and trade (US-centric, domestic reimbursement), so the exposure is concentrated almost entirely in **cost of capital** and, secondarily, **health-policy funding**.

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**Bear (higher probability given current momentum): "The second hike and the 5.5% 10Y."** October minutes already flag some members wanting a bigger move; if core PCE prints ≥3.2% and the Fed hikes again in December, the 10Y plausibly breaks 5.5% on term premium and deficit-supply concerns. A further +40bp on the long end, given TEM's observed ~3–4x rate beta, implies a **25–35% drawdown to the $36–41 range** (retesting the July low) with no change in operating performance. Compounding risk: a widening HY spread would close the convertible/M&A currency that funds TEM's acquisition strategy (Personalis).

**Bull (the real surprise): a disinflationary break in H1 2027.** Tariff pass-through laps, shelter cools and core PCE returns toward 2.5%, letting the Fed unwind the September hike. A 10Y back to 4.40% would mechanically restore ~60–80% of the multiple compression TEM has suffered since September; combined with 2027's $80M ASP step-up and $193M/5-yr data agreements, TEM could re-rate toward the $85 highs. The market is positioned for the opposite, which is what makes this the surprise.

**Monitoring list:** monthly core PCE and CPI prints, the December FOMC, the 10Y/30Y term premium, HY OAS (a move above ~400bp is the alarm), and CMS physician-fee/ASP rulemaking.
