## Sector Brief: Tempus AI (TEM) — Precision Oncology Diagnostics & Real-World Data

*Note on peer selection: the sector endpoint returns mega-cap pharma (LLY, JNJ, MRK), which is not a valid comparable set. I therefore constructed the peer group manually from revenue-cycle-comparable molecular diagnostics / genomics companies.*

### 1. Sector Overview

Tempus sits at the intersection of two adjacent markets: **clinical oncology next-generation sequencing (NGS)** — ~$2.61B in 2026, forecast to reach $5.46B by 2031 (15.9% CAGR, Mordor) — and **precision oncology** more broadly ($139B in 2026 → $202B by 2030, 8.1% CAGR, Grand View), of which diagnostics is the faster-growing sub-segment at ~10% CAGR. The industry is capital-intensive, regulated at the reimbursement layer rather than the product layer, and characterised by a **barbell structure**: very large diversified tools/consumables incumbents (Illumina, Thermo Fisher) and a set of high-growth, mostly loss-making clinical labs (Tempus, Natera, Guardant, Exact Sciences, NeoGenomics, Myriad). Consolidation is active — Tempus is acquiring Personalis for ~$1.5B in stock (closing late 2026) after its Feb-2025 Ambry pickup, and Personalis itself was in a sale process. The central economic feature of the sector is that **gross margins are already healthy (~63–70%) but operating margins are structurally negative**, because commercial spend (sales reps, payer navigation, physician education) is heavy and reimbursement per test is set administratively.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **NGS attach rates still have a long runway.** Oncology NGS is a ~16% CAGR market with tissue-based CGP still well under half of eligible advanced-cancer patients, and MRD/liquid biopsy is earlier still — Tempus reported MRD volumes **+300% YoY** in Q3 2026, off a small base.
- **2027 reimbursement reset is a net *tailwind* for FDA-approved tests.** The FDA/CMS rule moving approved IVDs off the CLFS onto ADLT pricing from 2027 benefits the few firms holding approved assays. Tempus guided that solid-tumour pricing changes add **$80–100M annually**, with a further **$250–300M** from the broader portfolio; the xT CDx indication alone is worth ~**$200/test** from Medicare in 2027, and Oncology ASP already ticked up from **$1,820 to $1,850**.
- **Pharma data licensing is a high-margin, capital-light second engine.** Multimodal clinico-genomic data is genuinely scarce; Tempus signed data agreements worth **$193M over five years**, and Data & Applications grew **+33% YoY** in Q3 2026 — faster than Diagnostics (+20%) — with partnerships across BMS, Moderna, Merck, AstraZeneca and GSK.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **CLFS/PAMA cuts of ~15% hit the non-approved tail of the menu.** CMS's preliminary CY2027 fee schedule shows average reductions of ~15–16%, concentrated in molecular codes, with a 15%-per-year cap applying 2027–2029. This suppresses realised pricing on legacy LDT codes and is the main reason lab margins remain fragile.
- **Buyer concentration and administrative pricing = no real pricing power.** Medicare and two-to-three national commercial payers set the price; the ~$200 ADLT uplift is a policy concession, not a competitive win, and is reversible.
- **Rivalry is intensifying in a winner-take-most market.** Guardant, Natera, Exact Sciences, NeoGenomics, Myriad plus Roche's Foundation Medicine compete for the same oncologist order flow; several are diversifying into Tempus's data niche. Consolidated peers (EXAS, NEO) show how quickly growth decays as penetration matures.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Strong (above-average on growth and moat; below-average on profitability)**

- **Positives:** fastest grower in the peer set, sole scaled owner of a bidirectional clinico-genomic data asset (a genuine network effect — more tests → better data → more pharma dollars → subsidised test economics), improving GAAP losses every quarter, and the best-positioned holder of FDA-approved assays for the 2027 ADLT repricing. Adjusted EBITDA has inflected positive (Q3'26 $20.5M).
- **Negatives:** operating margin of −19.9% in FY2025 vs peer median −8.7% — the gap is commercial spend (SG&A ~$232M/quarter on $412M revenue). Beta of **3.61** and a $9.8B cap on $1.6B revenue means the equity discounts a lot of this. It is a serial acquirer, so growth is partly purchased, and Personalis adds integration and dilution risk.
- **Porter's Five Forces:** Rivalry **High**; Buyer power **High** (administrative pricing); Supplier power **Moderate-High** (sequencing oligopoly); Entry threat **Moderate** (CAP/CLIA + FDA LDT oversight + data scale); Substitutes **Moderate** (liquid biopsy cannibalising tissue).

### 6. Sector Rating: **Attractive — but narrow and low-margin**

The end-market grows at ~8–16%, comfortably above GDP and healthcare-sector averages, with a durable secular driver (targeted therapy selection requires sequencing) and a 2027 pricing catalyst. However, **profit pool quality is poor** — the median peer loses money at the operating line and only the tools suppliers (ILMN, VCYT) and scaled lab (EXAS) earn real margins. This is a sector where you must own the volume leader, not the average participant. Tempus is the right *kind* of asset; the open question is whether ~$232M/quarter of SG&A converts into operating leverage before growth normalises toward the high-teens peer median.
