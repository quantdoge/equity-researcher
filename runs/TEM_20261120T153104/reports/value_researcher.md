## Tempus AI, Inc. (TEM) — Value Investment Analysis

**Snapshot.** Price used: **$55.45** (vendor snapshot, FY2025 10-K basis; most recent observable prints are higher — $62.33 per a Form 4 on 17 Nov 2026, and analyst notes referenced ~$78 in Nov 2026). Shares 174.3M diluted; market cap ~$9.7B; net debt $722M; **EV ~$10.2B** (≈$11.5B at $62). Fiscal data through FY2025 (10-K filed 24 Feb 2026) plus quarterly results through Q3 2026.

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: Narrow (trend: widening, but unproven by returns)**

Tempus is two businesses stapled together, and they have very different economics:

- **Diagnostics (~75-80% of revenue)** — next-gen sequencing (xT/xR), PCR, anatomic pathology, plus Ambry's germline/hereditary franchise. This is a *volume-driven laboratory business*: capital-light but reimbursement-capped, competed against Roche/Foundation Medicine, Caris, Guardant, Natera, Exact Sciences. Gross margin here is structurally mid-50s to 60s. Real advantages are scale in ordering (Tempus Hub embedded in >50% of US oncologists' workflows) and a very large in-network payer footprint.
- **Data & Insights / AI (~20-25% of revenue)** — licensing de-identified multimodal clinical/molecular/imaging data to pharma (AstraZeneca, GSK, Merck, Recursion, Pathos). This is the franchise value: 80%+ incremental gross margins, multi-year contracts, and a genuinely compounding asset.

**The moat is the data flywheel, not the tests.** Every ordered test adds a de-identified record to a repository reported at 200+ petabytes, and that corpus is what pharma pays for and what improves the algorithms. This is a real, self-reinforcing network effect — the best thing about the company. It widened materially over five years: revenue went from $258M (2021) to ~$1.6B (2026E), gross margin from 32% to 63%, and Ambry added a hereditary-cancer channel that feeds the same data engine.

**But three things stop this being a Wide moat:**

1. **The tests themselves have no pricing power.** Laboratory-developed tests under CLIA face no meaningful regulatory barrier to entry, and CMS/private payers set the price. Pricing is a function of coverage policy, not brand.
2. **ROIC is negative and has never cleared WACC.** ROIC of −13.9% (2025), −131.8% (2024), and even the optically positive 21.9%/38.1% in 2022-23 is an artifact of negative equity, not genuine returns on capital. By the test that matters — sustained ROIC > cost of capital — there is no demonstrated moat.
3. **The flywheel requires continuous external capital.** $1.33B of debt and $125M/yr of stock compensation are the price of building the dataset.

The honest framing: a narrow, widening, data-driven moat inside a structurally mediocre-margin industry.

**What earns the 4 rather than a 3:** revenue durability is genuinely impressive (no year of decline in company history), gross margin has expanded 30 points in four years, and FY2026 guidance calls for **$1.60-1.605B revenue (+26%) and ~$65M adjusted EBITDA** — the first positive EBITDA year, with Q3 2026 delivering positive GAAP EPS ($0.07).

**What caps it at 4:**

The debt is mostly 0%/0.75% converts (2030/2032), so cash interest is modest — but the dilution feature is a call option *against* shareholders.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

OPTIONS:
- **At the current price, TEM trades at ~6.4-7.2x EV/2026E sales and ~160-180x 2026E adjusted EBITDA.**

**Reverse DCF — what the price demands.** With EV of $10.2-11.5B and an 11% required return / 3% terminal growth, the market is capitalizing roughly **(EV × 8%) ≈ $820M-920M of perpetual annual free cash flow**. At a generous 15% mature FCF margin, that requires **~$5.5-6.1B of revenue** — i.e., roughly a 4x increase from FY2026E, at 15%+ FCF margins. That is achievable by the mid-2030s *if* everything goes right. Nothing is being paid for the possibility that it doesn't.

**Base-case DCF (my numbers, not the vendor's mechanical model, which is meaningless on negative trailing FCF):**

| | 2026E | 2027E | 2028E | 2030E | 2032E | 2035E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 1,600 | 1,970 | 2,400 | 3,400 | 4,550 | 6,400 |
| Growth | 26% | 23% | 22% | 18% | 15% | 11% |
| FCF ($M) | −100 | −20 | +85 | +300 | +540 | +860 |
| FCF margin | neg | neg | 3.5% | 8.8% | 11.9% | 13.5% |

Discounting at 11% with a 3% terminal growth rate: **enterprise value ≈ $5.5B → equity ≈ $4.8B → ~$27/share.** Even lifting terminal FCF margin to 18% and extending growth two extra years only reaches the **mid-$30s**.

**Bull case (the "everything works" scenario):** 22% growth through 2032, data licensing becomes the profit engine, FCF margin reaches 22% by 2035 on ~$9B revenue. Fair value ≈ **$70-75/share**.

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value: ~$35-40/share.** Current price of $55.45-62 is *above* the base case and halfway to a bull case that requires flawless 10-year execution. At $62 you are paying a premium to a generous bull-case midpoint.

**Relative valuation — the one genuine bull argument.** Against true diagnostics peers, TEM is the cheapest on sales:

| | EV/Sales (2026E) | Profitability |
|---|---|---|
| Natera (NTRA) | ~20x | Near-breakeven FCF |
| Guardant (GH) | ~25x | Negative FCF |
| **Tempus (TEM)** | **~6.4-7.2x** | Adj. EBITDA positive 2026 |

That gap is real and is the strongest case for owning TEM rather than NTRA/GH. But relative cheapness inside an expensively priced peer group is not a margin of safety — it is a statement about other people's valuations. Note also the sector-peer tool returned mega-cap pharma (LLY, JNJ, MRK) as "peers," which is analytically useless; I substituted the molecular-diagnostics comps above.

**Verdict on price: no margin of safety. Negative expected return under a base case.**

## 4. Management Quality Assessment: **5 / 10**

**Positives**
- **Founder-led, deeply aligned.** Eric Lefkofsky (CEO & Chairman) holds ~24.3M shares (~14% of shares outstanding). He built the company from 2015 and has consistently raised guidance through 2026 — and delivered, including a Q4 2025 pre-announce above guidance and beats in every quarter of 2026.
- **Execution velocity is genuinely excellent.** Digesting Ambry, integrating it, and still hitting 26% growth with EBITDA turning positive in roughly 18 months is not easy.
- **Zero-coupon / 0.75% convert financings** (2030 and 2032) in mid-2025 to term out the Ambry acquisition debt was a shrewd, shareholder-friendly piece of liability management.

**Concerns**
- **Dual-class supervoting structure** means Lefkofsky controls the company with a minority of the economics. Shareholders cannot remove him. For a Buffett-style investor this is a permanent, non-diversifiable governance discount.
- **Serial-promoter history.** Groupon, Echo Global Logistics, Mediaocean — a pattern of roll-ups and financial engineering. The market-timing of the 2024 IPO and the 2025 converts reflects a promoter's instinct, not a steward's.
- **Non-GAAP emphasis is aggressive.** Reporting "adjusted EBITDA" while burning $245M of cash and expensing $125M of SBC requires the investor to do the work. Guidance is framed entirely around a metric that excludes the single largest real cost of running the business.
- **Dilution.** SBC of $125M (~10% of revenue, ~1.3% of market cap annually) plus $534M of SBC in 2024. No buybacks, no dividend, no share-count discipline. Net share count rose from ~155M (2023) to 174M.
- **Empire-building bias:** Ambry, Deep 6 AI, and multiple tuck-ins in under two years. Goodwill is already $825M on a $491M equity base — the balance sheet has little room for another misstep.
- **Insider selling** in Sept and Nov 2026 (multiple Form 4 sales), largely pre-scheduled but still a signal worth noting at the margin.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL (avoid / do not initiate)**

**Reasoning in one paragraph.** Tempus is a genuinely good, fast-growing, data-moat business run by a founder with real skin in the game, operating in a large and important market. That is not the issue. The issue is price relative to what can be *reliably* earned. The company has never produced positive free cash flow, has never earned a return on capital above its cost of capital, funds roughly $125M/yr of compensation through dilution, and still carries $722M of net debt. At ~7x forward sales and ~175x 2026 adjusted EBITDA, the shares discount something close to a flawless decade — 15%+ compound revenue growth *and* a tripling of mature margins. My base-case intrinsic value is **~$30-38/share**, a bull case tops out near **$75**, and the probability-weighted figure of ~$35-40 sits 30-40% *below* the current quote. Per the discipline that a fair price is not a buy price: this is not fairly priced, it is richly priced. **Sell / avoid.**

**What would change my mind** (and I would revisit):
1. Two to three consecutive quarters of *positive free cash flow* with SBC growth decelerating below revenue growth — proof the model self-funds.
2. A clean, larger disclosure of the Insights/data segment's revenue and margin, showing the high-margin mix is inflecting faster than modeled.
3. A price below ~$30 (roughly 4x forward sales), which would begin to price in the execution risk.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to owning it (downside that supports the Sell):**
- **Reimbursement/policy:** CMS rate pressure, the FDA's LDT rulemaking, and payer coverage decisions are existential to the 75-80% diagnostics base and are entirely outside management's control.
- **Competitive intensity:** Roche/Foundation Medicine, Caris, Guardant, Natera and Exact all have capital and channel access. If a competitor prices aggressively, Tempus's gross margin expansion reverses.
- **Financing risk:** −$200M+ of annual cash burn plus $722M net debt means another equity or convertible raise is likely if FCF inflects later than 2027 — at whatever price the market offers.
- **Multiple compression:** with a beta of 3.6 and a 50% drawdown from the Oct 2025 peak of $103 to the Feb 2026 low of $49, this is one of the most rate- and sentiment-sensitive names in healthcare. A derating from 7x to 4x sales alone is ~40% downside.
- **Governance:** dual-class control and a promoter-CEO raise the odds of value-destructive M&A.
