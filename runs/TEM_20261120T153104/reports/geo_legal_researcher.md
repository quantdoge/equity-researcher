### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**#1 — US reimbursement regime reset (Probability: High | Impact: High)**
CMS's preliminary CY2027 Clinical Laboratory Fee Schedule implies a **~16% weighted cut across lab codes, with up to 15% per test on 1-Jan-2027 and 775 codes at the statutory maximum cut**; CMS notes ~1,171 of 1,528 codes price below current Medicare rates, and the 15%/yr cap runs through 2029 (CMS; HFMA; ASCP). Tempus's Diagnostics segment is $303.5M/quarter (~74% of revenue). **Offsetting positive:** management guides that approved pricing moves add **~$80M of oncology ASP benefit in 2027**, and Q3 2026 guidance was raised — so this is a two-sided swing factor, not a one-way cut. This is the single largest policy variable in the model.

**#2 — State genetic-privacy enforcement and legislation (Probability: High | Impact: High)**
The Illinois Genetic Information Privacy Act (GIPA) class actions (see §3) are the leading edge of a broader state-level patchwork (IL, TX, WA and others) that directly taxes the **Insights / Data & Applications** model ($108.1M in Q3 2026, ~26% of revenue, growing 33% YoY). Legislative tightening of "consent for secondary use of genetic data" — or a large GIPA verdict — would reprice the highest-margin, highest-multiple segment of the business. Statutory damages under GIPA are per-violation, which makes even a modest class economically dangerous.

**#3 — Federal fiscal and government-contract risk (Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium)**
Tempus's federal channel is expanding — VA agreement, DoD/veteran precision-oncology collaboration with SOTERIA (Nov-2026), and a Blood Cancer United pediatric AML registry — while DC faces a debt-limit confrontation as early as February (NYT, 18-Nov-2026). Federal-payer mix, NIH-funded research budgets and government shutdown risk all feed directly into Diagnostics volumes and Data contract signings. Also live: **DOJ's "bulk sensitive personal data" rule and CFIUS-style data-security scrutiny of genomic datasets**, which could complicate licensing data to non-US pharma partners or the SoftBank JV.

*Watch item (not top-3): US–China. Tempus has effectively no China revenue; the exposure is supply-chain (NGS instruments/reagents) and BIOSECURE-style data-provenance screens that actually **favour** US-native data owners like Tempus.*

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Genetic data privacy — the flagship exposure.** Two Illinois federal class actions (incl. *Barone et al. v. Tempus AI*, No. 1:26-cv-03516, filed 30-Mar-2026, and No. 1:26-cv-04246, filed 15-Apr-2026) allege that Tempus compelled Ambry Genetics — acquired Feb-2025 for ~$600M — to hand over its repository of customer genetic data **without written consent**, in violation of GIPA; one complaint names AstraZeneca as an aider/abettor. A Plaintiffs' Executive Committee was appointed 6-May-2026. Legal trade press is calling this a *"landmark"* AI-acquisition privacy case (Fisher Phillips; JD Supra; GenomeWeb; HIPAA Journal). **Why it matters:** the theory attacks the core diligence thesis — that acquired lab data is licensable. Ambry "consent" representations were a condition of the acquisition, which raises both GIPA and contractual/indemnity questions.

**Securities litigation (AI-washing).** A securities class action was filed 12-Jun-2025 in N.D. Ill. against Tempus and two officers, alleging the company branded itself an AI company on misleading claims; class period 6-Aug-2024 to 27-May-2025, with a **lead-plaintiff deadline of 28-Dec-2026** and roughly a dozen firms soliciting (Robbins Geller, Glancy Prongay, Howard G. Smith, KSF, Rosen, Wolf Popper; Stanford Securities Litigation clearinghouse). This is a reputational and disclosure-controls issue as much as a dollar one.

**Patent war with Guardant Health (the most financially binary item).**
- *Guardant Health v. Tempus AI*, D. Del. (filed 11-Jun-2024) alleges Tempus xF / xF+ / xM infringe Guardant sequencing patents; Guardant seeks **injunctive relief plus damages**.
- On **27-Aug-2026 a Delaware judge refused to dismiss** the Guardant suits against Tempus (Law360) — the case is proceeding, and Guardant is separately pressing for a US injunction in the **recurrence/MRD monitoring** space, which is exactly the capability Tempus is buying with Personalis.
- *Tempus AI v. Guardant Health* (N.D. Cal.) was **dismissed in Jan-2026**; Tempus's separate false-advertising/unfair-competition case against Guardant remains live (Bartlit Beck as trial counsel).
- Important clarification: the **$245M judgment ordered against Guardant on 24-Aug-2026 runs to TwinStrand Biosciences and the University of Washington — not to Tempus**. Do not model it as a TEM asset.

**State AG / billing integrity.** Tempus has disclosed, since its 2022 DRS/A and continuously through the FY2025 10-K and Q3 2026 10-Q, a **subpoena from the Office of the Ohio Attorney General (received 19-May-2022) requiring production of billing and patient records** — a long-running state healthcare-billing inquiry. Against the current backdrop (DOJ–HHS False Claims Act Working Group formed July-2025; **780+ qui tam complaints filed in FY2026**), lab billing remains a top enforcement target. No federal DOJ/SEC subpoena was identified in the material reviewed.

**M&A / antitrust — largely de-risked.** The **$1.5bn Personalis acquisition** ($16.25/share): **HSR waiting period expired, UK clearance obtained**, S-4 effective 21-Oct-2026, Personalis shareholder vote 30-Nov-2026, closing expected Q4-2026 (Q3 2026 10-Q). Residual risk is ordinary shareholder objection litigation (KSF announced a price/process investigation on 17-Nov-2026). Also closed: Center Health (7-Aug-2026, $18.8M), Paige AI (2025).

**FDA / device regulation.** Tempus is increasingly a **CDRH-regulated device company**, not just a CLIA lab: 510(k) clearances for Tempus ECG-AF, ECG-PH and ECG-MR (plus CE-marked Pixel). That pulls in QMSR, post-market surveillance and AI/ML change-control obligations — a real but manageable compliance cost.

**Sanctions / export controls / FCPA.** No OFAC, BIS or FCPA matters identified in filings or news. No operations in sanctioned jurisdictions. Exposure to Russia/China sanctions is effectively nil.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Consent architecture for acquired data (HIGH).** The GIPA theory — that an M&A transfer cannot substitute for patient authorization — is a structural challenge to Tempus's data-licensing economics and will be replicated in other states.
2. **Claims/billing integrity (MEDIUM-HIGH).** Ohio AG inquiry still open after 4+ years plus elevated national FCA activity; any adverse finding triggers repayments, CIA obligations and reputational damage with health-system partners.
3. **AI marketing substantiation (MEDIUM).** Overlapping securities "AI-washing" allegations and FTC-style substantiation risk on AI claims; disclosure controls are the mitigation.
4. **Data provenance for federal customers (MEDIUM).** DoD/VA work plus a SoftBank-affiliated Japan JV creates foreign-access-to-genomic-data questions under DOJ bulk-sensitive-data rules — a compliance gate on the international template.
5. **GIPA-adjacent litigation is now a recurring cost of the M&A playbook** — every future acquisition of a lab with a genetic-data repository is a new class-action vector.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**
*(Legal sub-rating: elevated Medium — argue High on a standalone litigation basis)*

**Rationale:** Tempus's geopolitical exposure is unusually clean for a $10bn healthcare name — essentially a single-country revenue base with no sanctioned-market, currency-control, expropriation, or US–China revenue exposure. The risk is not *where* it operates but *what it does with data and how it gets paid for it*. Three items dominate: (a) the CY2027 CLFS/ASP reimbursement reset, largely offset near-term by the ~$80M oncology ASP uplift; (b) the GIPA class actions, which are the genuine tail risk because statutory per-violation damages scale against a $1.6bn revenue base and strike at the 26%-of-revenue Data segment; and (c) the Guardant patent case, which survived dismissal in August 2026 and could constrain MRD expansion precisely as Personalis closes.

**Key monitoring triggers:** GIPA class certification or a motion-to-dismiss ruling; Delaware trial scheduling or an injunction motion in *Guardant v. Tempus*; the final CY2027 CLFS rule and Tempus's realized ASP; any escalation of the Ohio AG matter into a federal/DOJ referral; and closing of Personalis (30-Nov-2026 vote).
