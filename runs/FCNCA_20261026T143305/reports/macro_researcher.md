# Macroeconomic Assessment — First Citizens BancShares (FCNCA)
**As of 26 October 2026 | Data: FRED, FOMC, company Q3'26 release**

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional / late-cycle hawkish**

The regime changed in September. The FOMC **raised** the fed funds target range 25bp to **3.75–4.00% on 16 September 2026** — the first hike since 2023 — after holding the effective rate at ~3.63–3.64% for eight straight months (Jan–Aug 2026). The SEP now projects **4.00–4.25% by year-end 2026**, i.e. one more hike is the Committee's own base case. This is a "cut cycle that died," and it is the single most important fact for a rate-levered lender.

The reason is a **sticky-core inflation problem**. Headline CPI is actually *decelerating* — 3.7% YoY but only ~2.3% annualized over the last six months. Core is doing the opposite: **2.6% YoY but ~3.7% annualized over six months and ~3.4% over three**. The Fed is hiking into a headline that looks fine and a core that does not.

Growth and labour are, awkwardly, fine: real GDP +2.2% YoY (Q2'26), unemployment 4.2% and range-bound at 4.1–4.5% for a year, bank C&I tightening demand net **0.0%** (standards have normalized from 18.5% in Q2'25), and Baa–10Y spreads at **1.46%** near cycle tights. So this is not a recession trade; it is a **term-premium / real-rate shock**. Critically, the 10Y breakeven is only **2.34%** while the 10Y is ~5.0% — the entire long-end selloff is **real yields** (real 10Y ≈ 2.65%, up ~90bp since May). Rising *real* rates, not rising inflation expectations, is what hurts a bank's securities book and its borrowers' collateral values.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

FCNCA is a **$26.5bn-market-cap, Raleigh-based, essentially domestic** regional bank (beta 0.58) whose balance sheet is dominated by a very large, low-cost deposit franchise and an outsized securities portfolio inherited from the 2023 SVB acquisition. Q3'26 (reported 23 Oct) beat handily — **EPS $55.37 vs $46.61 consensus** vs $44.62 a year ago — with a new **$1.2bn buyback** (~4.5% of market cap) and $600m repurchased in the quarter. The stock rose **+8.3% on 23 Oct** to an all-time high of $2,388, +11.6% YTD. That sets a high bar for macro tolerance.

**Quantifying the credit channel:** on ~11.1m shares, every **10bp of incremental provision on a ~$140bn loan book ≈ $140m pre-tax ≈ ~$10/share ≈ 18% of quarterly EPS.** With allowance coverage reportedly thin, a modest macro deterioration transmits to earnings with unusual force — that is the asymmetry to watch.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Two genuinely powerful positives — a positively sloped, bear-steepening curve and a 2.2%-growth, 4.2%-unemployment economy with normalizing credit — are offset by a hawkish policy path actively compressing NIM, a real-rate shock that marks down the securities book and the SVB-legacy venture/CRE collateral, and a valuation sitting at all-time highs with no allowance cushion. I would characterize the tilt as **favourable on the curve and growth, unfavourable on inflation policy and AOCI**. Were the Fed done at 4.00%, this would migrate to Favourable within two quarters.

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise — **BEAR**

Consensus treats the September hike as a one-and-done and the sale of the long bond as a term-premium story that doesn't touch credit. The surprise: **core inflation stays above 3.5% and the Fed delivers its guided hike to 4.00–4.25% and holds through 2027.** With the 30Y testing 6% and the real 10Y near 2.9%:

- FCNCA's AOCI re-widens, slowing the $1.2bn buyback — the very catalyst that drove the 23 Oct re-rating;
- CRE (office/multifamily) refinancing at 5%+ and the venture/startup loan book (maximally sensitive to the discount rate) force a reserve build against thin coverage — the ~$10/share-per-10bp math above cuts EPS by 20–35%;
- deposit betas finish repricing, so NIM compression extends rather than bottoms.

A 10.8x multiple on a lowered, lower-quality EPS with a stalled buyback is a 20–30% drawdown setup. **Bull offset (less likely):** if shelter disinflation pulls core down, the December hike is skipped and the 10Y retraces to ~4.4%, the curve bull-steepens from the front end — deposit costs fall faster than asset yields, AOCI reverses, and the buyback accelerates into a re-rating toward 13–14x.
