# Forensic Short-Seller Analysis — First Citizens BancShares (FCNCA)

**Price $2,387.77 (26-Oct-26) · Mkt cap $26.5B · ~11.25M diluted sh · Regional Bank · Raleigh, NC**

**Bottom line up front: I cannot build an honest short here.** Two of the three forensic models that flagged "HIGH RISK" and "Distress" are producing provable artifacts, not evidence. I'll show the math. There *is* a real bear case — but it's a "quality of growth" case, not a fraud or solvency case, and it's priced roughly fairly. **Rating: No Edge.**

---

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment — **HIGH** (with two caveats)

| Metric | FY2025 | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Accruals ratio | **−0.0031** | Negative accruals = cash *ahead* of earnings. Clean. |
| Cash conversion (CFO/NI) | **1.33x** | Well above the 100% quality bar. |
| FCF conversion | 0.94x | Normal for a bank (capex $852M). |
| Composite quality score | 57/100 | Middling only because of capex/debt items. |
| Piotroski F | 5/9 | Moderate; misses on ROA trend, leverage, gross margin. |

My two caveats are **non-operating noise in "adjusted" EPS** and **a polluted multi-year history**:

- Q3'26 GAAP EPS **$51.23** vs. "adjusted" **$55.37** — an **8.1% gap** ($606M vs $652M net income). The bridge is built from items that recur every quarter: fair-value marks on **equity warrants** (+$31M in Q2'26, −$6M in Q3'26), fair-value marks on **marketable equity securities** (−$38M), realized gains on security sales (+$28M), tax-credit investment gains, and **losses on debt extinguishment** from Purchase Money Note repayments. This is textbook adjusted-metric creep — not fraud, but the "clean" number is managed.
- FY2023 is a data trap: **net income $11.47B on $12.64B of revenue**, vs. operating cash flow of only **$2.66B — 23% cash conversion.** The cause is the ~$10.9B pre-tax **bargain purchase gain** on SVB, a non-cash accounting gain. *Any* multi-year growth or quality screen run on FCNCA that spans 2023 is measuring an accounting event, not a business. If a bull shows you a 2022→2025 EPS CAGR chart, they're showing you the SVB bargain gain amortizing into a share count.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

1. **All EPS growth in excess of earnings growth is buybacks.** Q3'25→Q3'26: net income $568M→$606M (**+6.7%**), diluted shares 12.849M→11.251M (**−12.4%**), EPS $43.12→$51.23 (**+18.8%**). The share-count effect (~12.9pts) is *larger* than the earnings effect. Six-quarter share count: 13.575M → 11.251M, **−17.1%**. FY25 repurchases $3.03B on a $26.5B cap; new $1.2B authorization through 2027 announced 23-Oct-26. This is a ~9%-of-market-cap annual retirement programme. Organic EPS growth is mid-single-digit.
2. **Buybacks are above tangible book.** Estimated BVPS ≈ **$2,000** (YE25 equity $22.238B/12.363M = $1,798; adding ~$1.8B of 9M26 net income less ~$1.5B of buybacks), i.e. **~1.19x book**. Repurchasing at 1.19x book against a ~10.8% ROE is roughly EPS-accretive but **BVPS-dilutive** — it flatters per-share earnings while shrinking the equity base that generates them.
3. **NIM is compressing on a like-for-like basis, and the marginal funding spread has gone to ~zero.** NIM ex-PAA: **3.11%** (Q4'25) → 3.01% → 3.01% → **2.99%** (Q3'26) — four straight declines. Meanwhile the **rate paid on interest-bearing liabilities rose 6bp to 3.01%**, driven explicitly by "**growth in the Direct Bank and brokered deposits**." The marginal spread between ex-PAA NIM (2.99%) and interest-bearing funding cost (3.01%) is now **negative**. Loan growth is being bought with expensive, rate-sensitive, non-core money.
4. **PAA is now immaterial — a bear point that has already decayed.** NII $1.70B vs $1.66B ex-PAA (**$40M/qtr, ~8bp of NIM**). Anyone shorting FCNCA on "purchase accounting inflation" is two years late.
5. **Revenue has not grown in seven quarters on a YoY basis:** −4.81%, −2.27%, −2.35%, −0.92%, −1.33%, −0.52%, +0.14%. Annual revenue fell from $14.94B (2024) to $14.50B (2025), **−3.0%**.
6. **Growth is inorganic and episodic.** Total assets: $58.3B (2021) → $109.3B (2022, CIT) → $213.8B (2023, SVB) → $229.7B (2025). Sept-2026: 138 BMO branches, $4.9B deposits, $650M loans. This is a serial acquirer whose growth depends on the next deal and on FDIC/branch auctions.
7. **A ~$638M retained-earnings reconciliation gap (unresolved, not an accusation).** FY25 retained earnings rose $1.407B; net income less dividends implies $2.045B. The ~$638M difference is most plausibly AOCI reclassification or the AFS→HTM transfer, but I could not retrieve the AOCI schedule to confirm. Flagged for resolution, not asserted as manipulation.

## 3. Beneish M-Score and Accruals Analysis — **the models are wrong, and I can prove it**

- **Beneish M-Score: 35.93** ("HIGH RISK — likely manipulator"). This is **garbage output**. The entire score is driven by **DSRI = 43.22**, which in turn is driven by the "net receivables" line moving from **$3.49B (2024) to $146.36B (2025)** — a 42x jump with assets flat ($223.7B → $229.7B). That is a **line-item reclassification (loans into the receivables field)**, not a channel-stuffing event. A 42x receivables increase cannot coexist with flat total assets; it is definitionally a taxonomy artifact. The remaining indices are benign: GMI 1.013, AQI 0.317, SGI 0.97, TATA **−0.0031**.
- **Altman Z-Score: 0.32 "Distress."** Also **invalid**. Altman was fitted on manufacturers; for a bank, x5 (asset turnover) is structurally ~0.06 and x1 (WC/assets) is meaningless. Applying it to a $230B bank with $60.7B of liquid assets is a category error. Do not cite it.
- **Accruals ratio — the one model that works:** −0.0031 (2025), −0.0009 (2024), +0.0412 (2023, the SVB gain year), −0.0155 (2022). **Three of four years negative.** This is a genuinely clean accrual profile.

**Verdict: earnings are cash-backed. The manipulation hypothesis fails.**

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment — **moderate, not acute**

- **Deposit franchise is the weak link.** Regional banks compete for funding against money-market funds and the largest banks; FCNCA's answer is Direct Bank and brokered deposits. That's a rational but **lower-quality, higher-beta** funding model, and it's why ex-PAA NIM keeps sliding. CET1/ROE is being defended by shrinking the denominator (buybacks), not by widening the spread.
- **SVB's franchise is a decaying asset.** The venture/startup deposit base is cyclical and rate-sensitive, and competitor banks have spent three years poaching it.
- **Credit is genuinely strong — this kills the classic bank short.** Q3'26 provision **$55M** (vs. a $10M *benefit* in Q2), roughly **0.03% annualized** of loans; net charge-offs *declined* in CRE and investor-dependent portfolios. Liquid assets **$60.7B** at 3/31/26. There is no SVB-style run risk and no visible CRE air-pocket.
- **Genuine positives I won't talk down:** repayment of another **$8.0B of the Purchase Money Note** in Q3'26 structurally lowers interest expense; the BMO branch deal adds sticky Midwest/Greater Plains deposits; and revenue has inflected sequentially ($3.478B → $3.604B → $3.699B).

## 5. Management Red Flags — **net positive, which hurts the short**

- **Insiders are net BUYERS.** Recent Form 4s show **16 acquisitions vs. 1 disposition**, including CEO Frank B. Holding, Jr. purchasing **~$1M of Class B stock**. The single disposition is Olivia Britton Holding selling **7,000 preferred depositary shares at $19.50–19.60 (~$137K**, 15-Sep-26) — immaterial, and not common stock.
- The heavy Form 4 clusters (8 filings on 2026-03-04; 5 on 2026-06-08) are consistent with **equity-award vesting**, not discretionary distribution.
- **No FCNCA-specific enforcement action found.** My searches for SEC/DOJ subpoenas surfaced only the well-known probes of *SVB Financial's* executives — **not** First Citizens. I found no auditor change, no restatement, and no related-party red flag.
- Caveat: the **Holding family controls the company via Class B supervoting shares**. That's a structural governance discount, and it's also why management can run a multi-decade roll-up with minimal shareholder challenge. Real, but long-known and not actionable.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**The strongest version of the bear case (steelmanned):** FCNCA is a deal-fueled roll-up whose per-share earnings growth is manufactured by retiring 9% of its shares annually at 1.19x book, funded partly by draining liquidity and substituting FDIC Purchase Money Notes with senior unsecured debt and brokered deposits. Underneath, ex-PAA NIM has fallen four straight quarters to 2.99%, the marginal funding spread is negative, revenue hasn't grown YoY in seven quarters, and "adjusted" EPS excludes a recurring 8% of noise. Once the buyback arithmetic and the debt-repayment interest tailwind are exhausted — call it 2028 — the underlying machine grows mid-single-digit at best, and the stock is at an all-time high after a +63% move off the April-2025 low ($1,464 → $2,388).

**Why that case does not clear my bar:**
- **No fraud, no manipulation signal.** Negative accruals, 1.33x cash conversion, no receivables/inventory anomaly that survives inspection.
- **No solvency or liquidity stress.** $60.7B liquid assets, $55M quarterly provision, declining charge-offs.
- **Valuation is fair, not extended.** ~**12.0x** 2026E EPS ($198.63) and **11.6x** 2027E ($205.51); ~**1.19x** book against ~**10.8% ROE**. A Gordon-growth justified P/B (ROE 10.8%, g 5%, COE 9.5%) is **~1.29x** — i.e. the stock is, if anything, marginally *cheap*. A short has ~10% of multiple cushion and a 0.6% dividend plus a 9% annual buyback bid working against it.
- **Insiders are buying and the balance sheet is de-risking.** Those are the wrong conditions to be short.
- **It just broke out on volume.** 23-Oct-26: $2,196.76 → $2,375.84 (**+8.2%**) on 298K shares vs. a ~60K norm. Shorting into a post-earnings breakout with a fresh buyback authorization is a poor entry.

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **NO EDGE — reject the short thesis**

**Downgrade triggers that would make this a genuine short (watch these, not the price):**
1. **Ex-PAA NIM below 2.85% for two consecutive quarters** (currently 2.99% — this is the single best tell).
2. **Rate paid on interest-bearing liabilities exceeding ex-PAA NIM for two quarters** (narrowly true in Q3'26; one print is noise, two is a franchise problem).
3. **Provision above $150M/quarter or net charge-offs above 0.30% annualized** (currently ~0.03%).
4. **Buyback pace cut, or any common equity raise** — the EPS engine stalling.
5. **Adjusted-vs-GAAP EPS gap above 12% for two quarters** (currently 8.1%).
6. **Brokered + Direct Bank deposits above 15% of total deposits** — funding-quality break.
7. **Resolution of the ~$638M retained-earnings gap that is *not* AOCI-related.**

**Where I'd be wrong in the bulls' favour:** if the $8B Purchase Money Note paydown plus falling short rates re-expand ex-PAA NIM back above 3.15%, the 2027–28 EPS estimates ($205 / $224) are too low, the buyback keeps compounding at 9%/yr, and a 1.19x book entry re-rates toward 1.4x. That path produces a 25–30% return — a far better expected value than the short.

**Honest data gaps:** (a) The peer-multiple dataset returned index funds, not regional banks, so **I have no verified peer valuation set** and did not substitute estimates. (b) I could not extract the AOCI / HTM unrealized-loss schedule — the AFS→HTM transfer remains the one unquantified balance-sheet opacity. (c) Q3'26 period-end equity is **estimated** (~$22.5B) for the book-value math; all quarterly flow figures are as reported.

*This is a well-run, cash-generative, conservatively reserved bank trading near fair value with insiders buying. The bear case is real but it is a slow-burn quality-of-growth argument that the 9% buyback bid actively defeats. Pass.*
