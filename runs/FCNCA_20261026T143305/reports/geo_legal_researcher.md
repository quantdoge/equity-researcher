## FCNCA — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company:** First Citizens BancShares, Inc. (Nasdaq: FCNCA) — bank holding company for First-Citizens Bank & Trust Company, Raleigh, NC. Sector: Financial Services / Banks–Regional. Market cap ≈ $26.5B, ~18.1k employees. *(Company profile)*

**Threshold finding:** FCNCA is a **domestic US regional bank** whose risk profile is almost entirely *regulatory and legal*, not geopolitical. It has no material operations in sanctioned or emerging-market jurisdictions. The binding constraints on the stock are (a) Category IV enhanced-prudential/capital rules, (b) the unwinding of the FDIC loss-share shield from the SVB deal, and (c) credit concentration in innovation-economy and rail/industrial verticals.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**① US capital & supervisory recalibration (Category IV bank) — Probability: High | Impact: Medium**
FNCNA sits above $100B in assets and is subject to DFAST/enhanced prudential standards with tailoring relief. In the **2026 DFAST**, First Citizens posted the *lowest* stressed CET1 minimum of the cohort at **6.7%, a 4.5pp drop from its actual 11.2% — the steepest relative decline among peers** (Fitch Ratings, 6/29/26; Fed 2026 DFAST results, 6/24/26). Commentators note FCNCA may not receive a binding stress capital buffer above the 2.5% minimum until ~2028. Meanwhile the agencies' **March 19, 2026 capital proposals** rescind the 2023 Basel III endgame framework and modestly *reduce* large-bank requirements (Fed press release bcreg20260319a; Sullivan & Cromwell; Simpson Thacher). Net: near-term tailwind, but the bank is one supervisory cycle away from a binding buffer, and its stress-test sensitivity is the worst in its peer set — a genuine, quantifiable policy risk.

**② Credit-cycle concentration in the innovation economy + loss-share expiry — Probability: Medium-High | Impact: Medium-High**
Two compounded exposures: (i) the SVB-acquired venture/tech and fund-finance book in California; (ii) railcar and equipment finance (CIT Rail) levered to industrial/trade volumes and tariff-driven freight demand. The **FDIC shared-loss agreement** on SVB-originated commercial loans (signed March 2023) provides downside protection that **rolls off over the 5-year commercial horizon (~2028)**. FCNCA is simultaneously retiring the **Purchase Money Note** issued to the FDIC early ($8.0B repaid in Q3 2026 after $2.5B in Q2) — good economics, but it removes a cheap funding and downside-sharing structure. Sell-side has already flagged that "low allowance coverage remains a key risk if the economy weakens" (Seeking Alpha, 10/23/26). Tariff/trade-policy volatility transmits here via freight volumes and industrial borrowers.

**③ Cross-border conduct risk from a global client base — Probability: Low | Impact: Medium**
SVB's legacy clients and fund-finance borrowers sit across Cayman, Luxembourg, UK and Asia, and international FX/wire fees run through the SVB segment. That creates OFAC/sanctions-screening and BSA/AML exposure atypical for a US regional bank — the classic route to a consent order and civil money penalty. No such action is currently disclosed, hence low probability.

*Not a risk:* no Russia/Ukraine, China, or emerging-market operating exposure; no nationalisation, expropriation, or currency-repatriation risk; no FCPA exposure of consequence.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **First-Citizens Bank & Trust Co. v. HSBC Holdings et al. (N.D. Cal. No. 3:23-cv-02483)** — FCNCA's **~$1B suit** alleging HSBC ran an illicit scheme to hire 40+ SVB bankers and misappropriate trade secrets. In July 2024 Judge Laurel Beeler **dismissed most claims**, leaving certain trade-secret-theft and breach-of-contract claims (Reuters 7/10/24; Banking Dive). Litigation is **still active in late 2026**: defendant David Sabow filed counterclaims (docket 8/19/26) and HSBC/Sabow are "fiercely contesting" the claims (Bloomberg, 10/6/26; Claims Journal, 10/7/26). **Assessment:** FCNCA is plaintiff, so downside is cost and management distraction, not damages; adverse-counterclaim risk is modest but non-zero.
- **FDIC loss-share + Purchase Money Note** — structural, multi-year entanglement with a US regulator as counterparty; true-up/clawback mechanics and 2028 expiry are the key watch items.
- **BMO branch acquisition (closed 9/4/26)** — 138 branches, 11 states, ~$5.0B deposits, ~$650M loans; required and received bank-regulatory and antitrust clearance (Arnold & Porter; Smith Anderson). One-off acquisition expense already in Q3 ($10M sequential increase).
- **SVB integration completes Q4 2026** — systems/data conversion risk; operational and third-party risk is the most probable source of a near-term regulatory finding.
- **No disclosed DOJ, SEC, CFPB, OCC or Fed enforcement action against FCNCA.** Note the disambiguation trap: the Fed's **May 2024 written agreement with "First Citizens Bank of Butte" (Montana)** is an unrelated institution, despite the name collision (Fed enforcement action 5/7/24; Paul Weiss sanctions/AML year-in-review).
- **CRA and fair-lending** — subject to standard CRA exams and fair-lending scrutiny; no public downgrade or referral identified.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **BSA/AML & OFAC screening across offshore fund-finance and FX/wire flows** (SVB legacy) — the single most likely source of a future consent order or CMP.
2. **Brokered/Direct Bank deposit growth** — Q3 2026 interest expense rose $90M on "growth in the Direct Bank and brokered deposits." Deposit-classification and FDIC-insurance rules are a live policy area; misclassification is a supervisory finding risk.
3. **Data privacy** — GLBA plus state regimes (notably California, given the SVB book) and any residual GDPR touchpoints; the Q4 2026 conversion is the peak window for exposure.
4. **No material FCPA/anti-bribery exposure** (minimal foreign operations). **No crypto/BaaS or novel-charter red flags identified.**

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

Pure geopolitical risk is **Low** — a US-only franchise in the world's most stable large banking market, with no sanctions-heavy, expropriation-prone, or currency-controlled jurisdictions. The Medium rating is driven by **regulatory and legal** factors: Category IV capital/stress-test sensitivity (worst relative stressed-CET1 decline in the 2026 DFAST cohort), the 2028 roll-off of FDIC loss-share protection coinciding with thin allowance coverage, and an active, headline-generating $1B trade-secrets case against HSBC.
