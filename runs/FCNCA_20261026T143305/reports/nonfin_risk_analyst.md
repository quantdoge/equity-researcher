## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — First Citizens BancShares, Inc. (FCNCA)

**Snapshot:** ~$2,387/share, ~$26.5B market cap, ~18,100 FTE, Raleigh NC. Top-20 US bank (assets >$225B) built by serial FDIC-assisted and whole-bank acquisitions (SVB, March 2023; 138 BMO branches closing Q3 2026). Beta 0.58.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK** (structurally), with adequate operational execution

**Evidence:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality — confirmed.** Frank B. Holding, Jr. is simultaneously Chairman and Chief Executive Officer of both BancShares and First-Citizens Bank & Trust (IR board page; 10-Q Exhibit 31.1 CEO certification). No independent chair counterweight disclosed.
- **Dual-class control — confirmed.** Class A carries 1 vote; Class B carries **16 votes per share** and is "closely-held by the Holding Family" (SEC proxy language). The 2026 proxy confirms 16 votes/Class B share. This means a family bloc controls the company despite a minority of the economic interest — a permanent structural block on shareholder influence (no realistic proxy contest, no hostile bid discipline, MSCI/FTSE-style index voting-rights penalties).
- **Family insiders as 10% owners and directors** (e.g., Olivia Britton Holding disclosed as a >10% owner; family-affiliated entities Holding Properties, LLC and the Robert P. Holding Foundation file Form 144s). Governance codes generally treat this as a controlled company, with reduced independence expectations.
- **Related-party surface:** Holding-family LLCs/trusts transact in the stock and hold legacy real-estate interests. *Verification gap:* the FY2025 10-K risk factors and the 2026 DEF 14A full text were **not retrievable** in this session (SEC archive returned HTTP 403; the filing-text tool reported "No 10-K / No DEF 14A found"). Related-party lease disclosures and the independent-director count therefore remain **unverified** and should be read directly from the proxy before the memo is finalized.
- **Mitigant:** board includes independent directors who buy stock in the open market (Diane E. Morais purchased ~$108K in August 2026); insider ownership aligns family and shareholders on capital allocation, and the Q3 2026 $1.2B buyback plus $2.25/share quarterly dividend indicate shareholder-friendly capital return.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **No active SEC enforcement actions.** EDGAR full-text enforcement search for FCNCA returned zero results. No OCC/Fed consent order against First-Citizens Bank & Trust identified.
- **False positive to exclude from the memo:** the May 2024 Federal Reserve written agreement referenced in search results was with **First Citizens Bank of Butte (Montana)** — an unrelated institution. Do not attribute to FCNCA.
- **Active litigation (SVB-related, ongoing):**
  - *SVB Financial Trust v. First-Citizens Bank & Trust* (N.D. Cal. 3:25-cv-02267) — trademark dispute over the SVB name/logo, filed March 2025; First-Citizens is counter-claimant. Activity through August 2026; unresolved.
  - *First-Citizens Bank & Trust v. SVB Financial Group* (S.D.N.Y. No. 25-01044) — related declaratory action.
  - Financial materiality appears low (branding/IP, not damages-scale), but it keeps the SVB failure narrative alive in the press.
- **Contingent regulatory economics:** FDIC loss-share agreement on SVB commercial loans (true-up/cost-share mechanics) and an **FDIC equity appreciation right (value up to ~$500M)** remain outstanding claims on value from the 2023 transaction.
- **Forward-looking step-up risk — key:** total assets are approaching the **$250B Category III threshold**, which would trigger materially stricter enhanced-prudential standards (full LCR/NSFR, more frequent stress testing, resolvability planning, higher supervisory intensity). Combined with the Basel III endgame re-proposal, this is a multi-year compliance-cost headwind. Offsetting it: the current supervisory climate is described as a "lighter touch on banks," which flatters near-term earnings but **raises latent tail risk** from loosened standards across the sector.
- Recent 8-Ks (Sept 8, Sept 14, 2026) coincide with outside-counsel deal disclosure and the BMO branch closing — no adverse event indicated.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Serial-acquisition integration risk.** Three large, structurally different integrations in ~36 months: SVB (~$110B assets, ~$56B deposits assumed 2023), now a **138-branch BMO network** across the Midwest/Great Plains/West (~$4.9–5.3B deposits, ~$700M loans), plus prior CIT. Branch, core-deposit and treasury-management conversions are the classic failure point for banks of this size; management is simultaneously running "data center modernization" and digital investment (third-party processing fees +$7M, equipment +$5M QoQ).
- **Concentration in SVB-derived businesses.** "Global Fund Banking" (VC/PE capital-call lines) is the identified driver of commercial loan growth — a business line with high idiosyncratic credit sensitivity to the venture-funding cycle, founder-concentrated counterparties, and elevated reputational salience. Its performance is intertwined with the FDIC loss-share, so the true loss experience is partly obscured.
- **Reserve adequacy / earnings-quality judgment.** Sell-side commentary flags **low allowance coverage** relative to peers and heavy reliance on purchase-accounting accretion (PAA) — Q2 2026 NIM 3.10% GAAP vs. 3.01% ex-PAA, with adjusted net income ($691M/$57.09) materially above GAAP ($672M/$55.52). Not an accounting irregularity, but the adjusted-vs-GAAP gap and thin coverage are the first place a weakening economy would surface.
- **Key-person dependency:** the CEO/Chairman is a fourth-generation family principal; there is no disclosed succession plan at the top. **Third-party/cyber:** no FCNCA-specific breach, outage, or ransomware incident surfaced (the April 2026 Citizens Financial Group/Frost Bank event is an unrelated bank — exclude). Growth in online "Direct Bank" deposits raises BSA/AML, account-takeover and fraud exposure.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **LOW–MODERATE**

- No consumer backlash, boycott, recall-type event, or adverse ESG controversy found. News flow over the last 120 days is dominated by earnings beats, dividend declarations, buyback authorization, and 13F institutional-flow notes — neutral-to-positive.
- Persistent **SVB brand overhang**: the trademark litigation and continued use of SVB-derived assets keeps the 2023 failure association in the press; a future VC-cycle downturn that produces visible losses in ex-SVB loan books would be amplified.
- Minor reputational irritants: rising **overdraft/deposit fee income** (called out in Q2), aggressive online-deposit marketing spend, and a bank at $225B+ that markets itself as a relationship/community lender — a scale-vs-brand tension.
- The family-controlled structure invites periodic governance criticism from proxy advisors and index providers (dual-class, no independent chair).

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Governance structure is the weakest pillar (controlled company, dual-class 16:1 votes, CEO/Chairman duality, heavy family-affiliated selling), but it is offset by an absence of enforcement history, no credit/regulatory crisis, strong capital and liquidity, and a well-executed acquisition track record. Regulatory step-up at $250B in assets and integration execution on 138 new branches are the two live, quantifiable risks.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Structural minority disenfranchisement** — Class B 16-vote shares held by the Holding family plus Chairman/CEO duality. Index and proxy-advisor risk; any activist thesis is unactionable.
2. **Insider signal divergence** — ~$11M of family-affiliated/trust sales into a 25% rally vs. ~$0.6M of CEO purchases. Confirm 10b5-1 plan status and whether sales represent 100% of remaining holdings of specific entities.
3. **Reserve adequacy at the top of the cycle** — low allowance coverage + PAA-inflated margins + a $1.2B buyback while integrating acquisitions. Ask management for coverage ex-PAA and ex-loss-share.
4. **$250B Category III threshold crossing** — quantify incremental compliance/stress-testing cost and any capital-distribution constraint it imposes.
5. **Unresolved SVB trademark litigation** and the outstanding FDIC equity appreciation right — confirm no adverse economics beyond branding.
