## SECTOR BRIEF — COHR (Coherent Corp.)

**Sector:** Technology / Hardware, Equipment & Parts (optical components & photonics)
**Classification:** AI datacom optical interconnect + industrial lasers
**Market cap:** $37.97B | **Price:** $192.81 | **Beta:** 2.23

---

### 1. Sector Overview

Coherent sits in the optical/photonics supply chain — the laser, indium phosphide (InP) and module layer that converts electrical signals to light inside AI data centres, plus a legacy industrial laser franchise. The relevant market is not "Technology" broadly but datacom optics, where Yole estimates datacom optical component revenue exceeded **$18B in 2025, up >70% YoY**, on >55M units shipped. The industry is structurally a **duopoly-plus-oligopoly at the component level** (InP/EML lasers, where Coherent and Lumentum hold scarce capacity) but far more crowded and price-competitive at the module/transceiver level, where Chinese vendors Innolight and Eoptolink have been taking share. Growth is currently supply-constrained rather than demand-constrained — every credible player is sold out on laser capacity through 2027. The 2026 cycle is a **capacity-race hypergrowth phase**, with the 800G→1.6T transition and the coming CPO (co-packaged optics) architecture shift as the key technological inflection points.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **AI cluster optical intensity is inflecting faster than the market model.** Datacom demand is compounding >70% YoY at the component level; Coherent's own data centre revenue grew **+85% YoY** in FQ1'27. Management now guides **>60% pro forma revenue growth for FY2027** (more than double FY26's rate), with line of sight to a **$4B quarter in FY2028** vs $2.37B today.
- **Contractual demand visibility is unprecedented for this industry.** Coherent has a **~$20B five-year "PhotonLink" agreement** with an unnamed AI customer plus a separate NVIDIA supply agreement, with PhotonLink ramping toward **$250M/quarter by end-CY2027**. This converts a historically book-to-bill business into backlog-driven revenue.
- **US regulatory protection against Chinese module vendors.** The Trump administration/FCC is drafting a **ban on new Chinese-made optical transceivers and data centre devices** (Reuters, Aug 2026). Coherent (~17–25% transceiver share, the largest Western player) is the primary structural beneficiary if Innolight/Eoptolink are excluded from US hyperscaler builds.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Capacity capex is consuming cash and carries demand-timing risk.** Coherent's quarterly capex exploded to **$776M from $104M** a year earlier, and **operating cash flow turned negative**. The entire thesis requires contracted volume to arrive before depreciation and interest burden bite. This is the single largest financial risk in the name.
- **Severe customer concentration and dual-sourcing caps pricing power.** Hyperscalers and NVIDIA deliberately qualify two or more suppliers — NVIDIA has **parallel partnerships with both Coherent and Lumentum**. Orders can shift between suppliers on price, which is precisely why Coherent monetises "volume, not scarcity," unlike Lumentum's tighter allocation stance.
- **Consolidation is reshaping the competitive set, and industrial end-markets remain in a trough.** Marvell's **$115B all-stock acquisition of Lumentum** (announced 2 Nov 2026, closing H1 2028) creates a vertically integrated optical+silicon competitor with far deeper balance sheet and co-design reach. Meanwhile the non-AI half of the sector is weak: IPG Photonics grew just **+2.7%** with a **1.3% operating margin**.

### 5. Coherent's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **Vertically integrated InP ownership** is the decisive asset. The industry bottleneck is laser/EML supply, not module assembly; Coherent and Lumentum are the scarce Western sources. That is a durable barrier to entry (12–18 month qualification cycles, multi-billion fab economics).
- **Margin trajectory validates operating leverage**: operating margin 2.6% → 5.4% → 9.5% → 12.7%, and 15.9% in the latest quarter. Gross margin up ~660bp in two years.
- **Scale + backlog**: ~$7.9B TTM revenue is the largest pure-play in the comp set, backed by a $20B contract and NVIDIA qualification.
- **Caveats that keep it from "very strong":** it is the volume/price-taker rather than the scarcity-pricer, dual-sourcing risk is real, cash flow is currently negative on capex, and the Marvell–Lumentum combination will create a better-capitalised rival.

**Porter's Five Forces:** Supplier power **High** (InP substrates/EMLs — but Coherent is self-supplied); Buyer power **High** (hyperscaler/NVIDIA concentration, dual-sourcing); Rivalry **High** (Innolight, Eoptolink at +187%, Lumentum/Marvell); New entrants **Low–Moderate** (capital intensity, qualification); Substitutes **Moderate** (CPO threatens pluggables post-2028; copper at short reach). Top-4 share ~60–65%, implying an HHI near 1,100–1,300 — moderately concentrated and rising.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

Demand is contracted, supply is constrained, the cycle is mid-stage rather than late (1.6T ramp still ahead, CPO transition extends the runway), and US policy is actively excluding the lowest-cost Chinese competitors. The sector's central risk is not demand but **capital-cycle discipline** — negative free cash flow across the group while everyone races to build InP capacity, and the possibility that 2028 capacity arrives into a digestion phase. Within that, **Coherent is the best risk/reward in the comp set**: peer-median-to-better margins at the lowest multiple, with growth acceleration already visible in the latest quarter.
