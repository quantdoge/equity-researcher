### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (rising)

**Evidence for:**
- **78% of global electricity from renewables** (>550 GWh/yr), avoiding ~234,000 tCO2e in FY2024 — genuinely above semiconductor-sector norms, and cheap to defend because it is mostly contracted, not purchased offsets.
- Targets: net-zero Scope 1+2 by **FY2040**; FY2025 report added a **50% Scope 1+2 reduction by FY2030**.
- Joined the **Responsible Business Alliance in 2024** — late for a company of this scale, but it imports third-party supply-chain discipline.

**Evidence against / gaps:**
- **No interim Scope 3 target disclosed.** For a components supplier whose customers report Scope 3, this is the single biggest disclosure gap — and it is the exact metric hyperscalers now ask for in RFPs.
- Absolute emissions are likely **increasing**, not decreasing: revenue is compounding >50% y/y with heavy fab capex. A percentage-renewable headline can mask rising absolute load. **I could not retrieve verified absolute Scope 1/2 tonnage** (the FY2025 Global Impact Report is published only as a PDF that the retrieval tool could not parse) — treat intensity claims as unverified.
- Energy-intensive crystal growth/wafering and toxic-gas handling mean physical/operational environmental risk is structural, not discretionary.

**Verdict:** Not a stranded-asset story; it is a **disclosure-credibility and procurement-qualification** story. Medium, trending toward the risk side until Scope 3 targets and absolute tonnage are disclosed.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Workforce:** 51,478 employees; manufacturing concentrated in the US, China, Philippines, Vietnam, Thailand, Malaysia — i.e., the higher labour-risk geographies of the electronics supply chain. RBA membership only since 2024 means audit coverage is early-stage; **no published supplier-audit closure rates** located.
- **Safety:** Cited a "Sapphire-level Certification Champion" recognition from the Board of Safety Professionals — a **weak, non-standard credential**; I found **no disclosed TRIR or fatality rate**, which is the metric that matters. Disclosure gap, not evidence of harm.
- **Restructuring:** Post-merger integration produced multi-year layoffs, including a **California WARN filing for 108 permanent layoffs at 41762 Christy St., Fremont CA (effective Apr-2023)**. Social/reputational cost was real; financial cost immaterial (<$10M).
- **Talent/key-person risk is the live issue:** the CTO, Chief Supply Chain Officer and Chief Strategy Officer seats all turned over in the FY2026 proxy; a $20B customer backlog now depends on execution by a recently reshuffled team.
- **Product safety/quality:** No recall or field-failure event found. Optical component failure at a hyperscaler is the tail risk; nothing in the record suggests an incident.

**Verdict:** Medium — driven by supply-chain labour opacity and integration churn, not by any known violation.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.5 / 10**

**Positives:**
- All four Class Three nominees (Corasanti, Hatter, Skaggs, Vij) are **independent**; EY as auditor; single share class, **no dual-class voting structure**, no poison pill identified.
- Pay design is defensible: annual bonus split **equally between revenue and adjusted EBITDA**; long-term incentive **60% PSU / 40% RSU**, with PSUs on **relative TSR**. That is genuine at-risk alignment, not time-vesting camouflage.
- Board quality scored **A-** by Boardroom Alpha; 1-year TSR +62.2% — the FY2026 payout was large but the year was real, and >60% of FY27 revenue is already booked.

**Negatives (why not an 8+):**
- **Classified (staggered) board** with three-year terms — entrenched by design and a standing proxy-advisor objection. Corasanti's **24-year tenure** is exactly the refreshment problem staggered boards protect.
- **CEO pay $28.3M, +661% y/y** (stock awards $23.8M, 84% of mix) alongside a **190% EIP payout** — quantum is at the aggressive end and FY25→FY26 step-change invites a say-on-pay backlash. No say-on-pay result is available yet (AGM is 18-Nov-2026).
- **No lead independent director**; the CEO sits on the board as an employee director — separation of chair/CEO exists but the counterweight is thinner than best practice.
- **Rapid dilution (26%) and a strategic shareholder (NVIDIA, 4.0%)** create a related-party/independence question for a company selling into NVIDIA's ecosystem. Terms of the PhotonLink agreement and the equity issuance warrant scrutiny.
- **Gap:** no accessible DEF 14A full text (SEC 403 on direct fetch; the filing tool could not locate the primary document), so I could not verify committee composition, related-party transactions, or any ESG-linked compensation metric.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **United States:** SEC climate-disclosure rule remains **stayed** — low near-term exposure. **California SB 253/261** is the operative rule (COHR exceeds revenue thresholds and operates in CA): Scope 1+2 assurance-track reporting and Scope 3 disclosure begin in the 2026-27 cycle — this is precisely where the missing Scope 3 target bites. OSHA/EPA (RCRA, TSCA, toxic-gas Risk Management Program) apply at fab level.
- **Export controls (highest-probability regulatory hit):** EAR/BIS end-use and Entity List screening across a China manufacturing base; any optics/InP exposure to restricted end uses is a live enforcement theme industry-wide (cf. the Cadence and TE Connectivity cases). **ITAR/customer-flow-down compliance** persists even after the A&D divestiture.
- **EU:** **CSRD** — Coherent's EU subsidiaries (Germany, Finland, Netherlands) are in scope, pulling group-level ESRS reporting requirements up to the US parent regardless of SEC inaction. REACH/RoHS/ESPR add product-design obligations.
- **CBAM:** immaterial (little steel/aluminium import content).
- **China:** dual-use export licensing and critical-mineral controls — the key asymmetric exposure.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified; the mitigant is that COHR has been **deleveraging** and raised $2B of equity, reducing refinancing pressure regardless of ESG terms. **Unverified** — I could not review the credit agreement.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Above-median on decarbonisation *inputs* (78% renewable electricity, FY2040 net-zero, FY2030 interim Scope 1+2 target) and clean on headline governance structure (single share class, independent nominees, relative-TSR pay). Below median on disclosure *depth* (no Scope 3 target, no TRIR, no D&I metrics retrievable, RBA only since 2024) and on board structure (staggered, 24-year director, no lead independent director). No serious controversy on record. This is a **"good operator, thin reporter"** profile — not a Leader, and nowhere near Laggard.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL for the thesis**

ESG is **not** the swing factor in COHR — the AI optics capex cycle, PhotonLink ramp and margin expansion are. Nothing in the ESG record justifies a valuation haircut or a cost-of-capital premium: no enforcement actions, no stranded assets, no dual-class control, and a renewable-energy position that actively helps win hyperscaler business.

**Three ways ESG could become financially decisive — monitor these, not the ESG score:**
1. **China critical-minerals retaliation (asymmetric, underappreciated).** Ga/Ge/rare-earth licensing is the one ESG-adjacent shock that could compress gross margin faster than the market models. This belongs in the bear case ahead of any carbon narrative.
2. **Scope 3 disclosure as a gating criterion.** If COHR cannot produce auditable product carbon footprints and Scope 3 targets by the CA SB 253 / CSRD cycle, the cost is commercial (RFQ exclusion from hyperscalers), not regulatory.
3. **Say-on-pay at the 18-Nov-2026 AGM.** A sub-70% support vote on the $28.3M package — combined with a staggered board that cannot be refreshed quickly — would be the first credible signal of governance-driven multiple pressure on a stock already de-rated ~44% from its high.

**Recommendation:** treat COHR as **ESG-neutral / no discount required**. Do not underwrite any ESG-driven upside. Size positions on the AI-optics cycle and on the China materials supply chain, where the identifiable ESG risk actually lives.
