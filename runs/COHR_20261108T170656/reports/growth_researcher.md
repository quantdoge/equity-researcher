## Coherent Corp (COHR) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $192.81 | Market cap $37.7B | EV $39.2B | Nov 6, 2026 close**

---

### 1. Growth Story

Coherent has completed a transformation from a cyclical industrial-laser conglomerate into the **vertically integrated optical engine of the AI datacenter**. It owns the full stack — indium phosphide wafer growth, EML/DFB lasers, silicon photonics, and the 800G/1.6T transceiver modules — which is why **NVIDIA took a $2B equity stake and signed a multi-year, multi-product supply agreement in March 2026**. Datacenter & Communications is now 82% of revenue and growing **79% YoY**, yet the stock sits **55% below its June 2026 peak of ~$427** — a violent sector de-rating against *accelerating* fundamentals. My thesis: this is a dislocation, not a deterioration.

### 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **Optical transceiver TAM** ~$20B today → $40B+ by 2030 as 800G transitions to 1.6T and 3.2T, and as optics push *inside* the rack (scale-up) where copper hits physical limits. Coherent's DC&C segment at $5.27B FY26 (+40%) and a ~$1.95B quarterly run-rate implies **~20–25% share of its served optical market**.
- **CPO (co-packaged optics)** is the incremental TAM: the NVIDIA partnership explicitly targets "optics technology to scale next-generation data center architecture," i.e., external laser sources and photonic engines for future NVLink generations. This is a market that barely exists today and could be multi-billion annually by 2028–29.
- **Vertical integration is the moat.** Competitors (Fabrinet, Innolight, Eoptolink) assemble modules but buy lasers; Coherent grows its own InP. In a **supply-constrained market expected to persist through CY2026–27**, owning the laser supply is pricing power and allocation priority.
- **Differentiation vs. Lumentum** (the other half of NVIDIA's $4B optics bet): Coherent is broader — datacom + industrial lasers + aerospace/defense + SiC/GaN — giving optionality and non-AI cash flow, but also historical drag from slower segments.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **FQ2'27 earnings — Feb 3, 2027.** Guidance already implies **$2.57B revenue (+63% YoY) and $2.23 EPS**; another beat would be the 8th straight. Highest-impact near-term event.
- **1.6T volume ramp through CY2026–27** — management expects both 800G and 1.6T to grow significantly; mix shift to 1.6T is gross-margin accretive.
- **CPO/next-gen NVIDIA architecture qualification** (calendar 2027). Any design-win disclosure re-rates the terminal TAM.
- **Capacity unlock**: $1.1B FY26 capex (Sherman TX, Saxonburg PA, InP fabs) converting to revenue in FY27–28; capex intensity should fall as a % of sales → **FCF inflection**, the single biggest fix to the Rule of 40.
- **Non-core divestitures / deleveraging** — asset sales of slower industrial units would sharpen the AI pure-play narrative and fund buybacks.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **STRONG BUY**

The setup is the one I look for: **fundamentals inflecting up while the multiple compresses down.** Revenue growth accelerated from 22.5% to 49.9%; gross margins up ~820bps in two years; a 100% beat rate with 6.6% average upside; FY30 estimates revised up 19% in a quarter — and the stock de-rated 55% on an AI-photonics sector unwind and capex-burn anxiety. At **19.7x FY27E and 12.8x FY28E** (PEG 0.38), the market is pricing in a sharp deceleration that the backlog, the NVIDIA supply agreement, and the 1.6T ramp do not support.

- **Sell-side targets cluster at $350–455** (Stifel $450, Raymond James $448, Rosenblatt $400, Deutsche Bank $400, Bernstein $350, Susquehanna $350) vs. $193 today — **~100%+ implied upside**, with the only low target ($230 Northland) stale from May 2026.
- My 12-month fair value: **$300–375** (FY27E $9.81 at 30–38x, or FY28E $15.01 at 20–25x). 5-year implied revenue from the current EV — ~$28B by FY30 — is **achievable** if 1.6T and CPO ramp on schedule; the market is currently discounting roughly half of that.

**Position-sizing note:** beta 2.23 and a realized 55% drawdown mean this is a high-conviction, high-volatility holding. Scale in; do not chase 14% single-day rebounds.

### 7. Key Risks

1. **AI capex digestion / customer concentration.** This is now an AI-datacenter company (82% of revenue) levered to a handful of hyperscalers and NVIDIA. A single quarter of order pushouts would compress both estimates and the multiple simultaneously — a double hit.
2. **Free cash flow is negative and cash-hungry.** FY26 FCF of **–$1.03B** on $1.11B capex plus a $1.10B working-capital build, with operating cash flow of only $79M against $787M net income. If capex does not normalize by FY28, the equity story becomes a capital-consumption story.
3. **Dilution and leverage.** Diluted shares +30% in six quarters; $46M quarterly interest expense. The NVIDIA stake is strategically valuable but economically dilutive.
4. **Competitive pricing pressure.** Lumentum (also NVIDIA-backed), Broadcom, Marvell, and Chinese module vendors (Innolight, Eoptolink) are all adding 1.6T capacity; transceiver ASPs historically erode on each node transition. Vertical integration protects margin, not share.
5. **Execution/integration history.** FY24 revenue *declined* 8.8% post-merger and FY24 gross margin fell to 30.9% — proof this management team can stumble. The current ramp is being executed at 3x the pace that previously caused disruption.
6. **Valuation is not cheap on cash flow.** 25x trailing EV/EBITDA and negative P/FCF leave no cushion if growth decelerates to 20%.
7. **Sentiment/technical.** Already in a downtrend from $427; momentum funds exiting create persistent supply. Volatility around Feb 3 earnings will be severe (Nov 4–6 alone: –13%, then +14%).
