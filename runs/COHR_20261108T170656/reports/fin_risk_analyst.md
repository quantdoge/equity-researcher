## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: SUB-INVESTMENT GRADE (BB) — but a mid-cycle inflection, not a distress story**

Corroborated ratings: **S&P upgraded COHR to 'BB' with Positive outlook** (strong AI datacom demand + debt paydown) and **Fitch affirms 'BB', Stable** (10/11/2025). The upgrade was earned on deleveraging: gross debt fell from $4.30B (FY24) → $3.89B (FY25) → $3.54B (FY26) while EBITDA grew from $0.70B → $1.11B → $1.56B. Net leverage collapsed from ~4.8x to **~1.0x**.

**However, the credit trajectory reversed in the most recent quarter.** Total debt rose **+$809M in a single quarter** (to $4.347B) to fund negative free cash flow. This is the single most important credit development and it is not yet visible in trailing leverage ratios.

| Credit metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 | Q1 FY27 (LTM) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total debt ($M) | 4,303 | 3,894 | 3,538 | 4,347 |
| Net debt ($M) | 3,377 | 2,984 | 1,551 | 2,794 |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 4.8x | 2.7x | **0.99x** | **1.69x** |
| EBIT / interest | 0.5x | 2.3x | **5.45x** | **5.75x** |
| EBITDA / interest | 2.4x | 4.5x | 8.2x | 8.5x |
| Debt / equity | 0.54 | 0.46 | 0.31 | 0.37 |

*LTM = FY26 less Q4 FY26 plus Q1 FY27. Interest burden is falling (quarterly interest $58.7M → $41.1M → $46.3M) as higher-coupon debt is retired — a genuine positive.*

**Near-term default risk: LOW.** Short-term debt is only $19.9M; debt amortization is ~$107M/yr over the next twelve months against a **$700M revolver maturing 2030** (with ~$664M available per the 10-Q). There is **no refinancing cliff** before 2030. The distress risk here is not a maturity wall — it is a multi-year cash-consumption problem if the AI capex cycle rolls over while the balance sheet is still funding growth.

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue –20%

Baseline FY2026: revenue $7,118M, gross margin 37.5%, opex $1,767M, EBIT $1,038M, EBITDA $1,560M, interest $190M, net debt $2,794M (Q1 FY27).

| | **Base (FY26)** | **Mild –20%** (GM 35%, opex –8%) | **Severe –20%** (GM 28%, opex –15%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 7,118 | 5,694 | 5,694 |
| Gross profit | 2,669 | 1,993 | 1,594 |
| Opex | 1,767 | 1,626 | 1,502 |
| EBIT | 1,038 | 367 | 92 |
| EBITDA | 1,560 | 889 | 614 |
| **Interest coverage** | 5.45x | **1.9x** | **0.48x** |
| **Net debt / EBITDA** | 0.99x | **3.14x** | **4.55x** |
| Rating implication | BB | BB / BB– | **B / negative watch** |

**The credit survives a 20% revenue decline — but only barely, and only if management cuts capex.** At 3.1–4.6x net leverage the company sits at or inside typical BB leverage thresholds; S&P's own commentary anchors its BB view on leverage "below the 5x area," so the severe case lands right on the edge of a downgrade.

**The saving grace is that the burn is discretionary.** In the severe case, capex could fall from a ~$3.1B run-rate to a $500–600M maintenance level, and inventory (currently $3.34B, **211 days** — up from 140 days in FY25) would liquidate, releasing roughly $1.0–1.5B of working capital. That combination turns a –$3.5B annualised burn into **positive FCF of roughly $0.5–1.0B** in a downturn. Coherent is, ironically, more cash-generative in a mild recession than in the current boom, because the boom is what is consuming the cash.

**The genuinely dangerous scenario is not –20% revenue — it is a 2-year continuation of the current burn.** If the company funds another 3–4 quarters at the Q1 FY27 pace (–$868M FCF), net debt would reach $5.5–6.5B, and on flat EBITDA of ~$1.65B that is 3.3–3.9x *before* any revenue decline. Leverage would be double-counted against a cyclical peak. That is the path to a downgrade, not the demand shock itself.

**Liquidity runway:** cash of $1.753B plus ~$0.66B revolver = **~$2.4B**. Against a $868M quarterly burn, that is **~2.8 quarters** of coverage — but only ~1 quarter if you exclude the revolver. This is thin enough to make a capital raise or further debt issuance a live 2027 event.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Breakdown:
- **Credit risk: Moderate** (Sub-Investment Grade BB, 1.7x net leverage, 5.75x coverage, no maturity wall, upgraded by both agencies — the balance-sheet stock is fine)
- **Liquidity risk: High** (–$868M quarterly FCF, ~2.8 quarters of coverage, burn funded by new debt, cash declining $234M/qtr)
- **Market risk: Critical** (80% vol, 2.8 beta, –56% current drawdown, 18% single-day earnings moves)
- **FCF sustainability: Critical** (–$1.03B FY26, –$2.74% yield, capex at 33% of revenue, working capital consuming $1.74B over five quarters)

**Synthesis:** Coherent is a fundamentally sound, deleveraged BB credit that has chosen to convert its balance sheet into growth capex at precisely the moment its equity valuation is most sensitive to cash conversion. The company will not default on its debt in the next two years under almost any demand scenario. **The realistic risk is a 50–65% equity drawdown and a credit downgrade — not insolvency.** Position sizing should reflect the equity's near-option-like volatility (2.76 beta, 80% vol), not the credit's relative safety.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Working capital is the story, not capex.** Five consecutive quarters of working-capital drain totalling –$1.74B (FY26 –$1.10B; Q1 FY27 –$0.64B). Inventory went **$1.44B → $2.58B → $3.34B** in five quarters (+133%), while revenue grew 65%. Inventory days expanded **140 → 212**. In a market transitioning 800G → 1.6T transceivers, this is a direct obsolescence exposure and a demand-quality question: is the build a genuine order backlog or channel inventory?
2. **Negative FCF of –$1.03B with an *accelerating* trend.** FY26 –$1.03B; Q1 FY27 alone –$868M. TTM FCF is now roughly –$2.9B annualised.
3. **Cash conversion breakdown.** Net income $787M vs operating cash flow $79.5M in FY26 — an accrual gap of ~$708M. Piotroski flags this explicitly (F4 = 0).
4. **The burn is being funded with debt and equity simultaneously.** Total debt +$809M in one quarter; share count **+14.5% YoY** (154.8M → 177.3M, diluted 202M). Both the lenders and the shareholders are financing the same capex program. If the equity window closes, the debt burden rises faster.
5. **Cash buffer eroding.** $2.42B (Q1 FY26) → $1.99B (Q2) → **$1.75B** (Q3). Down $666M in two quarters.
6. **Valuation leaves no cushion.** ~44x FY26 EPS at $193, versus ~100x at the June peak. A 60% drawdown in five months on a business posting record revenue signals multiple compression risk that is not yet complete if cash flow does not inflect.

## 7. Data Gaps & Caveats (explicit)

- **Annual balance sheet PPE is corrupt.** FY26 annual `ppe_net` = $21.286B, which exceeds total assets ($18.30B). The quarterly feed for the same date gives $2.999B; the Q1 FY27 figure of $3.870B is consistent with a ~$1.9B capex program less ~$0.54B D&A. **I used the quarterly PPE.** Ratios in this report are unaffected (Altman x1 uses working capital/assets, not PPE), but the annual feed cannot be trusted for asset-composition work.
- **Q1 FY27 cash-flow row carries a ~1000x scaling error** (reported OCF –$92.68B, capex –$775.5B, net income $381.6B). I normalised to –$92.7M / –$775.5M / $381.6M, which reconciles with the capex ramp and the reported $2.37B revenue. Note the reported Q1 FY27 D&A of $77.7M looks too low versus ~$140M prior quarters; treat the D&A figure as suspect.
- **`net_debt` field inconsistency:** annual balance sheet states $2.376B while total debt ($3.538B) less cash ($1.987B) = $1.551B. The leverage tool uses $1.551B, which I consider correct; the $2.376B field may embed lease or other obligations.
- **No covenant schedule, no CDS spreads, no Sortino ratio** were retrievable. Covenant headroom is inferred from reported leverage (likely ≤4.0–4.5x net leverage on the term loan), giving very large current headroom — but this is inference, not disclosure.
- **No valid peer comparison.** The sector tool returns mega-cap Technology by market cap (AAPL, NVDA, MSFT, TSM, AVGO, SKHY), not optical/photonics comparables (LITE, FN, AAOI, MTSI). All ratio analysis above is therefore absolute and time-series, not cross-sectional. **Peer relative risk is unassessed.**
- Only three years of annual statements were available; the "3-year" volatility window (60.2% max drawdown) therefore spans a single cycle.
