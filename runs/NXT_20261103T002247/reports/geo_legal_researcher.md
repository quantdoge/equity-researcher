## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US clean-energy policy & tax-credit dependence — Probability: High | Impact: High**
The economic model is now effectively a US industrial-policy derivative. Section 45X credits reduced cost of sales by **$103.3M in Q1 FY27 alone** (~11% of revenue and roughly 31% of gross profit), with a **$311.6M 45X receivable** on the balance sheet. OBBBA's FEOC/prohibited-foreign-entity regime imposes a **material-assistance cost ratio starting at 50% in 2026, rising to 85% by 2030** for solar components, with phase-downs of the credit itself; IRS Notice 2026-15 (Feb-12-2026) supplies only interim guidance. Any adverse interpretation of tracker/steel content sourcing, or a demand hit from 45Y/48E FEOC compliance failures at the *customer* level, hits both volume and margin simultaneously. This is the single largest risk in the file and it is jurisdictional, not competitive.

**② Tariff and trade-regime churn — Probability: High (already occurring) | Impact: Medium–High**
Steel is the dominant bill-of-material input; Section 232 steel/aluminum derivatives tariffs, the **new Section 232 polysilicon-and-derivatives framework announced Aug-6-2026** (minimum import prices plus a 15% ad valorem layer), and AD/CVD orders are all live. NXT paid **~$1M in AD/CVD cash deposits** in the quarter and reported tariffs of **$3.9M net of refunds**; FY26 results included ~$47–67M/quarter of "45X vendor rebates and tariffs, net" — i.e., tariff and credit effects are large enough to be called out in guidance. Retaliatory risk against US-origin content in India/Brazil is a secondary channel.

**③ Emerging-market execution, FX and political risk — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium**
Rest-of-World revenue fell 40% YoY in Q1 FY27, driven by Latin America and the Middle East. Brazil is in a fiscally stressed post-election regime with active WTO litigation against US tariffs; India–US relations are tariff-frictional; the Middle East carries live US–Iran conflict and sanctions expansion risk (including secondary sanctions on third-country counterparties). The Saudi JV mitigates local-content risk but introduces partner/governance and reputational exposure (51% Abunayyan control, and the plant's output feeds a market adjacent to sanctioned counterparties).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action (In re Nextracker) — resolved favorably.** 10(b)/20(a) action filed 2024 (lead-plaintiff deadline Feb-25-2025). The court **granted defendants' motion to dismiss on March 9, 2026**, and the matter **concluded April 6, 2026**. Parallel **derivative actions** asserting claims on the Company's behalf were disclosed in the FY26 10-K; residual defense costs are possible but appear non-material. (Kessler Topaz case page; 10-K.)
- **GameChange patent war — active, two-way, US.** Nextpower sued GameChange Energy in **D. Del. on June 1, 2026**, alleging infringement of three patents (two "self-powered" tracker patents issued 2022/2024, plus TrueCapture software), seeking **treble damages**. GameChange has countersued alleging NXT infringes **US Patent 12,449,161** (high-tilt stow) and denies infringement. Precedent: NXT settled a prior tracker patent suit against Solar FlexRack/Northern States Metals in 2021. Litigation is IP-defensive (protecting ~#1 global tracker share per Wood Mackenzie) rather than existential; injunction risk on TrueCapture/self-powered features is the tail.
- **Tax/regulatory:** $393.2M **Tax Receivable Agreement** liability (85% of tax benefits to Flex/TPG) — a structural cash leakage, not a regulatory breach.
- **M&A/CFIUS adjacency:** Prevalon Energy (storage, up to $365M, closed 7/17/2026) and Zigor/Apex power-conversion assets (up to $80.5M, closed 7/30/2026) — foreign (Spanish) target assets warrant standard FDI screening review.
- **No disclosed DOJ, SEC enforcement, FCPA, AML or sanctions investigation** in the FY26 10-K or Q1 FY27 10-Q.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **45X credit substantiation / FEOC attestation (elevated).** A $311.6M receivable plus ~$400M/yr of P&L benefit rests on supplier-level material-assistance and PFE certifications. IRS interim guidance leaves gaps; a clawback or disallowance is a plausible adverse scenario. Highest-priority audit-integrity item.
2. **AD/CVD and tariff-classification exposure via steel suppliers.** ~$1M/quarter of cash deposits and supplier-origin certifications create transshipment/evasion exposure (EAPA-style risk), plus Section 232 scope questions on fabricated steel frames.
3. **Forced-labor/UFLPA supply-chain traceability** for steel and aluminum inputs — standard for the sector; no adverse finding disclosed.
4. **Sanctions/secondary-sanctions touchpoints** through the Saudi JV, Middle East FZCO and any Gulf counterparties — low current exposure, but Iran-related secondary sanctions expansion is an active 2026 theme.
5. **Data privacy / AI:** TrueCapture and new robotics/software layers add modest data-governance obligations; not material at present.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Legal risk has *decreased* (securities class action dismissed and concluded; IP disputes are symmetric and manageable). Geopolitical risk has *increased* and shifted decisively toward **single-jurisdiction policy dependence**: 77–83% US revenue, a large share of gross profit derived from a US tax credit now subject to FEOC content thresholds tightening from 50% to 85% by 2030, plus layered Section 232/AD-CVD tariff exposure on steel. International diversification is moving the wrong way (RoW −40% YoY) even as the company expands physically into Saudi Arabia, Europe and India.

**Sub-ratings:** US policy/regulatory risk **High**; trade/tariff risk **High**; EM political/FX risk **Medium–High**; litigation risk **Medium**; sanctions/FCPA/AML risk **Low**.

**What would change the rating:** upgrade to *Low/Medium* if RoW returns to 30%+ of revenue and FEOC safe-harbor guidance confirms NXT's steel sourcing; downgrade to *High* on any 45X disallowance, a FEOC-driven demand shock in US utility-scale bookings, or an adverse injunction in the GameChange matter.

**Gaps to note:** Country-level revenue below the US/RoW split is not disclosed; the status of the derivative actions and the exact docket posture of the GameChange counterclaim were not retrievable (SEC.gov and pv-tech direct fetches returned 403), and were sourced from secondary reporting.
