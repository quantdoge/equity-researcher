# Sector Brief: Nextracker / "Nextpower" (NXT) — Solar Trackers & Utility-Scale Solar Equipment

**Data note:** FMP classifies NXT under sector "Energy" (industry "Solar"), which pulls an irrelevant peer set (XOM, CVX, SHEL). I substituted the economically correct peer group — solar equipment/tracker vendors — and flagged where estimates are mine. NXT's fiscal year ends 31 March; latest data = Q2 FY27, ended 2 Oct 2026.

---

## 1. Sector Overview

NXT competes in utility-scale solar equipment — principally single-axis trackers plus software, and increasingly storage, electrical BOS and power conversion. Global tracker demand is growing at roughly 20% p.a. (Mordor: ~$38B in 2026 → ~$96B by 2031; note published market-size estimates vary 5–9x by definition, so I weight the *growth rate*, not the level). The end-market is booming but policy-timed: US utility-scale installs hit 9.6 GWdc in Q2 2026 (+61% YoY) as developers rushed the 4 July 2026 safe-harbour deadline, taking total Q2 to 11.4 GWdc (+45%); SEIA/Wood Mackenzie still model ~44 GWdc/yr through 2031, but 2026 is roughly flat because ~30% of the early-stage pipeline is stuck in permitting. Structure is oligopolistic — NXT is the clear #1, with Array Technologies a distant #2 (ARRY's 2025 revenue is only 36% of NXT's) and Shoals a niche #3 — and barriers are real (bankability track records, wind-load engineering, warranty books, domestic-content manufacturing).

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Utility-scale demand + AI/data-centre load growth.** 43.4 GW of utility-scale solar planned for 2026 (EIA); solar+storage = 70% of H1 2026 US capacity additions. Management describes an "energy super cycle" driven by data-centre electricity demand — a genuinely new, non-subsidy demand driver.
- **~216–240 GWdc safe-harboured pipeline with a hard 31 Dec 2027 placed-in-service deadline.** This converts to contracted, near-term demand: NXT's backlog is at a record **>$6B** as of Q2 FY27 (vs >$5B a year earlier), giving unusually high revenue visibility through CY2027.
- **Content expansion and internationalisation.** Trackers are standard kit (single-axis = 86.8% of the tracker market), and NXT is layering storage, electrical BOS and power conversion onto the same project relationship — raising revenue per project. Its 100% domestic-content tracker also captures IRA domestic-content bonuses that peers cannot easily match.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Policy cliff risk.** 45Y/48E credits are phasing down, safe-harbouring closed 4 July 2026, and projects must be in service by 31 Dec 2027 or lose eligibility. That pulls 2027 demand forward and creates a plausible 2028 air pocket absent legislative extension. Permitting delays hit ~30% of early-stage projects; FEOC/foreign-entity rules add compliance cost and supply-chain constraints.
- **Steel/aluminium/copper tariffs.** Section 232 duties are 50% on most steel, aluminium and copper articles, with only a transitional regime to 31 Dec 2027 (and a reduced 15% rate on certain grid equipment). Tariffs have repeatedly hit gross margin (Q2 FY26 disclosed ~$67M/$82M/$48M tariff items); NXT mitigates via domestic steel and pass-throughs, but the mechanism is volatile.
- **Price competition and buyer power.** ARRY grew 40% in 2025 and is fighting for share; utility buyers are large, concentrated and price-sensitive. NXT's gross margin fell 260bps in FY26 (33.7%→32.6%) and 130bps in Q2 FY27 (32.4%→31.1%) as tariffs and a richer mix of lower-margin BOS/storage diluted the P&L. Distributed solar is separately contracting (~−23% in 2026) and financing costs remain a drag.

**Read-across:** NXT is at the peer median on current growth but has the highest 3-year CAGR in the group (25.0%) — and, critically, it *converts* margin far better than anyone but First Solar: operating margin is **1.6x the peer median** on a gross margin *below* the median. That spread is the signature of a hardware business with software-like operating leverage and a lean opex base (SG&A only 9.6% of revenue). NXT's gross margin has also expanded ~1,750bps since FY23 (15.1% → 32.6%), the largest improvement in the group — the opposite of ARRY, whose gross margin has been erratic (13.0% → 32.5% → 26.4% → 23.3%) and whose operating margin swung to −24.8% in 2024. Share is clearly consolidating toward NXT: its revenue is 2.8x ARRY and 7.5x SHLS, and the equity market has rewarded that — NXT −10% vs peer median −27% over twelve months, with every peer down sharply.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **#1 position with a widening gap.** Revenue 2.8x the nearest pure-play rival; record >$6B backlog; ~200GW+ deployed creates a reference-base and data advantage (real-world operating data feeds its software/optimisation, a genuine moat per third-party analysis).
- **Best-in-class profitability conversion.** 19.6% FY26 operating margin vs 11.9% peer median, achieved despite a *below-median* gross margin — evidence of pricing discipline plus opex leverage. Only FSLR (a differentiated thin-film manufacturer with a tariff-supported ASP) earns more.
- **Diversified growth engine.** Tracker core + storage/BOS/power conversion attach + international projects reduce single-product, single-geography risk versus ARRY (trackers only) and ENPH (residential-exposed, revenue still 37% below its 2022 peak).
- **Caveats:** gross margin is compressing, customer concentration among large developers is high, and NXT's beta is 1.93 — the equity is a high-torque policy trade, not a defensive compounder.

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

Structurally attractive: ~20% volume CAGR, an oligopoly with real entry barriers, a consolidated leader with a record backlog, and a new structural demand driver (data-centre load growth) that is not subsidy-dependent. The discount is policy: the 31 Dec 2027 placed-in-service cliff, Section 232 input-cost pass-through risk, and permitting friction mean the sector's *earnings* are visible through CY2027 but the *multiple* is hostage to Washington and to long-term rate expectations — which is precisely why the whole peer group de-rated 10–55% over the past year despite double-digit growth. Attractive on 3–5 year fundamentals; expect above-market volatility and a 2028 demand air pocket that must be legislatively or internationally offset.
