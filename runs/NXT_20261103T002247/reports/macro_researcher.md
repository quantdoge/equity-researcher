## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: RISK-ON, RATE-HOSTILE — "hawkish reflation."**

The US economy is not slowing; it is re-accelerating *and* re-inflating simultaneously, which is the worst possible combination for a long-duration, capital-goods growth stock. Q3 2026 real GDP came in at **+3.4% SAAR** (advance, 29 Oct), up from 2.2% in Q2 and 2.5% in Q1, with unemployment at just **4.2%**. But September CPI printed **+0.6% m/m and +3.72% y/y** (index 336.30 vs 324.25 a year prior), a clear break higher from ~2.7% in mid-2025. The composition matters: headline is energy-led (**energy +20.4% y/y**), while **core CPI is 2.8%** — an oil-supply shock layered on a still-firm core.

The Fed has re-turned hawkish: it **hiked 25bp on 16 September 2026 to 3.75–4.00%**, held on 28 October (with two dissents), and a Reuters poll of economists now has a **December hike** as the base case. The market agrees: the **2Y is at 4.74%, 74bp above the effective funds rate of 3.88%** — that is a hiking-cycle curve, not an easing one.

The critical move for NXT is at the long end. The **10Y has repriced from 4.44% (30 June) to 5.13%**, a +69bp bear-steepening in four months, with the **10Y TIPS real yield rising from 2.20% to 2.60%**. For a business whose end-market is 25-year utility-scale power projects financed with debt, the real yield is the single most important price in the world.

Credit is the one genuinely supportive leg: **HY OAS of 344bp** is near cycle tights, though it has widened ~80bp from 263bp on 31 August — early stress, not yet a broken market. NXT itself carries **>$1B cash and zero debt**, so this is a demand-side, not a solvency, consideration.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Rates & monetary policy — NEGATIVE (highest-magnitude variable).** NXT's revenue is a derivative of project finance. A 40bp rise in the 10Y real yield compresses leveraged solar equity IRRs by roughly 200–300bp, which pushes marginal projects below developer hurdle rates and delays final investment decisions, particularly for 2028+ COD. Simultaneously, the discount rate hits NXT's own valuation: the stock **fell 51% from $156.78 (13 May 2026) to $76.48 (26 Oct 2026)** — precisely as the 10Y rose from 4.45% to 5.05% — while earnings estimates went *up*. That is textbook duration compression on a 1.93-beta name, and it is the dominant explanation for the drawdown, not company performance.

**The offset: the de-rating has largely done its work.** At $91.13 on the FY27 adjusted EPS midpoint of **$4.74**, NXT trades at **~19x** versus **~33x** at the May peak — a ~42% multiple compression against a raised guide. It is no longer a crowded long-duration asset; the rate shock is substantially in the price.

**Policy / fiscal — MIXED, tilting negative.** OBBBA ended the 45Y/48E runway: projects beginning construction after **4 July 2026** must be placed in service by **31 December 2027**. That creates a demand air-pocket risk in FY29 (calendar 2028) as the credit-supported cohort exhausts. The offset is that **Section 232 polysilicon tariffs (15% + minimum import price, effective 4 December 2026)** raise module costs, and higher module prices *increase* tracker attach rates and tracker value per project, since a tracker lifts yield per module by ~15–25%. NXT's distributed US manufacturing footprint (plus $40M capex for domestic NX Power Conversion) also insulates it from tariff pass-through better than import-dependent peers.

**Input-cost inflation — NEGATIVE and already visible in guidance.** Management explicitly flagged **higher diesel, steel, freight and logistics** costs, guiding H2 FY27 adjusted gross margin to the **high-20s from 31.1% in Q2**, and adjusted EBITDA margin to the **high-teens from 23%**. Construction materials PPI at +11% y/y and copper at +45% y/y validate that guidance as conservative-to-fair. This is the channel through which CPI re-acceleration reaches NXT's P&L — a genuine margin squeeze, not a theoretical one.

**Electricity demand / growth — STRONGLY POSITIVE.** This is the structural counterweight. US solar installed **11.4 GWdc in Q2 2026, +45% y/y**, with ~70 GW expected across 2026–27; solar plus storage was ~70% of all new US generating capacity in 2026. Data-centre load growth is pulling NXT into storage and power conversion — a behind-the-meter deal with a tier-one hyperscaler contributed to ~$100M of storage revenue in the quarter. Record **>$1B bookings and >$6B backlog** are direct evidence that demand is not rate-constrained yet. Critically, hyperscaler PPAs are underwritten on power scarcity economics, not tax credits — a far more durable demand driver than the expiring ITC.

**Currency — MILDLY NEGATIVE.** 82% of Q2 revenue was US, ~80/20 US/international for FY27, so direct translation exposure is modest. But international is the *growth* leg (+25% q/q; footprint now 68 countries post-Zimmermann), and the broad dollar has strengthened **3.2% in seven weeks** to 121.7. A stronger USD both reduces reported international revenue and raises USD-denominated equipment costs for Indian, Brazilian, Mexican and Middle Eastern projects, deferring those FIDs. EM FX weakness is a real 2027 headwind to the ~20% international mix.

**Company-reported macro-linked performance (context):** Q2 FY27 revenue **$1,109M (+23%)**, GAAP GM **31.1%**, adj EBITDA **~$250M (23%)**, adj EPS **$1.31 vs $1.10 consensus**; FY27 guide raised to **$4.2–4.45B revenue, $890–940M adj EBITDA, $4.61–4.87 adj EPS, >$500M FCF**; **zero debt, >$1B cash**. The 2 Nov +14.9% move was a Jefferies "franchise pick" designation on top of the beat-and-raise — an idiosyncratic catalyst, not a macro one.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE** (with a valuation cushion)

Every macro variable that sets NXT's discount rate and cost of capital is moving the wrong way at once: a Fed that hiked in September and may hike again in December, a 10Y at 5.13%, a 10Y real yield at 2.60%, breakevens contained at 2.29% (meaning the entire long-end move is *real* rate/term-premium, the most damaging kind of yield rise for capital goods), construction costs +11%, copper +45%, and a 3.2% USD rally. This is close to a worst-case macro mix for a 1.93-beta, project-financed capital-goods supplier.

Two things keep this from being a sell signal: (1) the multiple has already compressed ~42%, so the rate shock is substantially discounted; and (2) physical demand is decelerating *from strength*, not weakness — 11.4 GW installed, >$6B backlog, record bookings. The macro is hostile to the *multiple*, not to the *earnings*. That distinction is the core of the investment debate.

**Transitional watch-items:** the 7 December FOMC (a hike is consensus-adjacent), the 4 December Section 232 polysilicon effective date, the 16 November Capital Markets Day / FY28 framework, and whether HY OAS continues widening from 344bp.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario That Would Materially Move NXT

**BULL (under-appreciated) — "The energy shock makes solar the cheap power."** The market is treating the 20.4% y/y energy spike as purely inflationary and therefore rate-bearish. But the transmission mechanism cuts the other way for NXT: with oil and gas this expensive, marginal US electricity pricing rises, and solar-plus-storage PPAs become *more* competitive against the gas-fired generation that sets the clearing price. If the shock is supply-driven, the Fed hikes once more, **breakevens stay anchored at ~2.3%, and the 10Y actually falls as growth expectations in energy-importing economies soften**. A 10Y back to 4.50% with a >$6B backlog and FY28 revenue growth in the high-teens would re-rate NXT to 25x — roughly **$120–130**, a 30–40% move. The tell would be accelerating bookings from hyperscalers *outside* the tax-credit window.

**BEAR (more probable near-term) — "5.75% and a demand air-pocket."** A December hike plus a hawkish dot plot takes the 10Y through 5.50% and the 30Y through 5.75%, pushing 10Y real yields above 2.90%. Utility-scale FIDs for 2028 COD get deferred into 2027, backlog conversion slows, and the Section 232 module price floor collides with a post-July-2026 credit cliff — developers can't make the 31 December 2027 placed-in-service deadline at the new module cost. Multiples de-rate to 14x on *falling* FY28 estimates: **$60–65**, another ~30% downside. Note the asymmetry: the bull case requires the Fed to stop hiking into an energy shock, which the September and October meetings suggest it is unwilling to do.
