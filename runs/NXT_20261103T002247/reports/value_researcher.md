# NXT — Nextpower Inc. (NASDAQ: NXT)
**Value Investment Analysis | Price: $91.13 (Nov 2, 2026) | Market cap: ~$13.9B | Net cash: ~$1.0B | EV: ~$13.0B**

*Note on identity: NXT is Nextracker, the #1 global supplier of solar trackers, renamed Nextpower Inc. Fiscal year ends March 31. Latest reported period is Q2 FY2027 (quarter ended Oct 2, 2026).*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW (widening over 5 years, but peaking)**

Nextpower designs and manufactures single-axis solar trackers — steel structures that tilt panels toward the sun — plus the software (TrueCapture, NX Navigator) that optimizes their output. Trackers are ~10–12% of utility-scale solar project capex. The company is the global share leader (~30%, roughly double Array Technologies), with a localized manufacturing footprint (US, India, Brazil, Spain, Saudi Arabia, Australia) that matters for domestic-content and tariff regimes.

Evidence for the moat:
- **Scale cost advantage in steel procurement, freight, and duty structure.** Local-for-local manufacturing is a genuine structural edge in a product where landed cost drives the decision.
- **Intangible/bankability assets.** Trackers must be financeable — EPCs, insurers, and tax-equity investors underwrite the vendor. That narrows the approved-vendor list.
- **ROIC 29.2% (FY26) vs. WACC ~10–11%** — a consistent wide spread (38.5% FY25, 50.6% FY24, 26.5% FY23).
- **Gross margin went from 10.1% (FY22) to 33.7% (FY25)** — real evidence of pricing power emerging.

Evidence against a *wide* moat:
- **A 10% gross margin four years ago is the tell.** Trackers are engineered steel. Real moats don't produce 10% gross margins in a demand boom. The expansion to 33% was driven by scale, IRA-era domestic-content premiums, and tariff-related cost pass-through — tailwinds now normalizing (FY26 GM 32.6%; Q2 FY27 GM 31.1%).
- **No meaningful switching costs.** Developers dual-source and re-tender each project. Array, Arctech, GameChange and Chinese vendors compete hard.
- **Severe customer concentration.** Per filings, top-five customers dominate revenue with a single customer at ~15% (FY23); "loss of any one of our top five customers could have a materially adverse effect."
- **Demand is policy-and-rate-driven project capex**, not recurring consumption. Orders are lumpy (backlog now >$6B — genuine visibility, but pipeline, not annuity).

**Verdict:** A high-quality *operator* in a structurally competitive, policy-dependent, cyclical industry. Narrow moat, sensibly earning well above cost of capital while the cycle is friendly.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples:** 23.0x TTM EPS ($3.97) · 19.2x FY27 *adjusted* EPS (midpoint $4.74) · ~25x FY27 GAAP EPS (~$3.63) · 16.3x TTM EV/EBITDA ($796M) · 26x FY27 guided FCF → **FCF yield 3.6%** (4.1% on FY26 owner earnings).

**DCF (my own build; the platform DCF tool returned a corrupted discount rate — discarded).** Base FCF $600M, growth tapering 15%→12%→10%→8%→6%→5%→4%, terminal 3%, plus ~$1.0B net cash:

| WACC | Intrinsic value/share |
|---|---|
| 9% | ~$102 |
| 10% | ~$81 |
| 11% | ~$76 |

**Base-case intrinsic value: ~$85–90/share.** At $91.13 the stock sits at or slightly above fair value. A 25–30% margin of safety implies an entry near **$62–68**.

**What the price implies:** EV/FCF of 26x on guidance requires roughly 13–14% FCF compounding for a decade — i.e., the market is already assuming the tracker cycle extends, margins stabilize near 31–32% gross, and non-tracker (storage, inverters) scales into a second leg. That is a plausible outcome, not a discounted one.

**Context:** The stock peaked at $156 in late May 2026 (~40x forward) and fell to $76 by late October — a 51% drawdown — before jumping 15% on the Nov 2 Q2 beat and raised guidance. This is a high-beta name (β 1.93) where the *multiple*, not the business, has been the dominant driver of returns. Today's 19x forward adjusted EPS is a reasonable price for a narrow-moat cyclical — but "reasonable" is not "cheap."

**Analyst targets:** $101–$150 (median ~$138, i.e., ~50% upside). I treat these skeptically: most were set before the July–October derating and cluster well above my DCF range. The low end of the range (Mizuho $101) is closest to my work.

**Peer-multiple gap (disclosed honestly):** The platform classifies NXT under "Energy → Solar," but its peer list returned integrated oil majors (XOM, CVX, SHEL) — economically meaningless comparables. True peers (Array Technologies, Shoals, Fluence, First Solar) were not retrievable within budget. Relative valuation is therefore triangulated from NXT's own history and the analyst cross-check above rather than from a peer set.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Mixed-positive, with two real blemishes.**

- **Operator quality: high.** Founder-CEO Dan Shugar (since 2013; previously SunEdison, Solaria) built the category leader from scratch and has delivered 24% revenue CAGR, market-share leadership, and a debt-free balance sheet. Execution on localized manufacturing ahead of tariffs was prescient.
- **Capital allocation: adequate, not shareholder-obsessed.** No dividend, appropriately (growth business). A $500M three-year buyback was authorized in FY26, but only $0.4M was actually repurchased in FY26 — leaving $1.0B+ of cash idle while the stock traded between $76 and $156. Buying back stock at $76 would have been obviously accretive. Cash is instead being deployed into M&A (goodwill/intangibles up from $266M to $567M in two years) to build storage and inverter lines — strategically coherent but unproven, and it is compressing near-term margins.
- **Insider alignment: weak.** CEO direct holdings of ~1.2M shares (~0.8% of shares out); the TPG sponsor has sold down post-IPO. Insider activity skews to 10b5-1 sales, including option exercises at $21 sold at $128–137. I found no meaningful open-market insider buying during the drawdown. This is a hired-CEO-plus-sponsor equity structure, not an owner-operator one.
- **Disclosure/credibility: good.** Beat EPS estimates in each of the last eight quarters; raised FY27 guidance on Q2. Backlog >$6B gives visibility.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate at $91)

This is a genuinely good business trading at approximately intrinsic value after a 40% derating. The moat is real but narrow; the balance sheet is pristine; returns on capital are excellent; and management has executed. But the Buffett test is not "good business" — it is **good business at a discount**. At $91 I compute $76–102 of value depending on the discount rate (base ~$88), with FCF yield of 3.6% and GAAP earnings *flat to down* in FY27 while opex runs up 56%. There is no margin of safety, and the price already embeds a decade of high-teens FCF growth in a policy-sensitive cyclical with a 15%-of-revenue customer.

**Actionable discipline:**
- **Existing holders:** Hold. The franchise is intact and backlog provides two years of visibility; selling at fair value after a drawdown is not compelling.
- **New money:** Add to the watchlist. Initiate at **≤$68** (25% discount to base intrinsic value), where FCF yield exceeds 5% and forward adjusted P/E approaches 14x. Buy aggressively at ≤$60.
- **Falsify-and-upgrade trigger:** Two consecutive quarters of gross margin *expansion* toward 34%+ with FCF per share growing faster than revenue would support a higher intrinsic value (~$105–115) and justify buying nearer $85.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Policy/demand cliff (highest severity).** US solar demand hinges on 45Y/48E credits, domestic-content bonuses, and tariff design. Post-2025 legislation shortening the credit runway for wind/solar raises the risk that the >$6B backlog is a pull-forward of 2027–2028 projects rather than a durable plateau. A single adverse IRS guidance change on domestic content can move both volume and gross margin at once.
2. **Margin compression becoming structural.** Gross margin has fallen 260bp in a year; Q2 FY27 at 31.1% vs 35.9% the prior quarter. Mix shift toward international and toward lower-margin storage/inverters, plus steel inflation and integration costs, could settle mid-cycle margins at 28–30% rather than 33% — that alone cuts intrinsic value ~15–20%.
3. **Customer concentration.** A single customer near 15% of revenue; top five likely ~45%+. Consolidation among developers or a share shift at one account is a step-function risk.
4. **Competition.** Array Technologies' price aggression, Chinese/Korean entrants in international markets, and the fact that trackers are specified project-by-project. The 10% gross margin of FY22 is the downside case template for what this industry looks like when capacity outruns demand.
5. **Earnings quality drift.** FCF stuck at ~$500M for three years while revenue grew 42% and net income grew 91%. If working-capital absorption and acquisition capex persist, "FCF conversion >85%" decays and the quality score falls.
6. **Volatility and multiple risk.** Beta 1.93; a 51% peak-to-trough move in five months with no change in the underlying franchise. Position sizing, not stock selection, is where this name punishes investors.
7. **Capital allocation.** $1B+ of idle cash earning interest, minimal buyback execution, and rising M&A spend with unproven returns. Watch goodwill for impairment risk if storage/inverter acquisitions underdeliver.
