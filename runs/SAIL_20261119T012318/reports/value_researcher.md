## Value Investment Analysis — SailPoint, Inc. (SAIL)

**Price $22.25 (18 Nov 2026) · Market cap $12.50B · Net cash $340M · EV ~$12.16B · Shares 561.9M · FY ends 31 Jan**

*Data note: FY2026 (ended 31 Jan 2026) is the latest audited year; Q1–Q2 FY2027 actuals and Q3 guidance are from the earnings calendar. The DCF tool returned corrupted output (it parsed WACC as 1000%), and the 10-K text was unavailable, so I built the valuation manually and used the company's Q2 FY2027 press release/search results for operating metrics. Peer-multiple comparison was uninformative — the "peer" set returned mega-cap semis; I reference identity/security comps from my own knowledge and flag them as approximate.*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW**

SailPoint is the scale leader in Identity Governance & Administration (IGA): the system that answers "who should have access to what, and prove it to the auditor." Founded 2005, ~3,200 employees, re-listed Feb 2025 after Thoma Bravo's 2022 take-private.

**Evidence for the moat:**
- **High switching costs (the strongest leg).** Identity data, role models, access certifications and audit trails are deeply embedded in customers' compliance processes. Ripping out an IGA platform means re-doing years of entitlement mapping — which is precisely why DBNRR is **113%** and average ARR per SaaS customer exceeds **$400K**, with **235 customers above $1M ARR**.
- **Intangible assets:** brand trust in a security category where a failed audit is career-ending for a CISO; FedRAMP and sovereign certifications; 20 years of connector breadth (thousands of app integrations) that is expensive to replicate.
- **Cloud transition is working:** SaaS ARR **$847M, +36% YoY**; total ARR **$1.231B, +25%** — growth well above the high-single/low-double-digit IGA market, implying share gains.

**Evidence the moat is narrower than a Wide rating requires:**
- **DBNRR of 113% is good, not elite.** Best-in-class security SaaS runs 120%+. Only 13 points of expansion means growth depends heavily on new logos — a less durable engine.
- **Microsoft is the structural threat.** Entra ID Governance is bundled into E5 licences. In identity, "free from Microsoft" has historically capped pricing power and killed point solutions.
- **Contestable category:** Okta, Saviynt, CyberArk (now Palo Alto), IBM, plus a long tail. IGA is a feature-rich but ultimately bounded budget line, not a system of record with network effects.
- **No network effect, no cost advantage, no efficient-scale moat.** This is a good business, protected by friction — not a great one protected by structure.

I judge the moat **stable to modestly widening** (SaaS mix + AI-driven ARR >$70M), but not widening fast enough to justify a Wide rating.

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **4 / 10**

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 699.6 | 861.6 | **1,071.4** |
| Growth | +26.5% | +23.2% | **+24.4%** |
| Gross margin (GAAP) | 60.5% | 64.5% | **64.5%** |
| Operating margin | −47.6% | −21.9% | **−28.7%** |
| Net income ($M) | −395.4 | −315.8 | **−270.1** |
| CFO ($M) | −250.4 | −106.4 | **+70.6** |
| Capex ($M) | 4.5 | 13.6 | **18.8** |
| FCF ($M) | −254.8 | −120.0 | **+51.8** |
| **SBC ($M)** | 37.5 | 31.7 | **254.9** |

**The strengths:** revenue has compounded at a **24.0% CAGR** for four straight years with remarkable consistency (22.8% → 26.5% → 23.2% → 24.4%). GAAP gross margin has climbed 400bp in two years as the mix shifted to SaaS. The balance sheet was transformed by the IPO: net debt of $926M became **net cash of $340M**, with interest expense collapsing from $187M to $25M. Liquidity is no longer a thesis risk.

**The problem — and it is the whole analysis:** reported FCF of $51.8M exists only because $254.9M of stock compensation is added back. At **23.8% of revenue**, SBC is roughly 5x the reported FCF and 94% of the net loss. Two honest ways to measure true earning power:

- **Buffett owner earnings** = NI + D&A − maintenance capex = −270.1 + 210.8 − 18.8 = **−$78M. Negative.**
- **Economic FCF** (treating SBC as the real cost it is) = CFO − capex − SBC = 70.6 − 18.8 − 254.9 = **−$203M. Deeply negative.**

GAAP margins are also distorted downward by purchase accounting: of $210.8M of D&A, only ~$15M is PP&E depreciation (PP&E is just $37.8M); the ~$195M balance is amortization of Thoma Bravo-era intangibles. On a clean basis, gross margin is ~78–80% and non-GAAP operating margin ~13%. So the *unit economics are genuinely good* — there is a real ~**28.6% of revenue** pre-SBC cash generation engine here ($306.6M in FY2026). But shareholders do not own that cash until SBC normalises toward ~10% of revenue. **FCF conversion is negative, and that is a hard fail on my quality test.**

Asset quality is a further concern: goodwill and intangibles are **$6.54B against $6.85B of book equity** — tangible book is ~$304M, or 2.4% of market cap. The "1.83x P/B" is an accounting artefact, not asset protection.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples (EV $12.16B):**
- EV / FY2026 revenue: **11.3x**
- EV / FY2027E revenue (~$1.26B): **9.7x**
- EV / ARR ($1.231B): **9.9x**
- EV / pre-SBC cash flow ($307M): **39.7x**
- Reported FCF yield: **0.4%**; P/E: negative; P/FCF: **242x**
- FY2027E non-GAAP EPS ~$0.28–0.32 → **~70–79x**

**My intrinsic value work.** ARR +25% with SaaS ARR +36% is the genuine bull case; reported revenue growth (+17–18%) lags because term-licence revenue is recognised upfront while SaaS is ratable. I model ARR growth of 25% (FY27), then 18%, 15%, 12%, 10%, fading to 4–5% by year 10 — revenue reaching roughly $4.0–4.3B. I ramp true FCF margin (post-SBC) from ~5% today to 24–26% as SBC normalises and opex leverage accrues, and exit at 20x FCF.

That yields an enterprise value of **$11.5–12.5B**, plus $340M net cash → **equity value of $11.8–12.8B, or $21–23 per share.**

**The stock is trading at intrinsic value.** There is no margin of safety. Reverse-engineering the current price: to earn a 10% return over five years exiting at 25x FCF, SailPoint must produce ~$790M of true FCF by FY2031 — a ~31% post-SBC FCF margin on ~$2.5B of revenue. That is best-in-class SaaS execution, priced as the base case. **You are being asked to pay full price for a favourable outcome and accept the downside yourself.**

A price offering a 25% discount — my entry threshold — is roughly **$16–17/share**. Notably, the stock traded at $10.30 in April 2026 and $11.06 as recently as 9 April 2026; it has since more than doubled. Analyst targets cluster at $21–30 (median ~$25), which is consistent with "fairly valued," not "cheap."

### 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Positive:** Mark McClain is a **founder-CEO** (2005) who has run the business for two decades, through the cloud transition, a $6.9B Thoma Bravo LBO, and re-IPO. Founder-led continuity through an LBO is unusual and favourable. Capital allocation since listing has been conservative and sensible: **IPO proceeds went to debt paydown** (net debt $926M → net cash $340M), not to dividends or empire-building. No M&A. Q1–Q2 FY2027 both beat consensus on revenue and EPS — guidance credibility is intact.

**Negative:** The LBO sponsor loaded the company with debt and extracted a dividend; public shareholders now inherit a heavily levered intangible asset base and a **23.8%-of-revenue SBC burden** that the sponsor-era option pool created. Thoma Bravo retains a very large block (exact current percentage not verifiable from my data) — a real **supply overhang** via secondary offerings, and a governance consideration given their economic incentives differ from a long-term public holder's. No buyback and no dividend is correct at this stage. Insiders are not buying.

Verdict: **competent, honest, conservative operators — but not yet proven as public-market capital allocators, and shareholder-unfriendly equity comp is the one clear blemish.**

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate at $22.25)

This is a genuinely good business — the scale leader in a mission-critical security category, 24% revenue CAGR, 25% ARR growth, 36% SaaS ARR growth, high switching costs, a now-pristine balance sheet, and a founder-CEO. It is not, however, a Buffett business at this price, for three reasons:

1. **Owner earnings are negative** (−$78M) and economic FCF is −$203M. A business that must pay 24% of revenue in stock to retain staff is not yet converting growth into owner value.
2. **The moat is Narrow, not Wide** — protected by switching friction, not by network effects or scale economics, and permanently shadowed by Microsoft bundling.
3. **No margin of safety whatsoever.** Price ≈ intrinsic value ($21–23). My discipline requires a 20–30% discount before committing capital to a business with this much execution risk.

I would become a **buyer below ~$16–17** (roughly 7x EV/ARR, a 25% discount to intrinsic value), or if SBC normalises toward 10% of revenue while ARR growth holds above 20% — either event would materially raise intrinsic value without raising the price. Existing holders can reasonably stay for the ARR compounding; new money should wait.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

- **SBC never normalises.** If $250M+ of annual stock comp persists, dilution runs ~2%/year and true FCF stays near zero indefinitely. This is the single largest risk and my biggest uncertainty.
- **Growth deceleration.** ARR +25% but revenue +16.8% and guided +16.2%. If ARR converges *down* to revenue rather than revenue converging *up* to ARR, the 9.9x EV/ARR multiple is indefensible.
- **Microsoft pricing pressure.** Entra ID Governance bundled into E5 could compress IGA pricing and lengthen sales cycles, capping DBNRR below 113%.
- **Multiple compression.** At 70–79x non-GAAP EPS, a de-rating to a 30x software multiple (peer median P/E 30.8x) implies $10–12/share — the April 2026 low was not an accident.
- **Thoma Bravo overhang** and lock-up/secondary supply.
- **Goodwill impairment.** $6.54B of intangibles against $304M tangible book leaves no asset floor if the franchise disappoints.
- **Beta of 2.08** — this is not a defensive holding; expect 50%+ drawdowns in risk-off markets.
