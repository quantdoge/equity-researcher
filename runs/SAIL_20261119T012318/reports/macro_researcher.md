# Macroeconomic Assessment: SailPoint, Inc. (NASDAQ: SAIL)
**As of 19 November 2026 | Fiscal year ends 31 January (FQ3 FY2027 reports 8 December 2026)**

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional, tilting risk-off for long-duration equities.** This is not a recession scare — it is the opposite. The US is running an **above-trend reflation**: real GDP grew **+3.4% annualized in Q3 2026** (+2.1% YoY), unemployment has fallen to **4.1%** from 4.5% a year ago, and headline CPI has re-accelerated to **3.82% YoY** (Sep/Sep 3.72%; Sep→Oct alone +0.35% m/m, ≈4.3% annualized). That combination has stopped the easing cycle dead: the effective fed funds rate bottomed at **3.63%** in mid-2026 and has since drifted **up to 3.88%**.

The critical feature for equity valuation is *where* the yield rise is coming from. The 10Y Treasury has climbed to **5.13%** (intraday high 5.31% on 5 Oct), up **~67bp from 4.48% on 1 July 2026**, while the **5-year breakeven has fallen to 2.23%** from ~2.48% in June. Inflation expectations are *declining* as nominal yields rise — so this is a **real-yield / term-premium shock**, not an inflation-expectations shock. The 5-year real yield is now approximately **2.70%**. That is the single most punishing regime for long-duration, low-current-earnings equities: the discount rate rises with no offsetting nominal growth benefit.

The front end is fully hawkish: with the 2Y at **4.74%** versus an effective funds rate of 3.88%, the market is pricing roughly **85bp of hikes** over two years. The curve is positively sloped (2s10s **+39bp**, 30Y **5.47%**) — no inversion, no recession signal. Credit is still open: the BAA–10Y spread at **1.53%** sits near the tight end of its 12-month 1.39–1.69% range.

It is **GAAP-unprofitable** (net loss $50.4M in the quarter; $125.0M for H1 FY2027) with **SBC of $140.4M over six months — ~23% of revenue**.

Note also that the Feb-2025 peak ($25.70) to Apr-2026 trough ($10.49), **–59%**, unfolded precisely as the 10Y ground from ~4.5% toward 5%+.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

SailPoint sits at the worst intersection of this regime. It is a **long-duration, GAAP-unprofitable, floating-rate-levered, internationally-exposed growth stock** in a world of rising *real* term premia, a hawkish front end, and a strengthening dollar. The offsetting forces — solid nominal GDP, tight credit spreads, and genuinely non-discretionary compliance demand — protect the *fundamental* franchise but not the *valuation*. The 25% ARR growth is real; the multiple it commands is what macro is attacking.

Importantly, the rate pressure is now arriving at the worst moment: FQ3 FY2027 results land on **8 December 2026**, immediately alongside the December FOMC, with NRR already softening from 114% to 113%. Guidance set against a 4%-stronger dollar gives management a convenient excuse and the stock a fresh volatility event.

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**Bear (primary): the Fed is forced to hike, not hold.** The market's 5Y breakeven of 2.23% sits **~160bp below** current headline CPI. That is an enormous bet that inflation rolls over. If tariff pass-through and shelter re-acceleration keep CPI above 3.5% into H1 2027, the Fed delivers one or two 25bp hikes, the 10Y clears 5.50–5.75%, and the equity risk premium reprices simultaneously. For SAIL, that is a compounded hit: ~15% further multiple compression, ~$15–20M of added annual interest cost, and a stronger dollar. A retest of **$13–15** (roughly the April 2026 zone) becomes live — a further 30%+ downside despite the business compounding at 25%.

**Bull (the plausible variant): the breakevens are right.** If CPI breaks below 3% by spring 2027 as tariff effects lap and the labour market cools from 4.1%, the 10Y retraces to ~4.30%, real yields compress, and high-beta software leads a violent re-rating. SAIL, at 10–11x EV/ARR for 25% ARR growth with SaaS ARR +36%, would be among the highest-torque beneficiaries — a return to **$28–30**. On balance I weight the bear case more heavily, because the term premium is being driven by supply and fiscal dynamics that a soft CPI print does not fix.
