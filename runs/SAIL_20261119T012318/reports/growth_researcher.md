## SailPoint, Inc. (SAIL) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $22.25 (Nov 18, 2026 close) · Market cap ~$12.5B · EV ~$12.2B · Beta 2.08 · IPO Feb 2025 at ~$22*

---

### 1. Growth Story

SailPoint is the last scaled pure-play in identity governance — the control plane that decides *who and what* gets access to enterprise systems — and it is converting a legacy on-prem installed base (IdentityIQ) into a cloud-native SaaS platform (Identity Security Cloud) while extending the definition of "identity" from employees to machine identities and AI agents. ARR of **$1.231B (+25% YoY)** with **SaaS ARR of $847M (+36%)** is growing ~7 points faster than reported revenue (+17%), because perpetual-to-SaaS conversion temporarily suppresses recognized revenue — the mix headwind masks a business compounding in the mid-20s. The next leg of the TAM is non-human identity: this is the rare security franchise where the *number of identities to be governed* is about to explode rather than plateau.

**3-yr revenue CAGR (FY23→FY26): 24.6%. Consensus forward: 19.1% (FY28), 19.0% (FY29), 12.3% (FY30) → FY26→FY30 CAGR 17.2%.**

**Growth-quality diagnostics:**
- **Gross margin +13.9pts since FY23** — the clearest evidence of SaaS scale leverage. Still ~10pts below best-in-class security SaaS (~75–80%), which is the single largest source of untapped operating leverage.
- **Rule of 40 at 29–32 is the core flaw.** Using ARR growth instead of revenue growth (25% + ~14% FCF margin) it lands at ~39 — right at the threshold. On a GAAP-FCF basis this is not yet a "40-company," and I won't pretend otherwise.
- **Balance sheet transformed:** interest expense collapsed from $22.4M/quarter (Q1 FY26) to $0.26M/quarter (FY27) — LBO debt repaid with IPO proceeds. Book value $12.18/share, P/B 1.83. No leverage risk; the FY26 FCF depression was largely one-off (proceeds paydown + $116M working-capital drag).
- **The dilution tax is real:** SBC $254.9M in FY26 = **23.8% of revenue** (from $31.7M in FY25). Diluted shares 561.9M → 568.1M in two quarters (~2.2%/yr). GAAP EPS remains negative; non-GAAP FY27 EPS consensus $0.324.
- **Land-and-expand is middling:** dollar-based NRR **113%**, average ARR/customer >$350K (+18%), emerging products 20% of net new ARR. Growth is being carried by new logos, not expansion — 113% is below elite SaaS norms and is the metric I'd monitor most closely.

### 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

| Market | Size | Growth |
|---|---|---|
| Identity Governance & Administration (IGA) | $9.6B (2026) → $33.1B (2034) | ~15% CAGR |
| Broader IAM | $26.8B (2025) → $62.9B (2033) | 11–16% CAGR |
| Machine / non-human identity mgmt | $21.4B (2026) → $60.5B (2035) | high-teens+ |

SailPoint's serviceable market (IGA + non-human identity + AI-agent governance) is plausibly **$25–35B by 2030**. Against $1.27B of FY27 revenue, penetration of the core IGA market alone is **~4–5%**. This is the strongest plank of the thesis: a mid-20s grower with <5% share of a market growing mid-teens, plus an adjacent machine-identity pool that is larger than the core. Analyst commentary explicitly cites "potential addressable market expansion" (Cantor, Oct 2026) and **AI ARR already exceeds $70M** — small, but it is a real product line, not a slide.

**Differentiation:** SailPoint is the category creator in IGA with the largest governance-specific installed base. Its moat is *switching costs and regulatory entrenchment* (access certifications, audit evidence, SoD controls) — sticky, but not a data network effect. That is a durable moat, not a compounding one; the machine-identity franchise is where a genuine platform effect could emerge.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q3 FY27 earnings — ~December 8, 2026 (imminent).** Revenue guided $326–330M; Q2 ARR already beat the $1.218–1.222B guide. Watch ARR trajectory (a 25%+ print supports the re-rating case) and the FY27 EPS guide, which disappointed at Q2 and is the proximate cause of the recent multiple cap.
- **FY28 guidance, ~March 2027.** First clean year with debt retired, IPO comps lapped, and SBC normalized — the first "normalized economics" print the market can underwrite.
- **IdentityIQ→SaaS migration completion (2H FY27–1H FY28).** As perpetual/support revenue laps out, reported revenue growth should converge *up* toward ARR growth. This is the highest-conviction mechanical re-rating catalyst.
- **AI-agent / non-human identity product cadence.** AI ARR >$70M; any marquee agentic-identity GA or large enterprise AI-governance win extends the TAM narrative and the multiple.
- **Post-CyberArk vendor consolidation.** Palo Alto closed its **$25B CyberArk acquisition on Feb 11, 2026**. Disrupted channels and forced re-bids in the 2027 budget cycle create displacement windows for SailPoint — and make SAIL the scarce remaining pure-play IGA asset for any platform buyer.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (moderate conviction)

**Why Buy:** mid-20s ARR growth on **<5% TAM penetration**, a 13.9-point gross-margin expansion runway still ~10pts from best-in-class, a debt-free balance sheet, ARR growth 7 points above reported revenue that mechanically converges upward as the SaaS migration laps, a strategic AI/machine-identity adjacency already monetizing ($70M+ AI ARR), 80% beat rate, uniform analyst PT raises with a $25 median (~12% upside), and a **~10x EV/ARR multiple against CyberArk's ~$25B takeout (roughly 2.5x sales)** — the strategic comp says the category is worth far more than SAIL's current multiple.

**Why not Strong Buy:**
1. **Rule of 40 of 29–32.** Growth plus cash generation does not yet clear my bar for a frontier-innovation core position.
2. **NRR 113%** — growth is new-logo-dependent, not expansion-driven.
3. **Valuation is no longer asymmetric.** At $22.25: **~9.9x EV/ARR, 9.6x FY27 / 8.1x FY28 revenue, 68.6x FY27 and 55.4x FY28 EPS, ~70x FY27 FCF, PEG 3.7 (FY27) / 2.9 (FY28) / 2.05 (FY30)**. Reverse-engineering the EV requires ~20%+ revenue CAGR *with* margin expansion to a ~30% EBITDA margin by FY30 (consensus FY30 revenue $2.02B only supports **19.3x EV/EBITDA** and a 17.2% CAGR). The market is already paying for the good outcome.
4. **We missed the entry.** The stock has doubled off the April low and sits at the top of its 52-week range and at its IPO price. For a name with **beta 2.08** that drew down 54% in six months, buying at the high with Q3 earnings days away is a poor risk/reward setup.

**Action:** Build a starter position now, scale in on either (a) a Q3 print that shows ARR ≥25% with an intact FY27 EPS guide, or (b) a pullback into the **$18–19** zone (~8x EV/ARR). Target: **$27–30** (12-month, ~11x FY28 revenue), consistent with the bullish end of the Street. A close below $15 would invalidate the re-rating thesis.

### 7. Key Risks

- **Competitive squeeze (highest risk).** Palo Alto/CyberArk (closed Feb 2026) bundles identity with a platform — exactly the dynamic that has compressed point-solution multiples across security. Microsoft Entra ID Governance is "good enough and free" for the mid-market; Saviynt and Ping compete on price at the high end. SailPoint's answer must be superior governance depth, not breadth.
- **Deceleration.** Revenue growth 24.3% → 18.5%, and FY30 consensus implies further fade to 12.3%. If ARR growth slips below ~22%, the ~10x ARR multiple compresses toward mid-single digits (Okta-like). Rule of 40 stays sub-40 and the stock becomes a value trap.
- **Persistent GAAP losses + SBC.** 23.8% SBC/revenue means per-share value creation lags enterprise value creation; a market shift toward GAAP-based (not non-GAAP) valuation would hit this name hard. FY27 EPS guidance already disappointed once.
- **NRR at 113%** limits the durability of the compounding and makes growth rate increasingly dependent on new-logo productivity and sales headcount.
- **Sponsor overhang.** Thoma Bravo retains a large stake post-IPO; lockup expiries and secondary offerings create recurring supply — the likely explanation for several high-volume down days (13.6M shares on Mar 18, 2026).
- **Execution risk on the SaaS transition.** The migration that creates the ARR/revenue gap could also prove a churn event: IdentityIQ customers may evaluate alternatives rather than migrate, converting a revenue-timing headwind into permanent share loss.
- **Volatility/market structure.** Beta 2.08 on a ~$12.5B IPO with concentrated ownership — 50%+ drawdowns are a demonstrated feature, not a tail scenario. Size accordingly.
