## FORENSIC SHORT-SELLER ANALYSIS — SAIL (SailPoint, Inc.)

**Price $22.25 | Market cap $12.70B | EV ~$12.40B (net cash $304M) | TTM revenue $1.166B | TTM FCF ~$176M**

SailPoint is the Thoma Bravo take-private (Apr-2022, $6.9B) re-IPO'd at $23.00 in Feb-2025. It is not a fraud story. It is a **cash-quality and moat story dressed as a growth story** — and the stock is at a six-month high after +113% off its April-2026 low of $10.49.

---

### 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **MEDIUM**

Not Low, and explicitly not Fraudulent. The accrual evidence is *conservative*:

| Metric | FY2024 | FY2025 | FY2026 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accruals ratio | -0.019 | -0.028 | **-0.045** |
| Operating CF | -$250M | -$106M | **+$71M** |
| Net income | -$395M | -$316M | -$270M |
| SBC | $37M | $32M | **$255M** |
| SBC % of revenue | 5.4% | 3.7% | **23.8%** |

Negative accruals mean GAAP earnings are *worse* than cash — the opposite of manipulation. Capex is 1.8% of revenue (no capitalisation games), there is no inventory, and revenue recognition is genuinely conservative (point-in-time revenue actually *reduced* Q2 FY27 reported revenue).

**But the "profitability" is a definitional construct.** In Q2 FY2027 (Jul-26), adjusted operating margin was **+20.3%** while GAAP operating margin was **-19%**. The 39-point bridge is almost exactly D&A ($53.6M) + SBC (~$69M) = $122M, against a GAAP operating loss of $59.0M. That is **~43% of revenue excluded from the profit definition.**

**The killer number:** FY2027 FCF guided at ~$200M. FY2027 SBC run-rate is ~$245-255M. **SBC-adjusted FCF is roughly -$50M.** Every dollar of "free cash flow" — and the entire 62x EV/FCF multiple the stock trades on — exists only because employees are paid in stock. This is the core Chanos point: *reported earnings lie, and here even cash flow lies.*

Two secondary traps:
- **The narrowing-loss narrative is arithmetic, not operating.** Net loss improved from -$316M to -$270M while **GAAP operating loss widened from -$189M to -$307M**. Most of the net improvement is interest expense collapsing from $187M to $25M after IPO proceeds repaid ~$1.05B of debt. GAAP operating margin deteriorated again in Q2 FY27 (‑19% vs ‑15% YoY).
- **Pre-IPO per-share history is not comparable.** Share count ran 546.6M (FY23) → 138.4M (FY24) → 83.7M (FY25) → 561.9M (FY26) from the corporate conversion. FY25 EPS of -$12.91 vs FY26 -$0.52 is a restructuring artefact. Any model built on that series is broken.

### 2. Key Red Flags Found

1. **All FCF is stock-based comp** (~$250M SBC vs $200M FCF guide) — dilution is the funding mechanism, not a footnote.
2. **96% of book equity is goodwill.** Goodwill & intangibles $6.584B vs equity $6.862B. Tangible book value: **$278M** against a $12.7B market cap.
3. **Goodwill impairment judgment was arguably triggered and not taken.** In April-2026 SAIL traded at $10.49–$11.42, putting market cap (~$6.0–6.5B) at or below book equity ($6.84B at 30-Apr-26). No impairment was recorded; goodwill *rose* (Entro acquisition). The test appears to rest on DCF support from the FY2029 target of ≥$2.1B ARR rather than observable market value. Legitimate forensic question.
4. **Revenue growth decelerating hard.** YoY: +33.1% (Jul-25) → +22.7% (Jan-26) → +21.6% (Apr-26) → **+16.8% (Jul-26)**; Q3 FY27 guided to **+16%**. Consensus still models **+19% for FY2028**. That is a guidance-reset setup.
5. **Receivables outrunning revenue:** AR +29.6% vs revenue +24.4% (FY26) — flagged. DSO ~139 days (FY26), 133 (FY25), 139 (FY24). Elevated, and rising.
6. **Working capital is a persistent *use* of cash despite 25% ARR growth** (-$116M FY26, -$31M Q2 FY27, -$13M Q1 FY27). In a SaaS business billing in advance, deferred revenue should be a source. The shift to ratable SaaS billing in arrears is structurally degrading the float that used to fund operations — meaning non-cash add-backs do even more of the work.
7. **Gross margin expansion has stalled and turned down YoY.** FY23 50.5% → FY26 64.5%, but Q2 FY27 66.5% vs Q2 FY26 67.3%. The FY23-FY25 improvement is largely the roll-off of acquisition amortisation sitting in COGS — not pricing power. Now moving the wrong way.
8. **Related-party transaction:** 10-Q discloses a $10.7M cash payment to **Imprivata, a Thoma Bravo-majority-owned company**. Small, but it is exactly the self-dealing pattern to monitor.
9. **Securities-fraud investigations opened** (Pomerantz, Levi & Korsinsky) after the 11.5% one-day drop to $15.66 on 9-Jun-26 following cautious FY27 guidance. Routine, unproven, but a live overhang.
10. **Sponsor overhang is extreme.** Thoma Bravo owned ~96.7% pre-IPO; its residual stake is reported near $9.7B (~76% of the cap), implying a public float of roughly 10–25%. Twenty Form 4s in six months, clustered on identical dates (7 filers on 9-Jul-26, 8 filers on 8-Oct-26) — the signature of coordinated 10b5-1/RSU dispositions. **Zero filings indicating insider buying.**

**M-Score: -2.157** — "moderate risk" band (between -2.22 and -1.78). **I do not consider this a manipulation signal, and I will not pretend otherwise.**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.042 | 1.001 | 0.954 | 1.244 | 1.011 | 1.085 | 0.056 | **-0.0448** |

The score is driven almost entirely by **SGI 1.244** (the model penalises fast sales growth by construction) and **LVGI 0.056** (a mechanical artefact of repaying all debt at IPO, which actually *improved* the score). DSRI of 1.042 is a negligible receivables signal; TATA of -0.0448 is working *against* manipulation; GMI of 1.001 shows no gross-margin gaming.

**Composite earnings-quality score: 5/100** — but that metric is meaningless here because it divides by negative net income. Cash conversion ratio (-0.26) and FCF conversion (-0.19) are artefacts of a loss-making GAAP P&L.

**Altman Z-Score: 5.464 "Safe" — reject this reading.** It is a mirage. X4 (book equity/assets) = 9.11 because a private-equity sponsor paid $6.9B for the company; X3 (EBIT/assets) is **-0.0397**. Strip goodwill and equity/assets falls to **0.037**. The "safe zone" is an accounting residue of the 2022 buyout, not a solvency signal.

**Piotroski F-Score: 6/9** — fails F1 (negative ROA), **F7 (share issuance/dilution)**, and F8 (gross-margin improvement). Passing F4 (accrual quality) and F5 (lower leverage) is consistent with my read: honest accounting, poor economics.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

SailPoint sells identity governance (IGA). The installed base is **IdentityIQ** (on-prem, expensive, legacy) and the growth engine is **Identity Security Cloud**.

**The moat is being attacked at precisely the point of maximum dependence.** Management attributes ~4 points of its 25% ARR growth to on-prem IIQ migrations, at a claimed 2-3x ARR uplift. That migration pool is finite *and* it is Microsoft's target list. Microsoft publishes a customer case study describing SailPoint IIQ as *"a legacy solution to maintain and expensive, costing over $1.5 million per year,"* displaced by **Microsoft Entra ID Governance** — bundled into an M365 estate the customer already owns. When the incumbent's most profitable customers can be moved to a bundle at near-zero incremental cost, price and retention are structurally at risk. Okta, Saviynt, and Palo Alto/CyberArk (post-acquisition) compete for the same RFPs.

Near-term, the business is performing: ARR +25%, SaaS ARR +36%, net-new SaaS ARR +34%, **NRR 113%**, gross retention high-90s, RPO +30%, cRPO +27%, AI-driven ARR >$70M. Q2 SaaS mix hit 97% vs 90-95% guided. This is a real franchise with real momentum — which is why my rating is not "Strong Short."

**The structural squeeze:** management quantified it themselves — *each $5M shift between SaaS and term ARR moves in-period revenue by ~$10M*. Revenue is 2x levered to billing mix, and the mix shift is one-way and permanent (97% of net-new ARR is SaaS). ARR grows 25%, revenue grows 17%, revenue is guided to 16%. That 8-point gap cannot close by revenue accelerating; it closes by ARR decelerating. Reported revenue growth is the honest series, and the market is paying 9.8x it.

### 5. Management Red Flags

- **Guidance credibility:** "revenue miss due to accounting, not demand" is a recurring management explanation. Here it is *partly corroborated* by cRPO +27%, which is why I weight this less heavily than the pattern alone would warrant.
- **Promotional framing:** "$1.38B ARR," "$70M AI ARR," "30% of net new ARR," "FY2029 ≥$2.1B ARR / ≥$800M AI ARR" — heavy use of metrics that are not GAAP-audited, alongside a GAAP operating margin of -19%.
- **Serial re-packaging:** acquired for $6.9B in 2022; re-IPO'd at a ~$13B valuation at 5.4x the old share count. The equity story has been marked up twice without a GAAP profit ever being produced.
- **Dilution acceleration:** diluted shares guided to ~577M for Q3 FY27 from 568M in Jul-26 (+1.6% in one quarter, ~6% annualised) — plus Entro Security bolt-on shares/earnout for <$3M of acquired ARR. Paying M&A prices for sub-scale ARR is a late-cycle behaviour.
- **Mitigant:** CEO Mark McClain is a long-tenured operator; auditor has not changed; no restatement; no related-party pattern beyond the small Imprivata item.

### 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, but it is a valuation-and-quality short, not a fraud short. The weak version of this short is the one that rests on deceleration alone** — cRPO +27% and RPO +30% genuinely contradict that simple story.

The credible version:

**You are paying 62x EV/FCF for free cash flow that is entirely funded by equity dilution, for a business where 96% of book equity is goodwill from a leveraged buyout, with ~76-90% of the shares held by a sponsor who is a natural seller, facing a competitor that is publishing case studies about displacing its installed base.**

Quantified:
- EV/FY27E sales **9.8x**; EV/FY27E FCF **62x**; non-GAAP P/E **~69x**; **GAAP earnings: none**.
- Reverse-DCF: to justify a $12.4B EV at 25x FCF you need **~$496M** of FCF. Management's own FY2029 target is **$400M**. The current price sits *above* what the company's own three-year plan supports at a generous multiple.
- Nearest public comp **OKTA**: ~10.4x EV/S, **35x EV/FCF**, 29.6% FCF margin, and FCF *after* $544M of SBC is **+$319M**. SAIL's FCF after SBC is **negative**. Same sales multiple, half the cash quality. (Bull rebuttal: SAIL grows 17% vs OKTA 12% — fair, and it is why this is a "Short," not a "Strong Short.")

**Catalysts:** FY2028 guidance reset as revenue growth converges to high-teens; a second-half FY27 print where the mix headwind is larger than the $5M disclosed; goodwill impairment testing if the shares revisit book value (~$12/share); further sponsor sell-downs against a thin float; advancement of the securities investigations.

**Scenario range:** Bear (-35%, ~$14.50 at 20x FY29 FCF on a target miss and impairment) / Base (-20%, ~$17.75 at 25x FY29 FCF) / Bull (+25%, ~$28 if FY29 ARR reaches $2.3B and FCF $500M at 35x).

**What would make me wrong (stated plainly):** ARR holds at 25%+ for four more quarters; the revenue-recognition gap proves purely mechanical and closes upward as cRPO converts; AI/agentic identity (Agentic Fabric, $200M+ pipeline, 60%+ higher spend per adopter) creates a genuine new S-curve that expands the TAM faster than Microsoft compresses price; FCF compounds past $400M and SBC falls as a share of revenue post-IPO. If management converts the AI pipeline and SBC normalises to ~12-15% of revenue, the stock is not expensive. That is a real possibility, and it is why position sizing should be modest.

### 7. Contrarian Rating: **SHORT** (moderate conviction — not "Strong Short")

Entry conditions are favourable: the stock is at a six-month high, doubled off the April low (+113%), with decelerating reported revenue, a widening GAAP operating loss, a live securities investigation, and a sponsor who needs to sell. But there is **no evidence of accounting fraud** — accruals are conservative, receivables are a mild flag not a five-alarm, revenue recognition is if anything understated, and the balance sheet carries net cash and no meaningful debt. The short case must be argued on cash-quality and multiple, not on the P&L being fake.

**Preferred expression: long OKTA / short SAIL** to neutralise sector beta (SAIL beta 2.08) and isolate the FCF-quality and multiple gap. If single-name short only, size at roughly half a normal short position and respect that a 10-25% float with 76-90% sponsor ownership is structurally squeeze-prone.

**Contrarian counterpoint, in fairness:** the bull case is stronger than a typical short-seller's straw man. ARR +25%, SaaS ARR +36%, NRR 113%, cRPO +27%, 20%+ adjusted operating margins, $200M FCF and net cash is a genuinely good business improving on almost every operating metric. If you believe ARR is the right unit of account, this is a ~44 Rule-of-40 company at 9.8x sales, and the entire bear case reduces to "revenue is a lagging metric." My disagreement is narrow and specific: **ARR is not cash, and SailPoint's cash is not its own.**
