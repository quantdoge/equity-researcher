## SailPoint, Inc. (NASDAQ: SAIL) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Company:** Enterprise identity security (Identity Security Cloud SaaS + on-prem IdentityIQ). FY2026 revenue $1.07B; ARR $1.16B at 4/30/26; ~3,229 FTE; HQ Austin. Re-IPO'd 2/13/2025; Thoma Bravo retains ~84–86% control.

*No single foreign country is disclosed as material (>10%), which itself limits single-jurisdiction expropriation/currency-control risk. Middle East is called out in the company description as a served region — conflict-related disruption is an unquantified, likely sub-3% exposure.*

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1. FX translation on a 35% non-US revenue base — HIGH probability, MODERATE impact (already realized)**
- On 6/9/26 SailPoint beat on EPS and grew revenue strongly but guided cautiously and "warned that foreign-exchange headwinds would dampen annual recurring revenue growth." Stock fell 11.48% to $15.66 (Pomerantz). This is the clearest transmission channel from geopolitics/macro into the equity — a structurally unhedged ~one-third non-USD revenue mix. Expect recurring guidance noise each quarter; watch the **Dec 8, 2026** FQ3 FY27 print.

**2. US federal budget & procurement politics — MEDIUM probability, MEDIUM impact**
- SailPoint holds FedRAMP authorizations across Identity Security Cloud, Data Access Security, and Non-Employee Risk Management, explicitly targeting federal agencies and the defense industrial base (FedRAMP Moderate ATO; investor.sailpoint.com releases). Public-sector demand is therefore politically levered: a partial government shutdown began 1/31/26 (Forbes), and federal agencies are absorbing significant job cuts while under pressure to maintain digital services (FederalNewsNetwork, 10/2026). Federal IT budget impasses hit deal timing and renewal cycles.

**3. EU digital-sovereignty push against US security vendors — MEDIUM probability, MEDIUM impact**
- EMEA is ~22% of revenue and the company's most regulated growth region. The European Commission's June 2026 Technological Sovereignty Package plus NIS2, DORA, the Data Act, and the AI Act raise local-residency and operational-resilience requirements that structurally favour EU-domiciled vendors in public-sector and financial-services tenders (CSA; Digital-Strategy EU). Risk is asymmetric: compliance cost and share loss in EMEA versus a compliance-driven demand tailwind.

*Honorable mention: supply-chain/state-linked cyber. The 4/20/26 GitHub repository intrusion (via a third-party application vulnerability) fits the pattern of the TeamPCP/ShinyHunters-era SaaS supply-chain campaign (SecurityWeek, 5/11/26; FBI PSA 5/15/26).*

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities litigation (active, investigation stage).** Following the 6/9/26 FX-headwind guidance, Pomerantz LLP is investigating claims of "securities fraud or other unlawful business practices." Kuehn Law (9/30/26) is separately investigating whether officers/directors breached fiduciary duties, "concerns potential self-dealing." No confirmed filed complaint or class certification in my sources; typical post-guidance-cut strike-suit. **Materiality: low financially, moderate as headline/governance noise.**
- **Cybersecurity incident.** In its 5/8/26 filing SailPoint disclosed that on 4/20/26 it detected unauthorized access to a subset of GitHub repositories, contained it, and concluded there was no evidence customer data in production/staging environments were accessed and no service interruption; affected customers were notified directly. Reputational sensitivity is acute for a company that sells identity security — the "who guards the guards" problem. Residual risk: state AG/GDPR inquiries, follow-on negligence claims.
- **Governance / related-party risk.** Thoma Bravo holds ~84% (479.8M shares per WallStreetZen; 86.2% immediately post-IPO per the 424B4). As a controlled company, SAIL is exempt from majority-independent-board and independent-compensation-committee Nasdaq requirements — a structural minority-shareholder risk and the apparent focus of the "self-dealing" probe.
- **IP litigation:** no material patent disputes surfaced in my searches — a mild positive versus software-sector base rates. **Gap:** I could not retrieve Item 3 (Legal Proceedings) directly — SEC.gov returns 403 to the fetch tool and no FY2026 10-K was available in the filing-text index — so this rests on news/secondary sources.
- **Regulatory regimes touching the business:** GDPR/UK GDPR + CCPA (SAIL processes highly sensitive entitlement and access data as a processor), NIS2/DORA for EU customers, FedRAMP continuous-monitoring obligations, and US EAR encryption-export classification for its software.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

Rationale: revenue is concentrated in the US (~64%) where rule of law is strong, no disclosed sanctions/FCPA/IP problems, and no single foreign jurisdiction is large enough to be existential. The offsetting factors are a persistent FX drag on 35% non-US revenue that has already forced a guidance cut and drawn securities-bar scrutiny, ~84% private-equity control with associated related-party governance questions, EMEA regulatory nationalism, and elevated cyber/supply-chain exposure inherent to being a security vendor. Nothing here is thesis-breaking; the risks are recurring annoyance-grade rather than impairment-grade.

**Key dates to watch:** FQ3 FY27 results **12/8/26**; any motion-to-dismiss or consolidated-complaint filing in the securities matters; further Salesforce-style European sovereignty procurement rules; any Thoma Bravo secondary offering (which would both test the "self-dealing" narrative and address the 84% overhang).
