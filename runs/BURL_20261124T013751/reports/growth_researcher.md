## BURL — Burlington Stores, Inc.
**Price $235.16 | Mkt cap ~$15.0B | EV ~$19.9B | Consumer Cyclical / Off-Price Apparel Retail**
*(Data as of the Nov 23, 2026 close; FY2025 = year ended Jan 31, 2026)*

---

### 1. Growth Story

Burlington is a scaled, still-sub-scale off-price retailer running a two-engine growth model: mid-single-digit comps plus ~100 net new stores a year against a base of just 1,242 units and a stated 2,000-store opportunity — a ~60% unit runway with zero comp growth required to double revenue. The disruptive edge is structural, not technological: in a tariff-inflated, over-ordered full-price apparel environment, off-price buyers get first call on branded closeouts, and Burlington converts that into 43.9% gross margins (up 560bps since FY2020) because scale is finally outrunning its historically messy supply chain. The stock has de-rated ~37% from its July 2026 peak ($372.19) to 19.8x forward earnings — near a decade-low relative to TJX (28.5x) and Ross (32.8x) — on a deceleration in comps to +2%, not on a break in the earnings model: Q2 EPS beat by 35% and guidance was *raised*.

Normalizing growth capex out gives ~$770M of "steady-state" FCF, or ~5% margin and ~26x EV/FCF. This is the single number I would hold management to over the next four quarters.

### 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

- **Unit-runway TAM (the cleanest math):** 1,242 stores today → 2,000 long-term target (10-K language: "potential to expand store base to 2,000 beyond 2028"). At current average unit volume of ~$9.3M ($11.57B / 1,242), the plan alone equals **~$18.6B revenue** — 1.6x today — before any comp growth. With 2–3% comps, $21–22B is the 2030–2031 landing zone, which is roughly where the far-out consensus sits ($17.5B FY2030E, likely conservative).
- **Share:** at $11.6B Burlington has perhaps 6–8% of a US off-price apparel/home market we size at $150–200B (TJX $60B, Ross ~$22B, plus clubs, Rack, Ollie's, regional players). Off-price has taken share from department stores and mall specialty in every downcycle since 2008; the format's share gains are counter-cyclical and structurally sticky.
- **Penetration:** store density in under-penetrated Sun Belt/underserved markets remains the growth driver; small-format stores are broadening the real-estate addressability below the traditional 25–40k sq ft box.
- **Differentiation:** branded fashion/home mix with a treasure-hunt model and ~$9 average ticket — the "fashion off-price" niche between Ross (value/commodity) and TJX (brand), with sourcing scale that regional closeout players cannot match.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Nov 24, 2026 — Q3 FY2026 results** (consensus EPS $1.73, revenue $2.97B). The single most important event: does H2 comp reaccelerate to the guided 3–4%? A 4%+ print likely triggers a violent re-rating given the 37% drawdown.
- **Dec 2026 – Jan 2027 — Holiday/closeout season.** Off-price is the natural clearinghouse if full-price retailers end the quarter over-inventoried after tariff-driven pre-buying; strong closeout availability in spring 2027 would support gross margin and comps simultaneously.
- **March 2027 — FY2027 guidance.** Street models $13.43 (+13% EPS, +8.4% revenue). A guide of $13.50+ with 3%+ comps re-bases the multiple off 17.5x.
- **Store openings:** ~100+ net new units in FY2026 (path toward ~1,340), with new-market entries and small-format tests; each point of unit growth is worth ~$115M of revenue.
- **Margin recovery + capital returns:** operating margin at 7.3% vs. the 8.24% FY2021 peak; every 100bps of operating margin ≈ $115M EBIT ≈ $1.35/share. $278M of buybacks in FY2025 with room to accelerate as growth capex matures.

### 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Google Trends ("Burlington Coat Factory", US):** sharply seasonal, and the *level* is negative year-over-year — 52–54 in the second/third week of Nov 2026 versus 74–80 in the same weeks of 2025 (‑28% to ‑32%), while the mid-year base held flat at 30–37. This **corroborates the comp deceleration** rather than refuting it; treat the +3–4% H2 comp guide as the optimistic end until traffic data turns.
- **Hiring:** ~4,315 open roles on Indeed including seasonal stocking/retail associate postings — normal holiday ramp, no distress signal, but the proxy is qualitative only (no time series available).
- **Analyst behavior (the bullish counter-signal):** targets were cut 2–15% post-Q2 (Goldman $394→$382, Wells $375→$370, Bernstein $365→$355, MS $438→$432, DB→$323) yet six firms *raised* targets into mid-August ($440 UBS, $411 Barclays, $410 Jefferies, $400 Evercore) and Wolfe *initiated* Outperform at $356 in late October with the stock at $248. Estimates rose ~10% for outer years while the multiple collapsed — the definition of a de-rating, not an estimate cut.
- **Price/volume tape:** the Q2 gap-down (Aug 27, 3.96M shares, 3x normal) was followed by a slow drift to $227 (Sept 15) and a bounce to $277 (Oct 1), then a fresh leg down to $235 on 3.9M shares (Nov 23) *ahead of* earnings — positioning is bearish into the print, which raises the payoff to a clean beat.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (high conviction, not yet Strong Buy)

The growth engine is intact — 9.9% trailing revenue CAGR, gross margin up 333bps in three years, ~15% forward EPS CAGR, 7-for-7 beats averaging +15%, and a 60% unit runway — while the price has been cut to 19.8x FY2026E and 14.9x FY2028E, a 21–27% discount to off-price peers on EV/EBITDA. What keeps this from Strong Buy is that the FCF profile (1.5% margin, 88x P/FCF, Rule of 40 of 10) makes the story dependent on continued external financing of growth through leases and capex rather than self-funding, and the comp line is genuinely decelerating (+6% → +2%) with search-interest data confirming softer demand. My base case: comps normalize at 3%, revenue compounds ~9–10% to $15B by FY2028, EPS $15.82, and the multiple recovers to 22x — a ~$330–350 stock (40–50% upside) within 18–24 months. Downside case (16x FY2026E $11.87) is ~$190, so the risk/reward is roughly 2.5:1.

**What would make it a Strong Buy:** H2 comps ≥4%, or a credible path to positive FCF after growth capex with capex/sales falling below 7%.

---

### 7. Key Risks

1. **Comp deceleration / consumer execution risk (highest).** Q2 comps +2% vs. +6% in Q1; Google Trends down ~30% y/y into holiday. If H2 comps land at 2% rather than 3–4%, FY2027 consensus of $13.43 becomes unachievable and the "cheap multiple" is a value trap.
2. **Free cash flow and capex intensity.** $1.06B capex against $1.23B operating cash flow; two-year cumulative FCF of ~$143M. Any distribution-center cost overrun or slower new-store productivity directly hits the buyback and the equity story. EV/FCF of 88x is the number bears will use.
3. **Tariffs and import cost.** Heavy China/Vietnam sourcing exposure; duty costs can outrun vendor concessions and average-costing lags. Off-price's closeout advantage partially offsets, but gross margin (which has done all the heavy lifting) has limited near-term cushion.
4. **Competitive share loss to TJX/Ross.** Burlington's comp gap to the off-price leaders widened in Q2; Walmart/Target price investment adds pressure at the low end. The format moat is real but narrower than a true innovation moat — this is a scale-economics business, not a proprietary-technology one.
5. **Balance-sheet/leverage.** ~$4.8B of net debt including operating leases; beta 1.50 and three >15% drawdowns in 18 months mean position sizing matters as much as the thesis.
