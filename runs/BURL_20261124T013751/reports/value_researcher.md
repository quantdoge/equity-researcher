# Burlington Stores, Inc. (BURL) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price (Nov 23, 2026 close): $235.16** | Market cap ~$15.0B | EV ~$20.0B | Shares diluted 63.9M
*Stock is ~38% below its July 2026 high of $378 and near its 52-week low ($227).*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: NARROW

**What it is:** Burlington is the #3 US off-price apparel retailer (1,211 stores after 73 net new openings in Q1 FY2026), buying branded excess inventory opportunistically and selling it at 20–60% off full-price in a "treasure hunt" format. ~$11.6B FY2025 sales; ~$12.2B LTM. No e-commerce of consequence (arguably a cost advantage, structurally a limitation).

**The moat that exists:**
- **Buying scale / cost advantage.** $12B+ of purchasing volume gives access to closeout lots smaller buyers cannot absorb, at landed costs full-price retailers can't match. Six distribution centers shipping >99% of units create a genuine logistics cost moat versus sub-scale off-price players.
- **Structural low-cost operator.** Sparse fixtures, minimal staffing, essentially no advertising; SG&A is 33% of sales. This lets Burlington profit at a 43.9% gross margin — structurally below full-price retail and therefore hard for full-price competitors to attack.
- **Efficient scale at the local level.** A Burlington fills a 25–40k sq ft strip-mall box that anchors traffic; the format is not easily replicated by Amazon (no comparable browse/try economics) or by department stores (wrong cost structure).

**Where the moat is weak:**
- **No switching costs, no network effects, no customer captivity.** Loyalty is to the *deal*, not the banner. There is no intangible asset pricing power — Burlington's whole proposition requires it to *give away* price.
- **Third place in a three-player market.** TJX (~$56B sales, ~12% operating margin) and Ross (~$22B) are larger and more profitable. Scale in off-price buying is self-reinforcing, and Burlington is the sub-scale player. Its ~7% operating margin versus TJX's ~11–12% is the quantitative proof of a smaller moat.
- **The moat has *widened modestly* over 5 years** — gross margin +335bps since FY2023 (40.6% → 43.9%) and operating margin +290bps (4.4% → 7.3%) under "Burlington 2.0" merchandising discipline. But margins have only *recovered* to roughly pre-COVID levels (8.2% in FY2022), not exceeded them. This is repair, not widening.
- **Supply-side dependence.** The model feeds on other retailers' inventory mistakes. The merchandise is available, cheap and abundant in bad macro times (counter-cyclical — a real positive), but it is not controllable.

**Buffett circle-of-competence test:** Passes. Retailing economics are legible. But it is a *good* business, not a *great* one — the hallmark of a great business (consistently earning returns far above cost of capital with pricing power) is absent.

**Strengths:**
- **Revenue predictability is high.** FY2022→FY2025 CAGR of 7.5% (from a normalized pre-COVID base), with nine consecutive years of positive comps except the FY2023 freight/inventory trough. Growth is ~2/3 unit-driven, which is higher quality than comp-driven growth.
- **Margin trajectory is genuinely improving** — five straight years of gross margin expansion. Mix, markdown discipline and vendor terms are all better.
- **Earnings compounding is excellent:** EPS $3.49 → $11.14 LTM in three years. FY2026 adjusted EPS guided to **$11.77–$11.97 vs $10.17 in FY2025 (+17%)**.
- **Interest coverage is comfortable:** EBIT $887M / interest $71M = 12.5x.

**Weaknesses (why not an 8+):**
- **FCF conversion is poor — the single biggest flaw.** Cumulative FY2021–FY2025 FCF was $1.12B against $2.09B of net income: **54% conversion**, well below the 80% excellence threshold. Cumulative capex of $3.31B was more than 2x D&A of $1.59B. The company is *investing* its cash flow into ~100 new stores/year and four new DCs. FY2025 FCF was just $172M on $610M of net income (28% conversion) — and FY2025 owner earnings (NI + D&A – total capex) were **negative $32M**. FY2026 capex is guided to ~$875M net of landlord allowances; FCF should recover to ~$400–450M, a ~2.8% yield.
- **Zero-margin-of-safety balance sheet.** Net debt $4.77B (total debt $6.01B including ~$3.9B of operating leases) against equity of $1.81B. **Net debt/EBITDA = 3.3x.** For a discretionary retailer that is real, not cosmetic leverage.
- **ROIC of 10.8% versus an estimated WACC of ~9.5%** — a spread of only ~120bps. *Caveat:* reported ROIC puts lease liabilities in capital while charging rent against EBIT, so it understates true economics; on a pre-lease basis returns are likely high-teens. But on any measure, the spread is not wide.
- **High ROE (34%) is a leverage artifact, not a quality signal.** Equity is only 12% of assets.
- No dividend. Buybacks (~$250–290M/yr) have shrunk the share count from 65.6M to 63.9M over four years — barely enough to offset stock comp.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — Solid (7/10)

- **CEO Michael O'Sullivan** (since Sept 2019, previously COO and, before that, a senior executive at The Fresh Market and Walmart Canada) has executed the most credible turnaround in the sector: +335bps of gross margin, +290bps of operating margin, and a clean exit from the failed home-décor and e-commerce detours of the prior regime. He is a merchant-operator with a clear, numerate plan ("Burlington 2.0").
- **Capital allocation discipline is good, not exceptional.** No dividend (appropriate — the returns on new stores beat a payout). Buybacks of $250–290M/yr are steady and have offset dilution but barely reduced the share count, and were executed at average prices well above today's. Store growth is organic and self-funded; there is no empire-building M&A. Goodwill is only $285M — a legacy of the 2013 IPO era, not a serial acquirer.
- **Communication is candid.** On Aug 27, 2026 management took Q3 adjusted EPS guidance to $1.60–$1.70 versus $2.03 consensus and disclosed a 60–80bps EBIT margin contraction from tariffs and DC start-up costs, while simultaneously *raising* full-year guidance. That is honest disclosure of a bad quarter — a mark in their favor.
- **Concerns:** insider ownership is low (I could not verify exact figures — a data gap; historically in the low single digits, with the CEO's stake modest and largely equity-comp-driven). The company has missed margin targets before (2019–2020). And a $875M–$950M annual capex program plus a 3.3x net-debt/EBITDA ratio leaves limited slack for error.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

*(Do not initiate or add at $235. If already owned, retain — the compounding engine is intact — but this is not a price that offers protection.)*

**Why not Buy:** A genuinely improving, understandable retailer with a real unit-growth runway — but the margin of safety is absent. Base-case intrinsic value of ~$240 sits within 2% of the current quote. ROIC of 10.8% versus a ~9.5% WACC is a narrow moat, not a durable one; FCF conversion of 54% over five years means reported earnings overstate distributable cash; and 3.3x net leverage in a discretionary category is not what Buffett would call a sleep-well-at-night balance sheet. As the mandate states: *a business that trades at a fair price is not a buy.*

**Why not Sell:** The business is not impaired. Comps are positive (+2% in Q2 FY2026 on top of new-store cannibalization), gross margin is at a record, FY2026 adjusted EPS grows ~17%, store count has a credible path from 1,211 toward 2,000, and the off-price model is counter-cyclical — a weakening consumer *increases* access to cheap closeout inventory. At 19.8x forward earnings and 13.6x EBITDA the shares are not expensive in absolute terms.

**Action levels:**
- **Buy** below ~$180 (25%+ discount to base intrinsic value), where the owner-earnings yield exceeds 6.5% and the risk/reward turns asymmetric.
- **Trim** above ~$320–340 (bull-case value).
- **Reassess the thesis upward** if (a) operating margin sustainably clears 8.5%, (b) FCF conversion exceeds 70% once the DC buildout completes, or (c) net debt/EBITDA falls below 2.5x.
- **Near-term catalyst:** Q3 FY2026 results are due **November 24, 2026** — the guidance has already been reset sharply lower, so the bar is now low, but the print is a genuine binary event on a stock sitting at its 52-week low.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Tariffs (the dominant risk).** Burlington's entire proposition is low landed cost on imported goods, with meaningful China exposure. Management has already guided a 60–80bps EBIT margin contraction in Q3 FY2026 for tariff-related costs. Unlike a branded retailer, Burlington *cannot* easily pass through price increases — raising prices destroys the value premise. Structural, not transitory, risk.
2. **Thin margins = high operating leverage both ways.** At a 7.3% operating margin, a 100bps gross margin miss is a ~14% EBIT miss. This is a low-margin business with a high-margin valuation multiple.
3. **Growth-capex treadmill.** $875M+ of annual capex against ~$1.3B of operating cash flow. If new-store productivity (or DC start-up costs) disappoints, FCF stays depressed and the buyback shrinks. Owner earnings have been *negative* in two of the last four years on a total-capex basis.
4. **Leverage.** Net debt $4.77B, total debt $6.01B including leases, 3.3x EBITDA, equity of just $1.81B. Refinancing risk is modest today (coverage 12.5x) but the balance sheet amplifies any earnings shock.
5. **Consumer and competitive pressure.** Core customer is lower-income and stressed; comp guidance of only +1–3% reflects that. TJX and Ross compete on the same closeout lots with better buying scale; SHEIN/Temu and Amazon compress the value gap online.
6. **No e-commerce capability.** A structural, if currently benign, omission that limits long-term relevance with younger shoppers.
7. **Execution risk on the 500-net-new-store, 2024–2028 program** — new stores in smaller markets carry cannibalization and lower first-year returns (already visible in the Q2 comp of +2%).
8. **Data gaps in this analysis:** the Q2 FY2026 press release was inaccessible (HTTP 403) so exact DC start-up guidance and landlord-allowance detail are second-hand; insider ownership figures could not be verified; peer valuation medians came from a stale feed. My maintenance-capex estimate ($350M) is judgment-based and is the single largest sensitivity in the owner-earnings calculation — a $100M swing moves intrinsic value by roughly $25/share.

---

**Bottom line:** A well-run, improving #3 off-price retailer with a long store runway and a narrow moat, priced at roughly fair value after a 38% drawdown. Owner earnings yield ~5%, ROIC only ~1.2pp above WACC, FCF conversion structurally depressed by growth investment. **HOLD at $235.16; I would become a buyer at $180 or below.**
