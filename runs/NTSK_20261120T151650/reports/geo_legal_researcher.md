### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Taiwan Strait disruption (Probability: Low-Moderate near term; Impact: High)**
Netskope maintains significant Taiwan operations (10-K RF #23) and derives 18.5% of revenue from APJ. The cross-strait environment has deteriorated: China has threatened further trade measures against Taiwan, and the US House passed the **PROTECT Taiwan Act 395–2**, warning of severe economic consequences (CFR Global Conflict Tracker; Reuters). A blockade or quarantine would simultaneously (a) threaten engineering headcount continuity, (b) disrupt APJ sales motion, and (c) trigger the data-centre/network failover risk the 10-K already flags. *Mitigant:* Taiwan is an engineering/ops hub, not a revenue-recognition centre; the business is cloud-delivered and geographically replicable.

**Risk 2 — Middle East conflict & sanctions perimeter (Probability: Moderate; Impact: Medium)**
Netskope operates NewEdge data centres in **Saudi Arabia and the UAE**, both inside the blast radius of the ongoing US–Iran confrontation. Reporting through Nov-2026 describes an active Iran war, a US Navy blockade, OFAC's "unprecedented campaign" against Iranian networks spanning 20+ entities across the Middle East and East Asia, and Treasury action against a UAE-based Egyptian bank over Iran trade (WSJ; US Treasury; BBC). For a security vendor with Gulf data centres and channel-led distribution, this raises **sanctions-evasion and third-party screening exposure** — the precise scenario in 10-K RF #47. *Mitigant:* Saudi/UAE are allied jurisdictions; revenue is contractually USD-denominated; but reputational and enforcement tail risk is real.

**Risk 3 — US–China tech decoupling, tariffs and export controls (Probability: High; Impact: Medium)**
The 10-K explicitly warns that supply disruption "whether as a result of **tariffs** or otherwise" could raise costs or delay data-centre capacity expansion (RF #15), and that export/import controls and economic sanctions "could impair our ability to compete in international markets" (RF #47). Hardware and networking equipment for 125+ data centres sits in the tariff crosshairs. *Mitigant:* software-dominant gross margin (74%); modest COGS exposure relative to peers with hardware mix.

*Secondary risks monitored:* India regulatory/visa shifts; EU data-sovereignty rules forcing localisation of the NewEdge network; currency (RF #28) — partly offset since "sales contracts are primarily denominated in U.S. dollars."

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation**
- **zvelo, Inc. v. Netskope, Inc.**, D. Colo. No. 1:24-cv-00619 (filed 6-Mar-2024), Judge Nina Y. Wang. Trade-secret misappropriation claims under the **Defend Trade Secrets Act** and state law, concerning AI/data-classification technology. **Court docket records show plaintiff's claims were dismissed and the case terminated** — a favorable outcome, with no material reserve disclosed. *(Note: a widely circulated $59.4M trade-secret award figure relates to Insulet v. EOFlow, an unrelated matter — not Netskope.)*
- **Investor-side activity:** Johnson Fistel has opened a **pre-suit investigation** into potential claims on behalf of Netskope investors following the post-IPO share decline. No securities class action has been confirmed as filed in available sources. This is latent, not active, exposure — but with beta of 2.99 and a peak-to-trough drawdown of ~50% from the IPO price, the securities-litigation risk profile is elevated.
- **Structural risk factors:** IP infringement claims against the company (RF #40) and broad **indemnity obligations** for IP infringement in customer contracts (RF #42) — the latter is the more consequential latent liability for an enterprise software vendor.

**Regulatory**
- **Data protection/privacy** (RF #43): GDPR, CCPA, and sectoral rules; Saudi **PDPL** and SDAIA cloud-procurement guidelines mandate data localisation — Netskope markets dedicated residency support to comply.
- **AI/ML regulation** (RF #44): EU AI Act and emerging US state rules apply directly to Netskope's AI-driven detection and "AI security" features — a live product line, not a hypothetical.
- **Anti-corruption/FCPA** (RF #46) and **export controls/sanctions** (RF #47) — both explicitly disclosed.
- **Government procurement** (RF #21): ~11% of revenue; FedRAMP authorisation, protest risk, and exposure to federal budget/continuing-resolution cycles.
- **Tax** (RF #48–50): multi-jurisdiction transfer pricing and sales/VAT exposure; NOL carryforward limitations post-IPO.
- **Debt** (RF #51–52): convertible notes create redemption/covenant obligations.

**Governance/operational:** 69 risk factors across 6 groups in the FY2026 10-K; a **June 2026 restructuring cut ~5% of the global workforce** as part of an "AI-native" transition — a disclosure and execution risk worth tracking.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Channel concentration + high-risk geography.** The 10-K states Netskope relies on channel, technology, telecom, MSP and systems-integrator partners for "substantially all customers," while ~44% of revenue is international and includes Gulf operations. Third-party/intermediary due diligence under **FCPA** is the single most acute compliance exposure. *No evidence of any violation — flagging structural risk only.*
2. **Sanctions screening in an active conflict zone.** Gulf data centres + Middle East conflict + OFAC's Iran network designations require robust end-user/end-use screening; software licensing to sanctioned-adjacent parties is a known enforcement priority.
3. **Data localisation vs. global architecture.** Saudi PDPL, EU sovereignty requirements, and India's regime can conflict with a single global cloud platform — a compliance-cost and potential contractual-breach vector.
4. **AI regulatory exposure.** AI Act obligations attach to product features already shipping, not just future roadmap.
5. **Post-IPO securities exposure.** Pre-suit investigation active; disclosure-controls scrutiny heightened (RF #55).

*No evidence found of DOJ/SEC/FTC investigations, OFAC designations, or FCPA actions against Netskope.*

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale.** Revenue is well-diversified (US 56.5%, EMEA 25.0%, APJ 18.5%) with a stable mix year-over-year and no single high-risk jurisdiction dominating. The most visible litigation (**zvelo**) was dismissed, and no government investigation or sanctions action is on record. The rating is held at Medium rather than Low by three factors: (i) **Taiwan** engineering exposure beneath 18.5% APJ revenue, (ii) **Gulf data centres** inside an active US–Iran conflict and sanctions perimeter, and (iii) **~11% government revenue** subject to political and procurement cycles. Compliance infrastructure appears appropriately mature for a newly public company (FedRAMP High, published anti-corruption policy, data-residency programme), which offsets much of the structural concern.

**Key caveat:** the FY2026 10-K is the only annual report available (IPO Sep-2025); no prior-year 10-K exists for trend comparison, and the S-1/F-1 predecessor filing was not accessible during this review (SEC.gov and investor-relations pages returned HTTP 403). Litigation status is drawn from court docket metadata rather than the filing text itself.
