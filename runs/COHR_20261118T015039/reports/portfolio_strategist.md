## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT — but not in the way it appears**

The dispersion is not about facts. **Eleven of twelve agents agree on the same five facts:**
1. Revenue is genuinely accelerating (+17% → +20% → +34% → **+49.9%** YoY; Q2 guides ~+60%).
2. Gross margin is genuinely expanding (30.9% → 37.5% → **39.1% GAAP / 40.6% non-GAAP**).
3. Free cash flow is genuinely **deeply negative** (−$1.02B FY26, −$868M in Q1 FY27) and working capital is consuming ~$0.50–0.55 per incremental revenue dollar.
4. The customer base is genuinely **~5 buyers**, with one customer simultaneously 20% of revenue, a $2B shareholder, and a deliberate dual-sourcer.
5. The equity is genuinely a **beta-3.6, 97%-vol, 71%-idiosyncratic** instrument.

The split is entirely about **what to pay for those facts** — and that is a discount-rate and durability question, not an operating one:

- **Agreement level on the business: HIGH.** Nobody credible is bearish on the next four quarters. The Short Analyst explicitly rejected the fraud thesis; the Value Researcher explicitly said "the business is fine, the price is not."
- **Agreement level on the valuation: SPLIT, with a 3x range** ($135 prob-weighted IV vs $376–450 street targets). This is the widest dispersion in the file and it *is* the risk metric.

**How I resolve it.** Two conflict rules apply:
- **Risk agents override return agents when the risk is step-function.** Fin Risk, Non-Fin Risk, and Geo/Legal all independently land on HIGH, and they converge on the same mechanism: an $11.8B non-cancellable commitment stack and 212 days of inventory financed by negative operating cash flow. That is a truncated-left-tail structure, and it caps size regardless of the growth case.
- **The Macro Researcher casts the deciding vote on the valuation dispute.** Value's $135 and Growth's $376 are both "correct" in their own discount-rate worlds. At a 2.92% real 10-year and a Fed hiking, the discount-rate world wins *for now* — evidence: the stock fell 60% while beating and raising. The growth case is not wrong; it is **early**, and being early at beta 3.6 is indistinguishable from being wrong on a mark-to-market basis.

**Net: this is a real business in a real upcycle at a genuinely contested price. That supports a small position, not a conviction position.**

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **1.0% now → 2.0–2.5% maximum** (of portfolio)

**Recommendation: initiate at 1.0%, with a defined path to 2.0–2.5% on evidence.**

**Why not 3–5% (the "high conviction" band)?** Because conviction is not high — it is *bimodal*. The Kelly logic here is unforgiving: when two rigorous frameworks disagree by 3x, your true edge is close to zero and the correct Kelly fraction is close to zero. Three separate HIGH risk ratings, negative FCF, a plausible second equity raise (Fin Risk and Short both call it the base case), and +27% share-count growth are not a 4% position.

**Why not pass?** Four reasons: (a) demand is *contracted*, not forecast — a $20B LTA plus sold-out CY27 capacity is unusual visibility; (b) forward multiples are genuinely undemanding (20x FY27, 13x FY28, 8.2x FY28 EV/EBITDA) — you are not paying a bubble multiple for a 50%-growth business; (c) the forensic case was tested and **rejected** (flat DSO, expanding GM); (d) insider cluster buying by the CEO and Chair (~$1.22M on 9–10 Nov at ~$190) is the highest-quality signal in the file.

**The beta-adjustment that actually governs the size.** A 2.0% position in COHR contributes **~7.2% of portfolio beta** (2.0% × 3.60). That is economically identical to a 7% position in a market-beta index fund, or a 10% position in a beta-0.7 staple. **Nominal size massively understates the risk contribution.** I size to *beta-adjusted exposure of ~5–8%*, which is why even the full target of 2.5% is at the outer edge of prudent.

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector concentration — the decisive constraint.** COHR is IT / Electronic Equipment. If the portfolio already holds semiconductor or AI-infrastructure exposure (AVGO, MU, AMD, ASML, LITE, FN, or an SMH/SOX position), **COHR does not add a new exposure — it adds the highest-beta expression of an exposure you already own.**

**Correlation (252d) — measured, not estimated:**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **COHR** | 1.00 | **0.85** | **0.81** | 0.64 | 0.63 | 0.60 | 0.54 | **0.53** |

- **0.85 with Lumentum and 0.81 with Fabrinet** — this is effectively a single position. If the portfolio holds LITE or FN, COHR + LITE + FN is **one trade expressed three ways**; combined sizing must be capped at ~3% beta-adjusted, not 2% each.
- **R² to SPY is only 0.29** — **71% of the variance is idiosyncratic and unhedgeable by index shorts.** You cannot offset a Feb-3 earnings shock or an FCC-rule reversal. This is the core argument for small size: the risk is company-specific, non-diversifiable, and binary.
- **Factor tilts it worsens:** +High Beta (3.60, worst of any name screened), +Momentum-reversal (broken), **−Quality** (ROIC 6.1%, accruals +4.0%, Piotroski 6/9), −Low Vol (15/100). Adding COHR is a **directional bet that momentum-reversal and low-quality/negative-FCF factors mean-revert**. That is a legitimate bet — it is just not a diversification.

**Fit verdict:** acceptable *only* as a satellite/carve-out from an existing AI-infra budget, funded by trimming a lower-conviction optical or semi-infrastructure name (LITE is the natural funding source: 0.85 correlation, 16.9x sales vs COHR's 5.8x, and far less favourable reward-for-risk). **Do not add COHR as an incremental sector allocation.**

1. **Q2 FY27 GAAP gross margin below 39%** (i.e. no progress from Q1) or Q3 guidance implying margin *reversal* — this breaks the entire operating-leverage leg that both Growth and Value require.

3. **Inventory days above 250 with gross margin flat or down** — the Short's formal "Strong Short" trigger, and the clearest evidence that the 212-day build is an overbuild rather than a pre-build.
4. **A second equity raise inside 12 months**, or capex guidance raised again while operating cash flow remains negative. Both Fin Risk and the Short Analyst call this the base case; its occurrence confirms the dilution treadmill and the 6.1% ROIC are structural, not transitional.
5. **Chinese export-licence action on indium phosphide, gallium, or germanium** — the Geo agent's step-function downside; COHR would be volume-constrained precisely as the ban hands it share.
6. **Disclosure that the $20B PhotonLink LTA carries no minimum volumes, no take-or-pay, or shortened termination rights.** The Non-Fin Risk agent correctly escalates this to highest priority: the headline is total contract value, not backlog, and it underwrites the entire FY28+ consensus.
