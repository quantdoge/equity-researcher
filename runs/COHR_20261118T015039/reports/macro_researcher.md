## MACRO RESEARCH — COHERENT CORP. (COHR)
*Data as of 17–18 November 2026. All macro figures from FRED; company figures from Q1 FY27 (quarter ended 30-Sep-2026) release of 4-Nov-2026.*

---

### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Unfavourable-transitional ("re-acceleration with a real-rate shock")**

This is not a late-cycle slowdown, and it is not a soft landing. It is a **re-acceleration of nominal growth colliding with a Fed that has been forced back into tightening**, and the equity market is repricing duration accordingly.

- **Growth is accelerating, not fading.** Real GDP rose at a **+3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026** (from +2.2% in Q2), with YoY growth of **+2.1%**. Unemployment has *fallen* to **4.1%** (Oct) from 4.5% a year earlier. Labour market is tight, not cracking.
- **Inflation has re-accelerated ~80bp in ten months.** Headline CPI YoY went from **3.00% (Dec-25) to 3.82% (Oct-26)**. There is no disinflation story to tell.
- **The Fed pivoted from cuts to hikes.** Effective Fed Funds fell from 4.33% to a **3.63% trough (Apr–Aug 2026)**, then the FOMC **hiked 25bp on 16-Sep-26 (to 3.75–4.00%) and again on 28-Oct-26 (to 4.00–4.25%)**, with the September SEP median pointing to 4.00–4.25% through both 2026 and 2027. The market's "cut cycle" is over.
- **The curve has bear-steepened hard.** 2Y 4.74% / 10Y **5.13%** / 30Y 5.47%, 2s10s **+39bp** (no inversion). The 10Y ran from 4.38% (26-Jun) to a **5.31% peak on 5-Oct**, +93bp in ~14 weeks.
- **The move is almost entirely REAL.** 10Y TIPS yield: **2.07% (2-Jun) → 2.95% (5-Oct) → 2.92%**, i.e. **+85bp of real yield**. 10Y breakeven is only **~2.21%** — inflation expectations are anchored; it is the *real discount rate* that is repricing. This is the single most important macro fact for a 2.2-beta, long-duration growth stock.
- **But credit is not stressed.** HY OAS is **3.34%** (vs a 2.63% cycle tight on 31-Aug). That is still "easy money" territory. **No recession is priced in credit.** VIX 22.9, having spiked to 28.2 on 5-Nov — equity volatility is idiosyncratic/AI-sentiment driven, not macro-credit driven.

**Stance: risk-off at the index/duration level, risk-on at the credit and earnings level.** An unusual and hostile mix for high-multiple technology.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Context that reframes the rate question:** COHR is no longer the levered post-merger entity of 2023–24. FY26 net interest expense was **$50M vs $196M** the prior year, and the Q1 FY27 call disclosed a **debt leverage ratio of 1.1x**. The rate-sensitivity story for COHR is therefore **almost entirely a valuation/discount-rate story, not a balance-sheet/refinancing story.** That is a materially better position than a typical 2.2-beta cyclical.

|---|---|---|
| **Real yields +85bp** | **STRONGLY NEGATIVE** | COHR's value sits in a long-dated AI-datacom cash-flow stream. A 85bp real-rate rise mechanically compresses a 10yr+ duration asset by roughly 12–20%. This is empirically visible: COHR's **−64% drawdown (intraday $440 on 3-Jun → $160.23 on 5-Nov)** tracked the June→October real-yield selloff almost step for step. |

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL, tilted UNFAVOURABLE**

**Justification.** The macro environment is **bifurcated**, and the two halves pull in opposite directions:
- **Fundamental/operating macro: FAVOURABLE.** Real growth accelerating, unemployment falling, easy credit, pricing power intact, and a trade policy that is actively removing competitors.
- **Financial/discount-rate macro: UNFAVOURABLE.** A Fed in a hiking cycle, 10Y at 5.13%, **real** 10Y at 2.92%, and a bear steepener — the worst possible rate configuration for a long-duration, 2.2-beta compounder.

I weight these roughly equally, hence **Neutral**, but assign a **negative skew** because (i) the discount-rate channel operates faster than the demand channel (the stock fell 64% in five months on rates + sentiment while revenue grew ~46% YoY), and (ii) the trade-policy tailwind is a *policy option*, not yet an enacted rule, whereas the rate level is a *fact*.

**Positioning implication:** the macro backdrop supports the *earnings*, not the *multiple*. COHR can grow into its valuation but is unlikely to re-rate while real yields sit near 2.9%. This is a stock that is macro-hostage to the long end of the curve, and any entry thesis must underwrite a plateau or decline in real yields.

### 5. The Macro Scenario That Would Materially Surprise — **BEAR: "The Real-Rate Ceiling Breaks"**

**Thesis.** The consensus (and the September SEP) assumes the Fed's two hikes are insurance and that policy peaks at 4.00–4.25%. The genuine tail risk is that **3.8% CPI is not the peak**, and the Fed is forced into a *third and fourth* hike in H1-2027. If 10Y nominal breaks **5.75%** and 10Y real yields break **3.50%**, two things happen at once and they compound:

1. **Multiple compression deepens.** At a 3.50% real risk-free rate, a 2.2-beta growth stock cannot sustain a premium multiple regardless of earnings.
2. **The customer base flinches.** This is the non-linear part. Hyperscaler AI capex plans are increasingly financed with debt and vendor/circular financing structures. A further 100–150bp of long-end yield raises hyperscaler WACC, widens credit spreads from 3.34% toward 5%+, and makes 2027–28 capex plans revisable. **AI capex is a financing decision, not just a technology decision.**

**Transmission to COHR:** revenue growth decelerates from ~45% toward high-single digits in one or two quarters, operating leverage reverses on a 40%+ gross-margin structure with $776M/quarter of capex, and a 2.23 beta does the rest. Downside case: **−40% to −55% from $210.86**, i.e. back below the 5-Nov intraday low of $160 and toward $95–125.

**Trigger to monitor:** 10Y TIPS > 3.50%, HY OAS > 4.50%, and any hyperscaler capex guidance revision. Note that the equity market has *already previewed this* — COHR's 64% drawdown occurred partly on rate moves alone.

**The bull mirror-image (lower probability, higher surprise value):** the hikes prove a policy error and growth rolls over in H1-2027; real yields collapse back to ~2.30%, and simultaneously the Chinese transceiver import ban is *enacted* rather than merely reported. COHR would face falling discount rates, a competitor vacuum, and 45%+ gross margins — a plausible path back through the **$330–425** June highs. Given the credit market is showing zero recession stress (HY OAS 3.34%), I assign this scenario subordinate odds to the bear case.

---

**Summary line:** *COHR's operating environment is the best it has been in years — 3.4% real growth, tight labour, easy credit, pricing power, and a competitor-excluding trade policy. Its financial environment is the worst in years — a Fed hiking into 3.8% CPI with real 10Y yields at 2.92%. The stock is not trading on the macro that drives its revenue; it is trading on the macro that drives its discount rate. Own the earnings, but respect the curve.*
