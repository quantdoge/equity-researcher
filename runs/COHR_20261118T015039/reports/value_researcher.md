## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW** (improving, but unproven through a cycle)

Coherent is a vertically integrated photonics manufacturer: indium phosphide and GaAs fabs, lasers, optical components, datacom transceivers (800G/1.6T), and SiC. The AI datacenter's copper→optical transition is the demand driver.

**Evidence for a moat:**
- **Cost/scale advantage in InP.** Management has doubled internal indium phosphide output YoY and plans to more than double it again by end-CY2027. Owning the epitaxy/wafer step is a genuine structural cost and supply-security advantage that merchant assemblers (Innolight, Eoptolink) do not have.
- **Switching costs via qualification.** Hyperscaler transceiver qualification cycles run 12–18 months; once designed in, suppliers are sticky within a platform generation.
- **Intangibles.** 50+ years of laser IP, 51,478 employees, broadest photonics portfolio in the industry — a real "solutions breadth" advantage when customers want one partner across datacom, telecom and industrial.
- **Demand visibility.** Capacity is effectively sold out; FY27 pro forma revenue guided to grow **>60%**, with a first $3B quarter by end-FY27 and a first $4B quarter in FY28.

**Evidence against a wide moat (decisive):**
- **ROIC has been below WACC for four straight years**: −0.3% (FY23), 0.8% (FY24), 4.4% (FY25), **6.1% (FY26)**. With a 10-year Treasury at 5.10% and beta of 2.23, WACC is ~10–11%. A business that has never earned its cost of capital across a cycle does not have a wide moat — it has a strong position in a boom.
- **No demonstrated pricing power through a downturn.** Gross margin collapsed from 38.2% (FY22) to 30.9% (FY24) when demand broke. That is the signature of a cyclical component supplier.
- **Customer concentration** in a handful of hyperscalers whose capex is the entire demand curve, plus credible Chinese and merchant competition.

**Verdict:** narrow, and currently *widening on volume*, not yet proven on returns. This is a good business in an extraordinary upcycle — not a franchise.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

The DCF tool failed (negative base FCF). I built the valuation by hand on owner earnings, which is the correct lens here: **this business does not currently produce free cash flow**, so every conventional multiple is a bet on a future inflection.

**Current multiples:**
- Trailing GAAP P/E 51.2x; EV/EBITDA 27.4x; EV/Sales ~6.1x — a **61% premium** to the vendor's peer-median P/E of 31.7x (peer set is mega-cap semis; Lumentum/Fabrinet/Marvell are the right comps and were unavailable).
- Forward, the picture is friendlier: FY27E revenue ~$10.9B and non-GAAP EPS ~$10.20 → **~20.7x**; FY28E ~$14B revenue and ~$13.50 EPS → **~15.6x**.
- FCF yield: **−2.5%**. P/FCF: not meaningful.

**Scenario DCF (10% discount rate, 3% terminal, net debt deducted):**

| | FY31 revenue | FY31 owner earnings | PV of equity | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** — AI optical sustains, 45%+ GM achieved, capex normalizes | $20B | $3.6B | ~$37.9B | **~$194** |
| **Base** — growth moderates post-FY28, mid-cycle margins | $16B | $2.4B | ~$24.6B | **~$126** |
| **Bear** — AI capex digestion in FY28, margins revert | $11B | $1.2B | ~$13.5B | **~$70** |

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $135/share (30/45/25 weights).**

**The killer arithmetic:** even in my **bull** case — 45% gross margins, $4B quarterly revenue, and a 10% discount rate — the stock is worth ~$194, *below today's $210.86*. The market is already paying for the most favorable outcome. At today's EV of $43.6B, the bull case delivers roughly a 4–5% unlevered return over five years, funded by ~$2B/yr of capex, against a 5.10% risk-free rate. That is not a margin of safety; it is the absence of one.

**What the price implies:** ~$211 requires FY31 owner earnings of roughly $4B+ at a 10% discount — i.e., the bull case *and then some*. It assumes hyperscaler AI capex grows through 2028, Coherent holds share against Chinese competitors, 45%+ gross margins are achieved, and capex intensity falls without a demand pause. Any one of those failing cuts intrinsic value by 30%+.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Grade: Good operator, aggressive allocator — B**

- **Jim Anderson (CEO since June 2024, ex-Lattice)** has done exactly what an operator should: exited low-return businesses, imposed pricing discipline ("pricing optimization" is explicitly credited for margin expansion), and set credible, specific targets (45% GM, $3B and $4B quarter milestones). Execution against guidance has been excellent — five consecutive EPS beats, each accelerating.
- **Capital allocation is the concern.** FY26 deployed $1.1B of capex and is committing more, financed partly by a 26% increase in diluted shares and by working-capital expansion — all at the top of a cycle, into a customer base whose demand may not persist. Buffett's test — "does every retained dollar create more than a dollar of value?" — cannot be answered affirmatively at a 6.1% ROIC. It may be answered affirmatively in FY28; that is the bet.
- **Positives:** debt reduced and rating upgraded; no empire-building M&A since the transformative II-VI/Coherent deal; insiders aligned via equity.
- **Negatives:** the dilution, the absence of buybacks at any point in the last three years (including at $42–50/share, when repurchases would have been enormously value-accretive), and a capital plan that leaves zero buffer if the cycle turns.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (valuation-driven; not a business-quality call)

This is the best operating momentum Coherent has had in a decade: 50% revenue growth, 79% datacom growth, expanding margins, a sold-out order book, and a credible path to 45%+ gross margins. **The business is fine. The price is not.**

Applying my own discipline — require a 20–30% discount to a *conservatively* estimated intrinsic value — the math yields:
- Probability-weighted IV: **~$135** → price is **56% above** value.
- Even the bull case (~$194) sits **9% below** the current price.
- The stock has already fallen 48% from its May 2026 high ($405 → $211) while beating and raising — the market has begun de-rating exactly this kind of long-duration, capex-hungry AI growth, with the 10-year at 5.10% and VIX at 25.

**I would not own it here.** Value-oriented capital should wait for either (a) evidence of positive free cash flow and ROIC sustainably above 12–15% through a full year, which would materially raise the base case, or (b) a price in the **$100–125** range (~0.6–0.65× my bull case), which is where a genuine margin of safety appears.

*For existing holders with a cost basis far below current levels:* a partial trim into strength is defensible; holding a full position requires accepting that you are underwriting peak-cycle earnings at a peak-cycle multiple.

**Sell-side note:** the street is at $297–$450 (Rosenblatt $400, Stifel $450, Raymond James $448, Susquehanna $350, B. Riley $297). Those targets capitalize forward EPS at 30–45x. That is a growth-momentum framework, not an owner-earnings framework, and it is precisely the framework that leaves no room for error.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to owning it (i.e., to the bull case):**
1. **Single-customer-class concentration.** Hyperscaler AI capex is the demand curve. A single digestion year — as happened to optics in 2019 and 2023 — could halve revenue and return gross margins to ~31% (FY24 precedent).
2. **Technology substitution.** Co-packaged optics (CPO) from Broadcom and others could disintermediate pluggable transceivers; silicon photonics could erode InP's cost advantage. Coherent participates in both, but its incumbency is in pluggables.
3. **Competitive price erosion.** Innolight/Eoptolink and other Chinese vendors have repeatedly compressed transceiver pricing once supply catches up. Coherent's capacity additions are industry-wide.
4. **Capital-cycle trap.** $2B+/yr of capex committed against demand that is contracted loosely; if the cycle turns mid-build, the fixed-cost absorption reverses violently and the balance sheet (BB-rated, $2.4B net debt) has limited slack.
5. **Dilution and working-capital risk.** Inventory up 80% YoY carries obsolescence risk in a fast-node-transition business; 26% share-count growth dilutes the per-share recovery even if enterprise value holds.
6. **Discount-rate sensitivity.** Beta 2.23 and a 5.10% 10-year make this among the most rate-sensitive large caps in the market.

**Risks to my Sell thesis (what would make me wrong):**
- FY27 pro forma growth exceeding 60% *and* 45%+ gross margins arriving by mid-CY2028 *and* capex intensity falling to <8% of revenue would push FY31 owner earnings toward $4.5–5B, supporting $240–280/share. That is a real possibility — it is simply not the conservative case, and it is already substantially in the price.
- Historical note: I flagged this stock's predecessor at $42 in early 2024; the operating turnaround has been considerably more powerful than a conservative owner-earnings lens anticipated. Discipline says the correct response is to raise the base case with evidence of durable FCF, not to pay for the bull case today.
