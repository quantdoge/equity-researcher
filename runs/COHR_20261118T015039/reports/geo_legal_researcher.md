## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US–China photonics decoupling, two-sided (Probability: High | Impact: High)
COHR sits at the exact epicentre of the current US–China tech conflict, and is currently a **net beneficiary** of the US leg of it:
- The Trump administration is moving forward with restrictions on US imports of new models of Chinese optical transceivers on national-security grounds (FCC authority); reported 11/17/2026. COHR rose 8% on the report (LITE +4%, AAOI +7%). Chinese suppliers hold ~2/3 of the global transceiver market (Counterpoint), so a ban is a structural share-shift opportunity.
- **But** the retaliation leg is real and explicitly flagged by third parties: CoreWeave and Microsoft lobbied against the ban, arguing China could ban **indium phosphide** exports — the very input US transceiver makers need. Lumentum and Coherent "lack the scale to replace" Chinese producers (Foundation for American Innovation, cited by Reuters). AAOI's CEO said the industry including COHR/LITE could meet **<80% of customer demand** by end-2027.
- Mitigant: CEO Anderson stated COHR has secured InP substrates and critical inputs for its ramp; 2027 capacity "basically booked out," with customers booking into calendar 2028. COHR's vertical integration (in-house InP/GaAs) is a relative advantage vs. peers.
- Separately: a Commerce rule issued **11/16/2026** requires licenses to ship advanced AI chips to data centers anywhere that would serve Chinese companies — a demand-side risk to the AI optics cycle that is COHR's growth engine.

### Risk 2 — Tariffs and export controls on a 59%-offshore asset base (Probability: High — partially realized | Impact: Medium)
- **Section 232 semiconductor tariffs are in force**: a January 2026 presidential action imposed a **25% tariff** on semiconductor imports and certain derivative products (post-April 2025 Section 232 investigation).
- With 86.6% of headcount and ~59% of long-lived assets in APAC (Malaysia 13.8%, China 28.2%, Vietnam 3.5%), a large share of US-bound product crosses a tariff border. Malaysia/Vietnam rates are far below China's (~11% and ~18% trade-weighted vs. ~41% for China per Rhodium Group) — the Malaysia/Vietnam footprint is a partial hedge, not a full one.
- **Export controls (outbound):** COHR's industrial and semiconductor lasers are classic dual-use items under the EAR; 10-K flags "exposure to trade controls." I found no disclosed BIS enforcement action or Entity List designation against COHR — but I could not read the full Item 1A/Item 3 text (SEC EDGAR returned 403; the filing tool returned only exhibits). **This is a documentation gap, not a clean bill of health.**
- CHIPS Act: $50M preliminary memorandum of terms for the Sherman, TX expansion (ground broken 8/12/2026 with NVIDIA; multi-year NVIDIA supply agreement). This is *positive* geopolitics but introduces conditionality, clawback and political-cycle risk, plus milestone/compliance obligations.

### Risk 3 — China asset concentration: expropriation, licensing and "de-China" customer mandates (Probability: Low–Medium | Impact: High)
- $968.8M (28.2%) of long-lived assets in China. Beijing's export-control architecture (gallium since 2023; a December 2024 directive prohibiting in principle exports of gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials to the US; expanded rare-earth/magnet controls through 2026 restricting items with trace Chinese content) directly touches COHR's materials supply chain — the company issued a public assessment on gallium/germanium restrictions as far back as July 2023.
- 10-K flags: dependence on limited/sole-source suppliers, **rare-earth materials and noble gases, including Chinese export restrictions on certain minerals**, and InP substrates. Shortages "could raise costs, constrain output."
- Second-order: US hyperscaler customers are increasingly mandating non-China content in AI infrastructure. COHR's large China asset base may become a commercial liability even absent any Chinese action — a slow-burn margin/qualification risk rather than a shock risk.
- Customer concentration amplifies all of the above: **two customers each >10% of FY26 revenue**.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Securities class action (active).** Class period **September 29, 2020 – September 29, 2025**. The complaint alleges materially false/misleading statements connected to the **August 13, 2025** announcement of the agreement to sell the Aerospace & Defense business to Advent for **$400 million**, after which COHR fell **over 19% on August 14, 2025**. Multiple firms soliciting (Levi & Korsinsky filed; Rosen Law Firm and Pomerantz investigating). *Assessment: standard post-drop securities suit; likely a modest, insured settlement. Watch for an adverse ruling on the 2020–2025 class period — a decade-long class period is unusually broad and raises potential damages exposure.*

**Divestiture-related contingency tail.** Two completed sales create indemnification / reps-and-warranties exposure:
- A&D business to Advent ($400M), closed September 2025.
- **Munich, Germany-based product division**, sold **January 30, 2026**, at a **$96M loss**. Cross-border carve-out of a German entity = German works-council/transfer-of-undertakings (Betriebsübergang) and tax-indemnity risk.

**Regulatory perimeter.** Primary regulators: FCC (transceiver import rules — currently a *tailwind*), BIS/Commerce (EAR, Section 232, AI-chip licensing — headwind), CFIUS-adjacent considerations via the Denso/Mitsubishi Electric silicon-carbide JV (Japanese strategic partners hold non-controlling interests and convertible preferred), plus CHIPS Program Office conditions. No active DOJ, SEC, or FTC enforcement action against COHR surfaced in my searches.

**IP.** ~3,160 issued patents and ~1,280 pending applications globally (6/30/2026). No specific disclosed IP litigation; in transceivers/optics, patent assertion and ITC §337 actions are a routine industry hazard.

**Debt covenants.** 5.000% senior notes due 2029 plus term loans with restrictive covenants — relevant because a geopolitical shock to cash flow would collide with leverage headroom during peak capex.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **HIGH**

**Rationale.** Revenue is anchored in the US (65%) and the balance sheet is anchored in Asia (59% of long-lived assets, 87% of employees). COHR's largest growth product — AI datacenter optical transceivers — is the object of an active US–China policy fight in *both* directions: Washington is preparing to ban Chinese transceivers (upside to COHR's volume/share) while Beijing holds an indium-phosphide and critical-minerals lever over the US supply chain (downside to COHR's input costs and ability to fill that volume). Layered on top are 25% Section 232 semiconductor tariffs, outbound export-control exposure on dual-use lasers, a 28%-of-assets China footprint, a >10%-of-revenue dependence on two customers, and an active securities class action with an unusually long class period.

**Key nuance for the investment case:** the geopolitical risk here is **two-sided and currently net-favourable** — policy is, on balance, transferring share *to* COHR from Chinese competitors. The asymmetry is that the downside scenario (Chinese InP/Ga/minerals retaliation combined with a slow US capacity ramp) is a step-function impairment, while the upside is a gradual share shift. Legal/regulatory risk alone would rate **Medium**; the geopolitical overlay is what drives the overall **High**.

**Three monitorables:** (1) the FCC/Commerce final rule on Chinese transceiver imports and its in-force date; (2) any Chinese export-licence action on indium phosphide, gallium or germanium; (3) COHR's Item 3 legal-proceedings disclosure and class-certification timeline in the 2025 securities suit.
