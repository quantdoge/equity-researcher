### 2. Key Red Flags Found

**① Working capital has become the business.** Five consecutive quarters of negative WC change totalling -$1.59B. Every incremental dollar of revenue is now consuming roughly $0.50–0.55 of working capital. Growth here is cash-negative, which is the opposite of the operating-leverage story the multiple requires.

**② Inventory up 105% YoY vs revenue up 50%.** Inventory went from $1.63B (Sep-25) to $3.34B (Sep-26). Inventory days: **148.7 → 211.4 (+63 days)**, and 211.7 vs 139.8 on a fiscal-year basis — the highest in the dataset and a 52% increase in days.

**③ A depreciation cliff is being manufactured.** TTM capex $1,775M, and the September quarter alone was $776M (a ~$3.1B annualised run-rate). Annualised D&A is only ~$600M. Each capex vintage adds ~$430M/yr of depreciation on a 7-year life. By FY29, D&A plausibly triples to $1.8–2.2B. Holding GAAP margins then requires ~$3B of *additional* revenue. Consensus assumes exactly that — i.e., margin expansion is hostage to unbroken 30%+ growth.

**④ Serial dilution, at scale.** Basic weighted shares: 154.8M (FY25) → 177.3M (FY26) → 196.2M (Q1FY27), **+26.7% in twelve months** per the FY26 10-K. Total equity rose $2.76B in FY26 against net income of $250M + SBC — so ~$2.5B of equity issuance, of which NVIDIA's $2B stake is the bulk. Diluted 202.0M vs basic 196.2M = a further ~6M dilutive equivalents. At a $3B+ FY27 capex run with ~$0 OCF and $1.75B of cash, **another capital raise is the base case**, not the bear case.

**⑤ Customer concentration + related-party overlap.** One customer = **20% of FY26 revenue**, and that customer (NVIDIA) is simultaneously a **$2B shareholder** and a multi-year supply partner — and has invested in Lumentum on the same programme. That is a customer with equity, board-level information, and a deliberate dual-sourcing strategy. It can set price. This structurally caps the "gross margin >45%" bull target.

**⑥ Below-the-line earnings support.** FY26 operating income of $901.5M less interest expense of $190.3M implies ~$711M pre-tax; reported net income was **$805M**. Something like ≥$94M of benefit (other income / a near-zero or negative effective tax rate) is doing work below the operating line. Not fraud — but not repeatable quality either.

**⑦ Industrial segment shrinking (-10.3% in FY26)** while datacenter grows 85%. The "diversified photonics platform" is now a single-vertical bet, with the mature businesses (Aerospace & Defense sold to Advent for $400M; Materials Processing Tools to Bystronic) used to flatter "pro forma" growth comparisons.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

- **Pricing power: intact for now.** Gross margin 35.4% (FY25) → 37.5% (FY26) → 39.1% GAAP / 40.6% non-GAAP (Q1FY27). Expanding GM is strong evidence *against* commoditisation in the current window.
- **Receivables are clean — I could not substantiate channel stuffing.** DSO is **61.8 days vs 61.9 days a year ago**, dead flat. Receivables grew 49.6% YoY against revenue +49.9%. The "AR growing faster than revenue" flag (31.7% vs 22.5%) is a fiscal-year artefact; quarterly it disappears. **This is a genuine weakness in the bear case and I flag it as such.**
- **The real competitive risk is roadmap, not margin.** 211 days of inventory in 800G/1.6T pluggables, where the next node (3.2T, co-packaged optics) arrives inside 18–24 months. Coherent's franchise is in pluggables; CPO is the substitution threat. If NVIDIA's architecture moves, the inventory becomes the write-down and the margin becomes unabsorbed overhead — the reverse of the operating leverage now boosting gross margin.
- **Dual-sourcing is already policy**, not a risk: NVIDIA funded both Coherent and Lumentum ($4B combined). Market context is genuinely strong (TrendForce: AI optical transceivers $16.5B 2025 → $26B 2026, +57%), but COHR's own YTD performance (+60% at mid-September) badly lagged Lumentum (+148%). In a boom, the #2 by share-price performance implies share loss or a weaker mix.

Guidance is being managed on the most favourable metric available at each turn.
- **No evidence found of auditor change, restatement, or self-dealing related-party transactions.** I looked; the file is clean on those specific tests.
- Short interest **5.15% of float, 1.1 days to cover** — the short is *not* crowded, which cuts both ways: no squeeze fuel, but also no valuation support from shorts covering.

### 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, but it is a cash-quality/valuation bear, not an accounting-fraud bear.**

The thesis: *COHR is converting a genuine AI-optics order book into GAAP earnings and negative cash flow simultaneously. Every dollar of the current $963M TTM profit has been more than consumed by a $1.59B working-capital build and a $1.78B capex programme, funded by a $2.5B equity raise (share count +27%) and $600M of incremental net debt. The company has pre-announced its own depreciation cliff: ~$3B/yr of capex against ~$600M of D&A, which will compress GAAP margins precisely when growth decelerates — and growth must decelerate, because 211 days of inventory in a 12–18-month product cycle is either an unhedged bet on flawless execution or an eventual write-down. With 20% of revenue concentrated in a customer that is also an equity holder, a dual-sourcer, and the sole arbiter of architecture, Coherent does not control its own pricing or its own roadmap.*

**Where the short is weak — and the bulls are right:**
1. Revenue is *accelerating*: +17.3% → +17.5% → +20.6% → +33.7% → **+49.9%** YoY. Q2 guides to $2.55–2.75B (another ~+60%).
2. Gross margin is expanding 350bp+ — the opposite of moat erosion.
3. DSO is flat. **No channel-stuffing evidence.** This kills the most common short attack.
4. NVIDIA's $2B plus a multi-year, multi-product, headline-reported ~$20B supply agreement is real, contracted demand visibility.
5. Net debt $2.79B is only ~6% of market cap; interest coverage is comfortable.
6. **Valuation is not extreme on forward earnings**: ~20x FY27 consensus EPS of $10.41, ~13x FY28's $16.12. Shorting 13x forward earnings with 50%+ growth is historically a losing trade.

**And the timing problem:** the stock is already -52% from its June high and has been through three separate >12% single-day drawdowns. Annualised volatility is **86%**, with **34% of sessions moving ±5%** and 19 sessions ±10% in a year. A naked short here has a fat left tail against you in the event of one more guidance raise.

**The one genuinely under-priced item:** long-dated consensus. FY30 revenue of **$28.8B** (4x FY26) and EPS of **$27.87** rest on **two analysts**. That hockey stick — not the near-term numbers — is what supports the terminal value, and it is the most attackable line in the model. If FY30 revenue is $18B rather than $28.8B at 20x, the stock is a ~$120 asset. That is the short's real engine room, and it requires patience measured in years, not quarters.

**Position recommendation:** Do **not** run this as a headline short. It is a **small, volatility-adjusted, paired short** (short COHR / long a cheaper optical or semiconductor-capital-equipment name) sized to ≤1/3 of a normal short because 86% annualised vol will otherwise dictate the P&L. The forensic evidence justifies *avoidance and underweight*, not conviction selling. The strongest version of this trade existed at $350–430; at $210 with 13x FY28 earnings, the market has already priced much of the cash-quality story.

**What would flip me to "Strong Short":** (a) inventory days above 250 with GM flat or down; (b) capex guidance raised again *while* OCF stays negative; (c) any single quarter of datacenter growth below +30%; (d) evidence of net insider selling at scale in the Sep–Nov Form 4 cluster; (e) a second equity raise inside twelve months.

**Data gaps disclosed:** SEC.gov and the IR site returned 403s, so insider transaction direction and 10-K risk-factor text are unverified; FY26 annual PPE ($21.29B) is internally inconsistent and was replaced with the quarterly figure ($3.00B); FY24–FY25 SG&A is reported as $0 in the feed.
