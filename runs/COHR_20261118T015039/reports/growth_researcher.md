# Coherent Corp. (COHR) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $210.86 | Market cap $41.5B | EV $42.8B | Beta 2.23 | Data as of Nov 17, 2026*

---

## 1. Growth Story

Coherent is the vertically integrated photonic backbone of the AI datacenter — the company that owns the indium phosphide (InP) and gallium arsenide (GaAs) materials science, the laser sources, and the finished 800G/1.6T transceiver modules, rather than assembling modules from others' parts. The thesis just converted from "narrative" to "contracted": in September 2026 Coherent signed a **five-year, ~$20B PhotonLink agreement** with an unnamed AI datacenter customer (~$4B/year beginning early CY2028), while management states **calendar 2027 capacity is already sold out** and 6-inch InP capacity is doubling a quarter ahead of plan. This is a materials-science monopoly on the scarcest input in AI networking, scaling into visible demand — the archetype of a company moving from cyclical hardware vendor to contracted infrastructure supplier.

**Forward trajectory (consensus):** FY27 rev $11.25B / EPS $10.41 → FY28 $15.78B / $16.12 → FY30 $28.78B / $27.87. That is a **~42% revenue CAGR FY26→FY30** and ~90% EPS growth in FY27 alone.

**Rule of 40: 8.1** (22.5% growth − 14.4% FCF margin). This is the one genuinely weak number, and it is *entirely* capex-and-working-capital driven: FY26 capex was $1.10B and working capital consumed $1.10B to fund the ramp, producing −$1.02B FCF on $787M net income. **This is investment into contracted demand, not margin erosion** — but it is the single item I would monitor hardest.

**Earnings quality:** 7/7 beats, average surprise +6.6%, four consecutive quarters of accelerating sequential revenue ($1.58B → $1.69B → $1.81B → $2.05B → $2.37B). Revision momentum is **+21.5%** on the FY30 EPS estimate — an upgrade cycle, not a downgrade cycle.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

The merchant optical transceiver market is ~$17–18B in 2026, forecast to reach $36B+ by 2033 (~11–17% CAGR), with the AI-datacom segment (800G/1.6T/3.2T) compounding far faster than the blended figure. Coherent holds roughly **25% unit share** — the volume leader.

The penetration story is really a *content-per-accelerator* story: each generation (400G → 800G → 1.6T → 3.2T) roughly doubles bandwidth per rack unit, so Coherent's revenue per AI cluster rises even at flat unit volumes. Add co-packaged optics (CPO) — where Coherent's integrated-optics platforms are already generating early revenue — and the addressable pool expands again by pulling optics inside the switch. Against a ~$50B+ AI-optics TAM by 2030, the $28.8B FY30 consensus implies Coherent capturing well over half of *today's* total market — aggressive, but the $20B PhotonLink contract alone underwrites $4B/year of it, and management claims capacity sold through 2027. **Vertical integration in InP is the moat**: competitors can buy lasers, but few can scale 6-inch InP substrate capacity on a hyperscaler's timetable.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q2 FY27 results — ~Feb 3, 2027.** Guide $2.55–2.75B and GM 40–42%; a GM print above 41% would validate the scale-leverage thesis and likely re-rate the multiple. Street at $2.41 EPS.
- **6-inch InP capacity doubling — through CY2027.** Management says it is arriving a quarter early; each pull-forward de-risks the FY27 $11.25B revenue bar.
- **PhotonLink ramp — early CY2028.** Customer identity disclosure and any *additional* LTAs (a second hyperscaler would be the largest single re-rating event available).
- **OFC 2027 (March).** 1.6T volume and 3.2T/CPO product unveilings; historically a positive catalyst window for the group.
- **FCF inflection — FY28.** As capex plateaus against $4B/year of contracted PhotonLink revenue, the Rule of 40 math flips hard; the market is not paying for this yet.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **STRONG BUY**

The stock is down ~52% from its June 2026 high of ~$440 and ~60% peak-to-trough, yet every fundamental has improved: revenue growth accelerated from +22% to +50%, gross margin expanded 830bps in two years, and a $20B contract was signed. The November selloff (−13% on a *beat*, then a further leg down in the Nov 12 AI-complex rout) was a sentiment/positioning event, not an earnings event.

I would own this into the February print.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Negative free cash flow / capex supercycle.** −$1.02B FCF in FY26, with capex and working capital both ~$1.1B. If the 1.6T ramp slips even two quarters, Coherent is left with stranded capacity, a working-capital unwind, and a Rule of 40 of 8 that the bulls are currently forgiving. **This is risk #1.**
2. **Customer concentration.** A single unnamed AI customer represents ~$4B/year of contracted revenue. Concentration of that magnitude in one counterparty — with no public disclosure of termination or take-or-pay terms — is unhedgeable.
3. **Competitive share loss.** Lumentum reportedly holds 50–60% unit share in high-speed 100G/200G-per-lane EMLs — Coherent's volume leadership is not the same as component-level leadership. Chinese module vendors (Innolight, Eoptolink) are aggressive in 800G/1.6T, and Broadcom/Marvell are pushing CPO, which could disintermediate pluggables on a 3–5 year horizon.
4. **AI capex cycle.** Beta of 2.23 and a 60% drawdown in six months demonstrate what happens when the AI trade de-rates. The Nov 12 Nvidia-driven selloff showed COHR trades as a high-beta AI proxy regardless of its own results.
5. **Execution and integration.** 51,478 employees running a complex, capital-intensive, multi-site manufacturing footprint; 6-inch InP scaling is genuinely hard process engineering, and the FY27 consensus bar (+58%) leaves little room for a miss.
6. **Balance sheet.** ~$185M annualized interest expense and merger-era leverage constrain flexibility if the cycle turns mid-buildout.

---

**Bottom line:** A vertically integrated monopoly on the scarcest input in AI networking, currently mispriced because the market is punishing the capex that funds its contracted growth. I would size this as a core disruptive-innovation position, tolerate the FCF trough through FY27, and use the February guide on gross margin as the checkpoint that validates or breaks the thesis.
