## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US tariffs & the 2026 USMCA joint review — Probability: ~100% (already in force); Impact: High**
Section 232 duties of 25% on autos and non-USMCA-qualifying parts remain the primary vehicle of US auto trade policy, layered on top of MFN, with only narrow offsets (e.g. a 3.75% aggregate offset mechanism) (Baker Donelson; USTR 2026 USMCA Autos Report to Congress). Stellantis guides to **~€1.0bn of net tariff costs in FY2026** (~$1.1bn) and booked ~€300m in H1 2025 alone (Q3 2026 6-K; Reuters 21-Jul-2025; CBS News). Mitigation is capital-intensive and has already begun: a **$13bn US investment plan (Oct-2025)** raising US output ~50% with five new launches, plus contingency work to move Ram heavy-duty output from Mexico to Michigan. **Second-order risk:** the USMCA joint review is live through 2026, and USTR has signalled Section 232 duties will not simply be removed; a revamped USMCA "may not restore duty-free automotive trade" (Autonews). Canada has already cut tariff relief for Stellantis/GM imports in retaliation for production pulled out of Canada — a tit-for-tat escalation channel.

**② EU CO2/emissions and industrial policy — Probability: High; Impact: Medium-High**
The Dec-2025 Commission proposal and the **20-Oct-2026 EU lawmakers' deal cut the 2035 target from 100% to 90%**, effectively retiring the full ICE phase-out (Reuters 20-Oct-2026; Euronews). That materially de-risks the "factory closure or fines" scenario Stellantis itself flagged (Reuters 1-Jul-2025) — but the near-term 2025–2027 compliance years remain binding, and Stellantis **exited Tesla's CO2 pool for 2026** (with Toyota/Subaru), removing a purchased-credit cushion and shifting compliance onto in-house BEV/PHEV mix (Reuters 4-Mar-2026; Autonews). Related: Chinese export controls on heavy rare earths/magnets (April 2025 rules and the November 2025 list) disrupted EU auto supply chains and remain a live single-point-of-dependency (CLEPA; Reuters 21-Oct-2025) — directly relevant to STLA's e-motor sourcing for Europe-assembled EVs.

**③ European political/industrial friction and Chinese-partner dependence — Probability: High; Impact: Medium**
Italy output is only expected at ~450k units in 2026, below initial expectations, after two years of decline, keeping Stellantis in a permanent standoff with the Italian government and unions over plant utilisation (Reuters 6-Oct-2026; Autonews). The strategic answer — **using Chinese partners (Leapmotor) to fill EU plants and supply low-cost EV tech** — carries a geopolitical tail: Stellantis/Leapmotor have been reported as potentially subject to Chinese state pressure over investments in EU countries that backed tariffs on Chinese EVs (WardsAuto), and any technology in-licensing from a Chinese JV partner creates future export-control/IP and US "connected vehicle" policy exposure in its largest profit pool.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **US securities class action (SDNY), filed 2026.** Class period **26-Feb-2025 → 5-Feb-2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline **8-Jun-2026**; multiple firms (Rosen, ZLK, BLB&G, Kessler Topaz lineage, Berger Montague-adjacent). Trigger: the **6-Feb-2026 disclosure of ~€22.2bn in H2-2025 charges** and a business "reset" — STLA fell $2.26 (−23.7%) to $7.28 that day, and roughly 18–27% intraday in Milan, wiping ~€5–6bn of market value (ZLK; PRNewswire; Reuters 6-Feb-2026; Bloomberg). Allegations concern prior assurances on margin durability. **This is the single largest live legal contingency** and will overlap with the FY2026 20-F risk disclosure.
- **€22.2bn H2-2025 charge / EV retreat:** preliminary H2-2025 net loss of €19–21bn; FY2025 revenue €153.5bn (−2%); FY2026 in turnaround under CEO Antonio Filosa's "FaSTLAne 2030" and a €6bn Value Creation Program (Stellantis releases; Autonews).
- **US criminal legacy:** FCA US **pleaded guilty in 2022 to conspiracy to defraud the US** in the diesel emissions probe — $96.1m fine + $203.6m forfeiture ≈ **$300m total** (DOJ press release; Reuters 1-Aug-2022). Prior US EPA/DOJ diesel settlement ~$800m (2019). This establishes a **recidivism-enhancing compliance history** under any future DOJ/EPA resolution.
- **French "Dieselgate" criminal case:** Citroën charged with **consumer fraud** by French prosecutors over Euro 5 diesels sold 2009–2015; the Fiat Chrysler Italy entity is also in the probe; cases were approaching trial with the prosecutor confirming progress in July 2025 (Stellantis 2021 statement; Autonews; Global Investigations Review). Outcome risk: consumer-fraud convictions in France can carry material fines and civil follow-on claims.
- **US safety/NHTSA:** ongoing large recalls and defect investigations — 419,000+ Jeep Grand Cherokee/Grand Cherokee L (side-curtain airbag), >1.07m Jeep Wrangler/Gladiator (power-steering wiring fire risk), 2025 Ram 1500 stability-control recall, and industry-wide **ARC airbag inflator** exposure (Reuters 29-May-2026; KSN; Schmidt Law). Recall intensity is a recurring cost and a NHTSA civil-penalty channel.
- **EU/competition:** no live headline EU antitrust cartel fine identified for Stellantis in this review, but the company is a participant in the multi-year EU auto-sector steel-purchasing and end-of-life-vehicle (ELV) regulation debates; the ELV regulation (recycled-content mandates, dismantling rules) is the most consequential pending EU legislation for cost structure.
- **China JV:** DPCA shareholder-loan-to-equity conversion completed Sept-2026 — a de-risking of the JV balance sheet but confirms continued capital commitment in a jurisdiction with weak minority-investor protection.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Prior US criminal conviction (FCA US, 2022, DOJ fraud conspiracy).** Any new emissions or reporting issue would be treated as a repeat offence — elevated fines, monitorship risk.
2. **Active securities-fraud class action tied to a €22bn charge and a 24% one-day drop** — disclosure-controls (Item 307-style) scrutiny, potential SEC follow-on inquiry, and D&O exposure.
3. **French consumer-fraud criminal charges** (Citroën; FCA Italy under probe) — cross-border enforcement overlap with EU consumer law.
4. **CO2 compliance without a credit pool for 2026** — regulatory-penalty exposure sits directly on the P&L if BEV mix undershoots; historically Stellantis bought Tesla credits precisely to avoid this.
5. **Sanctions/Russia:** clean exit (Kaluga suspended 2022, €144m hit); no identified current sanctions breach, but the Moroccan/Algerian/Turkish export hubs require ongoing dual-use and re-export diligence.
6. **Data privacy / connected vehicles:** EU Data Act and GDPR exposure on in-vehicle data; US Commerce "connected vehicle" ICTS rules could constrain any Chinese-sourced software/telematics in US-market vehicles — a live risk given the Leapmotor technology dependency.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Revenue is concentrated in the two jurisdictions with the most adverse, fast-moving policy regimes for autos — the US (Section 232 tariffs, 2026 USMCA review, ~€1.0bn FY2026 tariff drag, plus a $13bn onshoring capex response) and the EU (CO2 compliance, rare-earth import dependence, politically charged plant closures). The legal profile is unusually heavy for a sector peer: an existing US criminal conviction, active French criminal consumer-fraud proceedings, an unresolved US securities class action arising from a €22bn charge, and chronic NHTSA recall intensity. Offsets: the Oct-2026 EU decision to soften 2035 to 90% is a genuine de-risking of the tail scenario; the Russia exit is complete and clean; South America is a stable, profitable growth offset (Q3'26 +3% revenue); and liquidity of €42.7bn (26% of TTM revenue) gives ample buffer to absorb legal and tariff outcomes.

**Key monitorables:** SDNY class-action consolidation and any SEC subpoena; French diesel trial dates and any conviction/civil follow-on; USMCA joint-review outcome and whether Section 232 relief is offered for USMCA-qualifying content; confirmation of 2027 positive industrial free cash flow (currently −€4.9bn YTD 2026); and whether the Leapmotor technology transfer triggers US ICTS or Chinese outbound-investment restrictions.
