## Stellantis N.V. (STLA) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $4.72 (≈€4.05) · market cap ≈$13.7B / €11.7B · 2.90B diluted shares · net debt €27.0B → EV ≈€38.6B. Financials in EUR as reported; FY2025 10-K filed 26-Feb-2026, Q3-2026 reported 28-Oct-2026.*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NONE** (narrow, eroding brand moats in Jeep/Ram)

Stellantis is a 14-brand, 258,000-employee volume carmaker (Abarth → Vauxhall) born from the PSA–FCA merger. Its historic profit engine was North American trucks/SUVs (Ram, Jeep), which subsidised structurally low-return European volume brands.

Evidence against a moat:
- **No pricing power.** Revenue fell from €189.5B (2023) to €153.5B (2025) — ~19% in two years — while gross margin collapsed from 19.0% → 11.7% → **−2.7%**. Customers walked when prices were pushed; that is the definition of a price-taker.
- **ROIC vs WACC is the killer test.** 21.2% (2022) → 20.6% (2023) → 4.6% (2024) → **−22.2% (2025)**. Peak returns coincided with post-COVID industry-wide pricing, not a durable advantage. Two of the last four years earned below any reasonable cost of capital.
- **Capital intensity.** Capex €10–11B p.a. vs D&A €7B; 258k employees and heavy European plant overcapacity against Chinese imports and EU CO2 mandates.
- **No switching costs, no network effects, thin intangibles** in a globally oversupplied industry with 100+ competitors.

The only genuine assets are brand equity in Jeep/Ram/Dodge and a strong LatAm (Brazil) franchise. Those are real but narrow, and Jeep's US share has been eroding under self-inflicted pricing and inventory errors. Over five years the moat has clearly **narrowed**, not widened.

- **Earnings quality is dreadful**: revenue predictability failed by 20%; FCF conversion is negative; FY2025 carried ~€22.2B of non-cash charges (EV programme cancellations, goodwill/intangibles written from €54.4B to €44.9B).
- **Balance sheet has deteriorated sharply**: net cash of €14B (2023) became net debt of €27.0B (Q3-26), including €5B of hybrid bonds issued in 2026. Debt €56B against €29.7B cash; current ratio 1.02; goodwill+intangibles €46B against total equity €61.8B — **tangible equity only €15.7B (≈€5.4/share)**, and another impairment is entirely plausible.
- The headline "16.4% dividend yield" in the data feed is **stale and misleading — the FY2025 dividend was €0.77 and management has stated there will be no dividend for 2026.** Treat the yield as 0%.
- Silver lining: Q3-2026 revenue +1.0% YoY with break-even AOI (€256M vs €90M), and liquidity of ~€30B means no imminent solvency crisis.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

A trailing-FCF DCF is meaningless here (trailing FCF is −€16B); I therefore triangulated on **normalized mid-cycle earnings** and **asset value**.

- **Cyclically-adjusted earnings power:** revenue €155–160B at a realistic 3.5–5% AOI margin (vs 12–13% in 2023, ~5.5% in 2024) = €5.5–8.0B AOI; after €1.8B interest and 25% tax → **€2.7–4.5B normalized net income**, i.e. €0.95–1.55/share.
- **Scenario values (equity):** bull recovery (5% margin, 9x) ≈ **€13.8/sh**; base case (3.5% margin, 7x) ≈ **€6.6/sh ($7.7)**; bear (structure loses money, further charges/dilution) ≈ **€3.5/sh**.
- Probability-weighted (25/50/25) ≈ **€7.6/share ≈ $8.9** — roughly 85% above the current price.
- **Asset backstop:** 0.19x book, 0.25x tangible book ($6.33/sh), EV/sales 0.24x. Cheap on any static measure.
- **But on current earnings it is not cheap:** 9M-2026 net income of €186M annualises to ~€250M → ~45x P/E; FY2026 consensus EPS ~€0.30 → ~13x. The "cheapness" exists only if you underwrite a full margin recovery that has not yet appeared in the numbers (Q3 AOI margin: 0.7%).

**Conclusion: the price implies roughly a 45–50% chance the turnaround fails.** That is a wide, not a comfortable, discount.

### 4. Management Quality: **2 / 10**

- **Governance churn:** CEO Carlos Tavares was forced out in Dec-2024 after a board revolt; Antonio Filosa (internal, ex-Jeep NA / Fiat Brazil) took over with a new CFO. Strategy has been reset twice in 18 months.
- **Capital allocation record is poor and value-destructive:** ~€5.4B of buybacks executed in 2023–24 at €20–25/share versus $4.72 today; €8.9B of dividends paid in 2023–24 into a collapsing earnings trend; then a €22B write-off, dividend eliminated (€0), and €5B of hybrid issuance — a quasi-equity instrument that ranks ahead of shareholders. That is the opposite of Buffett's "buy back stock only below intrinsic value."
- **Honesty is adequate, discipline is not.** The Feb-2026 "reset" was unusually candid about EV overcapacity, and 2026 guidance has been reaffirmed twice. But the 14-brand portfolio (a legacy of empire-building) remains unaddressed, and Exor (~14%) plus French/Italian political interests complicate rational restructuring.
- No meaningful insider buying to signal conviction.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

Stellantis fails the two tests that matter most in this framework: **it has no durable moat, and its returns on capital are below cost of capital and wildly unpredictable.** A business whose operating margin swings from +12.8% to −2.7% in twenty-four months cannot be valued with confidence, and when the intrinsic-value range is €3.5 to €13.8 per share, no discount to a point estimate constitutes a genuine margin of safety.

The stock is optically a deep-value asset (0.19x book, 0.24x EV/sales) and a probability-weighted recovery case does imply ~$8–9/share. But that case requires simultaneously fixing: US tariffs on Mexican/Canadian-built Rams and Jeeps, European overcapacity, Chinese import competition, EV cost gaps, pension deficits, and brand sprawl — with an unproven CEO, a leveraged balance sheet, and negative free cash flow. That is a 7-foot bar. The market is pricing a real risk of *permanent* capital impairment (further impairments, hybrid/equity dilution), not an irrational discount.

**What would change my mind (revisit triggers):** two to three consecutive quarters of AOI margin ≥4%, positive free cash flow, net debt falling below €10B, no further exceptional charges, and a credible plan to rationalise the brand portfolio. Until then this is a trading vehicle for distressed/special-situation investors, not an owner's business.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis
1. **Tariffs and trade policy** — heavy US exposure from Mexican/Canadian assembly; a shift in tariff regimes can erase the NA profit pool.
2. **Further impairments** — €46B of goodwill/intangibles vs €15.7B tangible equity; another write-down materially erodes book value.
3. **Balance-sheet leverage** — €27B net debt plus underfunded pensions (US/UK) with negative FCF; €5B hybrids already signal rating defence. Equity issuance risk is real.
4. **Structural EV/CO2 cost disadvantage** in Europe vs Chinese imports and vs Toyota/Hyundai cost bases.
5. **Demand cyclicality** — a US or European recession hits a 0.7%-margin business with brutal operating leverage.
6. **Governance/execution** — repeated strategy resets, board interference, and unresolved brand overcapacity; turnaround timelines have already slipped once ("cash flow positive by 2027" is a promise, not a result).
7. **Data caveats:** the vendor's live quote ($4.72) is below the last daily bar in the price feed ($7.73, 20-Feb-2026) and the reported "16.35% yield" is stale post-suspension — verify both before acting.
