# Macroeconomic Assessment — Stellantis N.V. (STLA)

**Data as of:** 3 November 2026 (FRED series through 30 Oct / 2 Nov 2026; Treasury curve 3 Nov 2026)

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Risk-off / late-cycle re-acceleration — the most hostile regime for a levered cyclical automaker.**

The US economy is not slowing into recession; it is *re-accelerating into an inflation problem*. Real GDP grew **+3.4% annualized in Q3 2026** (24,611 vs 24,408 chained $bn), unemployment has *fallen* to **4.2%** from 4.5% a year ago, and CPI has inflected from a **2.4% trough in February 2026 to 3.72% in September** — with the last three months annualizing at **~4.6%**. The Fed responded by ending its easing cycle and **hiking 25bp in September and again in October to 3.88%**. The real fed funds rate is now just **+16bp** — effectively zero — which is why the market is not buying the pause: the **2Y at 4.56–4.74% sits 68–86bp *above* the funds rate**, pricing further tightening.

Meanwhile the long end has broken out: **10Y at 5.13%, 30Y at 5.47%**, a **+39bp 2s10s bear steepener** driven by term premium, not growth optimism. This is the worst possible combination for STLA — a discount-rate shock layered on an affordability shock. The tell is the consumer: **University of Michigan sentiment has collapsed to 48.1** (from 61.7 in July 2025), near record lows, while the **48-month new auto loan rate sits at 7.47%** — down from 8.63% in 2024 but now at risk of reversing.

STLA's equity has already priced much of this: **$4.72, down 54% year-on-year**, and **-61% from its December 2025 peak of $12.22**, with a **-24% single-day collapse on 6 February 2026** when the company reported a 2025 net loss, **suspended the dividend**, and authorised up to **€5bn of new issuance**. Market cap of **$13.7bn** on ~€150bn of revenue is ~0.08x sales — a distressed multiple.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates — the dominant transmission channel (NEGATIVE).**
STLA is a high-operating-leverage, thin-margin (Q3 AOI margin just **0.7%**) manufacturer with a captive finance arm (Leasys, Stellantis Financial Services). Three distinct hits: (i) **demand** — at 7.47% auto loan rates, monthly payments are the binding constraint on NA volume, and the Fed's renewed hiking biases this back toward 8%; (ii) **discount rate** — a turnaround equity whose value is back-end loaded (management only guides to positive industrial FCF in **2027**) is maximally sensitive to a 5.13% 10Y; (iii) **refinancing** — after the 2025 net loss and dividend suspension, the board authorised **up to €5bn of issuance**. Issuing into a 5.47% 30Y market is a materially worse outcome than the plan assumed a year ago. This is the single most important macro-company link.

**Inflation — cost push without pricing power (NEGATIVE).**
Q3 2026 results explicitly cited **raw-material inflation** alongside "continued pricing pressure in Europe." STLA is caught in a margin squeeze: input costs rising at ~4% while European pricing is capped by Chinese competition and NA pricing is capped by consumer affordability. Unlike 2021–22, STLA no longer has the pricing power to pass it through — its 0.7% AOI margin proves it.

**Consumer sentiment — near-record lows (STRONGLY NEGATIVE).**
UMich at **48.1** is a level historically associated with recessions. Autos are the most deferrable big-ticket purchase in the CPI basket. This caps the North American recovery that is currently the *only* thing holding the equity up (Europe is still loss-making per H1 2026).

**Currency — a genuine but second-order offset (NET POSITIVE).**
STLA reports in EUR but earns the bulk of profit in USD via North America. **EUR/USD at 1.1163 from 1.1593 in two months** mechanically lifts EUR-translated NA earnings — a real tailwind to reported numbers. The offset: USD strength pressures the Brazilian real and Argentine peso, squeezing STLA's sizeable South American operations, and erodes European export competitiveness.

**Trade / tariffs (NEGATIVE, quantifiable).**
Sell-side work puts the tariff headwind at **~€578m in 2026 and ~€1.07bn in 2027** (~19–21% of the relevant base), with a live threat of a **25% US tariff on EU-built vehicles** (May 2026). STLA's mitigation is localisation, which is capex it can least afford at 0.7% margins.

**Supply side (NEGATIVE).**
A **chip shortage is disrupting Fiat output in Italy and may run through December 2026** — a volume headwind arriving precisely when the company needs every unit of production to hit reaffirmed guidance.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

This is close to a worst-case macro configuration for STLA specifically. Re-accelerating inflation has forced the Fed back into hikes, pushing the 2Y to 4.56–4.74% and the 10Y through 5% — simultaneously raising the company's cost of capital, its refinancing cost on a newly authorised €5bn programme, and the monthly payment on its customers' auto loans. Consumer sentiment at 48.1 removes the demand cushion. Tariffs and raw-material inflation compress a margin already at 0.7%. The only supportive variables — solid GDP, 4.2% unemployment, and a stronger dollar — are second-order relative to the rate and sentiment shock, and the dollar benefit is a translation artefact rather than an economic gain.

The equity's 54% decline is a rational repricing of this regime, not an anomaly. **Macro is a headwind, not a tailwind, for the turnaround thesis.**

---

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise — **Bear**

**"The Fed has to hike into a consumer that is already broken."**

The market prices roughly three more hikes (2Y at 4.56% vs funds at 3.88%). The surprise is that this is *not enough*: if the 3-month annualized CPI of ~4.6% persists — plausibly, given tariff pass-through and energy — the Fed is forced to 4.5%+ by mid-2027, the **10Y breaks 5.50%** and the 30Y through 5.75%. Auto loan rates return above 8%, and US SAAR rolls over in H1 2027 *precisely as STLA's North American recovery is the sole support for the equity*.

The compounding mechanism is the balance sheet: STLA must raise up to **€5bn** into that market while its dividend is suspended and Europe is still loss-making. A capital raise at a 5.5%+ long-bond yield, into a rolling-over SAAR, with a 25% EU tariff landing, would force the equity toward **0.05x sales (~$3.00)** — a further ~35% downside. Note the asymmetry: the bull case (inflation proves tariff-driven and transient, Fed pauses, 10Y back to 4.3%, sentiment rebounds, cost cuts deliver 2027 FCF) is worth perhaps +50–60%, but it requires *four* independent variables to break favourably, whereas the bear requires only that the current inflation trend simply continues.

**Watch:** monthly CPI prints (any 0.5%+ MoM core), the 10Y's hold of 5.00%, the 2s10s slope, UMich, and the monthly auto loan rate series — a move back above 7.75% would be the trigger.
