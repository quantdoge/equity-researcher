---

### 1. Growth Story

Stellantis is **not a disruptive innovator — it is the incumbent being disrupted**, and the bill for a decade of underinvestment in electrification arrived in FY2025: a €20B EV writedown produced the company's first annual net loss since formation (−€22.4B), negative gross profit, and a suspended dividend. The bull case is not TAM capture — global auto is a mature ~$4–5T market where STLA holds roughly 3% and is *losing* share. The case is a **margin-recovery / product-cycle turnaround**: a $13B US investment program and 11 all-new North American vehicles (7 below $40k, two below $30k) aimed at rebuilding Jeep and Ram profitability by 2027–2028. That is an event-driven deep-value thesis, not a compounding-growth thesis.

**The critical caveat on the headline 6.0% five-year revenue CAGR:** it is a merger artifact. Stellantis was formed in January 2021, so the 2020 base (€86.7B) is legacy-FCA-only; the +72% "growth" in 2021 is consolidation, not organic. Strip it out and organic revenue has gone **+20.2% → +5.5% → −17.2% → −2.2%** — a decelerating then contracting trajectory. Growth quality is poor on every axis: negative organic growth, collapsing gross margin (−2,160bps in 24 months), deeply negative Rule of 40, and €16.2B of cash burn against €10.7B of capex that is currently earning no return.

**Valuation:** P/B 0.25x, EV/2026E EBITDA ~2.8x, EV/2030E EBITDA ~1.6x, P/E 2.2x on 2030 consensus. Against TSLA at 17.2x book and 118x EV/EBITDA, the multiple gap is total — the market assigns essentially zero option value to the recovery plan. *(Toyota comparables were returned with currency-inconsistent book/EV figures and are unusable — noted as a data gap.)*

### 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

There is **no penetration runway here** — this is a share-defense story, and Stellantis is on the wrong side of the trade. Global light-vehicle TAM is large but low-growth (~2–3%), and the growth pockets within it (China EV, affordable EV) are precisely where STLA has no product, no cost position, and no brand. BYD and Chinese OEMs are compressing the affordable-EV segment in Europe and LatAm — Stellantis' two profit pools outside North America — while Tesla and Korean OEMs take the premium end. Its genuine, defensible asset is **Ram/Jeep in North America**, a high-margin full-size truck and SUV niche where it still has pricing power. The entire investment case reduces to: can management protect that niche while the rest of the portfolio shrinks? Serviceable addressable market is *not* expanding for this company.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **FY2026 results — 25 Feb 2027.** Consensus EPS $0.11, revenue $46.3B. The single most important datapoint: does H2 2026 confirm the Q3 inflection (net loss narrowed 90% YoY to €0.5B, revenue beat at €49.7B vs €47.0B)?
- **2027 Ram launch wave** — midsize Ram pickup, Ram 1500 Rumble Bee, plus gasoline Jeep Recon variant. First real test of whether the $13B US plan converts to volume and mix.
- **Belvidere plant reopening** (Jeep Cherokee expansion) — a visible execution milestone on the US manufacturing reset, and a partial tariff hedge.
- **Tariff mitigation** — ~$1.7B of 2026 tariff cost is the swing factor between breakeven and another loss year; any US-Mexico/Canada trade relief is a direct EPS catalyst.
- **Balance sheet actions** — €5B hybrid bond issuance completed; watch for further liquidity raises, asset sales (Comau/Leasys-style), or a dividend reinstatement signal for FY2027.

### 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Google Trends (US, "Stellantis"):** interest spiked to 57 during the February 2026 writedown, then **decayed to 17–18 by October 2026 — the lowest reading in the entire three-year series.** Consumer attention to the corporate brand is fading, consistent with share loss rather than a demand recovery. Directionally bearish.
- **Earnings surprise history:** 50% beat rate over six quarters, **average surprise −23.4%**, and three of the last four quarters were misses (−104%, −62%, −66%). Guidance has been persistently unreliable — a serial over-promiser. Only Q1 2026 beat (+63%).
- **Analyst revision momentum:** the tool flags "upgrade" (+15.4%), but this **rests on a single analyst's 2030 EPS estimate** ($1.85 → $2.14). That is statistical noise, not a signal. The high-quality signal is the opposite: near-term estimates have been slashed repeatedly.

Targets have fallen ~50–70% in nine months and two bulge-bracket banks moved to Underweight. The sell side is capitulating, not upgrading.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **SELL**

Judged against a growth mandate, STLA fails on every primary criterion: negative organic revenue growth, negative gross margin, negative free cash flow, a Rule of 40 score of −12.7, no innovation moat, and a TAM it is ceding to Chinese and Tesla-led disruption. The one positive signal — a 2.2x P/E on 2030 consensus — requires operating margins to swing **1,900bps from −14.5% to roughly +7%** in four years, in a business that just burned €16B of cash and suspended its dividend. Consensus itself only models a **2.6% revenue CAGR to 2030**; the entire recovery is margin, i.e., pure execution, with zero growth tailwind to cushion error.

The 0.25x P/B is real optionality and I do not dismiss it — but optionality on a serial disappointer, in a structurally disadvantaged cost position, with tariffs and Chinese competition intensifying, is exactly the profile of a value trap. **A growth investor should not own this at any multiple until revenue inflects positive and gross margin sustainably clears ~10%.** That is a documented, observable trigger to revisit — likely 12–18 months out.

---

### 7. Key Risks

- **Execution risk (primary):** 11 launches in ~24 months, a plant reopening, and a dealer/inventory reset simultaneously — Stellantis' recent track record on guidance is three misses in four quarters. Launch slippage breaks the 2027–28 EPS bridge.
- **Competitive threat:** Chinese OEMs (BYD et al.) compressing the European and LatAm affordable segments — STLA's non-NA profit pools — faster than the US truck franchise can offset.
- **Balance sheet:** €16.2B FCF burn plus €10.7B capex; already issued €5B hybrid bonds. A second consecutive loss year likely forces dilutive or expensive capital action.
- **Tariff/regulatory:** ~$1.7B 2026 tariff drag with limited pricing pass-through in the compact/midsize segments it must win.
- **Bear case for my Sell (the risk of being wrong):** expectations are so washed out that a single credible margin beat could re-rate the stock 40–60% in days. Positioning is crowded short after two Underweight downgrades. This is a **risk-management Sell, not a high-conviction short.**
- **Data gaps:** peer multiples for TM were currency-inconsistent and unusable; 2026–2030 consensus EPS rests on 1–5 analysts, so the forward curve carries wide error bands.
