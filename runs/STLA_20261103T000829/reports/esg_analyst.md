### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **HIGH**
- **Carbon intensity & transition risk:** Stellantis has *reversed* its transition: cancelled the Ram 1500 BEV, returned the HEMI V-8, and **revised its Carbon Net Zero target from 2038 to 2050** (2025 Climate Policy Report). Scope 1+2 down 26% vs 2021 — real but now driven by volume declines as much as decarbonisation.
- **Near-term compliance is manageable, not safe:** the EU's 2025-27 three-year averaging window plus its 2025 Tesla CO₂ pool entry bought time; **it exited the Tesla pool for 2026** (Reuters, 4 Mar 2026), removing its compliance buffer. Industry-wide, the pool is ~1–4 g/km shy of the 93 g/km target (ICCT/Alinnea) — a Stellantis miss implies low-hundreds-of-€m fines, not existential ones.
- **Policy tailwind ≠ lower risk:** the EU dropped the 2035 100% ban to −90% with e-fuel credits (Dec 2025) and US automakers won a 2-year EPA enforcement delay (Jun 2026). Both defer, and politically re-price, rather than remove, the 2030 (49.5 g/km) step-down — leaving a *convex* fine risk into 2030 for a V8-heavy mix.
- **Materialised environmental loss:** the ACC gigafactory cancellations and EV write-downs are the largest single realisation of climate-transition risk in the sector — but from **over**-commitment, not under-commitment.
- Disclosure quality weak: **no SBTi commitment, no CDP disclosure** (KnowESG, verified Nov 2026) — a genuine data-quality red flag for ESG-screened mandates.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**
- **Labour:** Unifor (9,000+ Canadian members) voted **97.3% for strike action** (Oct 2026) after Stellantis **reneged on a 2025 Brampton/Jeep investment commitment** and explored selling the idle plant to a defence contractor. UAW has filed NLRB unfair-labour-practice charges and is running global campaigns against management. Italian 2026 vehicle output now forecast ~10% lower (FIM-Cisl); Mirafiori carries long-running solidarity contracts.
- **Product safety:** >1.8m US recalls in 2026 — 1,076,999 vehicles (June, power-steering pump wiring overheating/fire risk, remedy partly OTA), ~230k Ram 1500 + rearview camera (Sept), 265,512 ProMaster vans (Nov). Estimated aggregate cost $300–600m (warranty/recall accrual, partly OTA-mitigated); reputational cost on the two profit-pool brands (Ram/Jeep) is larger.
- **Community/social licence:** plant closures and brand cull (15→4) across Italy, France and Canada create political friction with host governments — a live risk to future subsidies and EU/US industrial-policy support.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10**
- **Control structure:** Exor ~14% + Peugeot family + French state, amplified by **loyalty double-voting rights** — control is concentrated well beyond economic ownership.
- **Board/leadership churn & duality-adjacent structure:** John Elkann is **executive** chairman and was re-elected as an executive director at the 2026 AGM (two-year terms); Antonio Filosa became CEO only in July 2025 after Tavares' abrupt ouster in Dec 2024 — three strategy regimes in 24 months.
- **Compensation misalignment:** 2024 pay of €23.1m ($26.2m) to a departed CEO approved 66/33; the **2025 remuneration report drew more than one-third opposition excluding Exor's stake** (Glass Lewis) — a minority-shareholder revolt signal.
- **Accountability deficit:** the Feb 2026 reset destroyed ~€20bn+ of equity yet produced no visible clawback or board-level consequence; guidance was cut repeatedly through 2024-25.
- **Positives:** majority-independent board on paper, annual say-on-pay, single class of shares listed (no poison pill), Dutch statutory regime. Score: 4/10 — not predatory, but structurally insulated and with a poor capital-allocation and succession record.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure
- **EU:** CSRD reporting obligation (large EU-listed issuer) — first-wave reporting with Omnibus-phase delays; EU CO₂ reg. 2019/631 as amended (93.6 g/km 2025-29; 49.5 g/km 2030; −90% 2035 incl. e-fuel credits); EU Battery Regulation (passport, recycled content from 2027 — constrained by cancelled domestic cell capacity); CSDDD supply-chain due diligence; CBAM on steel/aluminium inputs (second-order).
- **US:** EPA GHG/multi-pollutant rule under rewrite with a 2-year enforcement delay; NHTSA/CAFE; CARB harmonisation (Stellantis signed a California emissions compromise in 2024 that is now in tension with federal deregulation — regulatory arbitrage risk either way); IRA credit repeal = direct EV demand/profitability hit.
- **Other:** Italian/French/Canadian political exposure around plant closures; French criminal emissions proceedings.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD (with ESG Risk characteristics)**
Not a "critical" ESG risk on any single dimension, but a laggard on the factors that actually matter for an OEM: **execution credibility (G), labour relations (S), and transition-strategy coherence (E)**. Weak disclosure (no SBTi, no CDP) plus a 2038→2050 net-zero retreat support the laggard classification. Environmental risk is *high but no longer rising* — the write-downs have already cleared a chunk of stranded-asset risk.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE (for the thesis)**
Three transmission channels:
1. **Cost of capital:** ESG-screened European and Japanese mandates face exclusion/engagement escalation on a laggard with a failed say-on-pay and a contested labour record; a structural governance discount on a €13.7bn market cap is material relative to an already-thin equity base.
2. **Cash-flow fragility:** strike risk at Canadian/Italian plants, $300–600m of recall accruals, and creeping 2030 EU CO₂ exposure all hit a company that has guided to positive industrial FCF only in **2027**.
3. **Cheap ≠ de-risked:** at 4.7x forward-type levels the stock discounts a lot, and the EU 2035 softening plus US deregulation are genuine near-term tailwinds for an ICE/hybrid-heavy mix. But the ESG signal here is *governance*, and the same board that destroyed ~€20bn of EV capital on a mis-forecast adoption curve is now making a second, larger bet on V8s and hybrids while cutting the dividend to zero and fighting its unions. **ESG does not obstruct the recovery trade — it argues the recovery deserves a higher discount rate and a smaller position size.** The specific triggers we would monitor before upgrading: a ratified Unifor/UAW settlement with a stated Brampton investment, a reaffirmed (not further weakened) decarbonisation target with SBTi validation, and a board/remuneration reset that re-links pay to ROIC rather than volume.

**Positive ESG optionality (under-appreciated):** the Wayve/Uber L4 robotaxi platform deal and Stellantis' L4-ready architecture are genuine low-carbon-mobility assets, and its micro-mobility entry (Fiat Topolino) plus commercial-vehicle leadership (Ram ProMaster, LCVs) give it a defensible compliance path if 2030 targets bite.
