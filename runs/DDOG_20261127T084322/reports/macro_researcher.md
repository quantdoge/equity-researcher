# Macroeconomic Assessment — Datadog, Inc. (DDOG)
**As of 27 November 2026 | Price $158.25 | Market cap $57.1B | Beta 1.51**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-Off / Regime Change**

The US economy is in a **re-tightening cycle inside a late-cycle boom** — the most hostile combination for long-duration growth equities. The Fed cut from 4.33% (Jan 2025) to a 3.63% trough by mid-2026, but **inflation re-accelerated** and the easing cycle has reversed: the effective funds rate has ticked back up to 3.88% (Oct 2026), and the market now prices a **further +25bp hike at the December FOMC to 4.00–4.25%** (Reuters poll, 10 Nov 2026; J.P. Morgan likewise expects a December hike).

The bond market has already moved. The 10Y has risen from 4.48% (early July) to **5.13%**, and the curve has **bear-steepened** (2s10s +39bp), which is a term-premium/fiscal-risk signal, not a growth signal. Critically, **5y5y forward inflation is *falling*** (2.36% → 2.11% since 1 Oct) while the **10Y TIPS real yield has jumped ~68bp** (2.24% July → 2.92% mid-Nov). This is a pure **real discount-rate shock** — the worst possible input for an asset whose value sits almost entirely in terminal cash flows.

That shock detonated in equities in mid-November: the **Nasdaq fell 5.28% on 19 Nov**, the S&P 500 entered correction and the S&P lost 8.6% in the week to 20 Nov, as an AI-led rout (Nvidia, Anthropic's post-IPO slide) spread. VIX spiked to **35.9** on 20 Nov (26.4 now) versus 14.8 in late September. Credit is following: **HY OAS has widened ~106bp from 2.66% (21 Sep) to 3.72%** — still tight in absolute terms, but the fastest tightening in credit conditions since this cycle began.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates — strongly NEGATIVE (dominant driver).** DDOG is a textbook long-duration asset: 35% revenue growth, thin GAAP margins (Q3 GAAP op income $75M = 6.3% of $1.197B revenue), and ~58x FY26E non-GAAP EPS ($2.74) versus ~11.4x FY26E revenue (~$5.03B). With the 10Y real yield up ~68bp, a 15–25% pure duration de-rating is mechanically defensible. **This is the single largest cause of the stock's 46% decline from its 9 Oct high of $293 to $158** — not fundamentals, which *accelerated* (Q3 revenue +35% YoY, up from +32% in Q1; $100k+ ARR customers up to ~4,860).

**Fed policy path — NEGATIVE.** A December hike would push the front end above the current 3.88% and validate "higher-for-longer." Every hawkish surprise extends the payback period on DDOG's growth.

**Growth — MIXED / marginally positive.** 4.3% GDP and 4.1% unemployment support enterprise IT budgets and cloud consumption (DDOG's usage-based model is levered to customer cloud spend). But above-trend growth is precisely what is forcing the Fed to hike — DDOG is a beneficiary of the economy that is causing its own valuation problem.

**Credit conditions — NEGATIVE (leading indicator).** DDOG carries no meaningful refinancing risk (net cash, convertible notes only), so it is insulated from the credit channel *directly*. But the +106bp HY widening is an early warning for **customer IT budget scrutiny**, and DDOG's usage-based model means consumption softness transmits to revenue within a quarter or two.

**Currency — mildly NEGATIVE, but smaller than it looks.** Non-North America was **29% of FY2025 revenue**, and international grew only ~24% in Q1 2026 versus 32% total — so international is already a *drag* on the blended growth rate. Importantly, DDOG's **sales contracts are predominantly USD-denominated**, so transaction/translation exposure is limited. The +4.8% USD rally therefore hits via **relative pricing competitiveness** (USD pricing becomes more expensive for foreign buyers, further pressuring that 24% growth) rather than via an FX translation line. This is a pricing headwind, not a P&L translation shock.

**Input inflation — NEGATIVE.** DDOG's cost base is people plus hyperscaler compute. 4.1% unemployment keeps wage inflation sticky, and AI-driven demand is raising cloud compute costs — both pressure gross and operating margins precisely when the market is no longer paying for growth.

**Tariffs — NEUTRAL.** Software has no direct goods exposure; only second-order exposure via customer verticals.

**AI — TWO-SIDED, currently net negative in sentiment.** Fundamentally a tailwind (management explicitly cites AI driving cloud demand; observability of AI workloads; 35% growth). But the November rout was explicitly an **AI-bubble de-rating**, and AI-native observability competitors threaten usage-based pricing deflation over time. DDOG is caught in the crossfire of a thematic repricing it did not cause.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The macro regime is aligned against this stock on every axis that matters: a Fed that is *hiking* rather than cutting, real yields rising ~68bp, term premium expanding, credit spreads widening at the fastest pace of the cycle, an AI-thematic de-rating, a strong dollar pressuring the international segment, and a VIX at 26. The only genuine macro offset — 4.3% GDP and a tight labour market supporting cloud consumption — is also the cause of the tightening, making it self-negating.

**Important qualification:** much of this is now *priced*. At ~11.4x FY26E revenue for a 35%-growth asset, DDOG trades at a steep discount to its own history (15–25x). The macro risk is no longer "the multiple is too high" — it is "the multiple can compress further if real yields keep rising." Macro is the swing factor for the next 12 months, not company execution.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BULL (asymmetric)**

Consensus is now unambiguously bearish: a December hike is priced, AI is in retreat, and the stock is down 46% in six weeks. The non-consensus surprise is a **fast disinflationary rollover**. Note the tell: **5y5y forward inflation has already fallen 25bp to 2.11% while real yields rose** — the bond market believes the Fed wins on inflation. If the December and January CPI prints roll back toward 2.5% (from 3.82%), the Fed skips the hike, and the "term premium" part of the 10Y move retraces toward 4.70%, real yields would fall ~50bp. On a long-duration asset already de-rated to 11.4x revenue with *accelerating* 35% growth, that combination would produce a violent re-rating — plausibly +40–60% to retest the $250–290 range — with no change to the fundamental story whatsoever.

The mirror-image bear trigger, for risk control: a December hike plus hawkish 2027 dots and the 10Y breaking 5.50% would compress DDOG toward 8x revenue (~$110), roughly another −30%. **The distribution is wide, and it is driven almost entirely by the 10Y real yield, not by Datadog.**
