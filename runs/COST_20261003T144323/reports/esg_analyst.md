### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **Medium**

- **Emissions & targets:** Costco has SBTi-validated near-term targets — ~39% cumulative Scope 1+2 CO₂e reduction by 2030 (2021 base) — and a net-zero-by-2050 pledge under the SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard; it committed to full **Scope 3** value-chain targets following an activist (Trillium/Green Century) campaign. Its 2025 Sustainability Report (published mid-2025) discloses CDP climate and forest data.
- **Execution gap:** Third-party assessments (e.g., The Commons) flag that progress is **lagging target pace**; climate goals are corporate-disclosed rather than independently audited. Energy efficiency and on-site solar are the main levers; no carbon-tax or stranded-asset exposure of note.
- **Deforestation:** Responding to shareholder proposals, Costco committed to a **deforestation assessment + action plan by end-2025**, with new 2025 commitments to reduce deforestation risk in its **avocado** supply chain (As You Sow commended, Dec 2025). This is a frontier-risk area (EUDR timing) rather than a realised cost.
- **Physical climate risk:** Moderate — warehouses are low-value, replaceable assets; supply-chain heat/water stress in produce sourcing is the primary physical channel.
- **Net assessment:** No environmental fines, spills, or controversies on record. Risk is operational and reputational (target-pace scrutiny) rather than regulatory-cost driven today. **Medium** — but trending positive on disclosure, with execution risk.

---

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **Medium**

- **Labour — best-in-class (defensive asset):** Jan–Feb 2025 Costco signed a new Teamsters contract **avoiding a strike**, with entry wage rising to **$20/hr** and top-of-scale above **$30/hr** (largest raises in contract history). ~18,000 unionised workers (~5% of workforce); non-union wages broadly mirrored, ~90%+ of US employees covered by health benefits. This underpins Costco's famously low turnover and high store-level productivity — a **social factor that is also a cash-flow positive**.
- **DEI stance — distinctive:** In Jan 2025, >**98% of shareholders voted down** an anti-DEI proposal (National Center for Public Policy Research); Costco has **not** rolled back diversity programmes (rebranded "People and Communities"), making it an outlier vs. US retail peers under political pressure. This carries both reputational upside (talent/consumer trust) and litigation/activist headline risk.
- **Product safety — the weak spot:** **Jan 2025 Kirkland Signature Organic Pasture-Raised Egg recall** (10,800 units, salmonella, supplier Handsome Brook Farms) was escalated by the FDA to **Class I** (highest severity). Precedent: 2016 rotisserie chicken-salad salmonella outbreak (19 illnesses) and Foster Farms rotisserie chicken recall. Volumes of fresh food make single-supplier contamination events an ongoing, low-probability/high-visibility recurring risk.
- **Supply chain labour:** Kirkland Signature sourcing from emerging markets; exposure to US UFLPA (Xinjiang) screening and EU CSDDD-adjacent diligence as reporting ramps. No substantiated forced-labour controversy found, but disclosure is thin relative to peers.
- **Net assessment:** **Medium** — strongest labour profile in US retail, offset by recurring food-safety events and thinner supply-chain labour disclosure.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **8 / 10**

- **No CEO/Chair duality:** Ron Vachris (CEO/President/Director) reports to a separate, non-executive **Chairman Hamilton E. James** — a structural positive.
- **Independent board:** ~12 directors, large majority independent; 2025 shareholder proposals seeking an independent-chair policy were opposed by management (a minor negative but common).
- **Single-class stock:** No dual-class wedge; standard one-share-one-vote; no poison pill.
- **Comp alignment:** CEO pay is modest vs. mega-cap peers, heavily equity-based, and tied to long-term operating metrics — strong alignment.
- **Shareholder responsiveness:** High — costco responded to climate/deforestation proposals with real commitments, and defended DEI against activist pressure (98% vote support).
- **Deductions:** (i) 10-K/Proxy risk-factor disclosure not independently verifiable here (SEC EDGAR filing retrieval failed — flagged gap); (ii) board diversity data not retrievable from primary filings; (iii) chairman-independence proposal resistance.
- **Score: 8/10 — strong, shareholder-friendly governance with minor transparency caveats.**

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **Leader** *(retail-sector) with execution caveats*

Costco sits at the favourable end of US discount retail: best-in-class labour, independently chaired board, SBTi-validated climate targets, defensive stance on DEI, and responsive shareholder engagement. Weakness is concentrated in **product-safety event frequency** and **Scope 3/deforestation execution pace**, neither of which is currently value-destructive.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **Positive**

ESG factors here are **net supportive of the long thesis** rather than a risk overlay:

1. **Labour premium is a moat, not a cost drag** — wage leadership drives the membership-renewal/employee-retention flywheel; ESG's "S" supports COST's gross-margin durability.
2. **Governance quality (8/10) lowers governance risk premium** and cost of capital.
3. **Regulatory tail exposure (EUDR, CSRD) is manageable and disclosed in advance**; no carbon-pricing or stranded-asset channel of consequence.
4. **Watch items:** (i) frequency of Class I food recalls → litigation/customer-trust erosion; (ii) Scope 3 target slippage → index-provider/activist pressure; (iii) DEI political/legal backlash spillover (state-level actions against corporate diversity programmes).

MBA-math check: ESG risk does not impede the base-case, and the labour/trust advantages are mispriced as non-financial. **Rating: Leader; implication: Positive.**
