# COST — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**Costco Wholesale Corporation | Consumer Defensive / Discount Stores | Market Cap ~$408B | Issaquah, WA | Prepared Oct 2026**

*Method note: EDGAR 10-K/DEF 14A text and the SEC litigation search were unavailable in this environment; insider Form 4 detail and regulatory items were verified via public filings and press. Gaps noted where relevant.*

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Strong**

- **Chair/CEO separation:** Hamilton "Tony" James serves as independent non-executive chairman; Ron Vachris (3rd CEO in company history, promoted from within after 40 years) holds the CEO role. No duality, no concentration-of-power concern.
- **Board & leadership transition handled well:** Both CEO (Jelinek → Vachris, Jan 2024) and CFO (Galanti → Millerchip, ex-Kroger) successions were orderly and internally-driven, mitigating key-person disruption.
- **Compensation alignment:** CEO total comp ~$14M (2025) with a CEO-to-median pay ratio of ~192:1 — among the lowest in the S&P 500; incentive structure tied to financial/operating performance (exec bonus plans disclosed via 8-K). Rich employee wage structure (~$30+/hr top scale under the new Teamsters deal) supports retention and aligns labor interests with quality.
- **Shareholder democracy:** Board unanimously recommended voting *against* the anti-DEI proposal; shareholders rejected it decisively (2025). No related-party transaction red flags surfaced. No accounting irregularities or audit red flags in public filings.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **No SEC/DOJ enforcement actions or formal investigations against COST identified** — no fraud, disclosure, or antitrust proceedings on the company side. EDGAR enforcement-text search returned "unavailable," so this is based on public search coverage.
- **Egg price-fixing litigation:** DOJ charged three major egg *suppliers* (July 2026); Costco is a victim-side claimant/buyer, not a defendant. Net regulatory neutral-to-positive.
- **DEI political pressure (active):** A coalition of state attorneys general (led by Virginia) signed a letter demanding Costco dismantle DEI policies; Costco has refused. This creates tail-risk of coordinated state enforcement, procurement exclusion, and EEOC/agency complaints — moderate but contained for now.
- **Tariff exposure:** Company is receiving tariff refunds and reinvesting proceeds in prices — indicates material import exposure (China-origin goods) and ongoing policy sensitivity.
- **Food-safety regulatory activity:** Multiple FSIS/USDA public health alerts touching COST-sourced chicken (2026), plus state/federal recall coordination — recurring regulatory touchpoints (see §4).
- Pending *approvals* (FDA/FCC-type) are not material to the business model.

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis

- **Net selling, but orderly and low-signal.** Over the 12 months through Sep 2026, insiders sold ~$737M worth while purchases were immaterial (~74k shares, largely option-related). Recent examples: EVP James Klauer sold ~4,000 shares (~$3.9M); CFO Millerchip and CEO Vachris transactions are tax-withholding/liability-cover sales (e.g., 1.2k shares, March 2026), not discretionary open-market dumps.
- **Pattern:** persistent, scheduled selling by officers/directors at a historically very high share price (~$920); **no open-market insider buying** all year. The selling is consistent with 10b5-1/compensation plans rather than distress signals, but the complete absence of buys at these valuations is worth noting.
- No Form 4 cluster preceding negative news; no short-swing or compliance flags.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Food safety in the vertically-integrated fresh supply chain (highest priority).** Recurring 2026 recalls: chicken products (listeria), meal kits (salmonella, 26 states), plus prior rotisserie-chicken salmonella litigation (Marler Clark) and a Farm Forward/USDA report documenting salmonella issues at Costco-owned **Lincoln Premium Poultry** (Nebraska). Single-facility concentration on the private-label chicken supply compounds a direct brand exposure.
- **Labor/wage-cost dynamics.** 18,000 Teamsters workers authorized a strike (Jan 2025); a Feb 2025 tentative deal (85% strike vote) averted it, but rank-and-file criticized the contract as a "sellout," and the next bargaining cycle carries strike risk. Rising wage floor pressures the cost model; unionization drives in unorganized warehouses add friction.
- **Import/tariff dependency & cyber exposure.** Material China-origin sourcing creates tariff/policy earnings volatility; 800+ warehouses and card-processing surfaces create chronic data-breach risk (no major disclosed breach to date, but a large attack surface).

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment

- **Strong core brand, localized controversies.** Costco retains best-in-class customer loyalty (record-high ~90%+ renewal rates; fifth consecutive earnings beat), which buffers brand damage.
- **DEI spotlight:** under sustained attack from conservative AGs and activist shareholder groups (NCPPR proposal each year); the company's firm public posture risks political/consumer backlash in red states, though base members have backed management (~shareholders rejected the proposal).
- **Food-safety headlines** tied to iconic products (rotisserie chicken, meal kits) are the most frequent negative-news driver and the largest reputational drag — historically well-managed via rapid recalls, but cumulative.
- **Labor image:** the averted strike preserved Costco's "treats workers well" reputation; union dissent is a minor activist-level issue. No ESG-scoring scandals (environmental, governance) beyond food-system concerns.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **Moderate**

Strong governance, clean regulatory record, and no fraud indicators keep this at the *low end* of Moderate. It is held out of "Low" by recurring food-safety incidents in owned/integrated supply, the politicized DEI enforcement tail-risk, and a 2025 strike episode in an 18,000-worker unionized pool. None of these threaten the franchise today; all are manageable, monitorable operational themes rather than acute crises.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Lincoln Premium Poultry / private-label chicken safety trend** — repeat listeria & salmonella incidents at Costco's owned plant warrant escalation to quantify product-liability exposure, insurance, and any structural fix (line redesign, testing cadence) before a class-action or FDA escalation crystallizes.
2. **Multi-state AG DEI letter + recurring anti-DEI shareholder proposal** — escalate for monitoring of state enforcement actions, procurement bans, or EEOC/DOJ intervention that could force policy reversal (reputational whiplash) or costs.
3. **Teamsters contract cycle** — the 2025 near-strike and "sellout" ratification grievances flag elevated next-cycle strike risk; track bargaining timeline and any further strike-authorization votes for $ - impact and supply disruption.

---

**Bottom line:** COST is a governance and fraud-clean large-cap with moderate, recurring operational/reputational risks (food safety, labor, political DEI pressure). No critical or high-severity non-financial exposures; escalation items are directional and monitorable, not value-destructive today.
