# COST — Costco Wholesale Corporation
**Value Investment Memo — [Price: ~$920.65 | Market Cap: ~$409B | FY2026 (ended 8/30/26)]**

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Wide (widening)**

Costco runs the most defensible model in discount retail: a membership tollbooth on top of a scale-advantaged warehouse operation.

- **Recurring membership economics**: Annual membership fee income (~$4.9B+ in FY26, roughly on the order of 13%+ of operating income) is earned *before* merchandising costs and renews at ~90%+. This is the highest-margin, most predictable revenue stream in retail.
- **Cost advantage via scale**: ~$303B annual revenue, 30%+ gross-margin ITMs suppressed to ~10%+; Costco deliberately prices at near-cost to monetize through fees and volume. Its purchasing power with national brands plus a growing Kirkland Signature private label (~30% of sales) compounds the structural cost edge — competitors cannot match buying scale or the treasure-hunt merchandising economics.
- **Switching costs / loyalty**: Physical membership + gas + pharmacy + optical + food court creates a sticky umbrella-of-services relationship. Renewal rates in the high-80s/low-90s % are the single best evidence of customer captivity in retail.
- **Efficient scale**: Club-format density in U.S./Canada/Mexico plus disciplined greenfield expansion (international, China, e-commerce) — no price wars of consequence.
- **Moat trajectory**: Widening over 5 years — margins up (gross 12.15%→12.82%, op 3.43%→3.85%), fee base growing, digital/same-day penetration rising. ROIC ~26% vs. a sub-10% WACC is the quantitative hallmark of a moat.

- **Consistency**: Revenue compounded ~9.1%/yr over three years with zero down years even through disinflation — a predictability very few retailers possess.
- **Cash conversion**: FY26 FCF $9.39B ≈ 102% of net income; five-year FCF CAGR strong. FY25–26 FCF was temporarily depressed by a heavy new-warehouse capex program ($6.4B capex vs. $2.7B D&A) — expansion, not decay.
- **Balance sheet**: Net cash of ~$11.6B (cash $21.3B vs. total debt $8.6B). No goodwill concerns; assets are self-funded warehouses.
- **Deduct one point** for structurally thin operating margin (3.9%) — a modest shock to wages or tariffs hits the bottom line hard in percentage terms, and for the shareholder-friendly habit of paying large special dividends that cap compounded buyback benefit.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **Trading ABOVE intrinsic value; no margin of safety**

- **Price**: $920.65 | **P/E** 44.4x (FY26 EPS $20.78) | **EV/EBITDA** 27.6x | **P/FCF** 43.6x | **FCF yield** 2.3% | **Div yield** 0.6%.
- **Peer context**: Sector/peer median P/E ~23x → COST trades at a ~92% premium to peers (WMT, KO, PG, PM, etc. — though the peer set is imperfect, the premium magnitude is still telling vs. its own history of ~28–35x).
- **DCF (9% WACC, 11.7% growth, 2.5% terminal)**: Intrinsic value ≈ **$296B ≈ $666/share** — i.e., the market price embeds *above-fair-value* expectations even granting 10 years of double-digit FCF growth. Sensitively: to justify $920 you'd need ~44x earnings to persist indefinitely, i.e., perfection priced in.
- **Owner earnings sanity check**: FY26 owner earnings ~$5.5B using full capex — meaningful reinvestment means distributable cash is lower than headline FCF.
- **What the price implies**: The market is paying for continued double-digit comp/membership growth *and* no multiple compression. There is no cushion for a consumer slowdown, tariff shock, or wage reset.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **High (8/10)**

- **CEO Ron Vachris**: 40+ year Costco insider, ex-COO; succession from Craig Jelinek has been seamless. Founder-instilled culture (Jim Sinegal's "lower prices, pay people well") intact.
- **Capital allocation discipline**: No empire-building M&A (goodwill negligible); buybacks modest and steady (~$0.7–0.9B/yr, anti-dilutive); dividends raised consistently ($1.02→$1.16→$1.30→$1.47/yr) plus a $15 special in Dec 2023. Honest, conservative guidance style.
- **Minor criticism**: Capex has run ahead of D&A for growth — fine at 26% ROIC, but the payout policy (special dividends) occasionally prioritizes optics over maximum compounding.

## 5. Investment Verdict — **HOLD**

This is a **wonderful business at an unwonderful price**. Everything Buffett looks for is present — wide and widening moat, 26% ROIC, net cash, honest insider management, predictable growth. But the entry math fails the margin-of-safety test: at 44x earnings / 2.3% FCF yield, I am being asked to pay a premium to my own DCF intrinsic value (~$666) with zero downside cushion. **A business trading above fair value is not a buy.** Existing holders should hold and let quality compound; new capital should wait for a meaningful pullback (P/E into the low-30s, price roughly $625–$725) before establishing a position.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Valuation/multiple risk (primary)**: 44x earnings is a long-duration asset; modest comp-sales deceleration or a rates-driven derating could mean 30–40% drawdown independent of fundamentals.
2. **Membership fee sensitivity**: Renewal strength is the model's keel; a severe consumer recession or erosion of the value halo would crack the tollbooth.
3. **Demographic/cultural shift**: Increasingly digital/urban consumption — warehouse trips are a habit that must be refreshed each generation.
4. **Tariff & supply-chain shocks**: Heavy imported general merchandise; tariffs hit the 90%+ margin *before* operating costs at paper-thin operating margins.
5. **Wage inflation**: Costco's generosity on wages is a feature for culture, a headwind for 3.9% operating margins in tight labor markets.
6. **Competition**: Amazon, Walmart+ and club-format entrants (BJ's, Sam's Club) — none currently threatening, but the moat is maintained, not static.
7. **International execution**: China/Europe expansion carries FX, regulatory, and format-fit risk disproportionate to current revenue contribution.
