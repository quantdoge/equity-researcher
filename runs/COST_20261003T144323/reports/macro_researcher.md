# MACROECONOMIC ASSESSMENT — Costco Wholesale (COST)
**Sector:** Consumer Defensive / Discount Stores | **Analyst role:** Macroeconomy Research | **Data as of:** early Oct 2026

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — easing at the front end, repricing at the long end.**

The Fed has been in an easing cycle (effective funds rate 3.75% in Sep-2026, down ~60 bps from the 4.33% plateau held through mid-2025 and ~110 bps below the Oct-2024 peak of 4.83%). But this is a *split* rate regime, not a conventional ease: the 10-year Treasury has **risen steadily through 2026** — 4.26% in April → ~4.5% in June → ~4.7% in Aug → **4.99% on Oct 3 (5.24–5.29% intraday at end of Sept)** — and the 30Y sits at 5.37%. The curve is steeply positive (2s10s +34 bps, not inverted), driven by term-premium repricing and Treasury supply rather than recession expectations. Real growth reaccelerated to ~2.2% annualized in Q2-2026 after a near-flat Q4-2025 (0.2%), unemployment is a comfortable 4.2%, but CPI is **sticky at ~3.0–3.4%** — above target, capping how far the Fed can ease. The USD broad index (~120.3) is at cyclical highs. For a defensive, membership-funded, import-heavy retailer this configuration reads **operationally favourable, valuation-unfavourable**: the consumer trading-down impulse is intact, but the 5% long end has already compressed multiples (COST: $920.65, −14% from the Feb-2025 peak of ~$1,069 despite solid comps).

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

- **Consumer inflation & trading-down — POSITIVE.** Costco is the beneficiary of inflation-distressed budgets: membership value-prop strengthens exactly when CPI sits at 3%+ and confidence is wobbling (see Sep-30-2026 consumer-confidence news). Elevated food inflation plays to Costco's ≤14% markup discipline and volume leverage (recently *lowered* egg prices ~10% as costs fell — pricing-power proof). Inflation at this level, below panic, is a net tailwind.
- **Interest rates — policy side POSITIVE, long-end NEGATIVE.** The company itself is barely rate-sensitive: modest debt vs a $408B market cap, funded ~fully by membership cash flows — negligible refinancing risk. The channels that matter: (a) 10Y ~5% keeps mortgage/auto/card rates high, which taxes middle-income members' discretionary wallet (Costco skews higher-income, so diluted); (b) as a long-duration growth multiple, COST is a discount-rate casualty — the ~5% long end is the single largest driver of the 14% derating from the 2025 peak. Fed easing helps the margin at the margin, but the 10Y is the swing factor.
- **Labor market — POSITIVE.** 341k employees; unemployment rising toward 4%+ and wage growth cooling relieve the biggest controllable cost line after COGS.
- **Tariffs / trade — POSITIVE-to-NEUTRAL (differentiating).** Costco is one of the largest direct importers of China-sourced general merchandise. It passed through IEEPA tariff costs quickly (near-term comp pressure), then **sued for refunds and is returning approved refunds to members — a rare competitive action** that should support renewal rates and footfall versus pass-through rivals. Residual risk: new tariff rounds in 2027 would re-stick COGS just as consumers fatigue.
- **Currency — MIXED/NEGATIVE tilt.** ~¼ of sales come from Canada, Mexico, Japan, Korea, UK, et al. A broad USD at ~120 translates ~1.5–2.5% growth drag on reported USD international sales and weakens Asian consumer purchasing power; partially offset by cheaper dollar-denominated import COGS across the ~60% of US merchandise that is imported.
- **Gasoline — NEUTRAL.** 636 stations are traffic drivers, not margin engines; fuel-price declines trim nominal comps but free household wallet (mildly positive for fill-in basket economics).

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL** *(operationally favourable; valuation-unfavourable)*

Operating fundamentals genuinely favour COST in this regime — sticky 3% inflation, trading-down consumer, easing policy rate, cooling wages, and a tariff-refund brand advantage — which is why the business keeps posting solid comps. But the 5% long end (10Y), soft consumer spending momentum into the holiday season ("back down to earth"), and FX translation risk hold the *forward-looking* macro balance at neutral rather than favourable. The macro risk is concentrated in the **discount-rate and consumer-confidence channel**, not the business model.

## 5. Material Surprise Scenario (Bear, with real traction)

**"Term-premium rout pushes the 10Y through 5.5% while the Fed must stay on hold."** If Treasury supply/fiscal concerns keep driving long yields up (already +75 bps YTD with the 10Y at 5.2–5.3% intraday late Sept), mortgage rates push toward 7.5%, auto/credit-card APRs stay pinned, and the ~3% CPI floor blocks further Fed cuts. In that world the holiday consumer pullback accelerates — discretionary general-merchandise comps (Costco's highest-margin mix) soften, membership *growth* stalls even as renewal holds, and the growth multiple compresses again (COST already −14% from peak; a second leg to ~$820–850 is plausible). This scenario has direct leading indicators in the tape: 10Y momentum, Sept-30 consumer-confidence deterioration, and the marked slowdown in US spending cited in coverage. *Bear-case trigger to monitor: 10Y >5.25% sustained, or renewed China tariff escalation.*

**Key sensitivities for the investment memo:** spread compression is the valuation risk; membership renewal + trading-down comps are the fundamental tailwind; IEEPA refunds to members are a 2026–27 upside idiosyncrasy; USD broad >122 becomes a real FX headwind into FY2027.
