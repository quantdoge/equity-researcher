## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — AST SpaceMobile, Inc. (NASDAQ: ASTS)
**Price $33.69 | Market cap $13.8B | Beta 2.85 | 1,126 FTE | Midland, TX | Assessment date: 11 Nov 2026**

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

| Evidence | Assessment |
|---|---|
| **CEO/Chairman duality** | Abel Avellan is founder, Chairman **and** CEO — an explicit concentration of power. Company's own governance guidelines acknowledge the Chairman is a non-independent director. |
| **Board independence** | Only **5 of 10** nominees are independent (Cisneros, Rubin, Sarnoff, Torres, Wibergh). Non-independent: Avellan, Ibbetson, Johnson, Knapp, Larson. Fails a majority-independent standard; consistent with a **controlled-company** structure where the founder holds substantial voting power (dual-class). Boardroom Alpha grades the board "B." |
| **Board refreshment** | Five directors have only ~1.8–2.4 years' tenure (Johnson, Larson, Wibergh), suggesting recent reshuffling rather than stable oversight. |
| **Compensation** | FY2025 program included a large **December 2025 CEO equity award** with a significant performance component; PSU operational targets are partly **confidential** — reduces verifiability of pay-for-performance. Cash bonuses tied to a $75M revenue goal. |
| **Auditor** | KPMG LLP (ratified June 2026) — acceptable, no disclosed auditor dispute or restatement. |
| **Capital-markets credibility** | Central governance issue: repeated "fully funded" assurances allegedly followed by **three separate ~$1.0B convertible note raises** (two confirmed priced, incl. 16 Jul 2026) plus two competitive downgrades. This is the factual core of pending litigation and is a **disclosure-controls and credibility red flag**, not merely a financing choice. |

**Verdict:** Founder-controlled, minority-independent board with dual-class voting and an unverifiable incentive structure — structurally weak checks on management. No evidence of accounting fraud, but disclosure credibility is impaired.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

**Positive (de-risked):**
- **FCC granted full commercial authorization (22 Apr 2026)** for the 248-satellite constellation and Supplemental Coverage from Space (SCS) with partner MNOs (AT&T, Verizon, FirstNet, 700/800MHz low-band). This was the single largest binary regulatory hurdle and it is now cleared. Stock rose ~15% on the news.

**Negative / active:**
- **Securities class action — *Hunter v. AST SpaceMobile*, No. 26-cv-00378 (W.D. Tex., Midland-Odessa).** §§10(b) & 20(a), Rule 10b-5. Class period **4 Mar 2025 – 15 Jul 2026**. Lead-plaintiff deadline **13 Nov 2026**. Multiple firms (Robbins Geller, Pomerantz, Rosen, Kaplan Fox, Portnoy, DJS) are soliciting — a crowded docket implies material alleged losses. Allegations include overstated capital adequacy ("fully funded") and **insider stock sales**. Filed by officers/company; this is a *civil* action, not an SEC/DOJ enforcement matter.
- **SEC enforcement search returned zero actions** via EDGAR full-text search. No known DOJ probe, no restatement, no auditor change. *Caveat: absence of EDGAR hits is not proof of absence of informal inquiries.*
- **Competitive regulatory asymmetry:** FCC approved **SpaceX for 15,000 direct-to-device satellites (7 Oct 2026)** — 60x AST's authorized fleet. Regulatory permission is no longer a moat.
- **International licensing** remains gating: Japan via Rakuten AST JV, plus other MNO markets (Vodafone, Verizon) each require sovereign approvals — execution risk, not yet a violation.
- **SEC filing status:** FY2025 10-K and Q3-26 10-Q on file; CEO/CFO 302 certifications executed 9 Nov 2026 (no disclosed material weakness). DEF 14A (2026) confirms a clean AGM with KPMG ratified.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Launch-provider concentration and launch cadence — SEVERE, already realized.** ASTS does not control its own ride to orbit. **BlueBird 7 was lost on 19 Apr 2026** (New Glenn placed it too low; de-orbited) with a **~$125.9M write-off against only $30M insurance** — a ~$96M uninsured loss. A Blue Origin pad explosion then pushed initial commercial service **3–6 months, into H1 2027**. The company needs **45 satellites by mid-2027** but has flown a handful; every missed slot is unrecoverable revenue because **carriers pay only for continuous coverage**. The "Blacklift" vertical-integration initiative adds cash burn without fixing the near-term bottleneck.
- **Key-person and single-site concentration.** The founder-CEO *is* the strategy, the balance sheet and the voting control. Loss of Avellan would be an acute event. Manufacturing is concentrated in Midland, TX (single campus), creating physical/continuity concentration.
- **Technology/dependency execution and capital intensity.** First-of-kind large-aperture phased-array D2D constellation, reliant on partner MNO spectrum and on ground/gateway integration; $3.2B liquidity is real but the burn rate implies recurring capital-markets dependence — which is itself the litigation risk. (Cybersecurity: no disclosed breach found, but scale-up of ground infrastructure raises attack surface; no independent assurance obtained.)

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **HIGH**

- **Securities-fraud litigation saturation:** at least six plaintiff firms advertising against ASTS simultaneously, generating a continuous negative-news drip through the 13 Nov 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline and beyond.
- **Product failure in public:** a lost satellite on a marquee Blue Origin mission is visible, well-covered (Fierce, SpaceNews, SatNews) and easy for partners/insurers to price.
- **Narrative damage:** stock **-75% from the $133.86 May 2026 high**, **-55% YTD**, down three straight weeks; press framing has shifted from "category creator" to "too many execution risks." The "fully funded" phrase has become a liability rather than a reassurance.
- **Partner/channel exposure:** AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Rakuten and Google/Alphabet (2.3% holder) are brand-linked to ASTS timelines; repeated slips transfer reputational cost to them and weaken AST's negotiating position (they pay only for continuous coverage).
- **Mitigant:** genuine goodwill in disaster-resilience/coverage-gap use cases (Rakuten AST JV framed around Japan disaster connectivity) — provides some reputational ballast.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **"Fully funded" representations vs. three ~$1B convertible raises** — the core 10b-5 allegation. Treat management's liquidity statements as **non-reliable**; model financing as recurring, not one-off. Escalate to disclosure review.
2. **CEO monetized ~$147M (plus ~$183M Form 144) while the "fully funded" narrative was live**, retaining voting control via pledged units. Economic de-risking by the controlling shareholder at the top is a first-order alignment failure.
3. **President sold at $126.64 (~$3.3M) weeks before the July 16 disclosure** that erased ~$3.5B of market value. Investigate whether sales fell inside a 10b5-1 plan adopted with knowledge of pending launch/converts news — potential **Item 408 / trading-window** exposure.
4. **Net insider selling ~$451.6M vs ~$0.8M buying (≈560:1)** over 12 months, with a lone token director purchase. No open-market CEO buy at any point in the decline from $133 to $34.
5. **Minority-independent board (5/10) + CEO/Chair duality + dual-class control** — no realistic internal check on the controlling executive; committee oversight (Audit, Comp) cannot bind capital allocation.
6. **Uninsured loss concentration:** $125.9M write-off vs $30M insurance on a single satellite, with total dependence on Blue Origin/other providers for constellation build-out. Verify insurance program adequacy for the 45-satellite campaign.
7. **Competitive regulatory asymmetry:** SpaceX cleared for 15,000 D2D satellites vs AST's 248 — the FCC win is necessary but not sufficient; escalate to the thesis as a structural, not cyclical, threat.
