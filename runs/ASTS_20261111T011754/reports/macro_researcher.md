# Macroeconomic Assessment — AST SpaceMobile (ASTS)

**As of 11 November 2026 | Price $33.69 | Market cap $13.8B | Beta 2.85**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Risk-off for long-duration equities inside a risk-on credit market — a "bear steepener with easy credit."**

The US economy is re-accelerating into a second inflation wave rather than slowing. Real GDP grew **+3.4% annualised in Q3 2026** (from +2.2% in Q2), unemployment sits at **4.1%**, and headline CPI is running at **~3.8% y/y** with core CPI up **0.3% m/m for a third consecutive month** (October print, released 10 Nov). The Fed **hiked 25bp on 16 September 2026 to 3.75–4.00%**, held on 28 October, and markets price a **December hike plus two more in 2027**.

The critical feature: with the funds rate at 3.88% and CPI at 3.8%, the **real policy rate is approximately zero**. Policy is not restrictive, which is precisely why the long end is repricing. The **10Y has moved from 4.44% (30 June) to ~5.13%, peaking at 5.31% on 5 October**, and the **30Y is at 5.47%** — a classic **bear steepener** driven by term premium, not by inflation expectations (10Y breakevens are *falling*, at 2.20%). That means the **10Y real yield is ~2.93%** — a genuinely punishing discount rate with no offset from inflation-expectation relief.

Credit, however, is wide open: **high-yield OAS is 3.34%**, historically tight, though it has widened ~70bp since 1 September (2.65%). This bifurcation matters enormously for ASTS — its *equity* is priced off the real long rate, while its *ability to raise* is priced off credit and volatility, both still accommodative.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

ASTS is close to the purest **long-duration asset** in public markets: FY26 revenue guidance of **$150–200M** against a **$13.8B market cap (~78x sales)**, negative earnings, and a value that depends almost entirely on cash flows arriving 2028–2035. Its macro sensitivity is therefore not cyclical — it is **discount-rate and financing sensitivity**.

**NEGATIVE — Long-end real rates (highest-severity).** With a 10Y real yield near 2.9% and rising, the present value of ASTS's terminal-value-heavy cash flow stream compresses mechanically. The equity's effective duration is arguably 15–20 years. The evidence is direct: on **10 November, shares fell from $36.89 to $33.69 (−8.7%) on the hot CPI print** specifically because, as coverage noted, a "hot inflation report may be weighing on shares, given AST's current loss-making business." ASTS is down **~75% from its May 2026 high of $133.86** and ~53% YTD, against an S&P 500 at 7,545 (+0.11%) — a textbook duration de-rating, not an operating failure.

**NEGATIVE — Financing cost and access.** ASTS has funded itself via **$2.2B of convertible notes across three separate $1B offerings plus $706M of ATM equity**. Every incremental 50bp raises the coupon on the next raise and, more importantly, reduces the conversion premium investors will accept. A hawkish Fed that pushes the 10Y through 5.5% would force another raise — and the lawsuit narrative already centres on "fully funded" claims followed by repeated $1B converts. Credit spreads at 3.34% are the one genuine mitigant: the convertible market is functioning, and ASTS's very high volatility supports convertible terms.

**NEGATIVE — Inflation on cost structure, with no pass-through.** This is underappreciated. ASTS's cost base is satellite manufacturing (Midland, TX), **launch services purchased from SpaceX and Blue Origin**, and aerospace labour — all inflating at or above CPI. Meanwhile revenue is **milestone-based payments from MNO partners** with no ability to reprice for input inflation before continuous service launches. This is a structural margin squeeze in a 3.8% inflation regime. Every quarter of delay between now and mid-2027 (the 45-BlueBird target, slipped from end-2026) adds real cost.

**MILD NEGATIVE — USD strength.** The broad dollar is up 3.3% since September. ASTS's commercial footprint is heavily international (Rakuten/Japan, Saudi Arabia, UK, Canada, Brazil, 60+ MNOs, 3bn subscribers), so translated revenue and the local-currency value of partner contributions decline. Partly offset: the **~$1B Rakuten AST Japan JV funding from Japan's MIC** is non-dilutive and non-USD, reducing dollar funding dependence.

**POSITIVE — Fiscal / government demand.** The company secured a **US priority DPAS rating** for its dual-use satellite programme, and services revenue of $21.8M is already being generated from both commercial and **US government customers**. Government and defence budgets are the least rate-sensitive, least cyclical revenue line ASTS has — a genuine macro tailwind and diversifier.

**NEUTRAL / MILD POSITIVE — Growth.** ASTS demand is not GDP-dependent; it is driven by rural coverage gaps and MNO capex, which is defensive. But 2–3%+ growth sustains risk appetite and carrier capacity to sign revenue-share deals.

**NEGATIVE (risk) — Trade policy / tariffs.** ASICs (TSMC, Taiwan), phased-array electronics, and rare-earth-dependent components sit in the COGS line. A hawkish, inflation-fighting administration with an active tariff agenda raises input costs and could pass through via launch pricing.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

This is close to the worst macro configuration possible for this specific business model: **re-accelerating inflation → hawkish Fed → rising real long yields**, precisely when the company remains loss-making, cash-consuming, and dependent on repeat capital raises. Real yields near 2.9% are the primary driver of the 75% drawdown, and the Fed's next move is more likely up than down. The offsetting factors — tight credit spreads, government/defence demand, non-dilutive Japanese funding, and a $3.2B cash cushion — are meaningful but are *solvency* supports, not *valuation* supports. A beta of 2.85 means ASTS will amplify any further move higher in real yields.

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**Bull surprise: a growth-driven Fed pivot that compresses real yields (probability ~25%, but high magnitude).**

Consensus assumes hikes continue. The surprise would be a **Q1 2027 growth scare** — a labour-market crack or a credit event — that forces the Fed to reverse. In that world the **10Y falls from 5.13% to ~4.2% and real yields compress 70–90bp**. Combined with ASTS reaching **45 BlueBirds by mid-2027** and converting from intermittent to **continuous coverage** (the trigger for full carrier payments), ASTS experiences a violent double re-rating: multiple expansion from duration *plus* the revenue step-change. A move back toward $60–80 is plausible in that combination. Note this is a "bad news is good news" trade for ASTS — it needs *weaker* macro data, which is an uncomfortable and fragile thing to underwrite.

**Bear tail to flag (probability ~35%):** core CPI's third consecutive 0.3% m/m print invites a **50bp hike** (already being floated). If the 10Y breaks 5.5%, ASTS — needing capital for a 100-satellite build through 2028 — funds at punitive terms or via heavily dilutive equity, and a stronger dollar compounds the translation drag. Downside $18–22.
