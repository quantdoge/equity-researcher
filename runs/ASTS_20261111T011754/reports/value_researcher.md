# AST SpaceMobile (ASTS) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price used: $33.69 (Nov 10, 2026 close). Market cap ≈ $10.3B basic (~305M sh) / ~$13.8B fully diluted. Net debt $1.32B (Q3'26). EV ≈ $11.5B.**
*Data note: the price feed initially returned a stale Feb-2026 quote ($80.20); the live figure is $33.69 — the stock is down 75% from its May 2026 peak of $133.09. FY2026 annuals are not yet reported; I use FY2025 audited + 9M26 10-Q.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NONE (potential "narrow," unproven)**

AST SpaceMobile is building a space-based cellular network (Block 2 "BlueBirds," ~2,400 sq ft arrays) that connects **unmodified** handsets directly, monetized through revenue-share agreements with mobile network operators (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Google as investor, plus international MNOs).

What could constitute a moat, and why I don't credit it yet:

| Potential moat source | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Intangible assets** — large-aperture phased-array patents, FCC supplemental-coverage authorizations, orbital filings | Real and the best argument. But unvalidated at scale; authorizations are partner-spectrum dependent, not owned spectrum |
| **Network effects** | Weak. Coverage depends on satellite count and partner pops; a competitor's constellation delivers the same end-user benefit |
| **Switching costs** | Nil for end users — the selling point is *no* new hardware or app. MNOs can multi-source (and are doing so) |
| **Cost advantage** | **Negative.** SpaceX owns launch and now holds FCC authority for a **15,000-satellite direct-to-cell constellation** plus acquired nationwide 800 MHz spectrum; ASTS must buy launches at market and rent spectrum from carriers |
| **Efficient scale** | No. AT&T/T-Mobile/Verizon have formed a satellite JV specifically to commoditize this layer |

**Decisive evidence:** in 11 years of operation the company has **never produced positive operating income**. ROIC was **−6.2% in FY2025** (−28.8% FY24, −77.6% FY23). A moat is demonstrated by sustained ROIC > WACC; ASTS has the opposite. The technology lead is genuine but has a shelf life measured in quarters, and the deepest-pocketed competitor in the industry is now licensed to attack it.

## 3. Valuation — Intrinsic Value ≈ **$11–16/share** vs. **$33.69** price

A DCF on trailing FCF returns a negative number, so I value the *outcome set* explicitly. At EV ≈ $11.5B the market is paying ~66x FY26 revenue guidance midpoint (~$175M). Reverse-engineering: to justify $11.5B today at a mature 25x FCF and 30% FCF margin, the company must reach **~$1.5B of revenue *and* be fully funded** — i.e., the market assumes the build finishes without materially dilutive rescue financing. That is the "everything works" case.

Scenario work (fully diluted share counts adjusted for expected future raises; 12% discount rate, justified by beta 2.85, single-technology risk, and financing dependence):

| Scenario | Prob. | 2031 revenue | FCF margin | Equity value today | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** — full 45–60 satellite coverage, pricing holds, funded at reasonable terms | 25–30% | ~$4.0B | 30% | ~$10–12B | **$25–28** |
| **Base** — partial constellation, revenue-share economics compress margins, one more large equity raise | 40% | ~$1.5B | 20% | ~$2.5–3.5B | **$7–10** |
| **Bear** — Starlink D2C wins the mass market; ASTS becomes a niche/government provider or recapitalizes | 30–35% | <$0.7B | <15% | ~$1–2B | **$3–5** |

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $12–15/share.** The current price is **~2.2–2.8x** my central estimate. To be worth $33.69 you must believe the bull case has a ~65–70% probability *and* that no further large dilution occurs — in a business that has already issued 5.5x its share count and needs several billion more to reach continuous coverage.

Sell-side targets ($43 UBS, $54–56 Piper/Scotiabank, $65 B.Riley) are above the price, but those are 12-month momentum-adjusted marks, not owner-value estimates; note the direction of travel — downgrades and target cuts through Oct–Nov 2026.

## 4. Management Quality — **Good operators, expensive capital allocators (score: 5/10)**

- **Founder-led** (Abel Avellan, CEO/Chair), substantial insider ownership, deep technical conviction. Execution credibility is real: six satellites launched in under 50 days in Aug 2026, on-schedule Block 2 manufacturing, and marquee carrier relationships (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Google) that a weaker team could not have assembled. Disclosure quality is transparent.
- **But capital allocation is shareholder-dilutive by necessity and increasingly by choice:** continuous convertible issuance, $4.1B of debt at a company with $143M of LTM revenue, accelerating SBC, and no buybacks or dividends (understandable). No evidence of value-accretive M&A — a small positive.
- The critical unanswered question is **at what price future capital is raised**. Each $1B raised near $34 costs ~30M shares (~10% of the company) and permanently transfers value from today's owner to tomorrow's.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / AVOID**

This is a genuinely interesting technology company and a poor *value* investment at this price. It fails every Buffett test that matters:

- **Circle of competence:** a pre-profit, capital-devouring, single-technology, financing-dependent space build is outside it. I cannot estimate its cash flows within a defensible range — which by itself is disqualifying.
- **Moat:** none proven; ROIC negative every year; the strongest competitor on earth just received authority to compete directly.
- **Owner earnings:** negative in every year of its existence (−$1.36B in FY2025).
- **Margin of safety:** none — the price *is* the bull case. I require 20–30% *below* intrinsic value; ASTS trades at 2–3x my central intrinsic value.

The 75% drawdown from $133 makes the chart *look* like an opportunity. It isn't: a stock that falls from $133 to $34 while intrinsic value is ~$12–15 is still expensive. **If held, I would exit; I would not initiate.** I would only revisit on (a) proof of continuous-coverage service with disclosed per-satellite unit economics, (b) funded capex plan without another large equity raise, and (c) a price near tangible book (~$7–10, i.e., 60–70% below today).

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to the bear/Sell call (i.e., what would make me wrong):**
1. A Tier-1 carrier makes a large strategic equity investment or take-or-pay commitment at a premium price, removing financing risk overnight.
2. Government/FirstNet-style contracts scale to hundreds of millions of predictable annual revenue at high margin.
3. SpaceX's D2C rollout stumbles on capacity or regulatory grounds, leaving the premium "full broadband D2C" niche to ASTS.

**Risks to owning it (the asymmetry an owner faces):**
1. **Financing/dilution:** $1.5B burned in 9M26 against $2.8B cash — roughly 5–6 quarters of runway at current burn; another raise is likely and likely dilutive.
2. **Launch/deployment risk:** a single launch failure or manufacturing slip delays revenue by 6–18 months and compresses the equity.
3. **Competition:** SpaceX's 15,000-satellite D2C authority + nationwide 800 MHz spectrum + carrier JV (AT&T/T-Mobile/Verizon) can commoditize D2C before ASTS achieves scale.
4. **Spectrum/partner dependence:** no owned US spectrum; revenue-share terms with MNOs are negotiated from a position of rising weakness as alternatives multiply.
5. **Convert overhang and $121M/yr interest** against tiny revenue; beta 2.85 and 50%+ annualized volatility.
6. **Key-person concentration** in the founder-CEO.
