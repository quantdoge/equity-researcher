## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Absolute footprint is small; intensity story is favourable.** A ~90-satellite architecture (vs. mega-constellations of thousands) with ≤700 km orbits, ~10-year design life and full burn-up deorbit within five years of retirement. This is a defensible "efficient constellation" argument for MNO and government counterparties.
- **The material E risk is orbital, not atmospheric.** BlueWalker 3 and BlueBird-class arrays are among the brightest objects in LEO; peer-reviewed work (IOP/AAS *Pub. Astron. Soc. Pacific*, 2025 on "satellite brightness and the limits of orbital…") and Rubin Observatory guidance frame large-area arrays as an astronomy externality. AST's mitigations — NSF coordination agreement, IAU Dark & Quiet Skies alignment, anti-reflective coatings, attitude changes during observation windows, sharing high-precision orbital data — are **best-in-class for the sector** and materially reduce the odds of restrictive conditions, but they are voluntary and unverified externally.
- **Live incident:** BlueBird 7 (Apr 2026) was inserted into an off-nominal, too-low orbit by Blue Origin's New Glenn upper stage; the satellite **was de-orbited**, so no long-lived derelict was created (debris risk contained; FAA grounded New Glenn, delaying initial commercial service to 2027). Net: contained E outcome, but it demonstrates that AST's deorbit compliance depends on *third-party* launch performance — a governance-of-risk issue, not a disclosure one.
- **Disclosure is the weak link:** no Scope 1/2/3, no TCFD/CSRD-style reporting, third-party ESG transparency score of 2.4 (sevva). Rated **Medium**, not Low, solely because E failure is a licence event rather than a cost event.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives (genuinely material, not marketing):** the product is a digital-divide solution — broadband to unserved/underserved regions, maritime and aviation; emergency/amateur-radio commitments (430–440 MHz emergency-only; beam-forming that "paints around" radio-quiet zones); FirstNet public-safety SCS application accepted by the FCC in April 2026. This is a real social-licence asset with US regulators and MNO partners (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Google ecosystem).

**Risks:**
- **Scale-up safety risk.** Headcount is expanding fast (1,126 FTE plus 500+ manufacturing hires in Midland/El Paso at $20–55/hr). Satellite assembly/test/integration and launch logistics are high-hazard; there is **no public TRIR, safety record or union disclosure**. A serious injury or fatality would create headline, OSHA and schedule risk simultaneously.
- **Key-person concentration** in founder-CEO Abel Avellan (also a governance issue): the Stockholders' Agreement and 2021 risk factors tie governance continuity to his tenure.
- **Pay-equity optics:** CEO $14.2M vs. median employee $138,560 = **103:1**. Defensible given 100%-equity, no-salary CEO structure, but a recurring proxy-advisor talking point alongside the controlled vote.

## 5. ESG Controversies (with financial-impact estimates)

1. **Securities class action — W.D. Tex. (filed 2026; class period 3/4/2025–7/15/2026).** Alleges AST overstated the *durability* of its D2C competitive advantage and the *sufficiency of its capital and liquidity*, with corrective disclosures from 9/8/2025 through the 7/15/2026 $1B convertible announcement. Lead-plaintiff deadline 11/13/2026. **Estimated cost: $25–75M gross settlement range** (large-cap, high-volatility issuer with ~$14B cap; heavily D&O-insured; note this is a planning assumption, not a disclosed reserve — no accrual is public).
2. **Prior securities class action** (class period 11/14/2023–4/1/2024; multiple firms) — same pattern of launch/technology overstatement allegations; settlement values not verified. Signals a **repeat episode**, which is what upgrades "litigation" to "governance culture."
3. **Derivative suit *Papa v. Avellan* (W.D. Tex., 2026)** — pleads Avellan as controlling shareholder at ~71.6% voting power; challenges related-party/control conduct. **Cost: legal defence $5–15M; the reputational cost is larger** because it formalises the minority-holder grievance.
4. **Kerrisdale Capital short report (Sept 2022)** — challenged satellite capability economics; stock fell >7%. No proven fraud, but it established the "narrative-versus-evidence" overhang that later complaints recycle.
5. **BlueBird 7 launch loss / FAA grounding of New Glenn (Apr 2026)** — de-orbited, so **no debris liability**; commercial service slipped to 2027 and the company raised $1B — i.e., ~3–6 months of delayed revenue and incremental financing cost. Environmental dimension: minor. Execution/regulatory dimension: **significant**.
6. **Astronomy/light-pollution criticism** — recurring NGO and press attention to array brightness. **Cost: $2–10M/year of mitigation engineering plus the residual risk of FCC conditions** on future licence modifications; low cash, high licence-to-operate relevance.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US FCC (highest):** April 2026 grant of 248-satellite authority plus the **first major commercial SCS grant**, subject to conditions — including 5-year post-mission disposal, collision-avoidance/NASA best practices, EPFD/interference limits. This single regulator controls AST's entire revenue model → ESG-driven licence conditions are effectively **credit events**.
- **FAA:** launch-mishap investigation regime (New Glenn grounding showed direct schedule transmission to AST).
- **ITU:** orbital-slot/spectrum milestone and deorbit obligations; coordination dependencies with MNO spectrum holders.
- **FirstNet / public safety:** SCS application accepted (DA-26-391) — social-licence upside but adds compliance surface.
- **EU CSRD / CBAM / EU ETS:** **not directly applicable** at current revenue scale and with no EU manufacturing; however, EU-based MNO partners under CSRD value-chain reporting will cascade supplier data requests onto AST (a low-cost, near-term disclosure obligation).
- **SEC climate disclosure rule:** currently stayed; AST has **no** climate disclosure obligation and publishes none — so no compliance cost, but also no demonstrable readiness.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** **none** — 2026 capital raised via plain convertibles. Positive: no sustainability-linked pricing step-ups or KPI-default risk. Negative read-through: lenders/investors do not view AST's ESG profile as bankable enough to attach an ESG-linked instrument.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD (governance-driven)**

E is *Medium* with above-peer space-stewardship policy but no measurement; S is *Medium* with a genuinely positive social mission and unmanaged scale-up safety/people risk; G is *weak* (3/10) on control, duality, related-party perception and repeated dilution. Because AST's valuation hinges on capital access and regulatory permission — both governance-mediated — the composite is a **Laggard**, edging toward **ESG Risk** on the governance pillar alone. It is emphatically *not* an environmental laggard in the climate sense, and the absence of a heavy carbon cost base means it screens clean on carbon but fails on accountability.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE for the thesis**

- **Direction of travel:** ESG factors do not threaten AST through carbon costs, CBAM, or physical climate — those are immaterial here. They bite through three channels that are directly valuation-relevant:
 1. **Cost of capital / dilution.** A 72%-controlled, dual-class issuer with two securities class actions and two $1B converts in one year is structurally a price-taker in capital markets. Beta of 2.85 plus a governance discount is the mechanism by which ESG shows up in the DCF. Expect continued equity-linked issuance to fund the 2027 service launch — a persistent drag on per-share value independent of operating success.
 2. **Licence-to-operate optionality.** FCC conditions (deorbit, interference, EPFD) are the whole business. The company's space-stewardship posture is strong and mitigates this — but it is *unmeasured and third-party-unverified*, so any Brightness/interference event would reprice the licence path with no disclosure cushion.
 3. **Trust deficit on disclosure.** Two litigation episodes alleging overstatement, zero climate/safety metrics, and an ESG transparency score of 2.4 tell the same story: management controls the narrative and the vote. For an investment case that rests on management's own schedule and coverage claims, that is a live, quantifiable discount rate.
- **What would move us toward Neutral:** dual-class sunset or an independent chair; a published TRIR and Scope 1/2/3 baseline; quantified deorbit/interference compliance reporting; disclosure of related-party transactions with founder-affiliated entities; a financing plan that does not rely on annual $1B equity-linked raises.
- **Positioning:** the ESG work does **not** independently break the equity story — the technology and FCC grant are real assets — but it removes the "clean growth story" framing and argues for a **larger governance/dilution haircut in the valuation, a smaller position size, and pressure on capital-allocation discipline** as the primary engagement lever.
