# SECTOR BRIEF: AST SpaceMobile (ASTS)
**Sector: Communication Services / Telecommunications Services (sub-segment: satellite Direct-to-Device, "D2D")**
*Data as of 10 Nov 2026. Company financials FY2025 (filed 2 Mar 2026).*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

ASTS does not really compete in "Telecommunications Services" as FMP classifies it — against Verizon, T-Mobile and AT&T it is a 0.003%-of-revenue rounding error. Its economic sector is **satellite direct-to-device connectivity**, a market worth roughly **$5.0bn in 2026, forecast to reach ~$13.8bn by 2031 (~22% CAGR)** per Mordor Intelligence (note: third-party 2026 sizings range wildly from $0.6bn to $5.0bn — a hallmark of an immature category). The structure is a **two-horse race**: SpaceX's Starlink Direct-to-Cell (commercially live with T-Mobile since July 2025, now launching dedicated D2C batches) and ASTS, with Globalstar (Apple's emergency-SOS partner), Iridium (Project Stardust NTN), Viasat and EchoStar/Amazon Leo as secondary players. Barriers to entry are genuinely high — scarce spectrum, launch capacity, ~$3–5bn of constellation capex and FCC/ITU licences — but the decisive asymmetry is that **ASTS's largest competitor also controls its ride to orbit**. The sector is in the **earliest growth phase**: revenue is a rounding error against TAM, unit economics are unproven at scale, and the industry is mid-capex-super-cycle with cumulative FCF deeply negative.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Carrier distribution is already contracted, not hypothetical.** ASTS holds agreements with ~45 MNOs covering roughly 3bn subscribers, including definitive commercial (revenue-share) deals with **AT&T and Verizon**, plus Vodafone, Google and American Tower as strategic investors. This is a capital-light go-to-market: ASTS supplies coverage, carriers supply billing, spectrum and 2.8bn existing handsets — no new device required.
- **Regulatory and standards unlock.** The FCC's Supplemental Coverage from Space framework plus 3GPP NTN standardisation (Releases 19/20) are converting D2D from experimental to mainstream. ASTS has secured D2D testing authority in the US, and public-safety/FirstNet optionality adds a non-consumer revenue leg with high willingness-to-pay.
- **Structural demand pull from carriers' worst economics.** Rural white space, maritime, aviation and disaster resilience are the highest-cost-to-serve segments in telecom; D2D addresses them at a fraction of tower economics. With sector-level gross margin at **50.9%** on **$2.43trn** of revenue across the 13 largest Communication Services names, even a 1–2% coverage-gap revenue share is a multi-billion-dollar pool for a sub-$1bn-revenue operator.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **SpaceX is a structurally advantaged competitor — and a supplier.** Starlink has a launch cost advantage of roughly an order of magnitude per kg, a far larger constellation already in orbit, a live T-Mobile product, and in 2025 acquired EchoStar spectrum (~$17bn) to go deeper into D2D. ASTS must buy launches from the same entity it competes with. Two competitive downgrades on this basis sit inside the current securities class action.
- **Capital intensity, dilution and credibility drag.** FY2025 net loss **$342m** on **$70.9m** of revenue; diluted shares went from 51.7m (2021) → 154.5m (2024) → **256.0m (2025)**, +66% YoY, after three separate $1bn convertible raises. Interest expense has climbed to **$36m** and will step up with the converts. Class actions allege "fully funded" assurances were misleading (class period Mar 2025–Jul 2026) — a real overhang on the equity's cost of capital.
- **Continuous-coverage threshold is the whole business, and it depends on rockets ASTS doesn't control.** Carriers pay for *continuous* service. ASTS had roughly 10–13 BlueBirds in orbit as of late 2026 against a target of **45 satellites by mid-2027** — implying a launch cadence several times anything achieved to date, with New Glenn/Falcon 9 scheduling, satellite-failure and D&A-scaling risk all ahead. Gross margin (53.4%) will compress as constellation depreciation arrives; FY2025 D&A was only $51m.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **AVERAGE**
*(Technology and strategic positioning: strong. Current commercial/financial position: weak. Net: average.)*

**For (strong):** ASTS has the industry's most capable broadband D2D payload — large phased-array apertures that connect to *unmodified* handsets at genuine broadband (not just SMS) rates, a genuine technological lead over Starlink's current text-first D2C service. It holds priority spectrum plus partner-MNO spectrum in the US, and the deepest carrier roster in the category (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Google). Its gross margin of 53.4% already sits *above* the Communication Services aggregate (50.9%), signalling that the underlying service economics are sound — the deficit is scale, not gross profitability. Peer gross margins of 64.8% (GSAT) and 77.3% (IRDM) show the ceiling if it reaches constellation scale.

**Against (weak):** Sub-scale revenue ($70.9m), a ~$288m operating loss with no path to GAAP profitability before 2028–29 on any plausible cadence, 66% annual share count growth, and a competitor that is simultaneously its launch provider, its best-capitalised rival and a price-setter. Management's FY26 guidance of $150–200m (implying +112% to +182%) was maintained but Q3'26 revenue, though up 187.1%, *missed* estimates — the growth is real but the execution record versus expectations is not clean. Relative share is being ceded: Iridium's market cap is a third of ASTS's despite 12x the revenue, and the equity market has spent 2026 correcting that gap.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**
*(Growth: Attractive. Risk-adjusted economics: Unattractive. Net: Neutral.)*

Structurally, D2D is one of the best demand stories in telecom — 20%+ CAGR, a multi-billion-dollar TAM, high barriers, and carrier demand that is contracted rather than speculative. But the *value capture* question is unresolved and increasingly unfavourable for anyone other than SpaceX: the competitive price-setter owns the launch cost curve, the category requires $3–5bn of capex before generating continuous coverage, and no pure-play operator in this peer set other than Iridium covers its cost of capital (Iridium required 22 years and multiple restructurings to get there). ASTS is a high-conviction *technology* story inside a **Neutral** sector — one where the sector's growth is unlikely to translate into attractive returns on capital for the average participant, and where the equity is best underwritten on launch cadence and coverage milestones rather than on TAM.
