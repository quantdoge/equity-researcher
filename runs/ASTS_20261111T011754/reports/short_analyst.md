## FORENSIC SHORT ANALYSIS — AST SPACEMOBILE (ASTS)

**Price $33.69 · Market cap $13.76B · Sector: Comm. Services / Telecom · Data as of Q3 FY2026 (10-Q filed 2026-11-09)**
**Note: stock is −75% from its May 2026 high of $133.86. This is a post-collapse analysis, which materially changes the trade.**

### 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **LOW**

Composite earnings-quality score **14/100**. Cash conversion **0.21x** (operating CF / net income) — for a company building a capital-intensive network, anything under 1.0x means reported results are not converting to cash. Altman Z-Score **0.605 (Distress zone)**; Piotroski **5/9**, with zeros on *positive ROA, positive operating cash flow, lower leverage,* and *no dilution* — i.e. it scores as a weak business on the four criteria that matter most.

**Important honesty check on the Beneish M-Score:** the tool returns **+30.13 ("likely manipulator")**, but this is a *model artifact*, not evidence. GMI of −24.97 and AQI of 82.29 are produced by a near-zero revenue base ($0 in FY2023, $4.4M in FY2024) and exploding fixed assets. Beneish was not designed for pre-revenue-scale infrastructure. **I explicitly do not rest the short case on it.** The real evidence is below.

### 2. Key Red Flags Found (with data)

**Red flag #1 — Receivables are exploding far faster than revenue.** AR went $27.5M → $79.3M → **$104.1M** across Q1–Q3 FY2026 (+279%) while quarterly revenue went $14.7M → $31.5M → $42.3M (+187%). Days sales outstanding is **~224 days** on quarterly revenue (~266 days on TTM revenue). A normal telecom/equipment business runs 60–90 days. **In Q3 alone, AR grew $25.1M against revenue of $42.3M — roughly 59% of the quarter's revenue was not collected.** AR is growing ~2.3x faster than revenue. This is the textbook signature of aggressive revenue recognition or channel stuffing, and it is the single most damning number in the file.

**Red flag #2 — Inventory days more than doubled.** Inventory: $12.0M → $16.8M → $28.4M → **$79.0M** in three quarters (+558%). Inventory days rose from **132.7 (FY2025) to ~284 (Q3 FY2026 annualized)**. For a satellite-production business, that is either genuine build-ahead of the 2027 launch or evidence of components accumulating for satellites that cannot be launched on schedule. Either way it is a cash sink, and "stable_or_falling" from the screening tool is wrong — the raw quarters show a clear ramp.

**Red flag #3 — GAAP losses are flattered by non-cash gains.** Income-statement net loss vs cash-flow-statement net income diverges persistently:
| FY | IS net income | CF net income | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | −$87.6M | −$222.7M | **+$135.1M** |
| 2024 | −$300.1M | −$526.3M | **+$226.2M** |
| 2025 | −$341.9M | −$461.0M | **+$119.1M** |

The P&L net loss is **$119–226M smaller per year** than the cash-flow figure — consistent with recurring non-cash mark-to-market *gains* on warrants/convertible derivatives flowing through the income statement. Headline "narrower than expected loss" beats are therefore low-quality. (Note also FY2025 operating income of −$287.7M vs EBIT of −$421.0M — a $133M gap that shouldn't exist.)

**Red flag #4 — The quarterly and annual gross margins do not reconcile.** Q4 FY2025 shows revenue $54.3M against cost of revenue $91.3M — a **−68% gross margin**. Yet the FY2025 annual statement shows gross profit of **+$37.9M on $70.9M of revenue (53.4% margin)**. Q3+Q4 COGS alone ($96.8M) exceeds the full-year COGS ($33.0M). Something is being reclassified (most likely launch costs or D&A). For a company whose entire thesis is gross margin at scale, this opacity is disqualifying on its own. Gross margin path: **−68.1% (Q4'25) → 24.9% → 25.2% → 40.2% (Q3'26)** — even the "good" recent quarters are far below the 70–80% the D2C bull case requires.

**Red flag #5 — The single positive operating-cash-flow quarter was manufactured by working capital.** Q4 FY2025 posted **+$65.0M OCF** — the only positive quarter — on a **$105.1M working-capital inflow**, in a year that closed at −$71.5M OCF. It coincided with the bizarre negative-gross-margin quarter. Narrative risk: "we're approaching cash-flow breakeven."

**Red flag #6 — Every dollar of "progress" is bought with dilution and debt.** Diluted weighted shares: **81.8M (2023) → 154.5M (2024) → 256.0M (2025) → 300.1M (Q3'26)** — +267% in under three years. The vendor market cap of $13.76B at $33.69 implies **~408M shares**, versus 300M in the P&L — i.e. **another ~36% of latent dilution** (Class B/C, converts, warrants) not in headline EPS. Stock-based comp is **$52–63M per quarter (~$209M/yr), equal to 147% of TTM revenue.**

**Red flag #7 — Interest expense is approaching revenue.** Q3 FY2026 interest expense **$30.3M/quarter (~$121M annualized) = 85% of TTM revenue of $142.9M.** Total debt **$4.12B**, up $1.13B in a single quarter; net debt $1.32B. Cash "rose" to $2.80B only because they borrowed — underlying FCF was −$464M in the quarter, and without the raise cash would have fallen ~$975M.

### 4. Competitive Threat Assessment — **Severe and intensifying**

- **July 15–16, 2026:** ASTS disclosed its launch campaign slipped to **early 2027** *alongside a capital raise*. The stock fell ~17% that day on 53M shares. **The securities class action class period ends exactly on July 15, 2026** — the day before.
- **SpaceX FCC approval for 15,000 additional satellites** drove a further −13.6% day. Starlink D2C is fielded at scale with T-Mobile; ASTS has a handful of BlueBirds. The capital asymmetry is the whole ballgame: SpaceX self-funds, ASTS borrows at scale and issues equity continuously.
- **"Technology advantage being commoditised"** is precisely what the class action alleges — that ASTS "misled investors about how lasting its advantage is against SpaceX's Starlink Mobile" and understated capital requirements.
- Moat that does exist: patent portfolio, priority spectrum, and genuine MNO partnerships (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Google). That is real and worth something. But spectrum doesn't launch satellites.

### 6. Short Thesis Summary — **Is there a credible bear case? Yes, a strong one.**

The bear case is not "fraud." It is: **a company with $143M of TTM revenue (96x EV/sales on ~$15.1B EV) that burns ~$1.8B of FCF a year, funds itself entirely with dilutive equity and debt, recognizes revenue it cannot collect (DSO 224 days), reports gross margins that don't reconcile between filings, flatters its P&L with non-cash derivative gains, and is losing a capital race against the best-funded company on earth — while its CEO monetized $147M weeks before admitting a launch delay.**

**The other side, stated fairly:**
- Revenue is genuinely inflecting: Q3'26 **+187% YoY**, and contracts are real (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Google, US government).
- **$2.8B of cash is a real multi-year cushion** — this is not an imminent-solvency short. Altman "distress" is distorted by convert-driven book leverage. Interest coverage is currently fine.
- **57.7% insider ownership** (172.9M of 299.8M shares) is genuine alignment, not a group of dumpers.
- **The 2030 analyst case ($3.64B revenue, $971M net income) is not absurd if you believe the launch cadence** — at 3.8x 2030 sales, the stock is not expensive *against that scenario*. But that 2030 number comes from **one analyst**; 2028's six-analyst estimate of $1.648B revenue implies 11.5x growth in two years, and its own $0.104 EPS is internally inconsistent with its −$73.5M net income.
- **The squeeze history is brutal:** ASTS went from ~$30 to $133 (4.4x) in 15 months. Beta 2.85, retail-heavy. Shorting this name pre-collapse destroyed bears.
