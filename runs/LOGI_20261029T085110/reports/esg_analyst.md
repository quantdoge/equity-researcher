## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (managed, improving)

**Positives (verifiable, third-party reviewed):**
- **Scope 1+2: −49% to −53%** vs 2019 baseline (FY25: −53%; FY26: −49% — the company appears to have restated/rebased, a minor disclosure-quality flag).
- **Scope 3: −33%** vs 2021 baseline; **200,000+ tCO2e avoided** in FY26.
- **81% of products** made with post-consumer recycled plastic (78% in FY25, ~75% FY24) — genuine, measurable, and ahead of most hardware peers.
- **100% of in-scope products carry a third-party-reviewed Product Carbon Footprint** — best-in-class granularity, and strategically important because it lets B2B customers (a growing channel) count Logitech purchases in their own CSRD Scope 3.
- **69 product lines** with iFixit spare parts/repair guides (from ~23 two years ago); PVC-free lines 44% → **55%**; 2,500t of packaging removed over 3 years.
- SBTi-aligned targets: near-zero Scope 1+2 by 2030, >50% Scope 3 cut by 2030.

**Residual risk:**
- **Carbon intensity is structurally high for the sector.** Gross footprint plausibly **~1.0–1.3 MtCO2e** against ~$4.7bn revenue ≈ **~210–275 tCO2e per $1m revenue** *(analyst estimate — Logitech does not disclose a consolidated absolute total in the retrievable excerpts; treat as directional)*. Absolute emissions still fall far short of the stated 2030 goal; the −33% Scope 3 figure rests on a 2021 baseline that itself reflected COVID-inflated logistics, which flatters the trajectory.
- **Circularity is design-led, not business-model-led.** No material refurbished/resale revenue at scale; growth in gaming and AI-era peripherals increases unit volumes and thus absolute embedded carbon — decoupling is not yet demonstrated.
- **No evidence of material environmental enforcement, spills, or remediation liabilities.** No CDP A-list confirmation located — gap.
- **Physical climate risk: Low** (fabless model, diversified contract manufacturing across China, Vietnam, Malaysia, Mexico, Thailand, Taiwan).

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Supply chain labour:** Logitech audits suppliers against the **RBA Code of Conduct**, runs forced-labour-specific audit protocols, and has a published Statement on Human Rights, Labor and Supply Chain Due Diligence. **No publicly reported forced-labour finding, UFLPA detention, or supplier delisting located** — a genuine positive versus apparel/EV-battery peers. Residual exposure: concentrated China manufacturing base means US tariff/forced-labour policy is a live lever (see §5).
- **Product safety:** Battery-bearing audio products (Ultimate Ears, Jaybird) create recall tail risk; no large recall found in the window.
- **Workforce:** ~7,300 FTEs; CEO transition and a multi-year restructuring create turnover/morale risk at the management layer. Gender-diversity metrics could not be independently verified in this session — **gap; reliance on self-reported databook only**.
- **Community/social licence:** Low-footprint consumer brand; no significant community controversies.

**Net:** Social risk is *contained but thin on evidence*. The one live item is consumer-facing conduct, not labour.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **5.5 / 10** (the weak link)

**Structural positives:**
- **Single class of shares** — one share, one vote. No dual-class control structure. Rare and valuable in a founder-influenced tech name.
- **Independent Chairperson separate from CEO** (Wendy Becker through Sept 2025; **Guy Gecht elected Chair Sept 2025**). Board overwhelmingly independent by Swiss standard.
- **Annual say-on-pay** adopted at the 2023 AGM at shareholder request — responsiveness signal.
- Compensation via Swiss "prospective" binding votes on aggregate board/executive pay, giving shareholders real teeth.

**Material governance negatives:**
1. **The 2024 founder–board war.** Co-founder Daniel Borel publicly demanded Chair Becker's immediate replacement, nominated Guy Gecht, and **took Logitech to a Swiss court — which enjoined the company from issuing its AGM invitation** over exclusion of his shareholder proposal. Becker was nonetheless re-elected (~86% approval), then departed a year later and Borel's nominee became Chair. Financial read-through: a low direct cost, but it consumed board bandwidth during a CEO transition and signals a **contested control dynamic with a large legacy holder** that can recur at any AGM.
2. **Leadership churn.** Long-time CEO Bracken Darrell departed mid-2023; interim CEO (Gecht); new CEO Faber from Jan 2024; chair turnover in 2025. Three years of discontinuous leadership at a company executing a margin-recovery and portfolio-reset plan. **This is the single most valuation-relevant ESG factor at LOGI.**
3. **Compensation alignment is financial-only.** Pay is tied to revenue/EPS/cash generation; **no meaningful ESG or capital-efficiency-linked metrics identified.** Given the company's own Scope 3 dependence and repairability regulatory wave, this is a misalignment.
4. **Swiss-law minority protections** are structurally weaker than US/UK (no proxy access, no special-meeting right at low thresholds, capital-band authorisations). The Borel episode demonstrated the limits of constructive engagement.
5. **Transparency:** FY25 Non-Financial Matters Report is board-approved under the Swiss Code of Obligations (art. 964a–964c) — legitimate, but that standard is lighter than CSRD/ESRS, and absolute-emissions totals are hard to extract.

**Verdict:** Not a governance scandal — an **unsettled** governance profile. 5.5/10 reflects good structure (independent chair, one-share-one-vote, binding pay votes) offset by contestability and churn.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**Why not Positive:** Governance is the binding constraint. A company mid-way through a margin-recovery and portfolio-reset plan, with a contested founder shareholder, a chair installed by that founder, and a CEO only 2.5 years into the job, carries a **higher cost of equity than its operational ESG quality would justify.** Add the $61m tariff-refund class action — which lands squarely on the earnings line just as FY26 results were flattered by that same refund — and the near-term risk skew is unfavourable. Comp/ESG alignment is absent, so capital allocation will not be disciplined by sustainability considerations.

**Why not Negative:** The environmental programme is real, third-party verified, and — crucially — **commercially load-bearing**. Product Carbon Footprints on 100% of target products and 81% recycled plastic content are exactly what enterprise customers need as CSRD Scope 3 obligations bite. That converts "E" from a cost centre into a B2B sales qualification advantage in the video-collaboration and peripherals channel. There are no forced-labour findings, no environmental enforcement, no dual-class structure, and an independent chair.

**What would move the call to POSITIVE:** (a) settlement or dismissal of the tariff-refund action without material charge; (b) two consecutive clean AGMs with no founder contest and no further C-suite turnover; (c) a disclosed absolute emissions pathway consistent with the >50% Scope 3 2030 target on a restated, consistent baseline; (d) introduction of a repairability/circularity metric into executive comp.

**What would move it to NEGATIVE:** an adverse ruling or regulator inquiry on the tariff pass-through (consumer-protection optics are unusually bad); renewed founder litigation; or an ESPR/DPP compliance failure on a major product line.

**Bottom line for the memo:** ESG is **not** the reason to own or avoid LOGI — but governance instability, not carbon, is the factor most likely to turn an ESG issue into an earnings issue within 12 months. Underwrite the $61m exposure and the AGM-contest optionality explicitly.
