## LOGITECH INTERNATIONAL S.A. (LOGI) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $105.16 (Oct 15, 2026) | Market cap ~$15.6B | Net cash $1.66B | EV ~$13.9B | FY ends March*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW** (widening modestly)

Logitech designs and distributes PC peripherals (mice, keyboards, webcams), gaming gear (Logitech G, ASTRO, sim racing), audio (Ultimate Ears, Blue), and video-conferencing hardware. It is an asset-light Swiss-domiciled, USD-reporting designer with outsourced Asian manufacturing.

**Sources of advantage — and their limits:**
- **Brand + design (intangible asset):** Logitech G and MX are genuine consumer brands with pricing power in a category where most competitors are anonymous. Gross margin of 43% in a hardware category is evidence.
- **Scale cost advantage:** ~$4.9B revenue and shelf-space dominance at Best Buy/Amazon/retail gives it unit-cost and distribution advantages over fragmented Asian competitors.
- **Efficient scale in niche adjacencies:** simulation racing wheels, conference-room cameras (Rally), and enthusiast mice are market sizes too small to attract a Microsoft or Samsung but large enough to support Logitech's #1 position.
- **Switching costs: low.** G Hub / Logitech Options software creates mild friction, but a mouse is a mouse. This is the ceiling on the moat.

**Moat direction:** *Widening.* ROIC has risen from 19.7% (FY23) → 25.4% → 29.5% → 34.1% (FY26). Non-GAAP gross margin hit a record 43.6% and operating margin 18.8% in FY26, and the company has now posted 10 consecutive quarters of constant-currency revenue growth. Management has exited underperforming acquired brands (Jaybird sold 2023, ASTRO sold 2024) rather than defend them — a sign of rational focus.

**Verdict:** A good business, not a great one. Predictable demand, high returns on capital, and a defended brand — but exposed to consumer hardware cycles, Asian commoditization, and tariff policy. Outside the "certainty" category Buffett would demand for a wide moat, but comfortably inside the circle of competence.

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **8.5 / 10**

| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 4,539 | 4,298 | 4,555 | 4,862 |
| Gross margin | 38.2% | 41.6% | 43.3% | 43.1% |
| Operating margin (GAAP) | 10.1% | 13.7% | 14.4% | 16.2% |
| ROIC | 19.7% | 25.4% | 29.5% | **34.1%** |
| ROE | 16.2% | 27.4% | 29.7% | 32.1% |
| FCF ($M) | 442 | 1,089 | 786 | 976 |
| FCF / Net income | 121% | 178% | 125% | **137%** |

**Strengths:**
- **ROIC of 34% vs. WACC ~9%** — a 25-point spread, and rising four years running. This is the single most important data point: it proves the moat is real and being exploited.
- **FCF conversion of 137%** — exceptional. Capex is only 1.3% of revenue (asset-light), D&A exceeds capex, and working capital throws off cash.
- **Balance sheet:** $1.75B cash vs. $89M debt = **$1.66B net cash (11% of market cap)**. Current ratio 2.2x. No refinancing risk whatsoever.
- **Owner earnings:** $730M in FY26 (NI + D&A − capex), up from $373M in FY23 — a 25% CAGR.

**Deductions:**
- **Revenue is cyclical and was COVID-distorted.** FY22 peak $5.48B, then −17.2% (FY23) and −5.3% (FY24). FY26 revenue is *still 11% below the FY22 peak.* Growth is mid-single-digit at best (FY26 +6.7%, but FY27 tracking +3-5%).
- **SBC of $112M** (~1.9% of market cap annually) is a real cost that flatters reported FCF.
- **Working-capital swings** make single-year FCF volatile ($209M in FY22 vs. $1,089M in FY24). Four-year average FCF is ~$826M — use that, not the FY26 peak.
- **Tariff exposure.** China/Vietnam/Malaysia/Mexico/Thailand/Taiwan manufacturing. Management guided a 700–950bp gross-margin hit in April 2025; mitigation (pricing, supply-chain shift) largely worked, and Q1 FY27 included a $61M tariff *refund*. But this is a live, unresolved structural risk.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples (on $105.16, 148.2M diluted shares):**
- P/E: 21.9x FY26 GAAP ($4.80); ~19x FY27E GAAP (~$5.50); ~17x FY27E non-GAAP (~$6.15)
- EV/EBITDA: ~15.3x (FY26 $908M)
- EV/FCF: **14.3x**; FCF yield **6.3%** (7.4% on 4-yr average FCF)
- EV/non-GAAP operating income: ~15x
- Dividend yield ~1.6% ($1.65/share, 34% payout) + ~3% net buyback yield

**DCF / Owner-Earnings Intrinsic Value** (10-year, WACC 9.0%, terminal growth 2.5%, net cash added):

| Scenario | Base FCF | Growth | IV/share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative (owner earnings net of SBC) | $790M | 3.5% | **~$97** |
| Base (normalized FCF, 4-yr avg ~$826M+) | $900M | 4.5% | **~$122** |
| Optimistic (FY26 peak FCF) | $976M | 5.0% | **~$137** |

**Intrinsic value range: $100 – $125, midpoint ~$113.**

At $105.16 the shares trade at a **~7% discount to the midpoint** and essentially *at* the conservative end of the range. **There is no margin of safety here.** My required entry for a 25–30% discount to the $113 midpoint is **$79–85**. Even a modest 20% discount implies ~$90.

**What the price implies:** ~4% perpetual FCF growth at a 9% discount rate. That is a *reasonable* assumption — not a pessimistic one being gifted to you. The market is pricing LOGI approximately correctly.

**Cross-check on sell-side dispersion:** targets range from $90 (Morgan Stanley, Underweight) to $143 (Berenberg), with Barclays at $101 and Wedbush at $130. When the Street is that dispersed, the honest answer is "fairly valued." The stock also shows a persistent pattern of double-digit single-day gaps around earnings (Jan 2026 −8%, Jul 2026 −8%) — the market has not settled on a multiple.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Rating: Good (B+)**

- **CEO Johanna (Hanneke) Faber** (since 2023, ex-Coca-Cola) has delivered: record non-GAAP margins, ten straight growth quarters, disciplined cost control, and a clear strategic focus on gaming, video collaboration, and personal workspace.
- **Capital allocation is genuinely excellent.** FY26: $537M buybacks + $233M dividends = $770M returned against $976M FCF (79% payout). Diluted shares have fallen from 170.4M (FY22) to 148.2M (FY26) — a **13% reduction in four years, ~3.2%/yr**, done at prices far below today's. That is value-accretive.
- **Dividend grown from $0.97 to $1.65 since 2022** — 14% CAGR, conservative 34% payout.
- **M&A record is the blemish.** Blue Microphones ($117M), Jaybird ($50M), and ASTRO ($85M) all underperformed and were largely written off or divested. To their credit, management has since stopped empire-building — but the historical evidence of capital-destruction on acquisitions is real, and a large deal would be a red flag.
- **Insider ownership** is modest (~1-2%); the founding Borel family retains a board presence. Alignment is adequate, not exceptional.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

Logitech is a high-quality, cash-generative, net-cash business with a narrow-but-widening moat, 34% ROIC, and shareholder-friendly management. It clears every quality hurdle I care about.

It fails the *price* hurdle. At $105 the stock sits inside my intrinsic-value range ($100–125) rather than at a discount to it. A 6.3% FCF yield and 17x forward non-GAAP earnings is a fair price for a mid-single-digit-growth hardware company with tariff and cyclical exposure — not a bargain. **A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy.**

**Action:**
- **Existing holders: hold.** Do not sell a 34%-ROIC compounder with $1.66B net cash at 14x EV/FCF; the compounding and buyback will do the work.
- **New money: wait.** Build a position at **$88–92** (20–25% MOS to midpoint), and buy aggressively below $85. That level was available as recently as June 2026 ($92.56) and repeatedly in 2025 ($63–75 during the tariff panic).
- **Revisit if:** FY27 non-GAAP EPS tracks above $6.50 without tariff-refund help, or the stock de-rates below 15x EV/FCF on normalized earnings.

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Tariffs (the largest, most binary risk).** Multi-country Asian manufacturing with heavy US exposure. April 2025 guidance was for a 700–950bp gross-margin hit; mitigation worked, but a renewed or expanded tariff regime could compress margins far faster than pricing can offset. FY26's record 43.6% non-GAAP gross margin is *not* the through-cycle norm.
2. **Revenue is not reliably compounding.** FY26 revenue remains 11% below the FY22 peak, and FY27 growth has decelerated to ~3-5% (Q1 +4.7%, Q2 +2.8%). A mature PC-installed-base replacement cycle is the engine, and it is slow.
3. **Commoditization.** Chinese competitors (Razer, Keychron, Ajazz, and unbranded Amazon brands) are closing the design gap at half the price. Gross margin has never exceeded 45% in a decade — there is a hard ceiling on pricing power.
4. **One-time earnings distortion.** The $61M tariff refund inflated Q1 FY27 non-GAAP operating income ($290M reported vs. $229M ex-refund, +14%). Excluding it, growth was solid but far less spectacular than the headline +44%. Investors extrapolating Q1 will be disappointed.
5. **FX.** Reports in USD, costs partly in CNY/EUR, cash partly in CHF. Dollar strength compresses reported results.
6. **Capital allocation risk.** A return to the Jaybird/ASTRO playbook — a large, expensive acquisition — would be the clearest reason to reduce a position.
7. **Cyclicality of gaming.** Logitech G is a discretionary consumer category that sells off hard in a downturn, as FY23 (−17% revenue) demonstrated.

---

**Bottom line:** Wonderful business, fair price, no safety margin. Hold and wait for Mr. Market to offer the discount he reliably offers this name every 18–24 months. Watchlist at $90; strong conviction buy at $82.
