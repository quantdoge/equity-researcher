## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US tariff regime volatility and the $61M refund clawback — Probability: High (litigation already filed) | Impact: Low–Medium**
- Logitech raised prices on **51% of its portfolio in April 2025**, some by up to **25%**, to offset IEEPA tariffs; the **US Supreme Court struck the IEEPA tariffs in February 2026**.
- Logitech received a **full $61M refund** ($15M in Q1 FY27, $46M in Q2 FY27), booked as a reduction of cost of goods sold — this materially flattered H1 FY27 gross margin (47.2% vs 42.6%).
- Proposed **nationwide class action filed August 18, 2026** in N.D. Cal. (San Jose) — *SJK Development & Ala Awadalla v. Logitech* — seeks disgorgement of the refund and of tariff-justified price increases. Peers sued on the same theory: Microsoft, Nintendo, Sony. Logitech's EULA contains arbitration provisions (Nintendo has moved to compel arbitration); an arbitration motion is the likely defence and would compress the exposure.
- Residual risk: refund proceeds may be non-recurring margin; a further round of tariff re-imposition would force another pricing/lobbying cycle.

**② Middle East conflict and EMEA demand shock — Probability: High (already occurring) | Impact: Medium**
- Q2 FY27: **EMEA net sales –6% y/y**, attributed to market weakness *and the Middle East conflict*, with "no clear timeline for recovery" (Q2 FY27 call). EMEA is ~40% of revenue — the company's highest-growth region in FY26 (+9%), so the reversal is a meaningful momentum break.
- Management cited "geopolitical pressures in the Middle East" alongside component/logistics cost inflation as reasons results only narrowly exceeded outlook (MarketBeat transcript, Oct 27, 2026).

**③ China-centric manufacturing plus semiconductor/component chokepoint — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium-High**
- Logitech is an asset-light designer relying on third-party Asian contract manufacturers; a **single semiconductor supplier's facility was offline and is only now ramping, with capacity still below Logitech's requirements**, expected to constrain product availability into Q3 FY27 (10-Q, Oct 28, 2026). This is a concentration risk with direct revenue consequence and is the principal reason the stock fell >12% post-earnings (Reuters: ~$2B market cap lost).
- Tariff differentials (US effective rates cited ~20% China vs ~6% Vietnam, ~13% Indonesia, ~4.5% Thailand, Reuters Sept 2026) create ongoing pressure to re-source, with transition cost and rules-of-origin/transshipment compliance exposure.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Active litigation:** IEEPA tariff-refund class action (N.D. Cal., Aug 2026) — the only identified material matter; no disclosed DOJ/FTC/SEC investigation, no ITC §337 action, and no patent litigation of note surfaced in the searches.
- **Tariff accounting/refund process:** ongoing with US CBP; $61M already received. Watch for whether any refund is contested or must be reserved pending the class action.
- **EU regulatory stack** (relevant given ~40% EMEA revenue): Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation and battery/energy-efficiency rules, the **EU Forced Labour Regulation (applies from 14 Dec 2027)** requiring supply-chain traceability for Asian contract manufacturing, EU AI Act obligations for AI-enabled features (e.g., Logitech AI/software features and Streamlabs), and GDPR/data-privacy obligations for camera, microphone and software telemetry.
- **Swiss/SIX obligations:** ad hoc publicity (Art. 53 LR) on results and SEC filings; Swiss capital band and conditional capital mechanics; CHF-denominated dividend (CHF 1.36 / ~$1.68 per share, $235M paid in H1 FY27).
- **Supply-chain resilience program:** "Chain Reaction" supplier decarbonization initiative (Oct 23, 2026) — evidence of proactive supplier-ESG management, marginally lowers forced-labour/CBAM-adjacent exposure.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Rationale:** Logitech's franchise risk is not expropriation or sanctions — it is **trade-policy whiplash and supply-chain concentration**. Roughly 60% of revenue sits outside the US, ~40% in an EMEA region now contracting on Middle East conflict, and the entire product line is dependent on Asian (largely China-linked) contract manufacturing and a component supply base that has demonstrably failed once in FY27. The $61M tariff refund episode has converted a macro tailwind into a live, public consumer class action with an unfavourable optics profile, and it inflated H1 FY27 gross margins in a way the market has partly discounted.

**What would move the rating:**
- *Upgrade to Medium* if the class action is compelled to arbitration/dismissed, refund retained, and tariff policy stabilises.
- *Downgrade to High* on: re-imposition of broad tariffs forcing further price actions; a second component-supplier outage; EMEA deterioration spreading to Americas; or any regulatory inquiry into tariff-related pricing disclosures.

**Monitoring list:** N.D. Cal. docket (arbitration motion); CBP refund reconciliation vs. the $61M; H2 FY27 gross margin ex-refund; Q3 FY27 availability impact from the semiconductor supplier; EU Forced Labour Regulation readiness ahead of Dec 2027; EMEA monthly sell-through.
