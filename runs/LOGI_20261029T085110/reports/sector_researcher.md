# Sector Brief — Consumer Peripherals & Computer Hardware (LOGI)

*Data as of 28 Oct 2026; LOGI last close $98.27, market cap $14.1B, beta 0.65. LOGI fiscal year ends 31 March.*

---

## 1. Sector Overview

LOGI sits in the consumer PC peripherals / computer-hardware complex — a roughly $8bn gaming-peripherals pool within a broader ~$40bn+ PC accessory market, structurally growing high-single digits (third-party forecasts cluster at 9–11% CAGR to the mid-2030s), but **currently in the middle of a violent cyclical shock rather than a normal replacement cycle**. The AI-datacenter buildout has absorbed DRAM/NAND supply: Gartner (Feb-2026) forecasts a **~130% surge in combined DRAM+SSD prices by end-2026, pushing average PC prices up ~17%** and cutting 2026 PC/smartphone shipments; IDC and channel data point to **PC shipments down >11% for 2026, with Q3-2026 down ~20% YoY** and notebook/desktop declines steepening into Q4. Because peripherals are an attach-rate business, unit deceleration in PCs transmits into accessory demand with a one-to-two-quarter lag. The market is fragmented and brand-led (LOGI the clear leader in gaming/streaming peripherals at roughly 25–30% share, Corsair ~7–10%, plus Razer, Turtle Beach, SteelSeries and white-label Asian brands), with low capital intensity and low switching costs but meaningful brand, channel and software-ecosystem moats at the premium end.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Premiumisation and gaming/creator mix shift.** Gaming peripherals is the fastest-growing sub-pool (9–11% CAGR); LOGI's Gaming segment did $1.414bn in FY26 (+6%), and Q2 FY27 grew gaming +7%, video collaboration +12%, pointing devices/keyboards +4% — mix is shifting toward higher-ASP, higher-margin SKUs.
- **AI-PC and commercial refresh as a unit catalyst (2027+).** Installed-base ageing plus Windows/AI-PC replacement demand should restore PC unit growth once memory pricing normalises (industry consensus: easing from 2028, partial relief earlier). Peripherals attach rates recover with a lag, giving a multi-year recovery runway from a depressed 2026–27 base.
- **LOGI-specific share capture and cost self-help.** Eleven consecutive quarters of YoY revenue growth through Q2 FY27 *despite* a shrinking PC unit market implies share gains; gross margin of 44.9% in Q2 FY27 is up ~150bps YoY, showing pricing power and supply-chain leverage that smaller peers (Corsair at 28.9% GM, ~0% operating margin) cannot replicate.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Memory supercycle inflating BOM and suppressing category demand.** DRAM/NAND shortages raise costs on memory-bearing products (webcams, conference cams, wireless audio, gaming consoles-adjacent hardware) and, more importantly, raise PC ASPs 17%, which defers consumer accessory spend. Management has already flagged **~$70–80M of supply-constraint-driven sales impact in Q3 FY27**, guiding sales to $1.36–1.43bn (**-4.3% to +0.6% YoY** vs $1.42bn) — i.e., growth stalls.
- **Cost inflation and tariffs compressing margins sequentially.** Gross margin fell from **49.5% in Q1 FY27 to 44.9% in Q2 FY27** — a ~460bp sequential collapse on component/logistics costs and Middle East geopolitical disruption. LOGI's China-centric manufacturing base leaves it exposed to US tariff changes despite ongoing geographic diversification.
- **Consumer electronics price elasticity in a soft macro.** Peripherals are discretionary; with PC prices up double digits, trade-down to value brands and extended replacement cycles are the base case, capping ASP-led growth. FY24 already showed how cyclical this is (revenue -17.2% in FY23, -5.3% in FY24).

## 5. LOGI's Competitive Position — **Strong**

- **Margin structure is the moat, empirically.** +10pp gross and +9pp operating margin premium to the peer median, and a 30-year brand/channel franchise (Logitech G, Blue, Ultimate Ears, Streamlabs software ecosystem). In a hardware category with no switching costs, sustained 43–45% GM is the proof of pricing power.
- **Growth quality beats the peer set.** +6.7% revenue growth in FY26 and 11 straight quarters of YoY growth *while the PC unit market is contracting* — that is share capture, not beta. Contrast HPQ (+3.2% with 20% GM) and HEAR (-14.2%).
- **Balance-sheet and cash-generative profile** (net-cash, buybacks, ~7,300 employees, beta 0.65) means it can absorb a 12–18 month memory shock without strategic retreat — unlike CRSR, which has essentially no operating cushion.
- **Caveat:** the Q3 FY27 guide (-4% to +1%) is the first crack — growth leadership is being overridden by an exogenous supply shock, and the sequential GM erosion (49.5% → 44.9%) suggests the pricing offset is not fully covering input inflation. Watch Q3 (late Jan 2027) for whether GM holds ≥44% and whether the $70–80M supply hit is one-off or extends.

## 6. Sector Rating: **Neutral**

Structurally attractive (fragmented, brand-led, 9–11% secular growth, high margins for leaders) but **cyclically in a trough-to-recovery transition, not early growth**. The 2026–27 memory-driven PC unit collapse is a genuine demand headwind with a visible 4–8 quarter tail, sector margins are compressing, and tariff/geopolitical cost risk is unresolved. Sector-level revenue growth is likely low-single-digit to negative through mid-2027 before the AI-PC refresh and easing memory prices restore the secular trend. Within a Neutral sector, **LOGI is the quality holding** — the right way to own the space is the share-gainer with the margin buffer, not the beta plays.
