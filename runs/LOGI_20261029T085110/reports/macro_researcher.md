## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Risk-off / early-cycle tightening — a "bear-steepening" regime.**

As of 28 Oct 2026, the US is running an unusual combination: **above-trend growth, re-accelerating inflation, a Fed that has resumed hiking, and a long end pinned near 5% on term-premium/fiscal concerns.** The Fed held the target range at 3.75%–4.00% on 28 Oct (two dissents) after a 25bp **hike** in September 2026, ending the easing cycle that had taken the effective rate from 5.33% (Aug 2024) to 3.63% by January 2026. Markets are pricing further tightening — the **2Y at 4.65% sits ~78bp above the effective fed funds rate of 3.75%**, and futures point to a hike at the 8–9 Dec meeting.

Equity/credit internals confirm the transitional character: high-yield OAS at **3.18%** is still near cycle tights but has widened ~25bp off the 2.93% low of 25 Sep, and the broad USD has appreciated **~2.9% since 9 September**. This is a market that is long growth, short duration, and increasingly nervous about inflation.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact — Logitech (LOGI)

LOGI is a Swiss-domiciled, USD-reporting, asset-light hardware designer with manufacturing in Asia, a majority of revenue outside the US (Americas ~45%, EMEA ~32%, APAC ~23% — approximately), and a **net-cash balance sheet**. FY27 Q2 (ended 30 Sep 2026): sales **$1.22bn, +3% (+2% cc)**, adj. EPS **$1.51** vs $1.45, GAAP $1.25, **gross margin 44.9%, +140bp on lower tariff costs** — yet the stock fell **12.1% on 28 Oct to $98.27** on margin/FCF-quality concerns (FCF just $148.9m).

| Macro variable | Direction | Impact on LOGI |
|---|---|---|
| **Tariff regime** | IEEPA tariffs struck down by SCOTUS 20 Feb 2026, replaced by a **10–15% Section 122 surcharge**; ~$175bn of refunds in train | **POSITIVE (realised).** The 140bp GM expansion in Q2 is the clearest macro tailwind the company has. But Sec. 122 is time-limited and Sec. 232/301 actions are the likely successor — the relief is **unstable, not structural**. |
| **Memory / component inflation** | DRAM contract prices +58–63% QoQ in Q2'26, NAND +70–75%; further +40–50% in Q3 and +30–40% in Q4 | **NEGATIVE — the dominant risk.** Management explicitly cited "elevated component and logistics costs" and "supply constraints." A **~245m unit PC market contraction in 2026** also shrinks the attach base for peripherals. This is an AI-capex-driven input shock Logitech cannot fully pass through in a value-oriented category. |
| **Fed policy / discount rates** | Hiking; 10Y ~5% | **MILDLY NEGATIVE.** No refinancing risk (net cash, negligible interest expense) — LOGI is among the *least* rate-fragile tech names. But the multiple is long-duration and 5% risk-free competes with a ~18x forward earnings profile. |
| **USD strength** | +2.9% since 9 Sep | **NEGATIVE, flipping.** Q2 got a ~1pt FX *tailwind* (+3% USD vs +2% cc). A firming dollar converts that to a translation headwind for EMEA/APAC revenue and creates unfavorable USD/CNY, USD/TWD COGS dynamics. |
| **Consumer health** | Unemployment 4.2%; real spending ~+2.3% | **POSITIVE.** Gaming and video-conferencing demand held up; 11th consecutive growth quarter. But rising device prices from memory pass-through is a 2027 elasticity threat in entry-level peripherals. |
| **Long-end yields / fiscal** | 30Y 5.37% | **NEUTRAL/NEGATIVE.** Raises the hurdle for consumer discretionary hardware and signals a "scarcity of goods" inflation impulse per RSM — bad for a volume-driven hardware model. |

**Net:** LOGI is caught in a **margin scissors**: tariff relief (positive, fading) versus memory/logistics inflation (negative, escalating) — and Q2 demonstrated which force the market believes dominates. Its defensive attributes (beta 0.65, net cash, buyback capacity reportedly ~$1.5bn) limit downside versus leveraged peers, but do not create macro upside.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE (mild)**

Justification: the environment delivers a genuine, already-banked tariff tailwind and a healthy consumer — but it also delivers (a) a memory-cost shock concentrated precisely in Logitech's bill of materials, (b) a Fed that has turned back to hiking with the 10Y at 5%, capping the multiple, and (c) a dollar that has reversed from tailwind to headwind. Two of the three forward-looking variables are moving against the company. Not severe enough for "strongly unfavourable" given the fortress balance sheet and pricing power in premium gaming, but the macro tide is out.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario (bear, ~25% probability)

**"Memory-driven stagflation lite."** DRAM/NAND prices rise another 30–40% into Q1 2027 on AI datacenter allocation, forcing Logitech to raise peripheral ASPs 8–12%. Simultaneously the December Fed hike lands with CPI sticky above 4%, pushing the 10Y to **5.4–5.5%** and the dollar another 3–4% higher. Result: volume declines in entry-level mice/keyboards, ~200bp of gross-margin give-back, and a *de-rating* of a hardware name already suspected of low earnings quality (FCF $149m on $1.22bn of sales). A 25–30% drawdown toward the $70s — the level touched intraday on 28 Jan 2026 — is the plausible path; note the stock already round-tripped from $127 (June) to $98.

**Bull mirror (~15%):** the Section 122 surcharge expires without a Section 232/301 replacement *and* memory contract pricing peaks in Q1'27 as new NAND/DRAM capacity lands. Gross margin re-expands above 46%, and a 15–20% re-rating follows. This is the asymmetric upside case the market is not paying for — but it depends on policy and supply, not on Logitech's execution.
