## Global Payments (GPN) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $91.83 (Nov 20, 2026) | Market cap ~$24.3B | EV ~$43B (incl. ~$19B net debt) | Net leverage 3.4x*

---

### 1. Growth Story

Global Payments has just executed the most consequential repositioning in its history: on **January 12, 2026** it closed the **$24.25B acquisition of Worldpay** while simultaneously divesting **Issuer Solutions to FIS for $13.5B** — transforming itself from a diversified acquirer/issuer hybrid into a **pure-play commerce platform** and, per TSG's 2026 rankings, the **largest merchant acquirer in the world (~$2.8T in processing volume)**. The thesis is not hyper-growth; it is **margin-and-EPS compounding off a depressed base**: normalized organic net revenue growth of ~4–5%, ~150bp/yr of adjusted operating margin expansion (42.5% in Q3'26, +220bp), a **synergy target already raised to $650M**, deleveraging from 3.4x to 3.0x, and >$2B of capital return — all against a **~6.7x forward adjusted EPS multiple**. A reported **~$50B takeout interest from GTCR (Worldpay's former PE owner)** adds a hard asset-value floor that the public market has been unwilling to award.

Important reporting note: FY2025 revenue of **$7.71B (-23.8% y/y)** is *not* deterioration — Issuer Solutions is now in discontinued operations. Pre-deal FY2024 revenue was $10.1B; pro-forma combined FY2026 revenue is ~$12.3B.

### 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **Global payments revenue pool:** ~$2.5–2.7T, but growing only **~3% in 2025** (McKinsey Global Payments Report 2026) — the pool itself has decelerated, which caps the multiple the market will pay for volume-led models.
- **Payment processor market:** ~**$71B (2026) → $122B by 2031, ~11.4% CAGR** (Mordor) — the software/processing slice grows far faster than the fee pool because of outsourcing and embedded payments.
- **GPN's realistic SAM:** mid-market and enterprise merchant acquiring + **vertical software/integrated payments** (restaurants, retail, healthcare, education, B2B) + global e-commerce (Worldpay is the largest e-comm acquirer by volume). Roughly **$150–200B** of addressable revenue.
- **Penetration:** #1 globally by processed volume (~$2.8T) yet **~4–5% of the global payments revenue pool** — enormous headroom in principle, but the incremental share is being captured by **Stripe (#4 and rising, approaching $1T volume) and Adyen** in digital/e-commerce, not by incumbents. GPN's edge is *distribution at scale* (26,000 employees, ISO/partner channel, 4M+ merchant relationships, Worldpay's global acquiring footprint) rather than proprietary technology leadership.
- **Innovation moat assessment: MODERATE-TO-WEAK.** Data network effects are real but not proprietary; the moat is switching costs in embedded vertical software and scale economics in acquiring. This is a scale-and-distribution franchise, not a disruptive-innovation franchise.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Reported ~$50B GTCR take-private exploration (Bloomberg, Nov 20, 2026)** — with other PE firms reportedly involved. At ~2x the current market cap, this is the single largest re-rating catalyst; the stock jumped up to 14% intraday on the report. *Timeline: weeks-to-quarters; binary and speculative.*
2. **Q4/FY2026 results — February 17, 2027** — first full-year Worldpay integration print plus **initial FY2027 guidance**. Key tells: normalized growth holding >4%, potential upgrade to the 5%+ handle, and FY27 synergy/leverage targets.
3. **Synergy step-ups** — run-rate expense synergies raised $600M → **$650M** in Q3'26; further raises are the highest-probability positive surprise mechanism through 2027 (each $50M ≈ $0.15–0.18 of EPS).
4. **Deleveraging to 3.0x by end-2027** (from 3.4x) — supports a **credit-rating/outlook upgrade** (Fitch BBB affirmed) and removes the discount the market applies to the balance sheet.
5. **Capital return acceleration** — >$2B targeted in 2026 ($1.4B returned YTD), with buybacks at 6.7x forward EPS being highly accretive; dividend $0.25/qtr.
6. **Further portfolio pruning** — remaining non-core assets (Payroll/HCM, Business & Consumer/Netspend) are logical divestiture candidates to simplify the story toward pure merchant commerce.

### 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (asymmetric GARP, not a "Strong Buy" on growth criteria)

I am deliberately not calling this a Strong Buy, because under a strict disruptive-growth lens GPN fails the first test: **organic growth is 4–5%, not 15–25%**, and the TAM's own growth rate has slowed to ~3%. The 17.5% EPS CAGR is manufactured from synergies, margin expansion, deleveraging and buybacks — durable, but financial-engineering-led.

What makes it a **Buy** is the asymmetry: **6.7x FY26E adjusted EPS, 5.7x FY27E, 0.4 PEG, 0.93x book, 100% beat rate, +16.6% upward revision momentum, and a reported ~$50B take-private probe against a $24.3B market cap.** The market is pricing GPN as a structurally declining legacy acquirer; the evidence (Q3'26 sequential acceleration to >4%, +220bp normalized margin, raised synergies) says it is a stabilizing, cash-generative #1-scale platform. Fair value on 9–10x FY27E ($16.23) is **$146–162**; even a 7.5x multiple gets $122. Median street target ~$106 understates the takeout optionality.

**Position sizing implication:** this is a 2–3 year re-rating/value-unlock trade with a takeover kicker, not a compounder to hold for a decade.

---

### 7. Key Risks

1. **Adjusted-vs-GAAP gap is extreme.** FY26E adjusted EPS ~$13.70 vs. GAAP running ~$4.2–4.5 (Q3'26 GAAP $1.06). The "cheap" 6.7x rests on ~$1.4B/yr of intangible amortization and restructuring add-backs. On GAAP earnings this is ~20x. Any shift in how investors capitalize software/intangibles hits the multiple hard.
2. **Worldpay integration and dis-synergy risk.** Worldpay was a chronically share-losing asset under PE ownership (~1–2% growth). Revenue attrition, client/ISO defection, and product-platform duplication could erase the $650M synergy story. This is the #1 execution risk.
3. **Leverage and interest burden.** ~$19B net debt at 3.4x; 2025 interest expense $650M and rising materially post-deal. Floating-rate exposure plus any downturn in volumes pressures the 3.0x target and the buyback.
4. **Structural share loss.** Stripe and Adyen are taking e-commerce share on superior product velocity; McKinsey's +3% pool growth means GPN's 4–5% requires *taking* share in a decelerating market. Pricing compression in acquiring is relentless.
5. **The takeover may not happen.** GTCR "weighing" a ~$50B bid is unconfirmed exploration; financing markets, antitrust (GPN + Worldpay concentration), and GTCR's own recent exit make a deal far from assured. If it evaporates, expect retracement toward the ~$83 pre-report level.
6. **Guidance conservatism cuts both ways.** A 7/7 beat record with tiny +2% average surprises suggests heavily sandbagged guidance — but also that upside may already be embedded in the FY27 $16.23 consensus.
7. **FX and Latin America/APAC macro** exposure is unhedged in places and can distort the "normalized constant-currency" narrative the whole thesis relies on.

---

**Bottom line:** GPN is the rare case where a scale-leading, cash-machine incumbent is priced for obsolescence while its fundamentals are inflecting upward — but classify it honestly as **deep-value-plus-catalyst**, not disruptive growth. Buy, with the $105–115 range as the base-case 12-month target and $137–160 as the bull case (multiple normalization or takeout).
