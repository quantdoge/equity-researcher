### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Hawkish Reflation / Transitional Risk-Off in Duration**

The US is in an unusual and hostile-for-valuations regime: **real growth is reaccelerating while inflation is reaccelerating**, and the Fed has been forced back into a hiking bias after a 2025 easing cycle. Real GDP grew **+3.4% SAAR in Q3'26** (vs +2.2% Q2, +2.5% Q1), unemployment is **4.1%**, yet headline CPI has climbed from **2.65% YoY (Dec'25) to ~3.8% (Oct'26)** and core PCE is stuck at **2.8–3.0%**. The FOMC **held at 3.75–4.00% on 28 Oct** on a 10–2 vote, but the 18 Nov minutes showed "almost all" officials expect further hikes, and Governor Cook dissented *in favour of* a hike.

The bond market has repriced violently. The **10Y went from a 4.38% low (29 Jun) to 5.31% (5 Oct)** and sits at **5.04%**, with the **30Y at 5.47%** and the **2Y at 4.74%** — i.e. ~80bp of additional hikes priced. The curve is **+39bp, bear-steepening on term premium**, not signalling recession. Critically, **credit is not stressed**: BAA spreads are **1.56%** (tight) and credit-card delinquencies *improved* to **2.85%** (Q2'26 from 3.08%). Meanwhile the **broad dollar has rallied 4.0% since 9 September** (117.88 → 122.65).

**Verdict: strong nominal economy + easy credit + expensive money.** This is benign for earnings, toxic for leveraged long-duration equity and for cross-border revenue translation.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact — GPN

GPN is a **levered (3.4x net leverage, post-Worldpay), internationally exposed, nominal-spend-linked acquirer**. That combination creates four live transmission channels:

**a) Interest rates / refinancing — NEGATIVE (the dominant channel)**
Q3'26 net leverage was **3.4x** on ~$18–19bn of net debt against a $24.3bn market cap, with a **3.0x target by end-2027** and $6.3bn liquidity. A BBB/BAA issuer refinancing into a **6.6% BAA yield** and a **5.0% 10Y** — versus coupons struck in a 4%-and-below world — faces structurally higher interest expense exactly as it tries to delever while returning ~$7.1bn. Every 100bp on $5–6bn of rolling maturities is roughly $0.15–0.20 of EPS, manageable but a persistent drag. More important is the **discount-rate effect**: GPN trades at only **~6.5x annualized Q3 EPS ($3.58)** precisely because the market capitalizes high leverage at high rates. **A 66bp rise in the 10Y is roughly a 10% de-rating on a 7x EV/EBITDA multiple** — the stock's biggest macro vulnerability, and the reason its low multiple offers no shelter.

**b) Consumer & nominal volume — POSITIVE on nominal, mixed on real**
GPN's revenue is largely ad valorem (bps of payment volume), so **3.8% CPI mechanically inflates ticket sizes into revenue**. Nominal GDP is compounding ~5%+ (2.1% real + ~3% deflator), with 4.1% unemployment and improving delinquencies supporting transaction volumes. This is why Q3 printed +10% adj EPS growth with 220bp margin expansion. **But** a 3.8% cost-of-living squeeze with real rates positive is eroding discretionary SMB/restaurant spend and hits the **Netspend / Business & Consumer Solutions** underbanked customer base hardest — the segment most exposed to a low-income consumer under inflation.

**c) US dollar — NEGATIVE**
With the Fed hiking while most DM central banks hold or ease, dollar strength is likely to extend. GPN generates roughly a quarter of revenue outside the US (UK, Spain, Central Europe, APAC). **A 4% dollar move ≈ 1.0–1.2pt of reported revenue and ~1pt of EPS drag**, partly offset by local cost bases and hedging. Expect management to flag FX as a 2027 guidance headwind.

**d) Credit conditions / float — NEUTRAL to modestly POSITIVE**
Tight BAA spreads (1.56%) and $6.3bn liquidity mean **no refinancing wall** — this is the key mitigant and the difference between GPN and a genuine credit story. Higher short rates also lift **interest income on settlement float and Netspend customer balances** (a small, underappreciated tailwind). Tariff pass-through raises merchant input costs, a mild second-order volume risk.

**e) The M&A wildcard** — the stock's 2026 action (+23% YTD, from $62.06 on 10 Jun to $101.59 on 10 Nov) is driven by **CD&R/GTCR take-private interest (~$40bn)**, not macro. GTCR's *first* effort reportedly ended and was revived on 20 Nov — and at a 5.04% 10Y and 6.6% BAA yield, **LBO financing math is the binding constraint on the premium**.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE** (mild)

Not because of recession risk — growth, labor and credit are all healthy — but because **the two macro variables that matter most to GPN's equity value (long rates and the dollar) are both moving against it simultaneously**, while the offsetting support (nominal volume growth) accrues to the income statement rather than the multiple. A 3.4x-levered, FX-exposed, 6.5x-multiple acquirer is structurally short duration in a bear-steepening, dollar-strengthening, hawkish-Fed regime. The saving grace is that GPN's **low absolute multiple and tight credit spreads cap the downside** — there is far less valuation air to come out than in a high-multiple compounder.

### 5. Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BEAR: the financing window closes on the take-private bid**

The stock carries a visible M&A premium: it fell to **$88.19 on 12 Nov** and **$83.52 on 19 Nov** on deal doubt, then jumped **+10% to $91.83 on 20 Nov** when GTCR refiled. That premium is a **rate bet in disguise.**

If the Fed delivers the ~80bp the curve prices — say to **4.50–4.75% by mid-2027** — the 10Y breaks **5.50%** and BAA yields approach **7%**. At that cost of capital, a $40bn LBO with Worldpay integration still incomplete (leverage 3.4x, 3.0x only by end-2027) becomes unfinanceable, and the bid is withdrawn *permanently* rather than repriced. GPN would then de-rate to standalone value — **$70–75, a 20–25% drawdown from spot** — with no earnings miss required. The confirming tells to watch: BAA–10Y widening from 1.56% toward 2.0%, another "effort ended" headline, and further flattening of the 2Y/10Y as the market shifts from pricing hikes to pricing growth damage.

*(Mirror-image bull: CPI rolls over on shelter/energy base effects in Q1'27, the Fed's hike bias is abandoned, the 10Y returns to 4.40% and the dollar gives back its 4% — GPN re-rates to 8x on cheaper money and a competitive auction pushes the stock toward $120+. That is the smaller-probability tail given the Nov minutes.)*
