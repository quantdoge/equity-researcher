# Global Payments Inc. (NYSE: GPN) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price: $91.83 (close 20-Nov-2026) · Diluted shares ~265M · Market cap ~$24.4B · Net debt (9/30/26) $18.1B · EV ~$42.5B*

## 0. Critical context: this is not the same company as the 2025 filings

GPN has been transformed in the last 18 months, and any analysis anchored on the FY2025 10-K is obsolete:

- **Sold Issuer Solutions to FIS** for $13.5B EV ($12.0B net) — closed January 2026.
- **Acquired Worldpay** (from FIS/GTCR) for $24.25B EV — closed Q1 2026, financed with debt, the Issuer proceeds, and GPN stock. FIS's remaining 45% Worldpay stake was subsequently monetised into GPN shares.
- **Result:** revenue line discontinuity (FY2025 $7.71B → run-rate ~$12.6B), goodwill/intangibles now **$45.6B**, diluted shares up from 240M to ~265M, and a Q1-2026 GAAP loss of $1.80B (−$7.49/sh) from deal-related charges/impairment.
- Current operating footprint: **Merchant Solutions (incl. Worldpay) + B2B/embedded payments**. The old "three-segment" description in the company profile no longer applies.

FY2026 guidance (reaffirmed at Q3): **adjusted net revenue growth ~4–5% normalized constant-currency, adjusted operating margin ~42.5% (Q3), adjusted EPS $13.60–$13.80, >$2B of capital returned.** Q3-26 adjusted EPS $3.58 (+10%), GAAP EPS $1.06.

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW (and narrowing)**

**What is genuinely good:**
- **Scale and network position.** A top-3 global merchant acquirer processing trillions of dollars across 175+ countries. In acquiring, scale lowers per-transaction cost and funds product investment — a real cost advantage at the margin.
- **Switching costs are real, if modest.** Once embedded in a merchant's POS/ISV software, pharmacy system, or vertical SaaS, displacement is painful. This produces sticky, high-retention revenue and explains 42%+ adjusted operating margins on a very large base.
- **Predictable, fee-based revenue.** No credit risk of consequence, no inventory, negative working capital dynamics in normal periods. Volume-linked revenue is highly recurring and diversified across ~4M+ merchant relationships.

**Why the moat is only narrow:**
- **The industry's economics are being competed away.** Stripe, Adyen, Block/Square, Toast, Shift4 and PayPal's Braintree have taken the high-growth integrated and enterprise segments. Worldpay itself was a *share loser* for years — that is precisely why FIS/GTCR sold it. GPN is buying scale in a segment with secular price compression (~1–3% per year in core acquiring), not buying pricing power.
- **ROIC ≈ WACC is the tell.** Reported ROIC has been 3.2% (2025), 5.7% (2024), 5.4% (2023). Even on management's adjusted basis — ~$3.6B adjusted net income on ~$42B of invested capital (equity $24.0B + net debt $18.1B) — the cash return on total capital is roughly **7–8.5%**, i.e. at or below a ~9% cost of capital. A business that cannot earn clearly above its cost of capital on the capital it deploys does not have a moat in the Buffett sense, however sticky its customers are.
- **The moat has narrowed over five years.** In 2021 GPN earned adjusted EPS of ~$9 and traded at ~$200. Organic growth has decelerated to 4–5%, and the company has had to buy growth (ActiveNetwork, EVO, now Worldpay) rather than compound organically.

**Circle-of-competence check:** the payments value chain is understandable. But the *reported* economics are not — a ~$11/share gap between GAAP EPS (~$2.5) and adjusted EPS ($13.70), serial portfolio churn, and a $45.6B intangible balance make this one of the more opaque "quality" stories in financials.

**Strengths:** high margins, fee-based revenue, non-cyclical volume growth, and a large recurring revenue base. Leverage is manageable at ~2.9x with $5.6B of cash.

**Weaknesses that matter to a value investor:**
1. **Earnings quality.** The $11/share GAAP-to-adjusted gap is mostly amortization of customer relationships — assets that *do* decay and must be defended with real spending. Adjusted EPS is a legitimate metric here, but it flatters returns on capital.
2. **Cash conversion is the swing factor.** 2026 reported operating cash flow is negative through nine months. Management's "adjusted FCF" strips out deal costs and settlement-timing swings, but until a full clean year is reported, the true owner-earnings number is an estimate, not an observation.
3. **No tangible equity cushion** — 73% of assets are goodwill and intangibles, and Q1-2026 already produced a ~$1.8B GAAP loss. Further impairment risk is non-trivial if Worldpay underperforms.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**What the market is paying:**
- P/E on 2026E adjusted EPS ($13.70 mid): **6.7x**
- P/E on FY2025 GAAP EPS ($5.83, pre-Worldpay): 15.7x — not comparable
- EV/adj. EBITDA (~$6.3B): **~6.7x**
- EV/adjusted FCF (~$3.0B est.): ~14.2x
- Equity FCF yield: **~12.3%** (on estimated normalized adjusted FCF of ~$3.0B)
- P/B: ~1.0x — but tangible book is deeply negative, so this is not a valuation anchor

**Historical and relative context:** GPN traded at 15–30x earnings for most of 2015–2022. It now trades at 6.7x. That de-rating reflects real facts (4–5% organic growth, share loss, leverage, integration risk, adjusted-metric skepticism), not just sentiment. Peer multiples were unavailable from my data feed (the sector screen returned ETFs and money-center banks), so I anchor on GPN's own history and on the DCF below rather than on unverified peer numbers.

**Intrinsic value — owner-earnings DCF (my own build, not the canned tool):**

The tool's default DCF (10-year, 7.8% growth from a 3-year FCF base that is distorted by divestitures) returned ~$200/share — I consider that unusable, because the FCF base and growth rate are both contaminated by the portfolio transformation.

My own base case:
- Normalized adjusted FCF: **$3.0B** (~83% of ~$3.6B adjusted net income; GPN historically converted in the high-80s/low-90s, and I haircut for integration costs)
- Growth: 4% years 1–5 (guidance-consistent), 3% years 6–10, 2.5% terminal
- WACC: 9.0% (levered, cyclical, integration risk)

→ PV of FCFs ≈ $25.1B + PV of terminal value ≈ $28.2B = **EV ~$53B**, less $18.1B net debt = **~$35B equity ≈ $132/share**

**Scenario range:**

| Case | FCF | WACC | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bear (integration slippage, FCF $2.5B, 9.5%) | $2.5B | 9.5% | ~$95 |
| Base | $3.0B | 9.0% | ~$132 |
| Low-growth ($3.0B, 8% growth path) | $3.0B | 9.0% | ~$118 |

**Intrinsic value estimate: ~$115–120/share midpoint, with a plausible range of $95–$135.**

**Current price $91.83 sits ~20–22% below my midpoint**, but only ~4% below the bear case. **The margin of safety is real but thin at the low end — and it depends entirely on a cash-flow number the company has not yet demonstrated it can produce.** That is not the asymmetry I want.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Mixed / C**

- **Leadership:** CEO Cameron Bready (since 2023) and CFO Josh Whipple are credible operators who are executing the Worldpay integration and have *raised* run-rate synergy targets to $650M. Delivery against guidance has been consistent through 2026 (Q1 $2.96 vs $2.78 est., Q2 $3.46 vs $3.44, Q3 $3.58 vs $3.58) — a genuine positive.
- **Capital allocation is the problem.** GPN bought back $2.6B of stock in 2021 and $3.0B in 2022 at average prices well above $110 — permanently destroying capital that would be worth far more today. It then pivoted to a $24.25B Worldpay acquisition at ~14x EBITDA, part-financed with **stock issued at ~$70–80** — near the bottom of the cycle. The Issuer Solutions sale at $13.5B was reasonable; the simultaneous swap of a higher-quality, higher-multiple asset for a lower-growth acquiring asset is a strategic bet I would not have made.
- **Dividend frozen at $1.00/share since 2021** — honest signalling that growth capital and deleveraging take priority, but it means shareholders are not being paid to wait.
- **Insider ownership** is low (typical of a dispersed-ownership S&P 500 company); no meaningful owner-operator alignment. I could not verify current insider ownership or buy/sell activity from available data — treat as a gap.
- **Shareholder returns:** >$2B planned for 2026 (>$1.4B delivered YTD). Repurchases at ~$65–90 are far more defensible than the 2021–22 program.

Net: competent operators, poor long-run capital allocators. That distinction matters enormously for a serial acquirer.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (not a buy at $91.83)

This is a **fair-to-attractive price for a fair business**, not a wonderful business at a wonderful price.

**The bull case is arithmetic, and it is real:** 6.7x adjusted earnings, 6.7x EBITDA, ~12% equity FCF yield, 42%+ margins, a duopoly-scale position in global acquiring, ~$650M of identified synergies, and a stated path to ~2.5x leverage. If management simply hits the reaffirmed $13.60–$13.80 and delevers, a re-rating to 9–10x earnings is $123–$138 — 35–50% upside — without any improvement in the underlying business.
