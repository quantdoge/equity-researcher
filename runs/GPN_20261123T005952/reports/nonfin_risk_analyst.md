# GPN — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**Global Payments Inc.** | Financial Services / Payment Processing (Merchant Acquiring) | Atlanta, GA | 26,000 FTE | Mkt cap ~$24.3B | Price $91.83 | Beta 0.82

> **Context that dominates every risk below:** GPN closed its **$24.25B acquisition of Worldpay on January 9, 2026**, funded partly by selling Issuer Solutions to FIS ($13.5B). It is simultaneously integrating the largest deal in its history, carrying ~$25B of debt (total debt ≈105% of market cap), trading ~19% below its Nov-2024 level despite a 29% YTD-2026 recovery, and is the subject of on-again/off-again private-equity take-private interest (CD&R, GTCR) at a reported ~$40B.

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (weakening at the operating layer)**

**Positives**
- **Independent Chair:** M. Troy Woods (ex-TSYS CEO) holds the Chair separately from CEO Cameron Bready — no CEO/Chairman duality. Genuine separation of power.
- **Board refresh:** Added Patty Watson, Archana Deskus (announced Nov 2025) and Vivek Sankaran (effective Feb 19, 2026) as independent directors — three new independents inside ~6 months, with fintech/technology depth (Deskus = CIO profile), which is directionally what an integration-heavy board needs.
- 12 director nominees at the 2026 annual meeting; disclosure posture is conventional (Section 302 certifications clean on the Q3 2026 10-Q; no restatement, no material weakness disclosed).

**Negatives (material)**
- **Principal Accounting Officer departed on deal-closing day.** CAO David Sheffield (EVP, principal accounting officer) announced retirement effective March 1, 2026 in an 8-K dated **January 9, 2026 — the exact date the Worldpay acquisition closed** — alongside a second senior executive exit (SVP/Chief Administrative Officer). Losing the PAO at the moment a transformative, highly complex purchase-accounting and integration-controls event begins is a textbook control-environment red flag, even when framed as planned succession.
- **TSYS-legacy chair.** The Chair is the former CEO of the company GPN merged with in 2019; combined with a board reshaped by two large transformative deals, there is a real question of whether the board is composed for oversight or for accretion math.
- **Short-tenure independents doing integration oversight** — new directors arrive *after* the Worldpay deal has closed, limiting their ability to have shaped diligence and integration governance.
- **Strategic pressure reduces board optionality:** a board simultaneously evaluating a take-private bid, running integration, and defending a compressed multiple has strong incentive to favour near-term optics (synergy targets, capital returns) over conservative disclosure.
- **Insider alignment unproven:** CEO 2025 total comp ~$17.4M against a stock down 19% over two years — pay-for-performance optics are strained, though 2026 TSR recovery (with the FY guidance cut in Q2 2026) partially mitigates.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **ELEVATED**

**Active/known exposures**
- **NY Attorney General — Netspend (Business & Consumer Solutions):** 2025 settlement requiring >$735K in consumer redress to tens of thousands of New Yorkers plus >$350K in state penalties, over allegedly illegal fees. This is a *consumer-finance* business (prepaid/debit, underbanked) and remains structurally exposed to state AGs and CFPB jurisdiction — an area where enforcement posture can shift abruptly with political cycles.
- **Securities class action (settled):** $3.6M settlement; final approval and **settlement checks mailed May 27, 2026**. Small in dollar terms; the signal is that disclosure quality around prior guidance has already been litigated once.
- **Consumer/privacy class actions:** Illinois BIPA suit over Xenial POS fingerprint capture (filed Apr 2024), 2019 Blue Bear breach litigation. Payment/software businesses carry persistent BIPA and CCPA/GDPR-style exposure via acquired POS software.
- **Merger clearance:** Worldpay cleared the **UK CMA**; no outstanding antitrust condition identified. However, the acquired Worldpay merchant book now sits inside a group with material **EU/UK** operations, importing interchange-cap and PSD3/Durbin-style fee regulation risk across a larger revenue base.
- **EDGAR full-text enforcement search: no GPN SEC enforcement actions returned.** No DOJ investigation disclosed. No pending FDA/FCC-style approval gating revenue.

**Forward risks**
- Post-close **pricing conduct** — Worldpay's new **$699 annual merchant fee** (reported Nov 9, 2026) plus broader repricing invites regulator and AG attention on fee transparency, and is a plausible hook for unfair-practice claims.
- Prepaid/Durbin and CFPB rulemaking on the Netspend franchise; state-level money-transmission licensing as the combined entity scales.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Worldpay integration — the single largest risk.** A $24.25B merchant platform combining two large, culturally distinct sales forces, two acquiring stacks, and overlapping ISV/agent channels, running on a ~$25B debt load. Synergy target has already been raised to **$650M run-rate by end-2028** (from $600M) — good execution optics, but raising targets mid-integration increases the risk that cost is taken out of service quality and controls. **Merchant attrition is already visible as a live concern** (Forbes, Nov 2026), and attrition is the classic value-destroying failure mode in acquiring mergers: revenue walks out faster than synergies come in.
2. **Loss of finance/control leadership at the worst possible moment.** The PAO and Chief Administrative Officer both exited on the closing date. Purchase accounting for a $24B deal, SOX scoping of newly acquired entities, and integration of disparate revenue-recognition and reserve (chargeback/credit-loss) processes all land in the first four quarters post-close — precisely when institutional memory in the controller's organisation is weakest.
3. **Pricing/merchant-relations fragility and platform concentration.** Imposing a new $699 annual fee on legacy Worldpay merchants, while the industry faces broad pricing pressure and competition from Adyen/modern platforms, creates churn and chargeback risk. Add single-supplier/network dependency (Visa/Mastercard rails, AWS-type hosting), a 2012 breach precedent (1.5M cardholders, Visa de-listing) and a payments-specific cyber threat model, and the continuity/reputation tail is real — albeit current external security posture scores well (UpGuard A, 888/950).

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **MODERATE–HIGH**

- **Takeover circus in public.** CD&R and GTCR walked away from a reported ~$40B take-private on Nov 12, 2026 (−12% in a day on 9.9M shares); GTCR then filed with the SEC on **Nov 20** saying it is *still evaluating* a bid, sending shares +9.95%. A company being publicly shopped by PE — twice, with an aborted first effort — signals to merchants, employees and regulators that strategic direction is for sale. This is an acute retention risk for the engineers and salespeople GPN needs to hold through integration.
- **Merchant backlash / consumer-advocacy friction.** Repeated fee introductions post-Worldpay, plus the Netspend AG action, position GPN as a "fee-gouging payments middleman" in regulator and press narratives — a durable reputational frame that is hard to reverse.
- **Credibility of guidance.** Q2 2026 guidance was cut on Middle East conflict travel-volume pressure; Q3 beat with raised synergies. Guidance that swings with geopolitical headlines invites "low-quality beat" skepticism and raises the odds of another securities suit.
- **Shareholder-relations strain:** stock is down ~19% from Nov-2024 levels (~$114 → $91.83), 2026 recovery notwithstanding, in a tape with VIX ~36. Activist/PE attention is a symptom of that.
- **Positives:** no ESG/labour/catastrophic-safety controversy identified; capital returns >$1.4B YTD provide a goodwill offset with the retail/institutional base.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

The financial-engineering and integration risk is concentrated, not diffuse: one enormous, debt-funded, newly closed integration; a finance/control leadership vacuum at the exact date that integration began; live merchant attrition and price-increase backlash; and a board under shareholder-pressure duress from a public PE process. None of these are fraud indicators, and there is no active SEC/DOJ enforcement — which is why this is **High, not Critical**. But the combination of a ~$25B debt stack with an unproven integration and weakened controllership means non-financial risk here is *highly financially reflexive*: an integration stumble transmits directly to covenant headroom and the take-private thesis.
