# AXP (American Express) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Prepared by:** Geopolitical and Legal Research | **Date:** ~Q4 2026 | **Coverage:** geographic exposure, geopolitical risk, regulatory/litigation exposure, compliance

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

American Express is a global payments network (~$204B market cap; FY2024 net revenues ≈ $65.9B) that *issues* cards in roughly **40 countries/territories** and is *accepted* at **190M+ merchant locations worldwide** (acceptance outside the U.S. has more than doubled in four years — Reuters, Sep 28 2026). Revenue is segmented into U.S. Consumer Services, U.S. Commercial Services, and **International Card Services (ICS)** — the fastest-growing segment (billed business +12–15% YoY in 2025, Fortune/hosted Q2-25 10-Q; investing.com Q2-25 slides). Amex does not publish full country-level revenue, so estimates below are derived from segment disclosures.

| Country / Region | Exposure (ind.) | Risk Level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | ~65–70% of revenue | **Medium** | Highest regulatory/political risk (2026 fee/APR attacks — see §3); domestic litigation concentrated here |
| **UK & Continental Europe** | ~15–20% (ICS core) | **Medium** | EU/UK interchange caps (0.3% credit); PSD3/PSR rule rewrites; Amex operates EEA card bank out of Ireland/UK |
| **Japan** | ~5% (large ICS market) | **Low-Med** | Stable; mature premium-card market |
| **India** | <5%, high growth | **Medium** | RBI data-localization restrictions hit Amex directly (2021 new-customer ban; lifted 2023 after compliance — FinTech Alliance) |
| **China** (JV: Express Hangzhou / LianLian DigiTech) | <5%, strategic | **Medium-High** | First foreign network with PBOC clearing license (2019); exposed to US-China decoupling, data regulation |
| **Mexico / LatAm, Australia, Middle East** | ~5% combined | **Medium** | EM FX volatility, currency controls, sanctions-exposure (AML screening burden) |

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Political Risks

**1. U.S. political assault on card economics (interchange + APR + late fees) — Probability: High | Impact: High**
Amex's premium closed-loop model depends on merchant discount fees and high effective interest. In Jan 2026 the U.S. administration proposed a **10% credit-card APR cap** — Amex stock fell ~7% on the news (Yahoo Finance; Reuters Jan 10 2026) and CEO Squeri publicly called it a "downward spiral" (American Banker). Bloomberg (Jan 13 2026) reports the administration is now also **targeting merchant interchange ("swipe") fees** — the direct core of Amex's business. Late-fee rulemaking remains politically contested (Senate letter Jan 2026 urging reinstatement of the $8 cap). Any material cap on interchange or rates would be structurally more damaging to Amex than to Visa/Mastercard.

**2. US–China decoupling / cross-border payment disruption — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium**
Amex was the first foreign network cleared in China (PBOC, 2019) — a strategic growth asset and a hostage of bilateral tension. Data-localization and export-control escalation could constrain JV operations; conversely, Chinese regulatory tightening on foreign networks (mirroring India's playbook) is plausible.

**3. Sanctions & secondary-sanctions environment (Russia legacy + expanding OFAC regimes) — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium**
Amex fully exited Russia/Belarus (2022) and now must ensure no laundering of sanctioned transactions through its global network. Escalating US sanctions (Russia, Iran, Venezuela, and new secondary-sanctions tools) raise screening and reversal costs for a network processing cross-border spend; violations carry reputational and civil-penalty risk.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**U.S. consumer-finance regulation (the dominant legal theme):**
- **CFPB late-fee rule:** The Bureau's 2024 rule capping late fees at **$8** for large issuers (which would have hit Amex's late-fee economics) was **vacated April 15, 2025** by Judge Pittman (N.D. Tex.) on a joint CFPB/industry motion (Nat'l Law Review). The issue is not dead — it is now a political football (2026 Senate pressure to reinstate).
- **2012 CFPB enforcement order:** ~$85M in restitution over deceptive credit-card marketing, billing and debt-collection practices — the standing precedent that Amex's consumer practices rank high on the CFPB's radar.

**Antitrust / interchange litigation (long-running, still active):**
- **In re Payment Card Interchange Fee and Merchant Discount Antitrust Litigation (E.D.N.Y.):** Amex is a defendant alongside Visa/Mastercard (the ~$30B Visa/MC settlements exclude Amex); lawyers told the Brooklyn court (Oct 2025, Bloomberg Law) settlement talks would restart — **unresolved merchant exposure**.
- **Anti-steering litigation:** Amex paid ~$17.5M to settle a federal antitrust class action over anti-steering rules; its anti-steering protections survived the **2018 Supreme Court ruling in *Ohio v. American Express***, but the political environment (Congressional scrutiny of network rules) keeps this front open.
- **DOJ v. Visa (2024)** (debit monopoly) is a peer-network risk: expanded litigation against network practices raises industry-wide regulatory heat.

**Pending legislation:**
- **Credit Card Competition Act** (reintroduced; would mandate card-routing competition) — Amex is one of the most exposed networks and lobbies intensively against it (Senate Judiciary testimony, Nov 2024).
- 2026 "affordability" initiatives (10% APR cap, lower interchange) flagged by Fitch as earnings risks across issuers — **most acute for Amex**.

**International:**
- **India:** RBI blocked new-customer onboarding (2021) over data localisation; lifted 2023 after compliance — a template for data-sovereignty risk across emerging markets.
- **EU/UK:** interchange fee regulation caps and PSD3/PSR rule rewrites constrain monetization of EU-issued cards.
- **China:** PBOC licensing (first foreign network) is a license to operate but ties Amex to Chinese regulatory discretion.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **Data localization / cross-border data flow:** India (resolved, but precedent-setting) and China JV face recurring localization mandates; GDPR/CCPA exposure on a global loyalty platform is material.
- **AML/KYC on a global closed loop:** Amex's proprietary network processes international B2B and corporate spend — a heightened channel for sanctions-evasion screening. No major recent OFAC/FinCEN penalty surfaced in this review (historical orders are consumer-focused), but exposure is structurally elevated.
- **Political-relational risk:** Amex is a marquee, politically visible firm — an attractive target for populist fee/rate policy, as evidenced by the Jan 2026 episodes.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **Medium** (US regulatory pillar: **High**)

**Rationale:** Geopolitically, Amex is a low-asset-international firm with managed exits (Russia) and diversified acceptance — country-concentration risk is modest. The swing factor is **domestic US policy and litigation**: direct political attacks on interchange fees and APR (2026), an unresolved merchant interchange MDL, and a pending routing bill that uniquely threatens the closed-loop model. These are unlikely to be existential in the base case (Amex won the last Supreme Court round), but the 2026 political agenda makes the US regulatory trajectory the single largest driver of legal risk. **Rating would escalate to High if the 10% APR cap or interchange legislation advances.**
