## SNDK — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
**Sandisk Corporation** (CIK 0002023554) | Pure-play NAND flash | HQ Milpitas, CA | 11,100 employees | Mkt cap ~$135B | Separated from Western Digital 21 Feb 2025

---

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

FY2026 (fiscal year ended 3 July 2026) net revenue was **$20.25B**, up 175% from $7.36B in FY2025 — a memory supercycle driven overwhelmingly by AI datacenter demand. The geographic mix is the single most important risk fact about this company.

| Region | FY2026 Rev | % of Total | FY2025 % | Risk Level | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hong Kong** | $5.13B | **25.3%** | 17.7% | **High** | Billing/transshipment hub; likely proxy for mainland China demand |
| **Rest of Asia** (Taiwan, Korea, Japan, SE Asia) | $4.61B | **22.8%** | 15.2% | Medium-High | Datacenter/Edge customers; Taiwan Strait exposure |
| **China (mainland)** | $4.50B | **22.2%** | 27.7% | **Very High** | Export-control and retaliation front line |
| **United States** | $3.71B | 18.3% | 19.7% | Low | Section 232 import duty exposure |
| **EMEA** | $1.73B | 8.5% | 17.4% | Low-Medium | EU regulatory/tariff alignment |
| **Other** | $0.56B | 2.8% | 2.3% | Medium | Emerging markets |

*Source: Sandisk FY2026 10-K (filed 17 Aug 2026), segment note; revenue-by-geography history via BusinessQuant compilation of filings.*

**The concentration is the story.** Asia = **70.3%** of revenue. **Greater China (Hong Kong + mainland) = 47.5%** — effectively half the business. Management's own disclosure notes Hong Kong's share rose from 11.3% in FY2023 to 25.3% in FY2026, and "Rest of Asia" grew 313% YoY. This is not a diversified global franchise; it is an Asian demand story with a US listing.

By product: **Edge $12.16B (60.1%)**, **Datacenter $5.15B (25.4%)**, **Consumer $2.94B (14.5%)**. Datacenter grew 437% and drove ~88% of FQ4 revenue growth — meaning the marginal revenue dollar is now concentrated in a handful of hyperscaler customers, several of them Chinese.

**Manufacturing geography is the hidden exposure.** Sandisk does not own fabs. It manufactures through **Flash Ventures**, its 25-year JV with **Kioxia** at Yokkaichi and Kitakami, **Japan**. In January 2026 the pair extended the JV framework through December 2034; in August 2026 they announced **>$31B of further investment in Japan**. Sandisk also pays Kioxia **$1.165B** annually for manufacturing services. *Source: Kioxia/Sandisk press releases, 27 Aug 2026 and 30 Jan 2026; FY2026 10-K.*

So the risk chain is: **fabs in Japan → wafers shipped to Asia for assembly/test → billed through Hong Kong → sold into China and the US.** Every link crosses a geopolitical fault line.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

#### Risk 1 — US–China export controls and Chinese retaliation (Probability: High / Impact: High)
This is the dominant risk. The company sits squarely inside the semiconductor decoupling regime:
- **BIS closed the export-controls loophole for foreign-owned semiconductor fabs in China** (*BIS News*, 2026), tightening the actor-based controls that have expanded since 2022 (CRS R48642).
- **Section 232:** A January 2026 presidential action imposed a **25% ad valorem duty on certain semiconductor and derivative-product imports** into the US (White House proclamation, Jan 2026; CBP CSMS #67400472). Sandisk imports Japan-made NAND into its US market ($3.71B, 18.3% of revenue) — if NAND and downstream SSDs are captured, that is a direct margin hit. Industry analysis (ITIF, 24 Jun 2026) warns these tariffs act as an export tax on ICT goods.
- **China's counter-measures:** Beijing announced **export controls targeting Japan** in January 2026 (dual-use items, rare earths) amid heightened China–Japan tensions — directly relevant given Sandisk's all-Japan fab base (*CM Trade Law*, Jan 2026). China has repeatedly extended suspensions of restrictions on 32 US-linked companies and six strategic export-control measures on a rolling two-month basis — i.e., the reprieve is temporary and re-decided every 60 days. A re-imposition would land on a company with 22% mainland revenue.

The key asymmetry: **Sandisk is a US-domiciled company manufacturing in Japan and selling into China.** It has no ability to relocate fabs and limited ability to relocate customers. Note that export restrictions on Chinese NAND production have been read by the market as *positive* for flash pricing (*Investing.com*, 2026) — so policy cuts both ways here, which makes the risk two-sided rather than purely negative.

#### Risk 2 — Chinese NAND capacity and YMTC share gain (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High)
**YMTC now holds ~14% of global NAND revenue share, up from 9%, while Sandisk's share has slipped to ~11% from 12%** (*Yahoo Finance/GMIF*, 2026). Reports that **Apple is testing Chinese DRAM/NAND** triggered a **>50% drawdown in SNDK from its June 2026 high** (*TradingKey*, 2026). Chinese state-backed capacity growth in commodity NAND raises oversupply risk precisely when Sandisk's revenue is 60% Edge/consumer — the most commoditised part of the mix. Mitigant: long-term contracts cover roughly half of FY2027 bits and two-thirds of FY2028 (*Seeking Alpha*, 12 Nov 2026), which blunts but does not eliminate price risk.

#### Risk 3 — Japan concentration and AI-demand contagion (Probability: Medium / Impact: High)
Two distinct threads converge:
- *Single-country manufacturing.* 100% of front-end wafer supply sits in Japan. Japan is a stable, treaty-allied democracy with strong rule of law — but it is now a target of Chinese economic coercion, sits in a seismic zone, and its fabs are 1,500km from the Taiwan Strait. Any disruption (quake, China–Japan escalation, Taiwan contingency affecting component supply) is unhedgeable.
- *Customer concentration in AI.* The Nov 2026 collapse in confidence following **Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's resignation** over a spying scandal knocked SNDK ~5% in a session and dragged the whole memory complex (*Motley Fool*, *Benzinga*, *Invezz*, 12 Nov 2026). Sandisk's beta is **5.65** — among the highest in large-cap tech. The datacenter segment that now drives growth is levered to a single AI capex cycle and to US–China AI policy. Separately, US officials have raised concerns about Huang's posture toward China and its effect on export controls (WSJ, 9–11 Nov 2026) — policy direction on AI chip exports is genuinely unsettled.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Securities litigation (resolved, but relevant to governance):** *In re SanDisk Securities Litigation* reached a **$50M settlement with final approval in 2026**, covering a class period running from October 2018 (Cohen Milstein; settlement administrator site). This is legacy Western Digital-era litigation, not a new matter — but it establishes a plaintiff bar that is already familiar with this issuer.

**Product liability / defect class action:** *SanDisk Extreme Solid State Drive Defect Litigation* — class action filed August 2023 against SanDisk and then-parent Western Digital alleging a series of SSD failures (SGT Law). Still a live contingency; consumer product quality claims are a recurring cost of the Edge/consumer franchise. Watch for escalation as consumer revenue ($2.94B) grows.

**Sector patent war — YMTC v. Micron (peer, not yet Sandisk):** The Munich I Regional Court granted **two injunctions against Micron in September 2026** over 3D NAND patents, part of a global YMTC campaign that also includes a California suit asserting 11 patents (JUVE Patent; IP Fray; Tom's Hardware). YMTC has not sued Sandisk in the materials I found, but Sandisk is the other Western 3D NAND manufacturer with a Chinese-exposed revenue base — it is the obvious next target. **Treat as a contingent, not-yet-filed risk.** Sandisk's own IP position runs through the Kioxia Flash Ventures cross-license, which itself constrains both parties from working with third parties on flash manufacturing — a structural limit on strategic flexibility.

**Regulatory/administrative:** No active DOJ, FTC, SEC enforcement action against SNDK surfaced in this review. The relevant regulatory exposure is trade-administrative (BIS/EAR, CBP/Section 232) rather than antitrust or securities. Note the 2025 separation from Western Digital closed without a reported SAMR complication — China's MOFCOM had cleared the original 2015 WDC–SanDisk deal, which matters because Beijing has historically used merger clearance as leverage.

**Tariff mechanics:** Section 232 duties are the most quantifiable near-term legal/regulatory cost. Applicability to NAND/SSD derivatives is the open question; the White House framed the action as "narrowly targeted," but the derivative-product scope is where exposure typically hides.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **Hong Kong billing concentration (25.3% of revenue).** A quarter of revenue booked through a jurisdiction that is the primary channel for China-bound semiconductor sales is a structural AML/export-control screening concern. It demands robust end-user/end-use diligence — the "know your transshipment" problem. This is a flag on *design*, not an allegation of wrongdoing.
- **Entity List / end-user screening.** With Chinese hyperscalers and OEMs as datacenter customers, every shipment requires denied-party screening that changes month to month. A single miscategorized customer is a BIS penalty and a reputational event.
- **Two-month rolling Chinese counter-measures.** Compliance planning against a 60-day policy horizon is inherently unstable.
- **No FCPA/anti-corruption action identified.** No evidence of FCPA, books-and-records, or AML enforcement matters against the company.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale.** 47.5% of revenue is Greater China and 70.3% is Asia; 100% of wafer supply is a single-country JV in Japan; the product mix is 60% commoditised consumer/Edge exposed to Chinese capacity; and the company sits directly inside the US–China export-control regime as both a potential tariff payer (Section 232) and a potential retaliation target (China's Japan-directed controls). Beta of 5.65 confirms the market prices this as a high-sensitivity policy asset. Legal exposure is moderate and largely legacy (a settled $50M securities case, a 2023 SSD defect class action), but the YMTC patent campaign is an asymmetric tail risk that has already produced injunctions against a direct peer.

**What would change the rating:** Downward to Medium if the China/HK mix falls below 35% of revenue through genuine customer diversification, or if Section 232 derivative scope is confirmed to exclude NAND/SSDs. Upward to Very High on any Chinese export-control action specifically naming NAND, a YMTC suit against Sandisk, or formal notice of Section 232 duties on memory imports.
