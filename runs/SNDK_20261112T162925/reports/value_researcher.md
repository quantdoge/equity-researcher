# SanDisk Corporation (SNDK) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price:** $950.68 | **Market cap:** ~$143B | **EV:** ~$138B (net cash ~$4.9B) | **Diluted shares:** ~150M

---

## 0. What Business Are We Actually Looking At?

SanDisk is the February 2025 spinoff of Western Digital's NAND flash business: SSDs (client/enterprise), embedded memory, removable cards/USB, and wafers. Critically, it is a **commodity memory producer** — fab-light in reporting terms (capex just $177M in FY2026) because NAND wafers are largely sourced through the long-standing **Kioxia Flash Ventures JVs**, supplemented by patent licensing income and the SanDisk retail brand.

The last four fiscal years tell the entire story before a single valuation model is built:

| FY (ends ~July) | Revenue | Gross margin | Operating margin | Net income |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2023 | $6.09B | **7.1%** | –33.4% | –$2.14B |
| 2024 | $6.66B | 16.1% | –7.0% | –$0.67B |
| 2025 | $7.36B | 30.1% | –18.7% | –$1.64B |
| 2026 | $20.25B | **71.5%** | 61.2% | +$11.43B |
| Q1 FY27 (Oct-26) | $11.03B | **85.0%** | 79.6% | +$8.10B |

Revenue +175% and gross margin +41 points in one year, then 85% gross margin in a single quarter at a NAND maker. That is not a moat widening — that is a **pricing supercycle**, driven by AI data-center storage demand, the nearline HDD shortage pushing QLC SSD adoption, and industry capex discipline. Management now guides adjusted gross margins of ~**80% through 2030**, with base-case EPS of $243 (FY27) and $272 (FY28).

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: NARROW (weak) — and only by legacy, not by economics.**

*Evidence for some advantage:*
- BiCS technology platform shared with Kioxia provides efficient scale and shared capex — a genuine cost-position asset.
- Patent/IP licensing (including royalty streams off the JVs) is high-margin and recurring.
- The SanDisk brand carries real pricing power in retail removable memory.
- New long-term agreements with hyperscalers may lengthen contract visibility.

*Evidence against a real moat — and it is decisive:*
- **Gross margin of 7% (FY23) and 85% (Q1 FY27) in the same business.** A moated business cannot lose money selling its core product at 93% cost of revenue. SanDisk is a price-taker on a commodity.
- **ROIC of –16.3%, –3.9%, –12.2%, then +76.8%.** The Buffett hallmark — ROIC above WACC *consistently* through the cycle — is emphatically failed. Cumulative four-year ROIC is roughly break-even at best.
- NAND has more competitors and less supply discipline than DRAM: Samsung, SK Hynix/Solidigm, Kioxia/SanDisk, Micron, plus YMTC in China. The technology roadmap (layer count) is a treadmill, not a barrier.

**Verdict on quality:** A cyclical commodity producer with a good balance sheet, a strong brand in a small niche, and no demonstrated ability to earn excess returns *through* a cycle. This sits at the edge of my circle of competence — I can understand the business, but **nobody can forecast NAND ASPs**, and ASP is what determines the value.

---

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **5 / 10**

**Strengths:**
- Balance sheet is genuinely fortress-grade: $5.14B cash/investments vs. $201M total debt → **net cash ~$4.9B (~$33/share)**. Equity $18.1B.
- Current-cycle FCF conversion is superb: FY2026 FCF $11.49B on net income $11.43B = **>100%**; Q1 FY27 FCF $6.31B. D&A is trivial ($149M), so accounting earnings are close to cash.
- Working capital is a *use* of cash ($2.79B in FY26) — receivables up sharply, consistent with a pricing boom. Watch this.
- Goodwill/intangibles $5.0B (spin-related) — 28% of equity, not alarming.

**Why not higher:**
- **Revenue predictability: effectively zero.** –37.6%, +9.5%, +10.4%, +175.3% YoY. Earnings swung from –$2.1B to +$11.4B in three years.
- Through-cycle FCF is deeply negative in downturns (FY23 –$932M, FY24 –$475M, FY25 –$120M).
- Reported capex of <1% of revenue understates true capital intensity, because JV fabrication capex and wafer prepayments sit outside the reported capex line. In a normalized model I would assume **10–15% of revenue** as true capex — not 0.9%. This is a material accounting caveat.
- Beta of 5.6 (per data feed) confirms the earnings, not just the stock, are hyper-cyclical.

Excellent liquidity, terrible predictability. A 5 is fair.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Anything based on trailing or forward multiples is a trap here, so start with the reverse DCF.**

At an EV of ~$138B, an 11% discount rate and 3% terminal growth, the price requires **perpetual normalized FCF of ~$11.0B/year** ($138B × 8%). SanDisk's FY2026 FCF was $11.5B — meaning **the market is capitalizing one peak-cycle year as a permanent annuity.** In its entire history as a memory maker, SanDisk has never earned anything close to $11B in a normal year.

**Normalized (owner-earnings) scenario analysis:**

| Scenario | Rev | Gross margin | Opex | Net income | EPS | Multiple | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear — history repeats | $18B | 32% | $2.8B | $2.5B | ~$17 | 13x | **~$220** |
| **Base — generous mid-cycle** | $25B | 45% | $3.0B | $6.6B | ~$44 | 15x | **~$660** |
| Bull — 80% GM holds to 2030 | $30B | 55% | $3.2B | $12.0B | ~$80 | 16x | **~$1,280** |

**Base-case intrinsic value ≈ $660/share; current price $950 = a ~44% *premium*, not a discount.** To justify $950 on base assumptions you need mid-cycle net income near $9.5B — roughly **4x** the through-cycle earnings power this business has ever demonstrated. Even the bull case (an unprecedented 80% NAND gross margin persisting for years) only gets you modestly above the current price, which means you are risking a 0.5x downside to chase a ~1.3x upside.

**Cross-checks:**
- TTM P/E 7.8x, P/FCF 8.5x, EV/EBITDA 6.6x — optically cheap, but exactly what a peak-cycle commodity always looks like (memory stocks at cycle tops routinely trade at 4–6x forward EPS).
- P/B ~8–9x on $121/share book — you are paying a huge premium to hard assets in a commodity business.
- Peer median P/E of 33.9x (broad Technology universe) is not a meaningful comparison; the true comps are Micron and SK Hynix, whose multiples I could not retrieve within budget. Note that gap.
- Sell-side targets ($1,600–$2,050) are momentum-anchored, and one house raised to $3,000 — a classic late-cycle signal.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Mixed — operationally credible, capital allocation questionable.**

- David Goeckeler (ex-Western Digital CEO) ran this asset through the prior downturn; execution through the spin has been clean, margins are real, and the balance sheet was preserved rather than levered.
- **Capital allocation:** 100% cash-return policy, no dividend. Buybacks of $4.52B in Q4 FY26 and $5.00B in Q1 FY27 — likely struck at average prices near ~$1,300. SanDisk was buying back its own stock at 59% above today's price. Memory managements have a long, poor record of buying at peaks and issuing at troughs; this looks like more of the same.
- **Promotional guidance risk:** guiding ~80% gross margins through 2030 is an extraordinary claim for a commodity producer and should be discounted heavily. It echoes the "this time is different" supply-discipline narratives of 2017–18 and 2000.
- Insider ownership/compensation detail was not retrievable (only a PSU award exhibit, which does include a Relative TSR modifier — a shareholder-friendly design element).

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (Avoid new purchase)

This is not a bad company and the current cash generation is spectacular. It is simply **priced for the cycle to never end**, and that violates every rule I have:

- No margin of safety: price is ~44% above base-case intrinsic value; I require 20–30% *below*.
- No durable moat: ROIC has been negative in three of the last four years.
- No predictability: NAND ASPs are the value driver and are unforecastable.
- Late-cycle evidence is already visible: **Kioxia (SanDisk's own JV partner) guided NAND price growth decelerating from ~70% to mid-20% and roughly flat ahead**, and multiple industry sources expect NAND supply to outgrow demand in 2027. The stock is already –59% from its June 2026 peak of $2,335 — the market is starting to price exactly this.

At $950 you are being asked to underwrite peak-cycle earnings persisting for a decade. At **$450–550** (a genuine discount to my $660 base case) the risk/reward would become interesting enough to reconsider. Until then: pass.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (what would prove me wrong)

1. **Structural, not cyclical, re-rating.** If AI inference workloads and HDD displacement make NAND a permanently tight oligopoly, mid-cycle EPS could be $120–150 and $950 would be ~7x earnings — cheap. This is the single strongest bear argument against my Sell call, and I hold it with only moderate confidence.
2. **Supply discipline persists.** Capex is being directed to HBM/DRAM; no greenfield NAND fabs are being built. If bit supply growth stays below demand growth past 2027, the current margin structure lasts longer than history suggests.
3. **Contractual locking.** Hyperscaler LTAs at fixed prices would compress the cycle's amplitude and justify a higher multiple than commodity memory has ever earned.
4. **Per-share compounding from buybacks.** $9.5B retired in six months; at <5x forward earnings this is genuinely accretive *if* the earnings hold.
5. **Strategic scarcity/M&A.** NAND capacity is strategically valuable; a premium bid is conceivable.

**Risks in the other direction (why the downside is severe):** Kioxia JV dependence (single-source wafers, governance), hyperscaler customer concentration, YMTC/Chinese capacity, export controls, working-capital reversal, buyback capital destroyed at the top, and a 5.6 beta that has already produced **–22% in one session (Nov 5, 2026)** and –59% from the peak.

**Data gaps acknowledged:** no 10-K text available (only a 10-Q exhibit), no dividend history, memory-specific peer multiples (MU, SKHY) unavailable — the 33.9x peer median is a broad-sector figure and should not be relied upon.
