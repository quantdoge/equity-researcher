## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE** (upper tier)

This is one of the strongest corporate balance sheets in the market. It is not close to a credit question.

| Metric | Value | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Total debt (Q3 FY27) | $38.15B | Immaterial vs. equity |
| Cash + ST investments | $97.73B | Covers **2.6x** all debt |
| Net debt | $9.06B | ~0.03x TTM EBITDA |
| Debt/equity | 14.3% (from 7% at FY26) | Low, but **doubled** |
| Debt/assets | 10.3% | — |
| Interest coverage (TTM) | **~595x** (EBIT $239.1B / int. exp. $0.40B) | FY26 tool: 503x |
| Altman Z-Score | **6.75 — Safe zone** (threshold 2.99) | FY26 basis |

**Debt trajectory is the one thing to watch:** $11.4B (Jan-26) → $12.8B (Apr-26) → $38.9B (Jul-26) → $38.1B (Oct-26). NVDA added **~$26B of long-term debt in two quarters** after years of running net cash. Maturity profile is benign (zero short-term debt, $32.4B long-term) — **no refinancing cliff**. Context matters: the raise is almost certainly funding the customer-ecosystem equity investments and supply pre-payments, which is precisely the riskiest use of proceeds.

**Verdict:** Zero near-term default risk, zero refinancing risk. Credit deterioration is *not* a plausible downside driver here.

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue −20%

**Base TTM:** Revenue $361.8B · GM 74.9% · Opex $32.0B · Op. income $239.1B (66.1% margin)

| Line item | Base | −20% revenue, margins hold | −20% revenue, −500bp GM + $20B inventory/commitment charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $361.8B | $289.4B | $289.4B |
| Gross profit | $271.1B | $216.8B | $202.3B |
| Operating income | $239.1B | **$184.8B (−22.7%)** | **$150.3B (−37%)** |
| Interest coverage | ~595x | ~460x | ~375x |
| Net debt/EBITDA | 0.03x | 0.05x | 0.06x |
| Implied EPS (24.1B sh) | $9.35 | ~$6.10 | ~$5.10 |

**Conclusion: a 20% revenue decline is a non-event for solvency.** Operating margins are so wide (66%) and fixed opex so small (8.8% of revenue) that NVDA still generates ~$150–185B of operating income, ~375x interest coverage, and ~0.05x net leverage. **Even a −50% revenue collapse** (~$181B) leaves operating income positive at roughly $85B with $97.7B of cash. The company survives essentially any demand scenario.

**The transmission mechanism to equity is valuation, not viability.** At ~$5.10–6.10 downside EPS and a de-rated 14–18x multiple (a cyclical semiconductor company growing 0%, not 100%), fair value is **$71–110/share — a −33% to −57% downside case** from $165.05. This is the asymmetry that matters: **credit is bulletproof, equity is not.**

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**
*(Credit: Low · Liquidity: Very Low · Market/Equity: High)*

The single-rating answer hides the real structure. **Balance-sheet risk is as low as it gets in public equities.** But "financial risk" for NVDA inverts the usual hierarchy: the danger is not *can they pay their debts* but *what happens to a $4T equity stub priced on peak-cycle earnings with $390B of irreversible supply commitments.*

---

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. 🔴 **$390B of supply/procurement commitments (Q3 FY27), up from $279B in July 2026.** This equals **108% of TTM revenue** and 4x cash. These are largely take-or-pay obligations. If AI capex rolls over, this converts from operating leverage into an operating trap. FY26 already carried **$7.2B of inventory/excess purchase-obligation provisions** (up from $3.7B). *This is the #1 risk in the entire report.*
2. 🔴 **Customer concentration: 4 customers = 61% of FY26 revenue** (22%/15%/13%/11%); top 2 were 39% of Q2 FY27 revenue. Over **25% of revenue is from Taiwan-based customers** — compounding geopolitical (Taiwan Strait) and single-node risk. Zero to 61% concentration in four years.
3. 🟠 **Circular financing / revenue quality.** Barron's reports NVDA holds "customer finance commitments" alongside equity stakes in AI customers (Anthropic, OpenAI-adjacent). NVDA is lending to and investing in the entities buying its product. The $26B debt raise funds this. Revenue quality is lower than the income statement implies.
4. 🟠 **Governance / key-person:** A California AG **investigative subpoena** was served in November 2026 related to the CEO. At a company where the founder-CEO *is* the strategy, this is a material, unhedgeable tail risk and the proximate trigger of the Nov 20 crash.
5. 🟠 **Earnings quality deteriorating:** FY26 net income ($120.1B) exceeded operating cash flow ($102.7B); accruals are 8.4% of total assets; Piotroski F4 fails. DSO up 12 days to 63.7, inventory days up to 124. Working capital was a **−$15.9B drag** in FY26 and is worsening as revenue scales.
6. 🟡 **Sharpe ratio 0.006 over 1 year, realized vol 56%, beta 2.44.** Investors are bearing equity-like risk with no compensation. Position sizing, not credit analysis, is the risk control here.
7. 🟡 **Export controls / China** — structurally reduced TAM and recurring policy whiplash (not quantifiable from tools).
8. 🟡 **Macro:** 10Y at **5.13%**, 2Y-10Y +39bp (no inversion), 30Y 5.47%. Long-end yields at these levels are a direct discount-rate headwind for a 14x EV/EBITDA long-duration growth asset.

**Bottom line:** NVDA's balance sheet cannot break — this is a Low-risk credit under any stress test I can construct, including a 50% revenue decline. The financial risk is concentrated entirely in (a) $390B of irreversible supply commitments written against a demand curve that must keep compounding at triple digits, and (b) a beta-2.4 equity at 56% volatility with a live −47.5% drawdown. **Downgrade triggers would be: procurement commitments growing faster than revenue for two more quarters, DSO above ~75 days, or the CA AG matter escalating to formal charges.**
