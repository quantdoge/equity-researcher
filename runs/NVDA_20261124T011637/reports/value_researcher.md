# NVIDIA Corporation (NVDA) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $165.05 (close Nov 23, 2026) | Market cap ≈ $3.99T | Shares diluted 24.19B | Net cash**

*Data note: the $165.05 print is the latest available close (Nov 23, 2026), the session in which the stock rose ~31% on CEO reinstatement. Fundamentals are TTM through Q3 FY2027 (quarter ended Oct 25, 2026).*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **WIDE** (with duration caveat)

NVIDIA designs accelerated computing platforms: GPUs, rack-scale systems (Vera Rubin NVL72), networking (NVLink, Spectrum-X, InfiniBand) and the CUDA software stack. The moat rests on four pillars, and the evidence that it is real is unusually strong:

| Moat source | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Intangibles / ecosystem** | CUDA is a ~20-year installed software base; developers, libraries and tooling create a switching cost that a faster chip alone does not overcome. |
| **Cost/scale advantage** | $362B TTM revenue buys priority allocation of CoWoS, HBM and 3nm/2nm wafer capacity — a structural supply moat competitors cannot replicate at scale. |
| **System-level lock-in** | Shift from selling chips to selling rack-scale systems (NVLink domains, networking, software) raises the switching cost from one component to an entire datacenter architecture. |
| **Pricing power (the acid test)** | Gross margin of **74.8%** at $115.8B *quarterly* revenue — expanding, not eroding, despite HBM cost inflation. Q3 FY27 data center revenue +110% y/y to $107.6B. |

**Moat trend: widening.** Operating margin went from 27% (FY21) → 54% (FY24) → 66% (TTM). ROIC of 77% versus a ~10% WACC is the textbook signature of a moated business; few companies in history have earned that spread at this scale.

**The honest caveat:** the moat is wide but its *duration* is the entire debate. Hyperscalers (Google TPU, Amazon Trainium, Meta MTIA, Microsoft Maia) have both the motive and the capital to internalize this workload, and Broadcom is monetizing exactly that shift. NVIDIA's share has not yet cracked — but a 10-year underwriting of a 75% gross margin in semiconductors requires a technology-leadership assumption I cannot verify. Moat: wide for 3–5 years, contested for 10.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Is the headline multiple misleading? Yes — dramatically.** The commonly cited 33.7x P/E uses FY2026 (Jan 2026) EPS of $4.90. On trailing earnings the stock is cheaper:

| Measure | Multiple |
|---|---|
| P/E (TTM EPS $9.31) | **17.7x** |
| EV/EBITDA (TTM $274B) | **14.4x** |
| FCF yield (TTM $149B) | **3.7%** |
| P/E on FY27E EPS ~$10.42 (incl. $2.87 Q4 guide) | **15.8x** |
| P/E on FY28E consensus (~$690B revenue → EPS ~$15) | **~11x** |
| Dividend ($1.00/yr) | 0.6% |

*Peer caveat: AVGO (65.7x) and AMD (171x) multiples in my dataset are computed against stale fiscal-year earnings and are directionally, not precisely, comparable. On any normalized basis, NVDA trades at a discount to slower-growing peers — unusual for the category leader.*

**Intrinsic value — scenario-based owner-earnings DCF** (10% WACC, 4% terminal, FCF base $149B):

| Scenario | Assumptions | Value/share |
|---|---|---|
| **Bear** | AI capex plateaus FY28, FCF peaks ~$200B then −6%/yr for four years, 3% terminal | **~$95** |
| **Base** | FCF compounds to ~$400B by year 5, then 5% fading to 4% terminal | **~$235** |
| Probability-weighted (30/50/20) | | **~$200** |

A cross-check on normalized earnings power supports this: mid-cycle EPS of ~$11.5–12.5 (deliberately well below FY28 consensus) at 16x = **$185–200**.

**Verdict on price:** at $165.05 the stock sits ~18% below my probability-weighted intrinsic value of ~$200 — but **still ~70% above the bear case**. That is not a margin of safety; it is fair compensation for a genuinely bimodal distribution. Note the reflexivity on display: $950B of market value added in a single session on a governance headline, after a 47% drawdown in seven weeks. The price is being set by narrative flow, not by cash flows.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Split verdict: A on capital allocation, C on governance**

**Capital allocation: excellent (A).**
- FY26 buybacks $40.1B; 9M FY27 $63.3B; **$227.3B remaining** under authorization (raised by $150B in Sept 2026) — repurchasing at ~15x earnings, which is demonstrably value-accretive.
- Dividend raised 25x ($0.01 → $0.25/quarter) — a credible signal of durable cash generation.
- No empire-building: goodwill and intangibles of only $24B against a $4T enterprise; SBC held to 2.1% of revenue. This is disciplined, owner-oriented behavior.

**Governance: deeply concerning (C).**
- Nov 8: the Board disclosed that founder/CEO Jensen Huang was the **target of a foreign intelligence effort** and opened an independent investigation.
- Nov 12: Huang **resigned** after acknowledging extramarital affairs (reportedly including an alleged Chinese intelligence connection). Colette Kress became interim CEO. The stock fell from $174 to $151 intraday; the trough on Nov 20 was **$125.53**, a 47% decline from the October high.
- Nov 16: the Board publicly **ruled out** his return. Nov 23: the Board **unanimously reinstated** him, stating it received input from "senior levels of the United States government" and was satisfied as to his candor. Harvey Jones remains independent chair.
- Separately, the **SEC is investigating whether NVIDIA misled investors** — reportedly examining 2025 changes to geographic revenue reporting — alongside a securities class action covering Dec 2024–Nov 2026.

A board that reverses itself in seven days under external pressure, for a CEO who resigned eleven days earlier over conduct involving a foreign-intelligence risk, has demonstrated that key-man dependence here is not a theoretical risk. The Nov 17 creation of a National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee is a constructive step, but the governance discount is earned.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

NVIDIA is the best business I have been asked to evaluate in years: a wide moat, 75% gross margins, 77% ROIC, net cash, a shrinking share count, and a 15.8x forward multiple that any value investor would ordinarily find compelling. It is emphatically **not a Sell** — the cash flows are real, and at $125 the market was pricing a scenario my own base case says is too pessimistic.

But it is not a Buy, for three reasons:

1. **No margin of safety.** ~18% below probability-weighted intrinsic value is not the 20–30% discount I require — particularly when the bear case ($95) is only ~40% below today's price and depends on a single unforecastable variable: the trajectory of global AI capital spending.
2. **The earnings are real, but their disclosure is under regulatory examination.** Buffett's first filter after "can I understand it" is "can I trust the numbers." The SEC probe into revenue-reporting methodology, combined with working-capital intensity and neocloud/vendor-financing exposure, means I cannot underwrite the revenue line with the confidence the valuation demands.
3. **Management quality just failed a live test.** Capital allocation is superb, but the eleven-day leadership vacuum and the manner of the reinstatement introduce a governance risk that is unquantifiable — and unquantifiable risks do not get paid for at 15x.

**What would make me a buyer:** a price of **$125–135** (≈12x FY27E EPS, ~35% below base intrinsic value) *with* the SEC matter resolved without restatement. Alternatively, two consecutive quarters of gross margin ≥72% alongside receivables days stabilising — evidence the working-capital build is growth, not channel stuffing — would justify buying on the way up.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI capex cyclicality (highest severity).** Revenue is up 5.5x in two years on a spending boom funded increasingly by debt and off-balance-sheet structures at customers. A plateau in hyperscaler capex would compress revenue 30–50% against a fixed cost base — the $95 bear case.
2. **SEC accounting probe / class action.** Adverse findings on revenue geography disclosure could force restatement, penalties and a durable credibility discount on the multiple. Outcome unknown; this is the single most important unresolved fact.
3. **Key-man and national-security risk.** The CEO was, per his own board, the target of a foreign intelligence operation. Recurrence would be catastrophic for leadership continuity and could invite government intervention in the customer relationship.
4. **Customer concentration and revenue quality.** A handful of hyperscalers plus a cohort of neoclouds (CoreWeave, Nebius, Nscale) drive the majority of data center revenue; NVIDIA increasingly invests in or finances the same customers who buy its product — classic circularity risk.
5. **Custom silicon.** Google TPU, Trainium, MTIA and Broadcom's ASIC franchise target the highest-volume inference workloads; each point of share loss hits the highest-margin revenue first.
6. **Margin compression already visible.** Q4 FY27 gross margin is guided to **71–72%**, down from 74.8%, on Vera Rubin ramp and HBM costs. If that becomes a trend rather than a launch-quarter artifact, the earnings power case weakens materially.
7. **China excluded entirely.** Guidance assumes zero China data center compute revenue — good conservatism, but it removes a large optionality and leaves exposure to further export-control tightening (hence the new Board committee).
8. **Law of large numbers.** Sustaining 60%+ growth on a $424B revenue base requires absolute annual additions of ~$250B — larger than the entire semiconductor industry a decade ago.

---

### Bottom line
A magnificent business at a reasonable — not cheap — price, run by a brilliant capital allocator whose position has just been revealed as alarmingly fragile, with its accounting under regulatory scrutiny. **Hold.** I would rather miss the next leg of this than underwrite a 2030 AI capex forecast I cannot defend or a disclosure process I cannot yet trust.
