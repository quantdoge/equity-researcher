## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Reported footprint (FY2026, ended 25 Jan 2026):**
- Scope 1: **9,822 tCO₂e** (trivial)
- Scope 2 (market-based): **568 tCO₂e** (≈95% renewable electricity — genuinely best-in-class for own operations)
- Scope 3: **10,700,940 tCO₂e (~10.7 Mt)** — **>99% of total**; nearly **tripled since FY2024**

**Why Medium, not Low:**
- **Scope 3 is the whole story and it is deteriorating fast.** ~10.7 Mt on a ~$200B+ revenue base implies low revenue carbon intensity, but the *absolute* trajectory is up sharply as shipment volumes scale. Greenpeace's September 2026 critique focused precisely on this tripling while the company markets itself as an efficiency leader.
- **Target architecture is the weak link:** the Scope 1+2 target is absolute (−50% vs FY2023 by FY2030 — easy, already near-solved via RECs), but the **Scope 3 target is intensity-based (per PFLOPS)**, which structurally permits absolute emissions to rise indefinitely with AI demand. That is a real greenwashing risk under EU CSRD assurance.
- **Product-use emissions** are increasingly a customer-visible cost line: reporting has compared Nvidia accelerator fleet electricity demand to a mid-sized national coal producer. This creates a **TCO attack surface** for custom silicon/ASIC competitors and invites datacenter siting/grid regulation.
- **Physical risk:** concentrated at TSMC (Taiwan water stress, typhoon/seismic) and in Arizona expansion (water-intensive). Nvidia bears the *supply interruption* consequence, not the direct cost.

**Controversy exposure:** no spills, no pollution fines, no remediation liabilities. Environmental litigation risk to Nvidia is **indirect (disclosure-based)**, not operational.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Strengths (genuinely strong):**
- Attrition **3.7% FY2026** — extraordinary for semiconductors (~3x below industry norms); Glassdoor top-ranked employer; CEO approval historically ~98% (note: pre-scandal).
- Headcount grew from ~34k (FY2023) to ~42–48k, with no material restructuring or layoffs — positive on employment practices.
- Filed FY2026 Forced Labor/Human Trafficking/Child Labour Statement and Form SD Conflict Minerals Report — disclosure hygiene above the semiconductor median.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Gender diversity: ~21% women** — materially behind technology peers (~30–35%). Thin pipeline at senior technical levels.
- **Supply-chain labour risk is structural and unhedged:** advanced packaging and HBM come from a small Taiwan/Korea cluster; board assembly spans China, Mexico, Vietnam. UFLPA and the **EU Forced Labour Regulation (in force Dec 2027)** create detention/seizure risk in critical minerals (3TG, cobalt, aluminium, copper).
- **Product social licence:** accelerators are the enabling layer for surveillance and autonomous weapons. Export diversion allegations (see below) convert this from abstract to acute.
- **Post-reinstatement workforce signal:** staff publicly celebrating the return cuts both ways — evidence of culture, but also of a personality cult that weakens institutional checks.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10** (Critical)

**What is still good:**
- **Single share class** — no dual-class super-voting structure; all directors elected **annually** with one-year terms.
- Independent directors retained **Wachtell Lipton (Joon H. Kim, former acting US Attorney)** for a genuinely independent investigation — the right call at the time.
- Creation of a **separate board chair** (first in company history) on 12 Nov 2026.
- CFO Kress reportedly working to **reduce customer financing** — a disclosure-quality positive.

**What has broken down — and it is severe:**
1. **Key-person risk realised and then re-embedded.** A ~47% drawdown ($239.24 on 6 Oct → $125.53 on 20 Nov 2026; ~$2.7T of market value) was driven almost entirely by one individual's conduct — the textbook definition of unpriced governance concentration.
2. **The board reversed itself under political pressure.** Chair stated on **17 Nov** that the board "does not intend to reinstate" Huang; **six days later** the board reinstated him **unanimously** after White House intervention and "reassurances from senior U.S. government officials" on national security. Whatever the merits, the sequence establishes that the board's judgement is **overridable by the executive branch** — a governance first for a Dow-scale company and a permanent minority-shareholder concern. Respected commentators (Charles Elson, Naveen Rao, Michael Burry) have publicly called it a mistake.
3. **The new chair is not fresh.** Harvey C. Jones has served since **1993** — the founding year. A 33-year-tenured director chairing a board that must now investigate its founder does not constitute independent challenge.
4. **Board independence was always thin.** No separate chair until forced; CEO simultaneously President; the lead independent director also chaired the nominating/governance committee — the very committee responsible for CEO oversight. Ten non-employee directors, but long tenures and founder deference undermine *independence of mind* (exact independence determination unavailable — proxy not retrievable; estimate **~7 of 10 nominally independent, materially fewer in practice**).
5. **Political entanglement / "too big to fail" status.** Reports of $20M+ company donations to a White House building fund and Huang's personal role in shaping AI export policy create **corruption and policy-reversal risk**. This is a two-way dependency: Nvidia benefits from protection today, and inherits the political risk of the next administration.
6. **Disclosure controls under question.** Receivables of **$81.0B**, DSO of **64 days**, and **$518B** of future commitments raise revenue-recognition and circular-financing questions that the governance crisis now amplifies. A **securities class action** (Rosen Law; class period 19 Dec 2024 – 12 Nov 2026; lead-plaintiff deadline 8 Jan 2027) alleges investors were misled about the relationship and its national-security implications.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **ESG RISK** (Governance-driven)

E and S fundamentals are **Average-to-Leader** (95% renewables in own operations, 3.7% attrition, annual elections, single share class, independent counsel). Governance is **Critical** and it is the pillar that governs everything else: it determines whether the E and S disclosures can be trusted, whether capital allocation survives a leadership shock, and whether the company's political protection outlasts the current administration. A single-factor override to ESG Risk is warranted because the governance failure has already converted into **realised financial loss** — the definition of materiality.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE** (relative to a no-ESG-view baseline)

**The ESG lens does not break the growth thesis — it reprices the risk in it.**

- **Cost of capital:** a governance premium of 50bp on a 2.25-beta name with long-duration cash flows is worth roughly **8–10% of equity value**. Add litigation and political-reversal optionality and a **10–15% governance discount** to intrinsic value is defensible. Nvidia's 2.25 beta already reflects this volatility regime.
- **Position sizing:** the 42% drawdown and 31.5% single-day rebound prove this is a **binary-outcome, event-driven** security as much as a compounder. Position sizes should be set on that basis, not on a Sharpe ratio estimated from a pre-scandal regime.
- **What would change the view (upgrade triggers):** (1) publication of the Wachtell investigation findings; (2) a genuinely independent chair with <10 years' tenure; (3) an absolute (not intensity-only) Scope 3 target with third-party assurance; (4) settlement of the AG subpoena without findings of disclosure failure; (5) credible evidence of reduced reliance on the founder for government relations.
- **What would make it worse:** formal SEC/DOJ charges, a second leadership departure, a restatement, or loss of export licences.

**Bottom line:** Own it, if you own it, as a **high-conviction AI infrastructure position with an explicit, quantified governance discount** — not as a core compounder. The Environmental and Social pillars are investable; the Governance pillar is not, on current evidence.
