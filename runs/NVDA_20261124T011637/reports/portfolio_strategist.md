**Conflict resolution.** My framework says risk flags take precedence over valuation enthusiasm, and here they do — but with an important qualification. The bearish agents are not arguing the business is impaired; they are arguing the *distribution of outcomes is unestimable*. That is a sizing input, not a rejection. The one genuinely disqualifying scenario is the SEC probe resolving into a restatement — a scenario no agent can price and which would invalidate the 74.9% margin and 77% ROIC figures that every bullish report leans on.

**Net: consensus on quality is real and should be owned. Consensus on entry does not exist.** A split on timing plus multiple unhedgeable risk flags caps the position at the low end of the sizing range — not zero, but not 5%.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation

### **Target: 2.0% of portfolio (hard cap 3.0%) — initiated at 1.0% now**

**Kelly check (and why I override it).** Value's probability-weighted outcomes (bear $95 / 30%, base $235 / 50%, bull ~$270 / 20%) give an expected 12-month return of **+21%**. A naive two-outcome Kelly approximation (p=0.70 of +42%, q=0.30 of −42%) implies f ≈ 0.40, or **half-Kelly ~20% of capital**. That number is unusable: it assumes the outcome distribution is estimable. The dominant risks here — restatement, export-privilege loss, a second CEO departure, a credit-led capex air-pocket — are fat-tailed and *not* diversifiable within the name (R² vs SPY is only 0.458; **54% of variance is idiosyncratic**). Kelly is large because the edge is real; I size small because the tails are unmodelable. Take the minimum of the two.

**Why 2% specifically — sizing to the bear case, not to the expected value:**

| Test | At 2.0% | At 3.0% |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio beta contribution (2.44 × w) | **+4.9%** | +7.3% |
| Standalone vol contribution (56.3% × w) | 1.13% | 1.69% |
| **Bear case loss** (−42% to $95, Value) | **−0.84%** | −1.26% |
| **Stress case loss** (−57% to $71, Fin Risk de-rated EPS) | **−1.14%** | −1.71% |

The discipline is simple: **a total thesis failure must cost less than ~1% of the portfolio.** 2% is the largest position that satisfies that constraint. This is exactly what Fin Risk and Quant both concluded independently — "position sizing, not credit analysis, is the risk control here."

**Why not pass (0.5–1%):** the quality is too high and the price too reasonable to forgo. At 17.2x FY27E EPS and ~10x FY28E on triple-digit current growth, with every sell-side target at or above spot, the market is already pricing substantial skepticism. A zero position is a bet that the SEC probe produces a restatement — a low-probability, high-severity event that does not warrant total abstention.

**Why not 3–5%:** that range is reserved for high conviction *and* low risk. This is high conviction on quality and **high risk** on governance, disclosure, concentration and beta. It fails the second test.

## 6. Risk Management Rules

**Price stop — soft.** Close below **$140** → halt tranches 2 and 3, re-underwrite. This is ~4.3 daily standard deviations; unlikely to be noise.

**Price stop — hard.** Close below **$125** (the 20 Nov low of $125.53, intraday $124.50) → the "reinstatement de-risks the name" premise is falsified. **Exit to 0.5% residual.** Note honestly: with a −9% gap on 5 Nov and a −14% day on 20 Nov, price stops are unreliable here. **Sizing, not stops, is the real protection.**

**Thesis invalidation — exit on any one of these, regardless of price:**
1. **SEC probe → restatement, revenue-recognition adjustment, or Wells notice** to the company or a named officer. This is the single most important unresolved fact in the entire memo.
2. **Gross margin below 70% for two consecutive quarters.** Q4 is already guided down to 71–72% from 74.9% on HBM cost and the Rubin ramp. Two quarters confirms margin *transfer* to the memory oligopoly, not a launch artifact.
3. **Procurement commitments ($390B, up from $279B in July) growing faster than revenue for two more quarters**, or **DSO above 75 days**. Both are Fin Risk's stated downgrade triggers.
4. **Loss or suspension of export privileges**, or renewed departure/removal of the CEO.
5. **2027 hyperscaler capex guidance cut** — specifically the $1.3T path revised toward $1.0T. This is Macro's bear mechanism and would take forward earnings power down 25–30% on its own.
6. **Another director sale without a 10b5-1 plan** (follows Stevens' $946M, no-plan, 10 weeks before the crash).

**Trim trigger.** Trim one-third at **$225–235** — the Street median target (~$222) through Value's base-case intrinsic value ($235). Trim a second third above **$250**. If the position exceeds 3% through appreciation, trim mechanically back to 2.5%. Do not let a governance-discounted name become an oversized position by drift.

**Upgrade triggers (would justify moving to the 3% cap):** Wachtell investigation findings published and clean; SEC inquiry closed without action; an independent chair with <10 years' tenure; two consecutive quarters of gross margin ≥72% with receivables days stabilising.
