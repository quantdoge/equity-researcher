## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**Risk 1 — US drug-pricing and tariff regime (probability: high; impact: medium)**
US = ~90% of revenue, so US policy is the dominant "geopolitical" variable. The April 2, 2026 proclamation/EO converted Section 232 pharma tariff pressure into enforceable law — up to 100% duties on patented pharmaceuticals and ingredients, effective July 31, 2026 for named companies and Sept 29, 2026 for all others, with a **three-year tariff exemption for manufacturers signing Most-Favored-Nation (MFN) pricing agreements** (26 manufacturers covering ~89% of branded spend as of Aug 31, 2026). Insmed does not appear on published MFN signatory lists. Because manufacturing is outsourced (possibly ex-US), imported BRINSUPRI/ARIKAYCE could face duties; **possible mitigant: Commerce orphan-drug 0% tariff relief** (ARIKAYCE holds orphan designation for MAC lung disease) — unconfirmed for Insmed specifically. Residual risk: MFN "best price" commitments and Medicaid Best Price attestation compliance.

**Risk 2 — Single-jurisdiction regulatory dependency: FDA (probability: medium; impact: high)**
The ARIKAYCE growth story rests on the sNDA for MAC lung disease (all patients, incl. newly diagnosed/recurrent). FDA accepted the sNDA in Sept 2026, granted **Priority Review with a PDUFA target of January 28, 2027**. ARIKAYCE still operates under an accelerated-approval construct in the US historically. Any CRL, delay, or added post-marketing requirement hits ~$460–470M of 2026 US-dominated revenue guidance. Related: a quarter of the pipeline (TPIP, INS1148, gene therapies) is FDA-gated; US political volatility at FDA/HHS is a live execution risk.

**Risk 3 — Ex-US pricing/reimbursement erosion as international mix grows (probability: high; impact: low–medium today, rising)**
EU: European Commission approval of BRINSUPRI (Nov 18, 2025, following CHMP positive opinion Oct 17, 2025) means EU launches now layer in national HTA/pricing negotiations and the EU HTA Regulation's Joint Clinical Assessment. Japan: MHLW approved BRINSUPRI Aug 2026, launched Oct 2026; PMDA submission for the ARIKAYCE ENCORE label expansion planned Q4 2026 — both expose Insmed to Japan's biennial NHI price cuts and yen translation. International revenue (80%+ of which is ex-US-priced ARIKAYCE) is the fastest-growing line (+21–26% y/y), so this risk compounds.

**Also on the radar:** Sanofi is reportedly weighing a takeover of Insmed (Bloomberg/Reuters, Nov 19, 2026) — a cross-border (France→US) deal that would draw HSR/FTC and EU merger review, and possibly US political attention on pharma pricing post-deal. Not a national-security (CFIUS) issue on its face.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Litigation: no material pending matters disclosed.** Both the FY2025 Form 10-K and 10-Qs use standard boilerplate: "From time to time, the Company is a party to various lawsuits, claims and other legal proceedings that arise in the ordinary course of business." No government investigation, DOJ/FTC/SEC action, securities class action, or product-liability programme identified in filings or news reviewed. (Note: product-liability tail risk is inherent — ARIKAYCE carries auditory/vestibular toxicity warnings requiring monitoring.)
- **IP:** ARIKAYCE US patents extend into the early 2030s; European Patent Nos. 2852391 and 4005576 cover ARIKAYCE/NTM use (per filing text; expiry dates partially truncated in the excerpt reviewed — verify). **BRINSUPRI becomes eligible for patent challenges on Aug 12, 2029** (DrugPatentWatch) → first Paragraph IV / IPR cycle is a 2029–2031 event, not a near-term one. Brensocatib is in-licensed from AstraZeneca (2016, $30M upfront) — ongoing tiered royalty obligation.
- **Regulatory calendar:** FDA PDUFA Jan 28, 2027 (ARIKAYCE sNDA); PMDA submission Q4 2026; EU launches post-EC approval; US launch of BRINSUPRI (Aug 2025 approval) — plus a Phase 2b/Phase 2 setback in rhinosinusitis and hidradenitis suppurativa (programs discontinued), which is scientific, not legal, risk.
- **Governance/optics:** CFO transition announced Oct 5, 2026 (Sara Bonstein stepping down Oct 30) — execution/governance flag, not legal.
- **Sector-wide policy contagion:** Congressional Democrats have requested unredacted MFN agreements from all signatory drugmakers, with subpoena power from January — an industry-wide disclosure/compliance risk even for non-signatories.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

No red flags identified. Watch items, in order of relevance:
1. **FCPA / UK Bribery Act** — new direct commercial operations in Japan and EU launches, plus HCP engagement; Insmed does publish a Global Healthcare Compliance program and policies, which is a positive control indicator.
2. **Pricing transparency/attestation** — MFN best-price attestations, Medicaid Best Price, EFPIA/Japan transparency reporting; misreporting is the most plausible enforcement vector for a company this US-price-dependent.
3. **Privacy** — GDPR (EU/UK) for patient-support and clinical data as European commercial footprint scales.
4. **Sanctions/export controls** — immaterial: no disclosed Russia, China, Iran, or other sanctioned-market revenue.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **LOW**

Rationale: ~90% US revenue, no sanctioned/EM exposure, no material litigation, no disclosed investigations, and a clean compliance posture. Regulatory concentration in a single agency (FDA) and US tariff/MFN policy are the material swing factors — this is a **US regulatory-and-policy story, not a geopolitical one**. Upgrade to Medium if (a) BRINSUPRI/ARIKAYCE imports become subject to Section 232 duties without orphan/MFN relief, (b) the Jan 28, 2027 PDUFA slips or draws a CRL, or (c) a formal MFN pricing agreement imposes binding best-price terms.
