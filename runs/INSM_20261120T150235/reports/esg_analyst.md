# INSM — Insmed Incorporated

**Price $146.93 · Market cap ~$32.2B · 1,664 FTE · Bridgewater, NJ**

## 1. ESG Materiality Map

Biotech is unusual: the environmental dimension is largely immaterial, while **product safety and pricing/access** carry nearly all the financial weight. My materiality ranking for INSM specifically:

| Rank | Factor | Pillar | Why financially material | Materiality |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Product safety & labelling (ARIKAYCE boxed warnings; BRINSUPRI dermatologic AEs) | S | Revenue is concentrated in two drugs; a label restriction or safety signal is a direct cash-flow event | **Critical** |
| 2 | Pricing & patient access (BRINSUPRI $88k list; ICER/HTA pushback) | S | Determines net price, the $7B peak-sales assumption, and IRA exposure | **Critical** |
| 3 | Board independence / Chair-CEO duality / disclosure integrity | G | Live securities-fraud investigation; M&A situation raises conflict sensitivity | **High** |
| 4 | Regulatory & reimbursement (FDA, NICE, EU HTA, Japan NHI, IRA) | S/G | Timing and ceiling of ex-US revenue | **High** |
| 5 | Executive comp alignment | G | $14.5M CEO package at a company with no GAAP earnings | Medium |
| 6 | Scope 1+2 GHG, TCFD disclosure | E | Cost of capital optics only; asset-light, no manufacturing | **Low** |
| 7 | Scope 3 / supply chain (CDMO-dependent) | E | Future CSRD-style disclosure burden; no internal plants | Low |
| 8 | Device/inhaler waste, water, biodiversity | E | BRINSUPRI is an oral tablet; ARIKAYCE uses a nebuliser, no HFC propellants | **Immaterial** |

**Key insight:** INSM is *not* an ESG-risk story in the conventional sense — its carbon profile is near-best-in-class — but it sits at the epicentre of the **US drug-pricing debate**, which is where its ESG risk actually converts into DCF risk.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW**

- **Scope 1: 2,622 tCO₂e; Scope 2 (location): 2,264 tCO₂e** (2025 reporting period, SCS Global Services verification statement). ~4,886 tCO₂e combined.
- **Scope 1+2+3 (2024): 70,522 tCO₂e** — i.e. ~93% of footprint is Scope 3 (CDMO manufacturing, clinical supply, distribution).
- **Intensity: ~2.7 tCO₂e per $M of 2026E revenue** (~$1.81–1.87B guided) — roughly an order of magnitude below large-cap pharma. Scope 3-inclusive intensity ~38 tCO₂e/$M, unremarkable but not a risk.
- **Renewable electricity: 19%** — modest, and the clearest gap; a plausible investor engagement topic but not financially material.
- **Positive:** publishes an annual Responsibility Report *and* a 2025 TCFD report with **independent limited assurance on Scope 1 & 2** — assurance is uncommon for a company of this size and is a genuine governance-of-ESG positive. The TCFD document explicitly addresses "readiness for emerging regulations."
- **No environmental controversies, fines, or remediation liabilities identified.**
- **Transition/physical risk:** negligible. No carbon-intensive assets, no CBAM-exposed imports, no F-gas/inhaler-propellant exposure (a real issue for GSK/AZN — INSM is structurally exempt).

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM** (elevated, concentrated in pricing/access)

**Labour & workforce:** 1,664 FTE, rapidly expanding with the launch; "people-first" positioning; no union disputes, strikes, or labour complaints found. Turnover and diversity metrics were not retrievable — **data gap**, and notably they are not prominently disclosed, which is a mild transparency weakness versus large-cap peers.

**Product safety (the dominant risk):**
- **ARIKAYCE** carries a **boxed warning** for ototoxicity (including deafness), nephrotoxicity and embryofetal toxicity, plus elevated respiratory adverse reactions (hypersensitivity pneumonitis, haemoptysis). It requires audiologic monitoring and restricted distribution. An **sNDA for expansion into newly diagnosed/recurrent MAC lung disease has Priority Review with a PDUFA of 28 Jan 2027** — approval *widens the exposed patient population*, mechanically increasing real-world safety surface and liability. This is the single largest binary ESG-risk event in the model.
- **BRINSUPRI** has a cleaner profile (rash, dry skin, hyperkeratosis, URI, hypertension; no boxed warning) but will be used in a **far larger, less severely ill NCFB population**. A serious off-target signal in a mass population would be far more damaging than anything in ARIKAYCE. Q3 2026 revenue of $400.1M already annualises toward $1.6B, so the exposure grows every quarter.
- No product-liability mass tort identified.

**Access & affordability (principal controversy engine):**
- BRINSUPRI list price **$88,000/year**; management guides net price ~25–35% below list.
- **ICER judged BRINSUPRI "massively overpriced,"** with a health-benefit price benchmark of **$7,500–$12,700/year** — a 7–12x gap to list. This is the most quotable anti-industry datapoint attached to the stock.
- Mitigants: **inLighten Patient Support** program, $0 copay for eligible commercially insured patients, patient assistance for uninsured/underinsured.

**Community / social licence:** Strong — first-in-disease therapy for a previously untreated chronic disease, patient-advocacy partnerships, Japan launch (Oct 2026). Genuine positive social utility, which is why the pricing critique is reputationally sharp.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **7 / 10**

**Positives:**
- **Single class of shares, one share one vote** — no dual-class control structure (better than many founder-led biotechs).
- **Lead Independent Director in place** (David Brennan); independent directors control CEO compensation.
- **Board refresh executed Oct 2026:** Paul Hudson (former Sanofi CEO) and Utpal Koppikar (CFO, Verily) added as independent directors — high-calibre, materially strengthens oversight of a company scaling globally.
- Shareholders **backed all directors and the auditor** at the 13 May 2026 AGM — no activist or withhold campaign.
- Elevated ESG governance: assured emissions data, TCFD, Global Healthcare Compliance program, published compliance policies.

**Deductions:**
- **Combined Chair & CEO:** William Lewis has been CEO since 2012 and Chair since Nov 2018. Mitigated but not cured by the Lead Independent Director.
- **Compensation:** CEO total pay **$14.47M** at a company still GAAP-loss-making ($0.46 Q3 2026 loss/share). Defensible on the back of a $1.4B launch, but it invites scrutiny of PSU metrics.
- **Disclosure integrity under question:** Pomerantz is investigating whether Insmed and officers/directors engaged in securities fraud (Nov 2026). Even a meritless investigation signals a disclosure-expectations gap.
- **M&A conflict optics:** an ex-Sanofi CEO joining the board weeks before reports that Sanofi is weighing a bid for Insmed. Hudson is a strong independent, but a prospective acquirer's former chief executive sitting on the target's board is a genuine related-party/governance sensitivity for the current process.

No controlling-family block, though the Lewis Family Trust is a disclosed holder.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US:** FDA labelling, post-marketing surveillance and REMS-type controls are the dominant regulatory channel. The **SEC climate rule is effectively dormant** in the current administration — low exposure. **Inflation Reduction Act:** BRINSUPRI is a **small molecule**, so its Medicare negotiation clock runs on the **7-year** timeline (vs 11 for biologics) — eligibility could arrive around **2032**, materially earlier than biologic-peptide peers. This is the most under-appreciated ESG-adjacent financial risk in the name. State drug-price-transparency and PBM/FTC scrutiny of launch pricing also apply.
- **EU:** **CSRD** — Insmed's EU turnover is likely below the €150M non-EU-parent threshold in the near term, but member-state transposition may capture the EU subsidiary; management is explicitly preparing ("readiness for emerging regulations" in the 2025 TCFD). **EU HTA Regulation (JCA)** currently applies to oncology/orphan products — BRINSUPRI in NCFB is not orphan-designated, so JCA is not yet triggered. **EU Pay Transparency Directive** (2026 transposition) will force gender-pay-gap disclosure. **CBAM: not applicable.**
- **UK:** NICE — active access risk (evaluation paused).
- **Japan:** PMDA approval and NHI reimbursement secured for BRINSUPRI (launched Oct 2026); Japan's biennial price revisions and cost-effectiveness assessments create medium-term net-price drag.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified. Capital structure is convertibles/equity, not sustainability-linked. *Gap: I could not verify the full debt covenant package.*

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (bordering Leader on E, Laggard-leaning on pricing/access)

The profile is genuinely bimodal. On **environmental disclosure and governance hygiene** INSM outperforms most mid-cap biotechs: assured Scope 1+2 data, TCFD, single-class shares, a lead independent director, and a serious board refresh. On **affordability and pricing conduct** it is on the wrong side of the most powerful social critique facing the industry, with a formal ICER rebuke and a paused UK HTA. Neither pole is severe enough to classify it as an "ESG Risk" name, and there are no systemic controversies (no fraud finding, no mass tort, no environmental breach).

*Data gaps, stated honestly:* third-party ESG ratings (MSCI/Sustainalytics) and the DEF 14A/10-K text were not retrievable (SEC EDGAR returned 403; no 10-K/DEF 14A in the local filing store), so board-size, exact independence ratio, PSU metric detail, and employee turnover/diversity figures are unverified. Conclusions on governance rest on press releases, the 2026 proxy search snippets, and the Q3 call participant list.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** for the thesis

ESG is **not the swing factor** for INSM — that remains BRINSUPRI's launch curve, the 28 Jan 2027 ARIKAYCE label decision, and the Sanofi bid. But two ESG channels are live in the DCF and deserve explicit modelling:

1. **Pricing is a real constraint on the $7B peak-sales story, not a branding issue.** When ICER publishes a $7.5–12.7k benchmark against an $88k list price and NICE pauses its review, the market is being told that realised net price will face structural erosion in every ex-US market and growing political pressure in the US. Combined with the **7-year IRA clock for a small molecule**, the long-duration terminal value of this franchise should carry a haircut that consensus peak-sales multiples typically ignore. This is the single most valuable ESG-derived insight for the thesis.
2. **Product-safety surface expands mechanically with success.** The Jan 2027 ARIKAYCE expansion and BRINSUPRI's mass-population rollout both widen real-world exposure at the exact moment revenue concentration peaks. A post-marketing signal is a low-probability, very high-severity event.

**Not thesis-relevant:** carbon, water, waste, CBAM, SEC climate rule, ESG-linked debt — all immaterial here. Do not let an ESG screen penalise INSM on environmental grounds; on that axis it is a leader.

**Governance is adequate but watch the conflicts:** combined Chair/CEO plus an ex-Sanofi CEO on the board during a reported Sanofi approach is a configuration that warrants scrutiny of process integrity and any management-led buyout or retention packages that emerge.

**Bottom line:** treat INSM as ESG-neutral for position sizing, but embed a **pricing/access and IRA duration discount** into BRINSUPRI's terminal economics, and flag the January 2027 FDA action as an ESG-relevant safety-surveillance catalyst alongside its commercial significance.
