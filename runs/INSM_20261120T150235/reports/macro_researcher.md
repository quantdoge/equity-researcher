## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — late-cycle re-tightening with risk-on asset prices.** As of 20 Nov 2026, the US economy is growing above trend (~2–3%) with unemployment at 4.1%, but inflation has re-accelerated to 3.8% and the Fed has *reversed course* — after cutting from 5.33% (mid-2024) to ~3.63% (mid-2026), it hiked 25bp in September 2026 and the 18 Nov minutes show "almost all" officials expect **more hikes in the months ahead**. The long end has repriced violently: 10Y at 5.13%, 30Y at 5.47%, a +39bp 2s10s *bear steepener* (not a recession signal). Critically, the **10Y real yield is ~2.97%** (5.13% nominal less 2.16% breakeven) — the highest sustained cost of capital in two decades. Credit is still open (BAA–10Y spread 1.52%, near cycle tights), which is why equities and biotech (XBI +28% YoY) have held up even as discount rates rose.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

INSM is a $32.2B commercial-stage biotech (beta 0.81) monetizing ARIKAYCE and the newly launched BRINSUPRI (brensocatib), which Q3'26 revenue growth of +268% YoY and management's $1.35–1.4B first-full-year / $7B peak-sales guide have turned from a pipeline story into a launch story.

**Negative**
- **Real discount rate (dominant).** INSM remains a long-duration asset: the majority of its $32B equity value sits in post-2028 BRINSUPRI/TPIP cash flows. With a high-beta biotech WACC near 9–11%, every +100bp of real rate compresses intrinsic value roughly 8–12%. The 30Y at 5.47% is the specific level that hits terminal value hardest.
- **Fed hiking bias.** The September hike plus a hawkish November minutes removes the "rate cuts = biotech re-rating" tailwind that supported the sector in 2025. If CPI's 5.7% 3-month run persists, another 25–50bp is live into 2027.
- **Wage/SG&A inflation.** CPI 3.8% with a 1,664-person commercial build-out and a US field force scaling for BRINSUPRI means real cost pressure on opex. Partly offset by ~80%+ gross margins on an oral drug.
- **USD strength (+3.9% in two months).** A headwind to ex-US ARIKAYCE/BRINSUPRI revenue translation and, at the margin, raises the EUR cost of a US target for a European acquirer like Sanofi.
- **Policy: Section 232 pharma tariffs + MFN pricing.** The April 2026 executive order made 100% default tariffs on patented pharma imports enforceable from 31 July 2026, and MFN pricing is now law. Direct tariff exposure appears limited if Insmed's supply chain is onshore/US-based, but **MFN is the bigger threat**: the $7B BRINSUPRI peak-sales thesis rests on premium US orphan/rare-disease pricing.
- **Refinancing cost.** Any convert/term-debt rollover now prices off a 5.1% 10Y rather than the 4.2% of 2025. *(Exact maturity ladder not available in this dataset — flag for credit work.)*

**Positive**
- **Credit conditions are benign** (BAA spread 152bp). Investment-grade pharma acquirers (Sanofi A-rated) retain cheap, ample financing capacity — directly supportive of the 19 Nov takeover report that drove INSM +21% in one session.
- **Growth and employment** (GDP +2.1% YoY, unemployment 4.1%) underpin commercial insurance coverage and patient volumes; rare-disease demand is close to non-cyclical.
- **Contained breakevens (2.16%)** suggest inflation expectations are still anchored, capping how far the Fed must go.
- **INSM is transitioning from long-duration to near-duration.** Management now guides to a **cash-flow-positive year ahead**. As it turns FCF-positive, its sensitivity to the discount rate mechanically falls — the single most important offset in this macro regime.

**Neutral**
- Yield curve shape and unemployment — no cyclical threat to the franchise.

**Cross-check:** despite a ~+80bp rise in long yields, XBI returned **+28.3%** (113.34 → 145.38) between 20 Nov 2025 and 20 Nov 2026, while INSM returned **−26.4%** (199.64 → 146.99). The 55-point underperformance is *almost entirely idiosyncratic* (the −17% 18 Dec 2025 and −23% 7 May 2026 drawdowns, then +15% on the 29 Oct Q3 beat and +21% on the Sanofi report). **Macro is a second-order driver of INSM's P&L — but a first-order driver of its multiple.**

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **Neutral** (leaning Unfavourable on rates)

The pure macro backdrop — real yields near 3%, a hiking Fed, and MFN/tariff policy — is unambiguously a headwind for a high-multiple, duration-heavy biotech. It is not, however, the binding constraint: INSM's own launch execution, the January 2027 ARIKAYCE label decision, and M&A optionality dominate. Tight credit spreads and the shift toward cash-flow positivity offset roughly half the rate drag. Net: neutral with a negative skew.

## 5. The Scenario That Would Materially Surprise (Bear)

**A hawkish Fed overshoot into a real-yield shock.** If the 5.7% three-month CPI run forces 50–75bp of additional hikes by mid-2027, the 10Y breaks 5.75–6.0% and the 10Y real yield exceeds 3.5%. Growth stocks de-rate on the discount rate *and* on the realization that a $32B takeout of a pre-profit biotech becomes harder to underwrite — precisely the M&A bid that has been masking the rate headwind (XBI's +28% decoupling is M&A-driven, not rate-driven). A 25–35% multiple contraction in INSM is plausible even with BRINSUPRI hitting guidance. The compounding risk: MFN pricing is implemented against BRINSUPRI, breaking the $7B peak-sales assumption at the same time.

**The bull mirror image:** a sharp labour crack (unemployment 4.1% → 4.7%) forces the Fed to abandon the hiking bias; the long end rallies 60–80bp, real yields fall back under 2.5%, and INSM's commercial de-risking plus a live Sanofi bid compound into a sharply higher valuation. Neither outcome is idiosyncratic — which is exactly why this macro call matters.

**Data gaps:** Insmed's geographic revenue split, debt maturity schedule, and COGS sourcing footprint were not available in this dataset; the tariff and refinancing conclusions above are therefore directional, not quantified.
