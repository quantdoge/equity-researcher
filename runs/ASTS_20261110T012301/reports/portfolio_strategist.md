**Where they split is the time horizon and the appropriate instrument:**

- *Neutral / risk-flag:* Sector, Geo-Legal, Alt Data (3).
- *Positive:* Growth (1) — and even Growth's buy is framed as a venture-style option with a conditional exercise price of 45 satellites.

**Resolution rule applied.** My framework gives precedence to forensic and financial-viability evidence over narrative optionality. The Short Analyst's finding — **$0 of revenue ever recognised from the SpaceMobile service that the entire market cap capitalises** — plus the unexplained $667M receivable, are not opinions; they are disclosure facts that degrade the reliability of every 2027–2030 estimate the Growth case rests on. The Growth thesis is not wrong about the *opportunity*; it is unverifiable about the *timing and per-share capture*.

**Directional consensus: negative on the equity at $36.89. Agreement level: MEDIUM-HIGH on the conclusion, LOW on the path.**

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation

### **0.0% in the core portfolio. 0.25–0.5% maximum, only in an explicitly designated venture/option sleeve.**

**The Kelly arithmetic kills the position.** I weight the bull case generously (Growth's own targets, median PT ~$78):
- P(success by end-2027) requires *three* joint conditions — launch cadence holds, capital raised without punitive dilution, and Starlink does not close the broadband gap: 0.7 × 0.6 × 0.6 ≈ **25%**.
- Payoff: **+110%** (to ~$78) vs **−55%** (to ~$17, still above Value's $12 IV).
- E = 0.25(+110%) + 0.75(−55%) = **−13.8%**.
- Kelly f* = E/σ² with σ_annual ≈ 106%: **negative → allocation = zero.** Even on Growth's more favourable probabilities (say 35%/2.5x) the edge is a rounding error against 106% volatility and a **fat left tail** (dilution spiral, class-action expansion, licence condition).

**Risk-budget framing (the decisive sizing input).** At β 3.61 and σ 106%, a 1% ASTS weight contributes roughly the same portfolio risk as a **3–4% weight in a normal 25–30% vol stock**. Against a 10–12% portfolio vol target, 1% ASTS consumes ~6–8% of the *entire* risk budget for one binary name. That is not a position; it is an unrewarded concentration. A 0.5% cap keeps a total loss at −50bp of portfolio — the correct definition of an option premium.

**Verdict on size: ≤0.5%, and only if the mandate can tolerate a −100% outcome on that sleeve.**

## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

**Sector — the classification is wrong, and that matters.** FMP files ASTS under "Telecommunications Services," which pulls GOOG/META/NFLX/VZ/T as peers. Those are **false comparables** (independently flagged by Sector, Value, Quant and Fin Risk). Economically ASTS is *satellite D2D / new-space*, so it does **not** add to a legacy telecom overweight — but it absolutely adds to a **speculative space / pre-profit growth cluster**, which is the exposure that needs the cap.

**Factor exposure — this is where the position is most damaging.**
- Adds **β 3.61** to a book already long high-beta growth (QQQ correlation 0.53).
- Adds **negative momentum** — 12-1 return **−12.75%**, worst in the comp set; a −77% max drawdown with no base.
- Adds **negative quality** (ROE −14%, no operating cash flow, F-Score 5/9) and **negative value** (P/E n.m., P/B 3.95, P/FCF n.m.).
- **One genuine positive:** it is not factor-crowded. It is uniquely expensive and uniquely volatile — there is no de-crowding risk because there is no crowd of institutions (institutions cut −3.56% QoQ).

## 6. Risk Management Rules

**Price stop.** Hard exit on a **weekly close below $27.50** (below the 6-Nov low of $27.58). That is −25% from here — wide by design, because with σ 106% and routine 15–30% daily moves, a tighter stop is noise-stopped, not risk-managed. On an option-sized 0.5% position, −25% costs 12bp of portfolio.

**Thesis invalidation — exit on ANY of these, irrespective of price:**
1. **Q4'26 revenue < $61M** → guidance credibility gone; the dilution math becomes self-defeating.
2. **< 25–30 satellites in orbit by mid-2027** (vs the 45 target) → the FY27/FY28 revenue base that supports 7.8x 2028E collapses; 22x forward becomes 60x+.
3. **The $667M receivable does not resolve to cash collected** by the FY26 10-K → forensic escalation; accounting risk migrates from "narrative" to "real."
4. **Another equity/convert raise priced below ~$45** before commercial service → maximum dilution at minimum valuation, the exact mechanism the class action alleges.
5. **Credible evidence Starlink achieves broadband-grade (not SMS/voice) D2D** ahead of ASTS scale-up → the aperture moat is stranded capital.
6. **SEC formal investigation, or the class period broadened to capture the Oct–Nov 2026 events** → governance-driven exit, price irrelevant.

**Trim trigger.** **$54–56: trim one-half.** That is where the sell-side cluster sits (Scotiabank $55.70, Piper $54, B.Riley $65, UBS $78, Cantor $90, Berenberg $92, DB $93) and it is **4x Value's base-case IV**. **Above $65: exit the remainder** unless ≥45 satellites are in orbit *and* quarterly revenue exceeds $150M. Do not let a 2.5x gain on an unproven thesis become a round trip.

**Event calendar (hard re-underwrite dates):** 13-Nov-26 lead-plaintiff deadline; 13-Dec-26 second class deadline; each BlueBird launch; any capital-markets announcement; late-Feb/Mar 2027 Q4+FV26 print.

## 7. Final Portfolio Action: **PASS — Do Not Initiate**

**Primary action: PASS at $36.89.** Place ASTS on the Tier-2 watchlist with the pre-committed trigger ladder above. For mandates holding an explicit venture/option sleeve, permit a **maximum 0.25–0.5%** position, entered only on the Tranche-1 condition — never at current levels.

**For existing holders: REDUCE.** Trim into any rally toward $50–55 (the sell-side target cluster), retain at most a half-sized option position, and exit fully below $27.50.

**Rationale in one paragraph.** This is a genuinely differentiated technology — large-aperture phased arrays delivering broadband-grade D2D to unmodified handsets, validated by AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Rakuten, Google and an FCC 248-satellite SCS authorisation. That is real, and it is why this is a **Pass and not a Short**. But the portfolio question is not "is the technology good," it is "does this position earn its risk budget," and on every measure that governs that decision it fails: **negative expectancy** (Kelly f* < 0), **the highest volatility and beta in any candidate set** (σ 106%, β 3.61, −77% drawdown), **0.76 correlation to the adjacent space bet** we would already own, **zero revenue from the service being valued**, **~$4–6B of unfunded capital to be raised at a −72% share price**, and a **governance cluster** — 71.6% founder control, a $147–183M CEO hedge three weeks before a value-destroying disclosure, and PSUs tied to *raising capital* — that structurally subordinates per-share outcomes to enterprise-value outcomes. The 78% drawdown has removed the *valuation* bubble; it has not removed the *dilution* mechanism. We should let the March 2027 print and the receivables answer arrive before paying for optionality.
