**Business context:** US-domiciled (Midland, TX), 1,126 employees, ~$15.1B market cap, beta 2.85. ### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① Taiwan / US–China flashpoint — Probability: Low–Medium (12–24m); Impact: High**
ASTS signed a strategic cooperation MOU with **Taiwan Mobile** (SCSP ISF Voices 2026; r/ASTSpaceMobile). Taiwan is the most acute cross-Strait flashpoint, and a US satellite operator embedding in Taiwanese civil resilience invites PRC pressure and raises the salience of the constellation as a wartime target. Separately, China's own Guowang/SatNet programs and ASAT/EW capabilities are a structural threat to any LEO D2D asset. Note the asymmetry: negligible revenue, material tail risk. This is a *strategic-optionality-vs-tail-risk* trade, not a revenue driver.

**② Spectrum sovereignty in Japan and Europe — Probability: High; Impact: Medium**
Both anchor markets are structured as **sovereign industrial programs, not commercial contracts**. Japan's MIC selected Rakuten's J-LEO proposal with ~$1B of state support and an explicit **"Japanese-flagged"** mid-band constellation requirement, with service mandated by **end-March 2029** (Business Wire/StockTitan, 9 Nov 2026). Europe is moving the same way: four incumbent MNOs signed an LOI on 19 Oct 2026 to jointly bid for **Mobile Satellite Services spectrum**, with EU "strategic autonomy"/IRIS² politics favouring European flags. Risk: ASTS supplies the satellites but may not capture the economics; local-content, data-residency, and flag-state conditions can compress margins and force technology transfer. Timeline slippage against a hard 2029 Japanese deadline is a realistic adverse scenario.

**③ Space-access chokepoint & export controls — Probability: Medium–High; Impact: High**
ASTS launches on **SpaceX Falcon 9 — a direct competitor** (Starlink Direct-to-Cell), a structural hostage dynamic. Management is responding by funding its own reusable rocket with Firefly (24/7 Wall St., 7 Nov 2026), which adds dilution and execution risk. Compounding this, satellite technology sits under **ITAR/EAR (USML Cat. XV)**: supplying satellites to a Japanese-flagged JV and cooperating with Taiwan Mobile creates genuine technology-transfer/licensing friction, and any tightening of US export-control policy toward allied-but-foreign programs directly gates deployment.

*Secondary:* US federal procurement concentration — government is a minority of backlog today but expected to "scale most significantly near term" (Q2'26 call). Continuing-resolution politics, protest risk, and DOGE-style program cuts are live variables for the fastest-growing revenue line.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active securities class action (material, ongoing).** *Hunter v. AST SpaceMobile*, **W.D. Tex. No. 7:26-cv-00378**, class period **4 Mar 2025 – 15 Jul 2026**, alleging §10(b)/Rule 10b-5 misstatements on the **sufficiency of the company's capital/liquidity position** and the **durability of its competitive lead** in D2D (Robbins Geller; Rosen; Kessler Topaz; Kaplan Fox; Levi & Korsinsky; Bronstein Gewirtz; SBS Law — PRNewswire/GlobeNewswire, 6–9 Nov 2026). Lead-plaintiff deadline **13 Nov 2026** (a second matter carries a 12/13/2026 deadline). One complaint summary ties the alleged exposure to the **Feb 2026 $1.0B convertible-note offering** and a subsequent ~15.2% share decline (Grabar Law). I view this as a *financing-narrative* suit — the recurring risk is that every future capital raise (convert, Firefly, constellation financing) seeds a new class period.

**FCC — favourable but conditional.** On **22 Apr 2026** the FCC granted commercial SCS authority covering an NGSO constellation of up to **248 satellites** (243 beyond the five approved in 2024), operating on **700/800 MHz low-band spectrum in coordination with Verizon and AT&T** (FCC DA-26-391; Business Wire; SatelliteToday). Caveat: trade press immediately flagged **questions about speed-to-market** (Communications Daily, 23 Apr 2026) — authorization ≠ capacity. Additional STAs (e.g. 30-day 800 MHz testing) show continuing reliance on temporary authority.

**Other regulators:** Japan MIC (J-LEO award — secured); EU MSS/S-band allocation (pending, political); ~25-country partner footprint implies dozens of national licensing proceedings, each with its own conditions.

**No DOJ/SEC formal investigation, no patent/IP litigation, and no antitrust action were identified** in this search cycle — a genuine positive, though absence of evidence here is weak evidence given the search budget.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **ITAR/EAR technology transfer (highest-priority flag).** Supplying a Japanese-flagged constellation and partnering with Taiwan Mobile requires deemed-export analysis, Technical Assistance Agreements, and foreign-national access controls. A USML Category XV misstep would be a program-ending event, not a fine.
2. **FOCI (Foreign Ownership, Control, Influence).** Rakuten is both a major shareholder and now JV counterparty *and* a foreign government-subsidised program participant. As Space Force/STR work scales, FOCI mitigation (board/security segregation) is a realistic requirement — monitor for proxy disclosure.
3. **FCPA / anti-corruption.** Selling into state-owned or state-linked telcos and sovereign programs (stc group, MIC-backed J-LEO, government tenders) with local intermediaries is textbook FCPA third-party risk. No evidence of issues — a control to test, not an allegation.
4. **Sanctions screening.** A 25-country partner footprint (GCC, Africa) requires ongoing OFAC/EU screening of MNO counterparties and any end-user exposure in sanctioned-adjacent markets.
5. **Data privacy.** Cross-border telecom metadata (GDPR, Japan APPI, Taiwan PDPA) plus US government data handling creates conflicting-obligation risk, particularly for EU traffic.
6. **Government contract compliance** — FAR/DFARS, DCAA auditability, and Cost Accounting Standards as the government book scales from a minority to a majority of revenue.

---

### 5. Overall Rating: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

Upgraded from a baseline "Medium" for a US-domiciled company with no China/Russia operations, on three specific grounds: (i) revenue is **gated country-by-country by sovereign spectrum regulators**, several of which are explicitly pursuing national-champion industrial policy (Japan, EU); (ii) an **active securities fraud class action** with a Nov 2026 milestone and a structurally repeatable trigger (each capital raise); and (iii) an unresolved **launch/export-control chokepoint** with a competitor (SpaceX) controlling access to orbit. These are offset by strong rule-of-law in all revenue-generating jurisdictions, a clean sanctions profile, and a favourable FCC outcome.
