# ESG Analysis — AST SpaceMobile, Inc. (NASDAQ: ASTS)

**Sector:** Communication Services / Telecommunications Services (satellite direct-to-device)
**Status:** Pre-scale commercial; FY2025 revenue $70.9M vs. ~$15.1B market cap; ~1,126 employees; beta 2.85
**Key context:** Founder-controlled, capital-hungry satellite operator whose ESG risks are overwhelmingly **governance and licence-to-operate**, not carbon.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM** (rising)

The carbon footprint is trivial; the **space-environment footprint is not**.

- **Constellation scale:** FCC licence for 248 satellites (96 for global coverage), orbits 508–740 km. First-gen antennas 64 m²; **next-gen 223 m² (~tennis-court-sized)**, an ~4× increase. FCC order DA-26-391 granted operations for 223 additional satellites, finding AST's orbital debris analysis "sufficient" — a genuine regulatory validation.
- **Debris / collision:** NASA has flagged that the very large arrays could act as a "flytrap" for debris. SpaceX has filed multiple FCC complaints alleging collision risk during deorbit manoeuvres and insufficient transparency (widely covered, incl. PCMag, Gizmodo). **No fines or licence sanctions to date** — the FCC has instead told the parties to "stay in their lane." Cost so far: legal/regulatory spend, not penalties.
- **Deorbit compliance:** AST asserts full demisability and 5-year atmospheric reentry, compliant with the FCC's 5-year LEO deorbit rule. Negative: passive-deorbit collision-probability modelling is contested by competitors.
- **Astronomy:** This is the best-documented environmental controversy. BlueWalker 3 was measured as one of the brightest artificial objects in the night sky (Nandakumar et al., *Nature*, 2023), with additional concerns about radio-astronomy interference in wideband receivers. **Mitigants:** an NSF coordination agreement, adherence to IAU *Dark and Quiet Skies* guidelines, precision beamforming to avoid radio-quiet zones, advanced coatings, and reported roll/tilt manoeuvres to reduce brightness (Nov 2026). These mitigants are real but unproven at 248-satellite scale.
- **Disclosure gap:** No Scope 1/2/3 inventory, no TCFD/CDP reporting, no quantified reentry-emissions (alumina) analysis. This is a genuine data void — we cannot compute carbon intensity, though it is unlikely to be material given revenue scale.
- **Physical climate risk:** Low. Midland, TX manufacturing faces hail/wind exposure; launch-site weather risk is operational, not financial.

**Trajectory:** Negative — risk scales with satellite count, and the debris/astronomy externalities are precisely the ones regulators and the ITU are now codifying.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **LOW–MEDIUM**

**Positives**
- Core product directly addresses the digital divide (unserved/underserved populations, ocean and aviation coverage) — a legitimate, verifiable social benefit that underpins government and MNO partnerships (Deutsche Telekom, Orange, Telefónica, Vodafone LOI on a joint MSS spectrum bid).
- Workforce small (1,126) and high-skill; no disclosed fatalities, strikes, or labour disputes. Say-on-pay passed at the 2026 annual meeting.
- Rapid revenue ramp (Q1 2026 $14.7M → Q2 2026 $31.5M; FY26 guidance $150–200M) implies hiring, not retrenchment.

**Concerns**
- **Human-capital concentration:** deep dependence on a small engineering cadre and on the founder; equity-heavy compensation ties retention to a volatile share price (beta 2.85).
- **Supply chain:** launch services are substantially supplied by SpaceX, a direct D2D competitor — a structural supply-chain vulnerability with social/operational dimensions. Supplier labour standards are undisclosed.
- **Privacy / lawful intercept:** direct-to-handset service inherently routes traffic through satellites; civil-liberties and government-access exposure is unquantified but rising as government contracts grow.
- **Disclosure:** no workforce safety (TRIR), turnover, or D&I metrics published.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10**

**Rationale — the dominant ESG weakness.**

*Negatives (weighted heavily):*
1. **Controlled company status.** ASTS qualifies as a Nasdaq "controlled company." Abel Avellan and permitted transferees hold all Class C shares and **~71.6% of combined voting power** (2026 proxy; previously ~80.4%). Multi-class structure with super-voting Class C (capped at 10 votes/share). Minority holders have effectively no ability to influence board composition or strategy.
2. **CEO/Chairman duality.** Avellan is simultaneously Chairman and CEO — verified from the Q3 2026 SOX certification signed "Chairman and Chief Executive Officer." No independent lead-independent-director counterweight disclosed.
3. **Board is not majority-independent by requirement.** As a controlled company ASTS is exempt from Nasdaq's majority-independent board rule. Board is 10 directors in an 11-seat structure with a vacancy — i.e., not fully constituted.
4. **Reporting-integrity history.** ASTS has previously identified **material weaknesses in internal control over financial reporting** and restated SPAC-era financials. Current status is unverified (see gaps) — but the pattern warrants a discount.
5. **Compensation design.** Non-CEO NEO base salary just $250k with bonuses at 95% of target, and PSUs explicitly tied to **capital-raising goals** — a design that can reward dilution rather than per-share value. (Mitigant: 50% PSU weighting to performance; new Nasdaq-compliant clawback policy adopted.)
6. **Insider monetisation.** Form 144 filings include a ~2.5M-share (~$183M) transaction by Avellan via AA Gables — insider sales that feed directly into the litigation narrative.

*Positives:*
- KPMG ratified as 2026 auditor (Big Four).
- All 10 director nominees elected; say-on-pay approved; independent directors receive meaningful equity ($192,653 each in 2025) aligning them to shareholders.
- Clawback policy, Stockholders' Agreement, and corporate governance guidelines (independent-director executive sessions) in place.
- Revenue-linked annual bonuses create some operational accountability.

**Verdict:** Structurally sound processes layered on top of a control architecture that subordinates minority interests. A 3/10, not a 1/10, because disclosure quality and auditor/board mechanics are adequate.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **FCC (primary):** SCS/D2D licensing, orbital-debris rules (5-year deorbit), spectrum transfer approvals. FCC granted full commercial approval of the 248-satellite constellation and found debris analysis sufficient for 223 additional satellites — **constructive, but discretionary and revisable**. Licence modification is the single most powerful lever a regulator holds over this equity.
- **ITU:** 194-nation Space Sustainability Forum; spectrum coordination filings are contested by SpaceX. International market entry depends on ITU/administration coordination.
- **NSF / NASA / IAU:** informal but real standard-setters on astronomy interference. An NSF coordination agreement is in place — evidence of engagement, and a template for future binding commitments.
- **U.S. national security / export controls (ITAR/EAR):** government business is a stated revenue source; export-control compliance is an unquantified governance risk.
- **EU CSRD / CBAM / SEC climate rule:** **Not applicable / low.** As a U.S. issuer with negligible Scope 1–2 emissions, CBAM is irrelevant. EU CSRD would only bite indirectly via European MNO customers' Scope 3 supplier demands. The SEC climate-disclosure rule's status does not currently create a filing obligation.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** **None identified.** The $1.0B and $400M convertibles are conventional, not sustainability-linked — a mild positive (no ESG pricing step-ups).

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD**

Driven almost entirely by the **G** pillar (controlled company, 71.6% founder voting power, Chair/CEO duality, active securities litigation, ICFR history, dilution-heavy capital strategy). E is Medium and deteriorating on trend but currently manageable and partly mitigated. S is genuinely positive on product impact and low on harm. The company does not screen as an "ESG Risk" name — there are no systemic ethical failures — but it fails the governance-quality bar most institutional mandates apply.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE (moderate) — a cost-of-capital and dilution drag, not a thesis-breaker**

- **Governance is priced, but under-modelled.** The controlled structure and serial convertibles are the mechanism through which the *equity story itself* is diluted. ESG analysis here converges with fundamental analysis: the governance score is the best single predictor of per-share outcomes. A 71.6%-controlled, Chair/CEO-led issuer with a live class action and PSUs tied to capital raising warrants a **100–200bp governance discount** to the required return.
- **Environmental risk is a licence-to-operate tail, not a base case.** The FCC's debris findings are supportive today. But the next-gen 223 m² arrays plus SpaceX's active FCC campaign and astronomy scrutiny mean a **non-trivial probability (est. 10–15%) of conditions or delays on future licence modifications**, which would push out the deployment timeline that the entire valuation rests on.
- **Positive offsets:** the digital-divide mission is authentic and commercially aligned with MNO and government partners; the NSF/IAU engagement shows a functioning stakeholder-response capability; no ESG-linked debt step-ups.
- **Actionable monitoring triggers:** (1) SEC settlement quantum and any ICFR re-disclosure; (2) any FCC condition or delay attached to a licence modification following SpaceX complaints; (3) further Avellan/AA Gables Form 144 sales; (4) whether the 11th board seat is filled with a genuinely independent director and a lead independent director is appointed; (5) first Scope 1–3 disclosure or TCFD-aligned report.

**For the memo:** treat ESG as **Neutral-to-Negative**. It is not a reason to avoid ASTS, but it argues for a governance/execution risk premium, position-size discipline, and scepticism toward management's capital-sufficiency claims — exactly the issues at the heart of the pending litigation.
