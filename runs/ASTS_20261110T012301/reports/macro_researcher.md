## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — late-cycle re-acceleration with a hostile policy reaction function. Risk-on for cash-generative cyclicals; acutely risk-off for long-duration, pre-profit growth.**

The US economy is *not* slowing into a recession — it is re-accelerating into an inflation problem. Real GDP grew **3.4% in Q3 2026** (advance, published Oct 29), up from 2.2% in Q2 and 2.5% in Q1, and the unemployment rate *fell* to **4.1% in October** from 4.5% a year earlier. At the same time CPI re-accelerated: YoY inflation bottomed at **2.39% in January 2026** and has climbed to **3.72% in September 2026** (3.30% Jul → 3.35% Aug → 3.72% Sep). Nominal personal outlays are running +6.3% YoY, corroborating the reacceleration.

The Fed has therefore reversed course. After cutting from 5.33% (Aug 2024) to a **3.63% trough (May–Aug 2026)**, it **hiked 25bp in September 2026** to a 3.75–4.00% target range and paused in October (effective rate **3.88%**). The Reuters poll of Oct 20 and market pricing (US Bank: December hike plus **two more in 2027**) point to a *resumed hiking cycle*, not a cutting one. The 2Y at **4.74%** sits 86bp *above* the effective funds rate — the market is pricing the hikes, not hoping them away.

The critical detail for ASTS is *where* the curve moved. This is a **bear steepener** driven by real yields and term premium, not by growth optimism: the 10Y TIPS real yield rose from **2.07% (June 1) to 2.95% (Oct 5)** and sits at **2.63% (Nov 6)**, while 10Y breakevens are contained at **2.22%** and actually falling. The 30Y at **5.47%** versus 2Y at 4.74% (+39bp 2s10s, no inversion) is a fiscal/term-premium signal. In plain terms: **the discount rate applied to long-dated cash flows is rising even if the Fed eventually stops.** Credit is still open (HY OAS 3.42%) but has widened **+74bp from 2.68% on Sept 22** — watch that trend, because ASTS's funding model depends on it.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Negative — Real yields / discount rate (the dominant channel).** AST SpaceMobile is the purest long-duration asset in the universe: a **$15.1bn market cap on $42.3m of Q3 revenue** (~90x annualised sales), with essentially all value residing in a 90+ satellite constellation delivering cash flows post-2030. At $21–23m capex per BlueBird, that is ~$1.9–2.1bn still to spend. For an asset with an equity duration of roughly 15–20 years, **each +100bp in the real discount rate removes ~15–25% of intrinsic value**. The tape confirms it: the 10Y real yield rose 88bp from June to the Oct 5 peak and **ASTS fell from $133.09 (May 28) to $27.58 (Nov 6) — a 79% drawdown**; shares are **-46% YoY** ($68.70 → $36.89) against a ~90bp rise in the 10Y. That is a textbook duration repricing, not a coincidence.

**Negative — Cost of capital and refinancing.** ASTS has funded itself with convertibles: $400m at 4.25% due 2032, $1bn at 2.25% due 2036 (Feb 2026), a $1bn 2034 issue, and a second $1bn convert this year — roughly $2.4–3.4bn of low-coupon paper, against ~$2.72bn of cash. Those terms were only available because the equity was at $100+. The 2036 converts carry a capped call at **$149.20** with the stock at **$36.89** — 75% below the cap. In a 4.9% 10Y / 3.7% CPI world, the next raise prices at a materially higher coupon or a much lower conversion premium, i.e. **more cash interest or more dilution**. The securities class action alleging ASTS "overstated the sufficiency of its capital position" (class period to Jul 15, 2026; lead-plaintiff deadline Nov 13) makes access to the capital markets *itself* a macro-sensitive variable: if HY spreads widen through 4.5% and equity volatility stays at this level, the funding window narrows precisely when capex peaks. This is the single largest macro-financial risk.

**Negative — Inflation vs. zero pricing power.** ASTS bears 3.7% CPI on a fixed-price capex programme (satellite manufacturing in Midland, launch services, ~1,126 employees including scarce RF/engineering talent in a 4.1%-unemployment labor market). It cannot pass through costs: revenue is set by pre-negotiated revenue-share agreements with MNO partners (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Rakuten). **Cost inflation with no price mechanism is a direct hit to the terminal margin assumption** — and it hits at the worst time, during the build.

**Negative — Strong USD / international revenue mix.** Almost all future revenue is non-US: Rakuten (Japan), Vodafone (Europe/Africa), Telefónica, MTN, Telstra, Smart. The broad dollar at **121.9**, up 3.4% in two months on the hawkish Fed, (a) reduces USD-translated revenue, and (b) squeezes EM/Japanese MNO partners whose local discount rates and dollar-denominated obligations both rise with US real yields — throttling their appetite for satellite revenue-share commitments. US real rates at 2.6%+ are a global tightening impulse.

**Slightly positive — Growth and credit availability.** 3.4% GDP and HY OAS at 3.42% mean capital markets are not shut and telecom demand is not cyclically impaired. Mobile connectivity is a staple, and a "no landing" economy supports consumer willingness to pay for premium coverage. Government/D2D demand (Space Force, DoD) is a real revenue line, though exposed to appropriations risk under a post-election Congress.

**Negative — Fiscal policy / term premium.** The 5.47% 30Y reflects deficit financing and a post-election policy mix. A fiscal-driven bear steepening in the long end hurts ASTS **regardless of what the Fed does** — this is the risk that a "Fed is cutting" thesis fails to rescue the stock.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Growth is fine and credit is open — but every variable that actually drives ASTS's valuation (real yields, cost of capital, inflation-on-costs, USD, term premium) is pointed the wrong way simultaneously. The regime is a resumed hiking cycle into 3.7% CPI with a bear-steepening curve and beta of 2.85. **Macro is currently a headwind to be survived with company-specific catalysts (satellite launches, Rakuten AST JV, US service activation), not a tailwind to lean on.**

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (Bull)

**Consensus is crowded short duration.** Nearly every economist expects a December hike and markets price two more in 2027 — so the *surprise* is the cycle ending early. If Q4 GDP stalls toward 1% as the 2025 rate increases and trade frictions bite and CPI prints 3.0% or below in December/January, the Fed skips December, and the 10Y real yield falls **75–100bp from 2.63% to ~1.7%**, ASTS's duration math inverts: the same 15–20 year equity duration that cost 15–25% per 100bp delivers **+60% to +100%**, before any company catalyst. Add a compression in HY OAS back toward 2.7% re-opening the convert window cheaply, and you get a violent re-rating off a $27.58 base — the Nov 9 session (+14% on the Rakuten JV, +22% off the low) is a preview of how much convexity sits in this name. **The bear mirror image:** a December hike plus two in 2027 with the 10Y breaking 5.5% and HY OAS >4.5% puts $20–25 in play. Position sizing should reflect that macro, not the launch schedule, is currently the primary driver of the share price.
