# AXON ENTERPRISE (AXON) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $313.05 | ~83.7M diluted shares | Market cap ≈ $26.2B | Net debt ≈ $0.75B | EV ≈ $26.5B | No dividend*

*(Data note: the vendor's ratio tool divides by a rounded 100M share count and a distorted diluted EPS, so its 207x P/E and $31.3B market cap are wrong. All multiples below are rebuilt from reported quarterly statements and the ~81.0M/83.7M basic/diluted counts.)*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow** (Wide in the core franchise)

Axon sells an integrated enforcement platform: TASER conducted-energy weapons **plus** cartridges (recurring consumables), body cameras and in-car Fleet systems, and the Evidence.com cloud (digital evidence management, chain-of-custody, transcription, Redaction/ Draft One AI), now extended into real-time crime centres (Fusus), drones and counter-drone (Dedrone), and emergency-call software (Carbyne/Prepared). Two segments (TASER; Software & Sensors) are increasingly sold as one bundled, multi-year subscription.

**Sources of moat — genuinely present:**
- **Switching costs (high):** Evidence.com is the system of record for evidentiary video. Migrating petabytes of case evidence, re-certifying chain-of-custody, and retraining thousands of officers is operationally painful. Contracts are typically 5–10 years with hardware refresh embedded.
- **Ecosystem / razor-and-blade:** TASER cartridges, docks, batteries and warranty renewals throw off high-margin recurring revenue tied to an installed base that is very hard to displace.
- **Intangible assets / efficient scale:** dominant installed base in US law enforcement, agency-to-agency reference selling, cooperative purchasing vehicles (Sourcewell/GSA) that lower procurement friction, and a brand that is effectively the category default.
- **Evidence the moat widened over 5 years:** revenue $681M (2020) → ~$3.62B (2026E), a **~32% five-year CAGR with remarkable consistency** (26.8%, 37.8%, 31.2%, 33.4%, 33.5%, ~30%), while gross margin was defended at ~60% — i.e., it grew without buying share through price.

**Why I will not call it Wide at the consolidated level:**
- Gross margin has **eroded from 61.5% (2021) to 56.7% (Q3 2026)** as mix shifts toward drones/counter-drone hardware and international/tariff-affected sales — the new frontiers (Anduril, Skydio, Motorola Solutions) are less moated than the TASER/Evidence core.
- A moat must show up in returns. Reported **ROIC was −1.2% (2025), 1.6% (2024), 6.9% (2023) — below WACC in every year.** Stock-based compensation (23% of 2025 revenue) and acquisition amortisation are the culprits. Economically the franchise is probably earning low-double-digit returns pre-SBC, but as reported it does not yet clear the bar.

**Strengths:** revenue predictability is exceptional for a hardware/software hybrid; adjusted EBITDA margin is scaling fast (25.6% guided 2026); balance sheet is sound with $1.7B cash and modest leverage; no dividend — all reinvested.

**Serious blemishes Buffett would flag:**
1. **FCF conversion is poor and erratic.** FCF/net income was 60% in 2025 and *negative* in each of the first three quarters of 2026 (−$54.6M, −$1.0M, −$18.9M) as receivables and inventory ballooned with growth (working capital absorbed ~$607M across the last four reported quarters). Growth is consuming cash, not generating it.
2. **SBC is enormous and dilutive.** $634M in 2025 = 23% of revenue, roughly *five times* net income. Shares went from 71.1M (2022) to 83.7M diluted (Q3-26), ~3%/yr. Buybacks of $157M in 2026 offset only a fraction of issuance. Deduct true SBC from the $375M guided FCF and **economic free cash flow is roughly nil.**
3. Owner earnings (NI + D&A − total capex) were **$71.8M in 2025** — 0.3% of market cap.

**My DCF (9% discount, revenue path +25% → 6% by 2036 reaching ~$12B, FCF margin ramping to 17–20%, 20x exit):** PV of interim FCF ≈ $9.2B + PV of terminal ≈ $21.5B = **≈ $30.5B → ~$365/share** on management's FCF definition (SBC added back as non-cash).

**But SBC is a real cost.** If I haircut terminal FCF margin to ~12% (SBC settling at 10% of revenue, which is what comparable software franchises bear), the same model yields **≈ $215–240/share**. Blending the two — treating SBC as roughly half "real cost, half growth-investment" — gives a **central intrinsic value of ~$300–310/share.**

- **Bear** (growth fades to high-teens, margin stalls at 15%, 18x exit): ~$175
- **Base**: ~$300
- **Bull** (25%+ sustained, 22% margin, 24x exit): ~$425

The stock has **fallen ~65% from its Aug-2025 peak (~$886) and ~44% year-to-date 2026** (from $563 in January to $313 after an 18% post-Q3 drop). That de-rating still leaves it sitting **at, not below, base-case intrinsic value** — a ~4% discount. There is **no margin of safety**; I require 25–30%, which implies buying around **$215–230**.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good operator, aggressive allocator (B+)**

- **Founder-led:** Rick Smith (co-founder, CEO since 1993) still runs the business with an owner's intensity; the "moonshot" culture (TASER 10, Draft One AI) is genuinely innovative, and the company has repeatedly hit multi-year targets. Founder economics align with long-term owners.
- **Candour:** guidance is set conservatively and usually beaten, though 2026 broke that pattern — revenue growth guidance was trimmed from 32–34% to ~30% and **FCF guidance was cut**, with a "rare miss on Dedrone lumpiness." That is the first real crack in the compounding narrative and worth monitoring.
- **Capital allocation is the weak link.** No dividend (fine). Buybacks only restarted in 2026 ($157M, at prices well above today's) and are trivial against SBC. The real story is **serial M&A**: goodwill and intangibles went from $77M (2023) to **$1.57B (2025)** while total debt rose from $711M to $1.91B (largely convertible notes, ~$115M annual interest) to fund Dedrone, Prepared, Carbyne and Fusus. Some of these are strategically smart (Dedrone in drones, Carbyne in 911); all of them add integration risk, complexity, and amortisation that suppresses reported returns. This is a mild case of empire-building.
- **Insider ownership:** meaningful founder stake (exact percentage not verified in this data pull), no evidence of aggressive insider selling in the data retrieved.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (Watchlist — Buy below ~$225)

**AXON is a wonderful business at a full price.** The core TASER + Evidence.com franchise has a durable, arguably widening moat, 30%+ top-line growth, 60% gross margins, a founder at the helm and a 25%+ adjusted EBITDA margin now in sight. If I could own one public-safety platform for ten years, this is it.

But my discipline is price paid versus value received, and three things stop me from buying today:

1. **No margin of safety.** Base-case intrinsic value (~$300–310) is essentially the current price. The market still pays **28.6x EV/EBITDA and 42x forward non-GAAP EPS** for a business whose *economic* free cash flow after true SBC is near zero.
2. **The moat is not yet converting into cash returns.** ROIC has been below WACC in each of the last four years; FCF conversion is 60% at best and negative in 2026 quarters. A great business that cannot convert earnings into distributable cash is a growth story, not a Buffett compounding machine — *yet*.
3. **Per-share value lags headline growth** because dilution (~3%/yr) and M&A absorb a large share of the value created.

I would be a **buyer at ~$220–235** (a genuine 25–30% discount to base-case value, ~21x 2027E EBITDA), or on evidence of two consecutive years of **FCF ≥ 80% of net income, SBC below 15% of revenue, and ROIC above 10%** — at which point the moat would be proven in the numbers and I would pay a higher multiple for it.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

- **Multiple compression (largest risk):** at 28x EV/EBITDA, a deceleration to <20% growth or a 200bp EBITDA-margin disappointment could halve the stock again — as the 65% drawdown from the peak already demonstrates.
- **SBC / dilution:** $580–640M annual stock comp against ~$375M FCF means shareholders are effectively funding employee wealth with equity; economic FCF may be nil for years.
- **Working capital & cash conversion:** receivables from municipal agencies and drone inventory build can keep FCF structurally below reported EBITDA; the 2026 FCF guide cut is a warning.
- **M&A integration and goodwill impairment:** $1.57B of goodwill/intangibles, earnouts, and lumpy Dedrone revenue; a write-down would hit both book value and credibility.
- **Competition:** Motorola Solutions in video/software, Anduril/Skydio in drones, regional and Chinese hardware vendors internationally; AI video-analytics could commoditise part of the Evidence value proposition.
- **Customer/political risk:** concentrated municipal, state and federal budgets; police-funding politics; TASER product-liability and wrongful-death litigation; export controls and tariffs on hardware.
- **Convertible debt:** ~$1.7B of converts carry real interest cost and future dilution above conversion prices.
- **Data-quality caveat:** the 2025 annual filing shows inconsistencies (negative operating income alongside positive net income; a rounded 100M diluted share count; a ~$200M non-operating gain inflating Q1-2026 net income). Reported figures are noisy; conclusions above rely on the quarterly series and company guidance, which are internally consistent.
