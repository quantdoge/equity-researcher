# Unity Software (NYSE: U) — Value Investment Analysis

**Current price: ~$52.81 (market cap ≈ $23.4B; EV ≈ $23.1B incl. $2.65B cash less $2.32B debt)**
*Data note: the daily price feed I have ends Feb-2026 ($18.25); the live quote/profile and Nov-2026 analyst notes (~$50) indicate the stock has since roughly tripled. Fundamentals are current through Q3-2026 (filed 2026-11-05).*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Narrow (and only recently re-earned)**

Unity has two businesses now, and they are economically very different:

| Q3 2026 | Revenue | YoY |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic Grow (AI ad network — "Vector") | $432M | **+90%** |
| Strategic Create (engine/subscriptions) | $165M | +11% |
| Total (incl. run-off legacy) | $622M | +32% |

**Create (the engine).** This is the genuine moat asset: switching costs are real — studios embed Unity in their asset pipelines, middleware, and hiring pools, and re-platforming a shipped live-service game is prohibitively expensive. But the moat was **stress-tested and found weaker than advertised**: the 2023 Runtime Fee revolt forced a public reversal and the CEO's exit, and Create revenue is now roughly $165M/quarter versus ~$350–400M historically. A business that cannot raise price without a customer insurrection does not have pricing power. Growth of +11% is a recovery off a damaged base, not proof of a widening moat.

**Grow (advertising).** Vector's AI is working — 15–20% advertiser performance lift, 41% adjusted EBITDA margin, +90% growth — and there are real data network effects (game telemetry → better ad targeting → more spend). But this is a **scale contest against AppLovin**, an opponent with better ML economics and far more ad-network data. Unity is currently the challenger winning on product, not the incumbent with a durable cost or scale advantage. Ad tech moats historically decay quickly and are hostage to Apple/Google privacy policy.

**Moat verdict:** Narrow. Deep in engine switching costs, shallow in pricing power; real but contestable in ads. **ROIC test fails the Buffett standard:** ROIC was −8% to −14% for 2022–2025. Annualizing Q3-2026 operating income (~$400M NOPAT) against invested capital of ~$3.2B gets you to roughly **10% ROIC — approximately WACC, not above it.** A moated business earns 15%+ consistently; Unity has one good quarter, not a track record.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **4 / 10**

**The good (genuinely improved):**
- Gross margin 74% → **81.9%** (Q3 2026), as ad revenue scales with near-zero marginal cost
- FCF TTM **$607M** (Q4'25 $119M → Q1'26 $66M → Q2'26 $202M → Q3'26 $220M) — a real inflection
- First GAAP profit in company history in Q3 2026 ($122M net income, $0.25 diluted EPS vs $0.05 est.)
- Balance sheet flipped to **$327M net cash** ($2.65B cash vs $2.32B debt)
- Operating leverage is real: opex discipline under the new CEO

**The bad (why the score isn't higher):**
- **SBC is the tell:** TTM stock comp ≈ $330M, ~15% of revenue and **more than half of TTM free cash flow.** Owner earnings — FCF less the true cost of compensating employees in stock — are roughly **$275M**, not $607M. On owner earnings the stock is ~84x.
- Diluted shares 498M vs 442M basic — a **~13% dilution overhang**. Per-share value compounds far slower than enterprise value.
- Accumulated deficit **−$4.39B**; goodwill + intangibles **$3.24B** of $3.53B equity — tangible book is near zero and impairment risk is live (Q1 2026 already carried a ~$350M cost-of-revenue charge that obliterated the quarter).
- Revenue history is erratic, not predictable: +44%, +25%, +57%, **−17%**, +2%. That is not the revenue stream of a franchise business.
- FCF conversion is driven by working capital and deferred revenue; Q3 benefited from collections. One quarter is not a trend.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

Trailing multiples at ~$52.81:
- EV/TTM revenue ($2.18B): **10.6x**
- EV/TTM FCF ($607M): **38x** — and **~84x on SBC-adjusted owner earnings**
- EV/TTM adjusted EBITDA (~$550–600M est.): **~40x**
- Sector-peer median P/E is 33x, but the tool's peer set (AAPL, NVDA, MSFT) is uninformative; the relevant comps are AppLovin, Roblox and Take-Two, all of which trade at premium multiples for far better economics.

**Reverse DCF (the honest way to frame it).** With ~$700M of forward FCF and a 10% discount rate / 3% terminal growth, the current $23.1B EV requires **~18–20% FCF growth for a decade.** At 15% growth the intrinsic value is ≈ **$21.8B (~$44–49/share on 442–498M shares)**; at 20% it is ≈ **$30B (~$60–68/share)**.

My base-case intrinsic value is **$45–55/share** — which is to say the stock is trading *at* fair value, having already priced in several years of successful Vector execution.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment: **Mixed / Improving, unproven**

- **Positive:** CEO Matthew Bromberg (since May 2024) has done what a good operator should — cut the cost base, exit non-strategic lines, ship Vector, and convert a cash-burning business into a cash generator. Debt is being retired; the balance sheet is conservative. He deserves credit.
- **Negative:** Capital allocation history is poor. Unity repurchased **$1.5B of stock in 2022 and $250M in 2023** at prices 2–4x today's level — value destroyed at exactly the wrong moment, funded partly by debt. No buyback since; no dividend (appropriate). SBC remains excessive at 15% of revenue, which transfers shareholder value to employees.
- **Governance:** Founder-led era is over; insider ownership is thin. Track record under current management is ~2 years — too short to award an "owner-operator" premium.
- **M&A:** The ironSource merger (2022) was dilutive and strategically muddled; the cleanup since is the right response, but it was self-inflicted.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD (do not initiate at $52.81)**
### Would become a Buy below ~$35 (≈30% discount to base-case IV)

This is a materially better business than it was two years ago, run by a more disciplined team, with a real product cycle in Vector. It is *not* a Buffett business at *this* price:

1. **No margin of safety.** Price ≈ intrinsic value (base case $45–55). Rule one is not violated by buying, but rule two — never pay full price — is.
2. **Moat unproven.** One quarter of 41% adjusted EBITDA margins after five years of losses does not establish durable ROIC > WACC. I need to see 6–8 quarters of GAAP profitability and 15%+ ROIC before I widen the moat rating.
3. **Owner earnings are ~45% of reported FCF** because of SBC. The stock is not 38x; on economic earnings it is closer to 80x.
4. **Circle of competence caution:** an AI ad-auction business competing with AppLovin and exposed to Apple/Google policy is a fast-moving technology bet, not a predictable 10-year cash flow stream.

If you already own it, hold — the trajectory is right and the balance sheet is strong. If you don't, wait for either (a) a price near $35, or (b) evidence of two more quarters of GAAP profitability with SBC below 10% of revenue and Grow growth still above 40%.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

- **Competitive disintermediation in ads.** AppLovin (Axon) or a Google/Meta entrant compresses Vector's take rate; the +90% growth rate is the single load-bearing assumption in the valuation. Consensus-adjacent multiples of 40x EBITDA leave no room for deceleration.
- **Platform dependency.** Mobile gaming ad demand is cyclical and gated by Apple ATT / Google Privacy Sandbox changes.
- **Create still shrinking relative to history.** Unreal (AAA) and Godot (indie, free) squeeze both ends of the market; the engine's trust damage may be permanent.
- **Dilution.** 13% diluted share overhang plus continued SBC means EPS grows much slower than EBITDA.
- **Goodwill impairment.** $3.2B of intangibles against $3.5B of equity; another large charge (as in Q1 2026) would hit book value and sentiment.
- **Valuation compression.** Beta of 2.1 — this stock fell 24% in one day in Jan-2026 and another 26% on FY-2025 results. A 20% growth disappointment likely costs 40%+ of the share price. That asymmetry is precisely why a margin of safety is required here.
