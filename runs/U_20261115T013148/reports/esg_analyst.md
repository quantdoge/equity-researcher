## 1. ESG Materiality Map

Unity is unusual: it is a **two-business company** — a game engine (developer trust is the licence to operate) and an AI ad network (Vector, now the growth engine, ~90% YoY Strategic Grow growth). Materiality splits accordingly.

| Factor | Materiality | Why it hits cash flows |
|---|---|---|
| **S — Developer trust / community relations** | **Critical** | Engine users are the proprietary data moat (LevelPlay/Vector signal). The 2023 Runtime Fee revolt proved that pricing governance can destroy billions of market cap in 72 hours. |
| **S — Data privacy & ad integrity (product safety)** | **Critical** | Ad-tech is now the majority of value; COPPA/GDPR/CCPA/DSA exposure and the AppLovin data-scraping suit go directly to the growth asset. |
| **G — Capital allocation & board/sponsor dynamics** | **High** | Sequoia/Silver Lake-affiliated directors, ironSource ($4.4B) and Weta ($1.6B) deals, repeated impairments, SBC dilution. |
| **G — Management stability** | **High** | 3 CEOs in ~19 months (Riccitiello → Whitehurst → Bromberg), CFO turnover; discount-rate and execution-risk channel. |
| **S — Workforce/restructuring** | **Medium-High** | ~43% headcount reduction (≈7,700 in 2022 → 4,412); 2026 French office closure (HDRP/VFX Graph team). |
| **E — Carbon / data-centre energy** | **Low** | Scope 1 is trivial (356 tCO₂e in 2021); exposure is Scope 2/3 cloud + rising AI inference load. |
| **E — Physical climate** | **Negligible** | Asset-light, no manufacturing, no water stress. |
| **G — Shareholder rights** | **Medium** | Dual-class super-voting has been eliminated (single class of common per 2026 ownership disclosures) — a genuine improvement — but a **classified/staggered board** remains. |

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW** (trending mildly up)

- Asset-light software/ad-tech; no manufacturing, no spills, no remediation liabilities, no environmental litigation found.
- Disclosed emissions footprint is small and historically improving (2021 Scope 1: 356 tCO₂e; 2021 emissions intensity per revenue improved 13.3% per third-party analysis of Unity's reporting).
- **Watch item:** Vector is an ML inference business. Model training/serving load shifts emissions from Scopes 1–2 into Scope 3 (cloud) and raises energy-cost sensitivity — a cost issue more than a carbon-liability issue. No disclosed science-based target or net-zero commitment was found in the 2025 Impact Report landing materials → disclosure is framework-aligned but **not target-led**, which will matter under CSRD.
- No CBAM, stranded-asset, or carbon-tax exposure.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **HIGH**

**a) Developer trust (the core licence-to-operate failure).** The September 2023 Runtime Fee — a per-install charge applied retroactively — triggered an open revolt, studio boycotts, and a partial reversal within days, followed by full withdrawal in 2024. This is the single most financially consequential "S" event in the company's history: it cost the CEO his job, damaged the franchise with the indie/mobile constituency that supplies Vector's signal, and handed share to Epic/Godot. Mitigants: Unity 6 (2024), the Unite 2025 **Epic cross-platform partnership**, and a return to seat/subscription pricing. Trust is recovering but remains structurally fragile.

**b) Workforce.** Cuts in every year 2022–2025, including ~1,800 roles (~25% of staff) in January 2024 and ~284 in a later round; 2026 plans to close the French operation (~91 roles, including core HDRP/VFX engineering). Cumulative reduction >40%. Risks: institutional knowledge loss in graphics R&D, elevated voluntary attrition of top engineers, employer-brand damage among graduates who are Unity's future funnel. No WARN/discrimination litigation surfaced in my search (gap: no EEOC or wage-claim data retrieved).

**c) Product/data safety — ad tech.** This is where social risk becomes financial. Unity's ad stack serves large volumes of mobile-game inventory with a material child/adolescent audience (COPPA, GDPR-K, UK Age Appropriate Design Code, state AADC laws). Legacy ironSource/Unity Ads mediation and the "Ad Quality" SDK are now the subject of litigation (see §5). Ad-fraud and attribution-integrity risk is sector-wide (AppsFlyer 2026: organic traffic = 52% of fraudulent installs), and Unity's AI bidding model is only as good as its measurement integrity.

**d) AI/data provenance.** Asset Store content used in generative tooling, plus EU AI Act obligations for GPAI and transparency, create an unquantified but rising content-licensing and disclosure exposure.

**e) DEI.** The board's compensation committee has been renamed "Human Capital and Compensation Committee" and DEI is de-emphasised relative to 2021-era "Inclusion & Diversity" positioning — consistent with the 2025–26 US corporate rollback. Low direct financial risk, but a governance-signalling negative for European institutional holders.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **4.5 / 10**

**Positives (+):**
- **Dual-class structure eliminated** — voting now tracks economic ownership; a real improvement from the 2020 IPO structure.
- 2026 proxy: "enhanced pay-for-performance executive compensation and **stricter dilution controls**" — directly addresses Unity's worst G sin (SBC running >20% of revenue in 2022–23).
- October 2023–May 2024 CEO transition was handled by the board rather than allowed to fester; Bromberg, Smith and Whitehurst stand for election in 2026 → board refresh in progress.
- The 2022 securities class action (Gardner/In re Unity, N.D. Cal. 5:22-cv-03962) was **dismissed with final judgment (April 2025)** — a resolved, not open, legal overhang.
- EY ratification, virtual/hybrid AGM, all 2026 proposals passed without disclosed activist campaign.

**Negatives (−):**
- **Classified (staggered) board** with three-year terms — the single biggest structural accountability gap; only ~1/3 of directors are electable in any year, and there is no disclosed move to declassify.
- **Sponsor-affiliated directors.** Chairman/lead independent director Roelof Botha (Sequoia) and Egon Durban (Silver Lake co-CEO) sit on a board that approved the all-stock ironSource merger — a deal in which both firms had large economic interests (including a pledged ~$1B investment) and which has since been substantially written down. Independence in substance is weaker than in form.
- **Capital-allocation record:** ironSource ($4.4B, 2022) largely unwound (ironSource Ads Network discontinued by Apr 30, 2026); Weta Digital ($1.625B, 2021) largely unwound; Q1 2026 alone carried a **$346.9M net loss including ~$279M of impairments tied to ad-network changes**. Destroyed capital of this scale is a governance outcome, not bad luck.
- **Management churn at the top:** three CEOs and a CFO change (Visoso → Yahes, 2024) within ~2 years.
- **China JV opacity:** Unity China (2022) and the current exploration of a China exit raise related-party, IP-transfer and data-security questions that are only thinly disclosed.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (controversy-adjusted: below average)

- **E: Leader-ish by default** (immaterial sector footprint) — but disclosure-led, not target-led.
- **S: Laggard.** Two self-inflicted social crises in three years (Runtime Fee; ad-data litigation) plus >40% workforce reduction.
- **G: Below average (4.5/10).** Structural fixes are real (single share class, dilution controls, pay-for-performance) but the classified board, sponsor-affiliated directors and a documented pattern of capital destruction keep the score well short of "good."

Critically, **every one of these issues is already inside the equity story** — the market is pricing developer trust and ad-tech legal risk, not an unpriced ESG externality.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (mild negative tilt on Governance)

**Verdict:** ESG is **not a thesis driver** for U and should not be marketed as one — this is not a "sustainable" holding. But the *material* ESG channels are authentically core to the investment case, and they are all risk-side:

1. **Developer trust is the moat.** The engine's installed base generates the first-party signal that makes Vector work. Any repeat of the 2023 pricing-governance error would be a direct hit to the growth engine. This is a **monitorable leading indicator** (Unity 6 adoption, Unite sentiment, indie share loss to Godot/Epic). Currently improving.
2. **Ad-data legal risk is the underappreciated tail.** The AppLovin action is a competitive/contract dispute dressed as data protection — but if a court restricts Unity's SDK data collection, the Vector signal advantage narrows. Recommend sizing this as a discrete downside scenario (~10–15% haircut on Vector terminal multiple) rather than an ESG footnote.
3. **Governance is the persistent discount.** A classified board with two sponsor-affiliated directors and a track record of $6B+ of value-destructive M&A justifies a durable governance discount to peer software multiples. Watch the 2027 proxy for board declassification and further dilution discipline — genuine improvement here would be a re-rating catalyst.
4. **Environmental is filler.** Do not pay for it; do not penalise it. Only monitor Scope 3/AI compute disclosure ahead of CSRD.

**Positioning conclusion:** No ESG-driven exclusion or exclusion-relief argument applies. Treat ESG as an overlay: **neutral for the thesis, with the AppLovin litigation the one item that could become a genuine fundamental risk if the court grants provisional relief.**
