## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Unity Software Inc. (NYSE: U)
*Price $52.81 · Market cap ~$23.4B · Sector: Technology / Software–Application · Employees 4,412 · Beta 2.11*

> **Context that dominates everything below:** On Nov 5–6, 2026 Reuters reported Silver Lake (~8% holder) and Saudi PIF held talks to take Unity private at ~$25B (~$55/share) — a valuation discussed *before* the Q3 beat (first GAAP-profitable quarter; "Strategic Grow" revenue +90% YoY). Stock re-rated from $42.46 (Nov 4) to $48.80 (Nov 5, +15%, 51M shares) and $52.81 now — i.e. the market is pricing a high probability of a deal at a premium of only ~4% to the last traded price. This single situation now drives most of the non-financial risk profile.

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK** (conflict-specific; structurally only adequate)

**Board (8 directors):** James Whitehurst (Executive Chair), Roelof Botha (Lead Independent, Sequoia), Egon Durban (Silver Lake), Robynne Daly, Bernard Kim, Barry Schuler, Keisha Smith, Matthew Bromberg (CEO & Director). Classified/staggered board — Class III directors elected in 2026 serve to 2029. Say-on-pay passed at the May 13, 2026 annual meeting (next vote 2027); E&Y ratified.

Evidence for "Weak":
- **Unresolved, structural bidder-on-board conflict.** Egon Durban is Managing Partner/Co-CEO of Silver Lake *and* a Unity director, and sits on the Nominating & Corporate Governance Committee. Silver Lake is simultaneously (a) an ~8% shareholder, (b) a 2020 strategic investor whose principal took the board seat, (c) the reported acquirer alongside PIF. No public disclosure yet of a special committee, its membership, or MFW-style protections (independent committee + majority-of-the-minority). This is the textbook setup for Delaware entire-fairness / breach-of-fiduciary litigation.
- **Other potential conflicts:** Botha's Sequoia is a long-duration large holder (and a 2022 convertible-note backer with Silver Lake); Whitehurst is the former interim CEO now Executive Chair (not independent of management); the CEO sits on the board.
- **Serial leadership and restructuring churn:** founder-CEO exit (Oct 2023) → interim CEO → Bromberg (May 2024); job cuts in 2022, 2023, 2024 (~25% of staff) and 2025, with a 2026 plan to close the French operation. Headcount now 4,412.
- **Staggered board** blunts shareholders' ability to reconstitute the board mid-deal.

Mitigants: majority-independent board, a named Lead Independent Director, annual say-on-pay passing, and clean §302 certifications in the Nov 5, 2026 10-Q (no disclosed material weakness or fraud in the CEO/CFO certification).

### 2. Regulatory & Legal Risk Summary
- **SEC enforcement:** EDGAR full-text search returns **no SEC enforcement actions** naming Unity. Clean on that screen.
- **Legacy securities class action** (class period Mar 2021–May 2022): dismissed; final judgment ~Apr 14, 2025 (Stanford Securities Litigation clearinghouse). Bloomberg Law noted an earlier dismissal "for now" with leave to amend — now resolved at the district level, though appeal risk cannot be excluded.
- **AppLovin v. Unity (active, material):** filed ~Sept 2026 in California state court; AppLovin alleges Unity's free Ad Quality SDK harvested per-impression MAX auction revenue and other AppLovin ad data to feed Unity's own bidding models. **TRO/injunction denied Oct 2, 2026**, so no operational halt — but the case proceeds, with discovery into Unity's data-collection practices. Adverse outcome = damages, possible SDK restrictions, and a roadmap for privacy regulators/plaintiffs.
- **Data privacy & child-directed advertising:** Vector/Grow monetizes mobile games, many child-directed. The updated COPPA Rule compliance deadline (Apr 22, 2026) plus accelerating EU/UK/US privacy enforcement in 2026 is the highest-probability regulatory tail for the ad business. Unity refreshed its Developer Privacy Policy on Nov 12, 2026 (active program management, but also signals ongoing scope changes).
- **AI/IP:** Nov 2026 Terms-of-Service changes restricting third-party AI training on Unity data/derived data drew immediate developer pushback — potential antitrust/IP/contract disputes and reputational friction.
- **M&A regulatory:** A PIF-linked take-private would need CFIUS review (Saudi sovereign capital + gaming/AI-adjacent technology + China JV) and multi-jurisdiction antitrust clearance. **Unity China:** a strategic review/sale of the China business (reported Feb 2026, >$1B) remains an unresolved regulatory/execution item and complicates any CFIUS analysis.

Watch for 13D/13G amendments from Silver Lake/PIF and any Form 144 acceleration.

### 4. Top Operational Risks
- **Revenue concentration in ad-tech (Vector/Grow) riding on other platforms' data and policies.** 90% YoY Strategic Grow growth and the first GAAP profit quarter mean the equity story now hinges on an auction/bidding business exposed to Apple ATT, Android privacy sandbox, app-store policy and — per AppLovin's suit — the legality of its own data ingestion. A single adverse platform or court ruling hits the growth engine, not a peripheral segment.
- **Key-person and execution risk after five years of cuts.** A ~2-year-tenure CEO is the sole architect of the pivot; repeated layoffs, a 2026 French-operation closure and a China divestiture review raise institutional-knowledge loss, attrition and delivery risk precisely when the ad business is scaling.
- **Competitive/technology displacement of the core engine.** Epic (Unreal), open-source Godot and in-house/AI-generated tooling compete for developers whose trust was damaged by the 2023 Runtime Fee episode; the engine is the data and distribution funnel that feeds Grow. A take-private leveraging cycle (M&A integration, cost cuts, debt) would compound both competitive and continuity risk.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Elevated**
- **Developer trust is structurally impaired.** The 2023 Runtime Fee ("the trust is gone forever" — GamesIndustry.biz) forced an apology, a walk-back, then full cancellation; Unity Pro repriced +8% to $2,200/seat. That history means any future pricing change is read as bad faith.
- **Live controversy:** the Nov 2026 ToS restricting AI training on Unity data, plus the AppLovin data-harvesting allegations, are converging into a "Unity monetizes the ecosystem's data" narrative — corrosive for a two-sided developer platform.
- **New ESG/sovereign-capital backlash vector:** a PIF-backed buyout invites governance, human-rights/sanctions and index-eligibility criticism, and likely activist noise from developers and some institutional holders.
- **Deal-process perception:** reports that ~$55 was discussed *pre-earnings*, and a Motley Fool piece arguing $55 "is not enough" after the beat, invite a "sold cheap to an insider" narrative.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**
Operationally this is a mid-teens-growth software business with real (but manageable) litigation and privacy exposure and no SEC enforcement history. What lifts it to High is the **governance integrity of the current control transaction**: a bidder-affiliated director, a staggered board, a thin ~4% premium to market, no disclosed special-committee guardrails, and a CEO share sale into the leak-driven rally. Any of (a) a challenged/leaky process, (b) an adverse AppLovin data ruling, or (c) a privacy enforcement action against Vector would be individually material; they are correlated through the same data-collection engine.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation
1. **Durban/Silver Lake conflict — highest priority.** Demand disclosure of a special committee composed solely of directors unaffiliated with Silver Lake/Sequoia/management, its retained advisors, and a majority-of-the-minority condition. Absence of these = materially higher appraisal/entire-fairness litigation risk and a plausible path to a higher price or a broken deal.
2. **Leak integrity and insider trading hygiene.** Verify the exact adoption date and any amendment of Bromberg's Nov 25, 2025 10b5-1 plan; check whether talks preceded any plan adoption, and review all Form 4s between the start of talks and Nov 5 for non-plan sales.
3. **AppLovin litigation escalation.** Discovery in the MAX/ad-data case is a channel for privacy-regulator and class-action follow-on. Monitor for amended claims, preliminary injunction renewals, or referral of data practices to the FTC/state AGs.
4. **COPPA / child-directed ad compliance at Vector** — the single most likely source of a first regulatory fine; no reserve or disclosed inquiry currently visible.
5. **China divestiture + CFIUS overlay** — an unresolved asset sale sitting inside a sovereign-backed take-private; both timelines can slip and the China JV's technology-transfer terms are a live national-security question.
