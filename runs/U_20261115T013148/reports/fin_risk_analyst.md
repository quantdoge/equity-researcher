## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: Sub-Investment Grade (indicative BB–/Ba3-equivalent), improving trend**

No agency rating or CDS spread was retrievable from the tools — this is a **data gap**; the rating below is my own synthesis.

The credit story is **bifurcated**. The balance sheet is genuinely strong: $2.65B cash against $2.32B total debt gives a **net cash position of −$327M** (net debt/EBITDA negative), and the debt is unsecured convertible paper with ~1.0% blended cash cost ($24M annual interest on $2.32B). Liquidity is not the issue. The problem is **earnings quality**: on a TTM basis the company still posts a GAAP operating loss of −$328M and a net loss of −$339M, with an accumulated deficit of −$4.39B. Interest coverage on TTM EBIT is therefore **negative (−13.6x)** — meaningless as a credit metric, since the company services debt from cash flow and its cash balance, not from EBIT.

The trajectory matters more than the level. Q3 FY2026 delivered **$622M revenue, $100.6M GAAP operating income (16.2% margin), $121.2M net income, and $224M operating cash flow** — the first cleanly profitable quarter in the dataset. FY2025 by contrast was a −$479M operating loss. The F-Score of 7 and the improvement in eight of nine F-Score components confirm the direction of travel. What keeps this out of investment grade is that TTM profitability is still negative, goodwill/intangibles are 92% of equity, and cash flow is heavily SBC-dependent (see §6).

**Critical caveat on the Z-Score:** the tool's 0.10 reading uses *book* equity in x4. Because Unity's equity is loaded with $3.24B of goodwill and its retained earnings are −$4.39B, the book-equity variant is structurally punitive. Substituting market capitalisation into x4 ($23.4B / $3.44B = 6.82) gives Z ≈ 3.7, safely in the safe zone. **The truth is in between**: the company is not a bankruptcy candidate (net cash, positive FCF), but its *book* balance sheet is fragile and would only be validated by sustained GAAP profitability.
