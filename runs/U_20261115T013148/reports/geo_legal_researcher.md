## Unity Software (NYSE: U) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Snapshot:** US-domiciled (San Francisco) engine + ad-tech platform; market cap ~$23.4B, price ~$52.81, beta 2.11; 4,412 FTEs. FY2025 revenue ~$1.85B. *Note: Q3 FY2026 (reported Nov 5, 2026) was the first GAAP-profitable quarter since IPO — $622M revenue, Grow/Vector advertising +90%/126% YoY, Grow now ~70% of revenue.* The revenue mix shift toward programmatic advertising is the single most important driver of the regulatory risk profile below.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**#1 — China: forced fragmentation and JV divestiture (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High)**
- Unity China JV was formed in 2022 (~$1B valuation) with Alibaba, Sequoia China and GGV; Unity retained majority ownership and control (unity.com; WSJ; Reuters).
- From mid-2025, **Unity 6 and later versions are no longer provided to Chinese developers**; the local "Tuanjie Engine" takes over. Unity says the removal is "not tied to tariffs" (GameDeveloper). This is de facto decoupling of the product roadmap in an 18%-of-revenue market.
- Feb 2026: Bloomberg/Technode report Unity is **exploring a sale of its China business at >$1B**; as of Nov 2026 reports indicate talks/process but **no announced agreement**. Q1 2026 results included **$279M of impairment charges** tied to the ironSource Ads sunset *and a divestiture* (Unity Q1 2026 release). Watch for deconsolidation of ~18% of revenue, PRC regulatory approval requirements, and buyer-identity/CFIUS-type sensitivities.

**#2 — Ad-tech data/privacy regulation (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High)**
- With Grow ≈70% of revenue, Unity's economics now depend on cross-app behavioural data and measurement in an environment of GDPR/UK GDPR, ePrivacy, COPPA, US state privacy statutes, Apple ATT and Google's Privacy Sandbox. Peer AppLovin's "new legal cloud" (247 Wall St, Nov 2026) shows enforcement/litigation risk is sector-wide, not idiosyncratic.
- Secondary: EU DMA-driven changes to Apple/Google app-store fees and distribution directly affect the developer ecosystem Unity monetises. EU AI Act disclosure duties touch Unity's ML-based ad models.

**#3 — US–China tech/export-control and outbound-investment tightening (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium)**
- China trade truce extensions (tariff suspensions rolled to Jan 2027) reduce near-term tariff shock, but the direction of travel is toward tighter technology-transfer/outbound-investment screening (e.g., 2025 US outbound-investment national-security legislation) and Chinese counter-rules (trade-secret and export-control lists). Any Unity China sale or IP-transfer arrangement sits squarely in this crossfire.

*De-escalated risk:* Israel/Middle East exposure (ex-ironSource R&D) is shrinking with the ironSource Ads network shutdown (Apr 30, 2026) and the planned Supersonic divestiture. Russia operations were exited in 2022; residual sanctions-screening obligations remain for engine licences to defence/simulation customers.

**Live M&A wildcard:** Multiple secondary sources (Motley Fool, Nov 11, 2026) cite reports of a **~$25B (~$55/share) valuation in acquisition talks, with a reported Saudi PIF link** (shattered.io). If a foreign sovereign/state-linked buyer is involved, **CFIUS review and possible China-divestiture conditions** become a first-order legal/geopolitical issue. Treat as unconfirmed — I could not verify from a primary filing.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights
- **In re Unity Software Inc. Securities Litigation** (N.D. Cal. No. 22-cv-03962): 2022 class action over advertising-platform/IDFA disclosures. Largely dismissed; Bloomberg Law reports Unity and two large shareholders won dismissal "for now," with claims recast as derivative claims (10-Q language: claims "recast… as derivative"). Residual derivative exposure; plaintiffs' firms (Rosen, Robbins, Glancy) continue soliciting.
- **Credibility/execution legacy:** the 2023 runtime-fee misstep and repeated restructuring (ironSource write-down, Supersonic sale, layoffs) raise disclosure-quality and litigation-follow-on risk, particularly around any 2026 drawdown.
- **Restructuring-related:** $279M impairment (Q1 2026); GAAP profitability restored by Q3 2026 ($122M net income) — reduces going-concern/liquidity-driven legal pressure.
- **Regulators of record:** FTC and state AGs (privacy/children's data), EU DPAs + ICO, EU Commission (DMA/AI Act), SAMR/CAC in China (data localisation, JV approvals), OFAC/BIS (sanctions/export licensing).
- **Gap declared:** SEC direct fetches of the FY2025 10-K and the Q3 2026 10-Q were blocked (HTTP 403) and the filing tool returned only an SOX exhibit — so the **latest legal-proceedings note and contingency accruals are not verified from primary text**. This is the main evidentiary hole in this report.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags
1. **End-user/end-use screening** of engine licences for defence, government and simulation customers (EAR/ITAR, OFAC).
2. **SDK-level data collection inside third-party games** — Unity has limited visibility into whether developer apps are child-directed, a classic COPPA/FTC and EU enforcement vector.
3. **Greater China definition includes Hong Kong and Taiwan** — cross-strait and PRC national-security-law data-request exposure.
4. **Divestiture controls**: any China sale or IP licence requires PRC approvals and may attract US outbound-investment/CFIUS scrutiny — deal-timing and value risk.
5. **Antiboycott/EU settlement-trade restrictions** affecting Israeli operations and European customers (low materiality, but a policy item given the legacy Israeli R&D base).

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

Justification: no sanctions-designated-country revenue, litigation largely contained, and the China exit reduces the highest-risk geography. Offsets: 18% Greater China revenue during a live, unclosed divestiture; a 70%-of-revenue ad-tech business in the most actively regulated area of digital law (privacy/AI/DMA); and an unresolved securities/derivative overhang. A **High** rating would follow if (a) the China sale stalls or is blocked and revenue must be written down further, or (b) a foreign state-linked acquirer emerges and CFIUS/China approvals become a condition to closing.
