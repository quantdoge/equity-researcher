# Equifax Inc. (EFX) — Value Investment Analysis

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW** (narrowing at the margin)

Equifax is one of three U.S. credit bureaus and the owner of **The Work Number**, the dominant independent database of employment and income records. Three segments: Workforce Solutions (~40% of revenue), USIS (~40%), International (~20%).

**Where the moat is genuinely wide:**
- **Workforce Solutions** earned a **44.2% operating margin in 2025** (44.1% in Q3'26) on ~$2.4B of revenue — i.e. this one segment produces roughly *all* of the company's ~$1.2B of operating income. The Work Number is a proprietary, employer-contributed dataset with high switching costs, embedded in lenders' underwriting and government/verification workflows. It grew 7% in Q3'26 with diversified markets +9% (Talent Solutions +20%).
- **Oligopoly structure**: three-bureau, regulator-entrenched data infrastructure with FCRA-driven authentication requirements. New entrants cannot replicate 150 years of dispute-handling infrastructure and lender integrations.
- **Data-as-moat in the AI era**: management's argument that proprietary, non-public data becomes *more* valuable with AI is directionally correct; new-product Vitality Index hit a record >17% in Q3'26.

**Where the moat has narrowed — this is the crux of the investment case:**
- **Mortgage credit-score pricing is being regulated into a commodity.** FHFA/HUD approved VantageScore 4.0 for Fannie/Freddie/FHA, and on 9/9/2026 expanded it to *all* approved lenders, removing the prior-approval requirement. Equifax responded by **cutting VantageScore 4.0 mortgage pricing to $1 — a 90% reduction — extended through 2027**; TransUnion went to 99¢. That is the opposite of pricing power, in the one product where the bureaus historically earned their highest-margin per-unit economics.
- **Gross margin has structurally declined**: 59.8% (2021) → 55.7% (2024) → 56.5% (2025), and operating margin 23.1% → 18.0% over the same period. Some of this is Cloudbreak acquisition mix and EFX Cloud costs, but the direction is unmistakable.
- **ROIC ~11% vs. WACC ~9%** — positive spread, but thin for a purported wide-moat franchise, and depressed by $8.2B of goodwill/intangibles (tangible equity is *negative*). ROIC has improved (9.0% in 2023 → 11.1% in 2025) as cloud spending normalized, which is encouraging.

**Verdict:** A good business with one crown-jewel asset (Workforce Solutions) bolted to a commoditizing, mortgage-levered credit-reporting business. Moat = Narrow, and narrowing in the scored-data layer.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — IV ≈ **$170** (range $150–$185); price ≈ **4% above** base case

The built-in DCF tool is unusable here (it extrapolates a 30% FCF growth rate from a distorted 3-year base and returns a $138B "intrinsic value"). I ran my own owner-earnings DCF:

- **Base:** TTM FCF $1.10B, 10-yr growth 6% (revenue +7–9%, offset by score price deflation), WACC 9.0% (beta 1.34, ~10.5% cost of equity, ~4% after-tax debt), terminal 2.5% → **EV $22.6B → equity ~$17.7B → $150/share**.
- **Normalized owner earnings** (adding back excess growth capex; maintenance capex ≈ PP&E depreciation) of ~$1.25B → **~$176/share**.
- **8% FCF growth** (mortgage recovery, AI productivity) → **~$181/share**.
- **8.5% WACC / 6% growth** → **~$167/share**.

**Intrinsic value band: $150–$185, base ~$170.** At $176.54 the stock trades *at or slightly above* my base-case value — there is **no margin of safety**.

Current multiples: **20.8x FY26E adjusted EPS ($8.50)**, 28.5x TTM GAAP EPS, **12.8x EV/EBITDA**, 18.9x P/FCF, **5.3% FCF yield**, 1.3% dividend yield. These are the cheapest multiples EFX has seen in a decade (it historically commanded 28–35x earnings), and the de-rating is *rational*, not a mispricing: the market is repricing the mortgage-score franchise from a priced oligopoly to a regulated utility.

**What's priced in:** ~20x adjusted EPS implies the market already assumes mid-single-digit long-term earnings growth with no re-rating. That is close to fair, not pessimistic. A buyer today gets roughly a 9–10% annualized return (10% EPS compounding + 1.3% dividend at a constant multiple) — respectable, but not the 15%+ Buffett demands.

*Peer multiples note: the sector-peer feed returns mega-cap Industrials (CAT, GE, BA) — meaningless for a data analytics firm. `compare_peer_valuations` shows a "peer median P/E" of 38.5x on that irrelevant set. True comparables — SPGI, MCO, VRSK, FICO, TRU — could not be pulled within budget; on memory, those trade at 25–35x, i.e. EFX is at a genuine discount, warranted by its lower margins and mortgage/score-pricing exposure.*

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good, with one capital-allocation blemish**

**Mark Begor (CEO since 2018):**
- Executed the multi-year **EFX Cloud** migration on time/on budget — genuinely rare, and it lowered the structural cost base; now targeting **$150M of AI-driven productivity savings**, which is visibly cushioning the mortgage decline (Q3'26 EPS beat by $0.20 despite a $20M mortgage headwind).
- **M&A discipline is solid**: bolt-ons (Cloudbreak, Appriss, and the pending Círculo de Crédito in Mexico) extend the data moat rather than build empires. No transformative, multiple-stretching deals.
- **Shareholder returns**: dividend raised from $0.39 to $0.56/quarter (+44%) in two steps; $927M of buybacks in 2025 plus ~$652M YTD 2026; diluted shares down from 125.0M to **118.1M (−5.5%)** in five quarters. Owner-operator-friendly behavior.
- **The blemish:** the Q4'25 ($500M) and Q1'26 ($260M) buybacks were executed at roughly $205–220 — 25–30x adjusted EPS and *above* my estimate of intrinsic value. Repurchasing stock above IV transfers value from continuing holders to sellers. Buyback discipline improved by Q3'26 (~$184 average), but the pattern suggests the capital-allocation muscle follows the share price rather than intrinsic value.
- **Disclosure gap:** the proxy (DEF 14A) was unavailable in this data environment, so I could not verify insider ownership or compensation alignment. Flagged as unresolved — Begor's tenure and insider alignment are widely reported as reasonable, but I could not confirm it here.

**Grade: B+** — operationally excellent, honest communication (guidance cut was transparent and small), capital allocation generally good but price-insensitive at the top.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (accumulate below ~$140)

This is the archetypal "great business at a fair price" — and, as Buffett reminds us, **a business that trades at a fair price is not a buy**.

The 29% de-rating is *not* an opportunity by itself; it is the market correctly recognizing that the mortgage credit score is being turned into a $1 commodity by FHFA policy while mortgage originations sit ~50% below historic levels. The durable asset — The Work Number and Workforce Solutions at 44% margins — is intact and growing 7%, and adjusted EPS still rises ~11% in 2026 ($7.65 → $8.50). But at $176.54 I am paying roughly full value for a business whose returns on capital only modestly exceed its cost of capital and whose pricing power is being regulated away.

**Action:**
- **Hold** existing positions; do not add at $176. Do not sell either — the franchise is too good and the FCF too reliable to discard at 12.8x EBITDA.
- **Buy aggressively at ≤$140** (18% discount to base IV), which is where the stock traded on 10/2/2026. That is the price at which the market pays you for the mortgage cycle and the score-price reset.
- **Trim/avoid above ~$200** — that requires the market to re-rate a narrowing moat to a wide-moat multiple.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Score commoditization is worse than modeled.** Equifax's $1 VantageScore pricing through 2027 and TransUnion's 99¢ establish a reference price. If per-unit score revenue collapses faster than unit volumes (+230% VantageScore 4.0 volumes) offset, gross margin could fall another 200–300bps and push IV toward $150.
2. **Mortgage volumes.** Management explicitly cut FY26 revenue guidance for a weaker H2'26 mortgage market as rates rose. Mortgage-linked revenue is ~15% of the total but a disproportionate share of incremental profit; a further 20% volume decline removes ~$0.30–0.40 of EPS.
3. **Regulatory/legal.** FCRA class actions, state AG actions, CFPB and FHFA rule-making, and cyber risk (the 2017 breach remains the sector's cautionary tale). A single adverse event can be a $500M+ hit and a multi-year distraction.
4. **Goodwill impairment / negative tangible equity.** $8.2B of goodwill and intangibles against $4.7B of equity. If International or USIS growth disappoints, an impairment would be non-cash but would confirm that acquisition capital earned poor returns.
5. **AI disintermediation narrative.** If lenders and employers can derive verification and risk data directly (payroll APIs, open banking, LLM-driven underwriting), the bureau intermediary layer loses toll-taking power. Speculative today, but it is exactly why the multiple has compressed.
6. **Financial engineering masking stagnation.** 5.5% share-count reduction flatters EPS; underlying operating income growth (TTM EBIT $1.25B vs. 2022's $1.11B) is only ~4% annually over four years.

**What would make me wrong (upgrade to Buy):** a durable mortgage recovery plus stable score economics — i.e., revenue growth re-accelerating to 10%+ with gross margin holding ≥56% — would support an IV near $200 and make today's price a discount. I would want to see the Q4'26 guide and the Círculo de Crédito close before revising.
