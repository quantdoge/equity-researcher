## 2. Consensus Assessment

**Agreement level: LOW on price/timing — MEDIUM on business quality.**

Everyone agrees on the *asset*: a three-firm oligopoly with an irreplaceable payroll-verification franchise (Workforce Solutions, 44% operating margin, producing essentially the whole company's operating income) and 55%+ gross margins. **Zero agents allege fraud, accounting manipulation, or solvency risk.** Quant's accruals score (−8.05%, cash conversion 2.4x) and Short's 2.45x OCF/NI independently confirm the books are clean.

The split is entirely about **what you pay and when**:

- **Constructive (2):** Growth, Sector.
- **Neutral / unconfirmed (4):** Quant, Alt Data, ESG, Short.
- **Cautionary (5):** Value ("not at this price"), Macro, Non-Financial Risk, Geo/Legal, Financial Risk.

**Conflict resolution — three cases, resolved:**

1. **Growth BUY vs Value HOLD.** These are not actually in conflict. Growth says "be an aggressive buyer at $150–160." Value says "buy at ≤$140, IV is $170." **Both imply $176.54 is too high.** I resolve toward the caution: the two most price-disciplined agents converge on a materially lower entry.

2. **Sector ATTRACTIVE vs Macro UNFAVOURABLE.** Sector is looking at the mortgage cycle (late-trough, optionality un-priced); Macro is looking at the *rate regime* that determines when that trough ends. Macro's 7.40% mortgage print occurred **after** quarter-end, meaning Q4's $20M mortgage hit is already stale-good. The cycle is not turning on a 2–3 quarter view — Macro wins the near-term, Sector wins the 2027–28 view. That argues for **staged entry, not a full position**.

3. **Short Analyst vs the bull case.** Per my framework, a fraud/risk flag outranks a bullish value view. This is *not* a fraud flag — so no veto. But it earns a **haircut to the Growth model**: consensus requires ~380bp of one-year EBITDA margin expansion to an all-time record, in a business whose operating margin has *fallen* 510bp since 2021. I discount Growth's 11.2% EPS CAGR to ~8–9% for sizing, which is what drops expected return from ~13–15% to ~10–11%.

**Net: a high-quality franchise at approximately fair value, carrying one live binary policy risk and an unfavourable macro tape. Conviction tier: MODERATE-LOW (~55/100).**

| **Expected value** | | **+3.3%** |

Full Kelly = edge/variance = 0.033 / 0.193 ≈ 17% — **absurd for a single name.** Applying ~1/10 fractional Kelly for a 44%-vol name with an unmodelable binary regulatory risk gives ~1.7%, then haircutting for the live FHFA rulemaking (which cannot be priced by any model I have) yields **1.0–1.5%**. I set the starter at the bottom of that range and hold the rest as a pre-authorized add ladder.

**Why not 3%?** Because at 44% annualized volatility, a 1.0% weight already contributes ~0.44% of portfolio volatility — equivalent to a 2.5% position in a normal 18%-vol equity. **Size on volatility, not on conviction.**

**Why not pass?** Because the bear-case floor is a $137 retest (−22%) against a bull case of +25% to $220, the balance sheet is investment grade, FCF is durable at $1.1B, and the market is paying 12.8x EV/EBITDA for a 30%-EBITDA-margin data oligopolist. That asymmetry deserves a real, if small, position — with the capital preserved for a better price.

## 6. Risk Management Rules

**Price stop: $131 close-basis (−26% from entry).**
Honest caveat: for a 44%-vol stock, a 26% stop is only ~0.6 standard deviations — it is a **discipline device, not a protection device**. The real protection is the 1.0% starting weight, which caps a full stop-out at ~0.26% of portfolio. Do not tighten the stop toward $155–160; at this volatility that is pure noise-harvesting.

**Thesis invalidation — exit on any of these, regardless of price:**
1. **FHFA finalizes a bi-merge / two-bureau rule** — a structural cut to revenue-per-origination across USIS *and* Mortgage Solutions. Cut to ≤0.5% or zero and reassess from scratch.
2. **USIS mortgage revenue turns negative YoY while the mortgage market is not shrinking** — this is share *loss*, and it destroys the thesis's single strongest pillar (Q3 mortgage +15% into a −20% market).
3. **Workforce Solutions organic growth <5% for two consecutive quarters**, or an adverse CFPB/FTC action against The Work Number. This segment is the entire operating income.
4. **FY27 revenue growth guided below 7%** at the 3-Feb-27 print — breaks the 8–12% long-term framework and the 20.8x forward multiple.
5. **Gross margin below 55% for two quarters** — score-price commoditization (VantageScore at $1 through 2028, TRU at 99¢) running ahead of unit-volume offset.
6. **Net debt/EBITDA >3.0x while buybacks continue** — the capital-allocation policy has already run at 128% of FCF; that converting into leverage is the balance-sheet failure mode.
7. **Goodwill impairment in Workforce Solutions** — confirms acquisition capital earned sub-par returns on 68% of assets.

**Trim triggers:**
- **$205–210 (~1.2x base IV, ~21x FY27 EPS $9.84):** trim one-third. At this level you are being paid full value for a narrow-moat asset.

**Review cadence:** 3-Feb-27 (Q4 + FY27 guide), Q1-27 print, Dec-26/Jan-27 FCRA settlement final approvals, Q4-26 close of Círculo de Crédito ($750M EV — watch goodwill, already +$1.18B in Q3), and any FHFA NPRM.

**What would move me to Add aggressively:** price ≤$155, or an FY27 guide ≥8% revenue growth with margin expansion, or a clean resolution of the FHFA bi-merge proceeding. Any two of the three justifies the full 2.5%.
