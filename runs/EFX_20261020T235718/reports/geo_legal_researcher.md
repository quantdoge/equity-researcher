## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**#1 — US FCRA litigation & the post-CFPB enforcement vacuum (Probability: High / Impact: Medium)**
Federal enforcement has hollowed out: CFPB enforcement caseloads were reportedly transferred toward DOJ and the Bureau has sharply curtailed activity (Compliance Week, 2025); the CFPB's Dec-2024 proposed FCRA data-broker rule was **withdrawn** (Lexology). The Jan-2025 **CFPB order fining Equifax $15M** for inadequate dispute investigations is emblematic of the prior regime. The risk now migrates to **state AGs and private class actions**, which are less predictable and not subject to federal policy cycles. Equifax is already accruing: **$100M accrual** on the credit-score coding-issue FCRA case ($60M insurance receivable → $40M net charge; final approval hearing Jan-2027) and **$30M accrual** on inquiry-dispute cases (final approval Dec-2026) — Q3'26 10-Q. A single-digit-$40-60M/quarter tail is plausible but not thesis-breaking.

**#2 — Mortgage credit-score re-architecture (FHFA/HUD) — regulatory-driven revenue model risk (Probability: High / Impact: Medium, mixed sign)**
FHFA expanded VantageScore 4.0 availability to **all approved lenders on Sept 9, 2026**, and it is the sole score on >9% of GSE-securitized mortgages (FHFA; VantageScore). Equifax has pre-emptively **capped VantageScore 4.0 mortgage score pricing at $1 through end-2028** to drive adoption (~2,000 lenders, PRNewswire 8-Oct-2026). The unresolved **bi-merge** (2-bureau vs. tri-merge) proposal is the real threat: it structurally reduces bureau pulls per origination. Equifax's Q3 USIS mortgage revenue +28% and mortgage score revenue are currently benefiting from the *score choice* transition, but bi-merge is a direct revenue-per-unit headwind and a policy decision outside management control.

**#3 — Emerging-market FX, controls and rule-of-law in Latin America (Probability: Medium / Impact: Low–Medium)**
International grew 9% reported vs. 7% local currency in Q3'26 — i.e., FX was a modest *tailwind* this quarter, but the signal reversed on a dime historically (Argentina, Brazil). Argentina (repatriation controls, chronic inflation) and Mexico (judicial reform, security, US trade-policy linkage) are the sensitive nodes. Dividend repatriation and peso/real translation are recurring EPS noise of roughly ±$0.02–0.05/quarter. Also flag **Australia's Privacy Act reform tranche** (statutory tort of privacy / direct right of action) as a rising litigation-cost vector in a ~6% revenue market.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **FCRA coding-error class action:** $100M accrual, $60M insurance receivable, $40M net charge; preliminary approval only; final approval Jan-2027 (10-Q, Q3'26).
- **Inquiry-disputes matters:** $30M accrual; final approval hearing Dec-2026 (10-Q).
- **CFPB:** $15M order (Jan-2025) for improper investigations of disputes; FTC had issued a coordinated civil investigative demand. "Ongoing investigation" disclosure across CFPB/FTC noted by American Banker. Direction of travel under the current administration is *less* federal enforcement, *more* state/private.
- **2017 breach legacy:** ~$650M+ total ($175M state AG fund, $50M CFPB, up to $425M consumer fund); 20-year consent-order-style compliance obligations remain a standing supervisory hook.
- **Regulators of record:** CFPB, FTC, state AGs (esp. CA, NY, MA), state DFIs (mortgage/auto lending customers), UK FCA/ICO, Australia (OAIC, ACCC, ASIC), Canada (OSFI-adjacent, provincial), Mexico (CNBV/Banxico-adjacent), Brazil (ANPD under LGPD), EU (GDPR, AI Act).
- **EU AI Act:** creditworthiness scoring is Annex III high-risk — Equifax's UK/Spain/Portugal credit-scoring products require conformity assessment, technical documentation, and human-oversight controls on the Aug-2026 timeline. Cost of compliance, not existential; but a plausible source of EU supervisory action.
- **Antitrust/IP:** no material active antitrust case against EFX identified; historical FICO-vs-bureaus antitrust claims were dismissed. VantageScore advocacy against FICO is competitive, not litigation risk per se.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Consumer-data/AI governance concentration** — the entire business is regulated personal data (FCRA, GLBA, GDPR, LGPD, Privacy Act, CCPA/CPRA + ~20 state laws, plus new state AI-in-decisioning statutes). Highest-frequency, lowest-severity risk bucket.
2. **Unexplained/soft costs of regulatory change** — hedged by pricing actions (VantageScore at $1, cash-flow data via Mastercard Open Finance) but margin-dilutive.
3. **No sanctions/FCPA/bribery exposure flagged** — no Russia/China operations; government-contract revenue (state agency ACV $550M, Q3'26) carries procurement-integrity and bid-protest risk but no bribery exposure identified.
4. **Insurance recoveries masking cash cost** — $60M of the $100M FCRA accrual is an insurance receivable; verify collectability and whether D&O/cyber towers will absorb future events.

## 5. Overall Rating

**Geopolitical risk: LOW** (diversified, US-centric, no sanctioned-market exposure) — but **Regulatory/Legal risk: MEDIUM-HIGH** for a data-and-scoring business.

**Composite: MEDIUM**

The binding constraint on EFX is Washington and Sacramento, not foreign governments: FCRA class actions ($130M of accruals booked), FHFA credit-score/bi-merge policy, and an EU AI Act regime that treats credit scoring as high-risk. Watch items in order of importance: (i) bi-merge decision, (ii) final approval of the two settlements in Dec-2026/Jan-2027 and any new state AG action, (iii) Argentina FX/repatriation and Mexico close of Círculo de Crédito.
