# Equifax Inc. (NYSE: EFX) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Price $176.54 (close 10/20/26) | Market cap ~$20.9B | EV ~$26B | Net debt ~$5B**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Equifax has finished the painful, decade-long rebuild from a legacy mainframe architecture into a native cloud platform ("EFX Cloud"), and is now monetizing it: a record **>17% new-product Vitality Index** in Q3 2026 against a 10% long-term goal, 54 AI-enabled products launched in H1 2026, and a $150M AI-driven productivity program that is letting the company *hold* EPS guidance while cutting revenue guidance. The disruptive asset is not the credit bureau — it's the **proprietary verification graph**: The Work Number's 140M+ payroll-based employment/income records, which is expanding from mortgage underwriting into government eligibility, tenant screening, and fintech decisioning. This is a data-network-effect business with pricing power, 56% gross margins, and a cyclical mortgage option that is currently priced near trough pessimism.

Caveat up front: this is a **quality compounder with a re-rating catalyst, not a Cathie Wood-style exponential grower**. Growth is 8–11%, not 30%+.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration

Three stacked markets, my estimates (company TAM disclosure is not granular in the data I could verify):

- **Credit & financial decisioning data (USIS/International):** ~$25–30B. EFX is a mature oligopoly participant (~35% of US revenue). Low growth, high moat, regulatory-moat dynamics. This is the cash cow, not the growth engine.
- **Employment/income verification (Workforce Solutions — ~35–37% of revenue):** The real prize. The Work Number's payroll-derived dataset is effectively un-replicable; competitors must build employer-by-employer. Addressable spend across mortgage, auto/consumer lending, **government eligibility & IRS-adjacent verification**, tenant screening, and background screening is ~$10–12B, with EFX capturing maybe 20–25%. Government is the least-penetrated vertical and is now accelerating to mid-single-digit growth sequentially.
- **Identity/fraud/analytics (EFX.AI, Kount):** ~$15B+, growing low-double-digits. Cloud-native delivery into fintech (InnovationX) opens a customer segment the legacy bureau could not serve.

Total SAM I'd size at **$50–55B** vs $6.7B revenue = **~12% penetration**. The growth math works if the verification/fraud halves grow 10–12%; it does not require heroic credit-bureau assumptions.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4 2026 results & initial 2027 guide — Feb 3, 2027** (est. $2.22 EPS, $1.67B revenue). The pivotal event: does management frame H2 2026 as the mortgage trough? Any 2027 revenue growth guide ≥8% with margin expansion re-rates the stock.
- **Círculo de Crédito acquisition close — Q4 2026.** Bolt-on Mexican credit bureau; adds LatAm data density and cross-sell into a high-single-digit-growth region. Cheap M&A funded from FCF, not leverage.
- **Mortgage-rate inflection.** 10-yr Treasury at 5.12% today — mortgage revenue was guided down for H2 2026, yet **USIS Mortgage grew +28% and total US Mortgage +15% in Q3**. Mortgage is only ~13–15% of revenue; a 100bp rate decline would turn the biggest headwind into a 300–500bp revenue tailwind.
- **EFX2028 framework update** — long-term 8–12% revenue growth with ~50bp/yr EBITDA margin expansion toward a 39%+ goal. Margin beats (Q2 2026 was 70bp above the 50bp framework) are the trigger for multiple expansion, since the market is pricing EFX as a low-growth cyclical.
- **AI productivity ($150M goal) + buybacks.** $92M repurchased in Q3, $927M in 2025; diluted shares down from 125M to 118M. At 15x 2028E EPS, repurchases are highly accretive.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (moderate conviction)

The thesis is a **margin-and-mix compounder bought after a 47% drawdown**, not a hyper-growth disruptor. Numbers: 11.2% EPS CAGR 2026–2030 ($8.50→$13.00), FCF margin expanding from 18.7% toward 21%+, plus a 1.2% dividend and a 2–3%/yr buyback → **~13–15% annualized return** if the multiple merely holds at ~21x. If margins reach the 39% EBITDA goal and mortgage normalizes, 24x on $13.00 = $312 (+77%) by 2030.

Why not Strong Buy: Rule of 40 of ~26–30 is genuinely below standard; the stock already rallied 29% in three weeks to consensus target; and 2029–2030 consensus revenue growth decelerates to 2–5%, implying the market's terminal view is a GDP+ story. I would be an aggressive buyer on any pullback toward $150–160, which is where the risk/reward becomes asymmetric.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Mortgage/rates (highest-probability).** 10-yr at 5.12%; management already cut FY26 revenue for a "significantly weakened" H2 mortgage market. A further leg up in rates hits both revenue and the recovery optionality embedded in the price.
2. **Regulatory/legal on the verification moat.** The Work Number's model (employer-provided payroll data resold for eligibility decisions) faces CFPB/state scrutiny; FCRA litigation and data-broker privacy rules are a structural tail risk that could hit Workforce Solutions — the highest-margin, highest-multiple segment.
3. **FICO/VantageScore and customer pricing pressure.** "Ex-FICO" revenue growth of ~10% at the top of a 7–10% framework shows customers actively working around score-based pricing; margin leakage is possible.
4. **Execution on AI-driven cost savings.** $150M of productivity is carrying the EPS guide. If these are one-time cost cuts rather than durable, 2027 margins disappoint and the "quality compounder" framing breaks.
5. **Leverage.** Net debt ~$5B; interest expense up 19% YoY ($62.3M vs $52.2M/qtr) — refinancing into a 5%+ rate world constrains buyback capacity and adds sensitivity to a revenue miss.
6. **Adjusted-vs-GAAP gap.** 2025 GAAP net income of $664M against ~$1.0B adjusted implies $350M+ of add-backs; if amortization/restructuring persists, headline multiples are less flattering than the adjusted screen suggests.

**Gaps in my work:** I could not verify segment-level revenue dollars, precise TAM figures, or quantitative job-posting/app-download series; the peer set was hand-built (the Industrials sector screen returned aerospace/railroad conglomerates) and FICO/VRSK EV/Sales are approximations from available data.
