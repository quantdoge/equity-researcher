## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **RISK-OFF / LATE-CYCLE TIGHTENING (regime break)**

The macro regime has inverted versus the 2024–25 consensus. The Fed **ended its easing cycle and hiked 25bp on 16 September 2026 to 3.75–4.00%** — its first increase since 2023 — under Chair Kevin Warsh, after cutting from 5.33% to 3.63% between Aug-2024 and Aug-2026. The driver is a genuine inflation re-acceleration: September CPI rose **+0.65% m/m (≈7.9% annualized)** and **+3.72% y/y**, up from +3.30% in July.

Critically, this is a **bear-steepening, not a growth scare**: growth is intact (real GDP +2.2% y/y), unemployment is 4.2% on a full-employment plateau, yet the long end has sold off hard (**10Y +72bp since 5 May, to 5.15%**; 30Y at 5.37%). That is a term-premium/inflation-expectations repricing, and it has pushed the **30Y mortgage to 7.40%** — the highest in at least two years and +142bp from the 5.98% February 2026 low. The brief refi/housing-thaw window that opened in Q1-2026 has been slammed shut.

Credit is the one soft spot in the hawkish story: **HY OAS at 3.29% is still near multi-decade tights**, but it has widened from 2.65% on 1 September (+64bp, ~25% relative) — funding is available but cracking at the margin. Additionally, the USD has firmed (**broad index 121.4 vs ~118.0 in May**), a translation headwind.

For EFX — a levered, long-duration, mortgage- and hiring-linked data utility — this is close to the worst feasible mix: short-rate hikes don't help mortgage volumes (those price off the 10Y), while the 10Y at 5%+ hits the multiple and the refinancing cost of its debt stack.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Negative — mortgage channel (the dominant macro transmission).** EFX's USIS mortgage business (roughly 10–12% of company revenue, but a high-incremental-margin, sentiment-setting line) is a direct derivative of origination volumes. Management just disclosed a **$20m Q3 revenue hit from a "sharp late-quarter weakening in the U.S. mortgage market"** and cut FY2026 revenue guidance — and the 7.40% mortgage rate printed *after* quarter-end, meaning Q4 is likely worse. Housing turnover is frozen; refi economics are dead above ~6.75%.

**Negative — discount rate / duration.** Beta 1.34, a premium-multiple analytics compounder, and a ~$4.5–5bn debt stack inherited from the 2020–23 cloud build (approximate). A 5.15% 10Y both compresses the multiple on long-duration cash flows and raises marginal refinancing cost by roughly 150–200bp versus legacy coupons. This mechanically explains much of the 45% de-rating from the Oct-2025 high of $251 to the $137 low on Sep 30 — the stock bottomed almost exactly as the 10Y peaked (5.29% on Sep 30).

**Neutral-to-positive — employment/verification.** Workforce Solutions (largest profit pool) is levered to hiring and income verification, not to rates. Unemployment at 4.2% with no deterioration is a stable, supportive base; state/government eligibility contracts ($550m ACV signed in Q3) are counter-cyclical and largely insulated from the rate cycle.

**Neutral-to-positive — nominal consumer spending, negative — delinquencies.** +6.1% nominal PCE supports lending volumes and credit-monitoring demand; rising delinquencies (HY spreads cracking) lift collections/risk-decisioning revenue but suppress origination appetite — net roughly a wash.

**Negative — FX.** ~20% of revenue is International (UK, Canada, Australia, Spain/Portugal, LatAm, India). A broad dollar at 121.4 is a translation headwind; Q3 grew +9% *local currency* vs +10% reported only because of mix — a further 3–5% USD appreciation would cost roughly 60–100bp of consolidated growth.

**Beyond macro, flagged for the team:** the FHFA-directed admission of **VantageScore 4.0** alongside classic FICO (Fannie/Freddie/FHA accepting since Apr-2026) and EFX's decision to price VantageScore mortgage scores at $1–$4.50 through 2028 is a *structural pricing* risk, not a macro one. It is probably a larger swing factor for 2027 revenue per mortgage transaction than any plausible rate move, and it is the reason a mortgage-volume recovery may translate less cleanly into EPS than in prior cycles.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Rationale: three of four macro transmission channels (mortgage rates, discount rate/refinancing, FX) are negative and intensifying, and the one supportive channel (employment) is merely stable rather than accelerating. The rating is not "severe" only because the equity has already discounted a great deal (‑45% peak-to-trough), credit spreads remain tight enough that lenders are still lending, and the July-to-September collapse in the share price coincided with the 10Y peak — implying meaningful macro pessimism is in the price at $176.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BULL: disinflation breaks the term premium**

Consensus (and the Fed's September vote) now assumes inflation is sticky and the Warsh Fed tightens further. But the **Sep 30 CPI print was already softer than feared**, and the entire +72bp move in the 10Y since May is term-premium, not growth. If core disinflation resumes through Q4 — CPI back to ~3.0% and the September hike proving to be a one-and-done — the 10Y could retrace to ~4.3% and mortgages to ~6.3–6.5%.

The torque is asymmetric: (a) a ~100bp mortgage move would thaw refi and purchase volumes into spring 2027, and EFX's mortgage line is high-incremental-margin; (b) every 50bp off the 10Y is worth multiple points of re-rating on a 1.34-beta long-duration compounder; (c) a weaker dollar adds 60–100bp of reported growth. That combination plausibly supports $210–230+ versus $176 today, which is why today's +11% reaction to a guidance *cut* suggests the market is already hunting for this turn.

The mirror-image bear risk to monitor: 30Y at 5.37% is only ~60bp from pushing mortgages to 8%, which would fully freeze housing, spike delinquencies, and force a second guidance cut while VantageScore pricing deflates per-unit revenue — that is the path to a retest of the $137 September low.
