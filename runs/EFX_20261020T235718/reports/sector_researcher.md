## SECTOR BRIEF — EQUIFAX INC. (NYSE: EFX)

**Classification note:** FMP tags EFX as *Industrials / Consulting Services*. That is a data artifact. EFX is one of the three US consumer credit bureaus — a subscription-plus-transaction risk-data and analytics business. Benchmarking it against Caterpillar and Boeing would be meaningless, so I benchmark both ways: against the assigned Industrials aggregate (for compliance) and against the true risk-data comp set (Experian, TransUnion, Verisk, FICO, CoStar).

---

### 1. Sector Overview

The credit bureau / risk-data sector is a mature, high-barrier oligopoly now overlaying a structural growth layer (identity, fraud, employment/income verification, government eligibility). The US credit agency market is roughly **$19.9B in 2026, growing ~6% CAGR toward ~$26B**; the three bureaus — Experian (~$8.5B revenue), Equifax ($6.6B TTM) and TransUnion ($4.6B) — control the overwhelming majority of US consumer credit data, with a de facto HHI well above 2,500. Barriers are extreme: proprietary data contributory networks (lenders supply data only to bureaus), FCRA/regulatory licensing, and deep embedding in lenders' underwriting workflows and model validation. Revenue is annuity-like (subscription/contract) but has one highly cyclical appendage — US mortgage origination — which is currently the sector's swing factor.

Against the *assigned* Industrials aggregate (25 largest names, FY2025: $822.0B revenue, 30.6% gross margin, $79.5B net income = 9.7% net margin), EFX is a structurally superior business: **55.2% gross margin vs 30.6%**, **11.3% net margin vs 9.7%**. EFX's economics resemble a data monopolist, not an industrial.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Mortgage recovery optionality is un-priced.** Originations sit near a multi-year trough (BofA models 2027 originations ~$1.72T with elevated rates, weak refis, subdued resale). EFX grew mortgage revenue **+15% in Q3'26 despite a weakening market** — i.e., share gain and pricing, not volume. Every incremental refi/purchase dollar drops through at very high incremental margin.
- **Government/verification is the new structural leg.** EFX booked **$550M of annual contract value in state-agency signings** — Medicaid/work-requirement income verification built on The Work Number. This is contracted, less cyclical, and largely a volume-of-eligibility story tied to policy, not credit.
- **Pricing power + cost inflection.** Three-player structure supports annual pricing. The EFX Cloud migration is reaching completion, so the cloud D&A burden ($196M/quarter = 11.6% of revenue) should stop growing faster than revenue, unlocking GAAP operating-margin recovery toward the 2021 peak of 23.1%. FHFA's approval of VantageScore 4.0 also injects score-provider competition — the bureaus co-own VantageScore, so bureau data volumes benefit even as FICO's score economics face pressure.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **US mortgage is EFX's largest single cyclical exposure** and is deteriorating: Q3'26 revenue landed *below* the July guidance midpoint on a **$20M US mortgage impact**, forcing a 2026 outlook cut. EFX is more mortgage-levered than Verisk or FICO.
- **Regulatory and legal overhang.** FCRA litigation, CFPB scrutiny of data-broker pricing and dispute handling, and proliferating state privacy statutes that raise data-acquisition cost and complexity. Adverse credit-reporting rulemaking is the sector's tail risk.
- **Consumer credit normalization.** Mortgage delinquency reached **4.44% in Q1'26 (+18bps YoY)**; delinquencies are stabilizing at a higher level, which makes lenders cautious on new origination — the opposite of what bureau transaction revenue needs.
- **Margin structure lags peers.** GAAP operating margin of 18.8% versus a 34.6% peer median is partly accounting/mix, but it is also real: heavy SGA ($390M/qtr) and cloud amortization keep EFX's EBITDA margin (30.4%) below Verisk (48.3%) and FICO (47.8%), roughly matching TransUnion (31.8%).

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG** (franchise) / **AVERAGE** (profitability)

**Rationale — why strong:** EFX holds an irreplaceable data asset inside a three-firm oligopoly, has re-accelerated TTM revenue growth to 11.4% (above TransUnion and above the 5-year 9.6% CAGR), and is winning share in mortgage despite a shrinking market. The $550M state-contract ACV win demonstrates genuine pricing/product optionality beyond credit data. Beta of 1.34 and ~15,000 employees understate the annuity quality of the revenue base.

**Rationale — where it lags:** Operating margin of 18.8% sits 16 points below the peer median and 4 points below its own 2021 level (23.1%); net margin of 11.3% is roughly half the peer median. The EFX Cloud program consumed years of margin and still carries $196M/quarter of D&A. Until that leverage shows up in GAAP operating margin, EFX deserves to trade at a discount to Verisk and FICO. Watch adjusted EBITDA margin (currently 30.4%) as the scorecard for whether the cloud spend was value-accretive.

**Net:** A top-tier competitive asset producing second-quartile financial returns — a margin-recovery story rather than a moat-deficit story.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

Mature-oligopoly structure, ~6% structural market growth, strong pricing power, prohibitive barriers to entry, and multiple independent secular legs (verification, government eligibility, fraud/identity) layered on a cyclical mortgage business that is close to trough — an asymmetric setup. The principal caveat is that the sector is **not cheap**: ~30x P/E and ~16x EV/EBITDA median embeds continued mid-single-digit-plus growth, and the group is exposed to a single regulatory headline (credit-reporting rulemaking, VantageScore economics). Within the group, EFX offers the best growth-to-multiple combination but requires the margin-recovery thesis to hold; Verisk and FICO carry better economics at richer multiples; TransUnion is the cheaper, lower-quality expression of the same mortgage-recovery trade.

**Sector cycle position:** Mature growth — not in the Industrials business cycle at all. The relevant cycle is the **US mortgage/consumer-credit cycle, which is in late-trough**, with delinquencies stabilizing at elevated levels and originations depressed. That is the single most important swing variable for 2027 sector earnings.
