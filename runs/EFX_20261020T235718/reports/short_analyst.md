## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**1. Operating deleverage in FY2025 — SG&A outgrew revenue 3:1.** SG&A +$163.7M (+11.3%) vs revenue +6.9%. SG&A ratio back to 26.6%, the highest since 2021. This is the opposite of the "completed cloud transformation drives margin expansion" narrative.

**2. Structural margin erosion.** Operating margin −510bp (23.1%→18.0%) and gross margin −340bp (59.8%→56.4%) since 2021. Partial gross-margin recovery in 2025 (55.7%→56.4%) is real, but the operating line kept compressing. Read: pricing power has weakened and cost inflation is not being passed through.

**3. Adverse mix shift in the highest-margin segment.** Q2 2026: total revenue +11%, **Workforce Solutions +8%**, Verification Services +10%, **USIS +17%**. The crown-jewel, highest-margin verification business is growing *slower* than the group, while the lower-margin, mortgage-cyclical USIS grows twice as fast. That is mechanically margin-dilutive.

**4. Consensus requires an all-time-record margin.** FY2026 consensus EBITDA $2,326.7M = **34.6% margin**, vs. FY2025 actual 30.8% and an all-time peak of 32.8% (2022). A ~380bp one-year expansion in a business whose margins have *fallen* for four years is the single most fragile assumption in the model. Management's raised guidance (FY26 adj. EPS $8.39–8.69, revenue $6.71–6.78B, +100bp margin expansion) is well short of the consensus margin trajectory — a gap to monitor.

**5. Goodwill and intangibles exceed total equity.** G&I $8,172M = **69% of assets**, versus total equity $4,738M. Impairment risk is concentrated in Workforce Solutions — precisely the segment facing the greatest moat threat (§4). A 30% impairment would erase more than half of book equity.

**6. Liquidity is thin and the current ratio is 0.60.** Current assets $1,412M vs. current liabilities $2,335M (gap $923M); cash of only **$180.8M (1.5% of assets)** against **$1,038M of debt due within 12 months**. Current liabilities jumped $524M in 2025. Manageable against $1.6B of OCF and a revolver — but there is no balance-sheet cushion to absorb a litigation or impairment shock.

**7. Leverage rising, coverage mediocre.** Net debt $4,912M = 2.63x EBITDA; total debt/EBITDA 2.73x; interest coverage (EBIT/interest) 5.4x. Piotroski F5 (lower leverage) fails.

**8. DSO drift.** Receivables days: 53.9 (2021) → 61.1 → 63.0 → 61.5 → **60.8** (2025). Not channel stuffing (receivables grew 5.75% vs. revenue 6.92%), but a ~7-day structural deterioration in collections from the 2021 base.

**9. GAAP-to-adjusted gap is 44%.** FY2025 GAAP EPS $5.32 vs. adjusted ~$7.65 — a $2.33/share (~$290M after-tax) gap, almost entirely recurring amortization of acquired intangibles. Defensible for a serial acquirer, but investors are valuing a number that is 44% above the audited figure. When the acquisition treadmill slows, amortization does not.

**10. Revenue deceleration the screening tool missed.** The `detect_revenue_deceleration` function returns "stable." I disagree. YoY growth ran 3.79 → 7.44 → 7.15 → 9.24 → **14.35 (Q1'26) → 10.61 (Q2'26) → 9.55 (Q3'26)**. Three consecutive quarters of deceleration off the Q1'26 peak. The tool's threshold design missed a genuine inflection.
