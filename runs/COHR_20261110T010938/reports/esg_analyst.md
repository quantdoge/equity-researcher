## Coherent Corp. (COHR) — Full ESG Analysis

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives:** 78% of global electricity renewable (>550 GWh/yr; 234 kt CO₂e avoided, FY2024) — top-quartile for semiconductor hardware; targets of **−50% Scope 1+2 by FY2030** and **net-zero Scope 1+2 by FY2040**, framed as 1.5°C-aligned; CDP discloser; joined Responsible Business Alliance (2024).

**Concerns:**
- **Scope 3 is the dominant emissions pool and is ungoverned by targets.** For a photonics/materials maker, purchased substrates, gases and specialty chemicals plus downstream energy use of pluggable optics/lasers almost certainly exceed Scope 1+2 by a wide margin. That is the number customers and EU CSRD will demand.
- **Absolute Scope 1+2 tonnage and revenue intensity were not retrievable** (Global Impact Report PDF and CDP questionnaire fetches returned unparseable/403 content; no 10-K text via the filing tool). Directionally, at ~$7B revenue a fab-heavy footprint implies roughly 40–70 tCO₂e per $1M revenue — **estimated, unverified**.
- **Growth is emissions-additive:** Sherman, TX expansion (~$650M, 4× wafer capacity, $50M CHIPS LOI + Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund grant) plus SiC crystal growth (extremely power-intensive) will grow absolute energy faster than efficiency gains.
- **Hazardous waste/water:** NJ hazardous-waste and water-pollution violations (2003, 2004, 2019, 2023); Texas water stress for crystal growth and cooling.

---

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

- **Safety track record is the weak spot:** five US OSHA citations since 2017 including **2022 ($36.4k), 2023 ($11.2k) and 2025 ($18.9k)** — a repeat pattern rather than one-off, though dollar amounts are trivially small versus EBITDA. Company-reported China-site TRIR 0.03 / LTIR 0.02 (FY2023) is excellent on paper; the "Sapphire Certification Champion" award is a vendor certification, not independent assurance — discount it.
- **Disclosure quality is thin:** no publicly verified turnover rate, no gender/ethnicity pay-gap data, no RBA audit outcomes. Workforce metrics outside China sites are largely narrative.
- **M&A integration and restructuring:** II-VI/Coherent integration plus divestitures create retention and culture risk in a tight photonics engineering labour market — a genuine operational constraint given the AI-optics ramp.
- No material discrimination, EEOC, or product-safety litigation found in searches. No union-related controversies identified.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**For:** ~96% say-on-pay support (2025 AGM) — investors are not revolting; all four 2026 Class III nominees independent; PSU/RSU mix 60/40 with relative-TSR PSUs (reasonable alignment design); EY as auditor; one share–one vote (no dual class); third-party board grade A−; 1-yr TSR +62%, i.e., pay outcomes track performance.

**Against:**
- **Classified board** (three classes, three-year terms; Class III elected to 2029) — the single clearest structural weakness; slows removal of underperforming directors and has drawn proxy-advisor scrutiny.
- **Pay magnitude and optics:** FY2026 CEO total comp **$28.3M, pay ratio 1,381:1** (vs 163:1 prior year), following a ~$101.5M FY2024 new-hire package that put the CEO atop US highest-paid lists. FY2026 EIP paid out at **190%** on record revenue. Legitimate pay-for-performance logic, but the sign-on grant complicates retention/alignment optics and invites proxy-season friction.
- **Over-tenure director:** nominee Joseph Corasanti at ~24 years on the legacy board — independence compromised in substance.
- **Related-party watch:** **NVIDIA holds ~4.0%** of shares and is simultaneously a large customer and co-investor in the Texas expansion. Powerful commercial signal, but a governance item to monitor for arm's-length terms.
- **Capital structure / subsidy dependence:** CHIPS Act LOI ($50M) and state grant carry workforce and compliance covenants plus clawback risk; goodwill-heavy post-merger balance sheet limits heritage as an ESG-negative.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **China critical-minerals export controls (HIGH):** gallium/germanium/antimony licensing regime (since Aug-2023; expanded Apr-2025; paused Nov-2025 but not rescinded). Coherent's InP/GaAs substrate supply is directly in scope — the largest ESG-adjacent threat to the cash-flow thesis.
- **US export controls (BIS) / Entity List dynamics (MEDIUM):** China-domiciled manufacturing and sales exposure; product-level controls on advanced optics/AI hardware could expand.
- **SEC climate disclosure rule (LOW, for now):** stayed; no mandatory Scope 1/2/3 attestation in the US. Deregulatory drift reduces near-term cost but also reduces comparability.
- **EU CSRD (MEDIUM, deferred):** EU turnover above the non-EU-parent threshold puts COHR in scope in a later wave (post-Omnibus delay, ~FY2029 reporting); requires limited assurance and full double-materiality analysis — COHR's current Scope 3 gap is the main readiness item.
- **CBAM (VERY LOW):** minimal direct steel/aluminium import exposure.
- **Conflict minerals (SEC 13p-1) & UFLPA (MEDIUM):** tantalum, tungsten, tin, gold, cobalt sourcing; forced-labour traceability for China-sourced inputs — customer audits (RBA) are the practical enforcement channel.
- **CHIPS Act / Texas Semiconductor Innovation Fund (MEDIUM):** workforce, childcare and community-investment conditions with clawback provisions; creates politically contingent "social licence" obligations.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified — no sustainability-linked bond outstanding.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE (compliant operator, not a leader)**

Strong on renewable electricity and disclosure ambition; weak on Scope 3 governance, board structure (classified, over-tenured directors) and safety consistency. Clean enforcement record. This is a "passes the screen, doesn't earn a premium" profile — not a Laggard, and not close to Leader status until it discloses Scope 3 with assurance and declassifies its board.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL for the thesis**

ESG factors are **not** a driver of COHR's valuation — no divestment screen binds (no fossil-fuel, tobacco, weapons exposure; index-inclusion risk negligible), and fines/litigation are financially immaterial. Two caveats that could turn it **Negative**:

1. **Critical-materials geopolitics is the ESG factor that can actually move the P&L.** Treat Chinese gallium/germanium licensing as a supply-chain risk item in the model, not an ESG footnote — a renewed controls escalation is the highest-probability ESG-triggered earnings event (margin compression or lost revenue, not a fine).
2. **Customer-mandated Scope 3 disclosure** could become a procurement gate with hyperscaler/NVIDIA-linked customers within 2–3 years; COHR is behind peers on this and would need capex/opex to catch up.

**Governance watch items into the Nov-18-2026 AGM:** say-on-pay given the 1,381:1 ratio, and any shareholder proposal to declassify the board. A vote result below ~80% support, or an adverse proxy-advisor recommendation, would be the first genuine ESG-driven cost-of-capital signal for this name.
