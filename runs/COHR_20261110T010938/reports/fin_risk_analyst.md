### 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **SUB-INVESTMENT GRADE (BB), improving**

| Agency | Rating | Outlook/Note |
|---|---|---|
| Fitch | **BB** (senior unsecured 5.0% notes due Dec-2029) | Revised to **Positive** (Feb-2026) |
| S&P | **BB**; secured debt BB+ / unsecured BB− (upgraded from B) | Upgraded |

This is upper-tier high yield, but the trajectory is materially positive. Net leverage has collapsed from **4.82x (FY24) → 2.70x (FY25) → 0.99x (FY26)** on the back of an EBITDA doubling and modest absolute debt paydown. Interest expense fell from $288.5M (FY24) to $190.3M (FY26) as pre-merger II-VI debt was refinanced. EBIT/interest has gone from **0.49x to 5.45x** in two years. This is not a credit in distress — it is a credit that has just fixed itself.

The offsetting weakness is cash generation (Section 5), which is what keeps it at BB rather than investment grade: **TTM operating cash flow is negative** despite ~$949M of net income.

### 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue −20%

Base case is TTM revenue $7,443M / EBITDA $1,651.9M / EBIT $1,123.5M. Holding the FY26 cost structure (COGS 62.5% of sales, ~20% of COGS fixed; SG&A $1,044.6M, ~75% fixed) and letting operating leverage work:

| Gross profit | 2,820 | ~2,087 | −26% |

| **Net debt / EBITDA** | 1.57x | **~3.2x** | ~2x headroom |

**Interpretation: a 20% revenue decline is survivable on a credit basis but removes all financial flexibility.** EBITDA falls ~2x faster than revenue (operating leverage cuts both ways), yet net leverage only reaches ~3.2x — still inside typical covenant thresholds and far from distress. Two partial mitigants: (a) **working capital unwinds in a downturn** — inventory of $3.34B and receivables of $1.60B would release perhaps $0.7–1.0B of cash, roughly offsetting a year of losses; (b) **capex is discretionary** — the $1.78B TTM program can be cut to maintenance (~$400–500M), which is the single most powerful lever available.

**The tail case that matters:** −20% revenue *combined with* capex held at growth levels (management commitment to AI capacity) pushes net leverage to **~4.0x+**, which would trigger a downgrade to B+/B and put the revolver's net-leverage covenant under real pressure by year two. That is the scenario to monitor — not a liquidity event, but a **strategic-capex-versus-covenant conflict**.

| FCF yield (on $44.3B mcap) | — | — | −2.34% | **−4.15%** |
| Capex / revenue | 7.4% | 7.6% | 15.6% | **23.9%** |

The deterioration is dramatic and recent. COHR generated reliably positive FCF of ~$195M/yr for three years, then swung to **−$1.84B TTM**. The quarterly sequence is unbroken: −$95.7M → −$383.5M → −$493.4M → **−$868.2M**. Two distinct causes:

1. **Capex up 4x** ($440.8M → $1,781.7M, 23.9% of revenue) — a deliberate capacity build for AI/datacenter optics. This is growth investment, not maintenance, and is the defensible part of the story.
2. **Working capital absorption** — FY26 change in working capital **−$1,102M**; Q1 FY27 alone **−$644M**. **Inventory rose 29% in one quarter ($2.58B → $3.34B)** while revenue rose 16% sequentially. Inventory days have deteriorated from **58 to ~69**, receivable days improved (69 → 62). This is the genuinely worrying half: it is either disciplined pre-building ahead of demand, or a cash trap.

**Funding is already visible in the balance sheet:** in the September quarter, cash fell $234M and total debt rose **$809M** (to $4,347M). The capex program is being debt-funded. Static cash runway on the current quarterly burn is only **~2 quarters**, but this overstates the risk materially: operating cash flow burn was just −$93M, and the company has revolver availability plus demonstrated access to term-loan markets (the $1.25B Term A in 2025). **Liquidity is adequate; cash *quality* is poor.**

### 6. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE** (with market risk sub-rating **HIGH**)

The correct way to read COHR is that **its balance sheet is in the best shape in years while its cash flow statement is in the worst.** Leverage (1.57x net), coverage (5.75x EBIT), liquidity (2.29x current, $1.75B cash) and the maturity ladder (nothing until 2029) are all sound and improving. Against that: TTM FCF of **−$1.84B**, **negative operating cash flow**, a ~$1.0B accrual gap, BB ratings, 3% annual dilution, and a beta of ~3.

Nothing here suggests a solvency or covenant event within 12–18 months. The risk is that equity holders carry a 2.8–3.4x levered exposure to a capex cycle the company is itself funding with debt — and that if that cycle turns, the equity takes a 55–65% hit (as it has before), not that the company fails.
