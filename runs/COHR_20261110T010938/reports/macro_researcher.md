## 1. Macro Environment Summary — *Transitional, hawkish re-acceleration*

The US economy is **not late-cycle decelerating — it is re-accelerating into a Fed tightening again.** Real GDP grew **+3.4% annualized in Q3 2026** (+2.1% YoY) after +2.2% in Q2, unemployment has *fallen* to **4.1%** from 4.5% a year ago, and CPI has re-accelerated to **3.72% YoY** (Sep-26) from a **2.43%** trough in Feb-26 — with the **3-month annualized rate at ~4.6%**, i.e. inflation is firming at the margin, not rolling over.

The Fed responded: the effective funds rate bottomed at **3.63%** (Jun–Aug) and has been walked up to **3.75% (Sep)** and **3.88% (Oct)** — roughly **+50bp of hikes in two meetings**, with the trough-to-peak reversal putting policy back near neutral (real fed funds just **+0.16%**).

The long end has borne the adjustment: **10Y 4.86%** (6-Nov, FRED; the curve snapshot for 10-Nov shows 5.13% — I flag the discrepancy but both point the same way), **30Y 5.47%**, after a 5.31% peak on 5-Oct. The **2Y at 4.74% sits ~85bp ABOVE the effective funds rate** — the market is pricing three-to-four more hikes. The curve is a **bear steepener** (2s10s **+39bp**, no inversion), the classic signature of term-premium/fiscal risk rather than growth collapse.

Critically, **credit is not signalling recession**: HY OAS at **3.42%** is still historically very tight — but it has **widened 77bp since 2-Sep (2.65%)**. That combination — tight-but-widening spreads, rising real yields, firm dollar (**+3.4% since 9-Sep**) — is the signature of **risk appetite cooling at the margin without a credit event**. The pain is concentrated in long-duration equity, which is precisely COHR's category.

**Regime call: transitional / hawkish reflation. Fundamentals supportive; the discount rate is the enemy.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

COHR is a **~$9–10B revenue run-rate** optical/laser platform (Q1 FY27 revenue **$2.37B, +49.9% YoY**; Q2 guide **$2.55–2.75B**), levered to two cycle-sensitive end markets: **AI datacom optics** and **industrial/semiconductor lasers**. Its macro sensitivity is unusually high (beta 2.23) and almost entirely channelled through **two variables: hyperscaler capex and the long-bond yield.**

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL** *(barbell: favourable fundamentals, unfavourable discount rate)*

- **Demand leg — Favourable.** 2–3% real GDP, firm labour, and a >$1T 2027 AI capex pool give COHR the strongest revenue backdrop in its history. Macro is not the constraint on COHR's P&L.
- **Valuation leg — Unfavourable.** With the Fed hiking into 3.7%+ CPI, 30Y at 5.47% and the market pricing 3–4 more hikes, there is **no macro catalyst for multiple expansion** in 40x long-duration tech. A beta of 2.23 means COHR absorbs roughly 2x any index-level rate shock.
- **Net: the two roughly offset — hence Neutral — but the *distribution* is fat-tailed to the downside**, because earnings beats are already priced while rate shocks are not.

**Key caveats/gaps:** (i) I could not retrieve COHR's exact geographic revenue split or debt maturity ladder from available feeds — the leverage and currency figures above are directional; (ii) the 10Y reading differs between the FRED series (4.86%, 6-Nov) and the curve snapshot (5.13%, 10-Nov); (iii) core CPI/PCE price index was unavailable — PCE data returned is nominal spending ($22.45T, +6.3% YoY, implying ~2.5% real consumption growth), not the deflator.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Would Materially Surprise — **BEAR: the "WACC shock"**

**The setup is already 70% in place.** With the 3-month annualized CPI at **4.6%** and oil/energy pass-through building, the plausible surprise is that **headline CPI prints above 4.5% in Q1-2027**, forcing the Fed to hike past 5%, taking the **10Y through 5.75% and the 30Y toward 6%**.

Why this is uniquely damaging to COHR: the AI buildout is increasingly **debt-funded**, and hyperscaler CFOs have already been asked to justify ROIC. A 100bp+ rise in project WACC alongside a higher cost of equity is the one thing that can turn a **>$1T 2027 capex number into a trim**. That is the double-hit: **multiple compression (40x → 28x) *plus* estimate cuts.** COHR has already demonstrated it can lose half its value in five months *with earnings beating* — a repeat with an actual capex guide-down would be far worse, and a beta-2.23 stock offers no shelter.

**Mirror-image bull surprise (lower probability, high magnitude):** shelter disinflation resumes, CPI breaks back below 3%, the Fed pauses, the 10Y retraces to 4.2%, and the >$1T 2027 capex figure is confirmed. On that path COHR's $2.55–2.75B quarterly run-rate re-rates toward the **$440 Citi target** — the same +40% two-day move just witnessed, extended. **The asymmetry in the macro outcome, not the business, is what should size the position.**
