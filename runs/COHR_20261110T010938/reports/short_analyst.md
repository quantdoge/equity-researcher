# FORENSIC SHORT-SELLER ANALYSIS — COHERENT CORP (COHR)

*Price $225.19 (Nov 9, 2026) | Market cap $44.3B | EV $47.1B | Beta 2.23 | FY-end June*

---

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **LOW** (not fraud; but aggressively financed growth)

COHR just printed the best-looking income statement in its history — Q1 FY27 revenue $2.37B (+49.9% YoY), gross margin 39.1% (up from 30.9% in FY24), GAAP operating margin 15.9%. And it simultaneously generated **negative operating cash flow of –$92.7M and negative FCF of –$868M in a single quarter.**

| Metric | FY26 | Q1 FY27 (TTM basis) |
|---|---|---|
| Net income | $805M | $963M |
| Operating cash flow | $79.5M | **–$59M** |
| Capex | $1,110M | $1,782M |
| Free cash flow | **–$1,031M** | **–$1,841M** |
| Cash conversion (OCF/NI) | **9.9%** | **–6%** |
| Accruals ratio | 0.040 | n/a |

Cumulatively over the last five quarters COHR has reported **~$1.19B of net income and consumed ~$1.75B of working capital** (changes: –$159M, –$259M, –$392M, –$292M, –$644M). That is the whole story. Reported profit is real on paper; the cash is going into inventory and factories.

**Critical caveat in the other direction:** gross margin is *expanding* 250bp+/year while revenue accelerates. Channel-stuffing and revenue fabrication almost always show up as margin compression and receivables blow-out. Neither is happening here. This is the signature of genuine demand pull, not accounting fiction — which is why I am not calling this a fraud.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**1. Inventory build is extreme and out of proportion to revenue.**
Inventory $1.28B (Jun-24) → $2.58B (Jun-26) → **$3.34B (Sep-26)**. Inventory days have gone from 144 → 140 → 212 → **~249 (TTM)**. Inventory now equals **68% of annual COGS** (39% a year ago). $756M of inventory was added in *one quarter* against $325M of quarterly revenue growth. Either this is strategic pre-positioning for a guided >60% FY27 ramp, or it is a 800G→1.6T product-mix mismatch waiting to be written down. A 20% write-down is ~$670M — roughly two quarters of net income.

**2. Cash conversion is below any acceptable threshold.** 9.9% in FY26, negative on a TTM basis. The company has never converted earnings to cash in the AI era.

**3. Receivables outrunning revenue.** AR +31.7% vs revenue +22.5% (FY26); DSO 64 → 74 days. DSRI 1.075. This is a yellow flag, not a red one (~$220M of cash), but it is moving in the wrong direction.

**4. The earnings beat is partly a tax artifact.** FY26: EBIT $1,038M – interest $190M = ~$848M pre-tax, yet net income was $805M → **effective tax rate ~5%**. In Q1 FY27 net income ($385M) *exceeded* pre-tax income ($359M) — a net tax benefit. A normalized 20% tax rate would have cut FY26 EPS by roughly $1.15/share. This is not sustainable and is not in anyone's model.

**5. Massive dilution, and it is accelerating.** Basic shares 155.5M (Jun-25) → **196.2M (Sep-26): +26% in 15 months.** Diluted 202.0M, and diluted EPS ($1.90) is 3% below basic ($1.96). Book equity rose $3.18B in a year while retained earnings rose only $1.16B — i.e. **~$2.0B of equity/convert issuance**, on top of total debt rising $3.42B → $4.35B. Management is selling paper into its own momentum to fund working capital. Piotroski F7 (no dilution) = 0. FY23 shares were 137.6M — the share count is up 47% in three years.

**6. Goodwill + intangibles of $7.26B = 65% of book equity and 41% of total assets** — the undigested II-VI/Coherent merger. No impairment taken through a period in which the stock fell 49%. Fair-value marks on the legacy industrial segment could be required.

**7. Insider behaviour: selling, no buying.** ~14 open-market sales in six months totalling ~$9.9M versus zero purchases — including a director sale at $268 not under a 10b5-1 plan (Sept 2) and a CFO sale at $306.68 (July 22). Dollar amounts are small versus a $44B cap, so this is a *sentiment* flag, not a thesis. I could not verify direction on all 30 Form 4s in the window; treatment is directional only.

## 3. Beneish M-Score and Accruals — **the model is broken here, and I'll say so**

Reported: **M = –1.803 ("moderate risk," above the –1.78 threshold).** Do not trade on this number. The score is driven almost entirely by **DEPI = 9.515**, which is a *data artifact*: the annual balance sheet returns FY26 PPE of $21.29B, which exceeds total assets of $18.30B. The quarterly file shows actual net PPE of $2.999B. Recomputing DEPI on the correct figure gives ~1.54 and a corrected **M-score of roughly –2.72** — comfortably inside the "no manipulation indicated" zone. AQI (–0.887) is likewise non-economic.

**Accruals:** ratio 0.040 ("ok") — but this is flattered by a denominator that ballooned from $14.9B to $20.2B of assets. The honest accruals measure here is the absolute one: **–$1.84B of TTM FCF against $963M of TTM net income.**

**Verdict: no evidence of manipulation. Evidence of aggressive capitalization** (capex +$1.78B TTM, partially capitalizable ramp costs) and heavy use of "pro forma" framing — management leads with "pro forma revenue up 56%" and "non-GAAP EPS up 80%." Prefer GAAP and cash.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

- **No moat erosion visible in the numbers.** Gross margin up 830bp in two years is the single strongest counter-argument to a short. Vertical integration in indium phosphide (own wafer fabs, own laser sources) is genuinely scarce in the AI optical chain.
- **Real long-cycle threats:** (a) Chinese module vendors (Innolight, Eoptolink, Accelink) compress pricing on pluggables; (b) **co-packaged optics** (Broadcom, Nvidia photonics) displaces pluggable transceivers over 2028+; (c) customer concentration in a handful of hyperscalers whose capex is the entire demand curve.
- **The structural problem is cyclicality, not competition.** FY24 revenue *fell 8.8%*. This is a business that has already demonstrated it can shrink in a demand air-pocket — and it is now adding $3B+/yr of annualized capacity at the top of the cycle. Fixed-cost deleverage on the way down will be brutal.

## 5. Management Red Flags
Moderate, not disqualifying. (i) 26% share count increase in 15 months while guiding a hyper-growth story; (ii) pro-forma-forward disclosure style; (iii) tax benefits and other income lifting GAAP EPS; (iv) heavy Form 4 selling with no insider purchases; (v) capital allocation — raising debt and equity simultaneously to fund a capacity build at peak-cycle valuations.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, but it is a cyclical/valuation short, not a forensic one.**

The bear case: COHR is a historically cyclical, capital-intensive component maker trading at 5.6x TTM sales and 46.7x TTM GAAP EPS, while generating **–$1.8B of trailing FCF**, on consensus that requires revenue to go from $7.1B (FY26) to **$30.4B by FY30** — a 34% CAGR for four straight years, covered by *one* analyst at the far end. Inventory at 249 days and rising is the visible manifestation of the bet. If hyperscaler optical capex digests — even for two quarters — you get inventory write-downs, negative operating leverage on a much larger depreciation base (capex is annualizing >$3B), and a stock with a 2.2 beta and a demonstrated ability to fall 49% in four months (June high $440 → Oct low $232 → $160 intraday on Nov 5). S&P just upgraded to BB *while explicitly forecasting negative FOCF in FY27*.

**The bull case is stronger than I expected, and I will not pretend otherwise.** Revenue is *accelerating* (+49.9% YoY, guided >60% pro forma for FY27, first $3B quarter targeted by FYE), gross margins are expanding, net debt has fallen from $3.67B to $2.79B, interest expense is down, Piotroski is 6, Altman Z of 1.90 is grey-zone-but-not-distress (and that score uses book equity; on a $44B market cap there is no solvency issue). If FY28 consensus EPS of $15.13 is even close, the stock is at 14.9x and shorting it is a fast way to lose money. Note also: **management has repeatedly raised guidance** — the pattern of the last four quarters has been acceleration, not deceleration.

**What would make me pound the table short:** two consecutive quarters of flat-to-down revenue with inventory days still >200; any inventory write-down disclosure; gross margin rolling over below 37%; capex guided *up* while revenue decelerates; or a halt in the hyperscaler capex commentary.

---

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **NO EDGE** (on the fraud/manipulation question)

I cannot build a forensic short here. The receivables trend does not support channel stuffing, gross margins are expanding not contracting, the Beneish flag is a PPE data artifact that washes out on correction, and the accruals ratio is benign once you use absolute cash flow. The cash-flow divergence is real and severe, but it is **disclosed, quantified, and understood by the rating agencies** — that is a financing-risk observation, not an informational edge.

**Tactical addendum:** as a *cycle-and-valuation* short it is credible — negative FCF, 249 inventory days, 26% dilution, collapsing tax rate, and a consensus hockey stick covered by a single FY30 analyst. But with revenue accelerating at 50% and margins expanding, the timing edge is nil and the squeeze risk is enormous (the stock rose 33% in two trading days after the Nov 5 flush). **Position sizing must reflect a 2.2-beta momentum name, not a fraud unwind.** I would express this view through the AI-optics complex (pairs against a pluggable-module pure-play or hyperscaler capex) rather than a naked short of COHR.

The Beneish D/AQI/DEPI inputs are unreliable as a result; my corrected M-score (~–2.72) is an estimate. Insider transaction *direction* was only partially verifiable (aggregated third-party reporting, not the Forms 4 themselves). No 10-K text was retrievable.
