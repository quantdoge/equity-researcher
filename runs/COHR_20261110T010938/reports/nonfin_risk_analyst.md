## NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — COHERENT CORP. (NYSE: COHR)

**Company:** Coherent Corp. (photonics/lasers/optical networking; ~51,500 employees; HQ Saxonburg, PA; FYE June 30). CEO Jim Anderson (since June 2024), CFO Sherri Luther, CTO Julie Sheridan Eng, Chief Strategy & Legal Officer Rob Beard. Market cap ~$38–44B, beta 2.23. *Note: the vendor company profile returned legacy pre-II-VI-merger text ("wholly-owned subsidiary of II-VI"); I verified entity data via SEC filing metadata instead.*

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE** (strong structure, real soft spots)

**Positives**
- **CEO/Chair separation confirmed.** Enrico DiGirolamo has served as *independent Chair of the Board since June 2024*; the board explicitly cites separation of duties as strengthening oversight. Anderson is a director but not Chair.
- Board graded **A-** by third-party governance scoring; FY2026 proxy is conventional — EY ratification, annual say-on-pay, LTI mix 60% performance stock units / 40% RSUs, with PSUs tied to **relative TSR** (shareholder-aligned).
- No restatement, no auditor change, no material-weakness disclosure surfaced in the FY2026 (10-K filed Aug 2026) or Q1 FY2027 filings.

**Concerns**
- **Classified (staggered) board** — four Class Three directors elected to three-year terms to 2029. Combined with **24-year tenure for Joseph Corasanti** (still labeled independent), this is entrenched, low-refresh governance.
- **Incentive optics are awkward:** FY2026 short-term plan paid out at **190%** on revenue/adj. EBITDA in the same period the stock fell ~45% from its high. Pay is performance-contingent, but management was rewarded richly for a cycle that shareholders then re-rated sharply lower.
- **Capital allocation:** the ~$2B NVIDIA equity investment (announced Mar 2, 2026) came alongside **~26% dilution** (per sell-side commentary — treat as approximate, not company-confirmed). Equity issued near peak prices to a strategic counterparty is shareholder-accretive only if the capacity build pays off; it also cements a related-party dynamic (see §2).
- **Bain Capital fully exited** (7.5M shares at $143.37, Nov 2025, $1.08B, now 0% held) — overhang cleared, but the sponsor that installed the merged board is gone, leaving limited external scrutiny.

### 2. Regulatory & Legal Risk Summary: **MODERATE, trending worse**

- **No SEC enforcement actions or fines found** — EDGAR full-text enforcement search for COHR returned **zero** results. No DOJ/antitrust action identified.
- **Active securities litigation:** a case docketed May 7, 2026 under "Securities/Commodities/Exchanges" (D. Mass.), plus **Rosen Law has publicly announced an investigation of potential securities claims** against Coherent. Allegations likely centre on AI-demand/capacity disclosures during a period when the stock fell ~45%; exposure is unquantified. *I could not retrieve the complaint (Justia docket blocked, 403) — the specific allegations remain unverified.* Historical base rate: 61% of 2026 securities settlements carried parallel derivative suits, so board-level D&O exposure should be assumed.
- **Export controls / geopolitics (highest-probability recurring risk):** China has restricted **gallium, germanium, antimony and rare-earth** exports since 2023; enhancements have been paused/suspended at times, creating whipsaw licensing risk. Coherent has publicly issued an "Initial Assessment of Expected Export Restrictions of Gallium and Germanium From China" — i.e., management itself flagged materials exposure. Compound with tariff/Section-232 volatility and China-based manufacturing.
- **Government funding conditions:** June 16, 2026 **CHIPS LOI for $50M** to expand an AI-infrastructure fab — brings compliance covenants, audit rights and political/appropriations risk.
- **Concentration → antitrust/regulatory attention:** the proposed **Marvell–Lumentum ($115B) merger** is reshaping the optics market; as a principal remaining supplier to hyperscalers, Coherent's long-term agreements (incl. NVIDIA) invite scrutiny of exclusive/lock-up terms.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Extreme, deepening customer/supplier concentration with NVIDIA.** The **~$20B five-year PhotonLink agreement** (ramping toward ~$250M/quarter by CY2027-end) plus NVIDIA's $2B equity stake makes one counterparty simultaneously **largest customer, technology partner, and significant shareholder**. The FY2026 10-K confirms **two customers each >10% of revenue** — and that was *before* PhotonLink scales. Any design-loss, roadmap slip, or insourcing decision by NVIDIA is an existential revenue event, and it is also a governance/related-party conflict of interest.
- **InP capacity ramp, inventory and cash burn.** The AI optical buildout is capacity-constrained (indium phosphide), and Coherent is spending heavily to convert to volume: Q1 FY27 revenue $2.37B (+50% Y/Y), guidance >60% FY27 growth, "$4B quarter by FY2028." Reported large inventory builds are defended as intentional; if hyperscaler orders slip (Elon Musk has publicly warned AI-datacenter debt markets are seizing up; October 2026 saw exactly that fear hit the whole optics complex), this converts to **inventory write-down and utilization risk**. Single-site catastrophe exposure at key fabs (Sherman TX; Easton PA; Saxonburg) is unmitigated by redundancy.
- **Key-person and organizational churn.** Anderson is ~2 years into the job; the **CTO sold 24% of her stake** and the Chief Strategy/Legal Officer is also selling — at a moment when the company is executing its largest-ever capacity and customer ramp. Add ~51,500 employees post-merger integration, and execution depth is a genuine question.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **MODERATE**

- No product recalls, safety incidents, consumer backlash, or ESG controversy surfaced. The quantum-safe QKD partnership with CUbIQ (Sept 2026) and NVIDIA/CHIPS endorsements are reputation *assets*.
- Reputation is instead **market- and narrative-driven**: a ~45% drawdown, 13–14% single-day swings on AI-capex sentiment, sell-side framing that Coherent "sells volume, not scarcity" versus Lumentum, and visible dilution. That is a shareholder-confidence issue more than a brand issue — but it amplifies the litigation and activist-investor surface.
- Watch item: if the securities action matures into a derivative suit naming directors, the *Chairman's* non-plan sales at $306 become a reputational exhibit.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH** (lower end of the High band)

No fraud indicators, no enforcement history, no accounting irregularity, and structurally sound board independence keep this out of *Critical*. But three factors compound: (a) a **binary concentration** on one customer-shareholder-partner; (b) **live securities litigation with unknown exposure**; (c) **insider alignment pointing the wrong way** — discretionary selling by the Chairman and CTO with no offsetting buys. Move to *Moderate* if litigation is dismissed/quantified immaterial, insiders buy the dip, and PhotonLink ramps on schedule.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Chairman DiGirolomo and Director Neal-Graves selling outside 10b5-1 plans** at $306/$268 — immediate escalation; request the company's insider-trading policy, any Rule 144 plans, and whether sales preceded any material non-public information (e.g., PhotonLink terms, Q1 FY27 guidance).
2. **Securities class action (May 2026) + Rosen Law investigation** — obtain the complaint; assert D&O tail coverage and indemnification; diarize derivative-suit risk.
3. **NVIDIA's triple role** (customer + $2B shareholder + supply partner) with a $20B contract — demand full related-party disclosure and confirmation of arm's-length pricing/audit-committee approval.
4. **Gallium/germanium/rare-earth licensing exposure** — confirm inventory hedging, alternative sourcing, and whether any customer allocations have already slipped.
5. **~26% dilution from the $2B equity raise** — verify against the 10-Q share count; assess whether the capacity build is demand-committed or speculative.
