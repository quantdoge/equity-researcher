## 2. Consensus Assessment: **MEDIUM** (structurally split, not genuinely contested)

**High agreement (this is the important part):**
- **The business is real and accelerating.** Sector, Growth, Alt Data, Short, and Fin Risk all independently confirm: revenue +50%, gross margin expanding 250bp+/yr, 100% beat rate, contracted $20B backlog, fabs hiring. The Short Analyst explicitly refuses to call fraud. **The bear case is not an accounting bear case.**
- **Cash flow is the problem.** Growth, Fin Risk, Short, Quant, and Non-Fin all land on the same number: −$1.8B TTM FCF, negative OCF, inventory build outrunning revenue. There is no disagreement here — only disagreement on *interpretation* (deliberate capacity build vs future write-down).

**Genuine split (only two items):**
1. **Valuation.** Growth says 14.9x FY28E is cheap. Quant says P/FCF −42.7 and bottom-quintile factor scores. Resolution: **both are right about different things** — cheap on *consensus forward EPS that requires a 34% four-year CAGR*, expensive on every trailing cash-based measure. The Value Researcher's absence means I cannot adjudicate this with an independent anchor; I therefore treat valuation as **unresolved** and size for it.
2. **Insider behaviour.** Non-Fin flags discretionary Chair/CTO selling as an escalation item; Growth/Alt Data barely mention it. Resolution: dollar amounts (~$10–20M) are immaterial against a $44B cap and mostly post-vesting — this is a **sentiment/alignment negative, not a thesis-breaker**, but it removes any argument for sizing up.

**Conflict resolved in favour of the risk agents, not the growth agents:** the FCF and macro findings are primary-source and quantified; the growth case rests on estimates where the FY30 anchor is covered by *one* analyst. Per my framework, cash-flow/valuation risk outranks narrative optimism. **Net: medium conviction, elevated risk.**

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **2.0% of portfolio** (staged to a 2.5% max)

**Drawdown-budget method (binding constraint):** COHR's realized 3-yr max drawdown is **−60.2%** and it has already happened twice in twelve months. I cap the loss a repeat would inflict at **~1.2% of portfolio NAV** → 1.2% ÷ 60% = **2.0%**.

**Cross-check — beta budget:** realized β vs SPY is **3.59**. A 2.0% position adds **+0.072 of portfolio beta** (1.00 → 1.07). At 2.5% it adds +0.09, which is the ceiling I'm willing to let a single, negative-FCF, single-partner-dependent name contribute.

**Kelly sanity check:** bull $340 (+51%), bear retest of $160 (−29%); roughly 50/50 on a 12-month view → EV ≈ +11%, σ ≈ 60%. Full Kelly ≈ 0.30 of the *bet*; half-Kelly, then scaled for a 3.6 beta and unresolved valuation → **1.5–2.5%**. The drawdown method and Kelly converge.

**Why not larger (3–5%):** negative FCF, 26% dilution in 15 months, ~$1.0B accrual gap, a macro regime hostile to 40x long-duration tech, an unresolved value anchor, and insiders selling without a single open-market buy.
**Why not smaller/pass (0.5–1%):** the business quality is genuinely top-of-group (vertical integration into the scarcest input), the NVIDIA underwritten backlog, and the −49% de-rating already discount a capex stumble. A half-position would make the work irrelevant to returns.

- **Liquidity: no constraint.** 5–17M shares/day (~$1–4B notional) — a 2% position is a rounding error either way.

## 6. Risk Management Rules

**Price stop:** weekly close below **$165** (under the 5-Nov intraday low of $160.23) → exit tranche 1 and 2 in full. This is deliberately wide because the name's daily noise is ~5–6%; a tight stop simply donates money.

**Thesis invalidation (exit on any one; these outrank the price stop):**
1. **Any inventory write-down disclosure**, or inventory days staying >200 for two quarters while revenue growth decelerates. (Inventory $2.58B → $3.34B in one quarter is the single visible manifestation of the bet — this is the #1 monitorable.)
2. **Non-GAAP gross margin below 37%** — breaks the entire margin-catch-up thesis the whole bull case is built on.
3. **Two consecutive quarters of flat-to-down datacom revenue**, or a miss against the $2.55–2.75B Q2 guide.
4. **Capex guided *up* while revenue decelerates** — this is the Fin Risk "strategic-capex-versus-covenant" scenario (net leverage → 4.0x+, downgrade to B+/B). Cut immediately.
5. **Any adverse change to NVIDIA PhotonLink terms, a design loss, or evidence of non-arm's-length pricing** (NVIDIA is simultaneously top customer, $2B shareholder, and partner).
6. **Macro kill-switch:** 10Y >5.75% *and* HY OAS >4.25% simultaneously (the Macro Researcher's "WACC shock"). Reduce by half regardless of fundamentals — the stock is a duration instrument.
7. **Litigation escalation:** the May-2026 securities action maturing into a derivative suit naming directors, or any restatement/SEC follow-on.

**Trim triggers:**
- **$300 (~89% of the $340 blended FV):** trim one-third of the position.
- **$340:** trim to a 1.0% core.
- **$400:** trim to 0.5% runner; **exit above $440** (the June high) unless FY28 FCF is confirmed positive.
- **Upside-side trim trigger no one else flagged:** if the stock re-rates above $340 *before* operating cash flow turns positive, treat it as a gift and sell into it — you would be paid for a cash conversion that hasn't happened.

**Upgrade conditions (raise to 3.0–3.5%):** two consecutive quarters of positive OCF + FY28 capex guidance decelerating toward maintenance + gross margin ≥42% + a declassified board/insider open-market buying + the 10Y back below 4.5%.

**Rationale in one line:** COHR is the highest-quality *scale* asset in the strongest demand cycle in optical networking history, run by a management team that is beating and raising — and it is a 3.6-beta, negative-free-cash-flow, single-partner-concentrated instrument trading on a discount rate the Fed is actively pushing up. **Upgrade path under management:** a repeat of the Nov-2026 drawdown *on sentiment with fundamentals intact* (as opposed to on an inventory write-down) is an add opportunity, not an exit — that distinction is the whole discipline of this position.
