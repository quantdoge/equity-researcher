### 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — a re-tightening regime masquerading as a pause. Risk-off for rate-sensitive, levered cyclicals.**

The dominant macro fact is that the 2025 easing cycle has gone into reverse in the bond market even though the Fed has not moved. The FOMC **held at 3.75–4.00% on 28 October 2026** in an explicit "wait and see" posture, but the data forced that hold: CPI has re-accelerated from **2.7% YoY (Nov 2025) to 3.72% YoY (Sep 2026)**, with the last three months running at a **~4.5% annualised** pace. Meanwhile real GDP accelerated to **~3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026** and unemployment fell to **4.2%**. This is an economy that is too hot for the easing the market priced in a year ago.

The consequence is a classic **bear steepener**. The 10Y has backed up from **4.44% (30 June) to 5.13%**, having touched **5.31% intraday on 5 October**, and the 30Y sits at **5.47%**. Critically, the **2Y at 4.74% now trades ~86bp *above* the effective fed funds rate of 3.88%** — the front end is pricing roughly three hikes, not cuts. There is no inversion (2s10s **+39bp**), so no recession signal, but the term premium is expanding sharply. That is the worst possible rate configuration for a long-duration, debt-financed industrial equity.

Against this, the real economy is a genuine tailwind: industrial production is up **2.25% YoY** and rising every month since January, and RRX's own orders are accelerating. **The macro tension for RRX is entirely between a strong operating backdrop and a hostile discount rate.**

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL — with a distinct negative skew**

Justification: the *operating* macro (GDP +2–3%, INDPRO +2.25%, unemployment 4.2%, orders +6%, AI capex) is genuinely favourable and is what produced the **+11.8% single-day move on 3 November** on Q3 results. But the *financial* macro (10Y 5.13%, 2Y pricing hikes, 3.7% and rising CPI, firm USD, 6% coupons to roll) is unfavourable, and it hits RRX through the two channels that matter most for a levered industrial: the discount rate and the cost of refinancing. Net: **earnings momentum is winning for now, but the macro is a headwind that caps the re-rating.**

---

### 5. The Surprise Scenario (Bear, primary)

**"The 10Y breaks 5.75% and the Fed hikes twice."** If the 3-month CPI run-rate of ~4.5% persists into Q1 2027, the FOMC's own September SEP (median 4.00–4.25% for end-2026/2027) becomes a hiking path rather than a hold. A move to 5.75–6.0% on the 10Y would do three things simultaneously to RRX:

1. Compress the multiple from 11.8x to ~9.5x EV/EBITDA on unchanged EBITDA → equity value of roughly **$9.7bn, or ~$145/share**.
2. Force the **Feb 2026-maturity refinancing and 2027/2028 maturities at 6.5%+**, adding ~$25–40m of annual interest and stalling the deleveraging story at ~2.7x.
3. Crack the one thing holding the stock up: **AI/data-centre capex is not infinitely rate-insensitive.** If hyperscaler debt funding costs and project IRRs deteriorate, the +307% data centre growth decelerates precisely when the multiple is already under pressure — a double hit.

**Upside surprise worth flagging (bull):** inflation rolls over quickly as the 2025 tariff-refund pass-through normalises, the 10Y retraces to ~4.4%, and RRX re-rates toward **$230+** on 2.9x leverage and 6% organic growth. The asymmetry favours the bear case because the starting point is a 5.13% 10Y *and* a stock that just gapped 12% in a day — that gap is macro-beta that can be given back fast if the bond market pushes further.

**Positioning implication:** own RRX for the data-centre power franchise, but size it as a rate-sensitive name, not a defensive industrial. The macro hedge is duration — watch the 10Y, not the ISM.
