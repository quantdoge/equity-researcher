## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Waste Connections, Inc. (NYSE/TSX: WCN)

**Snapshot:** $45.4B market cap | Industrials / Waste Management | ~24,200 employees | Ontario-domiciled, Houston-area HQ (The Woodlands, TX) | Founder-led (Ronald J. Mittelstaedt, President & CEO) | Beta 0.48

Findings below are sourced from EPA, Cal DTSC/OAG, Q4-2025 earnings disclosure, Form 4 data, and company governance pages. Gaps are noted where material.*

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate** (structurally sound, with concentration flags)

**Evidence for:**
- **CEO/Chair roles are separated.** Mittelstaedt stepped down as Executive Chairman in April 2023 and became President & CEO; lead independent director **Michael W. Harlan** became **non-executive Chairman**. This is the correct structure for a founder-controlled company.
- Four standing committees (Audit, Compensation, Nominating & Corporate Governance, plus an Executive Committee historically), with independent chairs. Audit Committee comprises Florness, Harlan, Jordan and Sparks.
- All eight director nominees were elected at the 2026 annual meeting; say-on-pay is on the ballot, and the dividend was raised 10.0% (Oct 2026) with >$500M of buybacks in 2025 — capital return is shareholder-friendly.
- No dual-class share structure identified; one share, one vote.

**Flags:**
- **Key-person concentration.** Mittelstaedt is the founder and has been the dominant executive since 1997. He previously took a **"temporary leave of absence"** (returned July 2019), and the April 2023 CEO transition from Worthing Jackman was abrupt. Two unplanned CEO-level disruptions in seven years at a founder-led firm is a real succession-risk signal. CFO Mary Anne Whitney and COO Jason Craft are the only other named executive officers of scale — thin bench.
- **Small board (8 directors)** for a $45B company; limited room for fully independent committee rotation.
- **Weak insider ownership among non-CEO executives.** Post-transaction holdings are de minimis: Chief Accounting Officer **404 shares**, SVP Business Development **734 shares**, SVP Operations **1,250 shares**. Director Florness held **1,567 shares** (~$285K) after his April 2026 award — low "skin in the game" for a director of a mega-cap.
- Severance/change-in-control plan participation extends deep into the SVP ranks (e.g., July 2026 letter agreement for a new SVP — 70% bonus target, equity eligibility, CIC acceleration, plus aggressive data-return/inspection covenants). Broad CIC acceleration raises a modest takeover-cost/misalignment consideration.

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **Elevated — concentrated in one asset (Chiquita Canyon)**

This is the dominant non-financial risk in the WCN story.

**Chiquita Canyon Landfill (Castaic, Los Angeles County, CA) — 639-acre MSW landfill, operated by Chiquita Canyon, LLC, a WCN subsidiary**
- A **Subsurface Elevated Temperature (SET) reaction** began ~May 2022 and has expanded to a **30–35 acre** zone. Leachate generation rose from ~150,000 gal/week (Jan 2022) to **>1,000,000 gal/week** (Dec 2023). South Coast AQMD logged **>26,000 odor complaints** since Jan 2023 (107+ Notices of Violation by Feb 2024 under Rule 402).
- **Landfill closed to waste acceptance as of January 1, 2025** — so revenue exposure is gone, but cost, remediation and litigation exposure is not.
- **EPA:** Unilateral Administrative Order in place; **October 19, 2026 work order** (superseding Sept 24, 2026) directing flare operation, thermal oxidizer capacity, fugitive-emission capture at leachate storage, and sulfur-oxide treatment. A separate **EPA Finding of Violation was issued October 19, 2026**.
- **Cal DTSC (Jan 26, 2026):** found Chiquita Canyon LLC **and parent Waste Connections US, Inc.** out of compliance with hazardous-waste laws, subject to penalties of **up to $25,000/day**.
- **California Attorney General (filed 2026; DTSC announcement Oct 27, 2026):** 12 causes of action alleging the operator "repeatedly, consistently and pervasively" failed to properly manage hazardous waste; seeks **civil penalties of $2,500–$70,000 per violation, disgorgement of profits, injunctive relief, and an abatement fund**.
- **Civil litigation:** *Howse et al. v. Chiquita Canyon, LLC, et al.* (filed Sept 2023, LASC, removed to C.D. Cal.) plus multiple resident nuisance/class actions and a **Los Angeles County lawsuit**. Additional LA County landfill-expansion litigation is disclosed in the notes.
- **Financial evidence of materiality:** FY2024 operating loss included **$602.4M of impairments** "primarily related to the early closure of a landfill and adjustments to landfill closure and post-closure costs"; FY2025 included **$109.7M** of impairments and other items "primarily related to an environmental liability at an operating facility" plus a write-down of a non-operating E&P waste facility permit. **Two consecutive years of nine-figure environmental charges** on this matter-class is the key point.
- **Residual tail risk:** ongoing slope-stability concerns (EPA directed a soil buttress on the western slope in March 2026), leachate treatment capacity, and possible expansion of the reaction area. Aggregate exposure is unquantified in available sources — a genuine gap.

**Other regulatory items:**
- **Permitting/landfill-opposition risk** across the network: Seneca Falls, NY community agreement reported at **>$100M**; expansion fights in LA County, Bethlehem PA, Oneida TN, Flagler County FL. Permitting friction is a structural growth risk for the whole sector.
- **E&P waste segment** is state-regulated (not RCRA Subtitle C), with variability across states, injection-well/induced-seismicity scrutiny and produced-water rules. A non-operating E&P permit was already written down in 2025 — evidence of asset-impairment risk in this segment.
- **Labor/employment:** Violation Tracker records a **$1.1M wage-and-hour matter** and environmental violations (~$460K); a *Pinnacle v. Waste Connections* class action settlement (fairness hearing Dec 7, 2026) is pending.
- **No SEC enforcement actions** found in EDGAR full-text search — a clean result for securities-law/enforcement risk.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Single-site environmental catastrophe tail (Chiquita Canyon).** An ongoing, expanding subsurface reaction with slope-failure and leachate-management risk, four regulators (EPA, DTSC, South Coast AQMD, RWQCB), an active state AG suit with per-violation penalties and disgorgement, and no disclosed upper bound on cost. Already drove ~$600M and ~$110M of charges in consecutive years. **Highest-severity item in the file.**
- **Landfill permitting and community-opposition dependency.** Growth and margin depend on internalizing volume into owned disposal. The portfolio faces organized NIMBY/legal opposition to expansions (LA County, Bethlehem PA, Oneida TN, Flagler County FL) and a reported **>$100M community agreement** at Seneca Falls. A permit denial or delay removes disposal capacity and compresses the price/cost spread. Concentrated regional exposure (California, Northeast, Pacific Northwest, Texas, E&P in the Permian/OK/TX) adds state-regulatory idiosyncrasy.
- **Key-person and succession dependency on the founder-CEO**, combined with a thin named-executive bench, a small 8-person board, and a history of abrupt senior transitions (2019 leave, 2023 CEO change). Also relevant: sector-wide CDL driver/labor tightness and turnover — though management reports **three consecutive years of declining turnover and safety incident rates**, exiting 2025 at multi-year lows, which materially mitigates this leg.

*Not verifiable from available sources (gap): no cybersecurity incident history was disclosed in the material reviewed; no breach or ransomware event surfaced in search. Technology/AI-driven efficiency initiatives were cited as a growth lever but the associated execution and cyber-risk posture could not be assessed.*

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Moderate-to-High, asset-specific**

- Chiquita Canyon is a **named, multi-year, high-visibility environmental justice controversy** with state-AG involvement, ~26,000 odor complaints, resident relocation disputes, a congressional/state-legislative spotlight, and dedicated plaintiff-firm marketing ("Can I sue Waste Connections for the smell?"). The company's own response ("many of the allegations ... are without merit") invites continued adversarial coverage.
- **Brand damage is largely localized** to the Santa Clarita Valley and California regulatory politics; WCN's core franchise is B2B/residential collection where contracts are municipal and sticky, so consumer-backlash revenue risk is low. The greater risk is **license-to-operate**: a hostile California posture raises the cost and difficulty of permitting future capacity in the state.
- **Positives:** ESG/sustainability reporting is established; safety and turnover metrics are improving and publicly claimed; dividend growth and buybacks support the shareholder narrative. Analyst sentiment is strongly positive (consensus "Strong Buy," C$200 target), and the stock is +11% post-Q3'26 beat — implying the market currently treats Chiquita as contained.
- **No evidence of accounting fraud, restatement, auditor change, or whistleblower complaint** was found. Internal control over financial reporting received an unqualified audit opinion (per 10-K reference). Accounting-quality items are limited to recurring "impairments and other items" exclusions from adjusted EBITDA — legitimate, but investors should note ~$110M of 2025 charges were excluded from adjusted results.

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Rationale: Governance structure is genuinely good (independent chair, separated roles, clean SEC enforcement record, no fraud indicators), the business is essential-service and diversified across 300+ operations, and the CEO put $7.6M of personal capital to work in May 2026. Against that, one asset carries an unresolved, escalating, multi-regulator environmental and legal crisis with no disclosed cost ceiling, and the company has now taken two consecutive years of nine-figure environmental impairments. That combination justifies **Moderate** overall, with **regulatory/environmental risk on Chiquita graded High** on a standalone basis. This is not a "Critical" file — the liability is site-specific and the landfill is already closed to waste — but it is the item most likely to produce a negative surprise.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🚩 ESCALATE — Chiquita Canyon unquantified liability.** California AG suit (12 causes of action) seeking $2,500–$70,000 **per violation**, disgorgement and an abatement fund, layered on DTSC's $25,000/day exposure and an October 19, 2026 EPA Finding of Violation plus new work order. Request management's disclosed **reasonably possible loss range** and the accrual balance; two years of nine-figure charges without a stated ceiling is the single biggest gap in the investment case.
2. **🚩 ESCALATE — Expansion of the subsurface reaction.** EPA reports the reaction zone has grown to 30–35 acres and directed a **soil buttress to prevent slope failure** (March 2026). Physical escalation, not litigation, is what turns this from a $100M problem into a $1B problem.
3. **🚩 Founder key-person and succession concentration.** Unexplained 2019 leave plus abrupt 2023 CEO change; 8-person board; no visible heir apparent. Request the board's formal succession plan at the next management meeting.
4. **⚠ Monitor — Insider breadth.** 11+ insiders selling vs. one buyer (the CEO), several selling to token residual holdings and one non-10b5-1 sale. The CEO's $7.6M buy materially offsets this, but the alignment of the operating team is thin.
5. **⚠ Monitor — Permitting pipeline.** Seneca Falls ~$100M community agreement and multiple contested expansions; quantify how much of the 2026-27 internalization/EBITDA plan depends on permits not yet granted.
6. **⚠ Monitor — E&P segment.** A non-operating E&P waste permit was already written down in 2025; state-level produced-water/injection-well rules are a live re-regulation channel.
