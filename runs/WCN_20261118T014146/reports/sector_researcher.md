## SECTOR BRIEF — Waste Connections (WCN) | Industrials → Waste Management / Environmental Services

*All figures USD. Latest data: Q3 CY2026 results (reported 22-Oct-2026); FY2025 annuals. Peer set = true industry comparables (WM, RSG, CWST, GFL), not the broader Industrials sector screen.*

### 1. Sector Overview

Solid waste is a ~$100bn+ US revenue industry (broader global waste management ~$1.4–1.5trn) built on non-discretionary, contracted, recurring collection and disposal — roughly two-thirds of US landfills are owned by the top three players (WM ~24–26% of landfill volumes/collection share, RSG ~15%, WCN ~13%), i.e. the top three control >60% of disposal capacity. Barriers to entry are extremely high: permitting of new MSW landfills is effectively closed in most metros (US landfill count fell ~80% since 1990), route density creates local scale economies, and capital intensity (trucks, transfer stations, closure/post-closure obligations) is heavy — HHI is moderate nationally but very high locally. The sector is **mature but structurally growing at 5–8%** (GDP+ via CPI-linked escalators plus consolidation), currently in a **healthy mid/late-cycle phase: pricing 5–6% industry-wide, volumes mixed but inflecting positive on the disposal side, and M&A at peak activity** (178 waste/recycling deals in 2025; financial buyers 52.8% of volume). Q3'26 industry read-through: collection pricing +4.6–5.9%, landfill pricing +4.6%, third-party landfill volumes +5.3% — demand is holding despite a soft industrial economy. Relative to the Industrials aggregate (34 largest companies: $1.09trn revenue, 33.0% gross margin, **10.4% net margin**), waste is a below-average-growth, above-average-margin, far-below-average-cyclicality sub-sector.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Structural pricing power with contractual escalation.** Every major player printed 5–6% core price in Q3'26 (WCN +5.5%, WM +5.8%, CWST +5.9%, GFL +5.7%, RSG +5.3%) against a landfill-permitting backdrop that makes disposal capacity a scarce, appreciating asset. Pricing is covering cost inflation and then some — margins are expanding, not just holding.
- **Disposal/volume inflection.** Landfill tonnage turned positive across the group: CWST third-party landfill volumes +5.3% (revenue basis +12.8%), WCN landfill tonnage +3%, special waste +7%, C&D +5%; RSG saw its first simultaneous small- and large-container volume growth in 3+ years. This is operating-leverage-positive after two years of negative volume.
- **Consolidation runway and adjacent-market expansion.** Public haulers deployed heavily in 2026 (RSG ~$1.12bn 9M, GFL ~$1.42bn annualised revenue acquired, CWST 6 deals/$170m+, WCN ~$130m annualised revenue), buying private haulers at 6–8x EBITDA and re-rating them inside permitted disposal networks. Adjacent growth — environmental/industrial services (RSG ES organic +21%), RNG, recycling automation/Polymer Centers, healthcare waste (WM/Stericycle) — adds 2–4pts of growth at higher margins.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Diesel and fleet capex inflation.** Higher diesel was explicitly cited by CWST, WCN and GFL this quarter; fuel-recovery fees lag spot moves, and tariff-driven truck/equipment cost inflation raises the capex line while FCF is the sector's valuation anchor.
- **Underlying volume softness in the commercial/industrial base.** Residential and industrial collection volumes remain negative (WCN solid waste unit volumes **-1.2%**, RSG related-business volumes -0.7%, RSG total volume -0.6pts of revenue) — growth is price- and M&A-led, so any pricing deceleration (deflation in CPI-linked contracts, competitive open-market bidding) would expose thin organic volume.
- **Regulatory, closure and legislative risk.** PFAS/leachate and landfill methane rules raise compliance capex and closure/post-closure liabilities; state-level EPR and recycling mandates shift cost recovery; rising rates raise the cost of the acquisition roll-up that is currently supplying a third of sector growth.

### 5. WCN's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

- **Best margin in the industry on a like-for-like EBITDA basis:** ~34.0% Q3'26 adjusted EBITDA margin vs WM 31.0%, RSG 31.8%, GFL 31.5% — and the only large cap with a structurally rising operating margin (17.2% FY22 → 18.1% FY25 → ~18.5% 9M'26, with Q3'26 at 20.5%).
- **Price-led model is working and is self-funded:** WCN deliberately shed 1.2% of solid waste volume to hold 5.5% core price, yet still grew revenue 7.3% and EBITDA 7.9%. Management's initial FY27 framework (mid-to-high-single-digit revenue, double-digit adj. FCF/share growth) implies the pricing/volume trade-off continues to be accretive.
- **Above-median growth with the best FCF conversion:** 11.7% five-year revenue CAGR vs ~10.5% peer median, ≥$1.4bn FY26 FCF (~14% of revenue) funding >$1bn of YTD buybacks and a 10% dividend raise — a materially better capital-return profile than CWST/GFL, which are still absorbing acquisition dilution (CWST operating margin has fallen 9.5% → 4.9% over five years).
- **Where it is weaker:** (i) thinner disposal vertical integration than WM — WCN holds ~13% of US landfill volumes vs WM's ~24–26%, so more third-party disposal cost leakage; (ii) greater E&P/exploration-and-production and special-waste cyclicality (an advantage in the current special-waste +7% upcycle, a risk in an energy downturn); (iii) cash/liquidity and landfill closure-liability concerns flagged by sell-side; (iv) FY25 revenue growth of 6.1% was the slowest in five years, showing the model is not immune to volume weakness.

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**

Non-discretionary demand, oligopolistic disposal capacity, 5–6% demonstrated pricing power, and improving landfill volumes make this one of the highest-quality defensive-growth sub-sectors in Industrials — it is currently delivering mid-single-digit organic growth with expanding margins while the broader industrial economy is flat. The main offsets are full valuations (this is a well-understood quality compounder, not a mispriced asset) and the fact that the current growth algorithm leans heavily on price and M&A rather than volume. Within the group, **WCN screens as the best-in-class operator on margin, FCF conversion and pricing discipline**; the key monitorables are solid-waste volume stabilisation, diesel/fleet cost pass-through, and M&A multiples paid in a peak deal environment.
