## Waste Connections, Inc. (WCN) — Value Investment Analysis

**Reference price: ~$185** (last profile print $181.50; recent analyst notes 23–27 Oct 2026 reference ~$186). Shares out ~252M; market cap ~$46.5B; net debt ~$8.9B; EV ~$55.5B.

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **WIDE**

WCN is the #3 North American solid-waste player (~$9.9B TTM revenue), vertically integrated across collection → transfer → landfill → recycling, deliberately concentrated in **secondary and exurban markets** where it is often the only permitted disposal option within economic haul distance.

| Moat source | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Intangible assets (permits)** | Landfill permitting is effectively unobtainable de novo — NIMBY plus multi-year regulatory process. WCN owns ~60+ MSW landfills. This is the single most durable asset in the industry. |
| **Cost advantage / route density** | Local collection density plus owned disposal ("internalization") lets WCN avoid third-party tipping fees. Result: EBITDA margin ~32–33% vs WM ~29% and RSG ~30% — the highest in the group. |
| **Switching costs** | Low per-customer, but contracts are multi-year with automatic CPI-linked escalators. Revenue is ~70% recurring/contracted. |
| **Efficient scale** | Each landfill is a local monopoly; a second entrant cannot justify the capital. |
| **Moat trend (5 yr)** | **Widening.** Gross margin 40.6% (2021) → 42.4% (2025) → 43.1% (Q3'26). EBITDA margin 30.7% → 31.4% → 33.6%. Pricing has outrun inflation every year since 2021. |

This is a textbook "toll-road on other people's garbage" business: non-discretionary demand, GDP+ volume, CPI+ price. I understand it fully — within my circle of competence.

**Caveat on the moat:** ROIC including goodwill is only **9.7%** (2025), 8.4% (2023), 6.6% (2024). That is only modestly above my ~7% WACC estimate. The *underlying* assets earn 25–35%, but WCN has paid away much of the excess return to sellers of acquired businesses. Wide moat, but an acquisition-financed one.

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **8 / 10**

| Metric | 2021 | 2023 | 2025 | TTM (Q4'25–Q3'26) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($B) | 6.15 | 8.02 | 9.47 | 9.94 |
| Gross margin | 40.6% | 40.9% | 42.4% | 43.0% |
| EBITDA margin | 30.7% | 29.2% | 31.4% | 32.2% |
| Reported net income ($M) | 618 | 763 | 1,077 | 1,133 |
| FCF ($M) | 954 | 1,193 | 1,235 | ~1,300 |
| FCF / revenue | 15.5% | 14.9% | 13.0% | ~13.1% |

- **Revenue growth:** 5-yr CAGR **11.4%**; organic ~4–5% (price 4–6%, volume 0–1%) plus 3–4% from tuck-in M&A. Highly predictable.
- **FCF conversion:** FCF/net income **115%** in 2025 — excellent (>80% threshold comfortably cleared), helped by D&A ($1.23B) approximating total capex ($1.18B).
- **Capex intensity rising:** capex/revenue 12.1% (2021) → 12.5% (2025), and capex dollars +58% since 2021 vs revenue +54%. Fleet CNG conversion, MRF automation and RNG projects are real but consumption of FCF.
- **Balance sheet:** Net debt **$9.35B**; net debt/EBITDA **~2.8x** — investment grade, but **tangible equity is negative (~–$2.2B)**. Goodwill + intangibles are $10.4B against $8.2B of book equity. Interest expense has nearly doubled ($182M → ~$386M annualized) and coverage is 8.3x — adequate, not fortress.
- **Deductions:** acquisition-dependent growth, thin goodwill-inclusive returns, negative tangible book. Hence 8, not 10.

Note: FY2024 GAAP was depressed by ~$520M of non-recurring charges (EBITDA reported $2.39B vs ~$2.89B adjusted); 2025 normalized. Use adjusted figures for like-for-like history.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples vs. history and peers**

| P/FCF | **37.9x** | 34.8x | 32.8x |

WCN carries a **24% P/E premium to the peer median (35.1x)** and is the most expensive of the three on P/FCF, though cheapest on EV/EBITDA (a margin artefact). Historically WCN has traded 28–33x forward adjusted EPS; at ~$185 on FY26E adjusted EPS of ~$5.70 it sits at **~32.5x — the top of its own range.**

**Intrinsic value (owner-earnings DCF, TTM FCF ~$1.30B base):**

| Scenario | Growth | WACC | Terminal | Equity value/sh |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 4%/3% | 7.5% | 3.0% | **~$115** |
| **Base (7%→5%)** | 7%, then 5% | 7.0% | 3.0% | **~$150–160** |
| Optimistic | 9%, then 6% | 6.5% | 3.0% | **~$188** |
| Mechanical tool output (3.6% grower) | 3.6% | 7.5% | 3.0% | ~$79 |

Cross-check: 30x adjusted EPS (mid-cycle multiple) → $171; 17x EV/EBITDA on $3.2B → $181, or 15.5x → $162. **All convergent paths land at $150–170, versus ~$185 today.** Sell-side median target $203 (range $192–215) implies only ~10% upside — the Street is already extrapolating the margin story.

**What the price implies:** to justify $185 you must assume ~8–9% FCF growth for a decade with terminal margins above today's record 33.6%. Possible — Q3'26 was genuinely strong (+7.3% revenue, +14.5% EBITDA, +29% EPS) — but you are paying for that outcome with zero room for a stumble.

**Margin of safety: negative (~–15%).** My accumulation zone is **$120–135** (20–25% discount to a $155–165 base).

### 4. Management Quality Assessment: **9 / 10**

- **Ronald Mittelstaedt** — founder-architect, CEO 1998–2019, Executive Chairman, returned as CEO in 2023 with a near-30-year ownership mentality. Significant personal shareholding; serial acquirer with a documented 6–8x EBITDA discipline and a stated preference for small, contiguous, vertically-integrated tuck-ins.
- **Capital allocation:** dividend raised every year — quarterly $0.205 (2021) → $0.385 (Q4 2026), ~17% CAGR, ~28% of FCF. Buybacks are opportunistic, not mechanical: $425M (2022), $0 (2023), $0 (2024), **$505M (2025)** — the 2025 repurchase at an average of ~$180 was, in my view, poorly timed by value standards, though the 2022 buybacks were excellent.
- **No value-destroying mega-M&A.** WCN has avoided the transformative deal that has hurt peers. Share count is flat at ~258M — dilution is negligible despite constant issuance for deals.
- Sharper criticism: goodwill has grown $8.58B → $10.38B in three years, so a meaningful share of growth is bought, not built, and reported organic price is not independently verifiable.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

WCN is one of the finest businesses in North American industrials: a wide, widening moat; record margins; predictable, contracted, non-discretionary revenue; an owner-operator who understands capital allocation; and 13% FCF margins. I want to own it — **at the right price.**

At ~$185 it trades at **the top of its own 20-year multiple range**, a 24% P/E premium to peers, and a 15% premium to my base-case intrinsic value. Expected 5–10 year return is roughly FCF growth (~7–8%) plus dividend (~0.8%) minus modest multiple compression (~2%/yr) ≈ **6–7% annualized**. That is a fine business return, not a compelling investment return, and it sits below my opportunity cost.

- **Buy** below ~$150; **aggressively accumulate** at $120–135 (30%+ discount).
- **Trim/reduce** if the multiple exceeds ~38x adjusted EPS (~$215) without matching earnings revision.
- Do **not** short a compounder of this quality at 32x — the multiple is defensible; it is simply not a bargain.

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Multiple compression (largest risk).** De-rating from 32x to 25x adjusted EPS removes ~22% of the price even if earnings compound perfectly — the 2024–26 flat share price already previews this.
2. **Acquisition treadmill.** Growth requires deploying $700M–1B/yr of capital; competitive bidding from WM, GFL and private equity could push entry multiples to value-neutral territory. Watch incremental ROIC.
3. **Capex intensification.** Recycling, RNG and fleet electrification are raising capital intensity while FCF/revenue has already drifted from 15.5% to 13.0%.
4. **Leverage and rates.** $9.4B debt with ~$386M annual interest; refinancing at higher coupons and a 2.8x ratio limit flexibility if a large opportunity appears in a downturn.
5. **Volume cyclicality.** Commercial/industrial and E&P waste exposure ties volumes to construction and energy activity in WCN's western/tribal-adjacent markets.
6. **Goodwill/impairment risk.** $10.4B of intangibles on a $8.2B equity base; a string of underperforming deals would trigger writedowns and reveal a lower-quality growth record.
7. **Regulatory/environmental.** Landfill closures, leachate rules and local opposition can strand what look like perpetual assets; recycling and RNG credit values are commodity-linked.
8. **Price/volume mix.** 2022–26 earnings were flattered by exceptional 6–8% headline pricing; a normalization to 3–4% against 3–4% cost inflation would compress the margin story that justifies today's multiple.

---

**Summary:** Excellent company, wide moat, first-rate owner-operator — but the market has already paid for the excellence. **Hold; be a buyer only on a 20%+ drawdown.**
