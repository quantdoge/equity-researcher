# Macroeconomic Assessment — Waste Connections, Inc. (WCN)

**As of 17 November 2026** | Price $181.50 · Market cap $45.4B · Beta 0.48 · Industrials / Waste Management (US + Canada)

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary: **Transitional — "Reflation with a Term-Premium Shock"**

The US economy is **not** late-cycle-decelerating; it is **re-accelerating into an inflation problem**. Real GDP grew at a ~3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026 (from 2.2% in Q2), unemployment has *fallen* to 4.1% from 4.5% a year ago, and headline CPI has climbed from 3.30% (July) to ~3.8% (October) — with core CPI up 0.3% m/m for a third consecutive month. The Fed hiked 25bp in September to 3.75–4.00%, **held on 28 October**, and the market now prices a December hike plus two more in 2027.

The critical divergence: **credit markets are risk-on while rates markets are risk-off.** HY OAS sits at just 3.34% — near cycle tights, pricing no recession — while the 10Y has backed up 69bp since 30 June (4.44% → 5.13%) and the 30Y is at 5.47%. This is a **bear steepening**, not an inversion: 2s10s is +39bp, so the recession signal is off and the move is term premium / fiscal supply / inflation-risk premium. Notably, **5Y breakevens have fallen from 2.49% (28 Oct) to 2.26%** — the bond market believes the Fed will win, and that the inflation is partly the war-driven oil shock (WTI $69.60 on 6 July → $109.72 on 23 Oct → $89.62 on 11 Nov as Iran de-escalation signals emerge). Translation: **real yields are doing the tightening** — 5Y real is ~2.67%.

**Stance for WCN: risk-on at the operating line, hostile at the discount-rate line.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**POSITIVE — pricing power is the headline.** WCN is one of the few businesses where 3.8% CPI is a *revenue driver*. Residential and municipal contracts carry CPI-linked escalators, and fuel/environmental surcharges are contractual. Q3'26 (reported 22 Oct) beat and management raised FY26 guidance on "solid pricing," an "inflection in waste volumes," improving landfill activity, acquisition contributions and commodity-related revenue growth — the stock jumped 9.6–11% on the print. With CPI accelerating, the escalator floor rises into 2027 renewals. **Strongly favourable.**

**POSITIVE — volume backdrop.** GDP above trend into Q3, unemployment at 4.1%, and a normalised curve mean commercial collection and landfill tonnage see a stable-to-improving volume tape. This is the swing factor on incremental margin and it has turned.

**POSITIVE — E&P waste.** WTI at $89.62, still +29% from the July low, supports drilling activity and therefore WCN's E&P waste franchise (drilling fluids, drill cuttings, produced water). The tailwind is real but *decaying* — down 18% from the October peak as Iranian supply recovers.

**POSITIVE — M&A financing.** WCN is a serial consolidator. HY OAS at 3.34% keeps acquisition debt cheap and the private-landfill bid-ask workable — though see the refinancing caveat below.

**NEGATIVE — duration and the discount rate.** At ~30x forward earnings and a high-teens EV/EBITDA, WCN is a long-duration asset in a 5.13%/5.47% world. A 69bp rise in the 10Y on a 30x multiple implies roughly **15–20% of multiple compression** all else equal. The stock's June 2026 trough ($148.23 on 1 June) coincided with the collapse of Fed-cut hopes; the subsequent 33% rally to $197.30 (16 Nov) was **earnings delivery beating duration**, not macro help. That leaves less cushion.

**NEGATIVE — leverage and refinancing.** WCN carries meaningful net debt (~high-2s/low-3s x EBITDA; *exact maturity schedule not verified in this window — see gaps*). Incremental debt now prices at 5.5–6.5% rather than 4%. Acquisition multiples underwritten at 2024 financing costs look materially less accretive.

**NEGATIVE — diesel.** Fuel is a top-three operating cost in collection. Crude +29% from July lifts diesel with a 1–2 quarter lag; surcharges recover most of it but not all, and not immediately.

**NEGATIVE — strong USD.** DTWEXBGS +4.6% since 8 September and USD/CAD at 1.4256 translates the Canadian segment (roughly 10% of revenue) lower. Largely self-hedged since the cost base is also CAD, so the hit to operating profit is modest — but it is a visible revenue-line headwind.

**NEGATIVE — tariffs and capex.** Tariffed trucks, containers and heavy equipment raise per-unit fleet replacement capex and landfill development cost, in a business where capex is ~12–14% of revenue.

**NEUTRAL — wages.** 4.1% unemployment means CDL driver and hourly labour inflation persists, but it is the mirror image of the pricing power above.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Split the rating explicitly: **operating fundamentals = FAVOURABLE** (pricing power into 3.8% CPI, positive volume inflection, cheap acquisition credit, E&P tailwind). **Valuation/discount-rate channel = UNFAVOURABLE** (10Y at 5.13%, 30Y at 5.47%, Fed with a hiking bias, ~30x multiple, ~3x leverage).

The tie-breaker is **positioning**: WCN just re-rated 33% off its June low to an all-time high of $197.30 on 16 November, meaning the good operating macro is now largely *in* the price while the bad rate macro is not. The 8.5% drop on 17 November ($197.30 → $181.50 on 5.6M shares) was **idiosyncratic, not macro** — a methane gas eruption at a Los Angeles-area landfill plus a 10% dividend increase. That event risk is additive to, not a substitute for, the macro exposure.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario (Bear)

**Consensus is focused on whether the Fed hikes in December. The materially surprising outcome is that the long end keeps selling off even after the Fed stops.**

If the 10Y reaches 5.75–6.00% by mid-2027 on term premium and Treasury supply rather than on inflation — with breakevens *falling* (already 2.49% → 2.26%) — the curve steepens further while real yields climb toward 3.25%. That is precisely the configuration that punishes a levered, long-duration, bond-proxy-like equity with perpetual landfill cash flows: WCN's multiple compresses toward 24–25x on *unchanged* mid-single-digit EBITDA growth, implying **$140–150 (−20%)**, and the M&A roll-up that underpins the growth algorithm stops being accretive. A re-escalation in the Iran conflict pushing WTI back above $110 would compound it via diesel costs and consumer-volume strain.

*Bull mirror:* if the oil shock is confirmed transitory, the Fed stops after one more hike, and the 10Y retraces to 4.50%, WCN's 4–6% escalators plus cooling input costs deliver margin expansion *and* a re-rating to 32–34x — **$220+**.
