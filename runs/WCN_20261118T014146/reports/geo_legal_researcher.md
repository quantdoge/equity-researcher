## WCN — Waste Connections, Inc.: Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment
*Prepared November 2026 | Basis: FY2025 Form 10-K (filed 12-Feb-2026), Q3-2026 results (21-Oct-2026), regulatory/legal databases and trade-policy sources*

**Corporate structure note:** WCN is incorporated in **Ontario, Canada** (Woodbridge, ON registered office) with principal administrative offices in **The Woodlands, Texas**; dual-listed NYSE/TSX. As a Canadian-domiciled issuer it is subject to **both** SEC reporting and Canadian securities regulators (OSC/CSA) — a dual-compliance overlay that is unusual for a US-industrials comp.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US–Canada trade/tariff escalation (and the 2026 USMCA review) — Probability: High | Impact: Low–Moderate**
US tariffs on Canadian goods and Canadian retaliation reduced bilateral goods trade in 2025 (Global Affairs Canada, *State of Trade 2026*); Global Trade Alert tracks new Section 338 measures on Canada. For WCN the transmission channels are (a) price/capex inflation on Canada-sourced equipment and cross-border inputs, (b) friction on cross-border waste and intermodal container flows in the Pacific Northwest, and (c) FX translation of the Canada segment (FY2025 internal growth included a −0.3% FX drag). Impact is dampened by the essential, locally-delivered nature of waste service and by the low (~2%) intermodal share — but the Canada segment and any cross-border disposal economics are the swing factor.

**② PFAS / emerging-contaminant regulation — Probability: High | Impact: Moderate**
EPA's PFAS actions continued through 2025–26, including an interim final rule (May 2025) **delaying the TSCA PFAS reporting deadline to 13 October 2026** (per WCN's FY2025 10-K). CERCLA/Superfund designation of PFOA/PFOS plus state leachate rules (North Carolina is tightening oversight; WCN's Anson landfill is building leachate treatment capacity for up to 50,000 gal/day) create a multi-year cost and liability vector — higher leachate treatment capex, potential PRP liability at third-party sites, and disclosure/reporting burden. Waste Dive (June 2026) reports industry-wide cost escalation for PFAS leachate treatment. WCN has already booked a **$39.1M Q4-2025 charge "primarily related to an environmental liability at an operating facility"** — evidence this risk is already crystallising in the P&L, not hypothetical.

**③ Landfill permitting, local opposition and state/municipal policy risk — Probability: Medium-High | Impact: Moderate–High**
Disposal capacity is WCN's core moat and its core regulatory dependency: permits, host-community agreements and closure/post-closure obligations are granted and revoked at state/local level. The clearest precedent is the **~$602.4M impairment in Q4-2024 "primarily related to the early closure of a landfill"** plus $480.8M of closure/post-closure adjustments in FY2024, and a FY2025 write-down of a **non-operating E&P waste facility permit**. Any single adverse permit outcome is not existential, but the recurring pattern of permit/closure-driven write-downs makes this the highest-severity idiosyncratic risk in the book.

*Secondary monitorables:* Canadian federal/provincial energy policy (Western Canada E&P volumes), US recycling-commodity policy (China/SE Asia import standards for recyclables), and DOT/FMCSA motor-carrier rules affecting the ~24k-employee trucking fleet.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Active class action (settled): *Pinnacle Waste Services LLC v. Waste Connections US Inc et al.*, D.S.C. 7:21-cv-02600.** Alleged WCN entities imposed **rate increases and fuel surcharges without contractual basis** on commercial customers. **$1.4M settlement**; claim deadline 22-Oct-2026. Financially immaterial, but it is a template claim — pricing/fuel-surcharge litigation is a structural feature of the sector and WCN's 6.5% core price in FY2025 keeps it live.
- **Environmental liabilities and impairments:** FY2025 $109.7M of impairments/other items (environmental liability at an operating facility; non-operating E&P permit write-down); FY2024 $613.0M (landfill early closure, closure/post-closure). These are regulatory/permitting events, not litigation, but they are the largest legal-adjacent charges on the books.
- **Regulatory penalty history (Violation Tracker, Good Jobs First):** $2.97M in penalties since 2000 across 47 records for currently-owned entities — employment-related $2.02M (incl. a $1.10M wage-and-hour matter), environment-related $687.7K (incl. a **2025 EPA penalty of $32.6K** at L.P. Gill), safety-related $265.0K. Remediational scale is small relative to $10B revenue, but the mix (recurring wage-hour and OSHA items) suggests ordinary-course compliance friction rather than systemic failure.
- **M&A antitrust:** Strategy is explicitly tuck-in acquisition-led — **19 acquisitions for $966.8M net fair value in FY2025**, ~$130M annualised revenue closed YTD-2026. Each deal carries HSR/Competition Act notification and state-AG review; cumulative local-market concentration in secondary/rural markets invites scrutiny, though no active second-request or consent decree was identified.
- **Ongoing regulatory regimes:** RCRA Subtitle D and C&D landfill rules; Clean Air Act landfill gas/NSPS; Clean Water Act stormwater and leachate discharge; state solid-waste and recycling mandates (e.g., CA organics diversion); Canada: provincial environmental approvals plus federal Impact Assessment/CEPA oversight; OSHA and FMCSA for the fleet; dual SEC/CSA disclosure and continuous-disclosure obligations (including Canadian forward-looking-information rules).
- **No identified** DOJ/SEC enforcement action, securities class action, restatement, FCPA/AML matter, or sanctions exposure — consistent with a purely North American, non-hazardous, domestic-customer business.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **LOW**

**Rationale:** WCN is a near-pure North American, non-hazardous waste services business — no sanctions, export-control, FCPA, EM sovereign, nationalisation or currency-repatriation exposure worth pricing. Both operating jurisdictions (US, Canada) are high rule-of-law, low political-risk markets, and revenue is essential-service, contracted and price-escalated, which makes it unusually resilient to tariff and macro shocks. Known legal matters are individually immaterial (largest identified: $1.4M class settlement) and the disclosed regulatory penalties are de minimis versus a ~$10B revenue base.

**Where it could migrate to MEDIUM:** (i) a Superfund/CERCLA PFAS cost-allocation or state leachate-treatment mandate that converts today's $39M-type one-off charges into a recurring, multi-site liability; (ii) an adverse landfill-permit outcome or host-community action at a large-site disposal asset (precedent: the ~$602M early-closure impairment); or (iii) a genuine US–Canada tariff/border event that impairs cross-border waste-by-rail and Canadian-segment margins. None of these is currently base case, but all three are one headline away from being live.
