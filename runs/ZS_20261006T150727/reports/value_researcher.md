# Zscaler, Inc. (ZS) — Value Investment Analysis

**Note on data:** The dataset reflects Zscaler's fiscal years (ended July 31) through FY2026 (filed Sept 2026), with a current price snapshot of ~$211 (market cap ~$34B). Peer-multiple data was unavailable (the sector-peer tool returned only mega-cap semiconductor names, and web searches timed out); valuation comparisons rely on my knowledge of security-software comps (CRWD, PANW, NET, S).

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

**Moat rating: Narrow (leaning Wide, but contested)**

Zscaler is the category-defining leader in cloud-native security (SSE/SASE): ZIA for secure internet access and ZPA for zero-trust private access, delivered from a global network of enforcement nodes. Evidence for a real moat:

- **Switching costs (strong):** The platform sits inline on all user traffic — the security backbone of the enterprise. Rip-and-replace is disruptive and risky for security teams; retention is high and net revenue retention has historically been ~115%+.
- **Intangible assets (moderate):** Gartner-leader positioning, a trusted security brand, and a 15+ year head start in cloud-delivered security.
- **Cost/architecture advantage (moderate):** Cloud-native multi-tenant architecture gives structural cost and scale advantages over legacy appliance vendors (Zscaler's gross margin is ~77% vs. hardware-heavy peers).
- **Network effects (weak):** Minimal direct network effects; this is not a two-sided marketplace.

**Moat trajectory:** The moat has *widened* over the past five years operationally (revenue CAGR ~41%, FCF margin from ~21% to ~25%) but is now being **contested from two directions**: Palo Alto Networks (Prisma SASE) and Netskope in SSE, and Microsoft (Entra/Defender) bundling security into the Windows/365 install base. Pricing power is being tested — revenue growth has decelerated from 62% (FY22) to 23–25% (FY25–26). This is a genuine, durable franchise, but not an impregnable one.

**ROIC:** GAAP ROIC is negative (-3.0% FY26) because the company is GAAP-unprofitable; on an operating basis the economics are excellent but heavily distorted by stock compensation (see below).

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **6 / 10**

| Metric | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue ($M) | 1,091 | 1,617 | 2,168 | 2,673 | 3,353 |
| YoY growth | +62% | +48% | +34% | +23% | +25% |
| Gross margin | 77.7% | 77.6% | 77.9% | 76.9% | 76.8% |
| Operating margin | -30.0% | -13.3% | -5.7% | -4.8% | -4.0% |
| GAAP net income ($M) | -390 | -202 | -58 | -41 | -63 |
| Free cash flow ($M) | 231 | 334 | 585 | 727 | 852 |
| FCF margin | 21% | 21% | 27% | 27% | 25% |

**Strengths:** Elite gross margins (~77%, stable for six years); strong and improving cash generation (OCF $1.13B, FCF $852M in FY26); fortress balance sheet — $3.5B cash/investments vs. $1.9B debt (mostly convertibles), i.e., **net cash ~$1.6B**; consistent quarterly beats (EPS actual > estimate in every reported quarter).

**Critical weaknesses — the SBC problem:** Stock-based compensation was **$822M in FY26 — roughly 96% of free cash flow**. GAAP net income is negative in every year shown; diluted shares grew from 135.7M (FY21) to 160.2M (FY26), ~3.4% annual dilution. Buffett owner earnings (NI + D&A − maintenance capex) are **negative (-$148M FY26)**. True distributable owner earnings (FCF less SBC) are essentially **zero**. The market's "FCF" here is largely a non-GAAP construct. This is the single most important caveat to the bull case.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current market data:** Price ~$211; market cap ~$33.9–34.2B; EV ~$32.3B; P/FCF 39.8x; EV/EBITDA 173x (GAAP) or ~32x adjusted (excluding SBC); FCF yield 2.5%; P/B 13x; GAAP P/E not meaningful (negative earnings).

**Intrinsic value estimate (DCF, conservative):** Using FY26 FCF of $852M as base and a 9% WACC:

- *Base case* (FCF grows ~15%/yr for 8 years, then 3% terminal): **~$180–185/share**
- *Bull case* (20% growth for 5 years, 12% for 5, 3% terminal): **~$260/share**
- *Mechanical tool DCF* (30% growth for 10 years): $691/share — **rejected**; it implies FY36 FCF of $11.8B, ~3.5x current revenue, which is not a defensible assumption.

**Fair value range: ~$180–260, midpoint ~$220.** At $211, the stock trades **approximately at fair value on optimistic assumptions and above fair value on moderate assumptions**. There is **no margin of safety** — the 20–30% discount required by this framework is absent. What growth does the price embed? Roughly 15–20% FCF growth for a decade plus margin expansion — i.e., continued category leadership with no competitive disruption. That is a demanding, not conservative, assumption for a company whose revenue growth is already at 25% and decelerating.

**Peer context:** Zscaler's ~40x FCF multiple is at the premium end of security software (CRWD ~40x, PANW ~30x, NET ~35x on similar metrics) — justified by growth, but the group as a whole trades on non-GAAP earnings that ignore SBC.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Rating: Good (with one structural concern)**

- **Founder-led:** Jay Chaudhry (CEO, founded 2007) holds a meaningful insider stake (several percent, worth $1B+), aligning owner interests. Long, consistent execution record.
- **Capital allocation:** Disciplined — no empire-building M&A (small tuck-ins only), no dividends, no buybacks; funds growth internally and via convertibles. Net cash balance sheet.
- **Concerns:** (1) **SBC policy** — ~25% of revenue expensed as stock comp is aggressive; it masks true profitability and dilutes owners ~3–4%/yr. (2) No buybacks despite a $34B market cap and $850M+ FCF — acceptable at a rich valuation, but combined with SBC it means shareholders' cash returns are nil. (3) Guidance discipline has been good (consistent beats), which is a positive.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **Hold**

This is a high-quality business — 77% gross margins, 25% FCF margins, net cash, category leadership, founder-led — but it fails the value test on two counts:

1. **No margin of safety:** At ~$211 the stock is priced at or slightly above a conservative intrinsic value (~$180–260). A fair price is not a buy.
2. **Earnings quality:** GAAP profitability is absent, and SBC consumes essentially all reported free cash flow. The "owner earnings" the market celebrates are largely non-GAAP.

For a patient investor, ZS is a **watchlist holding**: a wonderful business to own at the right price. A pullback toward $150–160 (≈25–30x FCF, or a ~25% discount to base-case value) would convert this to a Buy. At $211, the risk/reward is balanced-to-unfavorable.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Competitive disruption:** Microsoft bundling security into Windows/365, Palo Alto's integrated platform push, and Netskope's SSE parity could compress Zscaler's premium pricing and slow growth below the ~15–20% the price assumes.
2. **SBC/dilution:** Continued ~25%-of-revenue stock comp compounds dilution (~3–4%/yr) and keeps GAAP earnings negative; a re-rating toward GAAP-based multiples would be severe.
3. **Growth deceleration:** Growth has already halved (62% → 25%). If SASE adoption matures faster than expected, the multiple (40x FCF) compresses violently.
4. **Macro/IT budget risk:** Security is mission-critical, but long deal cycles and budget scrutiny could pressure new-ACV growth and net revenue retention.
5. **Convertible debt overhang:** $1.7B of converts can dilute further if the stock appreciates.
6. **Valuation multiple risk:** At 40x FCF with ~zero true owner earnings, any disappointment on billings or guidance (as seen in the Nov 2025 drawdown) can produce 30–40% drawdowns regardless of fundamentals.
