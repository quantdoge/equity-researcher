## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1) US federal procurement contraction (DOGE-era budget cuts / shutdown volatility)** — Probability: **40–50%** | Impact: **Moderate**
Government is a flagship, high-visibility vertical (FedRAMP High authorization is a differentiator vs. most peers), but federal buying is discretionary and politically exposed. Contract cancellations, recompete delays, or extended continuing resolutions would hit new-business growth exactly where the AI/zero-trust narrative is being sold to investors. Peer sector (Palo Alto Networks) faces identical dynamics, so this is systemic rather than idiosyncratic.

**2) US-China decoupling, export controls & data localization (APJ/China)** — Probability: **~30%** | Impact: **Low–Moderate**
Zscaler is a US-origin cybersecurity exporter; BIS export-control activity around cyber items (2024-era Wassenaar-adjacent rules) and China's PIPL/CSL data-localization create compliance friction and de facto shrink the China market to zero. APJ exposure (~14%) is nonetheless dominated by benign markets (Japan, Australia, India), so direct revenue damage is limited.

**3) Russia sanctions residue** — Probability of material impact: **Low** | Impact: **Low**
Operations in Russia were suspended after the 2022 invasion, but Zscaler still maintains a published process to service Russia-based users via an "end-user statement," implying residual activity in a sanctioned-adjacent market. Revenue is immaterial, but this is a potential OFAC/reputational scrutiny item and should be monitored.

*Systemic footnote:* as an intermediary positioned inside every client's traffic path, Zscaler carries concentration risk if state-sponsored actors (e.g., PRC/state-linked APT campaigns such as Salt Typhoon-style telecom intrusions) exploit or are claimed to exploit its platform — a tail-risk headline that could trigger sovereign-customer churn.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **ACTIVE — Securities class action wave (primary legal risk).** On **May 26, 2026** Zscaler reported FY2026 Q3 results that beat on earnings but **lowered free-cash-flow margin expectations**; the stock fell ~23–31% intraday on May 27 — a record one-day drop (close $184.60 → $126.41 on 31.8M volume; ~$9B+ market cap loss). At least **six** plaintiff firms (Schall Brown & Schwartz, Pomerantz, Johnson Fistel, Bronstein Gewirtz & Grossman, Gross Law Firm, Ademi LLP, Shareholders Foundation) have announced securities-fraud investigations alleging false/misleading statements about financials and demand. Expect a consolidated complaint and motion-to-dismiss battle; typical outcome risk: settlement several-hundred-million $ or dismissal. The earlier ~$289→$143 slide in Feb 2026 (Q2 report) may extend the alleged class period.
- **Watch item — accounting scrutiny.** The billings-vs-revenue and FCF-margin guidance cuts raise the possibility of SEC or PCAOB-adjacent review of revenue recognition practices; no formal SEC action found.
- **FedRAMP (Moderate & High)** compliance continues; expanded FedRAMP data planes (Zurich, Singapore) support allied-government sales — a competitive moat but a compliance cost.
- **Privacy/GDPR:** as a processor of customer web traffic, Zscaler carries GDPR/data-processing and FTC (Safeguards Rule) compliance load; no enforcement action found.
- **Tailwinds:** SEC cyber-disclosure rules, CMMC 2.0, EU NIS2/DORA all structurally favor Zscaler's product. *No FCPA/AML/DOJ enforcement, no IP-ownership litigation of note* (10-Q discloses standard open-source ownership litigation risk).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Russia servicing path** — published "End-User Statement" process for Russia-based use; low revenue but a sanctions-compliance surface requiring OFAC re-export verification.
2. **Foreign state data-access demands** — as an inline traffic intermediary, Zscaler may face lawful-access demands in several operating jurisdictions; warrant-canary/due-process posture should be diligence-checked.
3. **Channel/dealer dependency in Gulf & SEA** — distributor-mediated sales in higher-corruption-risk geographies raise standard FCPA/AML screening requirements; no violations disclosed.
4. **No material flags on AML, export-controls violations, or bribery** found in public record.
