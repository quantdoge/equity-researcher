## SECTOR BRIEF — GENERAL MOTORS (GM)

**Sector:** Consumer Cyclical › Auto Manufacturers (global light-vehicle OEMs). Peer set below is the auto-specific subset, not the broader consumer-cyclical GICS bucket.

---

### 1. Sector Overview

Global light-vehicle manufacturing is a **mature, capital-intensive, low-concentration** industry (top-10 OEMs ≈ 60–65% of global volume; effective HHI low, so rivalry is structurally fierce). Aggregate sector revenue is roughly flat: legacy OEMs are running between **–2% and +5% growth**, and the industry has re-based from a 2022–23 price/mix boom (post-COVID scarcity pricing) into a **2025–26 margin trough** driven by U.S. tariffs, the expiry of the $7,500 federal EV credit on 30 Sep 2025, and normalising incentives. Sector profitability is now sharply bimodal: Toyota posts **16.7% gross / 7.4% operating** margins while Stellantis is loss-making (**–2.7% gross / –14.5% operating**). GM sits in the middle band, and its trajectory has been volatile — FY2025 GAAP operating margin collapsed to **1.6%** before a strong Q3 2026 recovery (18.5% gross margin, $4.1bn adjusted EBIT, +22% YoY). The cycle reads **late-mature with an affordability-driven down-leg**, and the sector is in the early innings of an EV-retrenchment / hybrid-and-ICE-cash-cow phase.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Structural tariff protection for North American footprints.** S&P Global Mobility expects the 25% U.S. tariff on vehicles/parts to hold through 2026 before stepping to 15%. GM's heavy U.S./Mexico/Canada assembly base and ~full-size truck/SUV franchise give it pricing power and a relative cost advantage versus import-heavy peers (Hyundai, some Stellantis/Mazda flows). Analysts estimate tariffs add **$4,000–6,000 per imported vehicle**, which subsidises domestic producers' mix.
- **Truck/SUV/full-size SUV mix plus cost discipline is delivering record NA profitability.** Q3 2026 revenue hit a record **$50.0bn (+3% YoY)** with adjusted EBIT of **$4.1bn (+22%)** and $12.3bn YTD; FY2026 guidance of **$14.5–15.5bn adjusted EBIT** was reaffirmed/narrowed. Gross margin rebounded to **18.5%** from 11.5% in Q1 and 7.6% in Q2 2026 (both charge-laden).
- **Software/services annuity and aggressive capital return.** Super Cruise, OnStar and connected services are scaling as a higher-margin, less cyclical revenue layer on top of the installed fleet. On the same print GM authorised a further **$20bn buyback**; diluted shares fell from 1.129bn (FY2024) to **887m (Q3 2026), ≈21% in under two years** — EPS is being supported by shrink even where net income isn't growing.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Tariff cost drag and policy volatility.** Dated 25% duties on parts and vehicles (with shifting USMCA treatment) are a direct COGS headwind that cannot be fully price-recovered in an affordability-constrained market; management flagged "intensifying" challenges and narrowed 2026 EBIT guidance, which drove a **–6.8% single-day share reaction** on 20 Oct 2026.
- **Post-incentive EV demand air-pocket and EV write-downs.** The Sept-2025 credit sunset gutted EV economics: GM's **Q4 2025 gross profit was –$1.1bn with a –$3.6bn operating loss and –$3.3bn net loss** on $45.3bn of revenue — the visible cost of EV impairments/charges — and GM is now closing a Detroit battery assembly plant. Sector-wide, EVs remain loss-making for most legacy OEMs and hybrid mix is winning the transition.
- **Affordability, incentive normalisation and China.** Average transaction prices near record highs against weak consumer confidence means pricing power is eroding and incentives are returning; volume growth is therefore hard. Separately, Chinese competition (BYD et al.) has destroyed the profitability of legacy JVs — GM's China International operations have required restructuring and recurring charges.

### 5. GM's Competitive Position: **AVERAGE (improving) — best-in-class in North America, structurally impaired elsewhere**

**Rationale for "average":**
- *Strengths:* #1 or #2 U.S. share by volume; the most profitable full-size truck/SUV franchise in the industry; highest per-unit NA margins among Detroit peers (F's operating margin is 1.4% vs GM's 6.8% in FY24); a fortress of domestic manufacturing that tariffs make relatively more valuable; and $20bn+ of annual buyback capacity funded by ~$18bn EBITDA.
- *Weaknesses:* GM is a **price-taker on cost** — gross margin has fallen from 20.3% (2021) to 10.9% (FY25), a ~940bp compression, which is the sector's worst multi-year margin erosion among majors ex-Stellantis. Revenue growth (5.1% CAGR) trails Toyota (12.7%) and Honda (8.8%). International operations (China especially) are a recurring source of charges rather than profit. And GM's earnings quality is poor — the FY25→FY26 swing from 1.6% to 7.2% operating margin in three quarters reflects charges, not a durable re-rating.
- *Net:* GM is the **highest-quality balance of the three U.S./European legacy OEMs**, but it is not a structural winner like Toyota, and it does not have Tesla's growth optionality. "Average" within a weak cohort.

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Legacy auto is a **mature, capital-hungry, tariff-and-policy-dependent sector with sub-GDP growth**, structurally low barriers to price competition, high buyer power and rising supplier power in batteries/semis — a poor long-run compounder. However, the sector is **not unattractive at current prices**: it trades at trough margins, offers mid-single-digit cash-flow multiples, tariff protection favours domestic capacity, and 2026 evidence (GM Q3, Toyota's resilience) shows the cost reset is working. The rating would move to **Attractive** on a durable tariff settlement that removes cost uncertainty plus evidence that post-credit EV losses have stopped bleeding; it would move to **Unattractive** on a tariff escalation to 25%+ on parts with no offset, or a U.S. SAAR break below ~15m units.

**Data caveats:** FX-non-normalised Toyota/Honda revenue; no consensus forward multiples available from the toolset; sector-aggregate endpoint unusable.
