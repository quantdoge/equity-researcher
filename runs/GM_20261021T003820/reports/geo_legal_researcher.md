## GM — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment
*As of Q3 2026 results (reported Oct 20, 2026). Note on sourcing: GM's 10-K was not retrievable via the filing tool (only the Q3 2026 10-Q/E exhibit 31.1 certification was returned), so revenue-by-geography percentages below are estimates derived from GM's segment structure and public reporting, labelled accordingly.*

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

| Jurisdiction | Revenue/volume exposure | Nature of exposure | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States** | ~85% of revenue (GMNA segment); ~69% of global vehicle volume | Core profit engine; tariff *creator* and beneficiary of onshoring; NHTSA/EPA/state AG jurisdiction | Low–Medium |
| **China** | ~12.5% of volume; equity-method JVs (SAIC-GM, SGMW) — *not* consolidated revenue; equity income only | JV structure with SAIC; structural share loss to BYD/local OEMs; repeated restructuring charges ($5B Dec-2024 incl. $2.6–2.9B equity impairment; $1.1B Jan-2026) | **High** (declining) |
| **Mexico / Canada** | Not broken out; >750,000 vehicles/yr imported into US | USMCA content rules, Section 232 25% auto tariffs; $4B US plant investment to relocate output | Medium–High |
| **South Korea** | Not broken out; GM Korea is a major low-cost export hub to US (Trailblazer et al.) | US–Korea 15% tariff framework; FX; strike risk; low margins | Medium |
| **Brazil / Argentina / South America** | Within "GM International" (~10% of revenue combined with rest) | Argentina currency/repatriation risk, Brazil rate cycle | Medium |
| **Middle East / Africa** | Small, primarily export sales | Gulf demand, shipping/Red Sea routing | Low–Medium |
| **Europe / Russia** | **~Nil** — Opel/Vauxhall sold to PSA (2017); Russia exited 2022 | Only Cadillac/boutique imports + EU CO2 regime on small volumes | Low |

Concentration takeaway: GM is effectively a **North American company with a shrinking China option value**. Roughly 85–90% of consolidated revenue and the overwhelming majority of EBIT sit in the US/Canada/Mexico bloc — geopolitically the *safest* set of jurisdictions but the *most exposed* to policy volatility originating in Washington.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US trade policy whipsaw (tariffs and counter-tariffs). Probability: High (ongoing). Impact: Medium–High.**
- 2026 gross tariff cost guided at **$2.5–3.5bn**, already trimmed from $3–4bn after the **Supreme Court's Feb 20, 2026 ruling that IEEPA tariffs were illegal**; GM expects a **~$500M refund** (CBP refund system launched Apr 2026; 330,000 importers paid ~$166bn). Section 232 auto/parts (25%) and steel/aluminium/copper (50%) duties **remain in force** — the structural cost did not go away.
- Residual risk is *legal-form* risk: tariff authority keeps migrating between statutes (IEEPA → Section 232 → Section 122/301), so GM's cost base can re-price on judicial or executive action with no operational change. Mexico has separately proposed **50% tariffs on non-FTA light vehicles**, adding a second front.

**Risk 2 — China JV erosion and geopolitical decoupling. Probability: High. Impact: Medium (lower than 2024).**
- Repeated write-downs and restructuring ($5B in Dec-2024, a further **$1.1B charge in Jan-2026**) confirm value destruction, but GM has posted several consecutive quarters of profitability in China and Q3-2025 deliveries rose ~10% — the position is stabilising at a much lower level.
- Two-sided tail risk: (a) further JV impairments if the price war resumes; (b) **export/import controls** — GM imports the Buick Envision from China into the US and exports Cadillacs into China, both flows exposed to a renewed US–China truce breakdown.

**Risk 3 — Policy reversal on electrification/emissions (a two-way risk). Probability: High. Impact: Medium.**
- The **$7,500 EV credit expired Sept 2025**, driving roughly **$6bn of charges** (Jan-2026) as GM cut EV/clean-vehicle investment — a real cash and strategy reset.
- The offsetting tailwind: NHTSA/EPA **rolled back the CAFE standard from 50.4 mpg (2031) to 34.9 mpg** and **waived fuel-economy fines retroactively to 2022**. That removes a legacy civil-penalty liability, but it also re-exposes GM to a *future* administration reversing course again — a classic regulatory ping-pong that penalises capital allocation rather than this year's P&L.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Data privacy — largest CCPA penalty on record:** California AG settlement (May 2026) requiring **$12.75M in civil penalties** plus injunctive terms; GM must stop selling OnStar driving data to consumer reporting agencies (data had flowed to **LexisNexis and Verisk**). Texas AG filed a parallel suit (Aug-2024), and consumer class actions (Scott+Scott et al.) remain live. This is GM's clearest, most current compliance failure.
- **NHTSA safety:** Preliminary probe into **~600K vehicles over L87 6.2L V8 engine failures** — notably **alleging the recall remedy itself failed** — plus a separate probe covering **>870,000 Silverado/Sierra/Tahoe/Suburban/Yukon/Escalade** units. Recall-remedy inadequacy is the pattern that historically escalates into consent orders and per-vehicle civil penalties.
- **Autonomy overhang (largely retired):** Cruise paid a **$500K DOJ fine** and operated under an NHTSA consent order following the 2023 pedestrian incident; GM has since pulled robotaxi funding and folded Cruise into the parent — which converts a headline risk into a residual litigation/indemnity risk.
- **Securities litigation:** Plaintiff firms (Pomerantz, Gross Law) have issued shareholder alerts/investigations around GM disclosures — episodic, low materiality historically.
- **Legacy:** The 2015 **$900M DOJ criminal settlement** (ignition switch) is paid, but it established the template under which current recall failures draw deferred-prosecution scrutiny.
- **Labor/political:** UAW and a Michigan Republican candidate jointly criticised GM (Oct 20, 2026) over the Detroit battery plant closure and truck-production pauses — US political risk is now as much *labor/populist* as it is trade-related.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags
1. **Consumer data monetisation (highest severity).** The OnStar Smart Driver programme produced the largest CCPA fine to date and multi-state AG exposure. Any residual telematics/insurance partnerships should be treated as an ongoing AML-style reputational and state-AG risk, not a closed matter.
2. **Recall-remedy adequacy.** NHTSA complaints alleging failed remedies is the single most likely trigger for significant civil penalties.
3. **Sanctions/FCPA: low.** Post-2017/2022 footprint (no Europe manufacturing, no Russia operations) leaves minimal direct sanctions exposure; GM's Iran/Syria exposure is indirect via supplier screening only. No active FCPA matter surfaced in this review.

### 5. Overall Rating: **MEDIUM**

Rationale: operations are concentrated in low-risk jurisdictions (US/Canada/Mexico), sanctions and FCPA exposure is minimal, and the China drag is shrinking and already substantially written down. The offset is an unusually *policy-dependent* earnings stream — $2.5–3.5bn of annual tariff cost set by courts and executive orders rather than by GM — plus an active, escalating privacy/state-AG matter and two open NHTSA probes. Notably, GM's own guidance swung from $13.5–15.5bn to $14.5–15.5bn EBIT-adj on tariff/input-cost noise, evidence that geopolitical/legal variables — not competitive position — are now the marginal driver of the earnings range.
