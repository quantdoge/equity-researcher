# General Motors (GM) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price ~$83.66 (last close 10/15/26) · Mkt cap ~$81B (973M diluted sh.) · FY ends Dec*

---

## 1. Growth Story

GM is **not a disruptive growth platform — it is a cash-generative cyclical executing a margin-repair and capital-return story**, with two genuine option-value growth vectors bolted on: software/services (OnStar + Super Cruise generated **$5.4B in 2025**, targeted at **$20–25B by 2030**) and a credible path to **eyes-off autonomous driving commercially launching in 2028** on Cadillac Escalade IQ. The near-term equity thesis rests far less on TAM capture than on an EPS recovery from a charge-depressed FY2025 base ($3.27 GAAP vs ~$10.6 adjusted quarterly sum) toward consensus **$13.47 in 2026 and $15.28 in 2027**, funded by a 39% capex cut, a shrinking share count, and repeated guidance raises. On my growth framework, GM screens as a **deep-value turnaround with embedded optionality — not a compounder**.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **Core TAM (light vehicles):** ~90M units/yr globally, ~$4.5T revenue. Terminal growth 1–2%; GM holds ~8% share (~6M units). **No structural share-gain story** — China is a share donor, not a gainer.
- **Software/services TAM (the actual growth market):** $5.4B today (OnStar/Super Cruise) → $20–25B target by 2030. Even hitting the high end adds only ~$20B to a $185B base (**~8–10% of revenue**), but at far higher margins it could move blended gross margin several hundred bps. **This is the single most important swing factor for a re-rate.**
- **Autonomy:** Super Cruise covers 600k+ mapped highway miles; eyes-off system (lidar + radar + camera) begins public-road testing now, commercial launch **2028**. A robotaxi re-entry remains uncommitted post-Cruise — pure optionality, not in numbers.
- **Penetration verdict:** GM competes in a mature TAM with sub-GDP growth and is *losing* margin share. TAM analysis argues for a value multiple, not a growth multiple.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4/FY2026 results + initial 2027 guidance — ~Jan 26, 2027.** FY26 adjusted EBIT guidance repeatedly raised/narrowed to **~$14.5–15.5B**; a fourth consecutive raise and above-consensus 2027 EPS framing is the highest-probability re-rating event.
- **Buyback/dividend acceleration.** Dividends of only $657M vs $11.1B FCF (~6% payout) leaves large room for a dividend increase alongside the $6B repurchase — a direct support for per-share growth.
- **EV loss inflection.** The $1.6B EV capacity-realignment charge is being absorbed in 2026; evidence of narrowing EV losses in 2027 removes the single biggest drag on consolidated margin.
- **Tariff/USMCA resolution (2026–27).** GM's Mexico/Canada footprint makes trade policy the largest single swing factor on North American margin — a durable relief package would be an immediate multiple event.
- **Autonomy/software disclosure.** Any robotaxi partnership, eyes-off launch milestone, or re-segmentation that isolates software revenue at a software multiple.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

*(Upgrade to Buy only under a value/capital-return mandate; it is not a growth-mandate position.)*

GM fails my growth framework decisively: **Rule of 40 of 4.7**, negative FY25 revenue growth, ~950bp of gross-margin compression, and consensus modeling **1–2% revenue growth through 2028**. There is no innovation moat in the Cathie Wood sense — software is 3% of revenue and autonomy commercialization is 2028 at the earliest. What GM *does* offer is a 6.2x forward P/E, a 100% beat rate, rising estimates, 14% share-count reduction, and ~21% upside to the median $101 target — an attractive **risk/reward for a value buyer, not a growth buyer**. I would upgrade on: (1) two consecutive quarters of *positive* revenue growth, (2) gross margin recovering above 15%, or (3) software revenue disclosed at a >$8B run-rate with a credible path to the $20–25B 2030 target.

## 7. Key Risks

- **Tariffs and trade policy** — binary, unhedgeable, and large relative to a ~$15B EBIT base; GM's Mexican/Canadian production is directly exposed.
- **EV demand air-pocket** — post-credit expiration, Equinox EV search interest is down 62% from peak; stranded EV capacity and further writedowns are likely.
- **Margin structure is deteriorating, not stabilizing** — FY25 GAAP operating margin of 1.6% (even charge-adjusted, ~5–6%) is structurally below the 2021–22 peak; cycle peak earnings may already be behind it.
- **FCF quality is poor** — the $11.1B FY25 FCF came from cutting capex 39%, not from earnings. This is not repeatable, and under-investment risks future competitiveness.
- **China** — continued share loss and restructuring drag.
- **Execution risk on autonomy** — 2028 eyes-off launch is two years out against Tesla/Waymo; a slip or a costly robotaxi re-entry would burn the optionality premium.
- **Cycle risk** — sub-6x forward P/E often signals peak earnings; a North American volume downturn would compress both EPS and the multiple.

**Data gaps disclosed:** Ford's trailing multiples are unusable (negative earnings from EV charges) and Toyota's are corrupted by FX scaling (P/E 0.06x), so no reliable peer-multiple triangulation; the stationary energy-storage angle could not be verified from available sources. FY2025 GAAP gross profit ($20.1B) appears distorted by large one-time charges relative to adjusted EPS — I have leaned on adjusted quarterly figures for the recovery math and flagged the discrepancy.
