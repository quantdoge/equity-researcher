# HP Inc. (NYSE: HPQ) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment

**Price $31.15 | Market Cap ~$28.1B | Technology / Computer Hardware | 55,000 employees | Beta 1.21**

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (with a material caveat)**

**Positives:**
- **Chair/CEO separation maintained.** Chip Bergh (ex-Levi's CEO) served as independent Chair throughout the interim period; Broussard will assume the Chair on Jan 4, 2027, meaning the permanent CEO (Walmsley) will *not* hold both roles. This is structurally sound.
- **Permanent CEO resolved.** Emma Walmsley (ex-GSK CEO; 7-year Microsoft board member) appointed Nov 4, 2026, effective Jan 4, 2027 — an external, credible, brand-and-regulated-industry operator with large-cap public board experience.
- **Board acted decisively.** Formed a CEO Search Committee, retained a global search firm, and did not rush a compromised internal candidate.

**Caveats / concerns:**
- **Chronic CEO instability is now a pattern, not an event.** Lores is roughly the sixth CEO departure in 25 years (Carly Fiorina, Mark Hurd, Léo Apotheker, Meg Whitman, Dion Weisler, Lores). Lores departed abruptly on Feb 3, 2026 — *no transition period, simultaneous board exit* — to become PayPal's CEO ("pursue another professional opportunity"). Boards that lose CEOs to rivals at short notice typically reflect strained relations rather than planned succession.
- **~11-month interim CEO tenure** (Feb 3, 2026 → Jan 4, 2027). Press openly questioned whether HP struggled to attract or persuade a candidate. Broussard is a capable steward (ex-Humana CEO) but a healthcare/financial operator, not a hardware technologist — the strategic agenda was effectively frozen for a year during the memory-cost crisis and PC demand inflection.
- **Succession bench is a real question.** No internal executive was elevated, suggesting either a weak bench or a board preference for external reset. Combined with an incoming CEO lacking PC/enterprise-hardware operating experience, execution risk during the turnaround is elevated.
- **Compensation sensitivity.** FY27 guidance was restated for a *new treatment of stock-based pay*, rendering the $3.30 midpoint potentially **4–11% below consensus on a comparable basis**. Changing equity-comp presentation while simultaneously guiding conservatively warrants scrutiny.
- **Serial restructuring.** Non-GAAP EPS routinely excludes "restructuring and other charges" every year; Q1 FY26 alone excluded $0.15/share. Recurring "one-time" charges are a mild earnings-quality flag, not fraud evidence.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Memory/DRAM cost shock — the dominant risk.** Memory has gone from ~15% to ~**40% of a PC's bill of materials in one year**, driven by AI datacenter demand. HP is a price-taker from a three-supplier oligopoly (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron); HPE has publicly guided memory "constrained until at least late 2028." HP has repriced PCs, but that is demand-destructive: **PC units already fell 7% y/y in Q2 FY26 despite 13% Personal Systems revenue growth**, and HP guides the CY2027 PC market down **high-single to low-double digits**. Margin recovery is a forecast, not a fact.
- **Pricing-led revenue masking volume decline.** Both segments show the same signature — revenue up, units down (PCs -7%; print hardware units -7%; consumer printing revenue -10%). The installed base and supplies annuity that fund the dividend and buyback are eroding at the foundation. This is a business-model risk, not a quarterly one.
- **Key-person and execution risk at the top.** An 11-month interim CEO, an incoming CEO with no PC/hardware operating background, and a CFO (Parkhill) and segment presidents (Patel, Olson, McQuarrie) who have been selling stock. Institutional knowledge is concentrated in a leadership layer that is actively de-risking personally.

*No material disclosed cybersecurity breach or data-privacy enforcement action was found in the review window (a genuine positive for a 55,000-person global hardware firm). Historical 2005–06 "pretexting" board-leak scandal is stale and non-recurring.*

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **MODERATE**

- **Leadership churn narrative is now the dominant story.** Coverage includes "HP Has Fired, Forced Out, or Lost 6 CEOs in 25 Years," "HP Needs More Than a New CEO," and analyst commentary that Lores' exit "is not a normal transition… it's a control move." This framing invites activist and acquirer attention and undermines the credibility of FY27 guidance issued by a lame-duck interim CEO.
- **Earnings-quality perception.** Multiple outlets characterized the Q4 beat as "low-quality" and "tariff-aided" — a $0.15/share refund plus cost-cut reliance. The 15% pop was relief at guidance, not a reputation recovery.
- **Printer-ink trust deficit persists.** "Monopoly," "malware," and cartridge-lock-in framing is durable consumer sentiment. HP is winning in court but losing the narrative, and the EU right-to-repair/EPEAT fronts give critics a regulatory channel.
- **Offsetting positive:** HP retains strong sustainability credentials and one of the most recognized brands in technology; ESG controversy scoring is not elevated.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

**Rationale:** HP scores well on the things that destroy companies outright — no SEC enforcement, no disclosed breach, no material litigation liability, independent chair, credible external CEO appointed, and litigation that is being *won*. The rating is held back from "Low" by a genuine pattern of leadership instability, near-total insider liquidation with no insider buying, and a once-in-a-decade input-cost shock (memory) colliding with structural unit decline. The non-financial risk here is **execution and credibility risk during a leadership handoff**, not fraud or legal jeopardy.

---

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🚩 CEO departed with zero notice and no transition, to run another public company (Feb 3, 2026).** Abrupt simultaneous board exit is the classic signature of a board-initiated or relationship-break departure, notwithstanding the "pursue another professional opportunity" language. **Verify** whether the 8-K/severance terms disclose cause, non-compete, or clawback provisions. Ask directly: did the board ask him to leave?
2. **🚩 Zero insider buying in 12 months while seven insiders sold ~$5M+, including ~91% liquidation by the Chief Commercial Officer and a full exit by the Printing President.** No director bought the $18.47 low. Escalate as a **conviction-vacuum** signal to be weighed against the bullish narrative.
3. **🚩 Guidance quality.** FY27 non-GAAP guide restated for a new stock-comp treatment, placing the midpoint potentially 4–11% below consensus on a like-for-like basis, while insiders characterize the guide as "prudent." **Request a reconciliation.** Presenting favorable-looking guidance on a changed accounting basis is a disclosure-transparency item I would put to management.
4. **🚩 Interim CEO for ~11 months, then moves directly to Chair.** Confirm the Chair role is independent-classified and that Walmsley has genuine latitude rather than inheriting a board-imposed strategy.
5. **⚠️ Structural threat to the supplies annuity** — EU Right to Repair plus EPEAT 2.0 certification risk. Under-modeled by consensus; could force firmware changes or procurement de-listing across HP's most profitable segment (Printing at 18.3% operating margin).
6. **⚠️ Serial "one-time" restructuring charges** excluded from every non-GAAP year. Not fraud, but confirm there is no accrual-reserve release cushioning reported margins during the memory-cost squeeze.
