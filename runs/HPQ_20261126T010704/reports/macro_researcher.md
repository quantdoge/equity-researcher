## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional / Hawkish Re-tightening (risk-off in duration, risk-on in credit-carry)**

As of **26 November 2026**, the US economy is in a "no landing, no ease" regime. Growth is intact — Q3 2026 real GDP of **$24.665T (chained 2017$), +2.3% YoY and ~+2.2% SAAR** — and unemployment has *fallen* to **4.1% (Oct 2026)** from 4.5% a year ago. But inflation has re-accelerated: **CPI +3.82% YoY in Oct 2026**, up from ~3.3% in Q1 2026. The Fed cut from 5.33% to 3.63% through 2025, **paused at 3.63% from January to July 2026, and has since drifted back up to 3.88%** — a de facto 25bp of re-tightening. **Real fed funds is ~0%.** There is no policy cushion.

The critical detail is *what* is driving the long end. The 10Y sits at **4.91% (24 Nov close; 5.13% on 26 Nov)** after **5.31% on 5 Oct**, versus 4.48% in early July — a **+45 to +65bp bear steepening**. Yet the **10Y breakeven has fallen from 2.38% (6 Oct) to 2.12% (25 Nov)**. This is not an inflation-expectation selloff; it is a **real-rate and term-premium shock**. Implied 10Y real yield is **~2.79%**, near cycle highs. That is a tightening of financial conditions the Fed did not vote for, and it lands directly on long-duration, high-payout equities.

Credit is still *open* but is cracking at the margin: **HY OAS at 372bp (24 Nov), up ~107bp from the 265bp low on 11 Sep**, though still historically tight. Meanwhile the **broad dollar has rallied 4.9% in ten weeks** (117.88 → 123.60) on the widening real-rate differential. Policy risk is live: tariff pass-through and AI-datacenter politics are both feeding directly into HPQ's cost line.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**The dominant macro variable for HPQ is not the Fed — it is DRAM spot pricing.** Per the Q4 FY26 call (24 Nov 2026), memory has gone from **~15% to ~40% of a PC's bill of materials in twelve months**. This is an AI-datacenter demand shock entering HPQ's cost structure through the input market. TrendForce has DRAM up another 10–15% in Q4 2026, with some supply partners guiding 10–20% *per month*; the pass-through is visible in the print: **Q4 revenue +9.3% YoY to $16.0B** while HPQ "shipped far fewer machines." That is price inflation masking unit contraction — a classic demand-destruction signature, and sell-side correctly called it a *"low-quality beat."* Full-year 2026 PC shipments are forecast **−12%**.

**Rate sensitivity — negative, but second-order.** HPQ is not a long-duration growth asset; at a high-single-digit forward P/E with a raised dividend (~3.7% yield, approximate), it is a value/income instrument. Rising long yields hurt through three channels: (i) the discount rate on a low-growth perpetuity compresses the multiple even as the dividend holds; (ii) HPQ's leveraged balance sheet — meaningful fixed-rate notes issued in a 3–4% coupon era will refinance into a 5%+ market; (iii) enterprise PC/print capex is a credit-sensitive line item, and HY OAS has already widened 107bp. Note HPQ's beta of **1.21**, yet it rallied **+12.4% on 25 Nov to $31.15** while the Nasdaq fell **3%** — the market is treating it as a defensive value refuge, which is itself a rate-driven rotation.

**Currency — a live, quantifiable headwind.** HPQ derives roughly two-thirds of revenue outside the US. The **4.9% broad-dollar rally since 9 September** is a direct translation drag on Personal Systems and Printing supplies, partially offset by natural hedges. The price action confirms the linkage: **HPQ peaked at $35.48 on 11 September** — two days after the dollar's low — then fell **~21% to $27.94 by 24 November** as the dollar rallied. Part coincidence, part genuine FX/rate transmission, all directionally adverse.

**Tariffs — compounding, not standalone.** Memory inflation and tariff pass-through are hitting the same BOM simultaneously, pushing PC ASPs up ~17%. HPQ is raising prices into a weakening consumer, which is why management guided **FY27 EPS above consensus on cost cuts and premium/AI-PC mix** rather than volume. Commercial/local-AI-PC demand is the offset; consumer is the casualty.

**The one genuinely favourable linkage:** HPQ's largest cost input is falling when the AI capex trade cracks. **Kalshi traders now assign ~57% odds to at least four states pausing data-center construction** (up from 52%, down from 75% post-midterms; New York already imposed a one-year pause in July 2026). TrendForce expects DRAM/SSD price declines from **Q1 2027**, with Korean analysts projecting a "dramatic drop" by mid-2027. Anything that cools hyperscaler memory demand is a gross-margin gift to HPQ — the inverse of the current squeeze.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE** (improving into 2H FY27)

Every high-weight macro variable currently points the wrong way: CPI re-accelerating to 3.82% with real fed funds at zero, a 2.79% real 10Y yield, a 4.9% dollar rally, 372bp HY spreads widening off the lows, and DRAM at 40% of BOM with PC units forecast down 12%. HPQ is executing well *against* this tape — a 12% EPS beat, a raised dividend, and FY27 guidance above consensus — which is precisely why the macro read matters: the company is being asked to grow earnings while its input costs, its translation rate, and its discount rate all move adversely. The rating improves to Neutral in 1H FY27 if the Q1 2027 memory-price relief materialises and the dollar stabilises.

## 5. Surprise Scenario (Bull, and materially under-priced)

**The AI capex air-pocket that helps HPQ.** Consensus assumes memory stays scarce through 2027 and that HPQ grinds out margin recovery via cost cuts. The non-consensus path: state-level data-center moratoriums and cooling hyperscaler orders trigger a violent DRAM unwind in 1H27. If DRAM falls 30% from spot, that removes roughly **12 percentage points from PC BOM cost** — with memory at 40% of BOM, that is potentially **300–400bp of Personal Systems gross margin** recovered in two quarters, on top of already-inflated ASPs held sticky by a less price-competitive market.

The tape has already previewed the mechanics: on **25 November, HPQ rose 12.4% on 39M shares (3x normal volume) while the Nasdaq fell 3%.** In this scenario HPQ becomes a genuine defensive re-rating — an 8x-earnings, 3.7%-yielding hardware name receiving a margin windfall *because* the AI trade broke. The bear mirror image is a Fed forced back to hiking on 3.8% CPI, taking the 10Y to 5.75% and consumer PC demand down a further leg; given a high-single-digit multiple, that is a demand problem, not a de-rating problem.
