## SECTOR BRIEF — HP Inc. (HPQ)
**Sector:** Technology › Computer Hardware (Client Devices & Print). Market cap $28.1B, price $31.15 (25-Nov-26). Fiscal year ends 31-Oct.

*Data note: FMP's annual income statement tags HPQ's Q4 FY26 as the "FY26 annual" row (rev $16.0B) and the quarterly row is sign-corrupted. I reconstructed FY26 from the four clean quarters and cross-checked to the company release — both agree at $60.5B revenue.*

---

### 1. Sector Overview
PC and print hardware is a ~$245–270B global revenue pool that is currently **de-rating in units while inflating in price**. Industry units fell **22.4% YoY in Q3 CY2026** (55.4M units, Gartner) after a +4% Q1, as the AI-datacenter memory super-cycle diverted DRAM/NAND supply: Gartner estimates combined DRAM+SSD prices rose ~**130% in 2026**, lifting average PC prices ~**17%** and pushing memory from ~15% to ~**40% of a PC bill of materials**. The result is a barbell: AI-infrastructure hardware (servers, nearline HDD, networking) is booming — Dell +18.8%, Arista +28.6%, Seagate +34.1%, Western Digital +35.7%, Super Micro +77.8% revenue — while client-device assemblers are being squeezed into negative unit growth and thin margins. Structure is moderately concentrated (top-3 ~62% of PCs; HHI ≈1,700–1,800) but pricing power sits with suppliers, not assemblers. Printing is the profit pool that funds the group: at HPQ, Printing is 27% of Q4 revenue but ~70% of segment operating profit.

### 2. Key Tailwinds
- **AI PC mix shift is real and fast:** AI PCs reached **~60% of HPQ's Q4 FY26 shipments**; commercial PS revenue **+23% YoY** vs consumer **−7%**, and Commercial PS units still fell 28% — i.e. mix/ASP, not volume, is the growth engine now.
- **AI infrastructure demand lifts the whole hardware complex:** Dell's FY26 revenue +18.8% to $113.5B with operating margin expanding to 7.2%; Arista OM 42.8%; these confirm enterprise hardware budgets are intact where AI is the use case.
- **Inflation is being passed through:** nominal revenue growth of +9.5% (HPQ), +18.8% (Dell), +77.8% (SMCI) shows the channel can absorb 17% price hikes — the damage is to gross margin, not to the top line, and it should reverse as memory normalises in FY28.
- **Printing annuity + capital returns:** print consumables/installed base supports 19.7% segment margins, ~$3.3B FCF (11.7% FCF yield), and a just-raised dividend ($0.31/qtr).

### 3. Key Headwinds
- **Memory cost shock is the dominant factor:** with memory at 40% of BOM and DRAM/NAND up ~130%, assemblers eat the delta. HPQ's gross margin fell **~240bp YoY in Q4** (17.5% vs 19.8%) and its PC segment margin bottomed at **3.0%** — a level that barely covers fixed cost.
- **Demand destruction / pull-forward risk:** Q3 CY26 units −22.4% industry-wide; HPQ Q4 units **−29%** with revenue +14%, an unsustainable divergence. HPQ inventory rose **11 days QoQ to 84 days** — either pre-buying ahead of further inflation or a demand air-pocket, both risky.
- **Structural commoditisation and supplier power:** buyers (enterprise tenders, retail) and suppliers (Samsung/SK hynix/Micron memory; Intel/AMD/Nvidia compute; panels) both hold more leverage than the assembler; rivalry from Lenovo (27.1% share), Dell, Apple and Chinese OEMs caps price/mix gains.
- **Execution/management churn and print erosion:** HPQ is running on an **interim CEO**, and Printing revenue is declining (−1% YoY, −2% cc) with third-party consumables pressure — removing the earnings cushion exactly when PCs need one.

### 5. HPQ's Competitive Position: **Average (weakening at the PC margin line, defended by Print)**
- **Positives:** #2 global PC share with genuine enterprise channel scale; AI PCs at ~60% of shipments (ahead of the industry average); Printing's 19.7% margin and installed-base annuity; 70% FCF payout with a rising dividend; scale-driven working-capital float ($22.7B payables, 155 days).
- **Negatives:** the lowest operating margin of any peer in the set (5.1% vs 24.2% median, 7.2% Dell) with no AI-datacenter offset; PC segment margin at 3.0% is effectively at cost-of-capital zero; −29% unit decline signals share/price risk; 84-day inventory is a warning flag; interim CEO during a margin-recovery programme that management itself guides "prudently."
- **Verdict:** structurally sound franchise, cyclically the worst-positioned business model in its own peer group — a price-taker between oligopolistic suppliers and price-sensitive buyers.

### 6. Sector Rating: **UNATTRACTIVE** (Client Devices & Print sub-sector)
The broad IT hardware sector is bifurcated and, in aggregate, attractive — but HPQ competes in the sub-segment where the cycle is working *against* the economics: units −22% industry-wide, input costs up 130% on the key BOM component, gross margins compressing ~240bp, and no pricing power to close the gap. This is a **late-cycle, inflationary contraction**: nominal revenue growth masks volume destruction, and margin recovery is management's FY27 *hope*, contingent on memory normalising and cost cuts landing. Until DRAM/NAND pricing rolls over, assemblers with ~3–5% operating margins face negative operating leverage and estimate risk, even at a 9.4x multiple and 12% FCF yield. The attractive capital in this sector sits upstream (memory: MU/STX/WDC; networking: ANET) and in AI servers (DELL) — not in client devices or print.
