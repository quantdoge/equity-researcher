# HP Inc. (HPQ) — ESG Analysis

**Company:** HP Inc. | Personal Systems, Printing, Corporate Investments | 55,000 employees, ~180 countries
**Market cap:** $28.1bn | **Price:** $31.15 | **FY25 FCF:** $2.9bn (revenue ~$55bn) | **Dividend yield:** ~6%
**Note on data:** The 10-K and DEF 14A were not retrievable through the filing tool ("No 10-K/DEF 14A found"). Findings below rest on press releases, the FY25 Sustainability Progress Report (via trade press), news, and litigation records. Board-independence statistics, Scope 3 tonnage, and current compensation metrics are unverified — flagged where relevant.

## 1. ESG Materiality Map — Computer Hardware / Imaging & Printing

HP's ESG risk profile is driven by a **linear hardware model with a razor/blade supplies annuity**, a **China-centric contract-manufacturing supply chain**, and **regulatory pressure on consumables lock-in**. Ranked by financial materiality:

| Rank | Factor | Why it's financially material | Horizon |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Supply chain labour & forced-labour exposure (S)** | China/SE Asia contract manufacturing; UFLPA detentions can halt shipments; CSDDD civil liability | 0–3 yrs |
| 2 | **Product/consumer protection & antitrust (S/G)** | Ink-cartridge firmware lock-out litigation, antitrust class actions; supplies = high-margin profit pool, so remedies directly hit gross margin | 0–3 yrs |
| 3 | **Governance stability & disclosure quality (G)** | CEO turnover, non-independent chair, securities-fraud settlement — affects cost of capital and credibility of guidance | 0–2 yrs |
| 4 | **Climate transition — Scope 3 & product energy (E)** | >95% of footprint is Scope 3 (components, logistics, product use); CSRD/SB 253 disclosure cost; customer-side procurement standards | 2–10 yrs |
| 5 | **Circularity / e-waste & packaging regulation (E)** | EU ESPR/Ecodesign, right-to-repair, EPR fees; also a genuine cost-saving lever | 1–5 yrs |
| 6 | **Workforce restructuring & social licence (S)** | 4,000–6,000 role eliminations by FY28 on top of prior cuts; attrition/talent and reputational risk | 0–3 yrs |
| 7 | **Physical climate & water (E)** | Low direct exposure; indirect via Asia supplier concentration and component-cost volatility | 5+ yrs |

**Low materiality:** biodiversity (beyond paper sourcing), executive-pay-vs-ESG linkage, board diversity per se, community relations.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives (genuine, verifiable):**
- Scope 1+2 operational emissions down **69% vs 2015**; total footprint down ~28% since 2019
- **66% renewable electricity** globally, **100% in U.S. operations**
- Suppliers cut **2.47 Mt CO₂e** (2010–2024), exceeding a 2.0 Mt goal
- **1.21 Mt** of hardware/supplies recycled since 2016; **72%** of single-use plastic packaging eliminated vs 2018
- Product-level credentials are real and audited (EPEAT Gold with Climate+ on new devices), which wins enterprise tenders increasingly gated on sustainability specs

**The material concern — target credibility:**
HP exhausted its 2025 goal set and **reset** ahead: value-chain emissions **−50% by 2030**, net zero by **2040**, and — critically — **100% renewable electricity globally now targeted for 2040** versus a 2025 goal it did not meet (reached 66%). HP simultaneously states it "met or exceeded all its 2025 sustainability goals." Both can be technically true depending on how the original goal was scoped, but investors should read this as **goal slippage on the flagship operational target**, not pure overachievement. The new 2040 RE target is effectively a non-binding aspiration for the next 14 years.

**Carbon intensity:** Directionally moderate for hardware. HP is a low-Scope-1/2 business (assembly is outsourced by design), so ~95%+ of emissions sit in Scope 3 — purchased components (aluminium, plastics, semiconductors, displays), outbound logistics, and product electricity use. On ~$55bn revenue this implies an order-of-magnitude **~400–500 tCO₂e per $1m revenue** — unremarkable for the sector and *improving*, but heavily dependent on supplier data quality rather than HP's own actions. This is the classic Scope 3 asymmetry: HP can hit net-zero claims on its P&L while its actual footprint is set by contract manufacturers.

**Physical risk:** Low. Palo Alto HQ and mostly leased office footprint. Indirect exposure via monsoon/typhoon and water stress at Asian manufacturing clusters, already visible in the memory/component cost inflation now pressuring PC margins.

**Environmental risk verdict:** Medium. No stranded-asset exposure, no meaningful carbon-tax cost pass-through (CBAM covers steel/aluminium/cement, not electronics — HP is an indirect aluminium importer). Downgraded from "Low" solely because of the renewable-electricity target push-out.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Workforce — the dominant issue.** On 25 Nov 2025 HP announced a companywide restructuring eliminating **4,000–6,000 roles through FY2028** (roughly 7–11% of headcount), framed as AI-driven productivity, on top of ~2,000 cuts in 2025 and the prior "Future Ready" programme — which reportedly ended with **substantially more departures than originally announced**. Repeated over-delivery on cost cuts is good for the P&L and bad for the social pillar: it signals restructuring credibility risk in both directions (further charges) and creates attrition/talent depth risk in a company whose recovery thesis depends on AI PC innovation. Repeated voluntary-early-retirement rounds also concentrate **age-discrimination litigation exposure**.

**Product safety & consumer protection.** HP's Dynamic Security firmware — which disables third-party ink cartridges — has produced a durable litigation tail: a **$1.5m settlement** barring reinstallation of the feature on affected printers, plus an Illinois-filed **antitrust class action** alleging monopolisation of the supplies aftermarket. One ink-cartridge class action was reportedly **dismissed with prejudice in September 2026**, which is a genuine positive, but the pattern of claims is structural: every margin point earned from supplies lock-in is a litigation and regulatory target. This is the single biggest S-factor for cash flows because Printing supplies carry the company's highest-margin profit pool.

**Supply chain labour.** HP sources heavily from Chinese contract manufacturers with downstream components from Asia broadly. **UFLPA** withholding-risk and **EU CSDDD** civil-liability exposure are live, unquantified tail risks. HP runs a supplier code and audit programme but I could not verify third-party audit coverage or Xinjiang-tier traceability — treat as a **disclosure gap**.

**Diversity/DEI posture.** HP was among the more vocal corporate DEI proponents (public racial-equity goals, HRC Corporate Equality Index participation) and now faces the same crossfire as peers: a **reverse-discrimination suit filed by a former white male employee (D. Conn., May 2024)** alleging a "shaming session," alongside a post-2025 political environment that has made DEI programmes a legal target. Expect **quiet retrenchment** on public targets with retained internal practice — lower headline risk than a public rollback, but a governance-consistency question.

**Social verdict:** Medium-High. Not a licence-to-operate crisis, but restructuring intensity plus consumables litigation is the highest-probability source of an ESG-related financial hit over the next 24 months.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6 / 10**

**Supports the score:**
- Single share class; no dual-class structure, no poison pill — one-share-one-vote preserved
- Say-on-pay passed with **>93% of votes cast (2025)**; board responsive to investor feedback per proxy language
- Substantially independent board with separated Chair/CEO roles in normal course (Chip Bergh as independent Chair through Jan 2027)
- Orderly, externally-benchmarked CEO search (retained global search firm, CEO Search Committee) that landed a credible two-time public-company CEO in **Dame Emma Walmsley** (ex-GSK, seven years on Microsoft's board) effective **4 Jan 2027**

**Detracts from the score:**
1. **CEO instability.** Enrique Lores departed abruptly on **3 Feb 2026** — "to pursue another professional opportunity," effective immediately, **also resigning from the board**. Immediate CEO exits with simultaneous board resignations are, statistically, rarely purely voluntary; investors get no explanation. HPQ will have had **three CEOs inside ~24 months** (Lores → Broussard interim → Walmsley), and a chair transition (Bergh → Broussard) at the same time.
2. **Chair will not be independent from 4 Jan 2027.** Bruce Broussard moves from **interim CEO directly to Chair** — a classic entrenchment pattern that compromises board oversight of the executive team he just led.
3. **16% of votes cast supported an independent-chair shareholder proposal** at the 16 April 2026 AGM (one of 16 ballot items). A double-digit dissent on a governance-structure proposal is a material signal of institutional dissatisfaction that the board has effectively overridden.
4. **Disclosure-quality blemish.** HP agreed a **$39m securities class-action settlement** (case filed Nov 2020) alleging false/misleading statements about the **printing supplies business** — specifically concealing deteriorating performance and inflated channel inventory in 2015–16. Paying to settle a disclosure case is a governance data point, not merely a legal cost.
5. **Succession skills mismatch (mild).** Walmsley's background is pharma and consumer brands, not hardware supply chains; she inherits a memory-cost margin squeeze. Fresh-eyes logic is defensible, but it lengthens the execution-risk window.

**Verdict:** Structurally sound (shareholder rights, pay alignment, search process) but **operationally unsettled**. The 2040 renewable-electricity push-out and the "met all goals" framing are the environmental mirror of the same trait — a management team comfortable with favourable reframing. **6/10: above-median structure, below-median stability.**

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

| Rule / jurisdiction | Applies to HPQ? | Exposure & timing |
|---|---|---|
| **EU CSRD / ESRS** | **Yes** — EU subsidiary turnover well above €150m; large-undertaking thresholds met | First mandatory sustainability statement for FY25 data, published FY26. HP's CSO explicitly framed the FY25 report as "aligned with a rapidly changing regulatory landscape" — i.e., already building the CSRD data architecture. Cost: low-to-moderate (systems, assurance). Risk: **Scope 3 data quality** and double-materiality disclosures invite challenge. |
| **California SB 253 / SB 261** | **Yes** (>$1bn revenue doing business in CA) | Scope 1/2/3 and climate-risk reporting; enforcement timing has been messy, but HP will file. |
| **EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive (CSDDD)** | **Yes** (EU revenue threshold) | Transposition ~2027; **civil liability** for supply-chain human-rights failures is the real tail risk given China-centric sourcing. |
| **EU ESPR / Ecodesign for PCs & printers, right-to-repair** | **Yes** | Raises the floor on repairability/durability/recycled content — HP is *relatively* advantaged here (recycled metals, ocean-bound plastics, EPEAT Gold Climate+). Net **opportunity**, mild. |
| **EU Batteries Regulation & WEEE/EPR** | Yes | Take-back and recycled-content obligations; fee-level cost, already partly internalised via 1.21 Mt recycled. |
| **CBAM** | **Indirect only** | Aluminium is a CBAM good, but HP imports finished devices, not raw aluminium — exposure passes through supplier pricing. Low. |
| **UFLPA (US) / forced-labour import bans** | **Yes** | Highest-severity tail: a CBP detention of finished goods is an operational stop-ship event. |
| **ESG-linked debt covenants** | Not identified | **Data gap** — HP's debt is largely conventional bonds/CP; I found no sustainability-linked bond with margin ratchets. |
| **SEC climate rule** | Stayed/uncertain | Currently not a binding driver; watch only. |

**Net regulatory read:** HP is *in scope for most major regimes simultaneously* — this is a compliance-cost and disclosure-quality story, not a stranded-asset story. No carbon-tax or CBAM cliff. The genuine legal tail is **supply-chain due diligence** (CSDDD/UFLPA), not climate.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

- **Environmental: above-average.** Best-in-class circularity and product certification; but a flagship 100%-renewable goal pushed out 15 years, and a "met all targets" narrative that doesn't survive scrutiny. Environmental leadership is real but **decaying at the margin**.
- **Social: at-or-slightly-below average.** Serial restructuring, consumables litigation, unverifiable supply-chain audit depth.
- **Governance: average on structure, below-average on stability.** Three CEOs in two years, a non-independent incoming chair, 16% independent-chair dissent, and a settled disclosure-fraud case.
- **Not a "Leader"** despite historically strong ESG scores (HP has long featured on CDP climate/water A-lists and mid-AA MSCI-type ratings) — because those scores lag the 2026 target reset and the governance turnover. **Not a "Laggard"** — no stranded assets, no systemic controversy, real product-level credentials, one-share-one-vote.
- **Not "ESG Risk"** — no evidence of existential licence-to-operate threat.

**Watch-list triggers that would move this to Laggard:** (a) any UFLPA/CBP detention or CSDDD-style supply-chain finding; (b) adverse ruling in the printer antitrust action touching supplies pricing; (c) a fourth CEO change or refusal to appoint an independent chair after Broussard; (d) a further climate-target retrenchment.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**ESG is not the swing factor in the HPQ thesis — the swing factors are memory/component cost inflation, PC demand, and AI-PC mix.** Nothing in the E, S, or G stack is currently large enough to re-rate the equity on its own:

- **Cash-flow impact is small and quantifiable:** ~$40m of settlement costs against $2.9bn of FCF is immaterial. Even a bad supplies-antitrust outcome is a multiple, not an existential, of the ~$3bn+ Printing profit pool.
- **Cost of capital:** the governance instability likely adds a modest execution discount rather than a financing penalty — HP's debt carries no ESG-linked covenants identified. Single-class shares and >93% say-on-pay support argue *against* a governance discount widening.
- **The one place ESG and the thesis genuinely overlap** is restructuring: 4,000–6,000 eliminations are simultaneously the EPS recovery lever and the principal social risk. Investors cannot be pro-EPS and anti-social-risk here; they're the same decision.

**Positioning:** Treat ESG as **neutral / non-differentiating** — do not underweight HPQ for sustainability reasons (its circularity and EPEAT credentials are a real, if modest, commercial advantage in enterprise tenders), but do not claim an ESG-leadership premium either. The **Governance pillar is the actionable axis**: the 4 Jan 2027 Walmsley start plus Broussard's move to a non-independent chair is a clean catalyst window. If the board retains a non-independent chair and the climate-reporting reset widens, expect a shareholder-proposal fight and possible index/ESG-fund de-rating pressure on an already-megacap-excluded, small-cap-adjacent name. If Walmsley imposes external discipline, refreshes targets honestly, and names an independent chair, the governance upgrade is a genuine though second-order positive.

**Bottom line: ESG Neutral, with a Governance monitor.** Rating: **Average**. The correct ESG-related question for the memo is not "is HP sustainable?" but "is a company in the third year of serial restructuring, on its third CEO, still able to fund a 2040 net-zero roadmap and defend its supplies profit pool simultaneously?"
