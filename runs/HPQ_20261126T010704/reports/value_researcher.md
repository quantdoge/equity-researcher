## HP Inc. (HPQ) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $31.15 (Nov 25, 2026) · Market cap ~$28.1B · Net debt $4.9B · EV ~$33.0B*

**Data note:** the vendor's FY2026 "annual" row is corrupted (it reports Q4 revenue of $16.0B as the annual total, and back-solves Q4 as −$28.5B). I reconstructed FY26 from the four quarters and cross-checked against the 10-K press release: revenue **$60.5B, GAAP EPS $2.62, non-GAAP EPS $3.33, FCF $3.3B**. Tool-derived ratios (P/E 37x, EV/EBITDA 19.4x) are therefore artifacts — I use my reconstructed figures throughout.

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: NARROW (and narrowing)

HP is two very different businesses bolted together:

**Printing (~$17B revenue, ~20% operating margin) — the entire profit pool.** Razor/blade economics: an installed base of ~tens of millions of printers monetized through high-margin supplies. Switching costs are real but modest; the annuity is in slow structural decline as print volumes digitize. Q4 Printing revenue −1% y/y at 19.7% margin. This is the moat — and it is a melting ice cube, not a widening one.

**Personal Systems (~$43B revenue, ~2–3% operating margin).** A commoditized assembly business competing with Lenovo, Dell, ASUS and Apple. No pricing power, no cost advantage, no switching costs. Q4 tells the story bluntly: **revenue +14% but units −29%** (Consumer −31%, Commercial −28%). That 60%+ ASP inflation is memory/storage cost pass-through — memory is now reportedly ~40% of PC bill of materials — not demand. 3.0% segment operating margin.

**Moat test — ROIC vs WACC:** adjusted NOPAT ≈ $2.45B on invested capital of ~$5.4B (equity $0.5B + debt $9.3B − cash $4.4B) = **~45% ROIC**, far above a ~9.5% WACC. But be careful: that return is high because decade-long buybacks shrank the equity base and because working capital is negative (payables 155 days vs inventory 84 days), *not* because there is a reinvestment runway. There is no growth capex; capex is ~$0.8B against $0.9B of D&A. High ROIC with no reinvestment opportunity is a distribution machine, not a compounder.

**Five-year moat trajectory:** revenue $63.5B (FY21) → $60.5B (FY26), i.e. **net shrinkage over five years** despite FY26's inflation-driven +9.5%. Gross margin 21.1% → 19.2%. Operating income $5.36B → $3.11B. Margins compress every quarter of FY26 (GM 19.6% → 20.9% → 18.8% → 17.5%; FY26 Q4 operating margin 5.3%). **The moat has narrowed.**

**Strengths:** FCF conversion is excellent (~130% of net income; NI + D&A − capex ≈ $2.6B, and FCF has held $2.8–3.3B for four straight years through a pandemic, a PC bust and a tariff war). Cash returns are fully funded: $3.3B FCF vs $2.3B returned (70%), dividend only ~40% of FCF.

**Weaknesses:** (a) Negative tangible equity (−$8.2B; goodwill+intangibles $8.7B vs total equity $0.5B) — book value is uninformative and ROE is meaningless. (b) Current ratio 0.81; the negative working-capital model is a fragility if memory inventory must be financed at higher prices (inventory +$3.8B y/y to $12.3B, 84 days). (c) Net debt $4.9B, ~1.1x EBITDA, interest cover ~7.5x — adequate, not fortress. (d) FY26 EPS was flattered by ~$0.26 of tariff refunds (non-GAAP $3.33 → ~$3.07 underlying). (e) EPS has fallen three years running *despite* a 25% share-count reduction — buybacks are masking, not offsetting, earnings erosion.

**Intrinsic value (owner earnings, my own DCF):**
- **Bear ($25–26):** FCF erodes to $2.4B (−3%/yr for 5 years, then flat), WACC 10%.
- **Base ($33–35):** normalized FCF **$2.9B** (stripping tariff refunds, crediting stable print supplies), 10-year explicit + flat terminal, WACC 9.5% → ~$30–31B equity value = **$33–35/share**.
- **Bull ($44–46):** FCF $3.2B growing 1.5%, WACC 9%, print supplies stabilize and memory normalizes.

The mechanical DCF tool returns $52/share, but it extrapolates the current inflated FCF at a 2.45% growth rate — I regard that as too generous for a business with 29% unit declines and a digitizing print annuity.

**What the price implies:** at 9.4x forward earnings and an 11.7% FCF yield, the market is pricing roughly **zero perpetual growth, with a modest decline bias**. That is not an optimistic price. But "not optimistic" is not the same as "cheap." At $31.15 the stock sits at roughly **an 8–10% discount to my $34 base-case value — well short of the 25–30% margin of safety I require.** My buy price is **$25 or below** (≈12–13% FCF yield, ~7.5x forward EPS).

The per-share compounding caveat: with ~5% of shares retired annually at 9x FCF, per-share intrinsic value can grow high-single-digits even with flat *total* FCF. That is real and is why this is a Hold rather than a Sell.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — Below average, in transition

- **Leadership churn:** founder-era discipline is long gone. Enrique Lores departed; **Bruce Broussard is Interim CEO** with a search under way — precisely as memory cost inflation and unit collapse demand decisive execution. Karen Parkhill (CFO since 2024, ex-Medtronic) is the continuity anchor.
- **Capital allocation:** mixed-to-poor over a full cycle. ~$15B of buybacks over five years cut shares 25% while EPS fell from $3.29 to $2.62 — value was returned, but buying back a melting asset at 7–9x did not stop per-share earnings erosion. FY26 repurchases ($1.2B, ~46.7M shares at ~$25.7) were well-timed. Dividends raised every year since 2015 — shareholder-friendly, and coverage is comfortable.
- **M&A:** the $3.3B Poly acquisition (2022) led to a goodwill write-down — a clear capital-destruction data point. Empire-building risk is low going forward (no capacity), but discipline is unproven.
- **Insider ownership:** negligible (<1%), standard for a dispersal-ownership mega-cap; no owner-operator alignment. Governance is board-driven, and the board is mid-search.

Net: honest stewards of cash returns, unexceptional stewards of capital, and currently headless at the top.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

**Not a Buy** because: (1) the price offers ~10% to base-case value, not 25–30%; (2) the moat is narrowing — revenue is flat-to-down over five years, gross margin has fallen 190bps, and the FY26 "growth" is memory-cost pass-through with **PC units down 29%**, which is demand destruction, not momentum; (3) FY27 guidance is explicitly below consensus ($3.15–3.45 EPS, $2.9–3.1B FCF), and the company has no permanent CEO; (4) roughly all segment profit now comes from a structurally declining Printing annuity.

**Not a Sell** because: (1) an 11.7% FCF yield with 4.0% dividend and ~5% annual shrink is a defensible ~9–10% expected return if FCF merely holds; (2) net debt is modest (1.1x EBITDA) and the dividend consumes only ~40% of FCF; (3) at 9.4x earnings the market already discounts perpetual decline, so the bar for a positive surprise is low.

**Action:** Hold existing positions, collect the dividend, do not add. I would become a buyer at **≤$25** (12%+ FCF yield) and a seller on a re-rating toward **$40+** without accompanying FCF growth.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Memory/storage cost shock (the dominant risk).** Memory at ~40% of PC BOM with no pricing power in Personal Systems. Q4 PS operating margin of 3.0% shows HP can pass cost through only by destroying volume. Further DRAM/NAND inflation could push PS to a loss.
2. **Printing annuity erosion accelerating.** Supplies revenue and margin are the whole equity story. A faster decline in pages printed, or aggressive third-party/clone cartridge share in emerging markets, breaks the base case.
3. **Unit decline proves structural, not cyclical.** If the 29% unit drop reflects a permanent step-up in device prices (tariffs + memory) rather than deferred replacement demand, the installed base — and future supplies revenue — shrinks with it.
4. **Leadership vacuum.** An interim CEO through a cost shock raises the odds of strategic drift, a value-destructive acquisition, or a reset of guidance.
5. **Balance-sheet fragility under the hood.** Negative tangible equity (−$8.2B), current ratio 0.81, 155-day payables and $12.3B of inventory (up 11 days q/q). A demand air-pocket forces inventory write-downs and squeezes the negative working-capital float that flatters reported ROIC.
6. **Tariff/trade policy.** FY26 GAAP and non-GAAP EPS each included ~$0.26 of tariff refunds — non-recurring, policy-dependent, and unlikely to repeat in FY27.
7. **Buyback dependency.** With FCF flat and payout already 70%, per-share growth depends on continued repurchases at low multiples; a re-rating toward fair value mechanically reduces the accretion rate.

**Bottom line:** a genuinely cash-generative, high-return-on-capital business with a narrow and narrowing moat, priced at roughly fair value. Buffett's test — "wonderful business at a fair price" — is only half met: the price is fair, the business is not wonderful. Wait for $25.
