# HP Inc. (HPQ) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**As of:** late November 2026 (FY2026 results reported Nov 24, 2026)

---

## 1. Geographic Risk Map

HP describes itself as operating in **more than 180 countries** with 55,000 employees; FY2026 net revenue was **$60.5B (+9.5% YoY)** and FY2027 guidance is $3.15–3.45 non-GAAP EPS (GlobeNewswire/HP release, Nov 24 2026). HP does **not** disclose individual country revenue, so the map below is built from the regional disclosure in the Q1 FY2026 10-Q and the disclosed manufacturing footprint.

| Geography | Revenue exposure | Basis | Risk level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Americas (incl. US)** — largest single market | ~39% | Q1 FY26 10-Q: Americas $5,578M of $14,438M total | **Low–Medium** (tariff/policy, not rule-of-law) |
| **EMEA (incl. EU, UK, Middle East, Africa)** | ~36% | Q1 FY26 10-Q: $5,225M | **Medium** (regulation-heavy; Ukraine/Middle East tail) |
| **Asia-Pacific & Japan (incl. China, India, ASEAN)** | ~25% | Q1 FY26 10-Q: $3,635M; fastest growth (+22% cc in Q3 FY26) | **Medium–High** (US–China, Taiwan Strait, tariff churn) |
| **China** — supply-chain anchor, not separately disclosed as revenue | <10% of revenue (est.; not disclosed) | Production hub: China, Vietnam, Thailand, India, Mexico, US | **High** |
| **Vietnam / Thailand / Mexico / India** — primary "China+1" manufacturing | Cost/margin exposure, not revenue | Supply Chain Dive; WSJ | **Medium–High** (new Section 301 exposure) |

Growth skew matters: in Q3 FY26 constant-currency revenue rose **22% in APJ, 10% in EMEA, 5% in the Americas** — HP's incremental exposure is shifting toward the higher-risk/higher-growth theatre (Investing.com transcript summary).

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① Tariff re-escalation after the IEEPA reversal — Probability: High / Impact: High**
The Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA-based global tariff architecture; the administration responded on Nov 25, 2026 with proposed **Section 301 tariffs on 14 trading partners, with China and Vietnam at 20%**, potentially effective January, on top of ~25% legacy China duties (WSJ; Fox Business; Proactive Investors). This is HP-specific: HP executed one of the most aggressive China+1 programmes in hardware — targeting **>90% of North America-bound product built outside China by end-FY2025**, adding capacity in **Vietnam, Thailand, India, Mexico and the US** (Supply Chain Dive, PCMag, WSJ). Vietnam and Thailand are precisely the named targets of the new action, so the de-risking has partly **relocated** rather than eliminated tariff exposure. Note FY2026 GAAP and non-GAAP EPS included a **$0.26 favourable tariff-refund impact** ($0.15 in Q4) — i.e., ~8% of FY26 non-GAAP EPS was a one-off reversal of duties. FY2027 guidance is "prudent" and does not assume a repeat.

**② The China supply-chain reversal vs. protectionist policy — Probability: High / Impact: Medium–High**
Semafor (Oct 28, 2026) reported HP is **moving some production back to China** to offset surging memory/storage costs driven by AI demand, reversing part of the China+1 plan despite rising Western protectionism. This is a structural tension: HP's cost competitiveness increasingly depends on China just as policy risk rises. HP's own risk factors explicitly name "trade between the United States and other countries where HP products are manufactured or sold, including China," trade sanctions/embargoes, and "tensions in the Taiwan Strait and South China Sea" (FY2025 10-K; FY2026 Q4 release forward-looking statements).

**③ Taiwan Strait / component-cost shock (memory) — Probability: Low–Medium (disruption) / Impact: High**
AI-driven memory demand lifted memory from **~15% to ~40% of PC bill-of-materials in a year** (24/7 Wall St; WSJ). Any Taiwan Strait or Korean peninsula disruption compounds an already thin hardware margin (Q4 PS operating margin 3.0%). HP has no ability to hedge this; it is a pass-through/pricing risk more than a legal one, but it is the single biggest swing factor in the geopolitical bucket.

*Lower-probability tail:* EMEA exposure to Ukraine conflict spillover and Middle East instability (both named in HP's own risk factors); loss of US federal/GSA demand under budget tightening.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Ink-cartridge / "Dynamic Security" antitrust and consumer litigation — trend is strongly favourable to HP.**
- *Robinson v. HP Inc.* (N.D. Ill.) — antitrust class action over firmware blocking third-party cartridges **dismissed with prejudice, Sept 1, 2026** (case 1:24-cv-00164) — the second dismissal after the court threw out amended claims (MLex; The Recycler; TonerNews).
- *In re HP printer ink consumer litigation* — dismissed Oct 2025 (Reuters).
- **EU/UK: Euroconsumers "Printergate"** — coordinated European consumer actions alleging firmware updates falsely signalled cartridge failure; HP has settled analogous claims elsewhere at **$100–150 per consumer** (Euroconsumers). One trade blog claims a **~€10M payment** over cartridge blocking — *unverified, treat as indicative only*.
- HP is also an active **plaintiff**: won an injunction in the District Court of The Hague against Digital Revolution BV over grey-market cartridges (The Recycler), and removed 1,700+ infringing listings in Q1 FY26 (HP newsroom).

**Securities / ERISA litigation.**
- *In re HP Inc. Securities Litigation* (N.D. Cal. 3:20-cv-01260) — HP and officers obtained a major win (Gibson Dunn); investor claims over the printing-supplies downturn failed (Reuters).
- *York County v. HP* (65 F.4th 459, 4th Cir. 2023) — derivative suit tied to HP's SEC channel-inventory settlement; *Hutchins v. HP* is on appeal at the 9th Circuit with briefing complete (Mayer Brown, Jan 2026). Residual, small-dollar risk.

**Regulatory/regulatory-adjacent watch items.**
- **EU Sustainable Consumption Law (in force Jul–Sep 2026)** restricts concealing updates that degrade devices — directly aimed at the firmware/Dynamic Security model HP has litigated over. This converts a litigation risk HP has been *winning* into a **compliance-design** obligation.
- **ESPR/Ecodesign & Digital Product Passport**, EU AI Act (AI PCs now ~60% of HP shipments — Benzinga), and **GDPR/CCPA/PIPL/India DPDP** obligations arising from subscriptions (Instant Ink) and device telemetry.
- **Antitrust**: no confirmed live EU/US monopoly investigation against HP; the cartridge-monopoly theory has now failed twice in US courts, which materially lowers this risk.
- **FCPA/sanctions**: no disclosed DOJ/SEC sanctions or FCPA matter. HP wound down Russia; residual indirect exposure via distributors in Central Asia/Middle East is standard-channel risk, not a flagged issue.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Tariff-refund earnings quality** — $0.26 of FY26 non-GAAP EPS came from duty refunds following the IEEPA ruling; classifying these as recurring would be a modelling error, and a Section 301 re-imposition would reverse the benefit. (Not a compliance breach, but the highest-signal accounting/policy item.)
2. **Firmware/aftermarket practices** — HP has won in US courts but faces live European consumer enforcement and a new EU statute; continued firmware restrictions are a reputational/regulatory friction point.
3. **Supply-chain traceability** — with production re-concentrating in China after a public China+1 commitment, forced-labour/UFLPA-style import screening (Xinjiang-linked suppliers) and country-of-origin certification become live compliance chores.
4. **No material FCPA, AML, or sanctions enforcement identified.** Data-privacy exposure is ordinary-course for a 180-country consumer device fleet.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** Legal risk is genuinely *low and improving* — HP has now defeated the core US ink-cartridge antitrust theory twice, has no disclosed government investigation, and operates almost entirely in high-rule-of-law jurisdictions with no meaningful expropriation or capital-control exposure. The rating is held at Medium, not Low, by **trade policy**: an administration actively rebuilding its tariff architecture via Section 301 against China *and* Vietnam, precisely where HP moved its production, combined with a cost-driven partial return to China and a memory-cost shock. FY2027 guidance embeds essentially none of this.

**Sub-ratings:** Trade/tariff policy — **High**; litigation — **Low**; regulatory (EU product/consumer) — **Medium**; sanctions/FCPA — **Low**; political stability of operating jurisdictions — **Low–Medium**; governance — **Medium** (Interim CEO Bruce Broussard following the CEO transition, during a tariff and cost shock).

**What would change the rating:** (a) final Section 301 rates ≥20% on Vietnam/Thailand with no exclusion for PCs/printers → upgrade to High; (b) a formal EU Commission antitrust or consumer-protection action on cartridge firmware → High; (c) a China–Taiwan escalation disrupting component flow → Very High.

**Data gaps to flag for the memo:** HP does not disclose country-level revenue or its China revenue share; the regional split cited is Q1 FY2026 (10-Q via quarterly report PDF), not full-year FY2026; the FY2026 10-K was not yet available through the filing tool at time of writing (FY2025 10-K used for risk-factor language); the €10M EU settlement figure is from a low-quality trade source and is unverified.
