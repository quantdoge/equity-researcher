## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Narrow (tentative)**

Carvana buys, reconditions, finances, and delivers used cars through a vertically integrated digital platform (~600k retail units in 2025, ~800k annualizing in 2026, 23,100 employees).

**Evidence for a moat:**
- **Cost advantage via scale in reconditioning/logistics.** Ownership of the ADESA auction/IRC network plus in-house logistics is the core of the story: gross margin went from 7.4% (2022) → 16.0% (2023) → 19.8% (2024) → 20.6% (2025). That is a genuine structural improvement, not just price.
- **Brand + customer experience**: NPS-driven repeat/referral, a real intangible in a low-trust category.
- **Operating leverage**: SG&A fell from 19.5% of revenue (2021) to 11.4% (2025) while revenue grew — evidence of a scalable fixed-cost base.

**Evidence against a wide moat:**
- **No switching costs.** A car is purchased every 5–7 years. There is no recurring revenue, no lock-in, no network effect. Price is the product.
- **No demonstrable pricing power.** GPU is already compressing: gross margin fell from 20.6% (FY25) to 18.9% in Q3-26 (gross profit $1,521M / revenue $8,040M), and operating margin from 9.8% to 8.9%, *despite* 42% revenue growth. Bulls call this mix; I call it the first evidence that scale does not confer pricing power in a commoditized good.
- **Competition is enormous and well-capitalized**: CarMax, AutoNation, tens of thousands of franchise/dealer groups, Cars.com/Carvox, private-party listings, and — at the margin — Amazon.
- **Two years of profit ≠ a moat.** The company was days from restructuring in 2022 (negative equity of -$1.05B, negative FCF of -$1.8B). FY2023–25 profitability coincided with an unusually favorable used-vehicle pricing environment and a housing-like supply shortage of 2–6 year-old cars.

**ROIC test:** ROIC of 19.3% (2025), 13.5% (2024), -1.3% (2023), -19.2% (2022) versus a WACC I estimate at 10–11% given beta 3.55 and $5.5B of debt. Above cost of capital for **two** years only. That is not yet the multi-cycle, high-return-on-capital record a wide moat requires.

**Verdict:** A real, improving, *narrow* advantage in reconditioning cost per unit and logistics density — the "efficient scale" moat, and it is one of the harder kinds to sustain. Not a franchise.

- **Share count up ~30% in three years** (546M → 688M → 721M weighted).

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Multiples (current):** P/E 40.4x (on inflated FY25 EPS) — but **~62x on annualized 2026 earnings** (~$1.10/share). EV/Adj-EBITDA **27x** on FY26 guidance of $2.7–3.0B. P/FCF 86x; FCF yield **1.2%** and falling. P/B 18.2x. No dividend, no buyback. Peer median P/E is 29.0x — CVNA carries a ~40% premium on trailing earnings and a far larger premium forward.

**Owner earnings (my build, FY2026E):** operating income ~$2.45B + D&A $0.28B − maintenance capex $0.28B − cash tax @ 25% ≈ **$1.9B** of total enterprise owner earnings (roughly equal to 2025's $1.895B consolidated net income).

**DCF scenarios (WACC 10.5–11%, terminal 2–3%):**

| Scenario | Growth path | Equity value | Per share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bear (GPU normalizes to cycle, margin reverts) | 15% → 4% | $37B | **$33** |
| Base (durable execution, modest margin drift) | 20% → 8% | $57B | **$51** |
| Bull (3M-unit runway realized, margins hold 10%+) | 30% → 12% | $110B | **$98** |
| **Probability-weighted (30/45/25)** | | **$64B** | **≈$57** |

**What the current price implies:** at $76.6B market cap you need ~$4.0B of durable owner earnings (at a 20x terminal multiple) to earn a 10% return — roughly **double** my 2026 estimate, delivered without margin slippage, while gross margins are already compressing. Achievable in the bull case; not the base case.

**Conclusion:** Fair value ≈ **$51–57/share** (range $33–98). The stock trades ~20–34% *above* intrinsic value. It fails the margin-of-safety test outright; a 25% discount to base case implies buying below ~$38–43.

## 4. Management Quality: **6 / 10**

**Positives:** Ernie Garcia III has executed one of the more remarkable operational turnarounds in recent retail history — cutting $2B+ of cost, right-sizing inventory, and restoring profitability without a value-destroying equity rescue. Founder-led; the Garcia family retains voting control via Class B and has a large personal stake. Capital intensity is being kept low. Debt reduction of $5.2B demonstrates real discipline versus the 2021–22 empire-building era (the ADESA acquisition at the top of the cycle was value-destructive).

**Negatives:**
- **Related-party complexity**: the founding family's DriveTime relationship is a persistent governance question, and the company has been the subject of public short-seller accounting allegations — regardless of merit, that raises the analytical burden of proof.
- **No per-share discipline**: 30% dilution in three years, zero buybacks, no dividend. Capital is reinvested at high incremental returns (acceptable) but earnings growth has partly been bought with shares.
- **Aggressive framing**: adjusted EBITDA and GPU metrics invite optimism; management has historically guided the market with "efficiency" narratives that reversed.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate; existing holders may hold or trim)

This is a genuinely better business than it was three years ago, run by capable operators with a large addressable runway. But Buffett's test is not "is it a good business?" — it is "is it a good business available at a sensible price?" At 27x EV/EBITDA, ~62x forward earnings, and a 1.2% FCF yield, with gross margins already rolling over, **the market has paid in full for the bull case.** The turnaround is now consensus (six of six recent analyst targets cluster at $75–100, implied upside a modest ~15–25% even to the most bullish).

I would become a buyer with conviction in the **low-$40s**, where the base case offers a real margin of safety and the bear case no longer implies permanent capital loss.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **GPU / margin normalization (highest-probability risk).** Gross margin has already fallen 170bps YoY in Q3-26. If this is cyclical (used-vehicle supply normalizing, incentives weakening), earnings could compress 30–40% from here.
2. **Credit cycle exposure.** Carvana originates ~$12.8B of receivables annually and sells them. A deterioration in consumer credit or ABS spreads hits *both* gain-on-sale revenue and funding costs simultaneously — the 2022 playbook.
3. **Used-vehicle price cycle.** Manheim values are the single largest driver of GPU and inventory marks; the current supply "valley" in 2–6-year-old vehicles will close by ~2028.
4. **Interest rate / refinancing risk.** $5.5B of debt and $505M of annual interest; beta 3.55.
5. **Valuation risk.** At 27x EBITDA, any growth deceleration de-rates the multiple and the earnings simultaneously — the classic double hit.
6. **Governance/accounting.** Related-party DriveTime dealings and prior short-seller allegations require ongoing scrutiny of receivables accounting.
7. **Dilution.** Continued equity issuance for growth or acquisitions transfers value away from the owner.

**Bottom line:** Good-to-very-good business, *tentative* narrow moat, competent but not shareholder-obsessed management, and a price that already reflects success. Quality improving; valuation full. **Hold — wait for Mr. Market to offer a discount.**
