# Carvana Co. (NYSE: CVNA) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $68.31 (29-Oct-2026) | Market cap ~$75B | EV ~$79.3B | Beta 3.55*

---

## 1. Growth Story

Carvana is no longer the "bankruptcy-to-comeback" story — it is now a **scale-up story**. In Q3 2026 it sold a record **217,385 retail units (+39.4% YoY)** at a record $8.04B revenue and $800M Adjusted EBITDA, putting it on a >$3B EBITDA run-rate, roughly double two years ago. The thesis: Carvana has cracked the unit economics of online used-vehicle retail (20%+ gross margins vs 7% in 2022) and is now converting that proof into **volume**, leveraging acquired ADESA auction/reconditioning sites to push capacity toward management's stated mid-term target of **3 million retail units/year at a 13.5% Adjusted EBITDA margin by 2030–35** — versus ~870k units annualized today. Meanwhile the stock is **~30% off its $97.38 January high** and down ~31% YTD at its October trough, as GPU compression de-rated the multiple even as revenue accelerated. That divergence is the opportunity.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **Core TAM:** US used-vehicle retail ≈ **36–40M units/year, ~$1.3–1.5T** transaction value. Carvana at ~870k annualized units holds **~2.3% share** — penetration is still de minimis for a 14-year-old company.
- **Path:** 3M units = **~8% unit share**. Consensus 2030 revenue of $60.4B ÷ today's ~$37k revenue per retail unit implies only **~1.6M units** — i.e. the market is pricing roughly **54% of management's own ambition**. Management does not need to hit 3M units to justify today's price; it needs ~1.6M.
- **Adjacent TAM not in consensus:** ADESA wholesale auctions (~9–10M wholesale units/yr), captive-adjacent F&I/finance origination, reconditioning-as-a-service, and eventual new-vehicle adjacency. This is the optionality Cathie Wood-style analysis pays for.
- **Differentiation:** vertically integrated reconditioning + logistics + proprietary 360° vehicle imaging + national delivery, at a GPU ($6,997) that CarMax-scale peers structurally cannot match online. ADESA's 56 sites enable "2M+ incremental annual retail units" per company materials.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Q4 2026 earnings — ~17-Feb-2027** *(highest impact)*: Guidance is for **sequential retail unit growth** and **FY26 Adjusted EBITDA at the high end of $2.7–3.0B**. Watch the GPU bridge: stabilization here re-rates; another $300+ GPU decline re-de-rates.
- **Reconditioning cost normalization (H1 2027)**: the Q4-2025 reconditioning cost overrun is the specific trigger that started the 2026 de-rating. Evidence of normalization is the single cleanest catalyst.
- **ADESA capacity ramp**: conversion of auction sites into reconditioning hubs toward 2M+ incremental units — watch concrete hub openings/announcements.
- **Rate path / Fed cuts**: a dual catalyst — lowers consumer monthly payments (demand elasticity) *and* Carvana's own interest expense (already down to $98M/qtr from $148M in Q4-24). Rate-hike fears were cited in the 2026 selloff.
- **Long-term target refresh**: any firming of the 3M-unit / 13.5% Adj. EBITDA margin objective with dated milestones (2030 vs 2035).

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY**

**Why Buy (not Strong Buy):** The growth engine is real and accelerating — 39% unit growth, +43% revenue guided, Rule of 40 of 53, a PEG near 0.9, and a market-implied 2030 unit count that is *less than 60% of management's own target*. At 2.7x 2026E sales and ~11x 2027E gross profit for a business compounding revenue at 25%+ through 2028, the multiple is no longer demanding after a 30% drawdown.

**Why not Strong Buy:** The **quality of growth has decayed** — GP growth (+32.5%) now trails revenue (+42.4%), GPU is down $365/unit YoY, and the mix is shifting toward low-margin wholesale/judication volume. FCF conversion is weak (4.4% margin, 86x P/FCF) because growth consumes working capital (-$480M in 2025). Until GPU stabilizes for two consecutive quarters, the de-rating risk remains live.

**The trade:** You are buying ~1.6M units of 2030 volume at a discount to management's 3M target, with a free option on ADESA wholesale and F&I. But size it as a high-beta (3.55) position — this name has fallen 42% in a tariff shock and 41% in a yield shock.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **GPU compression is structural, not cyclical (primary risk).** Retail GPU held at $3,547 but the finance/ancillary line fell to $2,666. If GPU keeps falling, gross profit growth decelerates below revenue growth and the "growth" becomes low-quality volume — the exact bear case the market de-rated for in 2026.
2. **Consumer credit / subprime auto exposure.** Financing-partner ABS execution and loss trends are outside Carvana's control; renewed subprime concern was an explicit 2026 selloff driver.
3. **Rate and macro sensitivity.** Beta of 3.55; the stock fell 42% in the 2025 tariff shock vs. -19% for the S&P 500. Used-vehicle depreciation and tariffs hit both inventory values and demand.
4. **Valuation has no margin of safety.** 42x 2026E P/E and 14x EV/gross profit price in continued execution. A single guide-down compresses the multiple 25%+.
5. **Balance sheet & dilution.** Concentrated convertible structure: 2025 diluted share count of 1.12B vs 708M basic. Interest expense ~$400M/yr still exceeds nothing trivial despite improvement.
6. **Competitive/AI disruption.** CarMax and EchoPark compete on scale; emerging AI shopping agents could disintermediate the front-end discovery layer Carvana's brand and UX currently own.
7. **Execution & governance history.** The 2023 Hindenburg allegations and prior state licensing disputes (Illinois, Michigan) mean accounting/regulatory credibility carries a residual discount. Note: vendor income statement shows Q3 net income of $289M vs the company's reported $483M — a reconciling non-operating item worth verifying in the 10-Q.
