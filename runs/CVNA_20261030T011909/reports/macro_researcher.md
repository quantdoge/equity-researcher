# Macroeconomic Assessment — Carvana Co. (CVNA)
**As of 30 October 2026** | Consumer Cyclical / Auto Dealerships | Market cap ~$74.9B | Beta 3.55

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: TRANSITIONAL — a "hawkish re-acceleration" regime. Risk-on in credit, risk-off in duration.**

The 2024–25 easing cycle is over. The Fed cut from 5.33% to ~3.63% through H1 2026, then **hiked 25bp on 16 September 2026** (IORB to 3.90%) and **held at 3.75–4.00% on 28 October 2026** by a 10–2 vote under Chair Warsh, with the statement flagging that "inflation remains elevated." CPI re-accelerated to **+3.7% YoY in September** (from 2.65% in December 2025), with the last three months annualising near **4.5%**. The long end has repriced violently: the **10Y is 5.05%** (28 Oct), up **+61bp from 4.44% on 30 June** and near the cycle high of 5.31% (5 Oct); the **30Y sits at 5.37%**.

Crucially, the curve has **bear-steepened** — 2Y 4.65% vs fed funds midpoint 3.875%, i.e. **~78bp of further hikes already priced**. That is the opposite of the 2024 inversion signal: this is a term-premium/fiscal steepener, which is the worst possible shape for long-duration, credit-funded consumer equities.

The economy itself is *not* the problem: real GDP grew ~2.1% YoY in Q3 (+3.4% SAAR), unemployment is **4.2%** and falling from 4.5% in November 2025, and nominal consumer spending is **+6.3% YoY** ($22.45T). But **University of Michigan sentiment collapsed to 48.1 in September — near the all-time record low** (vs 71.7 in January 2025). The misery index (CPI + unemployment) is **7.9%**, up from ~6.6% a year ago. This is a textbook stagflation-lite squeeze on big-ticket discretionary purchases financed with credit — precisely Carvana's product.

Credit markets are the disconnect: **HY OAS at 314bp (28 Oct) is near cycle tights**, yet it has **widened +49bp since 1 September (265bp)**. Spreads are complacent in absolute terms but deteriorating at the margin — and the underlying auto ABS market is worse than HY implies (see §3).

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE** (mild, not recessionary)

Three of the four highest-sensitivity channels for this specific business model — **long-end rates, subprime ABS funding/credit performance, and forward Fed path** — are all moving the wrong way simultaneously, and they compound: higher rates both raise Carvana's cost of funds *and* compress the multiple on its earnings. Record-low consumer sentiment removes the demand cushion.

The rating is deliberately **not** "Severely Unfavourable," because the offsets are genuine and structural rather than cyclical: tariffs inflate new-car prices (used-demand tailwind), 4.2% unemployment is keeping losses from spiralling, ~40% unit growth proves share capture, and the balance sheet is at its best net-debt position in ~5 years — which means Carvana is *funding* itself through this regime rather than being forced to raise capital into it.

Net: macro is a **~1-turn headwind to the multiple and a modest headwind to finance GPU**, offset by a favourable industry supply/price structure. Company execution is currently outrunning the macro, but that gap is the risk.

---

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BEAR**

**The consensus is hawkish-but-orderly. It is not pricing a credit event.**

HY OAS at **314bp** and equities near highs imply the market believes the Fed's September hike was pre-emptive and subprime stress is contained. The non-consensus risk: **the Fed hikes again in December 2026 into an already-deteriorating subprime consumer**, pushing the 10Y through 5.31% toward 5.50–5.75%, at which point the **auto ABS market — not the HY market — is where the break occurs.** Subprime 60+ DPD already printed a record 6.9%; if unemployment moves from 4.2% to 5.0% and used values (which Cox already cut to +0.2%) roll over, recovery rates fall just as required credit enhancement rises.

For Carvana the transmission is brutal and fast, because it is simultaneous on three fronts:
1. **Finance GPU collapses** — gain-on-sale compresses and Carvana must retain/subordinate more risk on its own balance sheet;
2. **Warehouse/floorplan costs reprice** upward at the same moment;
3. **The multiple de-rates further** as a 3.55-beta name is discounted at a higher rate.

A **−40% to −50% drawdown from $68** (back toward the March 2026 low of $55 and below) is plausible in that scenario — and note the stock is *already* −30% from its January high on record results, which tells you how violently the discount-rate channel operates here.

**The mirror-image bull surprise** (lower probability, high magnitude): if the September hike proves to be the terminal one and CPI rolls to ~2.5% as tariff price levels lap and used-vehicle supply normalises, the 10Y retraces to ~4.40%, ABS spreads re-tighten, and CVNA re-rates off a 40%-growth, positive-FCF base — a +50% move. The asymmetry favours owning the *rates* view rather than the *credit* view: CVNA's beta to the 10Y is currently a far more powerful driver than its beta to used-car volumes.
