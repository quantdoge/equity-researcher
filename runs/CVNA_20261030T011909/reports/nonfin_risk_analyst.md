## NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — CARVANA CO. (NYSE: CVNA)
**Date of review:** post-Q3 2026 (10-Q filed 28-Oct-2026) | **Sector:** Consumer Cyclical / Auto Dealerships | **Market cap:** ~$75B | **Beta:** 3.55

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

This is the single most important non-financial finding on CVNA. Evidence:

- **Controlled company with super-voting structure.** The Garcia family controls roughly 80–85% of voting power via Class B super-voting shares, despite a far smaller economic stake. Independent shareholders cannot meaningfully discipline management or the board — a structural takeover/proxy defence that entrenches the founding family.
- **CEO/Chairman duality.** Ernest C. Garcia III is simultaneously President, CEO **and Chairman** (confirmed on the Q3-2026 CEO certification filed with the 10-Q). A Lead Independent Director exists (Paul Conte) and John Mack chairs Audit, but a combined Chair/CEO plus family voting control concentrates power to a degree rarely acceptable at a $75B S&P 500 constituent.
- **Audit committee independence is contested.** Hindenburg (Jan-2025) alleged that two members of the "independent" audit committee previously served on the board of **DriveTime**, the family-controlled related party, and that member Greg Sullivan had been suspended by the NYSE over a payment to Garcia II in contravention of a prohibition order. I could not independently verify these specific allegations, but the company has not, to my knowledge, publicly rebutted them with board-level detail.
- **Control-person conflicts embedded in the board.** Director Dan Quayle's Cerberus affiliation was alleged to sit behind a ~$800M "unrelated third party" loan purchaser — the core of the undisclosed-related-party allegation.
- **Serial related-party architecture.** Carvana, DriveTime, Bridgecrest Acceptance and GoFi are all Garcia-family-linked and share the **same auditor (Grant Thornton)**. Carvana's own 10-K concedes: "We are party to a number of arrangements with DriveTime and its affiliates that **cannot be assumed to have been negotiated at arm's length**."
- **Insider pledge risk.** 13D/A disclosures show Garcia-linked Paired Interests and Class B shares **pledged as collateral** against call-option arrangements — forced-liquidation risk on top of ordinary selling.

**Mitigants:** the FY2025 10-K was in fact filed on schedule and no restatement has occurred, contradicting Gotham City's most aggressive predictions; management reports ICFR effective (Justice/service/audit-opinion detail not independently verified here).

### 3. Insider Activity Analysis — **PERSISTENT NET SELLING; NO OPEN-MARKET BUYING IDENTIFIED**

- **Roughly 40 Form 4 filings in the ~5 months from June to October 2026**, dominated by recurring filer groups (Garcia-family entities incl. CIK filer 0001607889, and multiple director/officer groups filing on the same dates). The cadence is consistent with systematic 10b5-1 or monthly vesting-and-sell programmes rather than one-off conviction selling — but it is relentless.
- **Ernest Garcia II (father, largest shareholder, DriveTime CEO):** ~$3.6B sold Aug-2020–Aug-2021 (immediately before a 99% drawdown); a further ~$1.4B sold 2023–2025; $245M sale filed Jul-2025. He remains a multi-billion-dollar net seller across cycles. This is the clearest historical "sell-before-the-fall" pattern in the file.
- **Ernest Garcia III (CEO/Chairman):** sold >$36M in October (reported weighted-average prices ~$178–190 pre-split); a further 7,067 shares withheld for taxes on RSU vesting 1-Oct-2026. Direct holdings ~4.71M shares post-transaction.
- **Other officers:** President–Special Projects Thomas Taira sold 5,597 shares for ~$364k on 12-Oct-2026; CFO Mark Jenkins reported small sales (e.g., 120 shares, 2-Jan-2026).
- **Directional conclusion: net selling, decisively.** The only insider *buying* I found was Lead Independent Director Michael Maroone's ~$1.5M purchase — at roughly **$12.36 per share**, i.e. the 2023 trough, not the current level. There is no evidence of insider accumulation anywhere near current prices.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Key-person and dynastic dependency.** Growth, capital allocation, and the entire related-party financing ecosystem route through one family. The CEO is also Chairman and controls the vote; his father controls the loan servicer and the captive-finance counterparty. There is no credible succession plan visible, and a Garcia-liquidity event (margin calls on pledged shares) would transmit directly into the equity.
2. **"Originate-to-sell" funding concentration and counterparty fragility.** Historically ~60% of originations went to a single buyer (Ally) with a purchase commitment amended five times amid redaction of key terms; the new buyers are opaque. If a counterparty withdraws financing for loans with 80%+ of non-prime ABS collateral in deep-subprime FICO bands, the revenue model — not just margins — is impaired. Given beta of 3.55, the equity discounts this abruptly.
3. **Technology, security, and AI-driven operating leverage.** A September-2026 breach exposing licence numbers is exactly the data set that enables identity theft and title fraud. Simultaneously, CVNA is aggressively substituting AI agents into customer service — cost-efficient, but it removes human error-correction from precisely the title, registration, and paperwork workflows that generated the Connecticut AG action and ~1,654 BBB complaints closed in 12 months.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **HIGH**

- **A three-front credibility war.** Gotham City Research (28-Jan-2026: 2023–24 earnings overstated by >$1B) and (18-Feb-2026: Bridgecrest/undisclosed transactions) followed Hindenburg's 2025 "father-son accounting grift" thesis. Management's response — that the reports are "100% inaccurate" and a "friendly request" to investors to "recognise the pattern" of short reports during quiet periods — is aggressive and will itself be scrutinised if the SEC matter advances. A 14% single-day drop (28-Jan-2026) shows how thin the trust buffer is.
- **Consumer backlash is documented, not hypothetical:** hundreds of Connecticut complaints, multi-year title/registration failures, and a BBB complaint file in the thousands. This is the kind of friction that erodes Net Promoter Score and repeat purchase in a business that depends on word-of-mouth.
- **Guilt by association:** the Mark Walter DOJ probe dragged CVNA down ~10–14% in a single week (Aug-2026) for reasons entirely outside management's control. Expect this to recur.
- **Positives:** record Q3-2026 revenue (>$8B), ~40% unit growth, FY2025 net income ~$1.9B, S&P 500 inclusion, and a clean audit opinion — the operating narrative is genuinely strong, which is what keeps the reputational damage contained to *valuation* rather than *solvency*.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not **Critical**, because: the SEC has filed no enforcement action; the FY2025 10-K was filed on time with no restatement; ICFR is reported effective; net debt is the best in ~5 years after the Aug-2026 $1.66B Term Loan B refinancing; and the underlying operating performance is verifiably excellent. But the combination of a **weak governance structure, an unresolved SEC subpoena, an unaudited-leaning related-party ecosystem, and continuous insider distribution** is precisely the configuration that has preceded permanent capital loss in this name before.

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🔴 CRITICAL — Unresolved SEC subpoena (June 2025)** tied to related-party and revenue-recognition allegations. Any escalation to a formal order or Wells notice is a de-rating event and a potential restatement risk. Require: quarterly confirmation of status; read the legal-proceedings footnote in every 10-Q.
2. **🔴 CRITICAL — Governance is structurally unfixable near term.** ~85% voting control, CEO/Chairman duality, and a contested audit committee mean shareholders have no remedy. Position sizing, not engagement, is the only risk tool here.
3. **🔴 CRITICAL — Undisclosed/under-disclosed related-party economics (DriveTime / Bridgecrest / GoFi).** Same auditor across all entities destroys the independence that is supposed to validate the arm's-length claim. Treat reported gross profit as having an unquantified related-party component.
4. **🟠 HIGH — Continuous insider distribution with zero insider buying at current prices**, including the largest shareholder whose prior selling episodes immediately preceded a 99% decline.
5. **🟠 HIGH — Mark Walter / TWG Global DOJ probe and ~$2B pledged stake.** A forced liquidation is a live, dated catalyst outside company control.
6. **🟠 HIGH — Pledged Garcia-family shares.** Margin-call mechanics could force selling at the worst possible moment.
7. **🟡 MONITOR — September-2026 data breach** (licence numbers; state AG notifications; class action) and the January-2026 tracking-software privacy suit — first evidence of a genuine cyber/privacy control gap.
8. **🟡 MONITOR — Consumer-protection enforcement creep** beyond Connecticut (title/registration, seller payments, misrepresentation).
