## 1. Geographic Risk Map

Carvana is a **single-country business**. Its 10-K describes the platform as facilitating purchase and sale of pre-owned vehicles "across the United States," with no disclosed foreign subsidiaries, foreign revenue, or cross-border operations. E-commerce retail, wholesale auctions (ADESA), reconditioning ("IRCs"), and logistics are all domestic. Geographic revenue disclosure is effectively **~100% United States**.

| Geography | Revenue exposure | Risk level | Driver |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States (all 50 states + DC)** | ~100% | **Low–Medium** | Not a geopolitical risk — a **regulatory-fragmentation** risk. Dealer licensing, title/registration, doc fees, advertising and financing rules are set state-by-state. |
| Arizona (HQ/IRCs) | Cost center | Low | Stable, business-friendly jurisdiction |
| No EM / no Russia / no China revenue | 0% | **Very Low** | No sanctions, currency-control, repatriation, or expropriation exposure |
| Indirect import exposure | n/a | **Medium** | Vehicle sourcing and parts (reconditioning) touch cross-border trade flows subject to Section 232 |

**Key point for the investment memo:** CVNA screens as **low geopolitical risk** on every conventional axis (no FX translation, no sanctions nexus, no nationalisation risk, no emerging-market volatility). The beta of 3.55 reflects **consumer credit and rate sensitivity**, not country risk. The real geographic risk is *sub-national*: 50+ separate dealer-licensing regimes, each with revocation power over a business that cannot sell a car without a license.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

### Risk 1 — US auto tariff regime distorting used-vehicle pricing and parts costs
**Probability: High (in force) | Impact: Medium**

Section 232 automotive tariffs (25%) remain intact and **explicitly cover used passenger vehicles and trucks**, with only a 25-years-or-older exclusion ([CBP Section 232 FAQ](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/entry-summary/section-232-additional-faqs-autos)). Additional layers landed in 2026 — a Section 301 layer effective July 24, 2026, and new Section 232 tariffs on trucks/parts from November 1, 2026 ([WCS](https://www.wcshipping.com/import-car-tariffs), [Butzel](https://www.butzel.com/alert-new-section-232-tariffs-on-trucks-truck-parts-and-buses-take-effect-november-1-but-the-auto-tariff-offset-expanded-to-support-suppliers)). A Supreme Court tariff ruling left Section 232 standing ([Caribou](https://caribou.com/resource-center/auto-refinance/what-the-supreme-courts-tariff-ruling-means-for-car-prices-and-your-car-loan/)).

**Transmission to CVNA:** (a) tariffs raise new-car prices, which *supports* used-vehicle values and CVNA's retail GPU — a tailwind; (b) but they raise **reconditioning parts costs** and any imported-vehicle sourcing cost — a margin headwind; (c) the net effect is a *price-level* shock that lifts revenue per unit while compressing the affordability of CVNA's subprime-weighted customer base. Policy reversibility is a two-sided swing factor on used-car pricing. No direct tariff line item is disclosed, so magnitude is unquantified.

### Risk 2 — State dealer-licensing and consumer-protection enforcement (multi-front, recurring)
**Probability: High (ongoing pattern) | Impact: Medium**

This is the most *repeated* regulatory risk in the name and it is not one-off:
- **Connecticut AG settlement, Jan 2025** — $1.5M total ($1M consumer restitution fund + $500K penalty, half suspended) following "hundreds of consumer complaints" over title/registration delays ([CT AG](https://portal.ct.gov/ag/press-releases/2025-press-releases/attorney-general-tong-announces-settlement-with-carvana-following-hundreds-of-consumer-complaints))
- **Georgia** — $250,000 civil penalty plus compliance undertaking under the FBPA ([GA Consumer Protection](https://consumer.georgia.gov/organization/about-us/accomplishments-0))
- **$850,000** paid in a four-county civil action for transacting **without proper licenses**
- **Illinois** — dealer license suspended (2022) over misuse of out-of-state temp permits, later restored by court order; **Michigan** investigation into operating without a license; Florida and Texas complaints ([WJXT/News4JAX](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IVt2MaXz4E8), [CarEdge](https://caredge.com/guides/carvana-lawsuit))

**Why it matters:** license revocation is an existential-per-state event with near-zero warning, and title/registration failures are a *systemic* byproduct of the model (Carvana holds titles across state lines at scale). Each settlement is individually immaterial (<$2M) but the pattern invites a multistate AG action and creates a template for private class actions.

### Risk 3 — Securities-fraud litigation and accounting-credibility overhang
**Probability: Medium-High | Impact: High**

*In re Carvana Co. Securities Litigation*, No. 2:22-cv-02126-MTL (D. Ariz.) — a 229-page consolidated complaint seeking **>$1.2B in proceeds**, class period May 6, 2020–June 24, 2022. Defendants' motions to dismiss were **defeated in 2024** and reconsideration denied (Robbins Geller). **Discovery closed May 29, 2026; dispositive motions due November 4, 2026** — meaning a summary-judgment ruling is imminent and trial-capable ([CourtListener docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/66654699/in-re-carvana-co-securities-litigation/), [RGRD](https://www.rgrdlaw.com/news-item-Fraud-Suit-Against-Carvana-Moves-Forward.html)).

Aggravating factors: a **Special Master was appointed August 25, 2026** to review in camera documents withheld as privileged, and the court ordered **follow-up depositions on thousands of previously withheld documents** — Carvana avoided sanctions but the privilege-handling episode is a credibility negative ([Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/no-sanctions-for-carvana-in-artificial-stock-inflation-case)). This traces to the Hindenburg Research report (Jan 2025 — Hindenburg's final report before shutting down) and Gotham City Research's related-party allegations regarding the Garcia family and DriveTime.

**Mitigant:** the parallel Delaware insider-trading claim against Ernest Garcia II was **affirmed dismissed** by the Delaware Supreme Court (*Schertz v. Garcia II*, C.A. No. 2023-0600-KSJM; appeal No. 446, 2024) — a meaningful adverse-signal reduction on the related-party narrative ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/carvana-ceos-father-wins-again-over-insider-trading-allegations)).

**Active matters**
| Matter | Forum | Status | Exposure |

| Delaware derivative/insider claims | Del. Ch./Sup. Ct. | **Dismissed; affirmed on appeal** | Resolved favorably |

**Regulatory posture — two live vectors:**
1. **FTC**: The CARS Rule was **vacated by the Fifth Circuit in January 2025**, removing a major pending compliance burden ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/02/fifth-circuit-strikes-down-ftcs-auto-retail-scam-rule)). But the FTC **re-entered auto-dealer enforcement in March 2026**, sending warning letters to 97 dealerships (publicly named May 28, 2026) on pricing/advertising ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/auto-dealers-the-ftc-is-back-in-the-drivers-seatwarning-letters-signal-renewed-federal-scrutiny)). CVNA was not identified in that tranche, but as the largest online dealer it is a natural target for the next wave.
2. **CFPB / auto finance**: CVNA originates loans and sells finance receivables (Ally commitment raised to **up to $8.0B** through July 2027; $4.5B of fixed-pool loan sales completed in 9M26). Servicing runs through **Bridgecrest**, a DriveTime affiliate. Precedent: **CFPB v. DriveTime — $8M civil money penalty** over unfair debt collection ([CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/enforcement/actions/drivetime/)). Add-on product and servicing practices are the live CFPB theory.

**Disclosed aggregate legal accruals are not material** relative to $21.8B 9M26 revenue, but the securities case is unaccrued litigation risk, not a reserved liability.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# **MEDIUM**

**Decomposition:**
- **Geopolitical risk: LOW.** No foreign revenue, no sanctions nexus, no expropriation or currency-control exposure. The only geopolitical channel is US trade policy acting *indirectly* through used-vehicle and parts pricing — and it currently cuts both ways.
- **Legal/regulatory risk: MEDIUM-HIGH.** Three simultaneous fronts (federal securities class action at summary judgment, open SEC subpoena, serial state AG/licensing enforcement) plus a re-energised FTC and an active CFPB posture toward auto finance. Mitigated by the favorable Delaware appellate ruling and by the small absolute size of the state settlements relative to a ~$30B+ run-rate revenue base.

**What would move the rating:**
- **To HIGH:** an adverse summary-judgment ruling or certified class in *In re Carvana* (Nov 2026+); a formal SEC enforcement action; or a multistate AG action / license revocation in a top-5 volume state (CA, TX, FL, NY, PA).
- **To LOW:** SEC matter closed without action, securities case settled within insurance limits, and two consecutive years of clean state licensing exams.
