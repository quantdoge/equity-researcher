## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-Off / Transitional, with a hawkish policy shock underway**

The US is in an unusual, late-cycle configuration: **growth is re-accelerating while inflation is re-accelerating faster, and the Fed has resumed hiking.** Real GDP grew ~3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026 after a near-stall in Q4 2025 (+0.2%), yet CPI has climbed from 2.4% YoY in February 2026 to **3.82% in October 2026** (+140bp in eight months), with core CPI running +0.3% m/m for three consecutive months. The Fed cut to 3.63% by mid-2026, then reversed: effective fed funds is **3.88% (Oct 2026)** and the September 16 FOMC statement explicitly cited inflation "remain[ing] elevated." Reuters (Nov 10) reports officials "open door to a quicker round of rate hikes," with futures beginning to price a possible 50bp move.

Critically, **the 2Y (4.74%) sits 86bp above effective fed funds** — the market is pricing several more hikes, not cuts. The curve has bear-steepened to +39bp (2s10s), with the 30Y at **5.47%** and 30Y mortgages back at **7.11%** after touching 5.98% in February. This is a term-premium/fiscal-risk steepening, not a growth signal — bad for long-duration equity multiples.

The sting is that **this inflation is largely energy- and tariff-driven, not demand-driven**: CPI (3.8%) exceeds core PCE (3.1%), and gasoline is **$4.00/gal, +31% YoY** (vs. $2.78 in January 2026). Hiking into a supply-shock inflation raises policy-error risk. Meanwhile University of Michigan sentiment has **collapsed to 48.1** (Sept 2026) from 74.0 in Dec 2024 — households are miserable despite 4.1% unemployment. That divergence (solid GDP, low unemployment, record-low sentiment, rising fuel costs) is precisely the setup that squeezes a value-oriented rural consumer.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact (TSCO)

Tractor Supply is a ~2,500-store, **~99% domestic** rural lifestyle retailer with needs-based consumables (feed, pet, animal health) roughly 55-60% of sales, big-ticket/seasonal (tractors, trailers, fencing, outdoor power) the balance. Q3 2026: sales +5.1% to $3.91B, **comps only +0.4%**, digital +16%, gross margin pressured, FY26 guide +2.5–3.5% sales. Beta 0.484; $19.7B cap; $37.95 post-split, dividend $0.24/qtr (~2.5% yield).

**Note on price action:** TSCO bottomed at **$28.94 on June 3, 2026** and has rallied **+31% to $37.95** while the 10Y rose 64bp — i.e., the stock is currently *decoupling* from the macro rate signal on idiosyncratic catalysts (tariff refunds, cost cuts, the Nov 12 Uber Eats marketplace partnership, short covering). That is a fragility: a further leg higher in yields removes the multiple support the rally has been leaning on. The stock is still **-27% YoY**.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Three forces dominate, and all three point the same way for this specific business model: (1) a Fed that has restarted hiking with the 2Y pricing more, (2) a long end at 5.1–5.5% that de-rates low-beta, dividend-paying consumer compounders and freezes rural real estate, and (3) **gasoline +31% YoY hitting a customer who is defined by drive distance and fuel-intensive rural life**. Against that, TSCO has only a +0.4% comp and a declining gross margin. The offsets are real but defensive in character — needs-based consumables share, trade-down behavior, a stronger dollar lowering import costs, and self-help cost cuts. Rating: **Unfavourable**, mitigated but not rescued by its low beta and 2.5% yield.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**Bull surprise (higher probability than the market assigns): a growth scare forces a Fed pause and a 75bp rally in the long end.**
The hikes are being administered into an *energy/tariff-driven* inflation with sentiment already at 48 and Q4 2025 growth having stalled at 0.2%. If Q4 2026 GDP prints below 1% and credit spreads widen, the Fed's September–October hikes look like a policy error. In that world: 10Y back to ~4.40%, mortgage to ~6.4%, and gasoline mean-reverting toward $3.20. For a rural household driving 25,000 miles/yr, that gasoline move alone is worth **~$1,000 of annual discretionary cash flow** — and TSCO, at a depressed multiple with 0.4% comps baked in, is exactly the low-beta defensive value name that money rotates *into* when AI/long-duration growth unwinds. A +25–30% re-rating is plausible and the setup is under-owned (stock still -27% YoY).

**Bear surprise (also live): the Fed delivers 50bp in December.** 10Y to 6%, mortgage to 7.75%, gasoline to $4.50. Rural big-ticket demand breaks outright, comps turn negative, tariff pass-through is blocked by a value customer, and guidance (currently +2.5–3.5% sales) is cut. Target back to the June low of ~$29.
