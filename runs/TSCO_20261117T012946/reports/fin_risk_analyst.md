### 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **INVESTMENT GRADE (BBB / Baa1)**

TSCO is a solid investment-grade credit with no near-term solvency question. S&P assigns **BBB** (issue-level BBB on senior unsecured notes) and Moody's **Baa1**; the notes are senior unsecured with **no secured indebtedness** outstanding, and the company has demonstrated primary-market access (recently priced $500M of notes at 5.2%). Interest coverage of **21.2x** (EBITDA/interest 28.4x) is strong for a retailer. The single most important analytical point: **~70% of reported "total debt" is operating lease liabilities, not funded debt.** Funded debt is only $1,765M (long-term; short-term debt = $0) against $194M cash, i.e. **net funded debt of $1,571M = 0.80x EBITDA**. Headline leverage of 2.93x is a lease-inclusive figure and overstates financial risk materially.

### 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Strong coverage, safe Z-score, low beta, low funded leverage and improving FCF margin are offset by a thin liquidity buffer, three years of margin and leverage deterioration, and payouts running ahead of free cash flow. This is a financially sound company on a mildly negative trajectory.

### 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Gross margin −302bps YoY** (36.26% → 33.24%) despite revenue +4.3%. This is the most serious signal — cost inflation (tariffs/freight) or price investment is not being recovered. Operating margin has fallen 65bps in one year and is now down 10.10% → 9.45% over three years.
2. **Zero incremental operating leverage.** Revenue rose $641M in FY2025 and operating income was *flat* ($1,467.4M vs $1,467.5M). Net income actually declined. Growth is not converting to profit.
3. **All three Piotroski trend tests fail** — ROA down, gross margin down, asset turnover down (1.57x → 1.50x). The F-score of 6 flatters a business whose *direction* is negative.
4. **Shareholder returns exceed FCF** — $849M returned vs $740M generated; net debt +$576M in FY2025. Leverage has risen every year since 2022 (net debt $4.07B → $5.75B, +41%).
5. **No standalone liquidity cushion** — cash is 0.94% of assets and 7.4% of current liabilities; quick ratio 0.16x. All liquidity is revolver-dependent. Current ratio has fallen three straight years (1.50x → 1.43x → 1.34x).
6. **Interest expense +126% over three years** ($30.6M → $69.1M) and rising faster than EBIT; coverage has halved from 46.8x to 21.2x. Refinancing the 3.70% notes at ~5.2% will accelerate this.

**Data gaps / caveats:** (i) no debt maturity schedule available — no near-term cliff inferred from $0 short-term debt, but the ladder is unverified; (ii) revolver size and covenant terms not confirmed; (iii) **price inconsistency** — profile shows $37.95 with a $19.7B cap while the latest close is $51.07 (≈$27.1B cap); both FCF-yield figures are shown above; (iv) the volatility/drawdown/Sharpe block is unreliable per the cross-check in §3; (v) sector peer tool returned only mega-caps (AMZN, HD, LOW) — no true rural-retail comparables, so no peer-relative leverage benchmarking was possible.
