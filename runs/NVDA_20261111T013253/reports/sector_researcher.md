## 1. Sector Overview

Semiconductors in 2026 has bifurcated into two businesses: an AI accelerated-compute complex growing at triple-digit rates, and a legacy analogue/PC/industrial chip market growing at low single digits. Aggregate hyperscaler capex is now guided to **$660–803bn for 2026 (+36% YoY)**, with J.P. Morgan projecting **$900bn of AI capex in 2026 alone and $5.5trn cumulative by 2030**. This is the most capital-intensive demand shock in semiconductor history, and it has pulled the entire value chain — TSMC (CoWoS packaging), ASML (EUV), SK Hynix/Micron (HBM) — into simultaneous supply scarcity and record margins. Concentration is extreme: NVDA holds an estimated **75–81% of AI accelerator revenue**, with Broadcom controlling ~60% of custom ASIC design partnerships and AMD at just 5–7%. The sector is in a **late-expansion phase**: record margins and utilisation, but decelerating growth rates and the first signs of pricing power peaking.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Capex supercycle still accelerating, not plateauing.** Big-five hyperscaler spend guided to >$600bn in 2026 (+36% YoY), with some tallies at $803bn. NVDA's Q2 FY27 Data Center revenue hit **$89.0bn, +117% YoY**, and Q3 FY27 guidance of **$108.0bn company-wide** implies an annualised run-rate above **$430bn**.
- **Supply constraints are easing, not binding.** TSMC advanced-packaging capacity additions and rising HBM bit supply (Micron FY26 gross margin of 80.7%, versus 39.8% in FY25, signals aggressive HBM pricing being absorbed downstream) mean volume, not allocation, is now the growth driver — supportive of revenue conversion.
- **Full-stack lock-in defends the moat.** CUDA, NVLink, Spectrum-X networking and AI Enterprise software raise switching costs well beyond the chip itself; NVDA's **60.4% FY26 operating margin** is ~26pts above the peer median and only achievable with software-like attach.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Custom ASIC disintermediation is real.** Broadcom's TPU/MTIA franchises and ASIC shipments growing **+44.6%** are steadily shifting share; NVDA's accelerator share has drifted from ~81% toward 75%. This caps long-run pricing power even as the pie grows.
- **China policy remains binary and unmodellable.** The regime has swung from H20 prohibition (a **$5.5bn charge** in FY25) to H200 licensing with a reported 15% revenue share. Each swing is a step-function on revenue with no visibility.
- **Margin and concentration risk are both rising.** Gross margin has compressed **74.99% (FY25) → 71.07% (FY26)** on Blackwell/HBM costs — the first real erosion in three years. Simultaneously, demand is concentrated in a handful of hyperscalers whose own capex is debt-financed and sentiment-sensitive.

## 5. NVDA's Competitive Position: **STRONG** (with an idiosyncratic overlay)

Structurally the strongest position in semiconductors: 2.0x the revenue of any peer, 18pts of gross-margin premium, 26pts of operating-margin premium, and $5.1m revenue per employee. The moat is genuinely durable — CUDA plus networking plus systems integration is not replicable on a two-year horizon, and NVDA's **~19–20x forward P/E** (on ~$390bn FY27E revenue and ~$8.70 EPS) is a substantial de-rating from historical levels, meaning the market has already priced considerable deceleration.

**However — the sector analysis and the single-stock analysis diverge sharply here.** NVDA is down **−29.4% from its 6 October close of $239.24**, on volume 3–5x normal (462m shares on 9 Nov vs ~100m typical). That drawdown is *not* sector-driven; sector capex is intact and peers trade fine. It is driven by a company-specific governance shock: an independent board investigation into an alleged foreign intelligence operation involving CEO Jensen Huang, a securities class action covering the Oct 2025–Nov 2026 class period, and open succession risk for a founder-CEO with 700,000% lifetime returns and no clear heir. Q3 FY27 results land **17 November** — a binary near-term catalyst.

## 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE** (late-cycle; single-name risk elevated)

The sector merits Attractive on fundamentals: capex visibility through 2027, supply easing, and the highest margins in the industry's history. But I would pair that rating with a **cycle caution** — the combination of decelerating growth rates, first-margin compression at the leader, ASIC share erosion, and demand concentrated in debt-financed hyperscaler budgets is the classic late-expansion signature. Position sizing, not sector selection, is the risk lever in semis today.
