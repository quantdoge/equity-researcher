## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional, tilting risk-off**

The US economy in November 2026 is in an awkward, late-cycle configuration: **growth is decelerating but positive, inflation is re-accelerating, and the Fed has been forced to reverse its easing cycle.** Real GDP grew 2.07% YoY in Q3 2026 (3.4% QoQ annualised), down from a 2.5% QoQ pace in Q1, while unemployment has *improved* to 4.1% from 4.5% a year ago. There is no recession signal in the labour market.

The problem is inflation. CPI has gone from **2.39% YoY in January 2026 to 3.82% in October 2026** — a 143bp reacceleration. The Fed cut the effective rate from 4.33% (mid-2025) to a 3.63% trough by May–Aug 2026, then **hiked 25bp in September 2026 to a 3.75–4.00% target and held on October 28**. The effective rate is 3.88% — a *real* policy rate of roughly zero against 3.8% CPI. That means policy is not restrictive in real terms, and the September 2026 SEP median pointing to 4.00–4.25% for end-2026/2027 implies the market should be pricing **further tightening, not cuts**.

The bond market has already voted. The 10Y peaked at 5.31% on October 5 and sits near 4.88–5.13%; the 30Y is at 5.47% with 2s10s at **+39bp**. This is *bear* steepening — a term-premium/long-duration repricing, not a growth-optimism steepener. Real 10Y TIPS yields are **2.66%, up ~59bp since June 1 (2.07%)**. That is the single most hostile variable for a multi-trillion-dollar, long-duration growth equity.

Credit is the tell. HY OAS at **3.34%** is still tight historically, but has widened **69bp from the 2.65% cycle tights of early September**. Spreads are only as tight as they are because GDP is holding at 2%+ and default expectations are low — that condition is not durable if the Fed hikes again.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact (NVDA)

NVDA is a **$4.07T market cap, beta 2.22** semiconductor designer with fab-lite manufacturing (TSMC Taiwan), ~45–50% non-US revenue, no net debt, and revenue growing >100% YoY with guidance of ~+70% for next year. Critically: **NVDA has no refinancing risk — its customers do.**

| Macro variable | Impact | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Real yields +59bp since June | **Strongly negative** | Discount-rate/duration effect on a long-dated cash-flow stream; with ~15–20yr effective duration, +59bp real ≈ **10–12% multiple compression** |
| Fed hike / on-hold, real policy ~0% | **Strongly negative** | Kills the "cuts coming" narrative that supported high-multiple AI; second hike is live |
| Credit spreads +69bp off tights | **Negative (underappreciated)** | AI capex is increasingly debt-funded; S&P warned (Oct 19) of undisclosed AI-linked hyperscaler debt. Higher funding costs → slower GPU orders |
| 30Y at 5.47% | **Negative** | Data centres are 20-year infrastructure; a 5.5% risk-free rate degrades project IRRs and capex approval math |
| USD +3.4% since September | **Mildly negative** | Translation drag on ~45–50% international revenue (~1.5% headwind); TSMC bills in USD so limited natural offset |
| 25% Section 232 tariff on advanced logic (eff. Jan 15, 2026) | **Negative** | Duty on imported H200/MI-class chips fabbed in Taiwan; raises customer TCO or compresses margin |
| China export regime (H200 → approved buyers, one-year waiver, 25% duty) | **Two-sided** | Reopened China revenue, but waiver is revocable and now politically radioactive |
| CPI 3.82% | **Neutral-to-positive** | Supply-constrained GPUs give genuine pricing power (ASP leverage) — but HBM/memory and power cost inflation cut the other way |
| GDP 2.07% / unemployment 4.1% | **Neutral** | Demand is capex-cycle driven, not GDP-driven; the absence of recession keeps the backlog intact |

**Confirmed in the tape.** NVDA closed at **$168.89 on Nov 10, down 29.4% from its Oct 6 peak of $239.24** and -10.4% YTD, including -9.3% on Nov 5 and -5.5% on Nov 10. The **SMH semiconductor ETF is down 23.5%** from its Oct 6 peak over the same window — this is a **sector-wide discount-rate de-rating, not an NVDA-specific one**. NVDA's ~6pt underperformance reflects a layered idiosyncratic governance shock (CEO scandal, independent board investigation, securities class actions covering Oct 2025–Nov 2026, Congressional scrutiny) with a geopolitical vector via China export licensing. Earnings on **November 17** sit five sessions away — an event risk stacked on top of macro risk.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

All four macro transmission channels — real discount rates, Fed policy direction, credit availability for AI capex, and trade/tariff policy — are simultaneously pointing the wrong way for a beta-2.2 long-duration growth asset. The rating is *unfavourable*, not *severe*, for three reasons: (1) no recession — 2%+ growth and 4.1% unemployment; (2) HY spreads at 3.34% are still far below stress levels, so the capex financing channel is strained but not broken; (3) NVDA's 2026 revenue is largely backlog/contract-backed, making near-term results insensitive to a macro wobble. The vulnerability is in the **multiple, not the numbers** — which is where beta 2.2 does its damage.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise (Bear)

**Consensus is watching the discount rate and ignoring the credit channel.** The surprise: **the Fed hikes again in December 2026**, 10Y breaks 5.5%, real yields exceed 2.9%, and — the part that matters — **HY OAS blows through 4.5%.** At that point the debt-funded AI buildout (neoclouds, single-tenant data-centre ABS, levered hyperscaler SPVs) hits a financing wall, and 2027 capex guidance gets *cut* rather than merely questioned.

That is the scenario equities are not priced for, because the current narrative treats AI capex as rate-insensitive and treats the selloff as purely a valuation event. If demand and discount rate move down together, NVDA at beta 2.2 has another **30–40% of downside** from $168.89 — and unlike the October drawdown, it would not be recoverable on an inflation print.

The mirror-image bull case is equally mechanical: because CPI's acceleration is partly base-effect driven, a soft November/December CPI that lets the Fed signal the hiking cycle is over would drop the 10Y toward 4.2%, weaken the dollar, and re-rate the entire complex — NVDA back to $220–240 with earnings leverage on top. But that requires inflation cooperation the last four quarters have not delivered.
