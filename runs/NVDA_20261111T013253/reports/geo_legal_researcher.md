# NVIDIA (NVDA) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

Note: direct SEC EDGAR retrieval was blocked (HTTP 403) and the filing-text tool returned no 10-K/10-Q for NVDA; geographic figures below are drawn from 10-K-derived aggregations and press/secondary sources. Latest reported quarter is Q2 FY2027 (July 2026); Q3 FY2027 results are scheduled for **November 17, 2026** and are not yet reflected.*

**Key structural read:** NVIDIA's revenue geography has *concentrated* into the US (69%) while China collapsed from 19% (FY2024) to ~6% of quarterly revenue — the company has largely de-risked its China P&L, but at the cost of making Washington, not Beijing, the dominant regulatory counterparty. Separately, manufacturing is single-sourced through **TSMC in Taiwan**, which is a far larger existential exposure than any revenue line (FactSet/247wallst, Oct 2026).

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — US export-control regime volatility and licensing conditionality (Probability: High; Impact: High)**
The policy has whipsawed: April 2025 H20 restrictions (~$4.5B charge disclosed), then reversal; **January 14, 2026 Proclamation 11,002** imposing a **25% Section 232 tariff on advanced computing chips (H200, MI325X)** and requiring China-bound H200s to transit the US (White House fact sheet; Reuters; CRS LSB11409); **January 15, 2026 BIS final rule** moving H200/MI325X China licenses to case-by-case review with ~10 Chinese firms (incl. Alibaba, Tencent) cleared at ~75,000 units each (Reuters, May 14, 2026; BIS). Reporting also indicates a **revenue-share/quid-pro-quo structure (~15% of China sales)** attached to China access. Every element is administratively reversible — no statutory anchor. Downside case: congressional action converts case-by-case discretion into a ban.

**Risk 2 — The Huang/Megaspeed espionage scandal politicises NVIDIA's China access (Probability: High for escalation; Impact: Medium–High)**
CEO Jensen Huang acknowledged an affair with Alice Huang, a former executive of **Singapore-based Megaspeed — an NVIDIA customer** — whom US intelligence suspects was recruited by China's Ministry of State Security (CNBC, Nov 10, 2026; BBC; WSJ; Reuters, Nov 8, 2026). NVIDIA's independent directors opened an investigation; no compromise of secrets established to date. Critically, the **Commerce Department's Megaspeed chip-diversion probe was transferred to DOJ/FBI after NVIDIA's general counsel Tim Teter intervened** (NYT, Bloomberg, NBC, Nov 9, 2026) — a criminal investigation now sits adjacent to the CEO's office. Politico (Nov 9) reports Democrats will push tougher chip export-control measures; the pending **AI OVERWATCH Act, MATCH Act, Chip Security Act and Remote Access Security Act** are direct legislative vehicles. Huang was simultaneously one of the most effective private advocates for loosening China controls — that influence channel is now impaired, raising the probability that NVIDIA loses the policy fight it had been winning (Yahoo Finance).

**Risk 3 — Taiwan Strait / supply-chain and tariff concentration (Probability: Low–Medium; Impact: Very High)**
Single-source TSMC manufacturing; ~20–28% of revenue billed to Taiwan-headquartered customers; new 25% Section 232 chip tariffs plus a US–Taiwan reciprocal trade agreement (USTR, Feb 2026) and the "Chips" investment-fee framework alter landed cost and routing. A Strait disruption is not a hedged scenario — it is a business-termination scenario. Secondary: Taiwan's own sanctions/export-control regime remains underdeveloped (Global Taiwan Institute, Sept 2026), so NVIDIA cannot rely on Taipei to filter diversion.

*Runners-up:* China's anti-sanctions/blocking statutes and its Nov 10, 2026 export controls on 16 US entities (Economist, Nov 2026) create a genuine **compliance Catch-22** — obeying BIS may breach Chinese law; China's two-month extension of its tariff/curb pause runs only to January 2027, i.e. a dated cliff.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **China SAMR antitrust:** probe into NVIDIA's adherence to the 2020 Mellanox approval conditions (Lexology; BRICS Competition). Paired with Beijing's **ban on domestic purchases of H20 and RTX Pro 6000D** and a stalled decision on the China-tailored **B30A** (IFP). This is regulatory coercion as trade policy — the risk is a large fine and/or forced divestment conditions bundling with the export-control negotiation.
- **France (FCA):** "approaching" a decision on whether to issue a formal **chargesheet** in its NVIDIA antitrust probe following September 2024 dawn raids (GCR, 2026; Reuters).
- **US DOJ antitrust:** two reported investigations into NVIDIA's AI-market practices, including inquiries into the **Run:ai** acquisition (American Action Forum; Forbes).
- **Securities litigation:** **Rosen Law Firm filed a new securities class action on NVDA investors' behalf on November 9, 2026** (BusinessWire) — timing indicates a disclosure-related claim tied to the scandal/export-control narrative. Note the prior long-running *In re NVIDIA Corp. Securities Litigation* (9th Cir./Supreme Court) precedent.
- **IP:** *Monolithic 3D Inc. v. NVIDIA Corp.*, D. Del., filed Aug 14, 2026 (Justia docket).
- **AI copyright:** consolidated authors' class actions alleging copyrighted works used in model training (BakerHostetler AI case tracker).
- **Tariff/trade:** Section 232 25% duty on H200/MI325X-class imports; US–Taiwan reciprocal trade agreement; entity-list and 50%-affiliates rule suspension extended one year (Nikkei).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Potential obstruction/appearance risk at DOJ.** A company GC contacting investigators in a probe of its own customer, followed by transfer of that probe from Commerce (civil/export) to DOJ (criminal), is exactly the fact pattern that invites scrutiny of *intent*. No wrongdoing established, but this is a live, unquantified tail risk.
2. **Undeclared conflict-of-interest exposure.** The CEO's personal relationship with an executive of a customer under diversion investigation was apparently not disclosed to the board or to the government despite prior warnings reportedly reaching the administration — governance and disclosure-control weakness.
3. **Revenue-share arrangement with the US government (~15% of China sales)** — an extraordinary, precedent-setting arrangement that carries FCPA-adjacent optics and sovereign-coercion disclosure risk.
4. **Billing-geography opacity.** Singapore at 18% of FY2025 revenue then zero in FY2026, and Taiwan billings at 20–28% for end-customers who are largely US hyperscalers, means reported "geographic" revenue does not map to end-demand. Diversion risk (chips reaching China via third-country billings) is real and is precisely what Megaspeed is alleged to have exploited; Senator Cotton's October 2025 referral confirms congressional attention.
5. **Dual-jurisdiction compliance conflict** (US BIS vs. China anti-sanctions/blocking law) — irreducible, and worsening.

*No evidence found of FCPA/AML violations, sanctions breaches by NVIDIA itself, or data-privacy enforcement actions. No Russia exposure of note identified.*

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Medium on classic country risk (69% US revenue, diversified developed-market demand), but **Very High on policy/regulatory risk**, because NVIDIA's core product is the primary instrument of US–China tech statecraft. The company is simultaneously a target of Chinese antitrust coercion, a counterparty to an unusual revenue-share arrangement with its own government, and now the subject of a criminal-adjacent diversion probe connected to its CEO. Mitigant: China is now only ~6% of revenue, so stated-earnings sensitivity to a full China cutoff is modest; the real exposure is **Taiwan manufacturing concentration**, which is unhedgeable.

**Monitor:** (1) Nov 17, 2026 Q3 FY2027 10-Q — China/Taiwan geographic note, any new legal-proceedings disclosure, and whether the independent-directors' investigation is described; (2) BIS action on B30A and any H200 license revocation; (3) SAMR outcome; (4) FCA chargesheet; (5) mark-up of the AI OVERWATCH / Chip Security Acts; (6) January 2027 expiry of China's tariff-pause extension.
