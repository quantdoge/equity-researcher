## Macro Assessment — Nebius Group N.V. (NBIS)

**Snapshot:** AI-focused cloud/GPU infrastructure ("neocloud"), HQ Amsterdam, data centres in Finland and Europe, R&D in Europe/Israel. Market cap $38.2B, beta 1.46. Price $158.93 (26 Oct 2026). **The macro regime is the single most important variable for this stock right now — more than demand.**

---

### 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Risk-off / transitional — an "inflate-and-hike" regime, not a cutting cycle.**

The defining macro fact is that the Fed *hiked* 25bp on 16 Sept 2026 to a **3.75%–4.00%** target range — its first increase since 2023 — and guided to **4.00%–4.25% by year-end 2026**, implying a second hike in December (UBS explicitly expects Sept + Dec). Inflation re-accelerated through 2026: headline CPI went from **+2.39% YoY in January to +3.72% YoY in September**; FOMC minutes put headline PCE at **3.4%** and core at **3.0%**. Critically, the minutes identify the **AI buildout — not tariffs — as the main driver of core prices**, which is a direct, structural tax on NBIS's own cost base (power, electrical equipment, construction) and simultaneously the reason policy is tightening.

Growth is decelerating while inflation rises — a stagflation-lite mix: real GDP **+2.19% YoY in Q2 2026** (vs **+2.64%** in Q1), unemployment **4.2%** (full employment, and strong payrolls were what tipped the Fed into hiking). The curve is positively sloped but **bear-steepening**: 2Y 4.65%, 10Y 4.99%, 30Y 5.37%, 2s10s **+34bp** — no inversion, so no recession signal; instead a rising term premium and heavy AI-related debt supply. Financial conditions are tightening at the margin: 10Y nominal **4.96%** (23 Oct) after peaking at **5.31%** on 5 Oct and **4.38%** on 8 May; 10Y TIPS real yield **2.64%** vs **1.93%** in May and **2.95%** on 5 Oct; US HY OAS **3.38%** vs **2.65%** on 11 Sept (**+73bp in six weeks**). Broad USD **121.40** vs 118.06 in May (**+2.8%**).

The market has begun to price this. NBIS is **+76.7% YTD** (from $89.95 on 2 Jan) but **–43.4% from its 17 Aug intraday peak of $280.83**, including **–36.4% in the 14 sessions since 6 Oct**. Tellingly, the last leg down happened *while* the 10Y rallied 35bp — so rates started the de-rating, but **sector-specific financing fear is now the marginal driver** (Musk's 26 Oct warning that "200+ neoclouds" face "guppy-level death" and consolidation as financing tightens; CoreWeave –38% in October, with its bond market described as distressed).

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates / real yields — STRONGLY NEGATIVE (the dominant channel).** NBIS is the archetype of a long-duration, negative-free-cash-flow asset: ~$38B market cap on revenues that are still scaling, with **total debt exploding from $1.22B to $10.06B in the year to June 2026** ($4.34B convertible notes in March; a $5.75B convertible issue upsized from $4.50B and closed 24 Aug) against a **2026 capex plan of $20–25B**. Value sits almost entirely in 2030+ cash flows, so a 70bp rise in the real discount rate mechanically cuts intrinsic value by double digits — the observed **–43% drawdown as real yields rose ~50bp** is the market doing that arithmetic. More dangerous: the *financing* channel. With a 1.46 beta and the shares down 43%, the equity-option component of convertibles is impaired, so the next $10B+ of funding costs more; widening HY spreads (+73bp) and tightening GPU/lease-ABS markets hit this company before they hit any hyperscaler. Note the self-referential trap: **the AI buildout is now the Fed's core inflation driver, so the boom raises the cost of capital for the companies funding it.**

**Growth — POSITIVE on the variable that matters, NEUTRAL on GDP.** 2.2% GDP is close to irrelevant; NBIS revenue is levered to AI capex, not consumer demand. That cycle remains enormous: big-five hyperscaler capex **>$600B in 2026 (+36% YoY)**, consensus **~$800B in 2026 and ~$1.1T in 2027**, with S&P projecting **>$1.3T by 2027 and $7T cumulatively 2025–2030** — though also negative free cash flow across the group. NBIS's 800MW–1GW connected-power target by YE2026 is sold into a genuinely tight capacity market. Demand is not the problem; funding it is.

**Inflation — NEGATIVE.** Power is the #1 operating cost for GPU clouds. Global data-centre electricity demand is running **~565 TWh in 2026, +26% YoY**, with European 100MW campuses costing **€44M–€170M+ annually** and Finland grid supply tightening after a large Google AI power deal — a direct threat to the low-cost Finnish power advantage underpinning NBIS's gross margin. Electrical equipment, copper and HBM/GPU costs are inflating faster than CPI. Pricing power in long-term take-or-pay contracts offsets some of this, but not in an air-pocket. **The company is both a victim and a cause of the inflation forcing the Fed to hike.**

**Currency — NEUTRAL, modestly positive on costs.** Broad USD +2.8% since May. Revenue is USD-denominated; a meaningful cost base is EUR (Netherlands, Finland) and ILS. A stronger dollar mechanically lowers USD-reported costs — a 1–3% opex tailwind — but it raises the effective price of USD-denominated GPU capacity for European and Asian customers and signals global liquidity tightening. Net: small positive on margin, negative on demand and on multiple.

**Commodities / tariffs — MILDLY NEGATIVE.** No direct commodity output; inputs are electricity, gas, copper and transformers. NBIS's European footprint gives it some insulation from US import tariffs versus US-based neoclouds, but US-based deployments and any EU–US trade friction raise equipment and server costs. Tariffs have been displaced as the inflation story — the AI supply chain is now the constraint.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The one macro variable that dominates NBIS's valuation — the real cost of capital — is moving against it on every measure (Fed hiking to 4.00–4.25%, real yields +51bp since mid-August, HY spreads +73bp, dollar +2.8%), while its own cost base inflates and its external funding need is at a record ~$20–25B. Demand tailwinds are real and large, but in a regime where **financing conditions, not orders, set the marginal price of AI capacity**, the macro backdrop is a net headwind. Upgrade triggers would be a Fed pause (no December hike) plus HY OAS stabilising below ~3.0%; downgrade triggers are a December hike plus any hyperscaler capex-digestion announcement.

### 5. Scenario That Would Materially Surprise (bear, under-priced)

**"Financing, not demand, becomes the binding constraint."** Consensus — including most sell-side AI coverage — treats October's debt fears as overblown and the fundamental case as intact. The surprise is that the December hike lands (to 4.00–4.25%), 10Y re-tests 5.3% and HY OAS blows out to 4.5–5.0%, closing the neocloud credit window exactly as NBIS needs to term out $20–25B of capex. The company is then forced to issue equity at $120–140 or fund at 9–11%, Musk's "guppy-level death" warning becomes self-fulfilling through financing rather than demand, and the shares retest the February 2026 low of **$73.52** (down ~54% from spot) — with a plausible landing zone of **$90–110** first. The under-appreciated point is that this needs *no* deterioration in AI demand at all; it only needs spreads to keep widening for six more weeks at October's pace. The offsetting bull surprise: if inflation rolls over as gasoline base effects fade and the Fed skips December, real yields back to 2.2% and spreads to 2.8% would re-rate the shares toward **$230–280 (+45–75%)**.
