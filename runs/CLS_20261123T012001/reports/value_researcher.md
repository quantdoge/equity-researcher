## Celestica Inc. (NYSE: CLS) — Value Investment Analysis

*Pricing date: last close $201.63 (Nov 20, 2026) · market cap ≈ $26.6B on ~132M post-offering diluted shares · net cash $3.14B → EV ≈ $23.5B. Financials: FY2025 audited (filed 27-Feb-2026) + Q3 2026 actuals (reported 26-Oct-2026).*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow (execution-based, and structurally fragile)**

Celestica is an electronics manufacturing / design services provider (EMS/ODM) with two segments: Connectivity & Cloud Solutions (~84% of Q3'26 revenue, +102% YoY) and Advanced Technology Solutions (aerospace/defence, industrials, HealthTech).

Evidence on the moat:

- **What has genuinely improved (the bull case for a moat):** Operating margin has gone from 3.0% (2021) to 8.6% (2025) and 8.5% adjusted in Q3'26 — a record. ROIC rose from 11.7% to 34.2% in four years. Celestica has migrated up the value chain from box assembly to "hardware platform solutions" (~40% of Q3'26 revenue — switches, custom compute racks, design and software). That is real, and it implies some switching costs at the *program* level, plus intangible assets in engineering capability.
- **Why it is not a Buffett moat:** Gross margin is ~12%. Pricing is set by competitive bid against Hon Hai/Foxconn, Quanta, Wistron, Jabil, Sanmina and Fabrinet; the customer owns the design roadmap and can dual-source or reallocate a program. **Three customers were 66% of Q3'26 revenue (35%/17%/14%) and the top ten were 85%** — up from 59% and 80% a year earlier. Concentration is *rising* as growth accelerates, which is the opposite of a diversifying moat.
- **The historical record is unkind:** revenue fell from $6.63B (2018) to $5.63B (2021) — a business that lost its prior anchor customers (Cisco/IBM heritage). There is no evidence Celestica can hold a revenue base it did not grow, and the current base is almost entirely three AI-infrastructure budgets.
- Net: the moat is *narrow and earned through execution*, not protected by structural advantage. It is the kind of business where returns on capital can be excellent in an upcycle and evaporate when a program moves.

Strengths: genuinely excellent ROIC (>30%), steady margin expansion, net cash of $3.14B, no pension/leverage risk, disciplined buybacks (share count down from ~140M in 2018 to ~116M in 2025, $153M repurchased in FY2025), zero dividends.

Weaknesses, in order of importance:
1. **Cash conversion is deteriorating, not improving.** TTM FCF of $545M on $1,246M of net income (44%). Management cut 2026 FCF guidance from $600M to $460M. Capex: $201M (2025) → ~$1.0B (2026E) → **up to $2.0B (2027E)**. GAAP earnings are increasingly an accounting figure; cash is going into customer-dedicated capacity.
2. **Working capital is ballooning:** inventory $4.51B (+107% YoY), receivables $3.76B (+43% vs FY25). Q3'26 alone: OCF $390M, capex $274M, FCF $116M on $397M of net income.
3. **Dilution:** a $3.45B gross equity offering completed in Q3'26 — roughly 9–10% of shares — to fund the ramp. Equity jumped from $2.48B to $6.21B in one quarter while ROE *fell* from 38% to ~27% on a run-rate basis. Growth is now partly purchased with shareholders' capital, not generated internally.
4. Customer and end-market concentration (above), and a business model with no recurring revenue, no backlog visibility beyond the next two quarters, and negligible deferred revenue.

### 3. Valuation Analysis — Intrinsic value ≈ **$190–230/share; fair value ≈ $205; no margin of safety**

Current multiples: **27.7× FY2025 EPS ($7.29)**; ~17.5× 2026E adjusted EPS ($11.55); ~9.4× 2027E adjusted EPS ($21.50); EV/2026E adj. EBIT ≈ 13×; **EV/FCF ≈ 51×** on 2026 guidance; P/B ≈ 4.3× ($47 book value per share).

Guidance being used: 2026 revenue $21.0B (+69%) and 2027 revenue **$40.0B (+90%)** at a 9.0% adjusted operating margin, EPS $21.50 — with management stating 2028 demand forecasts are "meaningfully higher." The stock was $474 in June 2026 and is now $202 (−57%), including −21% on Nov 5 and −16% on Nov 20 amid a broad AI/tech de-rating (VIX ~36). Sell-side targets ($375–520) are stale momentum artifacts; I give them little weight.

**Owner-earnings method.** On 2027, take revenue $38B (haircut to guidance), 8.7% adjusted operating margin → $3.3B; tax 20%; add D&A ~$0.65B; deduct maintenance capex. Here is the crux: if I treat the planned $2B as pure growth capex, owner earnings are ~$2.8B ($21/share) and the stock is 9.5× owner earnings — cheap. But a large share of that spend is capacity dedicated to three customers' programs; if it must be refreshed to retain the business, maintenance capex is closer to $1.0–1.2B, and owner earnings fall to **~$2.0–2.1B ($15–16/share)**, supporting $190–240 at a 12–15× multiple appropriate for a concentrated, cyclical, no-moat assembler.

**Scenario-weighted value:**

| Scenario | Probability | 2028 EPS | Multiple | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bull — $45–50B revenue, 9.5% margin, FCF inflects | 25% | ~$27 | 15× | ~$405 |
| Base — $33–36B revenue, 8.5% margin, capex stays high | 45% | ~$17 | 13× | ~$220 |
| Bear — one of three top customers resets; revenue back to ~$20B | 30% | ~$8 | 13× + net cash | ~$110 |

Weighted ≈ **$235**; conservative intrinsic value (using the haircut owner-earnings range) **$190–230, midpoint ~$205**. At $201.63 the shares trade essentially **at fair value**. A DCF that fully credits the 2027 guide produces a similar $215–225 result. There is no 20–30% discount — and given the binary customer risk, a discount is exactly what is warranted. My buy threshold would be ~$140–155 (a 25–30% discount to the conservative mid-point), or re-evaluation if FCF conversion recovers above 70% of earnings.

### 4. Management Quality: **7/10 — good operator, but discipline is being tested**

Rob Mionis (CEO since 2015, now also Chair — a mild governance negative) has a strong record: exited low-margin commodity programs, expanded margins for five consecutive years, and cut the share count ~17% while growing EPS from $0.82 (2021) to $7.29 (2025). The margin/ROIC track record is not luck.

Two yellow flags: (1) the decision to fund ~$2B of 2027 capex plus working capital with a $3.45B equity raise — issuing at near-record highs is opportunistic, but it converts a capital-light-ish franchise into a capital-hungry one, and per-share FCF is the victim; (2) guidance aggressiveness — 2026 revenue guidance has been raised from $16B to $17B to $19B to $21B within twelve months, and the 2027 number is 11% above where consensus stood. Management has been beating, not missing, but this is a forecast underwritten by three customers' capex plans. Little/no dividend and modest insider ownership; buybacks have slowed (only $37M in Q3'26) as cash is redirected to capacity.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD — do not initiate; not a Buffett business at this price**

The stock is no longer expensive on a forward basis — 9.4× 2027E EPS, 13× 2026E EBIT, $3.1B net cash — and the pullback from $474 has removed most of the froth. But the three things I require are all absent or unproven:

1. **No durable moat** — 12% gross margins, 66% revenue from three customers, no contractual stickiness, and a demonstrated history of losing revenue anchors.
