## SECTOR BRIEF — Celestica Inc. (CLS)
### Industry: Electronics Manufacturing Services / ODM (Technology — Hardware, Equipment & Parts)
*Data note: peers report on staggered fiscal years (CLS Dec, FLEX Mar, JBL Aug, SANM/PLXS Oct). All figures in USD, latest reported fiscal year unless marked LTM.*

---

### 1. Sector Overview

CLS competes in the **EMS/ODM** industry — contract design and manufacturing of electronics for OEMs and, increasingly, hyperscalers. The sector has been structurally re-rated from a low-growth, thin-margin "box builder" business (historically 4–6% gross margin, 2–4% operating margin) into the **physical build layer of the AI data centre**. Combined hyperscaler capex guidance is **~$600–725bn for 2026 vs ~$410bn in 2025 (+45–50%)**, and data-centre IT capex grew **92% YoY in 2Q26** (Dell'Oro). The industry is highly competitive and fragmented globally (Foxconn, Quanta, Wistron, Inventec plus Western EMS), with customer concentration at the top end of the market — the five largest hyperscalers effectively set price. Critically, 2026 marks a **mix shift**: every major peer is now allocating capacity to AI racks and switches, crowding out standard/industrial production and inflating component costs.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Hyperscaler capex is in vertical acceleration, not plateau.** 2026 spend of $600–750bn vs $410bn in 2025, with BNEF tracking **>23GW of data-centre IT capacity under construction** — a leading indicator of build revenue still to be recognised. Every comparable is guiding up: JBL FY27 +24%, FLEX FY27 +27%, SANM FY27 +27%, CLS FY26 +70%.
- **Rack-scale architecture raises the value of the integrator.** The shift from component selling to full AI rack integration (compute + switching + power + cooling) multiplies revenue per program and rewards vendors with design/engineering capability — precisely the pivot CLS, JBL and FLEX are executing.
- **Mix accretion and operating leverage.** AI/cloud programs carry better design and NPI content than legacy EMS work. CLS gross margin has risen **864bp over five years** (8.64% FY21 → 12.06% FY25); JBL's core operating margin expanded in FY26 on the same dynamic. Scale is now a genuine margin lever, not just a revenue one.

---

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Component cost inflation is being passed through, not absorbed — and it distorts margins.** AI data centre spend is consuming **~70% of global memory production in 2026**, driving DRAM/HBM shortages and price spikes. For EMS, this inflates revenue (pass-through) while *depressing* reported gross margin percentage. CLS's LTM gross margin of 11.46% is already **below FY25's 12.06%** for exactly this reason.
- **Sector-wide derating on AI-capex sustainability.** The **departure of NVIDIA's CEO Jensen Huang in November 2026** triggered a broad AI-linked selloff amid pre-existing funding, valuation and regulatory concerns (Reuters, 12 Nov 2026). CLS is reported **down ~56% from its 5-month high** despite raising guidance — sentiment, not fundamentals, is currently the dominant price driver. Expect elevated beta (CLS beta 1.35).
- **Structural buyer power caps the ceiling, and capital intensity is rising.** Hyperscalers dual-source, dictate cost-plus terms and can vertically integrate (in-house rack assembly is a live substitute). Simultaneously, peers are funding capacity expansion — CLS itself issued equity for capacity — meaning the upcycle is being financed with dilution and working-capital intensity.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG**

| Factor | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Margin premium | 11.5% GM / 8.3% OM vs 9.4% / 5.0% peer median — structurally, not cyclically, superior |
| Growth | Q3-26 revenue +82.5% YoY, adj. EPS +109%; FY26 guide $21bn, FY27 outlook $40bn — the steepest trajectory in the group |
| Moat | Design-led: leadership in next-gen data-centre switching (800G/1.6T) and rack-scale integration; AMD "Helios" rack-scale AI collaboration; early-stage design work creates platform lock-in |
| Scale | Smallest revenue base of the majors ($18bn LTM) — the *reason* the growth rate is achievable, and the source of upside optionality |
| Key risks | **Customer concentration** in a handful of hyperscalers; **inventory/working-capital risk** on rapid ramps; equity dilution funding capacity; margin mix dilution from pass-through compute content |

CLS is the **share gainer** in this cycle. Its premium is defensible on design capability rather than scale, which is the more durable form of advantage in EMS — but the concentration and inventory risks are real and are precisely what the market is currently pricing.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **ATTRACTIVE**
*(with high volatility / sentiment risk — not a low-beta sector call)*

**Cycle position: late-stage acceleration of an AI capex supercycle.** Demand visibility extends into 2027 (CLS's $40bn FY27 framework, JBL's $44.5bn FY27 guide), capacity is being added across the group, and pricing power sits with suppliers of constrained components (memory, accelerator silicon) rather than with EMS. Historically this sector is cyclical and mid-cycle margins are 3–5%; current margins are cyclically elevated and should be expected to normalise as pass-through content rises and competition for AI programs intensifies.

**Why still Attractive:** the magnitude of the capex tailwind (+45–50% industry-level spend growth) overwhelms thin-margin structural weaknesses, and CLS is positioned at the highest-value end of it. **Why not more bullish:** Porter analysis is unfavourable on four of five forces — **high buyer power** (hyperscaler concentration), **high supplier power** (memory/silicon allocation), **high rivalry** (global ODM overcapacity risk), and a live **substitution threat** (customer vertical integration); only barriers to entry are genuinely favourable. Combined with a sector currently in a sharp AI-sentiment derating, this is an attractive *operating* environment attached to a volatile *multiple*.

**Bottom line for the investment memo:** sector fundamentals are the best in a decade; CLS is the best-positioned operator in it on both margin and growth. The debate is not whether the sector is growing — it is whether the market will continue to pay for that growth.
