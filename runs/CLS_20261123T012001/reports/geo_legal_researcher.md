## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US tariff architecture hitting a 75%-Asia manufacturing base
**Probability: Very High (already in force) | Impact: High**

- **Thailand** — the White House has imposed **19% tariffs on Thai exports, plus 40% on transshipped goods** (CFR). Thailand is one of Celestica's principal AI/ML and networking manufacturing hubs and is being actively expanded.
- **Section 232 semiconductors** — a **25% ad valorem duty** on certain semiconductor imports and derivative products, with guidance effective **15-Jan-2026** (US CBP; Chrobinson 2026-Q1 advisory). AI server/switch subassemblies sit squarely in the "derivative products" debate.
- **Mexico** — US imports from Mexico were subject to a **10% tariff under Section 122 of the Trade Act from 24-Feb-2026 through 24-Jul-2026** (Congressional Research Service R48859), layered on top of USMCA and Section 232 auto/parts measures.
- Celestica's own guidance explicitly **"assumes no material changes to tariffs or trade restrictions compared to what are in effect as of January 28, 2026"** (company outlook document, 28-Jan-2026) — i.e., the $17bn→$21bn→$40bn revenue path is *not* tariff-stress-tested in guidance.

**Read-through:** Celestica is a tariff *pass-through* business in theory (contract manufacturing with cost-plus/reprice clauses) but a tariff *margin and working-capital* business in practice, because re-pricing lags and customers (three of whom are >10% of revenue each) hold the negotiating leverage. The Texas/Monterrey/Thailand tri-site strategy is the correct hedge, but it converts tariff risk into **capex and execution risk** (~$1bn/yr capex, ~6% of revenue, guided for 2026 and 2027).

### Risk 2 — US–China tech decoupling and AI export-control enforcement
**Probability: High | Impact: High**

- Enforcement intensity has stepped up materially in 2026: BIS announced a **$252m standalone settlement with a materials engineering company in Feb-2026** (second-largest in BIS history), and federal prosecutors brought **2026 criminal cases over advanced computing hardware moving through third countries** (Kharon; Alvarez & Marsal).
- The **Nvidia–Megaspeed** scrutiny illustrates the core vector: AI accelerators reaching China via Southeast Asian intermediaries (Atlantic Council). Celestica's AI rack-scale integration in **Thailand and Malaysia** places it directly on that corridor. Celestica is an ODM/integrator, not a chip designer, but rack-level assembly confers **"knowledge of end-use/diversion"** exposure under BIS deemed-export and red-flag rules.
- **AMD Helios rack-scale collaboration** and 1.6T switch programs raise the value and sensitivity of each diverted unit — and the compliance cost per unit.

### Risk 3 — Hyperscaler customer concentration as a geopolitical transmission channel
**Probability: High (structural) | Impact: Medium-High**

- **Top-10 customers = 79% of FY2025 revenue; three CCS customers each individually >10%** (FY2025 10-K). S&P Global notes concentration "remains elevated" despite growth (BB+ rating research).
- This is normally framed as an operational risk, but in 2026 it is geopolitical: hyperscaler capex is now explicitly policy-sensitive — domestic-content requirements, "AI sovereignty" procurement rules, and the US political cycle (a "blue wave" election outcome was cited as a direct driver of the November 2026 AI-sector selloff that took CLS down ~43% in four weeks). Any customer-mandated geographic shift (e.g., "build our racks in the US") forces Celestica to re-tool capacity ahead of revenue.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Active litigation:** No material *new* securities, antitrust or IP litigation was identified in FY2025/Q3-2026 disclosures. The Q3-2026 10-Q's "Legal Proceedings" item cross-refers only to **note 17 (Contingencies)** of the interim financial statements and to income-tax contingencies — the standard formulation for ordinary-course matters. The only *named* Celestica securities class action located is **In re Celestica Inc. Securities Litigation**, which was **resolved via a $30m settlement** (Labaton; BFA; Stanford SCAC — case settled ~2015). It is a **legacy, closed matter** and should not be presented as a live exposure.

**Government investigations:** **None identified** at DOJ, SEC, FTC or BIS in any source reviewed. I flag this as a *negative finding based on search*, not a positive certification — the 10-K Item 3 text itself was not retrievable.

**Tax and regulatory contingencies (the real legal line item):**
- Income-tax contingencies are disclosed as a distinct MD&A line item with a dedicated note (note 17/20 to the 2025 audited financial statements) — indicating ongoing multi-jurisdiction tax disputes/uncertainty, typical of a company with transfer pricing across Thailand, Malaysia, Laos, China, Mexico, Romania and Ireland.
- **Laos tax incentive: 100% income-tax exemption through 2025, stepping down to a reduced 8% rate thereafter** (Celestica SEC filing). This is a **known, quantified, forward earnings headwind** that begins hitting the effective tax rate from 2026 — and it is a *regulatory* risk that management guidance may under-emphasise.
- New Canadian and global **15% minimum-tax (Pillar Two)** rules are directly adverse to Celestica's low-tax-jurisdiction structure.

**Regulatory posture relevant to the business:** AI/data-centre regulation ("regulatory changes affecting AI and data center operations could increase compliance costs and impact demand") is flagged in the 10-K's own risk summary; power availability and permitting for US data-centre buildouts are becoming a gating factor for hyperscaler capex, which flows straight into Celestica's order book.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

# ⬤ HIGH

**Rationale.** The *legal* component is genuinely low — no live material litigation, no identified government investigation, a settled legacy class action, and ordinary-course contingency disclosure. The *geopolitical* component is what drives the rating to High: **~75% of revenue is manufactured in Asia** against a US tariff regime that already taxes Thai exports at 19% (40% on transshipment), semiconductors/derivatives at 25%, and Mexican imports at 10% under Section 122 — while **79% of revenue** comes from ten customers whose location decisions are themselves policy-driven. Celestica sits at the exact intersection of the two most active US enforcement frontiers of 2026: **AI-hardware export diversion** and **tariff origin/transshipment fraud**.

**What would move the rating:**
- ⬇️ **To Medium:** a disclosed multi-year customer-funded US/Mexico capacity commitment that structurally rebalances revenue below ~50% Asia, plus formal BIS/AI-compliance certifications.
- ⬆️ **To Very High:** any BIS inquiry or subpoena tied to Thailand/Malaysia transshipment; a USMCA review outcome that raises North American content thresholds; or a top-3 customer publicly reallocating programs to a competitor on geographic-compliance grounds.
