## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives:**
- **Scope 1+2 down ~87–88% vs. 2018 baseline**, against an SBTi-aligned target of −30% by 2025. Executed largely via renewable energy procurement and efficiency — a genuine, verified over-delivery.
- **Third-party verified GHG statements every year 2021–2025**, CDP disclosure continuously **since 2009**, GRI Standards: Core Option reporting since 2013. For a mid-cap EMS this is unusually mature disclosure infrastructure.
- **EMAS-registered** Valencia (Spain) facility — EU-grade environmental management.
- Operationally **asset-light in carbon terms**: Scope 1+2 intensity is roughly **3–4 tCO₂e per $M revenue** — negligible versus heavy industry.

**Risks:**
- **The decarbonisation trend is about to reverse.** 2024 Scope 2 *location-based* emissions rose **+15% YoY** as volumes ramped, and FY2025 revenue grew 28% (Q4 +44%). A target built on 2018-baseline absolute reduction is structurally incompatible with a business compounding at 30–40%. Expect the 2025 target to be met by a hair and then reset to an **intensity** basis — which is itself a disclosure-quality downgrade.
- **Scope 3 is the whole ballgame and Celestica has little leverage.** ~1.5 MtCO₂e total, **98% Scope 3**, dominated by purchased goods — i.e. GPUs, memory, and accelerators largely specified by the customer. Celestica cannot decarbonise NVIDIA's or AMD's supply chain; it can only pass through requirements.
- **Probable understatement of the true footprint.** Downstream use-phase emissions of the AI racks Celestica assembles are attributed to the hyperscaler, not CLS. Reported Scope 3 intensity (~121 tCO₂e per $M revenue) therefore flatters the company's real value-chain exposure — and this is exactly where CSRD double-materiality review will push.
- **CBAM:** indirect and modest. Electronics are outside Annex I, but precision machining and mechanical assembly create exposure to **aluminium and steel** inputs — small but non-zero.
- **Physical climate:** concentrated manufacturing in Malaysia, Thailand, Mexico, China and Mediterranean Europe (Valencia) carries flood/typhoon/water-stress exposure. The 2024 Valencia DANA flooding is a live illustration of the regional tail risk.

**Verdict: Medium.** Low operational carbon, high and growing value-chain carbon, and a target architecture that will need re-basing.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Positives:**
- **Responsible Business Alliance (RBA) Code of Conduct** adopted and audited across global facilities — the industry-standard framework, with third-party verification visits.
- **Modern Slavery Statement (FY2025)**, explicit ethical-labour policies covering **child labour, foreign migrant workers (employer-pays principle), and working hours** — the three highest-risk items in Asian electronics supply chains, addressed by name rather than elided.
- **Conflict minerals**: SEC Form SD filer with cobalt/tin/tantalum/tungsten/gold due diligence and smelter engagement — a recurring, auditable obligation that CLS is meeting.
- **Global Ethics Hotline** plus annual Business Conduct Governance Policy certification for all employees.

**Risks:**
- **Structural labour-risk geography.** The majority of 23,803 employees sit in Malaysia, Mexico, China, Thailand, Romania and India. Migrant-worker recruitment-fee abuse and excessive overtime are endemic to this footprint, and social-compliance audits are demonstrably poor at detecting forced labour (per US CECC findings). A single RBA audit failure at a Malaysian plant is a live tail risk that could cost a hyperscaler contract.
- **UFLPA exposure** on critical-mineral and component supply chains; enforcement has tightened materially since 2022.
- **Extreme internal pay dispersion.** The CEO-to-median-employee ratio is **1,257:1**, implying a median employee comp of roughly **$13.9k** — consistent with a low-cost-geography footprint. This is precisely the metric that drives social-licence criticism and weak MSCI/Sustainalytics social scores, even though Celestica is not an outlier versus EMS peers.
- **Evidence gaps (honest disclosure):** I could not retrieve **injury rates (TRIR), turnover, or gender/ethnic diversity percentages** in the time available. The sustainability site links to management approaches but does not surface hard metrics in accessible form — a transparency shortfall in itself.

**Verdict: Medium.** Credible process architecture, structurally high-risk footprint, under-disclosed outcome metrics.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.5 / 10**

**Score-adding (+):**
- **Dual-class structure eliminated.** The multiple voting shares were removed and subordinate voting shares reclassified to common — **one share, one vote** restored. This is the single most shareholder-friendly action in the file and removes a long-standing structural discount.
- **9 directors, majority independent**; Audit, HR & Compensation, and Nominating & Corporate Governance committees all conform to statutory/NYSE requirements.
- **Majority voting policy** for director elections — above Canadian minimum.
- **Annual say-on-pay with 96.20% support** (2025), and all nine nominees elected with **>94% of votes cast** in 2026. Zero activist pressure, zero proxy-advisor fight. Shareholders are content.
- **Board renewal with domain expertise:** David Reeder (semiconductor veteran) added May 2026 — directly relevant to the AI pivot. Long-time Chair Michael Wilson executed an orderly, planned retirement.
- **Robust ethics stack:** Business Conduct Governance Policy, Finance Code of Professional Conduct, global whistleblowing hotline, annual certification, and published CEO/Chair/Committee Chair position descriptions.

**Score-subtracting (−):**
- **CEO/Chair combination.** As of May 2026, **Rob Mionis holds both Chair and CEO roles** — a regression from an independent chair. Mitigant: **Laurette Koellner** was simultaneously appointed **Lead Independent Director** with authority to preside over executive sessions and convene independent directors. Combined with a majority-independent board and majority voting, this is a *tolerable but inferior* structure. Proxy advisors will increasingly vote against the Chair of the Governance Committee if this persists.
- **Compensation alignment is the weakest link.** CEO 2025 total compensation **$17.4M (+16% YoY)** with a **1,257:1 pay ratio**, and **"compensation actually paid" of ~$321M** driven by the AI-fueled share-price re-rating. A ~18x gap between granted and realized pay is a windfall, not evidence of durable pay-for-performance. It creates dilution risk, retention optics issues, and a real risk that incentives reward the multiple rather than the margin — precisely the wrong signal at a cyclical peak in AI capex enthusiasm.
- **Legacy governance blemish:** *In re Celestica Inc. Securities Litigation* — a **$30M settlement** over statements on restructuring, earnings and outlook. Resolved and dated; not currently relevant, but it is part of the track record.
- **Unverified:** board gender/visible-minority diversity percentages could not be confirmed from the sources retrieved.

**Verdict: 6.5/10 —** genuinely strong shareholder-rights mechanics (no dual class, majority independent, majority voting, near-unanimous say-on-pay) offset by CEO/Chair duality and a compensation architecture that has delivered a nine-figure realized windfall.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE (improving)**

CLS is **not a Laggard** — the disclosure infrastructure (GRI Core, CDP since 2009, verified GHG data, RBA audits, Modern Slavery Statement, Form SD), the SBTi-aligned targets, the 87% Scope 1+2 reduction, and the elimination of the dual-class structure all place it ahead of the median EMS peer and above the small/mid-cap average.

It is **not a Leader** — the decarbonisation is concentrated in the small operational slice of the footprint (2% of emissions), the dominant Scope 3 exposure sits with suppliers CLS does not control, absolute emissions are set to rise with volume, quantified social outcome metrics (safety, turnover, diversity) are not readily disclosed, and the 2026 move to a combined Chair/CEO with a nine-figure realized CEO pay package undercuts the governance narrative.

**Trajectory is positive** (dual-class removal, board renewal, CSRD-driven Scope 3 rigour) but the ESG profile is **reactive and compliance-led rather than strategic**.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**ESG is not the thesis.** The CLS investment case rests on AI data-centre demand, rack-scale integration share gains, and margin expansion (Q4 2025 earnings-from-operations margin 8.6%, +100bps YoY) — not on ESG positioning. ESG does not discount the multiple, nor does it justify a premium.

**Why Neutral rather than Negative:**
- No open controversy, no enforcement action, no ESG-linked debt, and a governance structure that has just been materially *improved* via the dual-class collapse.
- 96% say-on-pay and 94%+ director support mean no near-term proxy or activist friction.
- Low operational carbon intensity means no direct carbon-tax or stranded-asset exposure.

**Why Neutral rather than Positive:**
- ESG is a **cost centre and a gatekeeper**, not a value driver. CSRD alone will add real opex over FY2025–27 with no revenue offset.
- The company is **structurally unable to decarbonise its own Scope 3** — 98% of emissions sit with suppliers and customers it does not control. Any credit for the 87% Scope 1+2 cut is largely optical.
- Social tail risk (migrant labour, RBA audit failure) is asymmetric: low probability, but a contract-level loss if it hits.

**What would move it to Positive:** an independent Chair restored; a Scope 3 intensity target with supplier-engagement KPIs tied to hyperscaler scorecards; disclosure of safety (TRIR) and turnover metrics; and compensation redesigned around ROIC/margin rather than equity re-rating.

**What would move it to Negative:** any RBA audit failure or forced-labour finding in Malaysia/Mexico/China; a Scope 3 restatement under CSRD double materiality; a proxy-advisor "against" recommendation on the Chair; or an absolute emissions increase presented as on-target via base-year or intensity rebasing.

**Bottom line for the memo:** CLS carries **average-to-good ESG fundamentals with a positive governance trajectory**, is **not a source of valuation risk** at present, and should be modelled as a **modest, quantifiable compliance cost** (CSRD/assurance, ~10–25bps of margin over FY2026–27) rather than as a thesis input. The single ESG item genuinely worth monitoring is **supply-chain labour compliance**, because that is the only channel through which an ESG failure could impair the AI revenue ramp.
