## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Risk-off, with a hawkish re-acceleration regime.** The US economy is *reheating*, not slowing: real GDP rose at a ~3.4% annualised pace in Q3 2026 ($24.61trn vs $24.41trn), unemployment has fallen to 4.1% from 4.5% a year ago, and nominal PCE is running +6.3% y/y. Inflation has re-accelerated for three consecutive months — headline CPI +0.4% m/m in October, +3.82% y/y (Sep +3.72%, Aug +3.35%, Jul +3.30%), with tariff pass-through now visibly landing in consumer prices (Minneapolis Fed, Nov 2026). The Fed has stopped cutting and begun reversing: effective funds went 3.63% (May–Aug) → 3.75% (Sep) → 3.88% (Oct), the October 28 FOMC held at 3.75–4.00%, and markets price ~89% odds of a **25bp hike in December**, with Governor Waller publicly backing it.

Critically, the long-end selloff is **real-yield driven, not inflation-expectation driven**: 5Y breakevens have *fallen* to 2.18% (Nov 20) from 2.47% (Oct 26) while the 10Y rose to 5.13% and the 30Y to 5.47%. That is a term-premium/fiscal-supply repricing, and it implies a ~2.75% 5Y real yield — genuinely restrictive, and the most hostile possible backdrop for long-duration, high-beta growth equity.

Credit is the one non-confirming signal: HY OAS has widened ~92bp since Aug 28 (2.60% → 3.52%) but remains historically tight. Capital markets are open; equity volatility in AI names has not yet become a credit event.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact — Celestica (CLS)

CLS is a $25.4bn-market-cap EMS/ODM with beta 1.35, manufacturing in Asia/Mexico and selling design-and-build capacity to North American hyperscalers. Q3'26 revenue +83% y/y to $5.83bn, adj EPS +109% to $3.30, FY26 guide $21bn and a **$40bn FY27 revenue outlook**. Its equity has fallen 57% from the June 2 high of $472.40 to $201.63 (Nov 20), including −21% in the week to Nov 20, on an NVDA-led AI-complex de-rating (NVDA −35% from its Oct 6 high).

| Macro variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| GDP / activity | **Positive** | Volume-driven business; 3.4% GDP and +6.3% nominal PCE support enterprise/industrial and HealthTech segments alongside AI. |
| CPI inflation (3.8%) | **Mixed / negative** | Cost-plus pass-through protects revenue, but component inflation is savage: combined DRAM+SSD prices +130% in 2026, server DRAM +55–60% q/q early in the year. Hits working capital, inventory risk and margin visibility. |
| Memory/component super-cycle | **Negative** | Hyperscaler priority allocation helps CLS's core customers but squeezes its diversified/industrial programs. Biggest single earnings-quality risk. |
| Fed funds / Dec hike | **Negative** | Beta 1.35, high-multiple growth derivative. Valuation is a levered bet on 2027 revenue that doesn't arrive until 2027+. |
| 10Y at 5.13% / 30Y 5.47% | **Strongly negative** | Discount-rate compression on long-duration cash flows; also raises hyperscalers' hurdle rate for AI ROI, which is what funds CLS's $40bn target. |
| USD +4.0% since Sept | **Mildly positive (cost), negative (revenue)** | Reports in USD, manufactures in THB/MYR/MXN/CNY/CAD — a stronger dollar lowers the USD cost base and supports gross margin; partially offset by translation of non-US revenue and customer price-down pressure. Net ≈ neutral. |
| Tariffs | **Negative, partly offset** | Pass-through now inflationary to customers and disruptive to routing, but CLS's US footprint expansion is a share-gain opportunity as customers re-shore. |
| Credit (HY OAS 352bp) | **Neutral** | No refinancing stress; CLS has strong liquidity and recently raised equity to fund capacity. But equity issuance + 5%+ risk-free = higher WACC. |
| AI capex cycle | **The swing factor, currently negative-priced** | NVDA guides hyperscaler capex of ~$1.3trn in 2027 vs ~$800bn in 2026 and JPM expects further growth, yet 52% of surveyed investors now expect a capex *cut*. The market is pricing the second derivative, not the level. |

**Rate-sensitivity characterisation:** CLS is *not* a balance-sheet rate risk (low net debt, ample liquidity) — it is a **duration/valuation** rate risk. Its multiple is a claim on a 2027–2028 capex super-cycle whose own funding cost rises with real yields.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **Unfavourable**

The macro configuration — rising real yields, re-accelerating headline inflation, a Fed pivoting back to hikes, a 4% dollar rally in nine weeks, and a bear-steepening curve — is close to the worst possible mix for a high-beta, long-duration AI-capex derivative at 1.35 beta. Note the important asymmetry: **CLS's operating fundamentals are not the problem** (Q3 revenue +83%, guidance raised); the problem is that macro is compressing the multiple applied to those earnings while simultaneously raising doubts about the durability of the 2027 revenue that justifies the multiple. That is a macro-driven, not company-driven, drawdown — which cuts both ways.

## 5. Surprise Scenario (bear, and it is the live one)

**The December hike happens, and it isn't the last.** If October's +0.4% m/m CPI is followed by another 0.4% print in November/December — tariff pass-through plus memory-driven goods inflation plus 4.1% unemployment — markets reprice to a 4.50%+ terminal rate and the 10Y breaks 5.50% with the 30Y toward 6.00%. Two things follow that the current price does not discount: (a) the equity risk premium for AI-capex derivatives expands violently, and (b) hyperscalers facing a higher cost of capital trim 2027 capex plans — directly invalidating CLS's $40bn FY27 revenue outlook, which is the load-bearing pillar of the bull case. A capex trim plus multiple compression is a −30% to −40% scenario from $202 (i.e., $120–140), and HY spreads at 352bp have 90bp of room to widen before they even reach their long-run median.

**The mirror-image bull case is credible and is what breakevens are whispering:** at 2.18%, the 5Y breakeven says the market believes inflation rolls over as tariff pass-through laps in H1 2027. If so, the Fed pauses after one hike, the 10Y retraces to 4.50%, and a $1trn+ hyperscaler capex year is confirmed in Q4 guides — CLS at ~12x forward earnings would re-rate hard. The macro surprise risk is genuinely two-sided, but the *near-term* path of least resistance is unfavourable.
