# Investment Analysis: Chipotle Mexican Grill (CMG)

**Prepared by Equity Value Research | Price used: $30.99 | Market cap: ~$39.7B**

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Wide (with demonstrated fragility)**

Chipotle sits at the top of the US fast-casual category with a genuinely differentiated positioning — "Food with Integrity," real cooking in the restaurants, no freezers, no tier-3 franchise dilution, company-owned units only. That gives it a real, if intangible, moat: category-leading brand affinity, best-in-class restaurant-level economics (industry-leading ~$3M+ AUVs), efficient scale in supply chain, and deep digital integration (~35-40% of sales via digital).

**But the moat has cracks, and they showed again.** The price history is a tragedy in two acts. The stock ran from ~$17 (2020) to ~$69 intraday (June 2024), then — after the late-2025 E. coli outbreak expanded — collapsed from ~$58 (June 2025) to ~$31 (late 2026): a roughly 50% haircut. This is the *second* systemic food-safety event in a decade (2015's E. coli took years and a new CEO to repair). Q3 2025 revenue ($3.00B) fell below Q2 2025 ($3.06B) as the crisis broke; Q4 2025 revenue ($2.98B) bottomed; 2025 net income was flat versus 2024 ($1.536B vs $1.534B) despite 5.4% revenue growth.

The encouraging counterpoint: unlike QSR peers, Chipotle's customers *can and did* come back — the 2015 episode proves brand recovery is possible — and the 2026 quarterly beats (Q1-Q2 2026 EPS and revenue above estimates, Q2'26 revenue +9% YoY) show the recovery is underway. A moat that bends but self-heals is still a moat; one that requires a decade of trust restoration is not a safe one. **Rating: Wide under normal conditions, but materially impaired by food-safety recurrence risk — the moat is narrower and costlier to defend than it was in 2023.**

- **FCF conversion is elite**: FCF/NI = 94% (2025). Owner earnings ~$1.23B — a real compounding engine.
- **ROIC > WACC in every year** (15.9% vs ~9% cost of capital even in a crisis year) — the hallmark of genuine economic value creation — but the trend is *down* 23.4% → 15.9%, and treasury-grade returns are gone.
- Growth decelerated hard: 14-15% revenue growth collapsed to 5.4% in the crisis year with zero earnings growth. Gross margin down 130bps — food, labor, and crisis-remediation costs.
- Balance sheet: no interest expense at all, ~$1.0B cash, buybacks funded internally. (Note: reported "total debt" fields look like operating-lease liabilities and were internally inconsistent across periods — treat leverage detail with caution; the P&L and cash-flow files are the reliable ones.)

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **Current price ≈ 15-20% ABOVE conservative intrinsic value**

- **Trailing multiples**: P/E 27.2x on ~$1.14 TTM EPS; EV/EBITDA 21.2x; P/FCF 28.7x; FCF yield only 3.5%. CMG sits at a 14% premium to the sector-peer median P/E (23.8x).
- **What price implies**: at 27.4x FCF with a 9% discount rate, the market is paying for roughly **5% perpetual FCF growth** — a fair-to-optimistic ask for a business whose earnings were *flat* in 2025 and whose growth engine (8-10% unit growth + ~2-3% same-store) must now overcome a trust deficit.
- **My conservative intrinsic value** (FCF base $1.45B; 10% FCF growth for 5 yrs, 5% for 5 yrs, 2.5% terminal, 9% discount): **~$33-34B ≈ $26-27 per share**. The platform's own DCF ($83B) is not credible — it mechanically extrapolates the 19.7% 2022-25 FCF CAGR (a bull-recovery artifact) into perpetuity; I explicitly reject it.
- **Margin of safety**: A 20-30% discount to my intrinsic value implies an entry of ~$19-22/share. At $31, the stock is roughly a quarter expensive, not a bargain. There is no "wonderful business at a fair price" here — it's a wonderful business at a slightly *unfair* price.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Solid, with minor blemishes**

- **Scott Boatwright** (CEO since March 2025) is a career Chipotle operations insider — the right profile for a "rebuild the trust and run the restaurants well" mandate; his comp is heavily equity-linked (alignment good). Predecessor Brian Niccol (2018-2024) rebuilt the brand after 2015 — Boatwright inherits a proven playbook.
- **Capital allocation has been disciplined and owner-friendly**: ~$1.0B buyback 2024, **~$2.4B in 2025** (aggressively repurchasing into the distressed tape), $592M 2023; board added $500M more. No dividends — reinvestment into ~300 net new units/yr is the better use of capital at these ROICs.
- Blemish: small insider sales by the CEO (~$1.1M), CFO (~$327K), and others — immaterial in scale, but symbolically poor optics during a crisis-recovery window. Net: a competent, aligned team, not a legendary one.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **Hold**

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Great business? | Yes — category leader, ROIC >> WACC, 94% FCF conversion |
| Moat durable? | Wide but demonstrably crackable (two E. coli crises in a decade) |
| Price below intrinsic? | **No** — ~15-20% *above* conservative intrinsic; needs ~$26 to be fair, ~$20-22 for a margin of safety |
| Management in the boat? | Yes, with minor insider selling |

This is the classic Buffett dilemma resolved in one direction: I would not sell into the despair at $31 (the franchise is genuinely high quality, the recovery is real, and the multiple is merely rich, not bubble-grade), but I will not pay $31 when my conservative intrinsic is ~$26 and my entry price requires ~$20. **Hold — build the position on a pullback toward the low-to-mid $20s, or on proof that same-store traffic returns to pre-crisis levels for two consecutive quarters.**

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Food-safety recurrence is a systemic, recurring risk** — this is the second major outbreak, and each event re-inflicts a multi-quarter sales crater with permanent multiple damage. This is the single largest threat to the moat. The 2025 event triggered a *third* consecutive crisis (after 2015/2018); the pattern suggests process, not bad luck.
2. **Margin compression is structural, not cyclical**: 25.4% gross margin, wage inflation, and ~2% annual menu-price hikes (with further hikes planned for 2026) risk a value-deal backlash exactly when foot traffic is most fragile — pricing power is being tested at the worst possible moment.
3. **Valuation risk**: at 27x earnings / 21x EBITDA on flat earnings, any stumble in the traffic recovery re-rates the stock violently (the tape already demonstrates 20% single-day downside moves).
4. **Management continuity**: Boatwright is unproven as a full-cycle CEO; the equity-heavy comp is two-sided — it aligns, but also monetizes ("sells" bias) in distress windows.
5. **Data caveats**: balance-sheet leverage fields are internally inconsistent and include lease liabilities; the platform DCF's 19.7% growth assumption materially overstates intrinsic value — my conservative case should govern.

*Bottom line: a high-quality compounding business at a price that still embeds the pre-crisis multiple. Patience is the entire edge here — the margin of safety is what you'd be giving up for speed.*
