**Sector comparables:** MCD, SBUX are the relevant peers. Unlike MCD (which derives ~half of revenue ex-US), CMG's revenue is overwhelmingly domestic — its geopolitical risk is *de minimis* cross-border, but its **domestic regulatory and litigation profile is structurally elevated**.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1. US–Mexico trade friction on fresh-produce supply chain — Probability: High | Impact: Medium**
- Chipotle's CEO has confirmed **~50% of its avocados are sourced from Mexico**; Mexico supplies ~90% of US avocado imports.
- 2025 tariff episodes (25% threat on Mexican goods, paused/reinstated) directly pressure COGS. This is the single most live geopolitical channel for CMG: guacamole is a signature SKU and fresh produce is ~core input.
- Mitigant: sourcing diversification (>50% non-Mexico avocados already), but cilantro/other produce remain concentrated; margin already under compression (2026 analyst commentary cites margin pain even as revenue grew ~9%).

**2. US domestic labor regulatory environment — Probability: High | Impact: High**
- California's $20/hr fast-food minimum wage (effective April 2024) and the Fast Food Council regulatory regime hit CMG's largest state footprint directly.
- NLRB claims with merit found against CMG re: its unionized store (2024); unionization pushes at NYC/Detroit locations; wage-and-hour (CA PAGA, misclassification, OT) class actions are recurring. California wage/hour litigation is a structural, permanent cost driver.

**3. European expansion execution gap — Probability: Medium | Impact: Low-Medium**
- International footprint ~<5% of revenue today, so a European misstep cannot move the P&L much. But the stated ambition of "thousands" of EU sites raises exposure to EU food-safety/labor/data regimes, plus FX — with limited brand recognition outside North America. Low near-term impact; watch as unit count grows.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **DOJ food-safety criminal resolution (2020):** $25M fine — the largest ever in a food-safety case — plus a deferred prosecution agreement tied to the 2015–18 norovirus/E. coli outbreaks. The DPA term has lapsed, but the *criminal history* is the anchor for ongoing plaintiff-side food-safety litigation; any new outbreak re-opens criminal and civil exposure.
- **Recurring foodborne-illness incidents** (e.g., 2022 Ohio, 2023 Virginia) keep food-safety class actions active; plaintiffs' firm (Marler Clark) actively litigates CMG cases.
- **NLRB:** Board found merit to charges over treatment of workers at its only unionized store (Aug 2024); case 07-CA-325791 subsequently withdrawn (2025). Unionization pressure at several urban stores continues.
- **Wage & hour:** California wage/hour violations, meal/rest break, misclassification and unpaid-OT class/PAGA actions are chronic; CA penalties are per-employee, per-violation.
- **No material SEC, FCPA, AML, or sanctions matters identified; no nationalization/currency-control exposure** (non-US operations are all in developed common-law or EU markets).

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **Food-safety culture is the historical liability** — prior criminal conviction elevates regulatory scrutiny (FDA/CDC) and plaintiff interest in every future incident. Highest-priority watch item.
- **Supply-chain concentration & social compliance:** heavy sourcing from Mexico concentrates geopolitical, tariff, and labor/forced-labor due-diligence risk (US CBP scrutiny of imported food inputs).
- **Data privacy (moderate):** 38.3% of sales are digital → large loyalty dataset under CCPA/state privacy laws; no material breach litigation surfaced in research, but privacy cost base rises with scale.
- **No OFAC/FCPA red flags** — a genuinely low-exposure area given the US-centric model.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

Cross-border geopolitical exposure is genuinely minimal (one of the lowest in the large-cap restaurant group), but the rating is held at Medium by a heavy **domestic regulatory-labor stack** (CA $20 wage law, NLRB/unionization, chronic wage-hour class actions), a **criminal food-safety history** that keeps litigation risk structurally elevated, and **Mexico supply-chain tariff sensitivity** in the COGS line.
