## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **Investment Grade (low end / borderline)**

CMG's credit profile is deceptively strong once you decompose what the "debt" actually is.

- **Interest expense is $0 for five consecutive years** (2021–2025). CMG carries essentially **no interest-bearing borrowings**. The $9.85B "total debt" on the balance sheet is overwhelmingly **operating-lease liabilities** (ASC 842 accounting for its heavily leased, sale-leaseback portfolio — confirmed against vendor balance-sheet data: LT liabilities $4.98B, of which long-term debt proper is only a small residual).
- A lease-liability load, unlike coupon debt, has **no interest-coverage or refinancing cliff** — it's serviceable from operating cash flow, which is ample ($2.11B OCF in FY2025).
- Nonetheless, the **leverage ratios are real and deteriorating** (net debt/EBITDA 3.71x, book-equity down $0.8B YoY), which is why the Altman Z-score (2.47) sits in the **grey zone**, and the Piotroski F-score (6/9) docked points for rising leverage and shrinking equity. This is a **"financially safe but optically heavy"** profile — and the optics worsen every year buybacks outpace FCF.
- **Rating nuance:** If the $8.8B net-debt figure were true coupon-bearing debt instead of leases, this would be Sub-Investment Grade (3.71x leverage, grey-zone Z). Because the burden is non-interest-bearing and cash-generative, **Investment Grade (low end)** is the fair call — but trending negatively.
