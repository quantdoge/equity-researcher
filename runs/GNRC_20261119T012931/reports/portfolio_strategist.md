## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT — and the split is about price, not risk**

There is **near-unanimous agreement on the business** and **near-unanimous agreement on the risks**. The disagreement is entirely about whether $205 already pays for it.

**Consensus (8 of 11 agents, high agreement):**
- The data-center backlog is *real contracted visibility*, not a slide — corroborated independently by Google Trends, production-line job postings, and the Amazon warrant.
- The residential franchise is genuinely moated (~70–80% share, 9,900 dealers, 38–40% gross margin vs 32% peer median).
- The balance sheet is not the risk; the **equity** is.
- Customer concentration, execution on a 3x capacity build, and margin-dilutive mix are the core threats.

**Genuine disagreement:**
| Camp | Agents | Thesis |
|---|---|---|
| **Pay for growth** | Growth | 14.7x FY27 with 40% growth; PEG 0.35 is a mispricing |
| **Fairly valued** | Value, Sector, Fin Risk | IV ≈ price; no discount, no margin of safety |
| **Not yet / short** | Quant, Short, Alt Data, Macro | Consensus out-year numbers are unreachable; momentum and vol are adverse |

**Conflict resolution.** My framework gives fraud risk precedence over bullish fundamental views. **No fraud override applies here** — the Short Analyst explicitly and credibly cleared the accounting (Beneish −2.81, negative accruals in 3 of 4 years, cash conversion >100%). That said, the Short's *arithmetic* is the single most valuable input in this entire packet:

> Management's own guidance implies FY27 revenue of **~$5.3B** (C&I +30% ≈ $2.5B, residential flat ≈ $2.3B, other ≈ $0.5B) and ~$3.75B of large-MW capacity by Q3'27. Consensus is **$6.89B**. That is a **~$1.6B / 30% gap** — and clearing it requires C&I to roughly double in twelve months, from a standing start, against Cummins (~30–35% share) and Caterpillar (~20–25%) in a market that MarketsandMarkets sizes at only 2.7% CAGR.

This directly dismantles the Growth agent's central claim. **The "14.7x forward" cheapness is a function of a numerator the company's own guidance does not support.** Re-based to management-consistent FY27 EPS (~$10.50–11.00), GNRC trades at **~19–20x**, not 14.7x. On that basis the PEG argument evaporates and the Value DCF ($190–220 total range, including its *bull* case at $222) becomes the binding constraint.

**Convergence check — two independent methods land in the same place:**
- Value's DCF buy zone: **$150–165**
- Short's guidance-consistent valuation (FY27 EPS ~$10.75 × 15x): **$158–165**

When a conservative DCF and a bear-case arithmetic model converge on ~$160, that is a robust valuation anchor, not a coincidence.
