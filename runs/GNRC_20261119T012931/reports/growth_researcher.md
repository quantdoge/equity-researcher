## GNRC — Growth Investment Analysis
*Data as of 2026-11-18 close ($205.45); financials through Q3 2026 (filed 11/03/26)*

### 1. Growth Story
Generac is converting from a cyclical consumer-storm-play into an AI-infrastructure power franchise. In September 2026 it signed an **$8B agreement with Amazon** ($2.4B of committed generator deliveries in 2027–2028, plus a warrant for 1.69M shares at $200.93), and its data-center backlog tripled to **~$4.5B in 90 days** (from $1.6B in Q2). This is a genuine TAM expansion — not just share-shifting — layering a high-ASP, high-visibility megawatt business (2.25–3.25MW gensets) on top of a ~70%-share US home-standby annuity that is still only ~5–6% penetrated.

This is the single most important fact in the analysis — the historical score is structurally unrepresentative of the forward model.

- **Estimate revisions:** FY26 EBITDA margin guidance raised; FY30 EPS consensus revised **+10.5%**. Backlog trajectory is the strongest leading indicator (up 180% in one quarter).

### 3. TAM and Penetration
- **Data-center generators:** third-party sizing is $8.4–8.6B (2026) growing to **$12–20B by 2030/31** (CAGRs of 2.7% to 15.2% depending on whether AI hyperscale step-up is captured). Generac targets FY27 DC revenue **"well above $2B"** and ~**$4B of annual DC sales capacity by 2H27** — implying 20–45% share of stated market size, which means either share capture far above today's, or (more likely) the market forecasts lag actual AI power demand. OEM lead times are already past 52 weeks with backlogs into 2028 — a seller's market.
- **Residential:** ~5–6% US household penetration, Generac ~70–80% share of home standby; returning to **high-single-digit growth** in FY26. Structural driver (grid unreliability) is intact, penetration runway is a decade-plus.
- **C&I:** management flags a potential path to **doubling C&I net sales in 2027**.

### 4. Upcoming Catalysts
- **Q4 2026 results — Feb 10, 2027:** first formal FY27 guide; watch DC revenue vs. the $2B bar and whether backlog clears $5B.
- **Amazon delivery ramp (2027–2028):** conversion of the $2.4B initial tranche into revenue; potential expansion of the $8B envelope.
- **Capacity buildout to ~$4B DC capacity by 2H27:** plant/footprint announcements; management already cautions orders placed after Q1 2027 "may be difficult to fulfill" — positive for pricing, a red flag for upside capture.
- **Second/third hyperscaler or colo contracts:** the highest-value re-rating catalyst — proves the Amazon win is a franchise, not a one-off.
- **Tariff/price-cost normalization:** Q4 gross margin guided *down* to 37–38% (mix + inflation) — a swing factor.

### 5. Alternative Data Signals
- **Google Trends ("Generac generator", US, 5y):** baseline ran **13–19** through late 2025; sustained elevation to **23–41** from April through October 2026 (currently ~28–30, near double last year's base). Consistent with the residential growth inflection management cited. **Supportive.**
- **Job postings:** new data-center-specific roles appearing — *National Account Manager II – Data Centers*, *Senior Field Service Manager – Data Centers*, *Field Project Engineer – Data Centers*. Confirms genuine DC go-to-market and field-service build-out rather than a paper backlog. **Supportive** (qualitative sample only).
- **Price/volume behavior:** +18% on the Amazon announcement (Sept 17, 7.9M shares, 5x normal volume). Then a **−23% drawdown** from $224 (Oct 9) to $172 (Nov 5) during an AI-power valuation reset, before recovering to $205. Beta 1.86 — this trades as an AI-infrastructure derivative, not a machinery stock.
- **Surprise history:** 71% beat rate, average surprise **+15.4%**, but the beat magnitude is **decelerating** (+44.8% → +35.3% → +5.8%). Post-Q3 price targets drifted modestly *lower* even as ratings held (Baird $305→287, Piper $303→279, Cantor $297) — the bar is now rising faster than the beats.

### 6. Valuation
At $205.45 (EV ~$13.2B): **20.9x FY26E EPS, 14.7x FY27E, 11.9x FY28E; EV/EBITDA 12.5x FY26E → 9.6x FY27E → 8.1x FY28E.** PEG on FY27 growth ≈ **0.35**. Sell-side median target ~**$281** (+37%), range $250–325.
Critical read: consensus **decelerates to just 6.1–6.5% revenue growth in 2029–2030**, i.e. the Street models the data-center win as a 2027–28 step-function followed by reversion to a mature industrial grower. At 11.9x 2028E, the market is pricing skepticism about durability. If GNRC proves a repeatable multi-hyperscaler franchise, that terminal growth assumption is materially too low — that is the core mispricing.

### 7. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (high conviction, high volatility)
40% forward revenue growth at 14.7x forward earnings with a tripling backlog, rising estimates, and a land-grab market is the rare combination worth paying up for — and here you aren't even paying up. Upside to ~$280–310 on consensus execution; significantly more if the DC franchise proves durable past 2028. Not a Strong Buy purely because of execution and customer-concentration risk.

### 8. Key Risks
- **Large-MW execution risk:** 2.25–3.25MW industrial gensets are new territory against Caterpillar, Cummins, and Rolls-Royce, who have decades of heavy-power engineering and service networks. Scaling manufacturing to $4B capacity without margin and quality slippage is unproven.
- **Customer concentration with a cyclical customer:** the Amazon deal is both the thesis and the risk. One hyperscaler capex pause, a dual-sourcing decision, or an AI-infrastructure sentiment shock (already saw −23% in a month) and the backlog narrative inverts. The Amazon warrant (~2.9% dilution at $200.93) signals alignment but also cements dependence.
- **Residential cyclicality is not fixed:** 2023 (−12%) and 2025 (−2%) show that storm activity and housing turnover — not execution — drive the base business. Housing turnover near multi-decade lows is a live headwind.
- **Margin pressure:** Q4 gross margin guided to 37–38% from ~40% on mix and input/tariff inflation; DC mix is *dilutive* to gross margin, so EBITDA margin expansion depends on operating leverage, not gross margin.
- **Backlog ≠ revenue:** management's own warning that post-Q1 2027 orders may be unfulfillable cuts both ways — it caps 2027 upside and raises the risk that 2028 shows the step-function was a pull-forward.

**Data gaps:** no segment-level P&L or balance-sheet detail was available within budget, so DC vs. residential revenue splits rely on management commentary; peer multiples (CAT, ETN, GEV, CMI) were not retrieved — relative valuation is framed against GNRC's own history and consensus instead. The earnings-revision tool's +10.5% score compares FY2030 to FY2029 consensus and is therefore a period artifact rather than a clean sequential revision read — I weighted the raised FY26 EBITDA guidance and backlog trend instead.
