## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — a hawkish "bear-steepening" reflation, not a classic risk-on or risk-off.**

The defining feature of this cycle is a **policy/policy-market divergence**. The Fed finished an easing campaign (funds rate fell 4.33% → 3.63% between mid-2025 and April 2026), but the long end went the *other* way: the 10Y has risen from 4.48% (1 Jul 2026) to **5.13%**, the 30Y to **5.47%**, and the 2s10s has bear-steepened to **+39bp**. CPI has re-accelerated to **3.8% YoY**, and the Fed has already begun reversing — the effective funds rate ticked *up* to **3.88%** in October. Growth is not the problem (Q3 real GDP **+3.4%**, unemployment **4.1%**); inflation and term premium are.

For Generac this is close to a worst-case mix in the household channel. The easing cycle was a **false dawn for housing**: the Fed cut 70bp while the 10Y rose, so the 30Y mortgage rate went from **5.98% (26 Feb) to 7.11% (12 Nov) — +113bp**. Housing turnover has broken down: existing home sales **3.75M SAAR** in October, down from 4.19M in May and 4.11M a year ago, while housing starts fell to 1,309k (−14% from the March peak). UMich consumer sentiment at **48.1** is at levels last seen in 1980/2022.

Running against that is a genuine structural boom: AI/data-centre power demand. GNRC's data-centre backlog reached **$4.5B** in Q3 2026, anchored by a **$2.4B multiyear Amazon agreement**. So the macro backdrop is **bifurcated**: hostile to the residential franchise, strongly supportive of the commercial/industrial one.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates / mortgage rates — STRONGLY NEGATIVE.** This is the single most important variable. Residential standby generators are a $6–15k discretionary, frequently *financed*, homeowner purchase. Every buyer is a housing transaction or a homeowner with home-equity access. With existing home sales at 3.75M and mortgages at 7.11%, both the buyer pool and the financing channel are contracting. The stock's own tape confirms the sensitivity: GNRC fell from $270 to $205 (−24%) between 1 July and 18 November while the 10Y rose 62bp — roughly **3–4% of equity value per 10bp on the long end**, consistent with a beta of 1.86 and a long-duration growth profile. Note the correlation is with the **10Y, not the funds rate** — the Fed's 2026 cuts did nothing for GNRC because mortgages are priced off a rising long end.

**Growth — POSITIVE but misdirected.** Nominal PCE is running ~6.3% YoY (Sep'26 vs Sep'25), implying ~2.5% real consumer spending — the consumer is *spending*, but rotating toward services, not home-improvement durables. Strong GDP is unambiguously good for the C&I/data-centre segment (utilities, healthcare, telecom, and now hyperscalers), which is where the $4.5B backlog sits.

**Inflation / input costs — NEGATIVE.** Re-accelerating CPI plus a tariff regime on steel, aluminium and copper hits a manufacturer whose products are essentially engines, alternators and **steel enclosures**. The asymmetry is the problem: GNRC has real pricing power in residential (brand, dealer network), but the *fastest-growing* segment — multiyear hyperscaler contracts like the Amazon deal — carries the *weakest* inflation pass-through. Q3 commentary already flagged margin pressure alongside capacity expansion. Expect cost inflation to land disproportionately on the highest-multiple part of the story.

**USD — MODERATELY NEGATIVE.** The broad dollar is up 3.8% in ten weeks. With meaningful European/LatAm exposure, this is a ~1–1.5% translation drag and a competitiveness headwind on exports.

**Credit conditions — NEGATIVE at the margin.** Rising long rates raise the cost of the capacity expansion needed to convert a $4.5B backlog, and compress the multiple on back-end-loaded cash flows. *(Gap: no HY OAS series or GNRC debt-maturity schedule was available to this analysis — refinancing risk is flagged, not quantified.)*

**Policy wildcard — UNCERTAIN.** Reporting points to a Democratic sweep of Congress in the 2026 midterms. That plausibly cuts both ways: favourable for PWRcell/solar and storage incentives, unfavourable on tariffs (could ease input costs) and on corporate tax. Low confidence — single-source.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL, tilting UNFAVOURABLE**

The residential two-thirds of the business faces the worst household-financing backdrop in roughly two decades (7.11% mortgages, 3.75M turnover, 48.1 sentiment, 3.8% CPI). The data-centre third faces the best demand backdrop in the company's history. These roughly offset at the *fundamental* level, but they do not offset at the *multiple* level: the market has re-rated GNRC as an AI-power infrastructure name while its cash flows remain anchored to a housing cycle that is deteriorating. That mismatch is the risk. A rating of Unfavourable becomes appropriate if the 10Y holds above 5.25%; it upgrades to Favourable on any move back below 4.60%.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario (bear, underappreciated)

**"The 10Y goes to 6%, and the AI capex story meets its cost of capital."**

If CPI fails to roll over from 3.8% and the term premium keeps building, the 10Y at 5.75–6.00% takes mortgages to ~8%. Housing turnover breaks below 3.5M, residential deferrals compound into a second lost year, and — critically — the market begins discounting hyperscaler capex *at a higher hurdle rate*. The $4.5B backlog gets de-rated from "visibility" to "capital-intensive, fixed-price, margin-dilutive." GNRC re-tests the November low of ~$169 and trades into the $150s (**−25% to −30%**) despite headline backlog growth. The tell: a +9.9% single-day rally on 18 November into a 5.13% 10Y suggests the stock is now trading on narrative momentum rather than on the rate channel — which is exactly the condition that makes the downside gap violent.

**Bull mirror (secondary):** if a growth scare cracks the labour market and the 10Y snaps back to 4.30%, mortgages near 6% and GNRC is the highest-torque re-rating in Industrials — a 4.5M turnover rate plus multiple expansion could deliver +40–50%. The asymmetry favours owning the *option* on lower long rates, but the macro is not currently cooperating.
