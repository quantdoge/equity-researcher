### 1. Geographic Risk Map

Generac does not publish a full country-level revenue table, but FY2025 disclosures indicate **~$3.47B domestic vs. ~$0.74B international of $4.21B total net sales (~82% / ~18%)**, and the International segment was running ~$197M/quarter in 2025 (Q2 2025 transcript). International manufacturing sits in **Italy (Pramac), Germany (Motortech), Brazil, Mexico, UK (Deep Sea Electronics) and Canada (ecobee)**, with union/works-council labor in Mexico, Italy, Germany and Spain (10-K "Properties"/"Employees").

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**① US trade policy — tariffs on steel, aluminum, copper and Chinese inputs (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High)**
Generac is a heavy steel-enclosure and aluminum-content manufacturer that also imports engines/electronics. The administration raised Section 232 steel/aluminum to 50% (June 2025), then revised the regime twice in 2026 — an April 2, 2026 proclamation imposing a **flat 25% tariff on the full value of derivative articles substantially made of steel, aluminum or copper**, followed by a June 1, 2026 proclamation adjusting rate tiers (White House fact sheet; White & Case; C.H. Robinson). Management has publicly said it is "rethinking its supply chain amid tariffs" (CEO Jagdfeld), and Q2 2026 free cash flow benefited from **tariff-related cash receipts**. Net: margin volatility and working-capital swings tied to policy that can change by proclamation — not by legislation.

**② Hyperscaler/data-center concentration and AI-geopolitics (Probability: Medium / Impact: High)**
Data-center backlog reached **~$4.5B at Q3 2026, nearly tripling from ~$1.6B in July 2026**, including a large Amazon supply agreement (~$2.4B of deliveries expected across 2027–28). This is the single biggest driver of the equity story — and it converts GNRC from a housing/storm-cycle name into a levered play on **AI capex, US tech-decoupling policy, and Gulf/APAC data-sovereignty buildouts**. If export controls on advanced AI compute to the Middle East/Asia tighten, or hyperscaler capex digests, the backlog's conversion (not its existence) is at risk.

**③ Mexico manufacturing + USMCA review (Probability: Medium-High / Impact: Medium)**
Mexican plants are a low-cost export platform into the US, and the **USMCA joint review was scheduled for July 2026** (Congressional Research Service). A renegotiation that raises rules-of-origin stringency or threatens sectoral tariffs on Mexican-built goods would hit C&I/residential cost structure directly. Secondary: Brazil FX/import-duty and repatriation friction on the LatAm business.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **CPSC product-safety enforcement — the recurring theme.** Generac paid a **$15.8M civil penalty (settled 2023)** for allegedly failing to promptly report defects in 32 portable-generator models (Federal Register, May 9, 2023; Arnold & Porter). On **April 16, 2026** it voluntarily recalled **~149,400 portable generators** with CPSC, and a **class action alleging fuel-leak/fire design defect** followed (classaction.org). A separate portable-generator recall was re-announced after further finger-amputation reports.
- **Securities class action — resolved favorably.** The 2023 securities fraud case (arising from a ~25% one-day share decline on residential sales weakness) was **dismissed in February 2026** (Willkie).
- **In re: Generac Solar Power Systems Marketing, Sales Practices & Products Liability Litigation (PWRcell)** — **final settlement approval granted October 22, 2026** (generacsnapsettlement.com).
- **Ongoing legal/regulatory cost drag.** Q3 2026 Adjusted EBITDA included a **$20.2M add-back for legal, regulatory and other costs, of which $14.3M was clean-energy warranty-related** (Q3 2026 8-K/press release). This is a recurring, not one-time, line.
- **Emissions/energy-efficiency regulation.** Small off-road engine (SORE) certification (EPA/CARB) governs portable and small standby units; California's zero-emission SORE standards phase in for generator model years later this decade — a structural design/cost hurdle for the portable lineup.
- **Clean-energy policy reversal.** Termination of the residential §25D solar credit after 2025 (OBBBA) has impaired US residential solar/storage demand — consistent with the clean-energy warranty charges and the PWRcell litigation.

---

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Product-safety reporting controls (elevated).** A prior $15.8M CPSC penalty for *delayed defect reporting*, followed by another voluntary recall in April 2026, is a pattern investors should treat as a governance/controls issue rather than a one-off. Watch for a second civil-penalty referral.
2. **FCPA / third-party distributor risk (moderate).** Sales into Brazil, Mexico, India, Middle East and Africa run largely through independent dealers and distributors — the classic channel for anti-corruption exposure. No disclosed FCPA investigation.
3. **Sanctions/export controls (low).** No disclosed Russia/Iran/Dual-use exposure; Generac exited Russian operations post-2022. New Middle East data-center business warrants end-use/end-user diligence but no red flag identified.
4. **Data privacy (low-moderate).** ecobee (Canada) and Mobile Link remote monitoring collect household energy data — subject to GDPR/PIPEDA/CCPA and state privacy statutes; no disclosed enforcement action.

---

### 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** ~82% of revenue is US-generated under a strong rule-of-law regime, there is no material sanctions or expropriation exposure, and the largest litigation items (securities class action; PWRcell settlement) are now behind the company. That anchors the rating at Medium rather than High. Offsetting factors: (i) tariff policy is the most volatile variable in the P&L and is being set by proclamation rather than statute; (ii) product-safety enforcement history is a genuine, repeated compliance weak point; (iii) the growth engine — a $4.5B data-center backlog including a ~$2.4B Amazon commitment — is concentrated in a handful of hyperscalers and increasingly in geopolitically sensitive regions (Middle East, APAC, Europe); and (iv) Mexican production faces a USMCA review cycle.

**Key monitors:** Section 232 derivative-article rate changes and any China tariff escalation; USMCA review outcome; CPSC/DOJ follow-on action after the April 2026 recall; hyperscaler capex guidance and AI export-control scope; International segment growth rate and FX translation.
