# Insmed Incorporated (INSM) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Price $111.59 | Market cap $24.4B | EV ~$23.2B | 13-mo range $91.88–$211.41**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Insmed has executed one of the most impressive rare-disease-to-large-population launches in recent biotech history: **BRINSUPRI (brensocatib)**, the first and only approved therapy for non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis (NCFB), went from $28.1M in Q3 2025 to **$400.1M in Q3 2026** — a 14x ramp in five quarters — while total company revenue grew **267% year-to-date to $1.26B**. This is no longer a pipeline story: it is a commercial-scale franchise with 85% gross margins, a 4–5% penetrated US market, ex-US still at just $2M/quarter, and management guiding to **>$7B peak BRINSUPRI revenue** plus **>$6B from TPIP** in four Phase 3 respiratory indications. The market is pricing roughly 2029 revenue and assigning close to zero to the second molecule.

## 3. TAM Assessment and Penetration Opportunity

| Franchise | Management peak guidance | Current annualized | Penetration / status |
|---|---|---|---|
| BRINSUPRI (NCFB) | **>$7B** | ~$1.6B US | ~4–5% of diagnosed US patients |
| TPIP (PAH, PH-ILD, PPF, IPF) | **>$6B** | $0 | Phase 3, 4 indications |
| ARIKAYCE (MAC lung disease) | **>$1B** | ~$500M | Refractory-only; label expansion pending |
| **Total platform** | **>$14B** | — | — |

- **NCFB TAM:** An estimated 350–500k diagnosed US adults, with a far larger undiagnosed pool. At ~$88k annual list price, the current ~$1.6B US annualized run-rate implies only **~18,000 patients on drug**. Peak $7B implies roughly 80,000 global patients — still a low-double-digit share of the addressable population. This is a *prevalence* drug, not an ultra-orphan: the ceiling is set by diagnosis rates, payer access, and persistence, not by patient count.
- **International is the underappreciated lever:** Japan launched October 2026 (MHLW approved August 2026); EU approved November 2025. International BRINSUPRI was **$2.0M in Q3'26 — 0.5% of the franchise.** Even modest ex-US uptake adds $1–2B.
- **TPIP** addresses PAH/PH-ILD/IPF — collectively a multi-billion-dollar market where current standard of care is burdensome (infused/inhaled prostacyclins). PALM-ILD is >35% enrolled (completion H1 2028), PALM-PAH first patient July 2026, PALM-PPF initiated September 2026, IPF Phase 3 starting H1 2027.
- **Differentiation:** First-in-class, oral, once-daily DPP1 inhibitor with a 4–5 year regulatory head start in NCFB. Followers (Haisco's HSK31858, AstraZeneca, Zambon, CSL Behring, Chiesi) are behind; Verona's ensifentrine is only in Phase 2 for NCFB. The moat is a *commercial and diagnostic* one — Insmed is currently building the NCFB treatment category and the patient-finding infrastructure itself.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

1. **Jan 28, 2027 — FDA PDUFA for ARIKAYCE sNDA** (Priority Review): expands MAC lung disease label beyond refractory patients to newly diagnosed and recurrent. Converts a ~$500M product into a >$1B one.
2. **Feb 18, 2027 — Q4'26 results and first 2027 guidance.** Consensus Q4 revenue $586M; guidance implies BRINSUPRI ~$458M (+14% QoQ). The key event is 2027 revenue and, critically, the first **profitability/cash-flow breakeven** framework.
3. **Through 2027 — Japan and EU BRINSUPRI ramp.** Japan launched October 2026; a first full ex-US year off a $2M/quarter base is the cheapest incremental growth in the model.
4. **H1 2027 — TPIP Phase 3 IPF initiation**, plus continued enrollment in PALM-ILD/PAH/PPF. Each milestone de-risks the >$6B second leg.
5. **2027 — gene therapy readouts:** INS1201 (DMD, Phase 1 ASCEND enrollment complete) and INS1202 (ALS, ARMOR enrolling) — optionality the market is not paying for, plus INS1033 (DPP1 in rheumatoid arthritis) entering the clinic.
6. **Ongoing — business development.** Insmed added a monoclonal antibody asset alongside the December 2025 CRSsNP discontinuation; management is an active acquirer of adjacent platforms.

The market-implied 5-year revenue at a generous 5x EV/Sales is **~$4.6B** — i.e., today's $23.2B EV only pays for roughly *2029 consensus* and assigns **zero value to TPIP's >$6B peak** or to any revenue beyond 2030. Measured against management's own >$14B peak platform, the company trades at **1.65x peak sales**. A company with a first-in-class, category-defining asset and 53% seven-year revenue CAGR would normally command 4–6x peak sales. That gap is the entire thesis.

Street price targets (11 notes, Sept–Oct 2026) range **$154–$213, median ~$195 — +75% from spot**, with even the lowest target +38%.

- **Base case (12-mo): $180** — ~6.5x 2028E revenue, still a discount to the Street.
- **Bull case: $230** — TPIP de-risking plus ex-US inflection; ~1.6x the >$14B peak.
- **Bear case: $90** — US NCFB growth stalls under 2027 payer pressure and opex keeps FCF negative.

*Why Strong Buy rather than Buy:* the setup is unusually asymmetric. Consensus sits **below the low end of guidance**, revenue beats are widening, the long-dated EPS estimate has been revised up 53%, gross margin is climbing 200bp+/year, 2027 brings the first profit, and the stock is 47% off its December 2025 high on causes unrelated to commercial performance. High conviction, but this is a high-beta, single-asset commercial story — position size accordingly.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Sequential deceleration is real, and the model needs it not to be.** BRINSUPRI QoQ growth has gone +415% → +44% → +49% → **+29%**. At a $1.6B annualized base, getting to >$7B requires sustained growth well into the 2030s. If Q4'26 lands at the implied $458M, deceleration to the mid-teens QoQ is the bear's Exhibit A.
2. **Single-asset concentration.** BRINSUPRI is ~76% of revenue. Any safety signal is existential to the equity — the label already carries dermatologic and hypersensitivity warnings, and the DPP1 class has known gingivitis/periodontitis-type effects that matter in a chronic, largely non-elderly population.
3. **Opex escalation outrunning revenue.** R&D $270M/quarter (+45% YoY, TPIP Phase 3 ×4) and SG&A $253M/quarter together exceed gross profit. FY2026 EPS is still −$1.57 and FCF is still negative; another equity or convertible raise is plausible at peak TPIP spend.
4. **Binary pipeline risk is proven, not theoretical.** The December 2025 CRSsNP Phase 2b failure erased ~19% of market value in a single session — a reminder that Insmed's label-expansion bets can fail. Four simultaneous Phase 3 TPIP programs concentrate a great deal of capital on one molecule.
5. **Pricing and reimbursement.** At ~$88k/year in a *prevalent* rather than orphan indication, BRINSUPRI is squarely in the crosshairs of prior authorization, step therapy, and gross-to-net erosion — the most likely cause of a growth stall. As a small molecule it also becomes IRA Medicare-negotiation eligible ~7 years post-approval (early 2030s), a long-dated but non-trivial ceiling.
6. **Class competition arriving.** Haisco (HSK31858), AstraZeneca, CSL Behring, Chiesi and others are developing DPP1 and anti-inflammatory approaches; Insmed is hiring competitive-intelligence staff for a reason. First-mover advantage is durable but not permanent.
7. **Volatility and estimate-direction divergence.** The 13-month range is $92–$211 (±~60%). And while long-dated *EPS* estimates were revised up, *price targets* were being trimmed through September–October 2026 ($237 → $207 at Barclays; $195 at Mizuho) — the Street is raising 2030 numbers while cutting near-term conviction.
