## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

FY2025 (limited assurance by SCS on Scope 1 & 2 — a genuine plus versus small-cap peers):
- Scope 1: **2,622 tCO₂e**; Scope 2 market-based **2,069 tCO₂e** (location 2,264)
- Scope 3 (2024): **70,522 tCO₂e** → ~94% of footprint is value-chain; total ~75kt
- Carbon intensity ≈ **37–40 tCO₂e per $1M revenue** — roughly an order of magnitude below integrated large pharma
- Electricity 5,626 MWh, only **19% renewable** (weakest E metric)
- Water 1.5M gallons, **77% drawn from low-to-medium-high water-stress regions**
- Waste: 11.03t hazardous, 4.2t regulated medical, 1.74t non-hazardous; annual third-party hazardous-waste audit; green-chemistry principles; cooler/packaging takeback

**Negatives:** no public net-zero or SBTi-validated target was identified; renewable share is low; Scope 3 disclosure lags to FY2024. **Climate transition/physical risk:** minimal — no carbon-intensive assets, no CBAM-exposed goods, manufacturing outsourced to CMOs (which pushes transition cost into Scope 3 and supplier standards rather than owned capex).

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Strengths (evidenced, not just claimed):**
- 100% of employees completed Code of Conduct training; **98% completed role-specific quality training**; 100% PV training across employees and contractors
- VP Pharmacovigilance reports weekly to the CMO (ExCom); annual mock recalls with 3PLs; CMO/CRO/investigator-site GxP audits with CAPA escalation
- Serialisation and DSCSA traceability; Supplier Code of Conduct covering human rights, EHS and quality
- Access infrastructure: inLighten patient support, managed access programmes, proactive payer engagement, "plain-language" trial result summaries

**Risks:**
- **Safety liability is structural, not episodic.** ARIKAYCE's boxed warning and ototoxicity profile generate permanent pharmacovigilance cost and litigation tail. The January 2027 PDUFA to convert accelerated approval is a discrete social/regulatory risk event.
- **Pricing/access is the live controversy vector.** A >$100k/yr specialty respiratory drug in a small prevalent population invites payer restriction, IRA selection and political scrutiny — an ESG issue that arrives as *revenue*, not reputation.
- **Disclosure thinness:** no total recordable incident rate, no turnover data, no quantified clinical-trial diversity metrics (only a policy commitment that enrolment "reflect[s] the potential patient population"). This is a data gap, not evidence of poor performance.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.5 / 10**

**Positives:** 8 of 9 directors independent (89%); **33% women, 33% under-represented minorities**; single class of common stock (no dual-class voting); Lead Independent Director (David Brennan) with a codified, robust mandate; annual say-on-pay; EY ratified at 96.5%; formal WHO/OIG/FCPA/PhRMA/EFPIA-aligned compliance programme.

**Negatives:**
- **Chair/CEO duality** (Lewis holds both) — mitigated but not resolved by the lead independent director
- **Classified/staggered board** (Class II elected to 2029) — an entrenchment feature that reduces director accountability
- **Say-on-pay support of only ~87%** (162.1M for / 23.7M against, 13-May-2026). ~13% dissent is above the ~5–8% norm for a well-governed mid/large cap and signals compensation-design discomfort
- CEO pay ~**$14.5m** at a company still posting quarterly losses (Q3-26 EPS –$0.46) with a large equity component — defensible on TSR alignment, but a recurring proxy-advisor target
- Ongoing Form 144 insider sales by the Chair/CEO (~$0.9M in one reported tranche) — modest and consistent with planned diversification, but worth monitoring

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US SEC climate rule:** stayed; not binding. Climate disclosed voluntarily via TCFD (2025).
- **California SB 253/261:** likely **in scope** — >$1B revenue and doing business in California. CARB implementation is delayed and Scope 3 reporting is contested, but a Scope 1/2/3 inventory already exists (assured S1/S2), so compliance cost is low.
- **EU CSRD:** US-parented group with EU commercial operations; under the original framework a third-country group exceeding €150m EU turnover is in scope, but the Omnibus "stop-the-clock" has pushed non-EU wave reporting to ~FY2027–28. Insmed's existing TCFD + assured S1/S2 positions it better than most peers of its size.
- **EU HTA Regulation (from Jan-2025)** and **EU Pharma Package reform** — direct access/pricing relevance for the coming EU brensocatib launch.
- **IRA Medicare negotiation:** brensocatib (small molecule) reaches MFP eligibility roughly seven years post-approval (~early 2030s); ARIKAYCE could be selected in nearer cycles. This is the most financially consequential "ESG" rule for INSM.
- **Japan MHLW/NHI:** BRINSUPRI approved Aug-2026 — exposes the drug to biennial NHI price revision.
- **CBAM:** not applicable. **ESG-linked debt:** none identified (convertible notes are conventional, not sustainability-linked).

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

Insmed is a **reporting-quality leader for its size but a risk-profile laggard on S** — assured Scope 1/2 emissions, a TCFD report, 89% board independence and a genuine GxP/pharmacovigilance architecture place it ahead of typical $20B-cap biotechs; but the intrinsic product-safety profile, the access/affordability friction of a >$100k rare-disease drug, Chair/CEO duality, a staggered board, ~13% say-on-pay dissent, and the absence of any climate target keep it out of Leader territory. Environmental risk contributes almost nothing either way to the investment case.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

- **ESG is not a valuation driver here, and the non-financial risks that do matter are already the ones equity analysts underwrite:** brensocatib safety, ARIKAYCE's January-2027 approval conversion, and net-price trajectory. Frame ESG diligence as **regulatory/safety diligence**, not values screening.
- **No exclusion risk** for mainstream ESG funds: single share class, high board independence, no controversies, no fossil or tobacco linkage. Norms-based screens *could* raise pricing/access questions, but Insmed's MAPs and patient-support infrastructure are a mitigating disclosure.
- **Where ESG changes the thesis:** (i) a brensocatib dermatologic or class safety signal — the dominant ESG-adjacent drawdown risk; (ii) an FDA complete response on the ARIKAYCE sNDA (Jan-2027) — impairs ~25% of revenue; (iii) IRA/HTA-driven net-price compression arriving faster than modelled.
- **Governance watch-items for engagement:** separate Chair/CEO, de-stagger the board, and address the ~13% say-on-pay dissent via clearer performance-based vesting. None is thesis-breaking; all are low-cost fixes that would lift the score toward 8.
- **Data caveat for the memo:** INSM is under-covered by third-party ESG raters (1,664 employees, single-thesis biotech). Expect thin Sustainalytics/MSCI coverage and do not rely on vendor scores — the evidence above is drawn from primary disclosures and SEC voting records.
