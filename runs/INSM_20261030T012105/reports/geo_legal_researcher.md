## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**① US MFN pricing + Section 232 pharmaceutical tariffs — HIGH probability, HIGH impact**
The **April 2, 2026 executive order** converted twelve months of Section 232 pressure into enforceable trade law: a **default 100% tariff on imported patented pharmaceuticals and ingredients**, with reduced rates (≈15% for EU/Japan-origin, ≈20% for companies with approved onshoring plans) and a path to zero for firms signing **MFN pricing agreements** plus domestic-manufacturing commitments (Hogan Lovells, EY, White House EO text). MFN deals announced to date cover large caps plus a mid-cap cohort (Alcon, Astellas, BeOne, BridgeBio, CSL, Kyowa Kirin, Sun Pharma, Teva, UCB — *FiercePharma*) — **Insmed is not among the named signatories in retrieved sources**, and I found no Insmed onshoring/MFN announcement. Two channels of exposure: (a) duty on any imported ARIKAYCE/BRINSUPRI finished product or API, and (b) MFN-driven reference pricing pressure on BRINSUPRI, whose $1.35–1.4B guide carries essentially no ex-US price offset. Probability of some adverse pricing/tariff touch: **high (>70%)**; impact: **$150–400M of revenue/EBIT at risk in a severe case** — material against a ~$24B cap trading on 2027+ TPIP optionality.

**② Japan commercial/pricing execution — MEDIUM probability, MEDIUM impact**
Japan is Insmed's only scaled ex-US market and its most important non-US regulatory relationship (MHLW/PMDA). BRINSUPRI launched in Japan in October 2026 as the first approved NCFB therapy — a genuine pricing-power window, but one subject to Japan's annual NHI drug-price revision and reopening rules. Layer on **escalating China–Japan trade/export-control friction** (China's 2026 export-control lists naming Japanese entities; CMTradeLaw, Japan Policy Forum), which raises indirect risk to Japan-based operations and any regional sourcing. Probability of a meaningful Japan pricing/access setback: **medium (~30–40%)**; impact: modest today but rising as BRINSUPRI Japan ramps.

**③ EU/UK reimbursement fragmentation + parallel trade — HIGH probability, LOW–MEDIUM impact**
EC approval (Nov 2025) and UK approval (Feb 2026) are de-risked on the *regulatory* axis, but not on the *payer* axis: 27 national HTA bodies plus NICE mean country-by-country price negotiation, and reference-pricing/parallel-import leakage from lower-priced EU markets into the UK. Combined with EU HTA joint clinical assessment and GDPR/APPI data obligations across the four-arm global PALM program, this is a steady drag rather than a shock. The offsetting positive: EU/UK launches diversify away from a single US payer.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **FDA — ARIKAYCE sNDA (binary catalyst, Jan 28, 2027).** FDA accepted the sNDA for MAC lung disease with **Priority Review and a PDUFA date of January 28, 2027**, covering newly diagnosed and recurrent patients. Approval materially expands the label; a CRL would be the single largest near-term legal/regulatory event for the stock.
- **PMDA — ARIKAYCE label expansion.** ENCORE Phase 3b data (positive topline, March 2026) to be filed with PMDA in **Q4 2026**.
- **Litigation: no material pending matters identified.** Searches across 2026 news and SEC metadata returned **no active securities class action, DOJ/FTC/SEC investigation, or material patent suit** against Insmed. What surfaced is legacy and immaterial: a 2006 patent verdict ($7.5M + royalties) and the 2007 Tercica/IPLEX settlement — both long resolved. The 2016 Stanford Securities Class Action Clearinghouse entry relates to the withdrawn ARIKAYCE EMA filing and is historical.
- **IP cliff risk (underappreciated).** ARIKAYCE's 7-year orphan-drug exclusivity from its 2018 approval has lapsed, so it now rests on the patent estate — relevant as ARIKAYCE is still ~25% of 2026E revenue. BRINSUPRI, as a **small molecule**, faces the IRA's 7-year (not 13-year biologic) negotiation clock, putting it in the ~2032 Medicare-negotiation cohort unless the policy changes.
- **Pipeline-sourced IP:** INS1148 (anti-SCF248 mAb) acquired Dec 2025 from **Opsidio, LLC** (University of Michigan spinout) — a US academic origin, so **no China license/BIOSECURE-style exposure** in the current pipeline. INS1201/INS1202 are in-house/partnered AAV gene therapies under FDA INDs.
- **Trial/regulatory execution load:** four parallel Phase 3 PALM studies (PAH, PH-ILD, PPF, IPF) plus Phase 1 gene-therapy INDs — a large multi-country CTA/IRB/GDPR surface for a 1,664-person company.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

**No hard red flags identified.** Items to monitor rather than alarms:

1. **Anti-kickback / False Claims Act.** A new US launch (BRINSUPRI) with an expanding field force, plus Medicare/Medicaid/VA and 340B-adjacent channels, is the highest-risk commercial profile in pharma. No OIG/DOJ matter disclosed.
2. **FCPA/UK Bribery Act.** Exposure is limited to Japan and Europe (low-corruption-index jurisdictions) and rare-disease patient-support programs — low inherent risk; no disclosed matter.
3. **MFN/tariff compliance.** If Insmed executes an onshoring or MFN agreement, pricing and reporting covenants become enforceable undertakings — a new class of regulatory obligation.
4. **Sanctions/AML.** Effectively nil — no sanctioned-market revenue, no disclosed Russia/China operations.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** *Geopolitical* risk in the narrow sense is **LOW** — INSM earns nothing in Russia, China, or emerging markets, operates only in rule-of-law jurisdictions (US, Japan, EU/UK), and has no nationalisation, currency-control, or repatriation exposure. What lifts the composite to MEDIUM is **US policy concentration**: ~87–90% of 2026E revenue will come from a single market simultaneously implementing MFN reference pricing, Section 232 tariffs, and IRA negotiation, while Insmed has no identified MFN/onshoring accommodation. Add a binary FDA decision (Jan 28, 2027) and a lapsed ARIKAYCE orphan exclusivity. Legal risk proper is **low** — no material litigation, investigations, or compliance matters surfaced in any retrieved source.

**What would change the rating:** (a) evidence that ARIKAYCE/BRINSUPRI finished product or API is imported without a tariff-relief agreement → upgrade toward **High**; (b) an Insmed MFN/onshoring deal announced → downgrade toward **Low–Medium**; (c) any DOJ/OIG inquiry tied to the BRINSUPRI launch, or a CRL on the ARIKAYCE sNDA → **High**.

**Gaps to close in diligence:** the actual geographic revenue table (10-K Note on segment/revenue disaggregation), Insmed's specific manufacturing/API sourcing footprint, and confirmation of whether any MFN/tariff negotiation is underway.
