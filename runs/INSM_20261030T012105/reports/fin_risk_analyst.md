## 1. Credit Risk Assessment
**Rating: Sub‑Investment Grade — but improving, and NOT distressed in a cash-flow sense.**

Insmed is a commercial-stage biotech with a genuinely bifurcated credit profile: an *income statement* that still looks impaired (negative EBIT, negative FCF, negative interest coverage) sitting on top of a *balance sheet* that is net-cash and comfortably liquid.

The critical nuance is the trajectory. FY2025 was the trough of the J-curve (brensocatib launch spend): revenue $606M, opex $1.83B, FCF **–$968M**. Through 2026 the model inflected hard:

| Quarter | Revenue | Gross margin | EBIT | Net loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q4 2025 | $263.8M | 83.2% | –$307.7M | –$328.5M |
| Q1 2026 | $306.0M | 83.8% | –$142.0M | –$163.6M |
| Q2 2026 | $425.5M | 84.2% | **+$8.9M** | –$13.2M |
| Q3 2026 | $524.4M | **85.1%** | –$77.2M | –$100.3M |

Q3 2026 revenue grew **+268% YoY**. TTM revenue ≈ $1.52B at an 85% gross margin. Q2 2026 delivered the first positive operating quarter in company history. Interest coverage has improved from **–14.0x** (FY2025) to **–3.8x** (Q3 2026 annualised) and should cross 1.0x within 2–4 quarters on current gross-profit growth.

Leverage is modest in absolute terms: total debt $741M vs. equity $726M (**D/E 1.02x**), cash and securities $1.147B, **net debt ≈ $405M**. Against TTM EBITDA of –$494M the net-debt/EBITDA ratio is not meaningful; on a forward basis (annualised Q3 gross profit of $1.78B against ~$2.1B annualised opex) the company is within one to two quarters of EBITDA-positive.

**Debt structure:** Insmed redeemed all remaining $569.5M of its 0.75% converts due 2028 in April 2025 and refinanced into a secured senior term loan (SOFR-based, ~9.60% with a 2% exit fee), supplementing fixed-rate notes. Blended cost of debt is now ~11% ($82M annualised interest on $741M), which is expensive and reflects the sub-investment-grade profile. The company drew approximately **$161M of additional debt in Q3 2026** (total debt $580M → $741M), which is the first sign that internally generated cash is no longer sufficient to fund the launch without balance-sheet support.

Insmed has been an exceptional performer — roughly a **10x** from July 2024 lows (~$22) to the December 2025 peak — which is precisely why the risk is now asymmetric. The stock is **–29.6% off its high** and has been in a persistent downtrend for three months ($211 → $149), a classic post-launch "sell the news" de-rating.

Volatility of 55.5% annualised implies a one-day 95% VaR of roughly **–5.8%** and a one-day 99% VaR of **–8.2%**; a one-month 95% VaR is approximately **–26%**. Position sizing must account for this.

The low beta (0.84 vs. XBI) is **not** a comfort. It reflects that the variance is overwhelmingly idiosyncratic — clinical, regulatory, launch-execution and reimbursement news — rather than market-driven. In a broad market drawdown, expect roughly market-like participation, but with a high-multiple growth overlay that could amplify de-rating. The stock's correlation to the sector is low; its correlation to its own catalysts is near 1.

Sharpe of 0.60 on 55% volatility is a genuinely strong risk-adjusted record, though it is backward-looking and heavily dependent on the 2025 melt-up.

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis — Revenue –20%

**Starting point (TTM):** Revenue $1,520M · Gross profit $1,292M (85%) · Opex $1,810M · EBIT –$518M.

| Scenario (revenue –20%) | Revenue | Opex action | EBIT | Est. FCF | Cash | Runway |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base case, no cost action | $1,216M | none | –$595M | ≈ –$685M | $1,147M | **~1.7 yrs** |
| Partial mitigation | $1,216M | –10% opex | –$595M→–$414M* | ≈ –$505M | $1,147M | **~2.3 yrs** |
| Aggressive mitigation | $1,216M | –20% opex | –$233M | ≈ –$325M | $1,147M | **~3.5 yrs** |
| Combined (price + volume) shock | $1,064M | –10% opex | ≈ –$500M | ≈ –$590M | $1,147M | **~1.9 yrs** |

\*10% opex cut ($181M) applied from a $1,810M base.

**Interpretation.** A 20% revenue shortfall does **not** create a solvency event. It creates a **financing event**. Even in the harshest frame the company has roughly 1.7 years of liquidity, and the first maturity wall is not until 2027 (term loan) with the 2028 converts already retired. Interest is $82M/year against $1.15B of cash — trivially serviceable.

The real risk is **reflexivity**. A 20% revenue miss would almost certainly be accompanied by a 40–50% equity de-rating (this stock has repeatedly fallen 30%+ on single disclosures — e.g. –18% on 18 Dec 2025 alone). The company would then need to raise $500M–$1B of equity into a depressed share price, crystallising meaningful dilution — perhaps 4–8% of shares outstanding — at precisely the wrong moment. **The damage from the downside case is borne by the equity holder, not the creditor.**

A secondary consideration: at 16x TTM revenue, the valuation embeds brensocatib peak-sales assumptions. There is no valuation cushion to absorb an execution disappointment. Downside does not require a covenant breach to be painful.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating

# MODERATE

Insmed is **not** a financial-distress story — it has $1.15B of liquidity, a 2.86x current ratio, net debt of only ~$405M, no near-term maturity wall, and accelerating 85%-gross-margin revenue that puts EBITDA-positive within a quarter or two. Liquidity and going-concern risk are **low**.

The rating is capped below "Low" by three things: (i) **persistently negative free cash flow** and a ~$400–700M annualised burn that is now being part-funded by new debt draws; (ii) **serial dilution** — 78% share-count growth in four years and stock comp at 16% of revenue — which transfers risk to equity holders; and (iii) **extreme idiosyncratic volatility** (55.5% annualised, 56% max drawdown) in a stock priced for perfection.

Directionally, risk is **improving** at the fastest rate in the company's history. Twelve months ago this would have been a High rating; twelve months forward, on the current revenue slope, it plausibly reaches Low-to-Moderate.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Negative FCF of –$968M in FY2025 (–4.1% FCF yield)** — cumulative four-year burn of ~$2.6B. The single largest risk.
2. **Cash declining and debt rising simultaneously.** Cash and securities fell $1,430M → $1,147M while total debt rose $580M → $741M in Q3 2026 — the company drew ~$161M of debt to fund operations. Watch this closely; it is the leading indicator of a future capital raise.
3. **Negative interest coverage (–14.0x FY2025, –3.8x Q3 2026)** and an expensive ~11% blended cost of debt ($82M/yr).
4. **Serial dilution.** 123.0M → 218.8M shares since 2022 (+78%). Piotroski explicitly fails the dilution test. Stock comp is 16% of revenue.
5. **Altman Z in the distress zone on a book basis (–3.955)** — structurally driven by the $5.63B accumulated deficit; mitigated to ~+2.32 (grey zone) on a market-equity basis, but not a clean pass.
6. **Single-franchise concentration transitioning to a two-product model.** ARIKAYCE remains the base; brensocatib is now the entire growth thesis and is early in launch. Reimbursement or competitive setbacks would hit revenue, cash and valuation simultaneously.
7. **Valuation/multiple risk.** ~16x TTM revenue on a $24.4B market cap. The stock is –29.6% from its December 2025 high and has trended down for three months — momentum has already turned.
8. **Governance/disclosure:** no public credit rating and no CDS spread available (unrated convert/term-loan issuer). Credit risk must be assessed from statements alone.
