### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk #1 — Tariffs and trade-policy volatility hitting a hardwarised, Asia-centric supply chain**
- *Probability: High (already materialising). Impact: Medium.*
- FY2025 10-K added **two new risk factors**: (i) "Unfavorable changes in geopolitical conditions… Wars, regional instability, and U.S.-China tariffs, export controls, investment restrictions, and technology-transfer rules could disrupt supply and sales"; and (ii) "Tariffs and other import measures imposed by the United States or other countries may adversely affect our business."
- Contract-manufacturer risk factor was ~96% rewritten to add **Vietnam**, climate, labour practices and "limited cyber and data-security control" over CMs — confirming a CM footprint in Vietnam/Thailand/Mexico/Malaysia via partners (Fabrinet, Flex). Ciena has partly mitigated via **US manufacturing expansion with Flex to serve BEAD-funded projects** (Ciena press release).
- Evidence of live P&L impact: Q3 FY2026 gross margin (45.4% vs 41.3%) improved partly on **"tariff recoveries"** — i.e., tariffs are already in the numbers, with recovery not guaranteed to repeat.
- Watch item: purchase commitments to CMs/suppliers reached **$3.3B** at 1 Aug 2026 (from $2.1B at FY2025 year-end) — a large fixed obligation that is exposed to any tariff or export-control change of regime.

**Risk #2 — Extreme customer concentration in US hyperscalers, i.e. concentrated exposure to one policy bloc**
- *Probability of a demand shock: Medium. Impact: High.*
- FY2025: **top five customers ≈50% of revenue**, including one cloud provider at **18%** and one service provider at **11%** (FY2025 10-K; IR: "two customers represented 10%-plus… for a total of 28.4%"). This was newly *quantified* in the FY2025 10-K — a governance signal.
- 9M FY2026: **Cloud provider A $1,129M and Cloud provider B $692M ≈ 39% of total revenue** (10-Q, 1 Aug 2026).
- Consequence: Ciena's growth is now effectively a leveraged bet on **US AI capex policy and hyperscaler antitrust/regulatory treatment**, not on global telecom. Any US-driven shock (AI capex digestion, Section 232 scope, antitrust-driven capex scrutiny) transmits to ~40–50% of revenue with no offsetting geographic diversification.

**Risk #3 — US–China techno-nationalism cutting both ways (supplier exposure vs. vendor-ban tailwind)**
- *Probability: Medium-High. Impact: Medium.*
- *Tailwind*: FCC Secure and Trusted Communications Networks "rip-and-replace" programme plus **$3B in NDAA funding** to remove Huawei/ZTE gear — Ciena is a primary Western beneficiary, but funding has been chronically short (FCC: only ~14 companies completed replacement as of 2026), so the revenue is politically contingent and slow.
- *Headwind*: China's export-control architecture (rare earths, gallium, and 2026 additions covering AI/biopharma/solar-adjacent dual-use tech; new blocking/counter-sanctions and trade-secret tools effective late 2026) threatens sole/limited-source optical and semiconductor inputs. The FY2025 10-K newly elevated **sole-source optical/semiconductor supplier risk**.
- *Offsetting positive*: APAC revenue has fallen from $633M (FY2023) to $431M (FY2025) and 7.6% of revenue in Q1 FY2026 — Ciena has *already* de-risked from the highest-friction theatre.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation / investigations:** No material pending litigation, government investigation, or adverse judgment against Ciena surfaced in any search of filings or news. The FY2025 10-K reworded (rather than refreshed with facts) the standard risk factor to "**Legal proceedings, including government investigations and other claims or disputes, may be costly to defend**." Patent-litigation records for Ciena are thin (three historical cases; the Nortel patent dispute was settled years ago). **Conclusion: legal risk is currently a low-single-digit-percent-of-market-cap issue at most — narrative, not actionable.**

**Regulatory posture (mostly favourable, with contingent elements):**
- **FCC / BEAD / rip-and-replace** — structural demand tailwind in the US; Ciena is investing in domestic manufacturing specifically to qualify for BEAD-funded projects. Risk: programme funding gaps and political reprioritisation.
- **Product security & cyber regulation** — FY2025 10-K added a *new* risk factor on "increased regulation of product security, cybersecurity and data practices," plus a new AI-specific legal/regulatory risk factor. EU Cyber Resilience Act conformity will raise product cost/compliance overhead in ~15% of revenue.
- **Tax** — risk factor rewritten (~80%) to cover **global minimum tax (Pillar Two)**, profit-allocation and manufacturing-sourcing effects, and ongoing tax examinations. Expect a modest structural tax-rate headwind on non-US profit.
- **Export controls** — networking/optical gear is dual-use under the EAR; the 10-K flags export controls and technology-transfer rules explicitly. No BIS enforcement action disclosed.

**Capital structure (legal-adjacent):** June 2026 issuance of **$2.875B 0.00% Convertible Senior Notes due 2031**, alongside **$988.4M of note-hedge transactions and $873.4M of warrants** — a large, complex ISDA/equity-derivative documentation set with counterparty (bank) exposure. Not a legal risk per se, but it materially raises the complexity of any future restructuring or change-of-control scenario.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale for Medium (not Low):** ~76–78% of revenue sits in the Americas with an investment-grade legal and regulatory environment and no material litigation — that anchors the rating downward. But three structural features prevent a "Low":
1. **Single-bloc concentration** — US hyperscaler dependence (≈40% from two cloud providers) makes Ciena's revenue a derivative of US AI/trade policy.
2. **Asian manufacturing dependency** — Mexico/Thailand/Vietnam/Malaysia CM footprint plus China-linked component sourcing, against a $3.3B purchase-commitment book, is directly exposed to tariff and export-control regime shifts. Management has already booked "tariff recoveries" into gross margin, i.e. the exposure is real, not theoretical.
3. **Politically contingent US tailwind** — a meaningful share of US growth rests on Huawei rip-and-replace and BEAD funding, both of which have a history of under-funding and delay.

**Why not High:** no sanctions exposure, no active government investigation, minimal China revenue, an exited Russia position, APAC share already halved since FY2023, and a de minimis litigation docket.

**Key monitorables:** (a) any Section 232 action covering optical networking equipment or semiconductors; (b) CM country mix (Vietnam/Mexico) and tariff-recovery reversal; (c) FCPA/enforcement news in Gulf/India channel; (d) FCC rip-and-replace and BEAD funding decisions; (e) whether hyperscaler concentration rises further above 50% of revenue.
