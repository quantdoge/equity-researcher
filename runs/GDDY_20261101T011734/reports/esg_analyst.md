## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW** (disclosure quality: Medium)

- **Carbon intensity is structurally low.** ~$5.2B revenue against a footprint concentrated in leased/owned data centers and offices. GoDaddy reports **~88% reduction in Scope 1+2 (market-based) emissions vs. 2019 baseline**, against a **90%-by-2030 target** set in early 2024.
- **Caveat — "market-based" is doing the work.** The reduction is substantially renewable-energy-attribute driven; Scope 2 location-based and **Scope 3 (hardware, colocation, cloud, AI compute) are not quantified in the disclosure reviewed**. As the Airo/agentic-AI platform scales, energy demand inflects upward — a target set for a pre-AI workload profile is at risk of drift.
- **No material environmental controversies, fines, or remediation liabilities identified.** No stranded-asset exposure; no CBAM-relevant goods.
- **Physical climate risk: low** — hybrid work model has shrunk the owned real-estate footprint (former Tempe HQ closed).
- **Gap flagged:** absolute mtCO₂e figures and full Scope 3 inventory were not retrievable within budget (SEC.gov blocked automated access; 10-K/DEF 14A unavailable via the filing tool). Ratings above rely on the 2025 Global Stakeholder Impact Report and third-party republications.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Negatives (material):**
- **Cybersecurity/privacy is the single largest ESG liability.** The **FTC finalized a consent order (2025)** over alleged lax data security tied to breaches spanning **2019–2022**; the 2021 cPanel compromise affected ~1.2M WordPress customers, and a multi-year intrusion was disclosed in Feb 2023. The order imposes **prescriptive cybersecurity program requirements and independent third-party assessments every two years for 20 years**. Direct cost is modest (est. **$5–15M/yr**), but the *governance* cost is that security posture is now externally supervised — and the reputational asset ("trusted partner for 20M+ SMBs") is exactly what the brand monetizes.
- **Serial restructuring and morale.** Headcount has fallen from ~9,000+ (2020) to **5,845**, via repeated layoffs (2020 ~12%, 2022 ~8%, subsequent AI-related reallocations). Public employee reviews describe high turnover, declining morale, and offshoring of customer service — a **customer-experience and execution risk** directly tied to the Airo monetization thesis (ARPU growth depends on guided support).
- **Trust & safety exposure** is inherent to a registrar/host: abuse, extremist content, and domain-fronting incidents create periodic media and ICANN/payment-processor friction. GoDaddy's materiality assessment correctly elevates trust & safety as a priority topic.

**Positives:** 13-topic double-materiality assessment; formal pay-parity analysis and reporting under the Compensation & Human Capital Committee; community programs (Empower, Small Business Research Lab) that support the SMB social licence and are mildly brand-accretive. Net: **Medium**, not Critical.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **6.5 / 10**

**Strengths:**
- **Board: 9 directors, 8 independent (89%)** — strong; committee structure (Audit & Risk; Compensation & Human Capital; Nominating & Governance) assigns explicit sustainability oversight to the Nominating & Governance Committee, with a cross-functional Sustainability Working Group reporting to management.
- **Dual-class structure eliminated.** Class B had collapsed to 25 shares by Feb 2024 — GDDY is now effectively **single-class, one-share-one-vote**. This is a genuine, under-appreciated improvement in shareholder rights (no poison pill identified).
- Sustainability reporting aligned to GRI + SASB (Internet Media & Services) + TCFD, with a published double-materiality assessment — above-median for a $15B software company.
- Capital returns are aggressive (large buyback authorization) and FCF-focused, aligned with shareholder value.

**Weaknesses:**
- **Securities class action (SDNY, §§10(b)/20(a))**: plaintiffs allege materially false statements about the **"high-intent customer" acquisition strategy and promotional/front-loaded pricing**; stock fell **14.28% ($13.18) on Feb 25, 2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline Oct 2026. This is a **disclosure-controls** governance event, not a routine nuisance suit — it goes to the credibility of the customer-growth KPI the market prices.
- **Compensation architecture rewards EPS/FCF optics.** Heavy reliance on margin expansion plus buybacks supports EPS while revenue growth sits mid-single-digit; this creates incentive to under-invest in security/support (the exact area of regulatory failure).
- **Executive turnover and insider sales** (CEO 10b5-1 sales, e.g., 4,500 shares at $83.15 in Aug 2026) — routine but worth monitoring alongside the litigation timeline.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US FTC Act §5** — binding consent order, biennial third-party security audits for 20 years (highest-conviction exposure).
- **SEC** — climate disclosure rule (currently stayed/uncertain); GDDY's exposure is low given limited Scope 1/2, but Scope 3 would be the binding gap if reinstated. Securities-litigation exposure is live and higher-probability than any climate rule.
- **EU CSRD** — GDDY has EU operations (Amsterdam, European registrar entities); likely in scope of the wave-in for large non-EU groups via EU subsidiaries. Preparedness is **moderate**: double materiality done, but Scope 3 quantification and limited assurance are not evidenced.
- **EU NIS2 / DORA-adjacent cyber rules & GDPR** — material given hosting/registrar role and EU payment operations.
- **ICANN / registrar obligations** — abuse-handling and WHOIS/privacy compliance; quasi-regulatory.
- **CBAM:** not applicable. **ESG-linked debt covenants:** none identified.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE**

A low-carbon, broadly well-governed company whose ESG risk is concentrated in **one critical area — cyber/privacy/trust & safety — where it has already been formally sanctioned**, and in **disclosure credibility**, where it is now being sued. Governance fundamentals (89% independent board, single-class shares, committee-level ESG oversight, TCFD/SASB reporting) are better than the peer median for mid-cap infrastructure software. Environmental risk is genuinely low. The drags are regulatory-supervised security and litigation over customer-growth disclosure.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

ESG is **not a thesis driver here either way** — it neither creates a sustainable moat nor threatens the cash-flow engine. Three monitorables for the investment memo:

1. **Litigation outcome/scope** (SDNY class action) — if discovery shows KPI definitions were stretched, it undermines the "high-intent customer" ARPU narrative that underpins the multiple. Watch for settlement quantum and any restatement of customer metrics.
2. **FTC order compliance cadence** — any repeat breach post-2025 would be a severe licence-to-operate event for a company selling trust to 20M+ SMBs; treat as a low-probability/high-severity tail.
3. **Human capital vs. AI pivot** — a 5,845 headcount supporting an AI-led ARPU strategy with deteriorating employee sentiment is an execution risk, not an ESG scorecard item. Rising support costs or churn would show up before any ESG metric does.

**Not a reason to exclude; not a reason to pay up.** Treat governance/litigation as a valuation-risk overlay on the existing fundamental thesis.
