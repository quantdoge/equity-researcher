## Non-Financial Risk Assessment — GoDaddy Inc. (NYSE: GDDY)

**Snapshot:** $117.24/share, ~$14.85B market cap, Tempe AZ, CEO Amanpal (Aman) Bhutani, 5,845 FTE, beta 0.94. Data as of the most recent filings (Q3 2026 10-Q filed 2026-10-30).

---

### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE** (structure strong; disclosure culture weak)

**Positives**
- **Independent board chair** — Brian Sharples (ex-HomeAway co-founder/CEO, director since 2016) serves as Chair; Bhutani is CEO and a director. Chair/CEO roles are separated — meaningful check on power concentration.
- **Independent director sessions** and a combined **Audit and Risk Committee** with a published charter; board refresh visible (Graham Smith added 2024, additional director added 2026).
- Full governance suite published: Code of Conduct & Ethics, **Speak Up (whistleblower) Policy**, Corporate Governance Guidelines, Political Contributions Policy, Human Rights/Anti-Slavery statements.
- CEO is a professional manager (since 2019), not the founder — founder/insider control risk is low; PE sponsors (KKR/Silver Lake/TCV) have long since exited the register.

**Negative — the decisive one**
- The **Feb 2026 episode is a disclosure-controls failure**: management changed go-to-market pricing on .com domains (1-year promotional terms vs. typical 3-year) and investors/analysts learned of it only on the Q4'25 call, when bookings growth decelerated to 5% and guidance was missed. Even if the conduct is not fraudulent, it demonstrates that material commercial/strategy changes were **not surfaced through IR or disclosure channels** — a governance/culture issue inside an otherwise well-papered structure.
- **Compensation alignment not verifiable** — the DEF 14A was not retrievable (see Gaps). Insider behaviour (below) is the best available proxy for alignment, and it is one-directional.

---

### 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — **ELEVATED (two open matters)**

**a) FTC data-security consent order (final, May 21, 2025) — durable compliance burden**
- FTC alleged that **since 2018** GoDaddy failed to implement reasonable security and **misrepresented "award-winning security"**; complaint filed January 2025. Final order May 2025.
- **No monetary penalty** and no admission of fault (company confirmed). This removes financial-sting risk but not the burden: mandated comprehensive information-security program, **biennial independent third-party assessments**, and an FTC-listed status of **"Under Order"** as of October 2026. Failure of an assessment is an enforcement trigger and a headline risk each cycle.
- **Data-privacy class/general exposure remains** — GDPR/CCPA/state privacy plus hosting-customer contracts give a large plaintiff surface.

**b) Securities fraud class action — *Johnson v. GoDaddy Inc.*, No. 1:26-cv-07144 (S.D.N.Y., Judge Gregory H. Woods)**
- Class period **Sept 3, 2025 – Feb 24, 2026**; consolidated lead-plaintiff deadline ~Oct 20–26, 2026 (now passed).
- Allegation: defendants failed to disclose the promotional .com pricing with 1-year terms, which caused bookings deceleration and a guidance miss — i.e., **a Section 10(b) disclosure case about a commercial strategy change, not an accounting-integrity case**. Scienter must still be proven; the shareholders' bar is actively soliciting (Rosen, Pomerantz, Bleichmar Fonti, Glancy Prongay, Bronstein, Kaplan Fox, Kessler Topaz, Grabar).
- **Mitigating market fact:** the stock fell **14.28% on Feb 25, 2026** ($92.30 → $79.12, intraday low $73.06) but has since rallied to **$117.24 — above the pre-drop price**. Damages modeling is constrained by this recovery, but legal spend and the disclosure-quality read-through persist.

**c) Registrar-specific / other:** an **FTC warning letter dated Aug 31, 2026** concerning a GoDaddy-registered domain impersonating the FTC — indicative of persistent **domain-abuse and WHOIS/RDAP accuracy (ICANN compliance)** exposure inherent to the registrar business, plus money-transmission licensing for GoDaddy Payments. No SEC enforcement actions surfaced in EDGAR full-text search for the ticker.

### 3. Insider Activity Analysis — **NET SELLING, ZERO BUYING (negative)**

- **~37 Form 4s since December 2025; effectively all are dispositions.** No open-market purchases identified in the period.
- **CEO Aman Bhutani: ~500,053 shares / ~$57.4M** sold cumulatively. Recent cadence: 4,500 shares on Aug 3, Sept 1 (two tranches, $97.44), Oct 1 ($97.50) — a repeatable monthly quantum strongly consistent with a **pre-scheduled Rule 10b5-1 plan** rather than discretionary dumping, which is the key mitigating read.
- **CFO Mark McCaffrey: ~103,728 shares / ~$11.9M**; **CLO Jared Sine: ~70,164 shares / ~$8.05M**. Roughly **$2M net divestment in the last 90 days**.
- **Interpretation:** programmatic selling is not a red flag per se, but two things are: (i) sales at **$97–100 in Aug–Oct 2026**, well below today's $117, indicate these are mechanical plans not informed by a near-term view; and (ii) **not one insider bought the February/March 2026 drawdown at ~$80** — the absence of any conviction purchase during a 14% shock is the more telling signal about internal confidence in the growth narrative.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Customer-acquisition and bookings-quality fragility (the actual core risk).** The company reached for a 1-year promotional .com price to hit demand, which diluted contract term and average order value. Q3 2026 shows the model working (revenue $1.34B, +6%; bookings +6%; normalized EBITDA margin 33%, +120bps; EPS $2.08 vs $1.51), but **"customer growth remains modest"** — growth is now coming from **ARPU/monetization (Airo) rather than net new customers**. Mid-single-digit growth with a promotional lever available is a fragile operating posture.
2. **AI platform / vendor dependency on Airo.** Airo has doubled to a **>$100M annualized bookings run rate** with 70%+ of Airo customers holding 2+ paid products — it is now the growth thesis. That concentrates execution risk in third-party model/infrastructure dependencies, an unproven agentic product, and **visible product-quality complaints** (users reporting the AI altering site content unprompted). A material Airo quality or safety incident would hit both growth and brand.
3. **Cybersecurity posture under a regulator's microscope.** Breach history is unusually thick: **Nov 2021 — up to 1.2M Managed WordPress customers** exposed (email, customer numbers, admin credentials); subsequently disclosed the intrusion was part of a **multi-year campaign with source code stolen**; earlier 2018/2020 incidents. With only ~5,845 employees supporting a ~$5B revenue, ~80M-domain business and heavily outsourced infrastructure, security is both a regulator-mandated and reputationally loaded dependency under the 2025 order.

---

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **MODERATE**

- **Legal-notice saturation:** for twelve straight weeks GDDY has been recycled through the "GDDY LOSS NOTICE / INVESTOR ALERT / deadline today" press-release mill. Retail-investor perception damage is real but historically short-lived.
- **Consumer sentiment is mixed and thin on loyalty:** ~145,000 Trustpilot reviews (4-star average) with persistent gripes about renewals/pricing — exactly where the promotional .com strategy plays, and a reputational trap if promotional pricing converts to steep renewal increases.
- **AI overreach risk:** the "Ink Your Link" marketing push and Airo AI features invite scrutiny of unconsented content modification and data use in AI training — a live ESG/privacy controversy vector with EU/California regulators.
- **Bright spot:** the AI narrative is being received positively by the sell-side ("AI is an ARPU tool"), and third-party coverage of Q3 was constructive. Brand equity is not currently impaired.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE** (upper end of the band)

Governance structure is genuinely sound (independent chair, credible committee architecture, whistleblower channel), there are **no accounting-fraud indicators** (clean SOX 302 certification signed by Bhutani on 2026-10-29 at the Q3 10-Q; no restatement, no auditor or CFO change, no material-weakness disclosure, and Q3 beat consensus), and the litigation is a disclosure case rather than an accounting case. That caps the rating. What keeps it from "Low" is a **standing FTC compliance order, an active securities class action, evidence of a disclosure/IR control gap, one-directional insider flow, and a growth model leaning on promotional pricing and an unproven AI product**.

---

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Disclosure-controls gap (highest priority).** A material pricing/packaging shift was not disclosed until it broke the bookings guide. Ask management directly on the next call: what changed in the disclosure-committee process post-February, and what is the quantified legal accrual? Test whether sell-side models now carry promotional-pricing disclosure as a standing item.
2. **FTC order + breach history combination.** The biennial third-party assessment is a dated, predictable catalyst. Any adverse assessment finding, or new breach, converts a "no-fine" settlement into an enforcement and impairment event. Monitor FTC docket 2023133 for order-modification filings.
3. **Zero insider purchases through a 40% drawdown.** Confirm via the 2027 proxy whether CEO/CFO sales are 10b5-1; if any tranche is discretionary, treat as a conviction signal against the growth story.
4. **Growth is promotional and ARPU-led, not customer-led.** Recommend the memo explicitly challenge the durability of bookings quality and the .com contract-term mix in FY27 guidance; a second promotional surprise would likely convert the securities case from a nuisance into a pattern-of-conduct problem.
5. **Airo concentration.** No disclosure reviewed quantifies Airo's third-party model/hosting dependency. Flag for management questioning given Airo now anchors the growth narrative.
