# GoDaddy Inc. (NYSE: GDDY) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price: $117.24** (Oct 30, 2026 close, +10.7% on Q3'26 earnings) | **Market cap: ~$14.5B** (124M sh) | **Net debt: $2.72B** | **EV: ~$17.3B**

*Data note: the platform DCF tool mis-parsed WACC (returned a 900% discount rate); I built the valuation manually. Sector-peer screens returned mega-cap semis, which are not valid comparables — I have used the company's own history instead. TTM = Q4'25–Q3'26.*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW** (trend: stable, at risk of narrowing)

GoDaddy is the world's largest domain registrar and a bundled online-presence provider for ~20M+ small businesses. The moat sources are real but uneven:

**What works:**
- **Scale cost advantage**: 5,845 employees on $5.2B revenue; capex is only ~0.5% of revenue ($23M TTM). Incremental customers are nearly free to serve — FCF margin of 34%.
- **Switching costs**: domains, email, website, SSL, and payments are entangled. Retention **>85%** and rising with customer spend. Nobody migrates a working business website to save $30.
- **Intangible/brand**: default destination for first-time domain buyers; the aftermarket domain marketplace has genuine two-sided network effects.
- **ROIC of 27.7%** (2025) vs. ~8% WACC — up from 13.7% in 2022–23. That is the signature of a moated business, and it has *widened* on capital efficiency.

**What worries me — and this is the crux:**
- **Growth is 100% price, 0% customers.** Revenue +6% with ARPU at $253 and customer counts flat-to-down. A business that can only grow by raising renewal prices on a captive base is harvesting, not compounding.
- **The $4.99 first-year .com promotion** (which caused a Q4'25 bookings miss and a 14% one-day drop, plus securities-law investigations) is evidence that customer acquisition requires price aggression. A wide-moat business does not need doorbuster pricing.
- **AI is a genuine, unresolved threat** to the "Websites + Marketing" layer. If an LLM builds a competent site in 90 seconds, the $20/month website-builder SKU is commoditized. Management's counter — that agents still need "real estate on the internet," i.e. domains — is credible for the *registry/resolution* layer but does not protect the applications layer.
- **Contract risk is real**: expiration of the .CO registry contract is a ~200bp revenue drag in 2026.

**Verdict:** domains/email are a near-utility with a narrow moat; websites/commerce are contested. **Narrow moat, not Wide.** This is not a "hold forever" franchise — it is a high-cash-yield harvest with an optionality kicker (Airo, now >$100M annualized bookings).

Strengths: FCF conversion is elite; margin discipline has been relentless (op margin +1,840bp in six years); the business is almost capital-free; D&A has now collapsed to ~$13M/quarter as legacy acquisition intangibles fully amortize, so GAAP earnings ≈ cash earnings. Deductions from a perfect score: (a) SBC of ~$290M/yr (~6% of revenue) is a genuine cost embedded in FCF addbacks; (b) **shareholders' equity is negative (–$139M)** with $4.56B of goodwill — an artifact of buybacks, not distress, but it means book value offers zero downside support and P/B (76x) is meaningless; (c) $3.84B of debt at ~3.9% will refinance higher; (d) revenue growth has decelerated from 15% (2021) to 6%.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Multiples at $117.24:**
- P/E (TTM): **15.1x** | EV/EBIT: 12.6x | EV/EBITDA: 11.9x
- **P/FCF: 8.3x** | EV/FCF: 9.9x | **FCF yield: 12.0%** (10.0% treating SBC as cash cost)
- Implied: the market is paying ~8x cash flow for a business with 6% revenue growth and 34% FCF margins.

**Owner-earnings DCF (my own, base FCF $1.5B — haircut from $1.74B for SBC dilution and working-capital timing):**

| Scenario | Assumptions | Value/share |
|---|---|---|
| **Bear** — AI erodes franchise | FCF –3%/yr for 5 yrs, then flat; WACC 10%, g=0% | **$85** (–28%) |
| **Base** — narrow moat holds, harvest | FCF +6% for 5 yrs, +3% yrs 6–10, g=2%; WACC 9% | **$197** (+68%) |
| **Bull** — Airo monetizes, FCF +10%/10 yrs | g=2.5%; WACC 9% | **~$320** (+173%) |

Probability-weighted (45/45/10): **~$159/share**. Even a heavily bear-skewed 60/35/5 weighting yields ~$125. **Cross-check:** a zero-growth perpetuity at 9% on $1.65B FCF is worth ~$126/share — so the current price embeds *literally no growth, forever*, despite 6% revenue growth and 14% FCF growth guided for 2026.

**Intrinsic value estimate: $170–200 central; conservative floor ~$150.** At $117.24, the stock trades at a **~30–40% discount** — squarely inside my margin-of-safety requirement.

## 4. Management Quality: **Good (B+)**

**Positives:** CEO Aman Bhutani (since 2019) has executed one of the better margin turnarounds in software — operating margin 8%→27%, ROIC doubled, and **>95% of FCF directed to buybacks**. Share count has fallen from 158.8M (2022) to ~124M — **–22% in under four years, ~$6.2B repurchased**, and they bought hardest at $75–100 in 2026. That is disciplined, value-accretive capital allocation, not empire building (M&A has been limited to small tuck-ins).

**Negatives:** (1) The $4.99 promo mis-execution and subsequent disclosure investigations suggest imperfect investor communication and forecasting; (2) **insider selling — 22 sales, zero purchases in six months**, including two CFO sales in June 2026. Much is likely 10b5-1/SBC-driven, but there is no insider buying signal at what I consider a cheap price; (3) no dividend — acceptable given the buyback returns, but it concentrates the thesis on the buyback; (4) not founder-led (Bob Parsons departed long ago).

## 5. Investment Verdict: **BUY** (not Strong Buy)

This is a **good business at a very good price**, not a great business at a fair price. The setup is classically asymmetric: the stock has fallen 58% from its $181 high on a legitimate but over-extrapolated AI narrative, while the underlying business grew revenue 6%, expanded operating margin ~400bp, and grew FCF ~14%. You are buying a 12% FCF yield, a 28% ROIC, >85% retention, and a management team retiring 5–8% of shares annually — at 8x cash flow with essentially zero growth priced in.

I stop short of Strong Buy because the moat is Narrow and the AI question is genuinely unresolved; this is a **harvest-and-optionality** position, not a permanent compounder. I would size it moderately (2–4%) and add below $100.

**What would make me sell:** customer counts declining more than 2%/yr, ARPU growth stalling below 3%, FCF margin compressing below 30%, or buybacks slowing to preserve leverage.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI disintermediation** (highest severity): if agentic AI collapses the website/presence SKU, the applications layer — ~40% of revenue and the growth engine — commoditizes. My bear case is $85; a true disruption scenario is worse than my model.
2. **Price-only growth engine**: 6% growth on flat customers depends on annual renewal price increases; this has a social/competitive ceiling, and the $4.99 promo shows elasticity is alive.
3. **Registry/contract risk**: Verisign .com wholesale price increases squeeze registrar gross margin; the .CO loss proves contracts can vanish (~200bp drag).
4. **Leverage with negative equity**: $3.84B debt, 1.9x net leverage, no book-value cushion; refinancing at higher rates hits FCF directly.
5. **Legal overhang**: securities investigations into promotional-pricing disclosure — unquantified but a headline risk.
6. **Buyback dependency**: per-share value creation has been ~half business growth, half share shrinkage. If the multiple re-rates, that engine's returns fall, and at $117 the buyback is far less accretive than at $79.
7. **Insider selling with no offsetting purchases** — weak alignment signal at the margin.
8. **Data gaps**: I could not obtain verified current customer-count and net-add figures, nor a clean software-peer multiple set; both would sharpen the moat-trend call. Peer median P/E of 41.8x (broad tech sector) is not a valid benchmark and I have not relied on it.
