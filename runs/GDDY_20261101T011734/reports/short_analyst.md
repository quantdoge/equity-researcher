## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment

**Rating: HIGH (clean). This is not an accounting fraud.**

I went looking for manipulation and did not find it. The cash is real, and it is growing faster than reported earnings.

A company manufacturing earnings does not post a 184% cash conversion ratio and a 33.7% FCF margin while shrinking its reported net income. The FY2023 net income of $1,375M (which exceeded operating income of $547M) was a non-cash deferred-tax valuation-allowance release, not revenue manipulation — and it is the *only* year with positive accruals.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

1. **Growth is decelerating and is price-led, not volume-led.** YoY revenue growth: 10.3% (Q3'25) → 6.8% → 7.7% → 8.3% → 6.1% → 6.6% → **6.0% (Q3'26)**. Four consecutive quarters at ~6%. Consensus NTM growth: **4.9%**.
2. **Leading indicators are growing slower than revenue.** Q3'26: revenue +6.0% but **ARR +5.1%** and **billings +5.4%**. ARR growing slower than revenue means tomorrow's revenue is decelerating.
3. **Feb-2026 bookings fell *below* revenue.** Management attributed it to a one-year .com promo being "too successful" — i.e., customers traded *down* from multi-year to one-year terms. That is a demand/commitment signal dressed as a marketing win. It precedes revenue, and revenue decelerated three quarters later.
4. **Customer count has been declining (-2.4% reported in 2025).** Growth is being bought with ARPU/price increases on a shrinking base. That is the definition of late-stage price extraction.
5. **Capital allocation destroyed measurable value.** Cumulative buybacks FY21–TTM ≈ **$6.9B** (98% of cumulative FCF) retiring ~45M shares → **average cost ≈ $152/share** vs $117 today. FY2025 alone: **$1.6B repurchased at roughly $175 average** — a ~33% mark-to-market loss on the largest single year of buybacks.
6. **Shareholders' equity is now negative: -$139M** (Q3'26), retained earnings **-$3.39B**, tangible book value **≈ -$4.7B**. The equity cushion has been spent on buybacks.
7. **EPS growth is manufactured.** Consensus 2028 vs 2027: **net income +2.8%** but **EPS +8.4%**. The entire forward earnings-growth story is share-count shrinkage, which requires $1.5B+/yr of buybacks against negative equity.
8. **D&A is collapsing, flattering EBITDA.** Quarterly D&A: $30.8M (Q1'25) → **$12.7M (Q3'26)**, -59% YoY. TTM EBITDA +15.7% vs revenue +4.6%; roughly a quarter of the operating-income growth is acquired-intangible amortization rolling off, not operating improvement.
9. **Serial guidance disappointments:** three double-digit earnings-day declines in 18 months (Feb'25 -14%, Feb'26 -13%, Jul'26 -17%). Guidance credibility is impaired.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment

**This is the only bear thesis with genuine teeth, and it is unhedgeable on a 12-month horizon.**

- **Disintermediation risk.** GoDaddy's core (domains + websites + hosting, roughly 60% of revenue) assumes a small business needs a distinct website and domain. AI answer engines and AI agents progressively substitute for "find me a business on the web." The CEO himself conceded on the Feb-2026 call: *"a lot of companies are facing challenges of traffic from search"* and *"no one is insulated."* That is management validating the terminal bear case in real time.
- **Self-cannibalisation.** Q2'26 sold off -16.7% explicitly on fears that the Airo AI platform is cannibalising higher-priced SKUs. An incumbent that must cut its own price to defend the funnel destroys ARPU even if it keeps the customer.
- **Down-market entry.** AI-native site builders compress the "Websites + Marketing" price point toward zero; Wix/Shopify/Cloudflare compete on the domain and hosting layer.
- **Countervailing evidence (I must give it):** gross margin has been **63.0%–64.0% for five straight years with no erosion** — that is *not* what pricing-power loss looks like. CAC payback is 5.8 months, and .ai registrations grew 62.6% in 2025, showing the TLD market is rotating, not dying. The moat is eroding at the *volume* line, not yet at the *price* line.

## 5. Management Red Flags

- **Capital allocation, not integrity, is the failure.** $1.6B of FY25 buybacks at ~$175 average, equity driven to **negative $139M**, retained earnings **-$3.39B**. They leveraged the balance sheet to defend an EPS multiple that the market had already rejected.
- **Deflective language under pressure.** "The marketing campaign was too successful" for a bookings shortfall. Asked by Oppenheimer whether the go-to-market change was forced by pipeline deterioration, the CEO answered *"both things are true… the world is changing very quickly"* — a non-answer. Promotional framing of a bad datapoint is a soft red flag.
- **Insider activity:** 30 Form 4s in ~7 months with heavy same-day clusters (22 filings on 4–5 June 2026, 7 on 3 Sept 2026) — the cadence reads as scheduled vest-and-sell rather than conviction. **Gap: the feed gives filing dates only, not transaction direction or size, so I cannot quantify net insider selling.** No insider *buying* cluster is visible, which is mildly negative.
- No auditor changes, no related-party transactions, no restatements identified.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — is there a credible bear case?

**Yes, but it is a value-trap/terminal-decline thesis, not a fraud thesis.** The bear case:

> A maturing domain franchise whose unit growth is negative, whose 6% revenue growth is being purchased with price increases and trade-down promotions, and whose EPS growth now depends entirely on ~$1.5B/yr of buybacks funded from a balance sheet with *negative* equity. Into that, layer an unresolvable AI question: if SMB websites and domains lose value, a $5.2B revenue base with 26% margins has no equity cushion and $3.8B of debt.

**But the counter-case is stronger at today's price.** Critically, the balance-sheet alarm bells are largely false positives:
- **Altman Z = 0.44 ("distress")** is an artifact. Recomputing X4 at market value (14.85/8.05 = 1.85 instead of book 0.03) gives **Z ≈ 1.53** — still grey, but the drags are X1 (negative working capital, i.e. *customer prepayments* in current liabilities) and X2 (accumulated deficit from buybacks), not operating losses. A short seller leaning on "Z = 0.44" is making a naive error.
- **Current ratio 0.61** is the same artifact — a prepaid subscription model with deferred revenue in current liabilities. Net debt/EBITDA is **1.9x**; interest coverage **~9x**; $1.1B cash and an undrawn revolver.
- **Piotroski F = 5** fails only on leverage, ROA improvement, equity change and gross margin — all buyback/mix artifacts, not distress.
- Net debt is **flat since 2021** ($2.81B → $2.72B). Buybacks were funded by FCF, not by debt accumulation. That materially weakens the "leveraged financial engineering" bear point.

**Scenario math (125.7M diluted shares, $2.72B net debt):**

| Scenario | FCF | Multiple | Equity value | Per share | Return |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Base bear: 2% growth stalls, margin flat | $1.6B | 10x | $13.3B | ~$106 | **-10%** |
| Tail bear: AI disintermediation, -5%/yr | $1.2B | 8x | $6.9B | ~$55 | **-53%** |
| Bull: FCF $1.85B, re-rating | $1.85B | 14x | $23.2B | ~$185 | **+58%** |

Probability-weighted at 55% / 20% / 25% → expected return **≈ -2%**. That is a coin flip.

**Best evidence the bull is winning right now:** Q3'26 *beat* — revenue +6% vs consensus, GAAP EPS $2.08 (+9.6% beat), adjusted EBITDA $449M at 33.5% margin, FCF margin 35.8%, FY guidance reconfirmed, stock rallied from ~$96 to $117. Valuation is **~10% FCF/EV**, **~10.4x 2027E EPS**, **~9.7x 2028E EPS**. You do not short a clean-cash, 34%-FCF-margin, 1.9x-levered business at a 10% FCF yield on the strength of a 5-year-away disruption narrative.

## 7. Contrarian Rating

# NO EDGE — Reject Short Thesis (at $117)

I could not substantiate an accounting or governance short. Beneish -3.03, TATA -9%, accruals -9%, cash conversion 184%, gross margin flat for five years, and net debt flat since 2021 — this is a legitimately cash-generative business, and the "distress" screens are deferred-revenue and buyback artifacts that would burn a naive short.

The legitimate bear case — negative unit growth, price-led 6% growth decelerating toward 4.9%, EPS manufactured by buybacks executed ~30% above today's price, and an unresolved AI terminal question — is **real but already 44% discounted**, and the Q3'26 beat-and-reconfirm has shifted momentum against the short.

**What would convert this to a "Short":**
- Billings or ARR growth breaking below **4%** (the cleanest leading indicator);
- Customer count decline accelerating beyond **-4%** YoY;
- A **buyback pause or suspension** — that immediately removes the entire EPS
