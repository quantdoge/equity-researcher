**This is the single most misreadable part of the CHTR risk profile, so I want to be explicit:**

- **A 48–60% realised volatility is 3–4x the market's.** For a subscription business with 40% EBITDA margins, that is extraordinary and tells you the market is repricing the *business model*, not the sector.
- **The low/negative beta (0.51 over 750d, –0.05 over 252d) is not a sign of defensiveness.** It means CHTR's returns have **decoupled from the market entirely** — the stock is being driven by broadband subscriber losses, fiber/fixed-wireless competition, leverage, and Cox integration. **Beta is currently useless as a risk measure here.** A "low-beta, defensive" screen would classify CHTR as a low-risk holding. It is one of the highest-risk large caps in the market. Flag this to any portfolio construction process immediately.
- **–76% peak-to-trough drawdown** with an **ongoing –58% drawdown** in the trailing year. Negative Sharpe on both windows: shareholders have been paid nothing for the risk taken.
- Shares outstanding fell from 135.2M (2025) to **116.9M** (Q3'26) — buybacks masked some of the per-share damage, but the program has effectively **stopped** ($21M in Q3'26 vs. $1.03B in Q1'26). The support bid is gone.

## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis

**Base case (pro forma):** Revenue ~$66B annualized; Adj. EBITDA $26.4B (40.0% margin); interest $5.4B; capex ~$12.8B (19.4%).

### Scenario A — Revenue –20% (moderate cord-cutting acceleration)
Revenue $52.8B. Subscriber-driven losses carry ~55% variable cost take-out (content, marketing, variable labour); management's $3B announced savings ($2B opex + $1B Cox synergies) partially offsets.

| | Base | –20% Rev |
|---|---|---|
| Adj. EBITDA | $26.4B | **~$21.3B** (38.8% margin) |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 4.14x | **5.14x** |
| EBITDA / interest | 4.9x | **3.9x** |
| **EBIT / interest** | 2.7x | **~1.9x** ⚠️ |
| Capex (cut) | $12.8B | $9.0B |
| **FCF** | ~$6.5B | **~$5.5–6.0B** |
| FCF / debt | 5.9% | ~5.3% |

**Verdict: the debt survives comfortably; the equity does not.** FCF stays positive at $5.5–6.0B — Charter services its interest and maintains modest deleveraging. But at the **current 4.9x EV/EBITDA multiple**, EV = $104B against **$109.4B of net debt → equity value is approximately ZERO**. A 20% revenue decline, at today's depressed multiple, wipes out shareholders entirely while leaving bondholders whole.

### Scenario B — Revenue –35% (severe structural share loss to fiber + FWA)
Revenue $42.9B; EBITDA collapses to ~$15.0B (35% margin as fixed plant costs delever); EBIT ~$4.5B.

- Net debt / EBITDA: **7.3x** — deep high-yield territory
- **EBIT / interest: 0.83x — interest is not covered by operating earnings**
- FCF: ~$2.0–2.5B (positive only because capex is slashed to a $7B maintenance floor)
- At 4.9x, EV = $73.5B vs. $109.4B net debt → **$36B of debt impairment; recovery ~67 cents**

**FCF break-even** occurs at roughly **–35% revenue**: below that, Charter cannot cover interest plus minimum maintenance capex, and the $110B stack requires refinancing into a hostile market.

### Scenario C — The rate shock (most underpriced risk)
Every **100bp** on the $110B stack costs **~$1.1B** of annual pre-tax cash — equivalent to **~18% of base-case FCF**. At 2.65x EBIT coverage, a 100bp refinancing shock pushes coverage toward ~1.9x, matching the –20% revenue scenario. **Leverage and rates are not independent risks here; they compound.** With ratings one notch from the BB/BBB boundary, a downgrade to BB (flat) would raise marginal funding costs across the entire stack.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not **Critical** — and I want to be precise about why, because the case for Critical is superficially strong:

**Why not Critical:** FCF is positive and substantial (~$5.5B even in a –20% revenue world); maturities are well-laddered with no single-year cliff; the company executed **$5.5B of note exchanges and $1.0–1.5B of deleveraging in Q3'26 alone** plus tenders across eight note series and new 2038/2041 issuance in August — evidence of real, active liability management and market access; S&P affirmed with a stable outlook; and management has a credible $3B self-help program equal to ~11% of pro forma EBITDA.

**Why firmly High, not Moderate:** 4.1x net leverage, **2.65x EBIT coverage**, $573M cash against $3.17B of current debt, a 0.034x cash ratio, negative $10.4B working capital, BB-tier ratings, a $6.3B impairment already booked, and an **equity cushion of only 15% of enterprise value**. Interest consumes a quarter of EBITDA. Revenue is *declining* pro forma (–1.5% y/y) — a 4x-levered credit needs growth to delever.

**The asymmetry is the whole story:** ~$110B of debt sits senior to a $20B equity stub. Downside lands almost entirely on shareholders; bondholders are protected to roughly a –30% revenue decline.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **$6.3B franchise impairment (Q3'26).** Management has formally marked down the asset base. **Goodwill + intangibles remain $103.8B — 58% of total assets — against only $29.8B of book equity.** A further write-down of even one-third of intangibles would eliminate more than 100% of book equity and could trigger covenant/incurrence headroom issues. This is the largest single unrecognised risk on the balance sheet.

2. **Buybacks exceeded free cash flow.** FY2025: $5.13B repurchased vs. $4.42B FCF. Capital was returned to shareholders while leverage sat at 4.6x and before a $13B debt-funded acquisition. Mathematically defensible at the time; strategically value-destructive in hindsight. **The program has now effectively halted** ($21M in Q3'26) — confirming the liquidity constraint.

3. **No cash buffer whatsoever.** $573M cash (0.5% of debt), cash ratio 0.034x, current ratio 0.37x. Charter runs on **revolver access** (~$2.4B available per Q3'26 disclosure) and daily FCF. There is no cushion for a operational or market shock. This is normal for cable — but it means liquidity is a *facility* risk, not a *balance sheet* risk, and facilities can be repriced or pulled.

4. **Broadband subscriber erosion is accelerating.** Pro forma Internet customers **–233k in Q3'26** (–156k legacy Charter, –77k legacy Cox). This is the revenue engine of the entire capital structure. Video –77k. Mobile (+508k) is growing but is a *lower-margin* product that does not replace broadband economics.

5. **Cox integration risk layered onto an already-levered balance sheet.** Debt +$13.2B at close; 11M passings and 5M customers to integrate; $1B of synergies promised. Acquisitions of this type historically consume cash and management attention at exactly the wrong point in the cycle.

6. **Leverage-to-rates compounding.** 2.65x EBIT coverage + $110B floating/refinanceable exposure. The 2Y–10Y curve and the pace of Fed easing directly determine whether the 3.5x leverage target is achievable. **I was unable to retrieve current Treasury levels in this session — flag as a data gap**, but the structural point holds regardless of the current curve level.

7. **Retained earnings deficit of –$5.34B.** Accumulated losses persist despite a decade of profitability — a legacy of leverage-funded buybacks. Book equity is a plug figure, not an economic reserve.
