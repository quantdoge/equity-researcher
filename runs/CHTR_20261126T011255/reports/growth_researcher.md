# Charter Communications (CHTR) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Price $147.27 | Market cap $19.85B | EV ~$117B | Sector: Communication Services / Cable**

---

## 1. Growth Story

Charter is not a growth company — it is a **levered bet on stopping a decline**. The August 19, 2026 closings of Cox Communications and Liberty Broadband transformed it into a ~$109B-debt-principal cable consolidator with 13.5M mobile lines, at the exact moment its core broadband franchise tipped into structural subscriber loss (−233K pro forma internet customers in Q3'26). The bull thesis is *not* revenue growth; it is that capex falls from ~$13B to ≤$11B while $3B+ of cost cuts land, converting a $4.4B FCF business into $7–9B of FCF against a $19.85B equity stub — a self-funded deleveraging that could retire the entire market cap in roughly three years. That is a special-situation arithmetic trade, not a compounding franchise.

**Growth verdict: Hold — but explicitly not a growth buy.**

## 3. TAM and Penetration

| Market | US TAM | Charter Position |
|---|---|---|
| Residential broadband | ~$90B | ~30M internet relationships; **losing share** to FWA + fiber |
| US wireless | ~$200B | 13.5M lines; **+15%/yr, +1.6M/yr** — the one real growth vector |
| Video / advertising | ~$80B | Structural decline; losses improving (−77K vs −70K) |
| Commercial / enterprise | ~$60B | +0.3% pro forma (mid-market +2.1%) |

**The penetration story is mobile attach:** 13.5M mobile lines against ~32M internet relationships = **~42% attach**, versus a plausible 60%+ ceiling. Each incremental point of attach is ~$400M of high-margin revenue. This is genuine convergence economics and the single most durable growth asset in the company. But mobile ARPU is ~$40–45, so even 20M lines generates only ~$10B of revenue against a $109B debt load — it cannot offset residential erosion on its own.

**Critical point:** In Cathie Wood's framing, Charter is the *disrupted incumbent*, not the disruptor. Fixed wireless access and fiber overbuild are taking its highest-value customers, and it is answering with capital intensity (21% of revenue) rather than innovation. There is no innovation moat here — the moat is conduit in the ground, and it is being overbuilt.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Jan 29, 2027 — Q4/FY2026 results (imminent).** Consensus $9.34 EPS on $16.50B revenue. First clean read on Cox pro forma internet trends and, critically, the 2027 capex framework.
- **2027 capex guide ≤$11B** (from ~$13B pro forma 2026). Management has pre-committed; delivery is the entire FCF thesis. Any overrun is catastrophic for the equity stub.
- **$2B run-rate opex savings over 18 months + $1B+ Cox synergies** (target: mid-2028). Watch quarterly headcount and cost-to-service line items.
- **Deleveraging to 3.5x** from 3.93x → unlocks buyback/dividend reinstatement. $5.1B was repurchased in 2025; capital return is likely paused through the integration.
- **Broadband stabilization / FWA saturation.** Any evidence T-Mobile and Verizon are capacity-constrained in Charter's footprint would be the single largest re-rating catalyst.
- **Sector consolidation** — cable M&A speculation remains a recurring, unquantifiable call option.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD** *(not a growth buy — a levered FCF option)*

| | Bear | Base | Bull |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2028 EBITDA | $20.5B | $25.0B | $28.0B |
| EV/EBITDA | 4.5x | 6.0x | 7.0x |
| EV | $92B | $150B | $196B |
| Net debt | $100B | $98B | $96B |
| **Equity** | **−$8B** | **$52B** | **$100B** |
| **Per share** | **~$0** | **~$380** | **~$730** |

This is why P/E is 4x: the equity is the thinnest 17% of the capital structure sitting on a business with negative organic growth. **The payoff distribution is genuinely bimodal**, which is disqualifying for a growth mandate but attractive for a credit-turned-equity specialist. At $147 the stock has already rallied ~44% off the October 2026 ~$102 low and now sits **at or above the sell-side ceiling** (targets cluster $101–$150: Wells $101, Barclays $115, Wolfe $118/Underperform, Bernstein $120, Morgan Stanley $150/Equalweight, JPMorgan $150/Neutral — TD Cowen's $380 is the lone outlier). Risk/reward is balanced-to-poor for new capital, and every single growth screen in my framework fails.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Broadband erosion is accelerating, not bottoming.** −233K pro forma in one quarter against FWA and fiber overbuild. If this is structural rather than cyclical, the mobile growth engine never gets large enough to matter.
2. **Capex guidance risk.** The $2B capex cut is the whole thesis. Cable has a long history of network-spend overruns; a miss here hits equity, not debt.
3. **Execution risk on $3B of cost savings** across a unionized, newly merged 90K+ employee base — while simultaneously launching a "new operating model" and onboarding a new COO (Nick Jeffery). Two simultaneous transformations.
4. **Balance sheet asymmetry.** $109B principal, $5.8B annual cash interest, 3.93x leverage vs. 3.5x target. 11.1-year weighted average life and 5.3% weighted cost mitigate refinancing risk — but a 10% EBITDA shortfall impairs equity by ~60%.
5. **Estimate unreliability.** 2027 EPS dispersion of $16.43–$67.99 and 2030 coverage by two analysts means consensus is not an anchor.
6. **Cox integration / GAAP noise.** Programming-cost allocation is distorting reported residential revenue by ~150bps and gross margin by hundreds of bps — obscuring the true underlying trend.
7. **Secular / regulatory.** Price regulation, BEAD subsidy changes, and the risk that convergence (Comcast+Charter streaming JV, Xumo) never earns back its capital.

---

**Bottom line:** Charter is a legitimate, cheap, self-help deleveraging story with a real mobile attach engine and a wide maturity runway. It is *not* a disruptive growth company — revenue is shrinking, Rule of 40 is 7.5, and the TAM it operates in is being taken from it. **Hold at $147; do not initiate new growth capital.** The asymmetry only becomes compelling below ~$110–115, or on hard evidence that internet net adds have inflected positive.
