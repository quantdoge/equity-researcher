# Macroeconomic Assessment — Charter Communications (CHTR)

**As of:** November 26, 2026 | **Price:** $147.27 | **Market cap:** $19.85B | **Sector:** Communication Services / Telecom

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — Regime: **Reflation / Overheating, with tightening financial conditions**

The US economy is in a nominal boom that the Fed is actively fighting. Real GDP growth re-accelerated to **4.3% saar in Q3 2026** (vs 2.2% Q2, 2.5% Q1), unemployment is a low **4.1%** (Oct 2026), yet headline CPI has re-accelerated to **3.82% y/y** (Oct 2026) from 3.72% in September. This is not a landing, soft or hard — it is an economy running above potential with inflation stuck ~180bp above target.

The Fed responded by ending its easing cycle and reversing: after cutting to ~3.63% through mid-2026, it **hiked 25bp on September 16, 2026 to 3.75–4.00%**, held on October 28 (10–2 vote), and markets price **~93–95% odds of another 25bp hike at the December 9, 2026 meeting** (CME FedWatch / Kalshi). Governor Cook stated on November 20 that "rate hikes [are] needed to lower high inflation." The effective funds rate is already **3.88% (Oct 2026)**.

Crucially for a long-duration, leveraged equity like Charter, the move is at the **long end**. The curve has bear-steepened: **2Y 4.74% / 5Y 4.93% / 10Y 5.13% / 30Y 5.47%**, a **+39bp 2s10s spread** (no inversion). The 10Y has risen from **4.38% (June 29, 2026)** to a peak of **5.24% (Oct 9, 2026)**, now **4.91% (Nov 24)**. The 30Y fixed mortgage is **7.21% (Nov 25)** versus 6.23% a year ago — **+98bp**. This is a term-premium/fiscal-supply story, not a growth story, and it is precisely the wrong kind of rate rise for Charter.

Credit is the one supportive pillar, but it is cracking. **HY OAS is 3.72% (Nov 24)** — historically tight versus a ~500bp long-run median — but has widened **~109bp from the 2.63% trough on Aug 31, 2026**. Tight spreads mean Charter can still refinance; the widening trend means the window is narrowing.

**Stance: risk-off for rate-sensitive and levered segments; risk-on in credit and nominal GDP.** CHTR sits squarely in the first bucket.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Charter's macro sensitivity is unusual: it is a **~98% domestic revenue business with $108.6B of debt, ~$26.5B of pro forma EBITDA and only ~$2.7B of annualized free cash flow**. Equity is a thin levered stub on a rate-driven asset. That structure, not GDP, drives the stock.

**Interest rates — STRONGLY NEGATIVE (dominant variable).**
- Debt principal was **$108.6B at 9/30/26** (post-Cox). Each 100bp on the full stack is ~**$1.09B pre-tax** — roughly **40% of pro forma EBITDA**. My estimate of run-rate interest expense is **$5.5–6.0B annually** (~35–36% of EBITDA), based on $108.6B at a ~5–5.5% blended coupon (company figure not disclosed in the release; treat as an estimate).
- Refinancing risk is concentrated: **~$7.7B of 2027 maturities** existed pre-Cox; Charter has extended a large portion into 2030/2031 facilities, and in Q3 exchanged **$5.5B of secured notes for $4.0B cash plus new secured notes** (~$1.5B deleveraging) and repaid **$1.0B of legacy Cox notes**. On Nov 17–18 it ran tender offers for **eight series of notes (up to $1.5B)**. The company targets **3.5x net leverage within three years** (4.18x at 6/30/26 pre-Cox). Every quarter of elevated long rates pushes that target out and raises the coupon on each refinancing.
- The discount-rate effect is the bigger driver: with an equity FCF yield of ~7.3% ($2.7B / $19.9B), Charter behaves like a **15–20 year duration equity**. The **+75bp move in the 10Y from June to October** is arithmetically consistent with much of the **65% drawdown from the May 2025 peak ($427.25 → $147.27)**. The Oct–Nov rally (+42% off the $103.38 low of Oct 9) coincided with the 10Y falling from 5.24% to 4.91% — a near-pure rate-beta move.

**Housing / mortgage rates — NEGATIVE.** Cable gross adds are a function of **household moves**, and 7.21% mortgages are suppressing mobility. Charter's penetration fell **2.6pts y/y to 51.9%**, and pro forma Internet customers fell **233,000** in Q3 2026 (worse than the 171,000 loss a year earlier) with **customer relationships down 2.6% y/y**. Weak mover activity removes the natural gross-add engine that masks churn.

**Inflation — NEGATIVE, and asymmetrically so.** This is the subtle but critical point: Charter faces **3.8% input inflation (labor, programming, network gear, energy) with negative pricing power**. Residential revenue per customer fell **1.6% y/y to $117.85** despite the highest CPI in years. It is spending on **$1,000 savings guarantees** and free mobile lines to defend share. Costs are nominal and rising; prices are nominal and falling. The **$2B annualized expense-savings plan** (plus >$1B Cox synergies) is a direct admission of that squeeze. Inflation here is a margin tax, not a tailwind.

**Growth — NEUTRAL to slightly NEGATIVE.** 4.3% GDP should support small business and advertising, and small-business revenue per customer did rise **1.1%**. But residential connectivity is declining in absolute terms into a growing economy — the problem is **competitive (fiber overbuild, FWA adding 3.9M subs in 2026 while cable lost ~1.5M) plus a housing-mobility drought**, not cyclical demand. Strong GDP keeps the Fed hiking, which hurts Charter more than the extra volume helps.

**Credit conditions — NEUTRAL / deteriorating.** HY OAS at 3.72% is tight enough that Charter retains market access (evidenced by the Q3 and November tenders), but it is 109bp wider than August. A move back toward 500bp would shut the refinancing window on a $108.6B stack.

**USD / currency — NEUTRAL.** Revenue is domestic; a **+4.8% USD rally since September 9** has essentially no translation effect. Minor negative on imported set-tops/routers (WiFi 7 hardware), but not a thesis variable.

**Fiscal / trade policy — NEGATIVE (qualitative).** BEAD and state broadband subsidies fund **fiber overbuilders** attacking Charter's footprint — a rare case of fiscal policy directly subsidizing a company's competitors. Tariffs on imported network equipment and customer-premise gear raise capex (FY2026 capex guided ~**$13.0B** ex-transition capex) at exactly the wrong moment. *Note: I could not source quantified tariff exposure; treat as a directional risk, not a modelled number.*

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Charter is the single worst combination in this macro regime: **maximum duration, maximum leverage, no pricing power, and a revenue base levered to housing turnover**. The one variable that matters most — the long end — is moving against it, the Fed is hiking rather than cutting, and the FCF stub that supports the equity (~$674M in Q3, down $947M y/y) is too small to defend the $108.6B debt stack or to fund buybacks that would put a floor under the shares.

The offset is valuation: at $19.9B market cap against ~$26.5B EBITDA and a leverage target of 3.5x, the equity is priced for a refinancing accident that credit markets (3.72% HY OAS) are not pricing. **Bonds say fine; equity says distress.** That divergence is the whole investment debate — and it will be resolved by the 10Y and the December 9 FOMC, not by subscriber trends.

---

## 5. Surprise Scenario — Bear: the long end, not the Fed, breaks Charter

Consensus is positioned for a modest, well-telegraphed 25bp December hike and a pause in 2027. The material surprise is a **term-premium shock**: sticky 3.5%+ core PCE plus heavy Treasury supply pushes the **30Y through 6.0%** and the 10Y to ~5.75% while the Fed signals a 5%+ terminal rate.

Under that path:
- HY OAS widens from 3.72% to ~525bp (still only a normalization), pushing Charter's BB curve to ~11–12% marginal cost of debt.
- The **~$7.7B of 2027 maturities** refinance at 700–800bp above legacy coupons; the 3.5x leverage target slips to 2029+ and the tender/buyback program halts.
- **Mortgage rates above 8%** collapse housing turnover further, driving pro forma Internet losses to 300k+/quarter, and the $2B cost plan is consumed by the ARPU decline.
- With no dividend, no buyback and negative FCF after capex ($13.0B) and interest (~$6B), there is no valuation floor. **$80–90 (-40% to -45%)** becomes the clearing level, and a secured-debt layer build (already underway — secured notes are growing) subordinates equity.

**The mirror-image bull case** (roughly 30–35% likely) is that the December hike is the last one, CPI rolls to 3.0% by spring as shelter disinflation lands, the 10Y returns to 4.30%, and Charter's $3B+ of cost cuts plus Cox launch momentum drive an FCF inflection — in which case the November move (from $103 to $147) is only the first leg of a 50%+ re-rating. The asymmetry is real, but it is **entirely a rates call, not an operating call.**
