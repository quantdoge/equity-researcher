## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW–MEDIUM**

- Telecommunications is asset-light in carbon terms: Scope 1 is dominated by fleet and backup generation; Scope 2 by network power. Carbon intensity per revenue dollar is low versus Energy/Utilities/Materials; Fitch explicitly characterises Charter's **climate vulnerability as low** and ESG scores as neutral.
- Physical climate risk is moderate and rising: hurricane/storm exposure along the Gulf and Eastern seaboard requires diesel backup and field-restoration capex; wildfire risk in Western footprint.
- Transition risk is **indirect and cost-side**: rising electricity prices are a genuine EBITDA lever given 24/7 network load, which is the real financial translation of "E" here — efficiency is a margin story, not a compliance story.
- Disclosure quality is mediocre. Charter historically resisted standalone sustainability reporting (2019 shareholder proposal sought an annual sustainability report); renewable-energy share and Scope 1–2 data are disclosed but Scope 3 coverage is thin. California SB 253/261 reach Charter (doing business in CA, >$1B revenue) — Scope 1–2 assurance will be required; SEC climate rule remains stayed.
- No material environmental controversy (no spills, no superfund-scale remediation, no major EPA penalties identified).

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **HIGH**

**Customer / product:**
- Active class action over a **~$28/month deceptive TV surcharge**; separate litigation over data caps/overage practices across the cable industry.
- Spectrum has repeatedly ranked at or near the bottom of ACSI internet-service-provider satisfaction, an early-warning indicator for churn and pricing power in a market where fixed wireless and fiber are taking share.
- End of the **Affordable Connectivity Program** stripped a demand subsidy; internet subscriber declines (Q3 2025: −109k) followed. Politically charged affordability debates in NY (state low-income broadband mandate) and California create licence-to-operate risk.

**Labour:**
- EEOC disability-discrimination settlement (repeated denial of accommodation; **$60,000**), plus a Connecticut appellate ruling affirming a PTSD-related wrongful-termination finding — a pattern of accommodation failures, not a one-off.
- OSHA workplace-safety penalty on record; labour-relations violation records; a collective/class action filed in S.D. Ohio alleging labour violations (misclassification/overtime-type claims).
- 91,900 employees in a unionised, field-heavy workforce = elevated strike, wage-inflation and safety exposure. Technician safety (ladder/vehicle) is a chronic industry hazard.

**Cyber (social + governance):** April 2026 **ShinyHunters** intrusion via vishing against an employee's Microsoft Entra credentials; 4.9M customer accounts confirmed, ~42M records claimed and leaked. Class actions were filed (some voluntarily dismissed, others consolidated). **Estimated financial impact: $50–150M** across notification/credit monitoring, remediation capex, legal defence and potential regulator penalty, plus unquantified brand/churn cost — the SEC's recent $35M disclosure-failure enforcement precedents and its 4-day 8-K Item 1.05 regime make disclosure timing itself a liability.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **3 / 10**

**Rationale (negative):**
- **Controlled company / dual-class structure persists.** Advance/Newhouse Partnership holds a single share of Class B stock entitled to a vote count reflecting its Charter Holdings units — i.e., super-voting power disproportionate to its ~14.35% economic stake, capped by agreement at 19% economic / 15% voting. Public holders of Class A (one vote) cannot meaningfully outvote the controlling family interest.
- **Two family blocs, not one.** Post-merger, **Cox Enterprises owns ~23%** of the combined entity, governed by a May 2025 voting agreement with A/N. Two strategic family holders with board-designation rights sharpen entrenchment risk and weaken the market for corporate control.
- **Capital allocation record is poor.** Aggressive buybacks executed at $300–427 per share (2024–25) immediately before a decline to $110 — a >$9B drawdown cited in securities litigation. Buying stock at ~2x the eventual price is the clearest empirical test of governance quality, and it failed.
- **Disclosure/oversight failures.** Federal securities-fraud class actions (Rosen, Kessler Topaz, SaxenaWhite) alleging the company **misled investors about the ACP's financial impact**; a second wave of investigations tied to the Liberty Broadband merger (completed 20 Aug 2026) and the post-merger share-price drop; post-breach cyber-oversight questions.
- **M&A complexity:** $34.5B Cox combination plus Liberty Broadband roll-up, assuming ~$12B of Cox debt and ~$840M of Liberty Broadband net debt into an already levered balance sheet (~4x). Related-party-flavoured consolidation among connected families raises conflict questions.

**Mitigants (why not 1–2):** Class A shareholders retain one-share-one-vote for the listed line; 2026 annual meeting saw board and incentive plans approved (no disclosed majority revolt); high insider ownership aligns controlling holders with equity performance; the A/N voting cap at 15% is a partial self-restraint; and Charter has been acquisitive in a way that has at least preserved scale in a consolidating industry.

**Score: 3/10 — weak.** The structure is legal and disclosed, but minority holders bear a structural discount.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US federal (dominant):** FCC — broadband "nutrition label" transparency (partially relaxed in 2026, a modest relief), digital-discrimination rulemaking, data caps and merger conditions on the Cox deal; FTC — deceptive-fee and negative-option enforcement; SEC — Item 1.05 cyber 8-K (4 business days), Reg S-K human capital, and stayed climate rule; NLRB/EEOC/OSHA — labour and accommodation exposure.
- **State:** California SB 253/261 (Scope 1–2 disclosure + assurance, Scope 3 phased) — **applies** given >$1B revenue and CA operations; state broadband-affordability mandates (NY, CA) and state privacy laws (CCPA/CPRA and ~15 others) driving breach-notification and consent costs.
- **EU/UK:** immaterial — no EU revenues of consequence, so **CSRD and CBAM exposure is negligible**. This is a relative ESG-regulatory advantage.
- **ESG-linked debt:** no sustainability-linked bond covenants identified; conventional high-yield/leveraged-loan stack, so no coupon-step ESG trigger. Credit-rating ESG overlays (Fitch: neutral) do affect spreads marginally.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating — **LAGGARD**

Environmental is genuinely low-risk and would screen fine (low carbon intensity, low climate vulnerability, US-only regulatory perimeter). Social is high-risk on data protection, consumer protection and labour accommodation — and these are live, monetised liabilities, not hypotheticals. Governance is the binding constraint: super-voting family control, a second 23% strategic bloc, value-destructive buybacks, and an active securities-fraud docket. On a conventional MSCI-style lens this is a **BB-equivalent / Laggard**, and in an ESG-screened mandate it is exclusion-eligible on governance and cyber grounds.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication — **NEGATIVE for the thesis**

- **Governance discount is structural, not cyclical.** With two family holders controlling the outcome and public shareholders holding one vote per share, activist or strategic intervention is effectively foreclosed. Minority investors cannot force capital-allocation discipline — and the buyback record shows why that matters.
- **Social liabilities are converting to cash costs now.** The breach, the surcharge litigation, and the ACP-disclosure securities suits are quantifiable drags ($60–200M in aggregate plausible cost) arriving while EBITDA is already pressured by subscriber losses.
- **Cost of capital:** an ESG-laggard profile with high leverage and a weakened disclosure record argues for a higher required return; ESG-screened capital is a shrinking buyer base for the equity — a headwind to re-rating.
- **What would change the call (upgrade triggers):** collapse of the Class B super-voting structure; board refreshment with genuinely independent chair and cyber expertise; a credible, assured climate/human-capital disclosure package; and evidence of capital-allocation discipline (deleveraging ahead of buybacks). Absent those, treat the equity as a value/cash-flow story *despite* its ESG profile, not because of it — and apply an explicit governance haircut in the target multiple.

**Bottom line: ESG is a de-rating factor here, not a neutral overlay. Environmental is a non-issue; Governance is the thesis-killer.**
