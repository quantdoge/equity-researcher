## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW (was Wide)**

Charter is the largest U.S. cable operator post-Cox: ~34.3M internet, 13.8M video, 36.7M customer relationships, 13.5M mobile lines, serving ~41 states.

**What the moat was:** local franchise/right-of-way advantages, physical last-mile infrastructure that is uneconomic to duplicate, and high switching costs — a textbook "efficient scale" moat. For two decades this produced pricing power above inflation with essentially no unit growth required.

**What the moat is now — the evidence is unambiguous:**

| Evidence | Reading |
|---|---|
| Pro forma Q3-26 revenue **−1.5% YoY** | Pricing power is gone; revenue is shrinking |
| Pro forma Adj. EBITDA **−4.0% YoY** | Cost inflation outrunning price |
| Internet subs **−156K** legacy (−109K yr ago), **−233K** pro forma | Deterioration *accelerating*, not stabilizing |
| **$6.3B franchise impairment** | Management's own admission the long-term cash flow stream is worth materially less |
| KeyBanc: ~973K / 1.15M / 1.04M internet losses 2026–28 | Street expects multi-year erosion |

Four independent competitors have breached the castle wall: fixed-wireless access (T-Mobile/Verizon), fiber overbuilders, LEO satellite (Starlink), and increasingly aggressive telco fiber. Cable's historical ~60%+ broadband share is no longer defensible at prior prices.

**The one genuine bright spot:** Spectrum Mobile added 508K lines in Q3 (+1.8M LTM), and mobile raises revenue per passing. But mobile is an MVNO riding Verizon's network — it carries **no moat of its own** and structurally lower margins. It is a retention tool, not a franchise.

**Verdict:** The moat has **narrowed materially over five years**. This is now a business defending a declining franchise, not compounding one.

**Strengths:** Operating margin has widened ~340bps in four years — real cost discipline. Interest coverage is adequate (~4.8x EBITDA/interest). No dividend obligation. Predictable, recurring, subscription revenue (the best kind).

**Weaknesses that matter:**

- **Balance sheet is the binding constraint.** $109.4B net debt at 4.2x EBITDA means the equity is the thin slice above the waterline. The company just exchanged $5.5B of secured notes for cash + new notes to delever — a sign of balance-sheet management, but also of necessity.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Multiples are misleading at face value.** The 4.1x P/E and 4.6x P/FCF use FY2025 figures that are both pre-Cox and pre-impairment. The right lens is EV/EBITDA on a pro forma basis:

- Pro forma Adj. EBITDA ≈ **$26.4B** (Q3-26 $6.6B annualized)
- EV ≈ $109.4B + $18.5B = **$128B**
- **EV/EBITDA ≈ 4.8x** vs. ~8–9x CHTR's own history and 6–7x for Comcast/Verizon
- FCF ≈ $6.5B (EBITDA $26.4B − capex $12.8B − interest $5.6B − cash tax $1.5B); **FCF yield ~35%** on $18.5B

That 35% FCF yield is the entire bull case. But it is a *current-year* yield on an EBITDA stream shrinking 4% annually. **A declining annuity deserves a 12–13% capitalization rate, not a 3% one.**

**Scenario DCF (equity value, 10.5% cost of equity):**

| Scenario | Assumption | Equity Value | Per Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Harsh — cliff** (40%) | EBITDA −8%/yr, capex held at $12.5B to defend network; FCF → ~$0 by 2031 | ~$7B | **~$60** |
| **Central — slow bleed** (40%) | EBITDA −4%/yr, capex drifts to $10.5B, interest steady | ~$30B | **~$260** |
| **Stabilize** (20%) | $3B cost program lands, subs inflect 2028, EBITDA flattens | ~$56B | **~$480** |

**Probability-weighted intrinsic value ≈ $220–240/share.**

Charting the implied expectations: today's $147 price discounts roughly "permanent mid-single-digit EBITDA decline with no inflection and no value from the $3B cost program." That is *pessimistic but not absurd.*

**The problem is the distribution, not the mean.** Intrinsic value spans **$60 to $480** — a 8x range. At 4.2x leverage, small errors in the subscriber trajectory produce violent swings in equity value. That dispersion *is* the risk; you cannot underwrite a 20–30% margin of safety against a range that wide with confidence.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Chris Winfrey (CEO since 2023)** — operationally credible, and the Q3 "new operating model" with **$2B of annualized opex savings** on top of **>$1B of Cox synergies** is a serious, specific, self-funded program. That is a genuine positive.

**But capital allocation has been poor, and this is the decisive knock:**

| Year | Buybacks | Approx. avg. price | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $15.4B | ~$650 | **−77%** |
| 2022 | $10.3B | ~$450 | **−67%** |
| 2023 | $3.2B | ~$380 | **−61%** |
| 2024 | $1.2B | ~$320 | **−54%** |
| 2025 | $5.1B | ~$300 | **−51%** |

Over $35B of buybacks executed at prices 2–4x today's quote, financed with debt, at the exact moment the secular growth story was breaking. Share count fell ~36% (183.7M → 117M) yet equity value fell far more. **This is the single clearest failure of the Buffett test: they repurchased the most stock when it was most expensive and are buying almost none now ($21M in Q3-26) when it is cheapest.** That is anti-discipline, and it consumed the deleveraging capacity that would have provided today's margin of safety.

The **Cox acquisition** — closing into a secular decline, funded with $21.9B of new debt — will be vindicated only if scale and $1B of synergies offset competitive erosion. It doubles down rather than diversifies. Empire-building risk is real.

**Score: mediocre (4/10).** Competent operator, poor allocator, no dividend, low insider ownership profile. Not a management I would want compounding my capital through a structural transition.
