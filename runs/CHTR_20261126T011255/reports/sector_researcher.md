## SECTOR BRIEF — COMMUNICATION SERVICES / US CABLE–BROADBAND
**Target: Charter Communications (CHTR) | Peer set: US telecom & cable operators**

---

### 1. Sector Overview

The relevant sector is US fixed-broadband/cable distribution (a sub-industry of Communication Services), a ~$140B revenue pool serving ~115M homes passed. It is unambiguously **late-cycle/mature and now structurally contracting**: video is in secular cord-cutting decline and — for the first time — the broadband subscriber base itself is shrinking as 5G fixed-wireless (FWA) and fibre overbuilders take share. Sector revenue growth has flatlined (CHTR −0.6%, CMCSA −0.0%, VZ +2.5%, T +2.7% in FY2025), with growth accruing almost entirely to wireless-led operators (TMUS +8.5%). The industry is consolidating defensively — Charter closed its Cox acquisition in 2026, immediately booked a **$6.3B impairment** and a $2.4B quarterly net loss, while press speculation links Charter and Comcast. Competition, not demand, is now the binding constraint on the economics.

Note: the FMP sector-aggregate endpoint is unusable here — its "Communication Services" bucket is dominated by GOOGL/META (~$2.4T revenue, 49% gross margin) rather than cable operators, so I built the peer table bottom-up.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Cost self-help is the primary earnings lever.** Charter has launched a **$2B cost-reduction programme** to protect free cash flow; sector-wide opex discipline (headcount, call-centre automation, footprint rationalisation) can offset mid-single-digit revenue pressure.
- **Capex super-cycle creates a defensible speed tier.** Dell'Oro projects broadband equipment spending peaking at **$19.2B in 2028** on DOCSIS 4.0 and rural builds; D4.0 delivers 10 Gbps down / 6 Gbps up, letting cable match consumer fibre tiers without full FTTH rebuild cost.
- **Public subsidy tailwind.** BEAD's **$42.45B** federal programme subsidises unserved-passing builds; incumbents can capture subsidised edge-out economics and the FCC reports unserved-at-100/20 homes fell **23%** between June 2024 and June 2025, expanding addressable coverage.

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Fixed wireless is a permanent share taker.** FWA is forecast at **~9% of US broadband subscriptions by 2026**, priced 30–50% below cable at adequate speeds — the marginal, price-sensitive household has already left.
- **Fibre overbuild + price competition are compressing ARPU and volumes simultaneously.** Cox (now Charter) shed **103k broadband subs in Q2 2026** vs 76k a year earlier; Comcast lost 199k in Q1 2025. Charter's pro-forma Q3 2026 loss was **233k internet customers** with **adj. EBITDA −4% YoY** — negative operating leverage, the classic late-cycle signature.
- **Balance-sheet and regulatory/legal overhang.** Charter carries ~$5.0B annual interest expense, is tendering for notes, and faces a **shareholder investigation (Kahn Swick & Foti, Nov 2026)** plus integration risk on Cox. Capital intensity leaves little room for a price war.

### 5. Charter's Competitive Position: **Average (weakening)**

- **For:** Best-in-class operating margin (24.4% vs 20.7% peer median), 5-yr revenue CAGR (3.0%) above every peer except TMUS, a 32M-customer/41-state footprint that is a genuinely scarce asset, mobile MVNO offload that adds a bundling weapon, and D4.0 upgrade path.
- **Against:** Growth has gone **negative** in the most recent year while the peer median is +2.5%; operating leverage has inverted (EBITDA −4% pro forma); the Cox deal destroyed capital ($6.3B impairment) rather than building a growth engine; and leverage constrains the price response needed to defend share.
- **Verdict:** structurally advantaged cost position, structurally disadvantaged growth vector. Charter is a **cash-harvest asset in a shrinking pool**, not a compounder. The ~63% price decline ($395 June-2025 → ~$147) reflects this re-rating plus deal/legal overhang.

### 6. Sector Rating: **UNATTRACTIVE**

Mature-to-declining volumes, high rivalry, low switching costs, powerful substitutes (FWA, fibre, satellite) and subsidised new entrants now overbuilding incumbent footprints — a structurally challenged Porter configuration. Pricing power is gone; only cost curves remain controllable. The one offset is valuation: at ~4x earnings and ~5x EV/EBITDA, CHTR discounts a great deal of bad news. That is a **stock-specific mean-reversion argument, not a sector-attractiveness argument** — I rate the industry Unattractive while flagging that the price may already embed it.
