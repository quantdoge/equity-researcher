**Price $147.27 | Market cap ~$19.8B | Telecom Services | 91,900 employees | FY end Dec**
*Data as of Nov 25–26, 2026. Note: 10-K and DEF 14A were unavailable in the filing toolset; the most recent 10-Q returned only an exhibit (401(k) plan document), so governance/compensation conclusions rely on press releases, 8-K summaries, Form 4 data and secondary reporting.*

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE (with material caveats)**

**Positives**
- **No CEO/Chairman duality:** Chris Winfrey (CEO) and Alex Taylor (Chair) are separate. Eric Zinterhofer serves as **Lead Independent Director**, providing an independent counterweight.
- **Controller overhang removed:** At the Cox close (Aug 20, 2026), *"Liberty Broadband ceased to be a direct shareholder in Charter and no longer designates directors."* Charter's long-standing Malone/Liberty-affiliated control structure — the subject of prior derivative litigation — is formally unwound. Board expanded to 13 with new appointees Dallas Clement and Mark Greatrex.
- **Insider alignment:** Four directors/executives bought stock on the open market in April–May 2026 at $140–$175 (see §3).

**Negatives / concerns**
- **Non-independent Chairman representing a 10% blockholder.** Alex Taylor is the sitting chairman/CEO of **Cox Enterprises**, which holds ~46.2M shares (~10% owner) and is the *counterparty* to the $34.5B merger. A chairman drawn from the seller's parent is a structural conflict for related-party oversight, integration decisions and future M&A.
- **Board reconstituted mid-crisis** — the board changed composition the same month as the Cox close and the "fundamental redesign" launch, reducing institutional memory exactly when oversight intensity should be highest.
- **History of controller conflicts** (Liberty Broadband as controlling stockholder; prior derivative action settled). The pattern of dominant-blockholder influence persists, just with a new sponsor.
- **Possible finance leadership churn (verify):** Form 4 records list **Kevin Howard as "EVP/Interim CFO/CAO/Controller"** while Jessica Fischer received a CFO-level option award in August 2026. An "interim" CFO designation during a $34.5B integration and a securities class action would be a meaningful control concern — this needs confirmation from the 10-Q cover page or proxy.
- **Compensation:** Large post-merger option refresh on Aug 20, 2026 (Winfrey 412,747; DiGeronimo 210,860; Fischer 134,591; Ray 76,268; Haughton 71,782 options). Say-on-pay results and pay-vs-performance alignment could not be verified. Dilution from merger-related grants is worth monitoring.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Cox/Liberty integration while shrinking.** Charter closed a $34.5B Cox acquisition and the Liberty Broadband merger on Aug 20, 2026, and simultaneously launched a **$2B annualized cost-cut "fundamental redesign"** (~1,200+ roles cut; WARN notices in TX; management departures including Adam Ray and Sharon Peters). Integrating two networks, two workforces and a new chairman *while* cutting 18 months of opex is a classic execution-failure setup — service quality, billing migration and key-person attrition are the likely failure points.
- **Cybersecurity — a realized, recent, severe incident.** April 2026 breach attributed to **ShinyHunters**, via **vishing/social engineering**, compromising ~**4.9M accounts** (up to 42M records threatened); data appeared on a dark-web extortion site May 26, 2026. Charter stated no sensitive PII/CPNI was exfiltrated, but a class action alleges inadequate employee training. This is not a hypothetical risk — it is a demonstrated control failure, and follow-on attacks are common after a public breach.
- **Key-person and succession risk in the C-suite.** CEO Winfrey and CFO Fischer are both named defendants in a securities class action covering the exact period of the ACP disclosure failure, and are leading a turnaround that has already lost senior commercial and management executives. The possible "interim CFO" designation compounds this. Charter's strategic pivot is unusually dependent on a small, legally encumbered leadership team.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **HIGH**

- **The ACP narrative is damaging and durable.** WSJ's framing — *"How One Company Hoovered Up $3 Billion in Broadband Subsidies"* — plus an FCC Inspector General memo alleging improper enrollment, positions Charter as the emblem of subsidy abuse in broadband. This is a political and brand problem that does not go away when the lawsuit does; it invites congressional scrutiny and state AG action.
- **Customer satisfaction is structurally weak.** ACSI score ~68/100 (mid-pack, below Xfinity's 70); 14,000+ complaints filed; NPS/consumer-review scores in the 1.6/5 range on some platforms. Poor satisfaction is the *root cause* of the subscriber losses now driving the financial story — and it makes price-cap conditions and "junk fee" scrutiny more likely.
- **Layoffs + service quality + price caps** is a combustible combination heading into a cost-cutting program, with California already extracting price commitments as the price of merger approval.
- **Litigation cluster:** securities fraud, data breach, false advertising and ACP fraud claims simultaneously — each reinforces the others in the public narrative.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Charter presents a dense, correlated risk profile rather than one isolated problem: an alleged fraud exposure (~$3B of ACP subsidies, with a DOJ precedent settlement against a peer), a securities class action with a December 2026 dispositive hearing, a realized 4.9M-account cyber breach, a $34.5B integration colliding with a $2B cost-cutting program, ~233,000 pro-forma internet subscriber losses in Q3 2026, and a ~65% share price decline. The absence of any SEC enforcement action and the presence of genuine insider buying are the main offsets. This is not "Critical" — no going-concern or accounting-integrity signal was found — but the ACP/FCA tail is unquantified and could escalate the rating if the government intervenes.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **ACP/False Claims Act exposure is the single largest unquantified risk.** ~$3B of federal subsidy receipts, an FCC IG memo alleging improper enrollment (Oct 13, 2025), and a DOJ precedent (DISH, $17.3M, May 2026). Charter calls the claims "baseless." **No reserve or DOJ action has been confirmed — but the absence of disclosure is not the absence of exposure.** Escalate for legal review of the 10-Q's contingent-liability language.
2. **Securities class action MTD hearing Dec 16, 2026** — a survival ruling would materially raise settlement probability and put CEO/CFO personal liability in play. This is a near-term, dated catalyst.
3. **"Interim CFO" designation on Form 4 (Kevin Howard)** alongside Jessica Fischer as CFO — verify whether Charter has an interim finance chief during a merger integration and active securities litigation. Unexplained finance leadership ambiguity is a control red flag.
4. **Non-independent Chairman who is the CEO of a 10% shareholder and merger counterparty** — governance conflict requiring monitoring of related-party transactions and any further M&A.
5. **Asymmetric insider selling** — ~$291M sold vs. ~$3.8M bought; Liberty's exit mechanics explain most of it, but the $12.7M Rutledge liquidation and the $257.9M May block at $204 (vs. $147 today) deserve scrutiny for timing relative to disclosures.
6. **April 2026 breach materiality** — 4.9M accounts via vishing. Confirm whether any regulatory inquiry (state AG, FCC, FTC) has opened, and whether the "no CPNI exfiltration" claim has been independently validated.
