# CHTR — Forensic Short-Seller Analysis

**Charter Communications | Price $147.27 | Mkt Cap $19.85B | Shares 116.9M (Q3'26) | Data through Q3 2026**

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **MEDIUM** (not Fraudulent Risk)

This is the most important finding, and it cuts against a naive short thesis: **Charter is not cooking the books.** The accounting is clean and conservative. The problem is that the *business economics* are deteriorating faster than the accounting can hide. Do not short CHTR on a fraud thesis — you will lose.

- Accruals ratio **negative for four straight years** (−0.072, −0.062, −0.067, −0.068): cash earnings *exceed* book earnings. Manipulators show the opposite.
- Operating CF / net income **3.22x**; FCF conversion 0.89x. (Caveat: for a business with $8.7B of D&A, a >3x cash conversion is structurally normal, not a quality trophy. The composite score of 60/100 is the honest read.)
- Beneish M-Score **−2.65** — well below both thresholds. No manipulation signal.
- No inventory (service business) — inventory-days channel-stuffing test is not applicable.

Earnings quality is *honest*; it is simply insufficient. Book net income overstates distributable cash because capex ($11.7B) exceeds D&A ($8.7B) by $3B — a company living on a growing capital treadmill.

## 3. Beneish M-Score & Accruals Detail

**M-Score −2.65 (Low risk).** Components confirm no accrual games: GMI 0.993, AQI 0.970, SGI 0.994, DEPI 1.065, SGAI 1.001, LVGI 0.998, TATA −0.0719. Only DSRI (1.195) is elevated — the receivables issue in Flag #2.

*Presentation caveat:* COGS and SG&A were reclassified between FY2022 and FY2024 (COGS $30.6B→$33.3B→$29.8B; SG&A $11.2B→$8.8B→$12.1B). Gross-margin trend analysis across that boundary is unreliable — a reminder that "gross margin expansion" narratives at CHTR are partly accounting geography, not pricing power.

**Altman Z-Score: 0.60 — Distress zone** (threshold 1.81). x1 −0.053, x2 −0.035, x3 0.081, x4 0.154, x5 0.355.

**Piotroski F-Score: 7 — but overstates health.** F5 "lower leverage" scored a point only because *book* equity rose; cash-flow leverage rose materially. Treat as ~5, not 7.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment: **Severe and Structural**

The moat was a physical monopoly on the last mile. That moat is being bypassed on both flanks:

- **Fixed wireless (T-Mobile/Verizon)** is taking the low-end, price-sensitive customer at near-zero marginal cost to the carrier. This is permanently destroying the incremental-subscriber economics.
- **Fiber overbuilders** are taking the high-end, higher-ARPU customer in the densest, most profitable footprint.
- **Cox integration** adds scale but also adds a second underperforming asset into a declining industry — and the $6.3B write-down confirms it was priced for a growth case that no longer exists.
- **The cost-cut trap:** $2B of annualized savings over 18 months (~9% of opex) is being extracted from a customer-facing business that just lost 233K subs in a quarter. Cutting service quality to protect EBITDA accelerates churn — a textbook doom loop.

Offsetting positive: **Spectrum Mobile at 13.5M lines** is genuinely growing and bundles are sticky. This is the strongest part of the bull case.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — and the Bull Case

**Bear case (mathematical, not rhetorical):**
EBITDA ~$23B post-Cox, capex ~$10B, interest ~$5.4B annualized → true FCF ~$5–6B before growth capex, and reported FCF already down to $674M in Q3'26 (vs. $1,621M). Debt $108.6B at ~4.7x. If EBITDA merely *declines 15%* and the multiple compresses from 5.6x to 5.0x, enterprise value falls to ~$98B against $108.6B of debt — **equity is worth zero.** This is not a tail scenario; it is the base case if broadband losses stay at 200K+/quarter. Deleveraging at $3–4B/year against $108.6B takes a decade, and EBITDA is shrinking, not growing.

**Consensus is set up to be cut:** The Street models 2027 revenue of **$64.5B** vs. a FY2026 run-rate of ~$56–57B — roughly +13%, on a business losing 233K subs a quarter. EPS estimates span **$16.43 to $67.99** for 2027 — a 4x dispersion that signals zero analytical conviction. Negative revisions are the catalyst, not a hypothetical.

**But here is the other side, and it is real:**
- The stock is **already down ~82%** from its $818 peak (Sep 2021) and trades at ~5.6x EV/EBITDA with a mid-teens FCF yield on equity. Peers (CMCSA, VZ, T) sit at 6–7x. The easy money has been made.
- It has **rallied +44% off the October 2026 low of $102**. Shorting a heavily-shorted, deeply-depressed stock into a squeeze is how shorts die.
- Cable is an essential, cash-generative utility with 34.3M subscribers. It does not go to zero overnight.
- $2B of cost cuts are highly likely to be delivered — Charter has a strong integration track record.
- Capex is already falling 11.2% YoY; a 2027 FCF inflection is plausible, and private-market cable multiples sit above public ones. A PE take-private or asset sale (spectrum) is a genuine upside tail.

**Verdict on the trade:** this is a *credit story expressed in the equity*, not a fraud story. It is a slow-bleed short, not a blow-up short.

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **SHORT** (moderate conviction — not Strong Short)

**Reasoning:** The forensic work *clears* Charter of manipulation, which removes the highest-return version of the short. What remains is a leveraged structural decliner with accelerating subscriber losses, rising debt, negative tangible equity, an Altman score of 0.60, a begun impairment cycle, and the worst capital-allocation record in large-cap telecom — with consensus revenue estimates that are ~11% too high. That is a legitimate short.

I am **not** calling Strong Short because the 82% drawdown, the +44% bounce, and a ~5.6x multiple mean the risk/reward is far worse than it was two years ago, and a plausible FCF-inflection/PE-takeout scenario caps downside. Shorting at $147 after a 44% rally is materially better than shorting at $110, but position sizing should be small, entry staggered into strength ($155–170), and the stop must be disciplined on any credible 2027 FCF guide-up.

**What would make me flip:** evidence that broadband net losses have peaked below ~100K/quarter, or a credible deleveraging plan funded by asset sales rather than EBITDA extraction.

**What would make this a Strong Short:** a dividend initiation or resumed large buyback funded with new debt while EBITDA is flat — that would confirm management is still prioritizing the share price over solvency.
