## CHTR — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment
*Research cut-off: 25–26 Nov 2026 | Sources: SEC EDGAR, FCC, CPUC, court dockets, trade press*

### 1. Geographic Risk Map

**Headline finding: Charter is a single-jurisdiction company.** Post-Cox it operates in **45 US states**, ~59M homes/businesses passed and roughly 32M+ customers. There is **no disclosed non-US revenue**; the 10-Q/10-K segment structure is a single US cable/broadband reportable segment. Geographic diversification is effectively **zero** — this is the central geographic fact about CHTR.

| Geography | Rev. exposure (est.) | Risk level | Drivers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **United States — total** | **~100%** | **Medium** | Stable rule of law, but concentrated regulatory/political exposure |
| Legacy Charter footprint (41 states; Northeast, Southeast, Midwest, Texas, Carolinas) | ~70–75% | Medium | Municipal franchise renewals; state PUC oversight; legacy TWC/BHN markets |
| Cox footprint added Aug 2026 (CA, AZ, NV, OH, GA, VA, LA, OK, AR, FL, CT, RI, KS, NE, IA, ID, UT, MO, NC) — 11M passings, 5M customers | ~25–30% | **Medium-High** | California CPUC conditioned approval (Aug 12, 2026); CA net-neutrality/rate-regulation activism |

| **New York / Connecticut** | ~5–8% (est.) | Medium | NY first state to impose one-year pause on hyperscale data-center permits (Jul 14, 2026); CT is HQ state |

*Caveat: Charter does not disclose revenue by state. Percentages above are footprint-based estimates, not company-reported figures — treat as directional.*

**Concentration read-through:** 100% US revenue means CHTR has **no geopolitical hedge** (no Russia exposure to lose, but also no insulation from a single federal/state policy regime). This is the opposite of a tariff-and-sanctions risk profile; it is a pure **domestic regulatory-beta** profile.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Policy Risks

**Risk 1 — Federal and state re-regulation of broadband (Title II / rate regulation / BEAD strings)**
- *Probability: Medium (rising at state level) | Impact: High*
- The FCC has been deregulatory (approved Charter/Cox Feb 27, 2026 **without** behavioral conditions on interconnection or data caps — per Morgan Lewis). But the counter-pressure is state-level and litigation-driven. California has formally **challenged BEAD Condition 50**, the federal bar on rate regulation and net-neutrality-style requirements attached to BEAD-funded networks ([AEI](https://www.aei.org/domestic-policy/if-you-give-california-a-cookie-its-gonna-want-rate-regulation-and-net-neutrality/); [Truth on the Market](https://truthonthemarket.com/2026/11/12/california-wants-broadband-with-no-strings-attached/)). If California prevails, states gain a template for rate oversight of the highest-ARPU broadband market in the country. NTIA is simultaneously telling ISPs their grants are "at risk" if contracts don't include verbatim federal prohibitions.
- *Why it matters:* ~$6.33B franchise impairment booked in Q3 2026 signals the market is already repricing the regulatory/asset-value of the footprint.

**Risk 2 — Subsidy-program dependence and withdrawal (ACP precedent)**
- *Probability: High that another subsidy cycle is politicized | Impact: Medium-High, already realized*
- ~5M Charter customers received ACP benefits before the program's June 2024 lapse. The unwind drove the subscriber losses that now sit at the center of securities litigation. Any future Lifeline/ACP successor program becomes a political football post-midterms, and FCC enforcement in this area is active (the FCC proposed a **$4.4M Lifeline fraud fine** on Oct 29, 2026 — [FCC Daily Digest](https://www.fcc.gov/edocs/daily-digest/2026/10/29)).

**Risk 3 — Consolidation/megamerger antitrust path dependency**
- *Probability: Medium | Impact: High if pursued*
- CHTR's strategic logic now rests on scale M&A ($34.5B Cox, plus Liberty Broadband merger). Market Chatter about a **Comcast–Charter combination** is persistent, though Comcast executives publicly said in April 2026 they are not exploring cable mergers and the deal is "tough to get done" ([Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/04/comcast-cable-mergers-1236869475/); [Fierce](https://www.fierce-network.com/broadband/analysts-float-idea-comcast-charter-merger)). A second transformational deal would face a materially harder federal review than Cox did — and CHTR cannot easily grow without it.

**Additional, lower-grade items:** tariffs/export controls are a modest margin risk via network gear and customer-premises equipment sourced from Asia; data-center permitting moratoria (NY already imposed a one-year pause) affect infrastructure siting.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Data privacy / CPNI (highest-priority flag).** A May 2026 breach with ~4.85M+ affected accounts, class-action consolidation, and multi-state notification obligations. Despite the "no CPNI exfiltrated" claim, **state AG inquiries** under CCPA/CPRA and equivalent statutes are the likely next step. No formal FCC enforcement publicly announced as of late May 2026 — **no confirmed FCC action, flag as unresolved rather than clear**.
2. **Federal subsidy-program compliance (Lifeline/ACP).** Charter was a very large ACP participant; the combination of (a) the securities class action alleging it overstated its ability to retain ACP customers, and (b) an FCC actively issuing Lifeline fraud fines creates theoretical **False Claims Act** tail risk. No FCA suit identified — flag as monitoring item.
3. **Disclosure/IR controls.** Two overlapping shareholder actions (securities class + KSF derivative investigation) over the same ACP narrative is a governance signal worth noting to the investment committee.
4. **Sanctions / FCPA / export controls: minimal.** Domestic-only revenue, no disclosed foreign subsidiaries of consequence, no sanctioned-country exposure, no disclosed DOJ/SEC bribery or sanctions inquiry. **This is a genuine strength of the profile.**
5. **State net-neutrality compliance.** Operating obligations in CA (and similar states) create divergent network-management compliance requirements across 45 states.

*Not found / unable to verify:* no 10-K was retrievable via the filing tool (the most recent annual report attempt returned an error, and the 10-Q fetch returned only an exhibit, not MD&A/legal proceedings). **The Litigation and Contingencies note and full Risk Factors section could not be read directly — the above legal inventory is reconstructed from dockets, plaintiff-firm disclosures, FCC/CPUC records and trade press, not from the filing itself.** Any 10-Q-disclosed accruals or DOJ/subpoena disclosures would be additive.

### 5. Overall Rating

## Geopolitical Risk: **LOW** — Legal/Regulatory Risk: **HIGH** → Composite: **MEDIUM-HIGH**

**Rationale.** Strip out the label and CHTR has almost no *geopolitical* risk in the conventional sense: no emerging markets, no Russia/China sanctions exposure, no currency controls, no expropriation risk, no cross-border trade tension. That is worth stating plainly because it is unusual and it is real. The entire risk surface is **domestic US regulatory and litigation**, and there the picture is considerably worse than the geographic map implies:

- A ~$105B+ enterprise sitting on **one jurisdiction's** policy decisions, with the FCC's posture able to flip with an administration;
- **Two active shareholder actions** naming the CEO and CFO, plus a derivative investigation opened **nine days ago**;
- A **nine-case data-breach consolidation** in Connecticut;
- State-level regulators (California especially) now demonstrated to extract conditions at the state level even when the FCC extracts none;
- A **$6.33B franchise write-down** that is effectively management conceding the regulated asset base is impaired.

**Market context:** CHTR has de-rated from ~$250 (Apr 2026) to **$147.27**, with a −25% single-day break on **24 Apr 2026** on 13.3M shares and a further leg down through September. Much of this is competitive (fixed wireless, fiber overbuild), not regulatory — but the $6.33B impairment and the litigation calendar are entangled with the regulatory story, and the equity is now pricing a structurally smaller, more heavily supervised business.

**What would change the rating:**
- *Upgrade to Medium:* dismissal or cheap settlement of *Sandoval*; a favorable ruling upholding BEAD Condition 50 (foreclosing state rate regulation); no state AG action arising from the breach.
- *Downgrade to High:* any state AG or FCC CPNI enforcement action; a court striking down Condition 50 and triggering state rate-setting bills; federal reclassification of broadband under Title II; a DOJ second request on any Comcast transaction.
