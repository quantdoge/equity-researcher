## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — Extreme concentration in US government demand & political alignment (Probability: High | Impact: High)**
~54% of revenue (and accelerating: US gov +90% YoY) comes from one sovereign buyer bloc. Growth is unusually tied to the Trump administration's defense/AI priorities and to Karp's explicit pro-US-defense posture (his "pro-West manifesto," book *The Technological Republic*). A budget drawdown, procurement-policy shift (e.g., data-sovereignty mandates, Maven-style awards to competitors like Scale AI/Anduril), or administration change would hit revenue and the premium multiple simultaneously. The same alignment that wins Army/ICE contracts creates headline risk (deportation-crackdown role) and potential policy volatility.

**Risk 2 — European sovereign-tech backlash threatens the international growth engine (Probability: Medium-High | Impact: Medium-High)**
Europe is the only meaningful non-US market (~19% of revenue). US–EU trust is deteriorating (data-transfer legal fights, trade friction, "buy European" defensetech push). Concrete evidence: Bundeswehr is evaluating **European alternatives to Palantir** amid security concerns; UK political parties' grassroots motions to cancel the NHS contract (Labour conference blocked one, but pressure persists); AI Act, NIS2, DORA, Data Act compliance burden (explicitly flagged in 10-Q risk factors). If "AI sovereignty" translates into domestic-European preference, Palantir's international growth (and its sovereign-AI pitch via Nebius) faces structural headwinds.

**Risk 3 — Israel/Gaza & military-AI controversy spreading into Western public-sector procurement (Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium)**
Palantir's IDF work in Gaza/West Bank is at the center of an organized campaign (Amnesty UK, Just Treatment, openDemocracy, Business & Human Rights Resource Centre war-crimes allegations) that is actively targeting its **UK NHS and other Western public contracts**. This creates a credible pathway where reputational boycotts → lost tenders → EU/UK pipeline damage. It also raises export-license / responsible-use scrutiny as Western governments formalize AI-weapons rules. Ukraine exposure cuts the other way (defense-hawk tailwind), partially offsetting.

**Watch items (lower probability, large impact):** Russia/Ukraine settlement or NATO drawdown changing defense-tech demand; US–China AI export-control re-regulation hitting Palantir's open-model advocacy; EU Court data-transfer ruling disrupting EU cloud delivery.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation**
- **Securities class action — materially resolved:** A multibillion-dollar securities class action against Palantir was **dismissed with prejudice in April 2025** (Freshfields securing dismissal). Together with the earlier *In re Palantir Class F Stock Litigation* (settled FY2022), the headline shareholder-litigation overhang is largely cleared.
- **Derivative suits:** Company disclosures (FY2024 proxy/ARS) reference **ongoing purported derivative lawsuits** tied to the founders/board — standard post-IPO overhang, not yet material.
- **UK NHS litigation:** Prior NHS data-deal lawsuit won by campaigners (openDemocracy, over £23M Matt Hancock-era deal); the current £330M FDP is under legal threat + ministerial break-clause review. **Active, elevated** UK legal-political risk.
- **No active DOJ/FTC/SEC enforcement identified** in the research window.

**Regulatory exposure**
- **EU stack:** 10-Q risk factors explicitly flag GDPR, **EU AI Act**, NIS2, DORA, and the Data Act as compliance burdens on EU operations — AI Act obligations (high-risk systems) are the most consequential for Palantir's defense/intel products.
- **US:** FedRAMP/DOD ATO regimes, ITAR/export controls, potential AI executive-order/legislative regimes (Palantir lobbying against open-model restrictions and for accelerated military AI).
- **Data privacy:** UK/EU patient-data handling (NHS) is the highest-profile live privacy-regulatory issue; no fine recorded to date.

**Government-relations posture (double-edged):** Active public voice on AI policy (with Nvidia, urging US not to ban open AI models; Karp's national-security-AI advocacy). Influential, but increases regulatory-policy-pivot risk.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Human-rights-linked business (highest flag):** Israel/Gaza targeting allegations and US immigration-enforcement (ICE) work create exposure to export-license challenges, foreign-corrupt-practices-adjacent due-diligence scrutiny on foreign-government sales (Israel, Ukraine), and civil-society/activist litigation — no FCPA/sanctions findings to date, but the profile is high-risk by category.
2. **Foreign-government sales without clear compliance daylight:** Sales to intelligence/military agencies (Israel, Ukraine, NATO nations) increase BSA/export-control/Anti-Money-Laundering diligence burden; Ukraine wartime data handling raises data-governance questions.
3. **Single-country concentration:** ~81% of Q2'26 revenue was US — jurisdiction risk is low, but a single-country shock (budget/policy) is a systemic, undiversified exposure.
4. **Insider selling cadence:** recurring officer Form 4 sales (e.g., $5.4M after a 40% weekly gain) — routine for founder-led names but worth monitoring for sentiment.
5. **No sanctions violations, no FCPA actions, no material GDPR fines** were found in public record — the legal/compliance file is cleaner than the reputational file.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** Country-level sovereign risk is genuinely low — ~81% of revenue is US-generated within a stable, rule-of-law jurisdiction, and the marquee securities litigation was dismissed with prejudice. But the risk profile is elevated by: (i) extreme, politically contingent US-government revenue concentration (~54% and growing fastest); (ii) an active European sovereign-tech backlash hitting the lone international growth engine, including live UK legal/political attacks on the NHS contract and Bundeswehr's public move away from US vendors; (iii) sustained Israel/Gaza and military-AI controversy that has moved from headlines into procurement campaigns; and (iv) a fast-moving EU/US AI-regulatory regime that Palantir is simultaneously lobbying and complying with. Net: **High**, skewing to tail-risk from policy whiplash in defense/AI procurement and from Europe's trust deficit.
