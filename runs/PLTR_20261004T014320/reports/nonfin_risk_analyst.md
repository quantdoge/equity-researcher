# PALANTIR TECHNOLOGIES INC. (NASDAQ: PLTR) — Non-Financial Risk Assessment
**Report Date:** October 2026 | **Price:** ~$188.75 | **Market Cap:** ~$433B | **Sector:** Software – Infrastructure

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Weak**

**Evidence:**
- **Concentrated super-voting control.** Three co-founders — Alexander Karp (CEO), Stephen Cohen (President/Growth), and Peter Thiel (Chairman) — hold Class F super-voting shares that give them voting control disproportionate to economic ownership. The company's own equity plan carves out "Specified Stockholders" (Karp, Cohen, Thiel) from Change-in-Control provisions — a documented entrenchment mechanism. Board elections are effectively controlled by the founder bloc.
- **Chairman/CEO both co-founders.** Roles are technically split, but both occupants are founders with Class F control; this is separation in name, concentration in substance.
- **Board composition.** ~8-member board with some independent directors (e.g., Alexander Moore chairs Audit; independent members sit on Compensation). Formal independence exists **but** is undercut by founder voting control.
- **Compensation alignment.** Equity-heavy pay aligns with shareholders on paper, yet the dominant observable behavior is the opposite of alignment: relentless monetization of insider stakes (see §3). Proxy notes annual say-on-pay is advisory; with Class F control, shareholders cannot compel change.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **Moderate**

- **No active SEC/DOJ enforcement** found in EDGAR full-text search for PLTR; no fines or formal investigations surfaced. Clean on securities-law enforcement.
- **UK scrutiny is the live regulatory event.** A Westminster parliamentary report flagged "concerning" UK reliance on Palantir, including sensitive military data potentially accessible to US authorities. **The UK government must decide by December 2026** whether to renew the £330M NHS England Federated Data Platform contract (break clause). NHS England has admitted a public document describing Palantir patient-data handling "was wrong" — a transparency/data-governance failure.
- **EU attrition:** France's domestic intelligence service (DGSI) is removing Palantir in favor of a local vendor — a regulatory/political signal in a key strategic market.
- **US privacy exposure (immigration):** ICE awarded Palantir a ~$30M "ImmigrationOS" contract (July 2025) and uses Palantir AI to screen tip lines; critics and researchers have raised CCPA/privacy and civil-liberties concerns tied to mass-deportation operations. No enforcement action yet, but headline litigation/legislative risk is real.
- **Positive regulatory tailwind (disclose both sides):** FAA launched its AI-based SMART air-traffic system on Palantir tech (Sept 2026); Army awarded $480M (Maven prototype, firm-fixed-price) and a $795M Maven Smart System contract running to 2029.

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis: **Heavy, persistent net SELLING; zero buying**

- Chairman **Peter Thiel sold ~$457M (12.4M shares)**; Barron's reports **his 10b5-1 plan is now exhausted** — i.e., the largest insider is done selling because there are no shares left in the plan.
- CEO **Alex Karp sold ~$86M in August 2026 alone**; another 492,348-share sale filed September 2026 under a February 2026 10b5-1 plan.
- ~**$95.4M sold across six executives in August 2026**; **~$117M total insider sales over the trailing 90 days** (mostly 10b5-1); co-founder Cohen sold ~$43.5M in May 2026.
- I found **no open-market insider purchases** in the review window. Nearly all sales are pre-arranged plans, which reduces timing-suspicion, but the *aggregate* signal — founders and C-suite liquidating hundreds of millions into a record-high valuation — is unmistakably bearish on a conviction basis.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Government & policy concentration.** A large share of revenue comes from US federal/defense and UK government contracts; growth is increasingly tied to defense-AI spending (Maven) and European sovereign programs. Budget cycles, procurement protests, and geopolitical shifts (Ukraine, UK leadership change) can move revenue materially and abruptly.
- **Key-person risk.** CEO Karp is the public face, strategic architect, and a Class F voting controller; co-founders' ongoing de-risking concentrates both value and dependence on fewer committed hands. No obvious successor was signaled in available filings.
- **Cyber/security target profile.** As custodian of intelligence, military, law-enforcement, immigration, and patient data across GOV/AWS and customer environments, Palantir is a premier attack target. No public breach surfaced in the scan, but the 10-K itself flags elevated cyber risk — and a single material compromise would be catastrophic to the franchise's trust model.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **Moderate-to-High**

- Persistent, structural ESG-controversy exposure: surveillance/ICE deportation ties, NHS patient-data misstatements, Met Police contract extension backlash, and ongoing privacy-advocate campaigns. These have historically **not** dented commercial demand (defense/national-security tailwind is strong — stock +cybersecurity-rally outperformance), but they are being converted into **hard commercial losses** in Europe (France exit) and create contract-renewal risk in the UK (Dec 2026 NHS decision).
- Marginal governance-reputational item: reported revolving-door lobbying links in the UK (lobbyist with ties to then-new PM Burnham) invite scrutiny of government-contract integrity.
- Investor sentiment remains bullish on growth/AI narratives; reputational drag is concentrated in civil-society/regulatory circles, not (yet) customers or investors.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **High**

**Rationale:** No fraud indicators, no active enforcement, and no data breach — but the combination is what drives the rating: (a) **Weak governance** founded on permanent founder-class voting control; (b) **sustained, large-scale insider selling** by every co-founder into a ~$433B, ~60x-revenue style valuation with a rich multiple; (c) **live regulatory/contract decisions** (UK NHS, EU erosion) and **structural reputational exposure** in privacy and surveillance. These are the risks that don't show on the balance sheet but can re-rate the stock regardless of fundamentals.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Founder-class control + mass insider exit.** All three Class F controlling stockholders (Karp, Cohen, Thiel) have been heavy net sellers via plans at/near all-time valuation levels; Chairman Thiel's plan is fully exhausted at ~$457M. No insider buying exists to offset. **Escalate to governance committee.**
2. **UK NHS £330M contract decision (December 2026).** A counterparty break-clause decision is imminent; loss would be a material commercial + reputational event and could accelerate EU attrition (France precedent). Track closely.
3. **ICE/immigration data-processing exposure.** No enforcement yet, but CCPA/civil-liberties litigation over ImmigrationOS and tip-screening is a credible path to fines or injunctions; monitor dockets and state AG actions.
4. **Extreme valuation + high beta (1.62) + key-person concentration** in a policy-sensitive revenue mix — if sentiment or federal budget cycles turn, drawdown risk is amplified by the non-financial factors above.
