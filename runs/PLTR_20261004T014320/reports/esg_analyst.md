## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**
- Publishes annual GHG Emissions Reports (2023, 2024) — disclosure exists but is above the sector median, not best-in-class. Scope 1/2 minimal for a pure software company.
- Main E risk is **Scope 3**: AIP inference and cloud consumption grow with ARR; Palantir is a *seller* of decarbonisation software (Foundry net-zero solutions) but its own compute footprint is a rising indirect cost/emission line.
- No environmental fines, spills, or controversies found. **Backward-looking E risk is immaterial; forward-looking it is a growth-correlated cost line, not a valuation risk.**

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**
This is where Palantir's risk sits:
- **Immigration enforcement**: Active DHS/ICE programmes incl. "ImmigrationOS" tooling; EFF's Jan-2026 report ("ICE Using Palantir Tool That Feeds On Medicaid Data") and subsequent Palantir rebuttal; ACLU has catalogued Palantir's role in removal operations; NYC Comptroller formally requested a third-party human-rights risk assessment.
- **Civil-society opposition since IPO**: Amnesty International flagged the ICE conflict at the 2020 direct listing; ongoing "Delete Genocide Tech" campaign; Pax/Privacy International report on AI-at-war exposes military-contract ethical drift.
- **Europe friction**: Germany's Federal Constitutional Court (Feb-2023) constrained police use of automated analysis (Hesse), Germany excluded Palantir from federal procurement, and EU trade unions are pressing for contract termination; UK FCA and NHS deployments face lawmaker/NHS-staff scrutiny.
- **Investor impact already realised**: KLP (Norway) divested ~$24M on human-rights grounds.
- Counterweight: strong US government demand (TITAN, Maven, Army enterprise agreement) and Karp's explicit pro-Western/pro-defence positioning are demand tailwinds — social risk is **concentrated in use-case/political risk, not labour or safety**. Internal workforce risk is moderate (no major labour controversies found; 4.4k staff).

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **4 / 10**
- **Dual/triple-class control**: Class F shares (held in a voting trust by Karp, Cohen, Thiel) control ~49.999999% of votes; individually Thiel ~28.4%, Karp ~8.9%, Cohen ~3.1%. Minority shareholders cannot effect board change.
- **Charter entrenchment**: "Change in Control" definitions contain carve-outs for Specified Stockholders (founders) — including founder share transfers not triggering CIC, and director elections by >50%-vote holders exempt from CIC — explicit minority-protection dilution.
- **CEO-Chairman duality** (Karp); one founder-director (Thiel) still on board.
- **Shareholder democracy**: 2025 AGM re-elected all directors, ratified EY, approved pay, and **rejected three ESG-focused shareholder proposals** — a clear signal of non-responsiveness to ESG resolutions.
- **Pay governance weakness**: $17.2M CEO private-jet usage (2025) is an outlier perquisite vs. peers; comp is heavily equity-linked (appropriate for growth alignment) but oversight optics are poor.
- Positives: US public-company baseline (independent audit committee, SBC expensing, robust 10-K/10-Q risk disclosure), no corruption/fraud controversies found, government procurement scrutiny imposes real anti-corruption discipline.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure
- **EU AI Act**: Palantir's law-enforcement/high-risk deployments fall squarely in high-risk classification — EU ops face traceability, human-oversight and DPA-oversight duties; German privacy regulators are already vocal on the Act's law-enforcement provisions.
- **EU CSRD**: EU subsidiaries (sizeable UK/EU revenue) are likely in-scope for sustainability reporting in the 2025–26 waves; current GHG-report-only disclosure will need an uplift to CSRD standard.
- **German data-protection case law** (Federal Constitutional Court, Feb-2023): constrains automated police data analysis — a template risk for EU-wide rollout.
- **US**: SEC climate-disclosure rule vacated/contested — limited direct burden; **export controls on advanced AI models** (2025 final rule) are a dual-ended regulatory exposure given international government sales.
- **Anti-corruption/procurement**: heavy US federal contracting = FAR/DFARS compliance; FCPA exposure in international security-sales channel.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD** (leaning ESG Risk)
Palantir exhibits *low E risk*, *high concentration in S (product-ethics/human-rights) risk*, and *structurally weak G (founder-controlled capital structure, rejected ESG proposals, pay-perquisite excess)*. It fails no environmental or anti-corruption screen, but the combination of EU regulatory headwinds, civil-society campaigns, institutional divestments, and entrenched founder control keeps it out of the "Average" tier. The Social dimension's risk is *thesis-dependent*: it is a liability in Europe and under human-rights screens, yet simultaneously an asset with current US defence/intelligence demand.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL (with negative skew)**
ESG factors are **not the primary valuation driver** — the thesis rests on AIP-led US commercial growth and defence contracts (US gov revenue ~$970M in 2025, Army/Maven/TITAN scale). However, three ESG items warrant monitoring in the thesis:
1. **European licence to operate** — AI Act enforcement and German/UK political pushback could cap the international expansion that justifies part of the multiple;
2. **US political regime dependence** — ICE/DHS and defence demand can reverse with administrations, a two-sided tail risk;
3. **Minority-shareholder discount** — Class F control (~50% of votes) is permanent; the 2025 rejection of all ESG proposals confirms no near-term change.
Net: no ESG-driven short, but hold position sizing aware of headline volatility from surveillance/immigration narratives and EU regulatory rulings; a meaningful negative catalyst (EU contract loss, EFF-style probe escalation) would warrant de-rating.
