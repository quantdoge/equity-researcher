# Okta, Inc. (NASDAQ: OKTA) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price:** $201.57 | **Market cap:** ~$35B | **EV:** ~$33B (net cash ~$2B) | **FY ends Jan 31**
*Data as of the Nov-2026 profile; FY2026 = year ended 1/31/2026. Latest reported quarter: Q2 FY2027 (7/31/2026).*

**Context that matters:** OKTA has run from **$62.93 (Apr 2026) → $201.57**, including two single-day gap-ups of ~+30% (5/29/26) and +29% (8/27/26), plus S&P 500 inclusion effective Nov 4, 2026 (~$5B of index demand). This is a re-rating on narrative (agentic-AI identity) and flows, not a step-change in the business. That distinction drives my conclusion.

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow**

**What the business is:** The leading neutral identity provider — SSO, MFA, lifecycle management — plus Auth0 for customer identity, now extended to AI-agent and non-human identities. 77% gross margin, 6,366 employees.

**Evidence for a moat (switching costs, not network effects):**
- Identity is the control plane for every employee, customer and now agent. Ripping out an IdP means re-integrating thousands of apps and re-engineering access policy — genuinely high switching costs once embedded.
- **Neutrality is a real asset:** Okta works with Microsoft, AWS and Google; Microsoft's Entra ID is bundled into E3/E5 and effectively "free," yet Okta still grows because multi-cloud, regulated and best-of-breed buyers pay for independence.
- Gross margin improved 69.5% → 77.4% over four years; RPO $4.86B and cRPO $2.59B gives high revenue visibility.

**Evidence against a wide moat — and it is substantial:**
- **Growth has collapsed:** revenue growth 55.7% → 42.9% → 21.8% → 15.3% → **11.8%**, with FY27 guided to only **~10.5%** ($3.22–3.23B). A wide-moat compounder in a growing market does not decelerate to low-teens at ~$3B revenue. This is the signature of a company at the mercy of a bundled competitor.
- **No pricing power visible in returns:** ROIC 2.0% (FY26) after four years of losses; GAAP operating margin just 5.1%. ROIC has never exceeded WACC (~9–10%).
- Competitors are larger, better-capitalized and converging on identity: Microsoft (Entra), CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, CyberArk, Google. Okta is the specialist that must out-execute platforms with bundle economics.

**Moat trend over 5 years: narrowed.** The Auth0 acquisition broadened the franchise but coincided with decelerating growth and $5.6B of goodwill/intangibles sitting on a $7.0B equity base — 80% of book is acquisition accounting, not economic assets.

**Strengths:** Genuine operating leverage — GAAP op margin swung ~48 points in three years. FCF has compounded from $63M to $863M. Balance sheet is fortress-like: $2.55B cash vs $422M debt (**net cash ~$2.0B**), no refinancing risk. Beats every quarter in the data set (8 straight), and buybacks began in FY27 ($372M in H1) — no dividend, correctly.

**The flaw Buffett would fixate on:** FCF is *not* owner earnings here. **SBC is $544M against $863M of FCF and $235M of GAAP net income.** Strip SBC out and FY2026 owner earnings are **~$320M**, not $863M. FCF conversion of 3.7x net income is a red flag, not a virtue. SBC is improving (36%→18.6% of revenue, ~$460M annualized now) but remains 3–5x peer norms and is a real, recurring cost paid in ownership.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **No margin of safety; ~40% premium to intrinsic value**

Current multiples (EV ~$33B, FY27E revenue $3.225B, FY27E FCF ~$0.92B at guided 27–28% margin):

| EV / FY27E FCF **ex-SBC** (~$0.46B) | **~72x** | the honest number |
| P/E (GAAP, TTM) | **154x** | peer median P/E 28.8x |
| P/E (FY27E non-GAAP $3.90–3.94) | **~51x** | |
| FCF yield (reported) | 2.4% | vs ~4.3% risk-free |
| Owner earnings yield (ex-SBC) | **~0.9%** | |

**A tool I am deliberately rejecting:** the mechanical DCF returned ~$520/share ($91.7B) because it extrapolates a **30% FCF growth rate** from a 3-year base that starts at $63M. FY27 guidance is 10.5% revenue growth. Using that output would be malpractice.

**My own DCF** (base $0.92B FCF, 10-yr projection, 3% terminal):

| Scenario | Growth | WACC | Value/share |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reported FCF, conservative | 12% | 10% | **~$161** |
| Reported FCF, generous | 12% | 9% | ~$190 |
| **SBC-adjusted (true owner earnings)** | 15% | 10% | **~$105** |

**Intrinsic value estimate: ~$105–165, midpoint ~$135.** Even granting that reported FCF is real, the *bull* case (13–15% FCF growth for a decade, 25x exit) supports only ~$175–180 — **below today's price.** The market is capitalizing SBC as if it were free and assuming ~14% FCF growth for ten years (≈4 points of annual margin expansion) to justify $202. That is priced for perfection.

At a proper 25–30% margin of safety, my buy price is **<$100**. OKTA traded at $63 eight months ago.

## 4. Management Quality — **Good (6.5/10)**

- **Founder-led:** Todd McKinnon (co-founder/CEO) and Frederic Kerrest remain; that owner-operator alignment is a plus. Execution on the margin story is real and disciplined — promises on profitability were kept.
- **Capital allocation: mixed but improving.** Auth0 ($6.5B, 2021) was expensive and coincided with growth deceleration; $5.6B of goodwill is the scar. But recent behavior is genuinely good: **tuck-in only** (Permiso, ~$200M) with explicit refusal to "buy legacy revenue," and **$372M of buybacks in H1 FY27 executed around $80–90** — buying stock at ~5x sales was opportunistic and value-accretive. They are not empire builders.
- **Dilution is the offset:** SBC at 18.6% of revenue means buybacks partly fund employee compensation rather than reduce share count (diluted shares ~179M are roughly flat YoY). A buyback that only offsets SBC is not a return of capital.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (do not initiate; existing holders should reduce)

This is a **good business** — narrow moat, high switching costs, 77% gross margins, net cash, and a credible (if early) AI-agent identity opportunity — that is **not a value investment at $202**. Three independent tests fail:

1. **Returns test:** ROIC 2.0% is far below WACC after four years. A moated business earns excess returns consistently. Okta doesn't yet.
2. **Owner-earnings test:** ~$320M of true owner earnings on a $35B market cap is a **0.9% yield** — you are paid nothing to wait while SBC dilutes you.
3. **Margin-of-safety test:** fair value ~$135 vs. price $202 = **~50% premium**. Even the generous reported-FCF DCF and a bull exit-multiple model do not reach the current price without a sub-8% discount rate I won't underwrite.

The price now embeds (a) sustained AI-agent acceleration well beyond management's own 10.5% guide, (b) SBC treated as costless, and (c) index-inclusion momentum. The stock fell 50% between May 2025 and April 2026 on far smaller disappointments — that volatility is the price of a narrative-driven multiple. **I would be a serious buyer at $95–110**, where the SBC-adjusted owner earnings yield exceeds 3% and the AI-agent optionality is free.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis (i.e., what would make me wrong)

- **AI-agent identity inflection is real and early.** New products were 30% of Q2 bookings; cRPO accelerated 12%→14% and RPO grew 17% (ahead of the 11% revenue print). If agent identities become a genuine new S-curve, 20%+ growth returns and my "10.5%" anchor is too low. **This is the single strongest bull argument and the one I'd monitor: cRPO growth ≥17–18% with revenue guidance raised to mid-teens would force a re-underwrite.**
- **SBC could normalize faster than modeled** — if SBC falls toward 10% of revenue while FCF margin holds at 28%, owner earnings roughly double by FY28 and the SBC-adjusted case converges on the reported-FCF case (~$160+).
- **Microsoft/Entra price bundling** — the bear case that keeps the multiple capped.
- **Seat-based pricing at risk from AI** — if agents displace human seats, Okta's per-user model deflates just as the AI narrative inflates it.
- **Takeout optionality** — at $35B, low; but a platform (CrowdStrike, Palo Alto) could pay up, which would cap downside in a way fundamentals do not.
- **Data gaps:** I could not independently verify insider ownership percentages or the cause of the April 2026 drawdown (price data only). I did not obtain a full FY27 FCF guidance figure; I estimated ~$920M from the disclosed 27–28% margin. Peer multiples come from broad Technology sector aggregates (skewed by mega-caps), so the 28.8x peer median P/E is a loose benchmark, not a clean identity-peer set.

**Bottom line:** A narrow-moat business in a real growth market, managed sensibly, priced as though the AI-agent future has already arrived. Wonderful narrative, no margin of safety — **Sell.**
