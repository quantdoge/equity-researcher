## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT on the business — but HIGH consensus on the price**

This is the rare case where agents disagree sharply on *what the company is* and agree almost unanimously on *what to pay for it*.

**Agreed (high confidence):**
- Accounting is clean and the balance sheet is fortress-grade (net cash $2.13B, D/E 0.06, 37x coverage, no refinancing cliff). No solvency, fraud, or liquidity case exists.
- Margin inflection is real: GM 69.5%→77.4%, op margin −59%→+5.1%, FCF $863M.
- Forward bookings are healthier than the P&L: cRPO +14% (accelerating), RPO +17%.

**Contested:** whether accelerating cRPO + agent-identity optionality justifies 10.6x EV/sales on ~9% growth. Growth and Alt Data say yes; Value, Macro, and Quant say no.

**Where I resolve the conflict:** the framework says risk flags outrank bullish views. Here the fraud/forensic flag is *explicitly dismissed* by the Short Analyst, so it does not bind. What does bind is the **valuation + duration triple-convergence** — three independent methodologies (DCF, macro discount-rate transmission, and factor scoring) arrive at the same negative expected return from different directions. Meanwhile *every* agent with a price anchor names a lower buy level: Value <$110, Growth <$165, Quant $170–185, Macro bear $140–150. Not one agent underwrites $202.

**The decisive verifiable fact:** consensus FY31 revenue of **$4.47B implies an ~8.9% CAGR** off FY26's $2.92B. The sell-side's own five-year model forecasts *deceleration*, yet the stock pays 10.6x EV/sales. The $237 mean price target (+17%) was set 24-Sep–15-Oct, before the Nov drawdown — those targets **chase price, they do not lead it.**
