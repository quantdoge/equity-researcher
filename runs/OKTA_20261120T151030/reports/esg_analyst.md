**Conclusion:** this is a *Social-and-Governance* ESG story, not an Environmental one. Any ESG thesis on OKTA must be a cyber-governance and trust thesis. Environmental analysis is a compliance/disclosure box-tick.

## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **LOW**

- **Structure:** Asset-light. FY2025 GHG inventory shows Scope 1+2 down **23% YoY** and roughly **74–80% below the FY2020 baseline** (FY2026 inventory states an 80.25% reduction; FY2025 webpage stated 74%+ — the difference reflects baseline/methodology updates, worth flagging as a disclosure-quality wrinkle). Total all-scope emissions fell only **~3% YoY**, because Scope 3 dominates.
- **Scope 3 = the whole story:** management states ~**99% of emissions come from value-chain vendors** (AWS/Azure cloud, purchased software, travel, commuting). Purchased goods & services fell **17% in FY2025**; business travel rose with revenue recovery.
- **Targets & assurance:** 67% absolute Scope 1+2 reduction by FY2030 vs FY2020; Scope 3 reduction target on business travel/purchased goods. Inventory prepared under GHG Protocol (Watershed methodology) and **limited assurance by Apex Companies under ISO 14064-3** — better than most mid-cap SaaS peers. CDP questionnaire submitted annually.
- **Supplier engagement is the credible lever:** 34% of suppliers by spend had validated science-based targets in FY2025 (23% FY2024, 13% FY2023), targeting **65% by FY2027**; sustainability contractual addenda introduced in FY2025.
- **AI energy:** Okta has built an internal methodology to measure energy of its most-used AI software tools — a genuinely forward-looking move given its agentic-AI/non-human-identity product push. Watch Scope 3 inflation from AI workloads in FY2027–28.
- **Transition/regulatory cost:** immaterial. No CBAM exposure, no EU ETS exposure, no environmental remediation liabilities or fines found. No green bonds or sustainability-linked debt (capital structure is convertible notes — no ESG-linked covenants).

**Verdict: Low.** Environment is a disclosure-quality and supplier-data issue, not a financial risk.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **HIGH**

This is where Okta's ESG risk concentrates.

**a) Security incidents — the repeated pattern (see §5).** Two material breaches in three years (Lapsus$/March 2022; support-system compromise Sept–Oct 2023) plus exposure to the 2025 Salesloft Drift OAuth supply-chain event. Critically, in both Okta-specific cases the **initial public characterisation understated scope** (2022: "no evidence of breach," later 366 customers confirmed; 2023: "1%" initially, later *all* customers in the support system). For a company whose product is trust, that is a social-licence problem, not a technical one.

**b) Human capital — serial restructuring.** ~300 roles cut (Feb 2023, ~5%), ~400 (Feb 2024, ~7%), ~180 (2025, ~3%). Defensible on efficiency grounds (part of the market's operating-margin thesis), but it creates attrition, institutional-knowledge and morale risk in a talent-scarce security market, and management is now dependent on equity to retain engineers — linking S-risk to the dilution/comp governance issue in §4.

**c) Diversity & inclusion.** Disclosed: women at **33.9% of the global workforce** in 2023, up from 32%, with stated gains in Latinx representation and in leadership. Okta publishes a "State of Inclusion" report — reasonable transparency for a ~6,000-person company. **Forward risk:** the US policy environment has turned hostile to corporate DEI programmes (2025 executive-order and EEOC-driven scrutiny of tech-sector practices). Okta's exposure is reputational/legal-cost tail risk rather than a cash-flow item; expect softer DEI disclosure and possible programme renaming.

**d) Product safety / consumer protection.** Identity failures propagate to customers' own downstream breaches — Okta's liability is contractual and reputational, with indemnity and insurance buffering. No consumer-protection regulatory action located.

**e) Human rights / government use.** Public-sector and defence-adjacent identity deployments create a moderate surveillance/misuse controversy surface. No findings located, but it is a live NGO campaign vector for identity vendors.

**Verdict: High.** Not because of labour practices, but because security/trust failures are the material social issue and the recurrence rate is the problem.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **6.0 / 10**

**Positives:**
- **Dual-class structure is effectively unwinding.** Class B carries 10 votes, but by FY2026 the Class B block is a *de minimis* sliver of shares outstanding (~0.5% per the FY2026 balance-sheet conversion disclosure), and the co-founders together hold **well under 10% of total votes**. The 2022-era fear of founder control is now largely a legacy disclosure rather than a live voting distortion. This is a genuine improvement versus the IPO-era structure.
- **Board refresh is happening:** two Class III directors (Anthony Bates, David Schellhase) re-elected at the June 2026 meeting through 2029; **Shellye Archambeau, a long-tenured Class III director, was not renominated** — orderly rotation, and Archambeau's audit/tech-governance experience will be a loss worth monitoring on the audit-committee side.
- **Equity plan tightened, not loosened:** the 2026 amendment to the 2017 Equity Incentive Plan **eliminated the evergreen annual share increase and removed liberal share recycling** for options/SARs. Removing an evergreen is a shareholder-friendly, dilution-limiting change that most SaaS peers resist.
- Clean audit ratification (EY, ~222m votes for), no controlling shareholder, majority-independent board (Nasdaq standard), classified board with three classes.

**Negatives:**
- **Staggered/classified board** plus a co-founder CEO with outsized board influence means accountability is structurally muted; takeover defences are entrenched.
- **Compensation/dilution friction is visible in the vote:** the equity plan amendment drew **59.9m votes against vs 144.1m for (~29% opposition)** — a loud minority signal on dilution, even as say-on-pay passed comfortably (154.7m for). Removing the plan's termination date while removing the evergreen is a mixed trade investors should watch.
- **The decisive governance failure is cyber-risk oversight.** Two material incidents with scope understatement in both is an oversight and disclosure-controls issue, and the 2022 episode was litigated to a $60m settlement. Governance of the *core* product risk is the score limiter.
- **CEO/chair duality unverified** — proxy text was inaccessible (SEC 403). I will not assert duality without evidence; treat as an open item to confirm from the DEF 14A.

**Score: 6/10** — structurally sound and improving (voting rights consolidating, plan hygiene better, board rotating), but penalised for the cyber-oversight and disclosure record and for ~29% equity-plan dissent.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (controversy overlay: negative)

**Rationale:** Okta is not an ESG laggard on *structure* — carbon disclosure is independently assured with a real Scope 3 supplier programme, voting rights are consolidating, the equity plan was tightened, and board rotation is orderly. It is a genuine **leader on environmental disclosure** relative to SaaS peers and clearly improving on governance mechanics. But it is **persistently exposed on the one issue that matters most in its industry — privacy and data security** — with a repeated pattern of incidents and initial scope understatement, and that issue has already produced litigation and a $60m settlement. Materiality-weighted, that caps the rating at Average rather than Leader.

---

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL**

**Why not negative:** none of the structural ESG risks threaten the cash-flow engine. There is no carbon cost pass-through, no CBAM, no stranded assets, no ESG-linked debt covenant, no systemic labour or community conflict, and no controlling shareholder to override minority interests. The $60m settlement is ~0.2% of market cap — financially trivial. Absolute ESG-related capex/opex (CSRD readiness, NIS2/DORA attestations, assurance) is rounding-error against a ~$2.6–2.9bn revenue base.

**Why not positive:** the ESG factor that *is* material — trust — is the one where the record is weakest, and it operates through the discount rate and the multiple rather than the P&L. A fourth material incident would compress the multiple harder than any fine. Investors should also treat the ~29% equity-plan dissent as an early-warning signal on dilution discipline, which compounds with the already heavy SBC load needed to retain security engineers post-layoffs.

**Practical bottom line for the thesis:** ESG neither creates nor destroys the OKTA investment case. **Do not underwrite a premium for ESG leadership; do not haircut cash flows for ESG costs.** The correct treatment is a **cyber-governance risk overlay**: monitor (i) 8-K Item 1.05 disclosure speed and candour, (ii) net revenue retention and any disclosed breach-linked churn, (iii) board cyber-committee composition post-Archambeau departure, and (iv) equity-plan dilution and vote dissent at the 2027 meeting. Any one of those deteriorating is a thesis-level warning sign that arrives through trust, not through ESG scoring.

**Cheapest actionable ESG signal here:** Okta's Scope 3 supplier-target programme (34% → 65% by FY2027) and its AI-energy measurement work are the two places where ESG is being run as a genuine operating discipline rather than a report — worth tracking as a proxy for management quality generally.
