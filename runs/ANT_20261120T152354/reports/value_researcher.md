# Anthropic PBC (ANT) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price $86.12 | Market cap ≈$1.13T (13.1B sh) | EV ≈$1.1T pro forma | IPO priced $83.00 on 11/18/26**

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Narrow (and unproven)**

The commercial franchise is real and the velocity is extraordinary. Q3 2026 revenue was $16.96B, up **1,127% YoY**, and 9M 2026 revenue reached $33.2B. Q4 guidance sits near $21B, implying FY2026 revenue of roughly **$54B** — versus $4.6B in 2025 and $41M in 2023.

What genuinely impresses:
- **Developer mindshare in agentic coding.** Claude Code appears to be the reference product for AI software engineering, a classic beachhead with high switching costs once embedded in a repo/CI workflow.
- **Enterprise trust positioning.** The safety-first brand and PBC structure have won regulated-industry logos where OpenAI's consumer baggage is a liability.
- **Operating leverage is arriving fast.** Operating margin went from **-176% (FY25) to roughly -4% (9M FY26)**, with Q2 2026 actually printing +$974M of operating income.

But I cannot call this a wide moat, for four reasons:
1. **The moat is partly rented.** Amazon, Google, and Microsoft/Nvidia are simultaneously equity holders, compute suppliers, distribution channels, *and* competitors with their own frontier models. A supplier that is also your landlord and your rival is not a moat — it's a dependency.
2. **Gross margin moved the wrong way at scale.** Q2 2026 gross margin 51.9% → Q3 2026 **41.8%**. For a true software moat, gross margin should *expand* with volume. Here compute costs grew faster than revenue. That is the signature of a business buying market share with subsidized inference.
3. **Commoditization risk is live.** Open-weight models put a hard ceiling on per-token pricing over any 10-year horizon. I have no basis to forecast 2035 token economics.
4. **Only ~980M of ~13.1B shares are public (~7.5% float).** The quoted price is set by a thin float in a hyped debut. It is not evidence of what the whole enterprise would clear.

**Verdict: Narrow, transitional moat. Not demonstrably durable — and durability is the only thing that matters to me.**

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **3 / 10**

| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26E (9M actual + Q4 guide) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $41M | $386M | $4,585M | ~$54,200M |
| Gross margin | -956% | -553% | **-59.8%** | ~43% |
| Operating margin | -1,344% | -772% | -176% | ~-4% |
| FCF | -$1.63B | -$5.71B | **-$2.77B** | n/d |
| Cash + ST inv. | — | $3.1B | $20.3B | **$131.8B** (+~$81B IPO) |
| Total debt | — | $14.2B | $41.8B | **$133.3B** |
| Equity | — | -$6.9B | -$20.7B | +$7.6B |

**The score is low on balance sheet and cash generation, not on the P&L trajectory.**

Two things must be separated. Reported net loss was -$41.97B in 2025 and -$80.7B YTD 2026 — but operating loss was only -$8.06B and -$1.26B respectively. The ~$34B/yr gap is **non-cash fair-value remeasurement on the hyperscaler convertibles**. Owner earnings of -$42B (as the screening tool reports) is therefore meaningless; the honest cash number is **FCF of -$2.77B in 2025**. I credit management for that.

Against it:
- **$895.4B of contracted compute purchase commitments** disclosed in the amended S-1. That is 16.5x FY2026 revenue, and it is the single most important number in this analysis. Spread over six years it implies ~$150B/yr of compute spend against ~$54B of revenue today. This is a fixed obligation that behaves like debt but sits off the balance sheet.
- Negative retained earnings of -$156.2B.
- SBC $1.16B in 2025 and climbing steeply (9M 2026 SG&A $5.95B). Reported ROE of +202% and ROIC of -38% are both artifacts of negative/volatile equity — discard them.

Post-IPO liquidity is genuinely fortress-like (~$213B pro forma cash vs $133B debt). **Solvency is not the risk. Dilution and commitment risk are.**

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **No margin of safety; price embeds the bull case**

EV/Sales is **~20x on FY2026E** ($1.1T / $54B) and ~13x on the Q4 annualized run-rate ($84B). Peer median P/E is 28.4x — but peers are profitable; ANT has negative earnings, so the multiple comparison is not informative. No analyst price targets are published yet (none returned).

The tool-driven DCF is unusable (negative FCF base). So I ran it **in reverse**, which is the correct discipline here:

- To justify $1.1T at a 10% hurdle and 3% terminal growth, ANT needs roughly **$140B+ of owner earnings by the mid-2030s** — implying ~$300B of revenue at a 40% operating margin, with ~$900B of capital deployed to get there.
- My *generous* bull case — $300B revenue by 2035 at 40% margins, 25x exit — yields ~$950B of present value, or **~$73/share**. That is *below* today's $86.
- A reasonable base case (2030 revenue $150B, 35% margin, heavy reinvestment, $20B owner earnings, 25x, discounted 5 years) yields **~$310B, or ~$24/share.**

**Intrinsic value range: $25–$75/share. Current price $86.12 is at or above the top of that range.** At $86 you are not buying a business; you are buying the right to pay for flawless execution of the most capital-intensive growth plan in corporate history. That is the opposite of a margin of safety.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good operators, questionable shareholder alignment**

**Positives:** Dario Amodei is a founder-CEO with deep technical conviction and a coherent strategy; capital allocation into compute has been aggressive but *strategically* coherent (locking supply ahead of demand). Revenue execution has been exceptional. The company raised capital at escalating valuations rather than burning into a wall.

**Concerns:**
- **Public Benefit Corporation charter.** Directors are explicitly empowered to weigh mission alongside shareholder value. For a Buffett-style owner, that is a structural dilution of my claim on the cash flows.
- **Dual-class control.** The IPO floated only Class A; founders and insiders retain voting control. My influence is nil.
- **No capital return, ever.** No dividend, no meaningful buyback ($149M in 2025, immaterial). All FCF — and then some — goes back into compute.
- **$895B of purchase commitments** is the largest single capital allocation decision in the company's history, made pre-IPO, and it converts flexibility into obligation. If the demand curve bends, management has no exit.

I trust the operators. I do not trust that my interests are their primary objective, and I cannot underwrite the commitment book.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL / DO NOT OWN**

I want to be precise about what this verdict means, because the business and the security are different things.

**Anthropic is a remarkable company.** Revenue compounding at triple-digit rates, near-breakeven operating margins at a $54B run-rate, $131.8B of cash, and what appears to be the leading enterprise coding franchise. If it executes, it may well be one of the defining companies of the decade.

**ANT the stock is not a value investment at $86.** It fails my three non-negotiables:
- **Circle of competence:** I cannot forecast LLM pricing power or the competitive structure of frontier AI in 2035. This is a "too hard" pile.
- **Margin of safety:** The stock trades at or above my *bull* case. There is zero discount to intrinsic value — there is a premium.
- **Durable moat with high ROIC:** Gross margins are compressing at scale, and returns on capital are deeply negative. The hallmark of a moated business is absent.

Add the structural issues: a 7.5% float setting the price, a lock-up expiry that will release ~12B shares into the market, and $895B of fixed commitments against a growth rate decelerating from 144% to 47% to 24% sequentially.

**I would not own this at $86. My buy price — where a 30% margin of safety to my *bull* case exists — is roughly $50, and I would want the float to normalize and two more quarters of gross-margin expansion before I would even reconsider.** Given that gross margin *fell* last quarter, that evidence is not yet in.

If you already own it: the position is a momentum trade, not a value holding. Size it as such, and do not confuse a great company with a great investment.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

*Risks that could make me wrong (i.e., the stock is cheap):*
1. **Margin inflection.** If gross margin re-expands to 60%+ and operating margin reaches 25% at $150B revenue, my base case roughly doubles. Watch Q4 gross margin closely — this is the single most important datapoint.
2. **Commitments are optionality, not obligation.** If the $895B is cancellable or back-loaded with take-or-pay thresholds, the leverage concern shrinks materially. I could not verify the schedule; the S-1 text was not retrievable within budget.
3. **Agentic pricing power.** If Claude Code becomes the enterprise standard for software creation, switching costs could prove far stickier than I assume.

*Risks that reinforce the verdict (asymmetric downside):*
4. **Fixed-cost trap.** $895B committed against decelerating growth is the classic way high-growth businesses die — not from demand loss, but from obligations signed at peak optimism.
5. **Supplier-competitor squeeze.** Amazon, Google, and Microsoft can raise compute pricing, favor their own models, or both. ANT has no alternative supply at this scale.
6. **Open-weight deflation.** A capable free model caps inference pricing permanently and compresses the entire margin structure.
7. **Float and lock-up overhang.** With ~7.5% public float, the current quote is a poor estimate of clearing value; the price fell 11% in its second session. Expect sustained supply pressure.
8. **Dilution.** If the convertible instruments settle in equity, the ~13.1B share count grows materially, further eroding per-share value.

---

**Bottom line:** A superb business at a price that assumes everything goes right, run by people whose charter does not put me first, carrying $895B of obligations I cannot fully see. That is a pass — and a pass is a decision, not an omission. **Sell / Avoid.**
