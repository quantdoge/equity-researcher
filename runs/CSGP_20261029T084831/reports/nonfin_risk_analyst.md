# NON-FINANCIAL RISK ASSESSMENT — CoStar Group, Inc. (NASDAQ: CSGP)

**Price at assessment: $27.54 | Market cap ~$11.2B | 52-wk high $78.95 | Down ~67% in 13 months (~-59% YTD 2026)**

Context that frames every risk below: CSGP fell from $83.48 (Oct 2025) to $27.54, with -10% (Oct 29 '25), -8% (Jan 7 '26), -15% (Feb 3 '26) and -14% (Oct 28 '26) single-day dislocations. A company losing two-thirds of its equity value on disclosure events is, by definition, exhibiting a governance and credibility problem, not merely an operating one.

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: ADEQUATE (deteriorating; weak on capital allocation)

**Positives (real, not cosmetic):**
- Independent Chair (Louise S. Sams) separate from the CEO; 8 of 9 directors independent (Nana Banerjee added as ninth). No CEO/Chairman duality — a genuine strength.
- 2025 board refreshment added John Berisford, Rachel Glaser and Christine McCarthy (ex-Disney CFO) — substantive financial/governance expertise.
- All eight director nominees and say-on-pay were "overwhelmingly" supported at the June 2026 annual meeting despite an active activist campaign. Shareholders have not withdrawn legitimacy from this board.
- Support agreements were reached with D.E. Shaw and Third Point, with board-related governance changes — the board negotiated rather than merely stonewalled.

**Why not "Strong":**
- **Capital allocation oversight is the core failure.** ~$3bn cumulative Homes.com spend against an $11bn market cap, generating no demonstrable return; the investment was cut 35% only under activist duress (Third Point, then D.E. Shaw, Feb 2026), not through independent board challenge. This is a founder-driven bet the board ratified and defended, then reversed.
- **Entrenchment allegation.** A shareholder suit in Delaware Chancery (Feb 2026) alleged the board approved a compensation/retention plan specifically designed to frustrate the proxy fight. Resolved/dismissed with prejudice as to the plaintiff by stipulation (Feb 20, 2026) — unproven, but the *fact pattern* (pay plan adopted mid-proxy-fight) is a governance marker that merits scrutiny, not dismissal.
- **Disclosure credibility.** Management publicly corrected D.E. Shaw's claims as "highly misleading" (notably that Homes.com was never a separate segment) — technically defensible, but it means investors cannot independently underwrite the company's largest capital commitment. Segment opacity on the single most contested item is a governance deficiency.
- **Combative founder-CEO posture** (public attacks on Realtor.com/Zillow, litigiousness) has repeatedly converted commercial competition into reputational and legal exposure.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Go-to-market breakdown / sales-force attrition — the binding constraint.** Q3 2026 (Oct 27) delivered record revenue ($952M, +14%) and record adjusted EBITDA ($229M, 2x YoY), yet **net new bookings were only $37M and FY revenue guidance was cut $35M on sales-force attrition.** Bookings are the leading indicator for a subscription business; revenue is the lagging one. Cost cuts are manufacturing the earnings beat while the growth engine stalls — the worst possible composition. This is now the single most important operating metric in the company.

2. **Key-person concentration in a founder-CEO.** Andrew Florance has run the company since 1987 (~39 years), is the public face of every strategic decision including Homes.com, and is personally the target of activist campaigns. No credible succession disclosure. His departure or de-scoping would be a genuine re-rating event in either direction, and the strategic vacuum would be severe.

3. **M&A integration and AI-driven restructuring execution.** Concurrent, self-inflicted transformation load: Zonda acquired; Vexcel (~$730M) just signed; Homes.com net investment cut ~35% (~$300M); repeated layoff rounds (Feb 19, 2026; 100+ in Richmond; Domain/Australia) explicitly tied to AI adoption and offshoring. Cutting headcount in the sales organization while leaning on AI to sustain bookings is an unproven simultaneous bet, and it is precisely what the bookings number says is failing.

4. **Data supply, cyber and business continuity (secondary but underinsured).** CoStar's asset is proprietary data and the licensing/web-scraping ecosystem that feeds it; losing content or channel access is an existential operational risk. The **PHP2Shell** disclosure (CVE-2026-109618/109619, fixes Oct 21, 2026) shows active vulnerability management and no evidence of exploitation — creditable response. However, **cyber insurance is only a $10M limit** (CNA, renewed Jul 2026) against a ~$3.7B revenue base: risk transfer is thin, and the trust center confirms SOC 1/2, BC/DR and 99.9% availability commitments are in place.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: HIGH

- **Credibility with the buy side is impaired.** Two of the most sophisticated activist investors in the market (Third Point, D.E. Shaw) publicly argued the CEO's flagship bet destroyed value; D.E. Shaw sought majority board change. Even after winning the vote, the stock kept falling — the market has not accepted management's narrative.
- **Sell-side capitulation.** Buy ratings fell from 12 to 8; consensus remains "Hold" with a mean target near $40 that has been repeatedly cut. A wide target dispersion around a $27 stock signals narrative, not model, uncertainty.
- **Litigation-driven brand friction.** Being a defendant in a "data monopoly" MDL *and* a privacy class action, while suing Zillow for copyright, positions CoStar as the industry's aggressor — unhelpful when its customers (brokers) are the antitrust plaintiffs.
- **Workforce/community backlash.** Repeated layoffs in Richmond and Australia, framed publicly as AI substitution, generate local press and Glassdoor-type damage that raises future hiring and retention costs in a talent-dependent data business.
- **CEO communication style** amplifies volatility: combative public statements convert ordinary quarterly misses into 14% drawdowns.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** No fraud indicators, no SEC/DOJ enforcement, no going-concern-type operational failure, and heavy insider buying all argue against "Critical." But the combination of (a) an antitrust MDL attacking the business model itself, (b) a demonstrated collapse in sales productivity, (c) a founder-CEO with a $3bn capital-allocation failure and no succession plan, and (d) a board that ratified that failure until forced to reverse, produces a risk profile materially worse than a typical real-estate-information incumbent. Governance is structurally sound but has been functionally passive at exactly the moment it mattered. Upgrade triggers would be: MDL dismissed or settled on conduct-only terms; two consecutive quarters of ≥$60M net new bookings; a credible CEO succession/transition announcement. Downgrade triggers: class certification in MDL 3192 or allegations of bookings-metric manipulation.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Net new bookings of $37M in a quarter with $952M of revenue** — the quarterly bookings-to-revenue ratio implies severe growth decay. Demand a bookings definition and cohort-disclosure review; this KPI has whipsawed and is not GAAP-audited. **Escalate to accounting/quality-of-earnings, not just operations.**
2. **Attrition-driven bookings shortfall** — management attributed the miss to sales-force attrition. Quantify regretted attrition among quota-carrying reps; unquantified, this is the difference between a cyclical miss and a broken model.
3. **MDL No. 3192 class certification** — the single largest binary legal event; monitor docket. A hub-and-spoke lease-data theory implicates CoStar's customer relationships, not just its pricing.
4. **Delaware Chancery suit alleging a pay plan adopted to frustrate the proxy contest** — dismissed by stipulation, but the underlying facts bear on board independence and warrant review of the 2026 proxy compensation disclosures. *I could not retrieve the DEF 14A or 10-K text (see gaps below); this should be re-verified against the filed proxy.*
5. **Segment opacity on Homes.com** — no separate segment reporting on the company's largest and most contested capital commitment. Until disclosed, no investor can verify the $3bn claim or its return. Request the internal management reporting package.
6. **Cyber insurance of $10M** against a $11bn market cap and $3.7bn revenue base — risk transfer is inadequate relative to the data-centric asset base; also note the Oct 2026 PHP2Shell exposure (remediated, no exploitation found).
7. **Sales near the peak by senior insiders** (Saint, ~$4.6M at $90-93; Hill; Cann) followed by CEO buying at $29-45 — verify 10b5-1 plan adoption dates and whether any plans were modified during blackout windows.
