## 1. Credit Risk Assessment — **Investment Grade (low leverage, but weakening quality)**

CoStar is **structurally under-levered**: gross debt $1,026M against equity of $7,992M (D/E 0.13) and total assets $10,265M (debt/assets 0.10). TTM EBITDA of $576M gives gross leverage of **1.8x** and **net leverage of 0.9x** — well inside investment grade. Interest expense has collapsed to ~$11M TTM, giving **~21x EBIT coverage**.

The credit quality concern is not solvency — it is **deterioration in the composition of the balance sheet**. Net debt has swung from **–$589M (net cash) at FY25 to +$526M (net debt) at Q3'26**, driven by a cumulative cash drawdown from $5.22B (FY23) → $4.68B (FY24) → $1.73B (FY25) → $0.50B (Q3'26). Buybacks of **$575M in FY25 and $692M through 9M'26** were funded largely from the balance sheet, not from free cash flow. Goodwill + intangibles have risen to **$7,490M, or 73% of total assets and 94% of book equity**, leaving tangible equity of only ~$502M — a thin cushion against impairment.

**Rating: Investment Grade (BBB-equivalent profile), outlook stable on leverage, negative on capital allocation discipline.**

The genuine risk is that buybacks stop, the growth narrative breaks, and the $7.5B goodwill book requires impairment — which would wipe out ~94% of book equity in one charge.

**Probability assessment:** a –20% revenue decline is a *severe but not remote* stress. ~95% of revenue is subscription with ~90% renewal rates on 12-month-plus contracts, making a 20% hit require mass churn. ## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

**Justification:** The balance sheet is genuinely strong on the metrics that matter for survival — 1.8x gross leverage, sub-1x net leverage, 21x interest coverage, a safe-zone Z-score, and no refinancing cliff. The company cannot be forced into distress by its creditors under any realistic operating scenario.

The rating is held at Moderate rather than Low by three factors: (1) **free cash flow is not funding the business** — $65M TTM FCF against $552M of capex and over $1.2B of buybacks; (2) **liquidity has compressed violently** (current ratio 2.84 → 1.32, working capital $1,373M → $230M, cash $1.73B → $500M in three quarters); and (3) **73% of assets are goodwill**, meaning reported equity is a soft number that a strategic failure could erase.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Cash depletion at an alarming rate.** Total cash fell from $5.22B (FY23) to **$500M (Q3'26)** — a $4.7B drawdown in under three years — while net debt flipped from –$589M to +$526M. The balance-sheet buffer that justified the "safe" Z-score is largely gone. *Current ratio of 1.32 is the tightest in the company's recent history.*

2. **Buybacks funded by the balance sheet, not cash flow.** $575M (FY25) + $692M (9M'26) of repurchases against TTM FCF of $65M. Capital return is ~20x free cash generation. This is not sustainable and has directly consumed the liquidity cushion.

3. **Goodwill and intangibles = $7.49B (73% of assets, 94% of equity).** Grew $840M in Q3'26 alone (acquisition-driven, per the goodwill step-up and a $766M cash decline that is not fully explained in the reported cash flow statement — **flagged as a data gap; investing-section outflows not visible**). Tangible book equity is only ~$502M. An impairment of the residential franchise would be an equity event.

4. **Operating margin whipsaw.** Operating income went from **+$283M (FY23) to –$72M (FY25)** on 32% revenue growth, then back to positive. Gross margin has eroded ~500bp since FY22 (81.0% → 76.2%). Cost discipline is unproven; management is cutting Homes.com net investment by ~35% in 2026, which is a defensive signal.

5. **Chronically thin FCF: 0.35% yield (FY25), ~0.6% TTM.** Capex of $552M TTM exceeds D&A of $346M. The company is not yet self-funding its growth.

6. **Market risk is extreme relative to the business model:** 51% annualised vol, –59.3% max drawdown, Sharpe –1.86 — despite a reported beta of 0.796 that fails to capture narrative/idiosyncratic risk.

7. **Data anomalies to note:** the tool-computed beta (0.004) is unusable; FY25 interest coverage is reported as –3.79x (operating-income basis) vs. +1.6x on an EBIT basis — I used both; Q3'26 retained earnings is reported as $0 (inconsistent with prior quarters' $2.4B) and appears to be a feed error; and consolidated peer multiples/CDS spreads were not retrieved, so relative credit positioning is unverified.
