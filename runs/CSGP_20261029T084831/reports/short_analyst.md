# CSGP — Forensic Short-Seller Analysis

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **MEDIUM**

**Not a fraud. A capital-allocation and moat-erosion story.** I want to be precise about that distinction, because it changes the trade entirely.

The mechanical quality scores look alarming and are almost all artifacts of a collapsing denominator:

| Metric | FY2025 | Read-through |
|---|---|---|
| Cash conversion (OCF/NI) | **61.4x** | Artifact — NI is $7M |
| FCF conversion (FCF/NI) | **5.9x** | Artifact — FCF $41M |
| Accruals ratio | **−4.01%** | Genuinely clean (negative) |
| TATA (Beneish) | **−0.0401** | Negative accruals — clean |
| Composite quality score | 60/100 | Mediocre |

The cash conversion ratios are mathematically inflated by a near-zero numerator denominator and must **not** be read as quality. The honest metric is absolute: **FY2025 free cash flow of $41M on an $11.2B market cap = 272x.** Adjusting for $194M of stock-based comp (a real expense), economic FCF was **negative**.

Net income collapsed $374.7M (2023) → $138.7M (2024) → **$7.0M (2025)** while revenue grew 18.7%. Operating income went $282.3M → $4.7M → **−$72.0M**. That is the entire story in one line.

## 2. Key Red Flags Found

**A. The acquisitions masked organic deterioration.** Goodwill + intangibles exploded **$2.96B → $6.72B (+$3.75B)** in FY2025 while total assets rose only $1.28B. Cash fell **$4.68B → $1.73B (−$2.95B)**. This is Matterport plus the Domain Holdings (Australia) deal. Goodwill/intangibles now equal **64% of total assets and 81% of book equity**. Tangible book value is just $1.62B — the stock trades at **6.9x tangible book**, not the 1.34x P/B the screen shows.

**B. Gross margin compression — real but I'd characterize it as moderate, not catastrophic.** FY2023 79.98% → 2024 79.59% → **2025 75.24%**. Cost of revenue rose **+44%** on **+18.7%** revenue. However, quarterly GMs are noisy (Q1'25 68.7%, Q3'26 77.8%) and 2026 is running ~77-78%. So: **~250-400bp of genuine erosion**, consistent with pricing pressure in marketplaces, not a collapse. GMI of 1.058 confirms the direction.

**C. Capex regime change — permanent, and it is the real margin killer.** Capex went $25M (2023) → $638M (2024) → $389M (2025). FCF: **$464M → −$245M → +$41M**. D&A/revenue doubled from 4.4% to 8.1%. The business is now structurally more capital-intensive; the pre-2024 ~$460M FCF print is gone.

**D. Receivables outrunning revenue — flag present, but I'm discounting it.** AR +37.8% vs revenue +18.7% (DSRI 1.162). But DSO is **29.1 days (2025) vs 29.1 days (2023)** — 2024's 25.1 days was the anomaly. This is a base effect, **not** channel stuffing. For a subscription business with auto-pay, this is benign. I'll flag it because the model does, but I don't believe it.

**E. Revenue deceleration is the single most damaging trend.** YoY: **26.9% → 22.5% → 18.4% → 14.2%** (Q4'25 → Q3'26). Worse, *sequential* revenue is 900 → 897 → 925 → 952 — **+1.9% over three quarters, ~2.6% annualized**. Q1'26 revenue was *below* Q4'25. If sequential stalling persists, the 14% headline compresses toward mid-single digits by 2027, and the entire earnings-recovery thesis (which is a cost-cut thesis, not a growth thesis) breaks.

**F. SBC nearly doubled:** $89M → **$194M** (+118%), equal to **27.7x net income**. Any "adjusted EPS" that excludes this is not measuring owner earnings.

I found no evidence of fabricated revenue.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment: **SEVERE, and structural**

- **AI is commoditising the core asset.** CoStar's moat was proprietary CRE data aggregated at scale. LLM-driven data extraction attacks precisely that. The market has already begun pricing this — the consensus narrative shift is "AI-driven market shifts," not just Homes.com.
- **Zero competitive slack to absorb it.** Operating margin went 12.8% (2023) → 0.2% → **−2.2%**. There is no buffer to fund a defence.
- **Homes.com has consumed >$3B since 2021** against Zillow's entrenched network effects — a two-sided marketplace where the incumbent's scale advantage compounds. There is no evidenced path to returns on that capital.
- **Sequential revenue stalling** is the empirical confirmation that the moat is not repricing upward.

## 5. Management Red Flags

**This is the strongest part of the bear case, and it is quantifiable.**

$575M of buybacks in FY2025 **and the diluted share count still rose** — 407.8M → 420.7M (+3.2%). Through 9M 2026 they repurchased another ~$692M, including **$527M in a single quarter (Q1'26)**. Total ~**$1.27B** for a net reduction of ~18.8M shares = **average cost ~$67/share**. The stock is $27.54.

**Approximately $750M of shareholder capital — ~6.7% of the current market cap — has been vaporised on buybacks executed at 2.4x today's price.** This is not a governance footnote; it is direct evidence of management's capital allocation judgement, and it happened *concurrently* with $3.75B of goodwill-creating M&A. Retained earnings are flat at $2.35B — five years of cumulative earnings retained and equity holders are worse off.

Secondary flags: highly promotional management style historically (Homes.com "portal wars" campaign) versus delivered operating margin of −2.2%. **Note the gap between FY2025 consensus adj EPS of $0.838 and GAAP EPS of $0.0166** — a ~98% gap bridged by SBC and acquisition amortisation.

**Data gap I could not close:** the Form 4 feed returns 25 filings clustered on award-grant dates (2026-03-17, 2026-06-24, 2026-08-04) but the tool does not expose buy/sell direction, and no 10-K text was retrievable. I cannot confirm net insider selling. I will not assert it. No auditor-change signal surfaced.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary — and the Other Side

**The bear case is real:**
1. Revenue decelerating 26.9% → 14.2%, with **sequential revenue essentially flat for three quarters**
2. Goodwill/intangibles = 64% of assets, carrying **live impairment risk** from two large, unproven acquisitions
3. ~$1.27B of buybacks at ~$67 average vs $27.54 — demonstrated capital destruction
4. AI commoditisation of proprietary CRE data — unfixable by cost-cutting
5. Valuation still demanding: **EV ~$10.6B vs 272x trailing FCF, ~36x annualised 2026 FCF, ~50x+ GAAP EPS.** FY2026E adj EPS of $1.376 requires +64% growth over FY2025's $0.838 — a number management has already failed to hit on a GAAP basis.

**The bull case is stronger than the bears admit:**
1. **~$0.6B net cash.** No solvency risk. Altman Z 3.094, and even stripping the book-equity prop, there is no distress here.
2. **Earnings are inflecting, not collapsing.** 9M 2026 net income **$132M** vs **$7M** for all of FY2025. Q3'26 NI $74M, EPS $0.18. Cost discipline is working: SGA fell $575M → $504M.
3. **FCF has recovered** to ~$216M YTD 2026 (~$290M annualised) from −$245M in 2024. The capex spike is normalising ($82M in Q3'26 vs $313M in Q4'25).
4. **The crowd has already capitulated.** Buy ratings cut from 12 to 8. Stock −70% from highs, −14% on 18.3M shares on 28 Oct 2026. This is a *crowded short* into washed-out positioning.
5. Sum-of-the-parts: at $27.54 you are paying roughly 2.8x EV/Sales for a 77%-gross-margin data franchise and getting the residential bet at near-zero optionality value.

**My honest judgement: the bears are right about the past and probably right about the moat. They are wrong about the risk/reward at this price.** The fraud/misstatement short doesn't exist here — accruals are negative and quality scores, properly read, are medium. What's left is a quality-decline short that has already delivered 70% of its move, against a company with net cash and inflection-positive earnings. That is a bad short, not a good long.

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **NO EDGE (reject the short thesis)**

To be explicit: **I am not recommending a long.** I am saying the short is no longer actionable. Shorting an $11B liquid name with net cash, negative accruals, and earnings inflecting upward, at 20x forward adj EPS, after a 70% decline and a 14% capitulation day, is a poor risk/reward — the squeeze risk is asymmetric and the remaining downside requires a *second* leg of de-rating that needs new negative information.

**What would flip me to SHORT:**
- Sequential revenue turning **negative** (any quarter below $952M)
- Goodwill impairment from Domain/Matterport
- Another large buyback above $30 (would confirm management is unteachable)
- FY2027 consensus adj EPS cuts beginning — the current $1.742 is the load-bearing assumption
- Receivables DSO breaking above ~35 days (would upgrade flag D from benign to real)

**What would make me constructive:** two consecutive quarters of sequential revenue growth >3% with GM holding ≥78%, plus a halt to buybacks.

Management judgement is the red flag, not the accounting. But the market knows, and at $27.54 with net cash and an earnings inflection, **the short is late**.
