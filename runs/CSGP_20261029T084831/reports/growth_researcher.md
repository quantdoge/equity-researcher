# CoStar Group (CSGP) — Growth Investment Analysis

**Price $27.54 | Market cap ~$11.1B (401.6M diluted sh) | EV ~$11.0B | 52-wk range ~$27–$97**

---

## 1. Growth Story

CoStar is the dominant data utility for commercial real estate (~$2.3B commercial revenue, ~80%+ gross margin, near-monopoly on CRE comparables data) that spent 2024–2025 attempting a second act: displacing Zillow in residential listings via Homes.com. That second act has consumed **$1.5B+ of net investment, ~$600M of capex, and roughly 70% of the equity value** — the stock has collapsed from ~$97 (Jul 2025) to $27.54. The thesis has now inverted: this is no longer a "hyper-growth disruptor" story but a **margin-inflection / trough-multiple recovery** story where residential swung to +$49M adj. EBITDA in Q3 2026 from −$69M a year earlier, and the Homes.com spend is being cut 35% to $550M. You are paying 2.9x EV/Sales and ~9x 2026E EBITDA for a 16%-growing, 78%-gross-margin data franchise with a 100% beat rate.

## 3. TAM Assessment

Three stacked markets, total SAM ~$40B+:
- **CRE information & analytics: ~$8–10B** — CoStar is the leader (~$2.3B), penetration still low outside the US and in mid-market/analytics software.
- **US residential marketing: ~$25–30B** (agent/ad spend). Homes.com at $31M/quarter (+50% YoY) is <1% penetrated. This is the enormous, genuinely disruptive option — and the part the market has written to zero.
- **Multifamily/rental & marketplaces: ~$5B** (Apartments.com, LoopNet, Ten-X, Zonda/Vexcel adjacency).

The platform logic is real: same data-collection flywheel (thousands of researchers + proprietary imagery/parcel data now extended via Vexcel) powers CRE, rentals, and residential. **But penetration capture is the problem: Homes.com traffic is not compounding** (see §5), so the residential TAM is optionality, not a base-case earnings driver.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Feb 23, 2027 — Q4/FY2026 results & first FY2027 guide.** First full year with residential EBITDA positive; any 2027 revenue guide ≥11% with EBITDA >$1.4B re-rates the multiple.
- **Q1 2027 — Vexcel acquisition close ($730M all-cash).** Aerial imagery/AI data asset, sold through existing salesforce, guided accretive to adj. EPS in first full year; strengthens the proprietary-data moat narrative.
- **2028 adjusted EBITDA target (reaffirmed in Oct 2026).** Consensus sits at $1.83B (~40% margin). Every step toward that bridge is a re-rating event.
- **Step-down in Homes.com net investment** ($850M → $550M in 2026; further in 2027) → the FCF inflection (2025 FCF was only $41M on $389M capex; capex normalization alone is worth ~$250–300M of FCF).
- **CRE transaction-volume recovery.** Bookings are levered to commercial property sales; a bottoming in CRE values would accelerate commercial net new bookings.
- **Continued buyback** — $575M repurchased in 2025 (poorly timed, at 2–3x today's price); ~$400M/yr at $27.54 is meaningfully accretive.

## 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Google Trends, "Homes.com": bearish.** Interest peaked at 100 (week of Jun 7, 2026 — the Super Bowl-style ad campaign spike) and has decayed to **~30 by mid-Oct 2026, down ~70% from peak** and below the pre-campaign baseline of ~40. Consumer pull is not sticking; this is the single strongest refutation of the residential disruption thesis.
- **Hiring: neutral-positive.** ~624 open roles on LinkedIn — modest, consistent with the cost-discipline narrative rather than growth investment. Not a red flag, not a green shoot.
- **Earnings revisions: positive but low-confidence.** The revision tool flags "upgrade" (+23%), but this reflects out-year spacing on very thin coverage (1–2 analysts for 2028–2030). Near-term PT trend is **downward**: BTIG $55→$42, Goldman $46→$36, Baird downgraded to Neutral, Wolfe $32 — targets compressed ~30% in six months even as estimates rose.
- **Price action: capitulation.** −60% YTD ($67.24 → $27.54), with three separate >10% single-day gap-downs (Oct 29 '25, Feb 3 '26, Jul 29 '26) and a −14% drop on Oct 28 '26 on a slight revenue miss. Sentiment is washed out; 18M shares traded on the last down day.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (speculative, half-size)

Not a Strong Buy, because the *growth* half of the framework is deteriorating: revenue growth decelerates 18.7% → 16% → 11.3% → 10.6%, Homes.com traffic is fading, and the core CRE data moat faces a credible AI-disintermediation question. But at **2.9x EV/Sales and 9x EBITDA — versus a 10–15x sales historical range** — the market is pricing ~9% perpetual growth and zero credit for margin recovery that is already visible in the P&L (adj. EBITDA doubled; residential swung positive; 100% beat rate). Bull case: 2030 revenue $5.5B at ~38% EBITDA margin ($2.1B) at 12x = ~$25B EV, **~18% IRR from here**. Base case 12x 2027E EBITDA ≈ $17.5B EV ≈ **$43/share**. Bear case (AI erodes core pricing, residential never scales): 3.5x sales ≈ $13B ≈ $32, i.e. **limited downside**. Asymmetric — buy, but size for execution risk.

## 7. Key Risks

- **AI disintermediation (the existential one).** If LLM-native tools commoditize CRE comps/listing data, CoStar's 80% gross margin is a liability, not a moat. This is the primary reason the multiple collapsed and it is unresolved.
- **Homes.com fails to scale.** ~$550M/yr still being spent against Zillow's entrenched network effects; traffic trends say the ROI is not there. A third consecutive reset of the residential plan would break credibility.
- **Deceleration becoming high-single-digit.** 2029–2030 consensus implies ~8–9.5% growth; if CRE volumes stay frozen, that becomes 6–7% and the "growth" label dies entirely.
- **Earnings quality / capital allocation.** FY2025 net income $7M, FCF $41M, SBC $194M, capex $389M; three acquisitions in 12 months (Matterport, Zonda, Vexcel) into a depressed stock, funded from cash — integration risk and a thinner net-cash cushion (~$0.9B pre-Vexcel).
- **Thin analyst coverage out-years** — the 10.6% 2028 growth and 40% EBITDA margin embedded in consensus rest on 2 estimates. Treat the long-dated model with skepticism.
- **Cyclicality:** commercial real estate leasing/sales volumes and CRE-owner budgets remain the swing factor for the core franchise.
