## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-Off / Hawkish Repricing ("No-Landing, Inflation Re-Acceleration")**

The US economy is in a late-cycle, inflation-re-acceleration regime that is unambiguously hostile to a long-duration, real-estate-transaction-sensitive asset like CoStar. Three features define the moment:

1. **Disinflation has stalled and reversed.** Headline CPI is running **3.72% YoY (Sep-26)**, up from 2.65% in Dec-25; the last three months annualise at **~4.5%**. This has forced the Fed to stop cutting: after 70bp of easing in late 2025 (4.33% → 3.63%), effective fed funds has **ticked back up to 3.75% (Sep-26)** — the market has moved from pricing cuts to pricing a possible hike.
2. **The front end has repriced violently.** 2Y yields rose from **4.17% (mid-Aug) to 4.70% (27-Oct)**, +53bp in ten weeks. 10Y sits at **4.99%** after peaking at **5.31% on 5-Oct**; 30Y at **5.37%**. The 2s10s curve is positive (+34bp) — so no recession signal — but the driver is term premium and fiscal supply, not growth optimism.
3. **Housing is in a rate-induced deep freeze.** The 30Y mortgage rate has risen **+102bp since April** to **7.32%**, the highest of this cycle. Home sales are **-6% YoY** with inventory +1.9%; CRE development is decelerating (retail space under construction 58.3M sf, down from 63.3M in Q1).

Growth itself is fine (real GDP +2.2% YoY, unemployment 4.2%) — which is precisely the problem: **strong nominal growth + sticky 3.7% inflation + a 5% long bond = no policy relief for rate-sensitive real estate intermediaries.**

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

CoStar is a **two-engined, transaction-cyclical business**: (a) a mature, subscription-like Commercial segment tied to CRE leasing/sales activity and property-professional headcount, and (b) a capital-intensive, long-duration Residential bet (Homes.com) tied to home-sale volumes and realtor advertising budgets. Both engines are levered to the same variable — **the level and direction of long rates**.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

This is not a company-specific verdict alone; the macro backdrop is doing specific, identifiable damage:

- The **single most important macro input for CSGP — the 30Y mortgage rate — is at a cycle high (7.32%) and rising**, directly throttling the residential business that represents the bulk of the equity's option value.
- The **10Y near 5%** suppresses CRE transaction volumes, visible in **$37M of quarterly net new bookings**.
- **Policy is turning hawkish again (fed funds 3.63% → 3.75%)**, removing the "rate cuts will rescue the story in 2027" catalyst that supported the multiple.
- The one genuine macro offset — **2% real GDP and 4.2% unemployment**, which keeps the commercial subscription base intact and margins rising (Q3 adj. EBITDA doubled YoY to $229M) — is not enough to offset the duration and transaction-volume damage.

**Translation:** CoStar is a high-quality franchise trading at a de-rated multiple (2.9x EV/Sales, -71% from highs), but it is cheap *for macro reasons that have not yet reversed*. A "cheap because rates are high" stock stays cheap until the long end breaks lower.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario That Would Matter Most (Bear)

**"The 5% long bond becomes 5.75%."** The most underpriced macro tail is not a recession — it is a **fiscal-term-premium shock** in which 30Y yields push through 5.75–6.0% as the market absorbs heavy Treasury supply into sticky 3.7%+ inflation while the Fed is forced into one or two more hikes.

Chain of consequences for CSGP:
- 30Y mortgage → **8.0%+**; existing-home sales fall a further 10–15%; realtor count contracts.
- Homes.com listings/advertising revenue *declines* rather than grows, forcing management to either fund losses longer (cash burn at high rates) or write down the asset. Given Homes.com is the core of the bull case, this is a **$8–12/share** risk to the equity, taking the stock into the high-teens.
- CRE cap rates expand another 75–100bp → transaction volumes stay frozen → commercial net new bookings stay near-zero → consensus FY27 revenue growth (currently ~mid-teens) cuts to high-single-digit.
- **Net: a further 30–40% downside is plausible in this scenario, and the stock's 0.80 beta badly understates the true macro sensitivity.**

**The bull mirror (lower probability, high payoff):** a genuine disinflation surprise in Q4-26/Q1-27 — CPI back below 3% — takes the 10Y to 4.2% and mortgages below 6.2%. Housing turnover snaps back 15%+, Homes.com monetisation inflects on operating leverage already built in (adj. EBITDA doubling YoY proves the cost structure is now levered), and the stock re-rates from 2.9x to 5x+ EV/Sales — **$55–65, roughly a double.** The asymmetry is real, but it is entirely a bet on the long end of the curve, not on CoStar's execution.
