### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Legal Risks

**Risk 1 — U.S. antitrust monopolization cluster (probability: HIGH | impact: HIGH)**
This is the single most important legal exposure and it has materially worsened in 2026.
- **CoStar v. CREXi (Ninth Circuit, No. 23-55662):** the Ninth Circuit **reversed dismissal of CREXi's antitrust counterclaims** (June 23, 2025), finding CREXi plausibly alleged monopolistic exclusive dealing. CoStar petitioned the Supreme Court (No. 25-667); **certiorari was denied March 23, 2026** — *"CoStar must face rival's antitrust claims after US Supreme Court order"* ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/costar-must-face-rivals-antitrust-claims-after-us-supreme-court-order-2026-03-23/); [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/costar-group-inc-v-commercial-real-estate-exchange-inc/)). CoStar now defends on the merits in district court.
- **Private class actions filed 2026:** *Shapiro Hospitalities LLC d/b/a Grand & Co. v. CoStar Group* (E.D. Va., **1:26-cv-01027**, filed **April 15, 2026**) alleges CoStar holds **~80% of U.S. internet CRE listing/data markets** and used non-negotiable data-sharing restrictions, broker non-competes, watermarks, IP blocks, and scraping to foreclose rivals — Sherman Act §§1 & 2 ([DiCello Levitt](https://dicellolevitt.com/dicello-levitt-files-first-antitrust-class-action-against-costar-group-over-commercial-real-estate-data-monopoly/)). Related: *One Real Estate LLC v. CoStar* (D.D.C.; CoStar moving to transfer to E.D. Va.), and a **"hub-and-spoke" lease-data class action** on behalf of industrial/office/retail leaseholders across **49 U.S. metros** ([Courthouse News](https://courthousenews.com/commercial-brokers-landlords-sue-costar-over-rental-data-monopoly/)).
- **Why it matters:** the alleged conduct *is* the moat — customer-data exclusivity and broker lock-in. Injunctive relief (forced data portability, ban on exclusivity) would be more damaging than damages. Damages exposure on a nationwide broker class is hard to bound.

**Risk 2 — Data privacy / AI & consumer-data regulation (probability: MEDIUM | impact: MEDIUM)**
Homes.com is a mass-market consumer portal processing home-search, contact, and behavioral data; Apartments.com processes renter data; Matterport/Vexcel add **imagery of private property**. Exposure spans GDPR (UK/EU), CCPA/CPRA, and emerging state AI/automated-valuation rules. CoStar maintains a dedicated GDPR/CCPA rights portal ([privacy.costar.com](https://privacy.costar.com/)) and its filings warn that "any failure or alleged failure to comply with privacy, data protection or consumer protection laws could lead to government enforcement actions and litigation." Note the **2026 trend of courts expanding CCPA's private right of action** ([Troutman](https://www.troutmanprivacy.com/2026/06/courts-expand-ccpas-private-right-of-action/)) — this raises class-action probability for adtech-style data use.

**Risk 3 — Cross-border merger clearance & geospatial-data controls on Vexcel (probability: LOW–MEDIUM | impact: LOW)**
Vexcel (~$730M, close expected Q1 2027) is explicitly conditioned on **regulatory approvals**. Vexcel's aerial-imagery business has European operations and **government/defense-adjacent customers**, which invites (a) EU/UK and possibly Austrian FDI-screening review, and (b) questions about **foreign ownership of geospatial/surveillance-grade imagery** and potential export-control classification of high-resolution capture tech. Timing risk > substantive blocking risk; a Q1 2027 slip is plausible.

**Secondary (now largely resolved):** a putative **stockholder class action filed Feb 9, 2026** was closed by court order and characterized by the company as "resolves Delaware litigation" (CoStar press room, 2026) — though the page returned **403** to automated fetch, so treat as company-sourced, unverified by primary document.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

- 🟡 **Antitrust conduct risk (elevated).** Contract terms restricting customers from sharing their own data with competitors are the core allegation across *four separate* 2026 matters. Even if defensible, these clauses are now a documented litigation magnet.
- 🟡 **Data-portability/contract-design risk.** Any remedy forcing customer-data portability would erode switching costs — a structural, not one-off, risk.
- 🟢 **FCPA / sanctions / AML: no material exposure identified.** No Russia, Iran, China, or high-corruption-jurisdiction revenue concentration; customers are overwhelmingly real-estate professionals paying by subscription. Subscription revenue **>95%** of total with **~90% renewal rates** limits counterparty risk.
- 🟡 **Geospatial/AI data governance (emerging).** Vexcel + Matterport imagery of private property raises consent, surveillance, and potential export-control questions not yet addressed in public disclosure.
- 🟢 **Currency/repatriation: immaterial.** ~85–90% USD revenue; translation exposure only.

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating

> ## **MEDIUM** overall — but with a split profile:
> - **Geopolitical / country risk: LOW.** Pure DM exposure (U.S., Canada, UK, W. Europe, Australia). No sanctions, expropriation, currency-control, or US–China entanglement. Geopolitics is *not* a thesis variable for CSGP.
> - **Legal / regulatory risk: HIGH.** An active, consolidated **antitrust attack on the core data moat** — SCOTUS denial (3/23/26) plus at least three private class actions filed in 2026 — is the dominant, and underappreciated, risk to the franchise.

**Bottom line for the investment memo:** CSGP's risk is **endogenous, not exogenous**. Investors screening on geopolitical exposure will find a clean book. The real question is whether CoStar's ~80% share and customer-data restrictions survive scrutiny by the Eastern District of Virginia and the district of Nevada — and whether a forced data-portability remedy converts a high-switching-cost subscription monopoly into a contested market. The stock's YTD decline alongside record Q3 revenue ($952M, +14%) and doubled Adjusted EBITDA ($229M) is consistent with the market pricing this legal overhang rather than any country risk.
