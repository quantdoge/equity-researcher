# Sector Brief: CoStar Group (CSGP) — Real Estate Information, Analytics & Marketplaces

**A note on sector definition.** FMP classifies CSGP in "Real Estate – Services," which in practice means the 500-largest-cap REIT universe. CSGP is *not* a REIT — it is a data/software and marketplace business that monetises real estate. I therefore benchmark it against its genuine comparables (CRE services and residential portal/marketplace operators), and treat the REIT aggregate as context only. That distinction matters: the top-20 "Real Estate" aggregate shows revenue of $238.6bn (+5.5% in FY25, decelerating from +16.1% in FY23) with a gross margin that apparently fell from 62.6% to 44.6% — a cost-of-revenue classification artifact in one large REIT, not an economic signal. Data gap flagged.

---

## 1. Sector Overview

CSGP straddles two sectors at opposite points in their cycles. The **commercial real estate information & analytics** market (est. $8–10bn globally, high-single-digit CAGR) is a subscription oligopoly with 70%+ gross margins, entrenched workflow integration and very high switching costs — genuinely a data moat, with MSCI/RCA, Moody's and CompStak the only credible challengers. The **residential listings/lead-generation** market ($20bn+ of US agent marketing spend) is a Zillow-dominated, capital-intensive, commoditised traffic business where Homes.com is a sub-scale challenger. The binding sector question in 2026 is not cyclical — it is whether **generative AI and Google compress the value of proprietary property data and portal traffic**, and that question is why CSGP's shares have gone from ~$97 (Aug-2025) to ~$27.5 (latest quote, −72%) even as EBITDA guidance rose.

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **CRE transaction/leasing recovery is real and accelerating.** Office transaction volume is >80% above the 2023 trough, debt markets have thawed, and Q2 2026 CRE data show rising volumes and prices (Altus). Because CoStar Suite, COMPS, LoopNet and Ten-X revenue is tied to transactions, listings and seat counts rather than property values, this flows through with a lag and with almost no incremental cost.
- **Residential losses are being deliberately harvested.** Homes.com net investment is cut from ~$850m (FY25) to ~$550m (FY26), and the sales force cut 21%. FY26 adjusted EBITDA guidance was *raised* to $780–820m even as revenue guidance was cut to $3.715–3.755bn. Q3-26 proves the lever: revenue $952m (+14.2%), GAAP operating income $84m (8.8% margin), net income $74m vs −$31m a year earlier, record adjusted EBITDA of $229m (~24% margin) — roughly double YoY.
- **Rental market is tightening, supporting Apartments.com pricing.** Q3-26 national apartment vacancy fell to 7.8% and is trending down, which historically precedes higher than expected rent and higher advertiser budgets for the rental marketplaces.

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **AI/LLM and Google disintermediation of the information moat.** Google's nationwide rollout of Local Services Ads home-search (June 2026) and the general availability of LLM-based property research directly attack both the lead-gen funnel and the "pay for our database" model. This, not the fundamentals, is what has compressed the multiple; it is an unhedgeable structural risk whose terminal economics are unknown.
- **Residential competitive intensity is getting worse, not better.** Net new bookings fell 26% YoY in Q2-26 after the sales-force cut; Zillow remains dominant; and the August 2026 Zillow/FTC settlement requires Redfin to rebuild its rental advertising business within six months, reopening a rental market CSGP itself describes as already heavily discounted. Homes.com remains a distant #3 with no demonstrated path to profitability at scale.
- **Core commercial is rate/cycle-sensitive and office is still structurally impaired.** US office leasing volume remains below historical norms despite the recovery, and US retail space under construction fell to 58.3m sq ft in Q3-26 from 63.3m in Q1-26 — a direct drag on construction/permitting-linked product lines. Australian Domain integration and the $730m Vexcel acquisition add integration and amortisation drag.

## 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **Strong in core, Average consolidated**

- **Commercial information (strong, narrow-ish moat):** ~6m properties and ~11m lease/sale comps, built over three decades by the largest field-research force in the industry — not replicable from web scraping, and not quickly replicable by an LLM. This is the ~70–80% institutional share business that funds everything else.
- **Residential (weak):** sub-scale against Zillow's traffic, structurally loss-making, now being shrunk rather than won.
- **Consolidated rating: Average** — the residential drag, the bookings decline and AI uncertainty offset a genuinely superior core. The stock's −59% YTD move to ~$27.5 (~2.9x FY26E revenue, ~14x FY26E adjusted EBITDA on market cap of $11.2bn) prices in substantial moat erosion; that is a valuation question rather than a sector one, but it frames the risk.

## 6. Sector Rating: **Neutral**

Structurally the sub-sector deserves *Attractive* — recurring revenue, 75% gross margins, low capital intensity, high switching costs. But 2026's two live forces are a cyclical tailwind (CRE recovery, benefiting CBRE, JLL and CoStar's commercial segment) against a **structural headwind (AI and Google unbundling the information/lead-gen layer) whose magnitude is genuinely unknowable**. Cyclical recovery does not compensate for a possible change in the terminal margin structure of the moat. Net: Neutral, with a clear preference for the CRE-services names on near-term numbers and for CSGP only on a durable-evidence-of-moat basis.
