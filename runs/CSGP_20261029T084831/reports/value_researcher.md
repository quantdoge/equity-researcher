## CoStar Group (CSGP) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price: $27.54 (Oct 28, 2026 close) | Market cap $11.06B | Net debt $526M | EV ≈ $11.6B | 401.6M diluted shares*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **Narrow** (Wide in Commercial, None in Residential)

CoStar is two very different businesses inside one ticker, and conflating them is the analytical error the market is currently making in both directions.

**Commercial (moat: Wide).** CoStar Suite and LoopNet are the de facto standard for U.S. commercial real estate data — a proprietary database built over 35 years from 1,000+ researchers verifying leases, comps, tenants and for-sale listings. This is a classic intangible-asset + high-switching-cost moat: multi-year contracts, embedded workflow, 90%+ retention, and pricing power that has survived the worst CRE transaction drought in a decade. Q3 2026 segment EBITDA margin runs ~43% on ~$1.97B annualized revenue. LoopNet's marketplace has genuine network effects. The company's own 10-K notes it is the only provider with a unified national inventory — an efficient-scale moat that cannot be replicated with a scraper.

**Residential (moat: None-to-uncertain).** Aggregate Q3 2026 revenue was $952M (+14%), split roughly $460M residential (+23%; only +8% organic) and ~$492M commercial. Homes.com is a well-funded attacker against Zillow, a two-sided network with a decade of lead. CoStar has spent an estimated $1.5B+ in marketing to get Homes.com to ~100M monthly visitors while Residential EBITDA only just turned positive at $16M in Q3 2026. Apartments.com (inside Residential) is a genuinely strong franchise with network effects; Homes.com is not.

**Moat trend over five years: narrowed.** Not because the commercial franchise weakened, but because the consolidated business has been diluted by capital-intensive, low-return adjacencies.

**The test that matters:** ROIC has been 5.7% (2022) → 3.3% (2023) → 0.1% (2024) → **-0.8% (2025)**. By Buffett's own yardstick, the consolidated entity has not earned its cost of capital for four straight years. Pre-goodwill, the commercial business still earns excellent returns — but the holding company does not. That is the whole investment debate in one line.

### 3. Valuation Analysis

The provided DCF tool is unusable here (it seeds on $41M trailing FCF with a 0% growth rate and returns a nonsensical $4.6M value). I built the valuation manually.

| P/E on 2026E adjusted EPS (~$1.42) | — | **19.4x** |
| P/E on 2026E GAAP EPS (~$0.36) | — | ~76x |
| TTM FCF yield | $65M / $11.1B | **0.6%** |
| P/B | $7.99B equity | 1.39x |

**Method 1 — DCF.** FCF of ~$250M (2026E), $480M (2027E), $720M (2028E), then +7%/yr through 2032, 3% terminal, 9% WACC → EV ≈ $14.0B → equity ≈ $13.5B → **~$33/share**.

**Method 2 — Sum of the parts.** Commercial at ~$840M segment EBITDA × 16x = $13.4B; Residential at ~$1.8B revenue × 1.5x = $2.7B; less $0.5B net debt → $15.6B → **~$39/share**. This method is the bull anchor: at today's price you are paying roughly the value of the commercial franchise alone and getting Apartments.com, Homes.com, Domain, Zonda and Vexcel for near zero.

**Method 3 — Target-multiple.** 14x × 2027E EBITDA of $1.0B, less net debt → **~$34/share**.

**Intrinsic value: ~$35/share (range $30–$42).** Current price of $27.54 is a **~21% discount** — inside the 20–30% margin-of-safety band but at its shallow end.

**What the price implies:** the market is capitalizing 2026 EBITDA at 14.5x and applying roughly a 20% haircut to management's 2028 target, discounted at 9%. In other words, *there is no heroic assumption embedded* — but there is also no fat pitch. A 70% decline from the August 2025 high of ~$96 has done most of the de-rating work.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment: **C+**

**Positives.** Founder-CEO Andrew Florance is a genuine domain obsessive who built the commercial data moat from scratch; he is candid on calls and clearly understands the asset quality. He bought 83,300 shares on the open market at ~$29.89 in August 2026 (~$2.5M) — insiders buying with their own money is the single most reliable signal available, and it is a real one here. He also backed off the Homes.com marketing spree decisively once the profitability inflection was reachable.

**Negatives — and they are serious.**
- **Serial empire-building.** Five acquisitions in 24 months: Visual Lease (Nov 2024), Matterport (~$1.4B, Feb 2025), Domain (~$1.8B, Aug 2025), Zonda ($800M, Aug 2026), Vexcel ($730M, announced Oct 27, 2026). Roughly $5.4B of M&A against a current $11B market cap — twice the company's entire cumulative R&D spend. Goodwill and intangibles now stand at $7.49B, or **94% of shareholders' equity**. Tangible book is a thin ~$0.5B.
- **Value-destroying buyback timing.** $527M repurchased in Q1 2026 at ~$62, plus $84M in Q2 at ~$50 and $81M in Q3 at ~$33. Roughly $250M of shareholder capital was vaporized by buying at the high. That is not disciplined capital allocation.
- **No dividend**, and insider ownership of only ~1.2% ($151M) is low for a founder-led company — the alignment is thinner than the narrative suggests.
- **Guidance credibility**: the 2025 blowup in GAAP earnings was substantially acquisition- and integration-driven, and the market has stopped paying for "adjusted" numbers.

Management is honest and competent operationally, but has spent the last two years behaving like a conglomerateur at exactly the wrong point in the cycle. That deserves a discount, not a premium.

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

CoStar today is a wide-moat asset wrapped in a capital-allocation problem. The commercial franchise (43% segment EBITDA margins, 90%+ retention, efficient-scale data moat) is genuinely worth $13–14B on its own. At an $11.6B enterprise value, you are getting the entire residential portfolio — Apartments.com, Homes.com, Domain, Zonda, Vexcel — for approximately nothing, while management converts from land-grab mode to harvest mode (Q3 2026 adjusted EBITDA doubled year-over-year; Residential swung to positive EBITDA).

Why not Buy today:
1. **ROIC has been below WACC for four consecutive years.** Until the consolidated return on capital recovers above ~10%, the "wide moat" is a claim, not a fact.
2. **FCF conversion of 36% and a 0.6% FCF yield** give no cushion if the 2028 target slips.
3. **The margin of safety is only ~21%** to my $35 midpoint — below the 25–30% I require for a founder-led serial acquirer with a 94%-of-equity goodwill balance.
4. The Oct 28, 2026 14% single-day drop on slowing bookings suggests the 2027 consensus is still falling.

**Action levels:**
- **Below $24.50** (≈30% discount to intrinsic value): initiate a 2–3% position.
- **Below $22** (≈37% discount, ~11x 2026E EBITDA, ~7x 2028E): back up the truck — this is a genuine value opportunity.
- **Above $38–40**: trim; that is full value.
- Existing holders should hold; the risk/reward at 14.5x forward EBITDA and 9.3x 2028E is asymmetric to the upside.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI disintermediation of the data moat (the dominant risk).** If LLMs plus web-scale scraping can approximate CRE comps and inventory, CoStar's pricing power erodes. My judgment is that verified, licensed, continuously refreshed data with contractual redistribution rights is more defensible than the market fears — but this is the reason the multiple is 14x rather than 25x, and it will not be resolved quickly.
2. **Continued capital misallocation.** Another $1B+ acquisition at a full price, financed with debt, would break the balance-sheet argument and likely force a goodwill write-down of a meaningful slice of the $7.5B intangible base.
3. **Homes.com remains a cash furnace in a housing downturn.** Management has already spent the marketing budget once. A recession that cuts residential listing volumes would push the segment back to losses and remove the "profitability inflection" narrative.
4. **Commercial real estate cyclicality.** Office vacancies remain elevated; tenant moves and lease signings drive seat counts. A CRE transaction volume recovery is the single biggest swing factor for the commercial segment's growth.
5. **Capex has structurally re-rated** from $25M (2023) to ~$390M (2025). If $300M+ is the new maintenance level rather than a build-out spike, permanent FCF is ~$200M lower than my model assumes and intrinsic value falls to the low-$20s.
6. **Share count creep.** SBC of ~$150–190M/year, only partially offset by buybacks, is a persistent 2–3% annual headwind.

**Bottom line:** A good business at a fair-to-slightly-cheap price — not yet a great business at a wonderful price. Hold, and let the price come to you.
