# AST SpaceMobile (ASTS) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $25.49 | Market cap ~$7.6B (300M diluted sh.) | As of Nov 18, 2026*

## 1. Growth Story
AST SpaceMobile is building the first space-based cellular broadband network that connects **directly to unmodified smartphones** — no dish, no app, no new handset — using massive phased-array BlueBird satellites (~693 sq ft each) that act as cell towers in orbit. Its moat is the hardest to replicate in telecom: 60+ carrier partnerships covering **3B+ subscribers** (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, Rakuten, Google), ~3,000 patent claims, and scarce spectrum/regulatory filings. Revenue has inflected for real — from $0 in 2023 to a **$142.9M LTM run-rate** — but the stock has collapsed **-79% from its January 2026 high of $122** as launch cadence slipped, revenue missed for five of six quarters, and share count went from 154M (2024) to 300M (Q3'26).

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration
- **TAM**: global mobile wireless service is ~$1.1T/yr; the addressable "coverage gap" segment (rural, maritime, aviation, emergency) is estimated at $30–60B+ by 2030. ASTS's wholesale model captures ~$1–2/subscriber/month from carriers who keep the retail relationship.
- **Penetration today**: effectively **0%**. 12 BlueBirds in orbit vs. ~45 needed for continuous US/EU/Japan coverage and ~90 for global. Funded to build/launch ~90.
- **The math that matters**: $1.30B of revenue (enough to justify today's EV at 6x sales in 2030) requires only **~0.04% of ASTS's existing 3B-subscriber partner base at $1/month**. That is the single most compelling number in the thesis — the partnership footprint is already built; only the constellation is missing.
- **Differentiation vs. Starlink D2C**: ASTS's broadbeam, high-power design supports true broadband (video, not just SMS) on unmodified phones; Starlink's D2C is lower-capacity/text-first today. But SpaceX owns its launch and ASTS does not.

## 5. Alternative Data Signals
- **Google Trends — bearish**: search interest peaked at 69 (May 2026) and collapsed to **8–12** through Aug–Oct before spiking to 100 on earnings week. Retail attention and momentum flow have fully evacuated — historically the precondition for a contrarian base, but not yet a turn.
- **Job postings — bullish**: heavy Midland/Odessa, TX industrial hiring across **multiple sites and shifts** (composite technicians, material handlers, custodians) indicates genuine satellite production scale-up, not just marketing.
- **Analyst revisions — mixed**: the far-dated 2030 EPS estimate was revised **+100%**, but near-term PTs were slashed repeatedly (Deutsche $137 → $117 → $93; Piper $100 → $98 → $54 Neutral; B.Riley and Piper both downgraded to Neutral in Oct). Note one offsetting upgrade: Scotiabank to Outperform at $55.70 (Oct 23).
- **Earnings surprise history — bearish**: revenue missed in 5 of the last 6 quarters; EPS missed all 6. Guidance has been systematically over-optimistic.
- **Price/volume**: -81% from the $133 May peak; -17% single-day drop on July 16 (class action alleging dilution/misstatements); Oct 9 -10% on 54M shares. Distribution pattern, no accumulation yet.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD** *(speculative; upgrade trigger below)*
The TAM and partnership moat are genuinely Wood-style exceptional, and at ~45x LTM revenue — versus 130x at the January peak — the valuation has de-rated from absurd to merely demanding. **But this is a manufacturing-and-launch execution story, and execution is precisely what has failed for six straight quarters.** The pattern is consistent: satellites slip, revenue misses, dilution fills the gap. With $3.2B cash and ~20 months of runway at the current $400–700M/qtr burn, another raise is likely before the 45-satellite milestone is reached.

I would **not pay up ahead of proof**, and I would not short a company with a $1T TAM and 3B subscribers in its distribution footprint. **Upgrade to Buy on either**: (a) commercial service activation with disclosed paying subscribers/revenue in Q4'26, or (b) 24+ satellites in orbit with FY2027 guidance ≥$400M. **Downgrade to Sell** on a Q4 revenue miss below $50M or a dilutive raise priced below $25.

## 7. Key Risks
1. **Launch execution risk (highest)** — dependence on third-party vehicles; the May 2026 New Glenn anomaly triggered the initial de-rating. Each slip pushes the revenue curve out a full year.
2. **Competitive threat — SpaceX Starlink D2C** — vertically integrated launch, 8,000+ satellites, and carrier deals already signed (T-Mobile). ASTS's differentiation is real but narrowing.
3. **Serial dilution** — 154M → 300M shares in 24 months. Even a successful constellation may not produce per-share value if the equity is sold to fund it.
4. **Guidance credibility / litigation** — 5 of 6 revenue misses, plus a securities class action over dilution and statements. Management's schedule statements deserve a heavy discount.
5. **Capital intensity** — $1.15B capex in 9M'26 alone; ~90 satellites to fund. Gross margin at 40% today must reach 80%+ for the model to work, and it has been volatile (negative in Q4'25).
6. **Regulatory/spectrum risk** — service launch in five countries by year-end requires SCS approvals that are not fully in hand.
