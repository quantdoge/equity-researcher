# AST SpaceMobile (ASTS) — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment
*As of the November 2026 filing/news window. Note: SEC.gov direct fetches were blocked (HTTP 403) and the 10-K was not retrievable via the filing tool; quantitative geographic splits below are analyst estimates built from disclosures/press releases and are flagged as such.*

## Company snapshot
AST SpaceMobile (Midland, TX; ~1,126 employees) is a pre-scale, single-product company building a space-based cellular broadband network (BlueBird satellites) that connects directly to unmodified handsets, monetised through revenue-share/prepayment deals with mobile network operators (MNOs) and, increasingly, the US Government. **Q3 2026 revenue: $42.3M**, described as coming "from commercial and United States government customers." Ecosystem claim: "over 60 mobile network operators representing over 3 billion subscribers."

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical / Regulatory Risks

**1) Jurisdictional licensing & "territoriality" of spectrum — Probability: Very High (already binding) | Impact: High**
The business model requires country-by-country authorisation to radiate into *other operators'* licensed spectrum. The FCC's April 2026 SCS grant (248-satellite constellation, 223 additional satellites beyond the 5-satellite testbed) is US-only, and carries build-out milestones (50% of 248 launched by 2 Aug 2030; remainder by 2 Aug 2033). Critically, the FCC has moved to **deny use of US-licensed AST satellites to point at the EU**, i.e., the FCC tells both SpaceX and AST to "stay in their lane" on spectrum. Initial coverage is limited to the US, Canada, Japan, UK and Saudi Arabia by year-end 2026, "subject to applicable regulatory approvals." Every incremental market is a separate sovereign process (plus ITU coordination), so international revenue is gated by dozens of regulators, not by satellite capacity.

**2) US spectrum politics: the Ligado L-band dependency and D2D rulemaking — Probability: Medium (~40%) | Impact: High**
AST's US capacity roadmap leans on Ligado Networks' L-band (SkyTerra Next, satellite-modification application still pending at the FCC; Ligado told the FCC in June 2026 it is answering technical inquiries). Ligado is a restructured Chapter 11 debtor whose **$39B lawsuit against the Department of Defense** is unresolved; critics fear the government will approve a Ligado settlement and the AST transaction "as a package deal," and **aviation groups and Iridium continue to oppose** the Ligado–AST arrangement. Separately, the FCC's October 29, 2026 D2D modernisation NPRM and the approved **EchoStar→SpaceX (~65 MHz) spectrum sale** are reshaping the competitive-regulatory field: SpaceX/Starlink is converting from carrier partner to rival, and AST is actively lobbying the FCC on spectrum auctions (opposing Qualcomm's sidelink proposal to reserve 1675–1680 MHz for SCS). Political/competitive tilting in Washington is a live, non-diversifiable risk for a company whose US business is ~80% of revenue.

**3) Sovereign/partner dependence, export controls and state-linked capital — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium**
The growth model is funded and de-risked by foreign states and MNO prepayments: Japan's $1.0B MIC funding, stc's $175M prepayment, Vodafone/AT&T/Google strategic equity. That buys political cover but imports conditionality (Japan), state-linked counterparties (stc is PIF-linked), and export-control friction: satellite technology, technical data and gateway designs shared with Rakuten AST (Japan) and Saudi partners implicate **ITAR/EAR "deemed export"** rules, and gateway builds in Saudi Arabia require US export licences and anti-boycott compliance. The DPAS-rated, dual-use US Government program and Space Force testing raise the mirror risk: national-security priority can override commercial deployment schedules.

Secondary: launch-provider concentration on SpaceX and Blue Origin (national-security-sensitive US providers, with Blacklift — AST's own 20-ton reusable rocket — not until late 2028).

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights
- **Two securities class actions.** (a) 2024 Gross Law Firm case (class period 14 Nov 2023 – 1 Apr 2024) alleging concealment of Block 1 BlueBird production/launch delays tied to two suppliers. (b) A 2026 securities fraud class action covering **4 Mar 2025 – 15 Jul 2026**, with a **13 Nov 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline**; multiple firms soliciting (Rosen, Kessler Topaz, Pomerantz, Kuehn, ZLK, RGRD). The putative class period overlaps the launch-delay disclosures and coincides with a reported one-day drop of $14.70 (−15.17%). This is the principal legal overhang.
- **FCC:** commercial SCS authorisation (Apr 22, 2026) with AT&T, Verizon and FirstNet; experimental licence WL2XRE (SCS testing, expires 1 Apr 2028); pending Ligado licence modification contested by aviation and Iridium.
- **International authorisations:** Japan (with Rakuten, plus Rakuten AST JV), Brazil commercial authorisation (50 MHz), New Zealand gateway link, Saudi gateways/NOC; UK, Canada, Japan, Saudi Arabia targeted for initial service.
- **No disclosed DOJ/SEC/FCPA/OFAC investigation** surfaced in the search set. Absence of evidence, not proof of absence.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags
1. **FCPA / anti-corruption:** US-incorporated issuer with a state-owned counterparty (stc, PIF-linked), state-funded program (Japan MIC) and government-gateway builds in Saudi Arabia. Third-party/consultant and government-interface controls are the classic failure point; no allegation, but this is the highest-severity structural exposure.
2. **Export controls (ITAR/EAR):** technology transfer to a Japanese JV and to foreign MNO/gateway partners; deemed-export controls over 1,126 employees and contractors.
3. **Sanctions/roaming screening:** Vodafone's footprint touches higher-risk markets; D2D coverage is geography-blind — service over sanctioned territories or to sanctioned handsets is a plausible future OFAC issue requiring geo-fencing.
4. **Data privacy:** GDPR/UK GDPR and Saudi PDPL apply to traffic routed through partner gateways.
5. **Foreign ownership/CFIUS-Team Telecom:** foreign strategic holders (Vodafone, Rakuten, Samsung-adjacent parties) plus national-security work create a future mitigation-agreement risk.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**
Not because of conflict or expropriation exposure — ASTS has no meaningful revenue in unstable or sanctioned jurisdictions — but because **regulatory permission is the product**: a single-territory revenue base gated by FCC conditions and contested US spectrum politics, a country-by-country licensing treadmill abroad (including FCC-imposed limits on serving Europe), state-linked funding/counterparties, export-control friction on partner/JV technology sharing, and an active securities-fraud class action with a November 2026 lead-plaintiff deadline. Key monitoring items: Ligado FCC modification order and DoD settlement headlines, the FCC D2D/spectrum-auction NPRM outcome, Japan MIC funding conditions, and the 2026 class action's consolidation and any amended complaint.
