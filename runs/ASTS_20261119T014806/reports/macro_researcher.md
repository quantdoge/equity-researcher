### 1. Macro Environment Summary — **RISK-OFF IN DURATION, NOT IN GROWTH**

The US is in an unusual and specifically hostile regime for ASTS: **growth is fine, inflation is re-accelerating, and the Fed has resumed hiking.** The FOMC raised the target range 25bp to **3.75–4.00% on 16 Sept 2026** and held on 28 Oct; the median dot implies **4.00–4.25% through end-2026 and 2027**. This is not a cutting cycle — it is a stalled-then-reversed one (funds went 4.33% → 3.63% through mid-2026, then back up).

The critical feature is at the **long end**: the 10Y has risen from **4.44% (30 Jun) to 5.13% (19 Nov)**, a **+69bp** move in under five months, with the 30Y at **5.47%**. Ten-year breakevens are *contained* at **2.19%**, which means the entire rise is **real yields** — the 10Y real yield is now **~2.94%**, near the highest in four decades. This is a term-premium/fiscal-supply repricing, not an inflation-expectations event. For an asset whose value is ~100% terminal value in 2029+, this is the single most damaging macro configuration possible.

Meanwhile **growth and credit are still supportive**: Q3 real GDP grew **+3.4% SAAR** (+2.1% YoY), unemployment is **4.1%**, and HY OAS at **3.43%** remains historically tight. But spreads have **widened 78bp since 1 Sept (2.65% → 3.43%)** — risk premia are beginning to move, and ASTS equity has already repriced far ahead of credit.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**NEGATIVE — Real discount rates (dominant driver).** At ~60x FY26 revenue guidance ($150–200M; Q3 actual $42.3M) against a $10.4B cap, ASTS's cash flows are almost entirely post-2028. With equity duration of roughly 15–20 years and beta 2.85, each 100bp of real-rate increase is worth an estimated **15–20% of intrinsic value**. The +85bp move in real 10Y yields since June mechanically justifies a **~13–17% de-rating on rates alone** — ASTS has fallen **81% from its $133.09 May close** (−65% YTD), so the bulk of the drawdown is execution (launch delays, SpaceX competition), with rates acting as a *multiplier* on the downside rather than the primary cause.

**NEGATIVE — Cost and availability of growth capital (the key transmission channel).** ASTS is a serial capital raiser: it has completed **two $1B convertible raises in 2026** (incl. $1.0B 2034 notes) and held **$3.2B cash at 30 Sep**, of which ~$1.15B is convertible proceeds. The stock fell 13% on the second raise. Higher real yields raise the coupon and lower the conversion premium on each new convertible, while a $25 stock makes every equity-linked dollar more dilutive than at $100. This is a **reflexive feedback loop**: lower price → costlier capital → more dilution → lower price. At 5.13% risk-free, the marginal investor's hurdle for a pre-profit 2029 story rises sharply.

**NEGATIVE — Competitive capital asymmetry.** SpaceX is reportedly marketing a **$4B bond sale** funded at investment-grade-adjacent terms. In a 5%+ long-end world, scale players with cash flow and cheap credit compound their advantage, while single-asset developers pay a widening premium. Capital is flowing *up* the quality curve — visible in space stocks sorting into "deliverers" (SpaceX, RKLB revenue records) vs "promisers."

**MILDLY NEGATIVE — Strong dollar.** The broad USD is up **3.4% since September**. ASTS's model is wholesale capacity sold to telco partners (AT&T, Verizon, Vodafone, and dozens of international MNOs); international and EM partner revenue translates worse at 122.65, and a strong dollar tightens partner budgets in local-currency terms. Small today, material at scale.

**NEUTRAL / MILDLY NEGATIVE — Interest cost on inflation.** 3.8% CPI lifts satellite manufacturing, launch, and engineering-labour costs (unemployment 4.1%). ASTS has essentially no pricing power pre-commercial-scale, so it cannot pass costs through — unlike a telecom incumbent with CPI-linked contracts.

**POSITIVE (offsetting) — Credit markets still open; growth intact.** HY OAS of 3.43% is not a credit event. The convertible market is functioning, unemployment at 4.1% with GDP near 3.4% means telco capex budgets and consumer demand for connectivity remain healthy. No recession means the *demand* side of the D2D thesis is unimpaired.

**MINIMAL — Tariffs/trade.** Satellite assembly is domestic (Midland, TX) and launch providers are US-based; direct tariff exposure is low, though phased-array semiconductor content carries some import risk.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

This is close to a *worst-case* macro mix for a pre-revenue, capex-intensive, dilution-dependent long-duration equity: **re-accelerating inflation, a hawkish Fed at 4.00%, a 5.13% 10Y driven by real yields, and a rising dollar** — partially cushioned only by the absence of recession and still-tight credit spreads. The macro backdrop did not cause the 81% drawdown, but it removes the valuation tailwind that carried ASTS in 2025 and it materially raises the cost of the capital ASTS must raise to reach its 45-satellite target.

**Positioning implication:** macro is a **-15 to -20% multiple headwind** layered on top of idiosyncratic execution risk. It is a reason to demand a larger margin of safety, not a reason to short — the reflexive upside if real yields reverse is equally violent.

---

### 5. Scenario That Would Materially Surprise

**BULL SURPRISE (non-consensus): "The Fed's September hike was a policy error."** If Q4 CPI rolls from 3.82% back toward 2.5% — plausibly driven by the 3.4% dollar appreciation tightening conditions — and the FOMC is forced into emergency cuts in H1 2027, the 10Y could retrace from **5.13% to ~4.10%** and the 10Y real yield from **2.94% to ~1.8%**. For a 15–20-year-duration equity, that is a **60–90% re-rating** on the discount rate *alone*, before any satellite launch. Simultaneously, convertible issuance becomes cheap and high-premium again, and the dilution spiral reverses into a financing tailwind. ASTS at $25 is priced for a permanent 5% long end; it is not priced for a real-rate collapse. **This is the scenario consensus is least prepared for, and it is the single largest identifiable source of upside in the name.**

*(Bear mirror: a term-premium blowout to 6% on the 10Y with HY OAS widening through 500bp would close the convertible window and force an equity raise at $18–20 — another 40–50% down.)*
