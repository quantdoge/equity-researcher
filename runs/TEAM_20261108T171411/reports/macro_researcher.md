# Macroeconomic Assessment — Atlassian Corporation (TEAM)

**As of:** November 8, 2026 | **Price:** $153.68 (Nov 6 close) | **Mkt cap:** $38.9B | **Beta:** 1.14

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Transitional, tilting risk-off for long-duration assets**

The US economy is in a **reflation / "no-landing" regime**, not a slowdown. Real GDP re-accelerated to +3.4% SAAR in Q3 2026 after nearly stalling in Q4 2025 (+0.2% SAAR), unemployment has *fallen* to 4.1% from 4.5% a year ago, and CPI has re-accelerated from a 2.65% trough (Dec 2025) to **3.72%** (Sep 2026).

The critical policy inflection: **the Fed stopped cutting and resumed hiking.** The effective funds rate sat flat at 3.63% from January through August 2026, then rose to 3.75% (Sep) and 3.88% (Oct) — two 25bp hikes. Fed speakers have guided that "further rate hikes will likely be needed" (Barr, Sep 23). The 10Y has traded above 5%, the highest since 2007.

This is the worst possible macro mix for high-multiple, long-duration software: **growth is too strong to justify easing, and inflation is too sticky to allow it.** Rate-cut hopes — the primary support for software multiples in 2024–25 — have been withdrawn. However, there is a recent relief valve: the 10Y has retreated **47bp from its Oct 5 peak of 5.31% to 4.84%**, partly on midterm gridlock removing new fiscal stimulus. Credit remains extremely accommodative (HY OAS 348bp), so this is a *discount-rate* problem, not a credit problem.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Atlassian is a **seat-based, subscription software vendor** with ~50%+ non-US revenue, USD reporting, a Sydney HQ (AUD/INR cost base), and effectively **net-cash leverage** — no refinancing cliff. Its macro sensitivity is therefore almost entirely **(a) discount rate** and **(b) customer headcount/IT budget**, not balance-sheet.

| Macro variable | Impact | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| **10Y at 4.84% (5.31% peak)** | **Negative** | TEAM is a long-duration asset — the majority of its DCF value sits beyond year 10. A 50bp rise in the discount rate on a ~30x EV/EBITDA multiple compresses fair value ~12–15%. This is the single largest macro driver. |
| **Fed hiking (3.88%, more guided)** | **Negative** | Removes the easing tailwind that drove the 2024–25 software re-rating. Also raises the hurdle rate for the growth-oriented CIO budgets TEAM depends on. |
| **CPI +3.72%** | **Mild negative** | Wage inflation pressures opex (13,301 employees; R&D concentrated in Australia/India). TEAM has pricing power via per-seat list increases, but multi-year enterprise contracts (RPO $4.7B, +44%) lock in pricing — a ~150bp real erosion on the backlog. |
| **GDP +3.4% SAAR, low unemployment** | **Positive** | Seats are a direct function of customer headcount. Low unemployment and 24% revenue growth with +44% RPO confirm macro is *not* currently constraining demand. |
| **USD +3.0% since September** | **Negative (net modest)** | ~Half of revenue is non-US; a stronger broad dollar is a ~1.5% revenue translation headwind. Partially offset: AUD/INR-denominated engineering costs translate *down*. Net ~-100bp to revenue growth. |
| **HY OAS 348bp** | **Neutral/Positive** | No refinancing risk; but the +82bp widening off September's 266bp is an early-warning signal that risk appetite is thinning at the margin. |
| **Yield curve normalized (+10bp)** | **Neutral** | No recession signal. Bear-steepening (30Y 5.47%) is a term-premium/fiscal story, not a growth-collapse story. |
| **AI capex supercycle** | **Positive, with a caveat** | Enterprise AI adoption is genuinely driving cloud (+31%) and displacement wins vs. competitors. Caveat: AI is also the vector by which seat counts may structurally shrink. |

**Important nuance:** TEAM's 32% drawdown ($227.61 on Oct 23 → $153.68 on Nov 6) was **not** macro-driven. It was triggered by Q1 FY27 guidance for cloud growth to decelerate more than expected, despite a beat ($1.8B revenue +24%, EPS $1.65 vs $1.04) and a raised full-year outlook. Macro set the *ceiling*; the company set the *trigger*.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **Neutral (negative skew)**

Split verdict, because the two channels diverge sharply:

- **Operating fundamentals: Favourable.** Growth, employment, and enterprise IT demand are all supportive. Nothing in the macro data impairs Atlassian's 24% growth or its $4.7B RPO.
- **Valuation multiple: Unfavourable.** A hiking Fed, 3.7% CPI, and a 4.8–5.3% 10Y impose a hard ceiling on multiple expansion for long-duration software. Any re-rating to prior highs requires either (i) the 10Y sustainably below ~4.25%, or (ii) a demonstrated *re-acceleration* in cloud growth to offset it.

**Net: Neutral.** Macro is not the reason to own or avoid TEAM today — but it means the stock cannot be rescued by a multiple tailwind. Every dollar of return must come from fundamental execution.

## 5. The Surprise Scenario — **Bear: a third Fed hike and a 10Y retest of 5.5%+**

**The setup:** CPI has already risen ~110bp off its December 2025 trough to 3.72%, and the Fed has hiked twice. If Q4 CPI prints ≥3.9% — plausible given +3.4% GDP growth and 4.1% unemployment — the market must price a terminal rate near 4.5% rather than 3.88%.

**The transmission:** Each 25bp of additional tightening plus the associated term-premium repricing has pushed the 10Y through 5.31% to 5.50–5.75% (a level unseen since 2007). For a name trading at ~30x EV/EBITDA with 75%+ of its value in terminal years:

- A 65bp rise in the 10Y from 4.84% ⇒ ~15–18% derating at constant fundamentals
- Simultaneously, CIOs defer seat expansion ⇒ consensus FY28 growth cut from ~20% to high-teens

**The math:** $153.68 × 0.83 (derating) × 0.95 (estimate cut) ≈ **$120–125**, roughly a further 20% downside — and it would coincide with the already-evident cloud-growth deceleration, compounding the narrative. Note the stock already traded at **$56 in April 2026**; a 4x 12-month range proves how violently this name reprices on rate and narrative shifts.

**The mirror-image bull case** (the same logic inverted, and arguably the higher-probability surprise): midterm gridlock delivers no fiscal stimulus, commodity prices ease, CPI rolls back to ~2.8%, and the 10Y falls to ~4.25%. TEAM's 24% growth and +44% RPO then re-rate to 8x EV/Sales ⇒ **$230–250**, a ~55% upside. **The asymmetry is favourable on a 12-month view precisely because macro sentiment is already so poor** — which is why my Neutral rating carries a negative *current* skew but not a negative 12-month one.
