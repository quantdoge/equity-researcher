## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**
- SBTi-validated near-term targets; **net zero by 2040** (pulled forward from 2050); **100% renewable electricity achieved ~4 years early**; FY25 report cites **~82% reduction in Scope 1+2**.
- Structural risk is **Scope 3 / AI compute**: energy-hungry inference and model hosting. CSO Jessica Hyman has flagged supplier engagement to counter AI-related emissions — a cost-of-goods issue as much as a carbon one, and a potential disclosure-quality test as CSRD/SB 253 Scope 3 reporting bites.
- **No stranded assets, no carbon-tax exposure of consequence, no material environmental litigation or spills/fines identified.** No ESG-linked debt covenants (investment-grade-light balance sheet), so no covenant tripwires.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **MEDIUM-HIGH (deteriorating)**
- **March 2026 restructuring: ~10% of workforce (~1,600 roles), including ~900 in R&D**; CTO replaced; charges up to **$174M** plus **≥$62M** office-exit costs. Framed explicitly as self-funding AI/enterprise investment ("not 'AI replaces people'… but it would be disingenuous to pretend AI doesn't change the mix of skills").
- **Union/labour-relations escalation**: Professionals Australia (representing hundreds of AU staff) alleges redundancies were made **without consultation**, with consultation running 12–19 March and terminations 2 April. This is the clearest *legal* social exposure — Australian consultation obligations and reputational damage in the home market. Severance package (16 weeks' minimum, extended healthcare, pro-rata bonuses, parental leave payout) is above statutory and mitigates wrongful-termination/claim risk.
- **Talent-retention risk is the real cash-flow channel**: two layoff cycles (2024 and 2026) plus a CTO change plus a collapsed share price (employee RSU value impairment) in a competitive AI labour market. Watch voluntary attrition and whether equity refreshes require incremental comp spend.
- **Product security**: recurring critical vulnerabilities — Oct-2026 bulletin lists **162 high-severity + 14 critical third-party** issues; CVE-2026-21589 (Cloud patched, no exploitation found); Oct-2026 Data Center unauthenticated file-access flaw under active exploitation attempts; precedent of Confluence RCE → LockBit intrusions (2024-25). For a vendor whose customers' crown-jewel architecture/engineering data sits in Confluence, this is the single largest social-externalities tail risk.
- Diversity/DEI: disclosure is reasonable (gender representation incl. non-binary categories, senior-leadership split published; FY23 senior-leadership women ~38%); technical-role breakdowns are *not* disclosed — a disclosure gap. US "ESG backlash"/state-level DEI scrutiny is a modest political-risk overlay in public-sector sales.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **5 / 10**
**Positives:** majority-independent board with all standing committees independent (15 meetings FY26); nine directors up for election 1-Dec-26; EY ratified as auditor; **98.3% say-on-pay support**; FY26 revenue $6.6B (+26%), cloud $4.4B (+28%), FCF $1.3B (20% margin), GAAP operating profitability, and the most recent 10-Q carries an unqualified 302 certification (no material-weakness disclosure).

**Negatives:**
1. **Dual-class control: Class A 1 vote, Class B 10 votes.** Cannon-Brookes and Farquhar together control **~86% of voting power** while holding a minority economic stake — minority holders cannot effect change; Nasdaq controlled-company exemptions apply.
2. **Control uncertainty**: the founders' sell-down, plus reporting on Cannon-Brookes' divorce and whether his ~18.4% stake could be split in a settlement, creates an overhang on the Class B block and on any eventual one-share-one-vote sunset. No time-based sunset identified in available disclosure.
3. **Leadership churn/strategy volatility**: co-CEO structure dismantled (Farquhar exited 31-Aug-24), CTO replaced alongside a 10% cut, guidance-driven -14-16% single-day repricing (6-Nov-26) on FY27 growth deceleration. Founder-CEO with no independent chair verified (bylaws permit a combined Chair/CEO with a Lead Independent Director only in that case — **this is my one unresolved disclosure gap**).
4. **Related-party transaction**: ~$2.8M FY26 for flights on a CEO-owned aircraft (fees accruing substantially to the CEO). Individually immaterial, cumulatively a governance-quality tell.
5. **Insider selling cadence** through 2025-26 (routine 10b5-1 sales, and a pledged up-to-$250M personal purchase announced with an earnings release as a confidence signal — itself an unconventional governance lever).

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure
- **Australia**: Modern Slavery Act 2018 reporting (FY26 statement published — compliant); **Treasury climate disclosure law (AASB S2)** — Atlassian's Australian group likely Group 1 given revenue scale, first reporting FY27; Fair Work consultation obligations (live risk); Privacy Act reform.
- **UK** (Atlassian Corp. plc domicile): UK Modern Slavery Act statement; Companies Act strategic/TCFD-aligned climate reporting. Not UK-premium-listed, so UK Corporate Governance Code does not bind.
- **EU**: **CSRD** — as a non-EU parent with EU turnover likely above the €150M threshold, in scope for FY2028 reporting (subject to Omnibus scope/delay revisions); **CSDDD** supplier due diligence (post-Omnibus, delayed and narrowed); **EU AI Act** — deployer/limited-risk obligations for Rovo-type AI features (foundation-model obligations sit with its model suppliers); **DORA** contractual/resilience requirements flowing from EU financial-services customers; GDPR processor duties.
- **US**: SEC climate rule effectively withdrawn/stayed → low; **California SB 253/261** applies (revenue >$1B, doing business in CA) — Scope 1/2/3 disclosure with 2026-27 timelines; state anti-ESG procurement/pension activism as a minor revenue/political risk; FedRAMP/public-sector expansion raises compliance intensity.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (E: Leader | S: Average, deteriorating | G: Laggard-leaning)

Atlassian is a genuine **environmental and disclosure leader** for its sector — SBTi-validated targets, 100% renewable electricity four years early, a real sustainability report, human-rights policy and modern-slavery reporting. But for a software company, E is close to immaterial, and the two factors that actually matter — **human capital and shareholder rights** — are where it is weakest. ~86% founder voting control with no evident sunset, a CEO related-party arrangement, leadership churn, and a workforce restructuring executed with an active union objection put it below software peers on governance. It is not an "ESG Risk" name: no environmental liabilities, no corruption or accounting flags, no material fines, and solid pay governance.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL-to-NEGATIVE for the thesis**
- **Not a thesis-breaker; a discount-rate and mandate-access issue.** Nothing in the E or S stack threatens the license to operate or creates a quantified liability above ~$250M. The $236M restructuring charge is a P&L event, not an ESG liability.
- **The governance overhang is real and mispriced by most models**: 86% voting control means activist/litigation remedies are unavailable; a founder divorce or accelerated sell-down can pressure the register without any operating change; and the CEO-owned-aircraft plus CTO turnover signal a board that follows rather than constrains the founder. Apply a modest governance discount (I would use ~50-100bp on cost of equity) rather than a multiple cut.
- **Screening consequence**: Atlassian fails many dual-class/controlled-company screens and several European ESG-mandate universes; expect structural exclusion from a slice of passive ESG flows — a persistent, if modest, valuation headwind.
- **What would upgrade it**: a Class B sunset (time- or ownership-based), an independent chair, termination of the aircraft arrangement, a credible post-restructuring attrition/AI-reskilling disclosure, and quantified Scope 3 targets absorbing AI compute. **What would downgrade it**: a confirmed customer-data breach traced to an Atlassian CVE, an adverse Australian consultation ruling, or a founder block dissolution that destabilises the register.
