## ATLASSIAN CORPORATION (NASDAQ: TEAM) — Value Analysis
**Price: $153.68 · Market cap ~$38.9B · Net debt ~$0 · FY ends June 30**

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW** (widening in enterprise, unproven through AI)

Atlassian sells the operating system for software teams: Jira (issue/project tracking), Confluence (documentation), Bitbucket, Jira Service Management. It is the de facto standard in its niche, with ~13,300 employees and 85% gross margins sustained for a decade.

**Evidence for the moat:**
- **Switching costs (strong):** Jira/Confluence hold the institutional memory of engineering organizations — workflows, ticket history, permissions, integrations. Rip-and-replace is organizationally painful.
- **Ecosystem/network effects:** Marketplace apps, CI/CD integrations, and internal tooling create compounding lock-in.
- **Pricing power demonstrated:** Cloud price increases (Oct 2026) plus a shift to usage-based pricing for AI features (effective Dec 3, 2026). A company that can re-price into a platform shift has a moat; one that can't, discounts.
- **Revenue durability:** 5-yr revenue CAGR of **26%** ($2.09B FY21 → $6.57B FY26) with no down year; Q1 FY27 revenue +23.7%, **Cloud +31% to $1.30B**, **RPO +44% YoY**.

**Why not Wide:** (a) GAAP returns on capital are effectively zero (ROIC 0.45% in FY26), so the moat has not translated into reported owner returns; (b) the monetization model is **seat-based**, and agentic AI is the first genuine structural threat to per-seat rent in the company's history; (c) Data Center revenue is guided down ~16% in FY27, and the migration math masks underlying cloud momentum. A moat I can't confidently underwrite for 10 years gets a Narrow rating.

**Strengths:** Best-in-class 84.8% gross margin; revenue is ~100% recurring; net cash/debt-neutral; ROIC on *tangible* capital is enormous (invested capital ex-goodwill is roughly nil); FY26 was the first (barely) GAAP-profitable year.

**Serious blemishes:**
- **Free cash flow is shrinking while revenue grows 26%.** FCF fell from $1,416M (FY25) to $1,319M (FY26); margin collapsed from 32.5% to 20.1% in two years. That is a red flag, not noise.
- **SBC of $1,607M = 24.4% of revenue.** Deduct it from FCF and FY26 "owner earnings" are **negative (−$288M)**. Buybacks of $1.8B in FY26 (vs. FCF of $1.32B) exist almost solely to neutralize dilution — share count did fall 4.3% YoY, but the cash cost consumed all FCF plus $0.5B of the balance sheet.
- **FCF/net income conversion is uninterpretable** — net income is negative; the entire gap to "non-GAAP profitability" is stock compensation and acquisition amortization.
- **Balance sheet no longer a fortress:** cash fell $2.94B → $1.24B; net cash of +$1.27B became −$7M, funding ~$1.2B of M&A (DX, ~$1B, closed Jan 2026; The Browser Company/Arc-Dia) plus buybacks. Goodwill + intangibles are now **$2.73B, 45% of total assets**. Retained earnings −$6.1B.

### 3. Valuation Analysis — Intrinsic value **~$185/share** (range $155–$235)

Q1 FY27 showed the tension precisely: a 16% EPS beat ($1.65 vs. $1.43), 24% revenue growth, 26% FCF margin — and the stock fell ~16% because FY27 revenue growth is guided to **~13.75%**, implying only ~11% growth for Q2–Q4. The re-rating from $207 (late Oct) to $154 is a growth-expectations reset, not a business break.

**FY27E:** Revenue ~$7.48B; non-GAAP operating margin ~25.5% (guide) → ~$1.91B; non-GAAP EPS ~$6.00–6.30; FCF ~$1.7–1.9B (23–26% margin basis).

| Method | Output |
|---|---|
| FCF DCF (10-yr, 9.5% WACC, 4% terminal; treats SBC as non-cash) | ~$250/share |
| Owner-earnings DCF (FCF less SBC, SBC/rev fading to ~16%) | ~$150–165/share |
| Forward non-GAAP EPS $6.15 × 25–28x | $154–172 |
| EV/FCF 22x on FY27E $1.75B | ~$150 |

Multiples at $153.68: **~25x forward non-GAAP EPS · ~22x EV/FCF · 4.4% forward FCF yield · 38x book · EV/EBITDA meaningless (GAAP)**. Nothing here is distressed — this is a mid-20s multiple on a business whose true, SBC-loaded cash earnings are close to nil. Blending the SBC-honest and SBC-excluded methods gives a base intrinsic value of **~$185**, with an honest 30x-based bull case at $235 (premium multiple for a 25%+ grower exiting the Data Center drag) and a bear case near $120 if FCF margin stays at 20% and SBC doesn't come down.

**Current price = ~17% discount to base intrinsic value — short of the 20–30% margin of safety I require.**

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good, with one material governance flaw**

- **Founder-led since 2002** (Mike Cannon-Brookes, co-CEO with Scott Farquhar), founders remain among the largest shareholders — owner-operator orientation.
- **Operating execution is genuinely excellent:** six consecutive EPS beats, RPO +44%, Cloud +31%, non-GAAP margin expanding to 25.5% while absorbing an AI investment cycle and a pricing-model transition. Guidance is set conservatively.
- **Capital allocation is mixed.** No dividend; buybacks ramped $150M → $395M → $779M → $1.8B (FY23–FY26) — sensible as an anti-dilution tool. But M&A has turned acquisitive at the top of the AI narrative (DX ~$1B; The Browser Company), adding $1.2B of goodwill in one year and consuming the cash cushion. Paying up for an AI browser is outside the core moat.
- **The core flaw: SBC at 24% of revenue.** At $6.6B of revenue, that is ~$121K of stock comp per employee per year. It is the single reason a wide-moat, 85%-gross-margin asset produces zero GAAP profit and negative owner earnings. Management is reducing it (26.1% → 24.4%) — but far too slowly to matter yet.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

**Do not buy at $153.68; do not sell.** This is a high-quality, founder-led, 85%-gross-margin franchise with durable switching costs and a fresh, credible re-pricing mechanism (usage-based AI pricing) — but the price is *fair*, not cheap, and Buffett's rule is that a fair price is not a buy.

**Buy trigger: below ~$140** (≈25% discount to $185 IV), or evidence that SBC/revenue is falling below 20% while FCF margin recovers above 25%.
**Trim/sell trigger: above ~$235** (premium to the bull case).

The disciplined position is to hold any existing position and keep TEAM on a watchlist with a limit order. If forced to act at today's quote, I would not add capital.

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Stock-based compensation is the thesis risk, not growth.** At 24.4% of revenue, FCF is largely an artifact of adding back compensation. If SBC stays at this level, per-share owner earnings never compound and the "FCF DCF" of $250 is a mirage. My $185 IV already leans on SBC discipline materializing.
2. **AI disintermediation of seats.** Agentic coding assistants (Claude, Copilot, Cursor) plus nimbler tools (Linear, Notion) could compress human seat counts faster than usage-based AI revenue ramps. The 2027 deceleration may be partly structural, not just Data Center runoff.
3. **Data Center runoff ($-16% FY27) creating a growth air-pocket** — management has limited control over migration timing, and the market has shown it will not tolerate a 24% → 11% deceleration gracefully.
4. **FCF margin erosion.** 32.5% (FY24) → 20.1% (FY26). Sustained 20% margins permanently impair the valuation.
5. **M&A discipline / balance-sheet deterioration.** $2.7B of goodwill (45% of assets), cash down $1.7B in one year, net cash wiped out. A mispriced AI acquisition would be value-destructive with no cash buffer.
6. **Key-man and dual-CEO structure**, plus acquisition-driven complexity (Arc/Dia) that is hard to underwrite.
7. **Currency and enterprise IT budget cyclicality** — ~50%+ non-US revenue (Australian-domiciled); an enterprise software spending pause would hit the 11% guided growth immediately.

---

**Bottom line:** A genuinely good business at a not-quite-good-enough price. The 52% drawdown from the Feb-2025 peak has created the first interesting entry point since 2023 — but with owner earnings near zero after stock compensation, I want a 25% discount to my $185 estimate, not 17%. **Hold, with a buy limit near $140.**
