## BLOOM ENERGY CORPORATION (NYSE: BE) — ESG ANALYSIS
*Pricing/market data as of 2 Nov 2026 ($196.25); FY2025 financials ($2.02bn revenue, +37.3%); 10-K/10-Q/proxy disclosures*

### 2. Environmental Risk — **HIGH**
- **This is a fossil-fuel combustion-avoidance play, not a zero-carbon technology.** Bloom's own technical note puts emissions at **679–833 lb CO₂/MWh (308–378 kg/MWh)** vs **960 lb/MWh (435 kg/MWh)** for the conventional gas plants it benchmarks against. Against the *current* US grid (~370 kg/MWh), the advantage compresses toward single digits, and against new H-class combined-cycle gas (~400 kg/MWh) it is ~10–15%. Hydrogen and biogas are available but remain a small share; RNG and low-carbon H₂ cost and availability are the constraint.
- **Scale of the carbon liability is large and under-disclosed.** With ~1.8 GW deployed (1,100+ sites, 9 countries) running high capacity factors: **~4–5 Mt CO₂/yr of product-use emissions**. The planned **2.45 GW Oracle "Project Jupiter"** campus converts entirely to Bloom SOFCs: my estimate **~6–7 Mt CO₂/yr** incremental — more than Bloom's entire cumulative "avoided emissions" claim of **7.8 Mt CO₂e since 2011** (Ramboll-verified). In other words, **gross product-use CO₂ is already ~10x the cumulative avoided figure**; the avoided-emissions framing silently assumes a high-carbon counterfactual that is disappearing. **Scope 3 is explicitly "Not reported."** That is the single most important environmental disclosure gap and it is the one that matters most.
- **Permitting and physical/environmental-justice exposure is now the binding constraint**, not technology: the Oracle campus air-quality permit (Permit 10883) is contested by New Energy Economy; separately, the wave of litigation over unpermitted gas generation behind AI data centres (Earthjustice/NAACP actions against xAI's Memphis/Southaven gas plants, where a Reuters analysis found pollution burdens falling disproportionately on Black communities) defines the reputational and political weather for the whole sector. I could not confirm Bloom's direct role in the xAI litigation — treat as sector-level contagion, not established company exposure.
- **Credit where due:** no-combustion SOFCs genuinely cut NOx/SOx vs. diesel or older gas; on-site generation avoids transmission losses and land use; verified annual GHG inventory plus third-party verification of the avoided-emissions methodology is better practice than most Industrials peers.

### 3. Social Risk — **MEDIUM**
- Positive: small, high-skill workforce (2,214); no significant disclosed turnover, union conflict or material safety event; mission-driven hiring.
- Negatives: (i) **community licence** — fuel cells sited at schools and in dense communities have drawn persistent local opposition dating to 2019 (Brookside Elementary and the NBC Bay Area finding that Bloom quietly revised its emissions estimates); (ii) **supply-chain labour/traceability**: the 2025 Impact Report states Bloom is "unaware of any negative environmental or human-rights impacts in the supply chain" while its scandium route runs through a China-owned Thai subsidiary established (per Chinese filings) to avoid US tariffs — a statement that is not credible on its face; (iii) **product safety/performance** — fuel-cell lifetimes and output claims are contested by critics, and reliability promises sit behind critical-infrastructure customers.
- No material consumer-protection or discrimination exposures identified.

### 4. Governance Quality — **4 / 10**
- **Founder control:** Dr. K.R. Sridhar is simultaneously **Founder, Chairman and CEO**; a lead independent director exists (board includes John Chambers and Jeff Immelt) and the dual-class structure was collapsed in **July 2023** (all Class B converted, one vote per share) — genuine improvements.
- But: **classified/staggered board** persists; 2026 proxy proposes **officer exculpation** and cleanup of Class B references; PSU **maximum payout raised to 200%** and annual incentive cap from 1.5x to 2x on a 291% share-price year — comp design tracks revenue/margin growth but the payout architecture is now highly levered to a momentum share price.
- **Accounting/disclosure reliability is the core problem.** Three CFOs in 24 months (Berenbaum → Kurzymski (acting) → Edwards, Apr 2026). A securities class action (class period Feb 2025–Jul 2026) alleges repeated statements that the supply chain "is not dependent on China" and "there is no China supply chain for us" while scandium reached Bloom through Thai/Japanese/Korean intermediaries; the corrective disclosure cost **5.7% in a day ($15.28)**. Hunterbrook's 30 Oct 2026 follow-up alleges the route quietly restarted in Sept–Oct 2026 with sender/recipient fields blanked on bills of lading. Prior history: 2019 short report and subsequent restatement-related investor suit settled for ~$3m; related-party balances of **~$91–94m** add disclosure complexity.
- Regulator/environmental enforcement: **~$1.16m EPA RCRA hazardous-waste recordkeeping penalty**; Violation Tracker shows ~$210k environmental and $70k wage-and-hour penalties. Small dollars, but a pattern of compliance-process weakness.
- Auditor: Deloitte & Touche (clean); no going-concern or NT filing issues in the period reviewed.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure
- **US federal/state:** Clean Air Act permitting (every large campus); California **SB 253/261** climate disclosure (Bloom is a >$1bn-revenue California company — Scope 1/2 and eventually Scope 3, which it does not report); EPA GHG reporting; SEC climate rule stayed (limited current exposure).
- **Trade/sanctions:** China's **rare-earth export-control regime (scandium compounds added to controls in 2025)**, Section 301 tariffs, UFLPA/customs forced-labour scrutiny, and the newly tariff-motivated Thai routing route are the real ESG-adjacent financial exposures here — a customs penalty or a supply interruption is a plausible low-probability/high-severity event.
- **EU:** CSRD likely not yet triggered on EU turnover, but EU customers and the Net-Zero Industry Act create Scope 3 data demands; **CBAM** is not directly applicable to fuel cells (indirect steel/Ni exposure only).
- **Incentive dependence:** 45V hydrogen and 48E/ITC timelines are a material revenue lever; the 2025 federal rollback of clean-energy credits shortens the policy runway for the hydrogen/biogas optionality. *(Status should be re-verified with tax counsel.)*
- **Green-finance:** $2.2bn zero-coupon convertible and $600m revolver; no publicly disclosed ESG-linked margin ratchets identified, so no direct covenant trigger.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **LAGGARD** (narrow, and governance-driven)
Environmentally it is **better than a diesel or uncontrolled gas alternative but plainly a fossil-fueled generator** whose externalities scale linearly with the backlog. Socially it is unremarkable and clean. The rating is set by **governance and disclosure integrity**: a live fraud-on-the-market action whose central allegation is that management misstated supply-chain exposure, three CFOs in two years, a combined Chair/CEO, a staggered board, an assurance gap between the sustainability report ("no negative supply chain impacts") and the trade data, and a habit of revising emissions claims under scrutiny. For a company asking to be valued at ~140x trailing earnings and recently added to the S&P 500, that is the difference between a clean-energy compounder and a narrative stock.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE (risk overlay, not thesis-breaking)**
- **Do not pay a "green premium" for BE.** On my numbers its product is ~10–20% lower-carbon than new gas generation and *worse* than the decarbonizing grid it displaces — the equity should be underwritten as an **AI-power capacity-growth story with embedded fossil-fuel and political risk**, not as transition-aligned exposure.
- **The two asymmetries that matter:** (1) **permitting** — every incremental GW now depends on winning contested air permits in a politically hostile climate-data-centre environment; (2) **scandium/China** — an unhedged single-input dependency that management has now been accused twice, in public filings, of misrepresenting. Either can compress the multiple far faster than a demand miss.
- **Beta 3.78 and a 43% drawdown from the June 2026 high (~$346 → $196)** confirm the market prices this as a high-duration, high-touch narrative. ESG factors here raise the required return and argue for a **discount to the growth-equity peer group**, not a premium.
- **What would change the view:** audited **Scope 3** disclosure with a stated gas/RNG/H₂ mix; independent verification of scandium provenance (including "BE PALLET" routing); separation of Chair and CEO; CFO tenure >24 months; and a permit win at Project Jupiter without litigation delay. Absent those, treat ESG as **neutral-to-negative on valuation and a genuine tail-risk source** — and for ESG-mandated portfolios, **exclude** on the grounds that the "clean" classification does not survive a Scope 3 test.
