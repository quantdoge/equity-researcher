# Forensic Short-Seller Analysis: Bloom Energy (BE)

**Price $196.25 (Nov 2, 2026) | Market cap ~$58.0B | Diluted mkt cap ~$63.6B (324.3M sh) | Beta 3.78 | Sector: Industrials / Electrical Equipment**

Framing note: the dataset extends through Q3 FY2026 (filed 2026-10-22). This is not the loss-making Bloom of 2022–24 — it is a hyper-growth, GAAP-profitable, S&P 500 constituent trading at ~15x sales and ~60x forward non-GAAP EPS. The bear case has to be rebuilt for that reality, not recycled.

## 1. Earnings Quality Assessment: **Medium** (not Low, not Fraudulent)

The mechanical manipulation toolkit comes up **clean**, and intellectual honesty requires saying so up front:

| Test | Result | Read |
|---|---|---|
| Beneish M-Score | **-2.523** ("Low risk") | Below both -2.22 and -1.78 thresholds |
| DSRI (receivables index) | **0.834** | Receivables *lagging* revenue — anti-channel-stuffing |
| Accruals ratio | **-0.046** | Negative accruals (conservative) |
| Inventory days | 163 (2025) vs 186 (2024), 161 (2023) | Stable/falling — no demand-weakness signal |
| Receivables vs revenue | AR +14.5% vs revenue +37.3% (2025) | **No red flag** |

DSO is ~19 days on Q3'26 revenue of $1,236M. That is genuinely tight — Bloom is not stuffing distributors.

**But the accrual-based tests are the wrong lens for this business.** The quality problem sits in the cash conversion and in the revenue *counterparty*:

- **9M FY26: net income $517.2M → operating cash flow $272.9M → FCF $41.7M.** FCF conversion of net income: **8%**. Cash conversion: 53%.
- **Q3'26 alone: net income $250.3M, operating cash flow -$27.1M.** A $356M working-capital drain at the moment of "record" profitability.
- **Q4'25: OCF +$421M on net income of $1.1M** (a +$234M WC inflow). Full-year 2025 OCF was only $114M, or 5.6% of revenue. So cash generation arrives in violent, quarter-specific swings — consistent with customer deposits/prepayments and year-end collections, not with a steady earnings-to-cash engine.
- **Capex inflecting hard:** $153.5M in Q3'26 alone vs $56.8M for all of FY2025. FCF may go negative in Q4'26.
- **SBC is real and excluded:** $57.2M in Q3'26 (~$210M/yr). Non-GAAP operating income $285.7M vs GAAP $225.4M — a 26% uplift. Non-GAAP EPS $0.94 vs GAAP $0.78.
- **New contra-revenue asset:** a **"Customer Consideration Asset" of $215.5M** — "upfront share-based consideration payable to a customer's customer," amortized as a *reduction of revenue*. Bloom is paying equity to induce purchases and capitalizing the giveaway. ~7% of 9M26 revenue. This is economically a discount dressed as an asset, and it implicates revenue *quality* even where the arithmetic is clean. (Note: this line appeared as goodwill/intangibles at 6/30/26 in the data and zero at 9/30/26 — flag for disclosure inconsistency.)

## 2. Key Red Flags

1. **Circular revenue concentration.** Three customers = 43%, 13%, 12% of FY2025 revenue; the 43% customer is a **related party** (JV/equity-method structure). Hunterbrook alleges **74% of Q4'25 revenue ($574M of $778M) came from Bloom part-owned JVs with Brookfield**, with footnotes conceding the counterparty "may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user." Growth is being financed by a partner, not consumed by an end customer.
2. **Backlog vs RPO gap of ~40x.** Unaudited $20B "backlog" vs **RPO of $492.6M** (3/31/26) — 2.5% coverage. Among 10 peers the widest comparable gap is ~2x. Backlog reportedly includes service revenue customers aren't obligated to pay and **tax credits not granted**.
3. **Flagship projects slipping.** Oracle's Project Jupiter: no approved air permit, no gas pipeline; SemiAnalysis moved first power from 2027 to 2029. AEP's $2.65B order moved from "end-2028" to "no later than 2030," backstopped by an unnamed hyperscaler that can walk by covering costs +~10%.
4. **Supply-chain disclosure gap.** CEO stated at least five times (Feb 2025 through a June 10, 2026 WSJ stage interview) that Bloom has "no China supply chain" and is "not dependent on China for scandium." Trade data, Chinese corporate filings and supplier statements indicate four China-linked routes, with Hunan Oriental Scandium describing itself as Bloom's largest supplier and material "not exported directly." Bloom's July 7 blog post **did not repeat the flat denial**, conceding scandium plays "an important role" and that Bloom is the world's largest consumer. That is a quiet walk-back of a load-bearing claim.
5. **Scandium physics.** Hunterbrook's model: ~220t Sc₂O₃ needed for 5GW vs ~240t global supply against ~310t total demand (Lockheed/F-35 included). Bloom's own "25GW" claim implies ~620t — ~10x 2025 world consumption.
6. **Pattern of substitute financiers.** Delaware ratepayers locked in for 20 years at >5x original cost estimate; SK ecoplant propped two years of results then received three extra compliance years, then sold its stake — the same month Bloom formed the Brookfield JVs.
7. **Dilution.** Shares: 173M (2021) → 240M (2025) → **295M basic / 324M diluted (Q3'26)**; +23% in nine months. Convert-heavy debt of $2.78B at very low coupons (Q3'26 interest only $7.5M vs $14.4M/qtr in 2025) implies conversion at higher prices = structural supply.
8. **Extreme price/flow dependence.** Beta 3.78; 2026 range $119.51–$345.85. Peak-to-trough drawdowns of -65% (Jan–Mar) and -53% (Jun 22–Jul 29). S&P 500 inclusion in Q3'26 supplied passive demand that is unrelated to fundamentals.

## 3. Beneish & Accruals (detail already tabled)
M-Score -2.523 with an SGI of 1.373 correctly flags the growth, but GMI 0.946 (gross margin improving) and TATA -0.046 neutralise it. **Conclusion: this is not an accrual-manufactured earnings story.** It is a *revenue-counterparty and cash-conversion* story.

## 4. Competitive Threat Assessment
- **Genuine moat:** speed-to-power. Turbine OEMs (GEV, CAT/Solar Turbines, RTX) are backlogged to 2029+, which is precisely why hyperscalers are paying Bloom a premium. Product revenue +179% YoY confirms real willingness to pay.
- **Fragility:** the moat is a *shortage arbitrage*. It closes when turbine slots free up, when gas pipelines/air permits jam (already happening at Project Jupiter), or when a cheaper SOFC (Ceres, FuelCell Energy, Doosan) matches stack life. Gross margin of 33.8% is the tell — this is a hardware business, not a software-like franchise, and 10–15 year service contracts are underwritten on a stack whose life depends on a rare earth Bloom may not be able to buy.
- **Valuation as the real competitor:** at 15x TTM sales and ~112x TTM GAAP earnings, the stock discounts flawless 5GW execution.

## 5. Management & Governance
- **Four CFOs since the start of 2024**, including ~a year with no permanent CFO — the same window in which the Brookfield structure was built. CEO never changed. That is a textbook sequencing red flag.
- **Insider activity is net selling.** Reported aggregate ~$68M sold; CEO ~$34M at $170; a Feb 24 Form 144 for 200k shares; a 300k-share gift to GRATs (Aug 31, 2026 — estate planning that typically precedes sales); CCO Aman Joshi sold 8,437 shares at $277 on Oct 1, 2026. No meaningful open-market insider buys found. *Caveat: my Form 4 tool returned filing metadata only, so direction/totals rest on secondary reporting, not primary verification.*
- Bloom filed an 8-K (7/9/26) "categorically rejecting" the short report's **financial results and accounting** claims, then published a blog post the day before publication that corroborated several supply-chain findings. Selective rebuttal is itself informative.

## 6. Short Thesis Summary
**The credible bear case is circularity plus valuation, not fraud.** Bloom's growth is substantially funded by a partner (Brookfield JVs, related party at 43% of revenue) that is itself betting on unbuilt AI campuses with a captive anchor tenant; the RPO of $493M is 2.5% of a $20B unaudited backlog; the two flagship projects behind that backlog have both slipped on permits and pipelines; the supply-chain independence claim at the core of the DoD/security narrative does not hold; and none of it converts to cash (8% FCF conversion at the peak of the cycle). If the 5GW rapture is real, scandium supply is the binding constraint. If it isn't, the last six quarters were financing dressed as demand.

**The bull case, stated fairly, is strong on current data:** revenue genuinely doubled, gross margin is up 453bp, GAAP operating margin is 18.2%, guidance was raised three times in 2026, receivables and inventory show no manipulation, cash is $2.49B with net debt of only $295M, and even the sell-side's 2030 EPS of $13.77 implies 14x. The Altman Z of 0.148 is a **book-equity artefact** (retained deficit -$3.47B against a $58B market cap) — there is no near-term solvency short here.

**Valuation math:** $196.25 = 60x FY26 non-GAAP EPS midpoint ($3.25), ~77x GAAP, 15x TTM sales, 23x *2028* consensus. A 50% haircut to 2028 estimates (circular revenue unwinding to end-customer volumes) gives ~$3.50–4.30 EPS; at 25–30x that is **$88–$129**, i.e. 35–55% downside. A genuine worst case (permit cancellation + partner retrenchment) supports $40–60.

## 7. Contrarian Rating: **SHORT** (moderate conviction — not "Strong Short")

**Why not strong:** the classic fraud toolkit (Beneish, accruals, DSRI, inventory days) explicitly clears Bloom, the balance sheet is liquid, the customer demand is partly real, and the Hunterbrook thesis has been public since July 8, 2026 — the stock fell ~53% into late July and has since rebuilt to $196, so the initial catalyst is spent. Index-inclusion flows and beta 3.78 make timing treacherous.

**Why still short:** the single most important number in the file is **8% FCF conversion on record GAAP earnings**, paired with a related-party at 43% of revenue, a 40x backlog-to-RPO gap versus a ~2x peer ceiling, a demonstrably inaccurate supply-chain claim, four CFOs in ~30 months, and persistent insider selling into strength. Those are quality-of-revenue facts, and quality of revenue is what re-rates first when growth decelerates.

**Position construction:** size for a 3.8-beta instrument, not a normal industrial. Prefer defined-risk structures (put spreads) over naked short given the -50%/+50% quarterly ranges. Primary catalysts: Q4'26 cash flow (capex step-up should turn FCF negative), 2027 guidance framing on Oracle/AEP timing, any air-permit ruling in New Mexico, and the first quarter where a Brookfield JV is *not* the marginal buyer of record.

**Reject-short-thesis trigger:** if Bloom demonstrates two consecutive quarters of >80% OCF/net income conversion *with* Brookfield-related revenue below 25% of total, the circularity argument breaks and the stock is a legitimate compounder at 25x 2028.
