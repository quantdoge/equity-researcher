# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Bloom Energy Corp. (NYSE: BE)
**Price $196.25 | Market cap ~$58.0B | Industrials / Electrical Equipment | 2,214 employees | Beta 3.78**
**Assessment date: early November 2026**

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **WEAK**

**Positives (structural):** ~90% independent board, fully independent committees, a designated Lead Independent Director, annual say-on-pay, and genuinely credible directors (Jeffrey Immelt, John Chambers, Michael Boskin, Mary Bush, Jim Snabe, Eddy Zervigon). Equity mix is 50% PSUs tied to growth/margin metrics — directionally shareholder-aligned.

**Why the rating is still Weak:**
- **CEO/Chairman duality.** K.R. Sridhar is Founder, Chairman *and* CEO — a single individual controls the agenda, the board and the narrative. No independent chair.
- **Dual-class legacy control.** Class B shares carry 10 votes each; the 2026 proxy carries a charter amendment merely to *remove* Class B references, confirming founder-affiliated voting power has long exceeded economic ownership. Insider economic stake is only ~6.9% and falling.
- **Extraordinary CFO churn.** Four finance chiefs since the start of 2024: Berenbaum (Apr-24→May-25), Kurzymski (interim, ~12 months), Edwards (from Apr-26), after Cameron. The ~year with *no permanent CFO* overlapped precisely with the construction of the Brookfield JV structure that now drives reported revenue. This is a serious control-environment signal.
- **Officer exculpation amendment** on the 2026 ballot — reduces personal accountability for the very executives named in pending litigation.
- **Compensation tied to growth/margin PSUs** in a company whose reported growth is heavily related-party — an incentive to book volume rather than end-customer adoption.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **HIGH**

- **EDGAR full-text search returns no SEC enforcement actions** against BE — the formal record is clean. That is the only clean datapoint here.
- **Securities class action actively proceeding.** Class period Feb 27, 2025 – Jul 8, 2026; defendants: Bloom, Sridhar, CFO Edwards, Kurzymski, Berenbaum. Core allegation: statements that Bloom had "no China supply chain" and was "not dependent on China for scandium" were materially false because scandium reached Bloom via Chinese suppliers/intermediaries. Lead-plaintiff deadline passed Sept 28, 2026; multiple firms (Levi & Korsinsky, Rosen, Kessler Topaz, Kaplan Fox, Glancy Prongay) are involved. Motion-to-dismiss outcome is the key catalyst.
- **Export-control / national-security exposure.** China added scandium to its national-security export-licence list in April 2025, giving Beijing case-by-case discretion over the material at the core of every Bloom stack — and scandium is also an F-35 input. Bloom has actively courted the DoD *on the basis of* China independence, which raises the stakes of any contradiction.
- **Customs/tariff exposure.** Chinese filings indicate supplier Three Circle established Glory Winner (Thailand) in 2019 explicitly "to mitigate the impact of the additional tariffs on fuel cell separator plates," relocating production. Combined with Hunterbrook's Oct 30, 2026 finding that September–October shipments arrived with **shipper and consignee names redacted**, this creates potential customs-misdeclaration/duty-evasion exposure beyond the securities issues.
- **Permitting risk on flagship projects.** Oracle's Project Jupiter (New Mexico): original gas plant cancelled May 2026 and replaced with Bloom fuel cells, but the air permit remains contested and unapproved, opponents are litigating, the project has been downsized to ~1,600 MW, and the New Mexico Ethics Commission has questioned Oracle's $3.4M of advocacy advertising. Analysts have pushed first-power expectations from 2027 to 2029 and beyond.
- **Contract slip risk (quasi-regulatory/counterparty):** the $2.65B AEP order has reportedly shifted from "end-2028" to "no later than 2030," backstopped by an unnamed hyperscaler with a cost-plus walk-away.

## 3. Insider Activity Analysis: **HEAVILY NEGATIVE**

- **Trailing 12 months: ~853,820 shares sold for ~$165.2M on the open market; ZERO open-market purchases by any insider.** Eleven separate insiders sold; none bought.
- Largest sellers: **Shawn Marie Soderberg ~$48.4M** (252K shares, last sale Sept 16, 2026); **CEO/Chairman Sridhar 200,000 shares at ~$170 (~$34M)** in Feb 2026; **CCO Aman Joshi** 8,437 shares at $277.17 on Oct 1, 2026 (near the top of the range).
- Q3 2026 alone: net **−89,600 shares / −$21.95M**; insider share count down 1.79% QoQ and 4.59% YoY; insiders received 412.6K shares via awards and gifted away 307.5K shares (including Sridhar's 300,000 shares to GRATs on Aug 31, 2026 — estate planning, but still a supply overhang).
- **Read-through:** management sold aggressively into strength ($170–$277) while publicly insisting the China-supply narrative was intact. Zero insider buying during a 44% drawdown from the June high of $351.28 is the loudest possible signal of internal conviction.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Scandium single-point-of-failure.** Scandium is the electrolyte dopant; Bloom's own patents say reducing it accelerates stack degradation and undermines the 10–15 year service contracts that underpin recurring revenue. There is no commercially viable substitute and essentially no Western processing capacity. Hunterbrook's model shows ~220t needed for the 5GW ramp against ~240t global supply and ~310t total demand (Lockheed's F-35 included) — implying shortage by 2028. September–October 2026 data shows the Thailand channel (Glory Winner / Three Circle) **reactivated after a nine-month dormancy**, ~20 tonnes in 28 packages. The ramp is constrained by physics and geopolitics, not demand.
2. **Customer and related-party concentration.** 74% of Q4 2025 revenue ($574M of $778M) came from Brookfield joint ventures Bloom part-owns; Bloom's own footnotes concede the counterparty "may be a project-finance affiliate rather than the ultimate end user." Q1 2026 related-party revenue was $373.3M. Oracle-linked customer consideration asset is $306.5M. The AI-factory anchor tenant (Radiant) is itself a Brookfield captive with no publicly named CEO or disclosed customers — a circular financing structure that will unwind badly if hyperscaler capex pauses.
3. **Working-capital and revenue-recognition quality.** Q3 2026: revenue $1.236B (+138%), adj. operating margin 23.1%, but **free cash flow −$181M** and capex $153M; contract assets doubled to $858.6M and receivables rose to $634.7M. Bloom markets an **unaudited $20B "backlog"** against **auditor-reviewed RPO of just $492.6M** — a >40x gap versus a ~2x maximum across ten peers. The backlog includes non-obligatory service revenue and unlegislated tax credits.
4. **Key-person dependency.** Sridhar is the founder, public face, technical architect and the origin of the contested supply-chain claims. There is no visible succession plan; his credibility is now itself an asset at risk.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH / Deteriorating**

- Two Hunterbrook Media reports (Jul 8, 2026, "Bloom's Big Lie"; Oct 30, 2026, "Chinese Scandium Supply Route Restarts") with a disclosed short position, amplified by Barron's, Benzinga, Yahoo Finance and a widening pool of plaintiff firms.
- Weakest possible response pattern: no 8-K, no press release, no denial of specific facts — instead a single July blog post by the COO that **confirmed** scandium's "important role" and Bloom's status as the world's largest consumer, softened the China claim to "no single country can determine our destiny," and was the only blog post in company history to carry a securities disclaimer.
- Market vindication is visible: −44% from the June 2026 high of $351.28; −11% in a single session on cash-burn concerns; and Bloom is now traded as a proxy for "AI infrastructure debt" sentiment rather than on fundamentals.
- **ESG/greenwashing overhang:** natural-gas-fed fuel cells marketed on decarbonisation, on top of the long-running Delaware ratepayer controversy (costs exceeding original estimates by >5x, ~600 jobs short of projections) and the SK ecoplant episode (volume commitments propped up results, then a three-year compliance extension granted as Korean demand stalled — followed by SK selling its stake just as Bloom's Brookfield JVs launched). This is a recurring "find an intermediary to book the bet as growth" pattern that rating agencies and ESG screens will eventually price.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical — the technology is real, deployed capacity is 1.8GW, Q3 margins expanded materially, the board is majority-independent, liquidity was bolstered by $2.2B of 0% converts plus a $600M revolver, and no SEC/DOJ enforcement action is on record. But the concentration of governance power, the credibility of management's central supply-chain representation, related-party-driven revenue, a 40x backlog/RPO gap, negative free cash flow, four CFOs in three years, and $165M of insider selling with zero buys compound into a risk profile that is decisively above sector norms.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Potential securities fraud — CEO statements vs. trade data.** Documented, repeated, on-the-record denials of China scandium dependence (Feb 2025, Apr 2025, Semafor Sept 2025, WSJ June 2026) contradicted by bills of lading, Chinese corporate filings and supplier statements. Live class action; **the unresolved question is whether the SEC or DOJ opens a parallel inquiry**, particularly given the F-35/national-security dimension and Bloom's DoD overtures. Escalate to legal/special situations.
2. **Customs exposure.** Redacted shipper/consignee fields on resumed China-linked shipments, plus a supplier that publicly relocated to Thailand to avoid US tariffs on fuel cell plates. Potential duty-evasion and false-statement exposure independent of the securities case.
3. **40x backlog-to-RPO gap.** An unaudited $20B marketing backlog against $492.6M of auditor-reviewed obligations, containing unearned service revenue and unapproved tax credits. Treat any backlog-derived revenue model as unreliable until reconciled.
4. **Circular related-party revenue.** Brookfield JV revenue at 74% of a quarter, with the company's own footnote flagging that the buyer may not be the end user, and the anchor tenant being a Brookfield captive anchored by Nvidia. Watch contract-asset conversion and days-sales-outstanding each quarter.
5. **Finance-function instability.** Four CFOs since 2024 and a 12-month interim period spanning the creation of the related-party structure — a classic conditions-for-fraud indicator requiring auditor (Deloitte) rotation and audit-committee scrutiny.
6. **Insider selling into a falling tape.** $165M sold, $0 bought, over 12 months, including sales at $277 on Oct 1 2026 — three weeks before a 30% drawdown.
7. **Officer exculpation charter amendment** — reduces recovery prospects for shareholders in the pending action. Recommend voting against.
8. **Permitting single-thread risk.** Project Jupiter's unapproved air permit and absent gas pipeline sit beneath a meaningful share of the growth narrative.

**Monitoring priorities (next 90 days):** class-action motion-to-dismiss ruling; disclosure of any SEC subpoena or informal inquiry in the next 10-Q; resolution of the New Mexico air-permit hearing; whether the Thailand channel remains active in November–December trade data; Q4 contract-asset and DSO trends; and any Form 4 purchase by any officer or director.
