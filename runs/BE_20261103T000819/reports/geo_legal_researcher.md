## Bloom Energy (BE) — Geopolitical & Legal Risk Assessment

**Bottom line:** Bloom's *demand-side* geography has become remarkably favorable — the US is now ~90% of revenue — but its *supply-side* geography is a single-point geopolitical failure mode (scandium, China). Legal risk is elevated and live: a securities class action, derivative suits, and an unresolved short-seller campaign centred on Chinese supply-chain disclosure. Overall: **High**.

### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**Risk 1 — China scandium/critical-minerals chokepoint (Probability: High / Impact: Very High)**
Bloom's solid-oxide stack depends on scandium-doped electrolyte; China dominates global scandium processing. Beijing's April 2025 and October 2025 rare-earth export-control packages (covering scandium-group elements) remain the binding constraint, with only a one-year pause under Announcement No. 70 running to **10 November 2026** and no announced extension. Hunterbrook Media's 8 July 2026 report ("Bloom's Big Lie") alleged Bloom obscured a Chinese scandium supply route via a partner and inconsistent disclosure; the stock fell ~12%, and a **follow-up report on 30 October 2026** ("Bloom: Chinese Scandium Supply Route Restarts") restated the thesis. Bloom filed an 8-K on 9 July 2026 calling the claims "false and misleading" and asserting scandium supply visibility. This is *the* unresolved question in the name — and it is a geopolitical dependency, not merely an accounting dispute.

**Risk 2 — US energy-policy reversal / tax-credit dependence (Probability: Medium / Impact: High)**
The OBBBA compressed Section 48E clean-electricity credit timelines; fuel cell property beginning construction after 31 Dec 2025 is fixed at a 30% credit with no bonus adders, and the credit was placed on an accelerated elimination path (construction-start deadlines). Bloom's **own $20bn backlog definition "reflects anticipated ITC and other tax incentives"** — i.e., part of the marketed backlog is unlegislated policy. A further legislative or IRS-guidance tightening, or a change in the "beginning of construction" safe harbour, hits both demand economics and backlog credibility.

**Risk 3 — South Korea regulatory/auction and trade exposure (Probability: Medium / Impact: Medium)**
Korean fuel-cell demand is set by a government-run hydrogen power bidding market (2026 volumes ~500 GWh/yr for clean hydrogen power per the Korean published notice). Bloom has previously flagged volume impact from Korea's shift to this bidding structure. Layered on top: a 15% US tariff framework under the July 2025 Korea Strategic Trade & Investment Deal, and Korea's October 2026 formalisation of import/export restriction procedures in response to "unfair foreign trade measures" — a reciprocal-escalation channel. Competitive pressure from Doosan (Ceres-licensed SOFC) adds a domestic-champion dynamic.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Securities class action (active).** Filed in the wake of the 8 July 2026 investigative report; class period **27 Feb 2025 – 8 Jul 2026**; lead-plaintiff deadline **28 September 2026** (Rosen, Kessler Topaz, Levi & Korsinsky, Kaplan Fox, Federman alerts). Allegations: false/misleading statements about supply chain and financial results.
- **Derivative litigation / shareholder investigations** (Bragar Eagel & Squire, et al.) targeting officers and directors for the same conduct — the derivative track typically outlives the securities case.
- **10-Q/A for Q2 2026 (filed 29 July 2026)** — amendment to correct "three months/six months" transposition in the concentration-of-risk note. The corrected note discloses **one non-related-party counterparty at ~73% of Q2 2026 revenue**, and for the six-month period two counterparties at **44% and 21%, the latter a related party**. Benign on its face, but it is now a documented amendment immediately following a short report — plaintiff-friendly.
- **Contract-structure scrutiny.** The "customer" definition in Bloom's concentration disclosure permits the counterparty to be a **project-finance affiliate rather than an end user**; disclosure points to the **Brookfield-affiliated financing framework (up to $5bn over five years)**. Legal/accounting risk here is presentational and reputational rather than fraudulent, but it is the axis on which the class action turns.
- **Regulatory:** no DOJ/SEC/FTC enforcement action identified in available sources. Subject to export-control (BIS), FCPA/anti-corruption, and sanctions compliance obligations standard for a US manufacturer with Asian sourcing — no violations disclosed.
- **IP:** no material litigation identified. Note Doosan's Ceres Power licence and SK ecoplant's parallel data-centre MOUs as competitive-intelligence risk, not IP claims.

### 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Beneficial-ownership opacity in the scandium chain** — the Hunterbrook allegation is fundamentally a *supply-chain transparency* allegation (partner obfuscating Bloom's role in a Chinese securities filing). If accurate even partially, it implicates **Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act (UFLPA) traceability and Section 232/301 origin-declaration** exposure, beyond securities law.
2. **Tax-credit claims embedded in marketed backlog** — incentive representations that have not been granted create disclosure-control risk.
3. **Related-party revenue (21% of 6M 2026)** combined with a co-owned financing vehicle — a related-party-transaction governance flag for the audit committee and proxy.
4. **Customer concentration at 73%** — an existential disclosure-control and revenue-recognition-quality issue, not a compliance breach per se.
5. **Korea auction/tariff interplay** — low corruption risk, but the October 2026 Korean export-restriction formalisation creates a policy-retaliation channel for a company importing Korean-sourced content.

---

### 5. Overall Rating: **HIGH**

Split character: **Geopolitical risk — Medium** (demand concentrated in the US, arguably a geopolitical *advantage*; no Russia, Iran, or sanctioned-market revenue), **Supply-chain geopolitical risk — Very High** (China scandium), **Legal/regulatory risk — High** (live class action + derivative suits, tax-credit-policy dependence, 73% customer concentration, and an unresolved disclosure controversy that no company rebuttal has closed).

**Monitoring triggers:** (a) 10 November 2026 expiry of China's rare-earth export-control pause; (b) any IRS guidance on 48E "beginning of construction" for fuel cell property; (c) motion-to-dismiss ruling in the securities class action; (d) new Hunterbrook disclosures or any regulator opening a supply-chain inquiry; (e) Korea's 2027 hydrogen auction volumes.
