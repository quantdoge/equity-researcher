# Macroeconomic Assessment — Micron Technology (MU)

**As of 26 November 2026 | Price $601.25 | Market cap ~$680B | Beta 2.37**

---

## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: Transitional — a late-cycle reflation boom colliding with a hawkish policy pivot and a violent de-rating of long-duration AI equities.**

The US economy is not slowing — it is re-accelerating. Real GDP grew **4.3% SAAR in Q3 2026** ($24,665B chained), up from 2.2% in Q2 and 2.5% in Q1, with YoY growth of **2.3%**. Nominal consumer spending (PCE) is running **+6.4% YoY** (Oct-26 $22,562B vs Oct-25 $21,196B). Unemployment has *fallen* to **4.1%** from 4.5% a year ago.

That strength has re-ignited inflation. Headline CPI rose **+0.35% m/m in October** after +0.65% in September and +0.40% in August — a **3-month annualised rate of 5.7%**, versus 3.1% over six months. Headline CPI is **3.82% YoY**. The Fed, having cut to a 3.63% trough in mid-2026, has **resumed hiking**: the effective funds rate is **3.88%** (Oct-26) and markets price a **~97–98% probability of a hike at the 9 December FOMC**.

The bond market has repriced violently. The 10Y peaked at **5.31%** on 5 October and sits near **4.91%**; the 2Y–10Y spread is **+39bp** — a bear-steepening, not a recession signal. The 2Y at 4.74% implies the market expects roughly **86bp of further tightening**, i.e. a terminal rate near 4.75%. Meanwhile high-yield spreads have widened from **2.65% (11 Sep) to 3.72% (24 Nov)** — a 107bp deterioration in ten weeks that is the clearest sign of risk appetite cracking. The broad dollar is **123.6**, up **4.7% since 10 September**, on rate differentials. And trade policy is re-escalating: new Section 301 tariffs on 14 partners (China and Vietnam at 20%) were proposed after the Supreme Court struck down the prior IEEPA tariffs.

The result: **risk-off within a boom.** The AI complex has shed roughly $8 trillion in market value; MU itself is **-46% from its 2 October peak of $1,108**.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

### POSITIVE

**Nominal demand and pricing power.** MU is an unusual inflation *beneficiary*: its output prices are rising. DRAM contract prices are forecast **+10–15% QoQ in Q4 2026**, and HBM4 pricing is reported up to **2.7x** for 2027. FY2026 revenue hit **$133.19B (+256%)**, FQ4 revenue **$54.23B at an 86.8% gross margin**, with **$43.97B operating cash flow**. FQ1-2027 guidance is **$61.5B revenue and $38.15 adj. EPS** — a ~$246B annualised run-rate.

**AI capex concentration.** Memory is projected to be **68% of major CSP capex in 2027**, and MU has sold out its 2027 HBM capacity. As long as hyperscaler budgets hold, MU's revenue is contracted and price-visible. A 4.3% SAAR economy with 4.1% unemployment supports PC, smartphone and auto end-demand alongside the datacentre build.

### NEGATIVE

**Discount-rate and valuation duration.** With beta of **2.37**, MU is among the most rate-sensitive large caps. The 10Y at 4.91–5.31% and a Fed re-hiking is precisely what triggered the 46% drawdown. Note the subtlety: at ~**4x forward and ~8x trailing earnings** ($74.33 FY26 diluted EPS), MU is *no longer* a high-multiple long-duration asset — the market has already compressed the multiple. What remains is **cycle-duration risk**: cheap on peak EPS is not cheap if EPS is peaking.

**Cost of capital for the customer.** The key transmission channel is not MU's own balance sheet but its buyers'. Higher rates raise the hurdle rate on hyperscaler 2027–28 capex; any 10–15% cut to AI capex budgets flips DRAM pricing negative. This is the core of the "2028 memory glut" fear that drove BNP Paribas's downgrade to Neutral with a **$1,700 → $775** target cut.

**Currency.** The broad dollar is **+4.7% in ten weeks**. MU's revenue is overwhelmingly non-US by ship-to (China $8.34B in FY26, +216%; Europe $1.09B, +175%; remainder largely Taiwan, Korea, Japan, SE Asia). Dollar strength is a translation headwind and, more importantly, a **competitive disadvantage** against Samsung and SK hynix, whose costs sit in a weakening won.

**Trade policy.** Proposed Section 301 tariffs (China/Vietnam 20%, stacked on ~25% legacy duties) plus ongoing US chip export-control debate create **cost inflation on inputs, assembly/test friction, and retaliation risk**. MU carries roughly **$14.6B of China-linked revenue** (~11% of the total) that is directly exposed to Beijing's discretion — a lever China has pulled before.

**Credit conditions.** The 107bp widening in HY OAS is an early-cycle warning. It does not threaten MU directly — debt fell from **$14.58B to $5.18B** in FY2026 and the company is net cash — but it tightens financing for the leveraged AI ecosystem that is MU's demand base.

### NEUTRAL
- **Yield curve (+39bp):** steepening is growth-positive near term but reflects term-premium and fiscal-supply risk (30Y 5.47%) rather than easing.
- **Labour market:** tight, but MU's input costs are capital and energy, not labour-intensive services.
- **Refinancing risk:** negligible. With $5.18B debt against $680B market cap and $44B quarterly operating cash flow, MU has essentially **no balance-sheet rate sensitivity** — a genuinely important differentiator versus rate-sensitive cyclicals.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL** (with unusually wide dispersion)

The demand channel is **favourable** — reflation, record nominal spending, and a sold-out HBM book. The valuation and policy channel is **unfavourable** — a Fed hiking into a boom, 5%+ long yields, a 4.7% dollar rally, and tariff re-escalation. These two forces are roughly offsetting in fundamental terms, but they are **not offsetting in risk terms**: the December calendar is a collision — the **9 December FOMC** (hike expected) sits eight days before the **17 December FQ1 print**. A hawkish hike into a peak-cycle earnings report is an asymmetric set-up.

---

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **Bear**

**A 5.7% three-month CPI run-rate forces the Fed past 4.75% by mid-2027.** With real fed funds at just +0.06%, the Fed has almost no room to be "restrictive" without going well above 4.25%. If the December hike is followed by two more and the 10Y breaks **5.75%**, the 2027 hyperscaler capex plans — which are increasingly debt-financed — get trimmed. DRAM contract prices, up 10–15% in Q4 2026, flip negative in H1 2027; NAND rolls over first (TrendForce already sees 2027 NAND declines as Chinese CSPs cut orders). MU's $38.15 quarterly EPS guide becomes the cycle peak, and a stock at "4x earnings" is really at 12x normalised earnings on the way down. At beta 2.37, the drawdown from $1,108 to $601 is roughly half-complete, not complete. **This is the reflexive trap: the cheaper it looks on peak EPS, the more the market is telling you EPS is peaking.**

*Counter-scenario (bull):* if the December hike proves terminal, tariff-driven inflation is judged transitory, and hyperscalers lift 2027 capex at the 17 December print, an 87%-gross-margin, sold-out, net-cash franchise at ~3x forward earnings re-rates 40%+ in weeks. The distribution is genuinely bimodal — which is itself the macro message.
