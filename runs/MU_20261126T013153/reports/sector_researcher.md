## SECTOR BRIEF — SEMICONDUCTOR MEMORY (Target: MU / Micron Technology)
*Prepared for investment memo | Data as of 25 Nov 2026 | Price $601.25, mkt cap $680.3B, beta 2.37*

---

### 1. Sector Overview

Micron competes in semiconductor memory — DRAM (incl. HBM) and NAND flash — the most violently cyclical sub-segment of the semis complex. The industry is a tight oligopoly: Samsung, SK hynix and Micron control roughly 90–95% of DRAM, implying a segment HHI in the ~2,800–3,000 range (my estimate from share data), with NAND more fragmented (Samsung, SK hynix/Solidigm, Kioxia, SanDisk, Micron, YMTC). Barriers to entry are extreme — leading-edge DRAM/NAND fabs require $20B+ per fab, EUV-heavy nodes are power-intensive, and IP/process know-how is concentrated — but the barrier works both ways: capacity, once committed, is irreversible, which is precisely why the industry historically oscillates between super-profits and wipeout losses.

The sector is currently in an **extraordinary AI-driven upcycle**. DRAM contract prices rose as much as 60–70% QoQ in 2Q26 and roughly doubled across 2026; HBM absorbs the highest-quality DRAM wafer output and packaging capacity, starving commodity DRAM of bits. Micron's own numbers describe the cycle better than any narrative: FY26 revenue **$133.2B, +256% YoY**, gross margin **80.7%**, operating margin **74.6%** — versus −9.1% GM and −37.0% OM just three years ago in FY23. That 115-point gross-margin swing in 36 months *is* the sector's defining characteristic.

**Cycle position: late-cycle boom / peak-earnings zone.** Evidence: margins above even Nvidia's; supply response now visible (capex rises, China's CXMT/YMTC ramping); BNP Paribas has flagged "oversupply scenario is mounting" and cut its MU target from $1,700 to $775; and MU equity has fallen ~50% from its June 2026 high of ~$1,211 despite record prints. The market is discounting a 2027–28 rollover while management insists 2027–28 will be *tighter* than 2026.

---

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **AI datacenter demand is structurally bit-hungry and contracted forward.** HBM demand exceeds supply; as of 18 Nov 2026 management stated **75% of 2027 supply is sold**, with negotiations extending into 2028. Sold-forward capacity converts spot-cycle revenue into multi-year take-or-pay-like visibility — a genuine structural change versus prior cycles.
- **Capacity diversion creates commodity scarcity, not just HBM upside.** Every wafer shifted to HBM removes standard DRAM bits from the market; suppliers have no incentive to flood commodity DRAM. NAND is similarly constrained — global MLC NAND capacity is forecast to fall >40% in 2026 as Samsung exits and Kioxia/SK hynix/Micron scale back, making legacy-node pricing scarcity-driven rather than cost-driven.
- **Disciplined supply and rising capex hurdles cap the next downturn's depth.** EUV-heavy DRAM and high-layer NAND fabs are power-intensive, interest rates and energy costs are elevated, and node scaling no longer delivers historical cost-per-bit gains. Industry commentary suggests *"old-world ultra-cheap memory pricing is very unlikely to return"* — i.e., a higher-lows cycle.
- (Supporting) **Supply-chain security premium:** Micron is the only US-headquartered memory producer, positioning it for CHIPS-era subsidies and hyperscaler diversification away from Korean/Chinese supply.

---

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Cyclical mean reversion is not repealed.** MU's own record shows a −37% operating margin in FY23 and +74.6% in FY26. With 2027–28 capacity partly pre-sold, incremental upside is capped while fixed-cost leverage reverses hard if pricing rolls — precisely the setup that compresses multiples before earnings peak.
- **Supply response and Chinese competition.** Rising capex across the big three plus CXMT/YMTC ramp (with export-control uncertainty) is the classic precursor to oversupply; sell-side has begun modelling it. High prices are already destroying consumer demand — PC/smartphone unit growth is flat-to-low-single-digit and OEMs are being squeezed.
- **Customer/capex concentration and policy risk.** Hyperscaler AI capex is the marginal buyer of both HBM and DRAM; a digestion pause or efficiency step-change hits pricing immediately. Add tariff/export-control shocks, extreme equity volatility (beta 2.37, −50% drawdown), and a cost base inflating with energy and leading-edge capex.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position: **STRONG (with cyclical caveat)**

- **Best margins in the sector**, not just among memory peers — 80.7% GM and 74.6% OM in FY26 exceed Nvidia (71.1%/60.4%), the sector's margin benchmark. Cost of revenue at 13% of revenue in FQ4 is the lowest cost-of-goods structure in the comparison set.
- **Share gainer in the highest-value pool.** Third in DRAM by share behind Samsung and SK hynix, but the fastest-improving in HBM, and the only US-domiciled supplier — a structural advantage as hyperscalers diversify supply for geopolitical reasons.
- **Visibility better than history:** 75% of 2027 sold, negotiations into 2028.
- **Why not "dominant":** smaller scale than Samsung/SK hynix, still an HBM follower rather than leader, and the current margin level is a *cycle artifact* — an 86.8% quarterly gross margin is not a sustainable structural margin in a commodity industry with three capable suppliers and Chinese entrants.

---

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

Unambiguously **attractive fundamentals** — AI-driven structural demand, oligopolistic pricing power, rising barriers, and a genuinely higher-lows cycle floor. But the **cycle position is late**, the supply response is underway, and the equity market is already discounting a 2027–28 normalization. For a memory/semis allocation I would call this Neutral with a positive structural bias: the right posture is to own the cost leader into the downturn, not to underwrite current margins as permanent. The swing factor to monitor is DRAM/HBM contract pricing in the next two quarters versus the 2027 supply commitments — a pricing plateau confirms the higher-lows thesis; a rollover validates the oversupply bears and the ~2–4x forward multiple is a value trap, not a bargain.
