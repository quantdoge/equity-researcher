## 4. Downside Scenario Analysis

Base FY2026: revenue $133.19B · gross margin 80.7% · opex $8.16B (derived as GP − EBIT) · D&A $9.50B · interest $0.11B · cash $43.43B.

| | Base | −20% Rev |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $133.2B | $106.6B |
| Gross profit | $107.5B | $69.3B |
| EBIT (opex $8.5B) | $99.3B | **$60.8B** |

| Net income (~25% tax) | $85.0B | **$51.0B** |
| Interest coverage | 937x | ~570x |
| Net debt / EBITDA | −0.35x | −0.52x |

Verdict: a −20% revenue decline is a **non-event** for solvency. The company remains wildly profitable, net cash, coverage above 500x.

**Scenario B — Severe memory downcycle (FY2023 analogue: revenue −49% YoY, GM −9%). Applied as revenue −50% to $66.6B, GM 20%, opex $8.5B**

- Gross profit $13.3B → EBIT **+$4.8B**
- D&A rises to ~$12.0B (new fab base) → EBITDA **+$16.8B**
- Interest coverage ~12x; net cash remains positive
- **Altman Z would stay above 1.81** given the equity base of $138B

Verdict: **still profitable at the EBIT line.** This is the crucial finding — the FY2026 margin structure is so extreme that even a halving of revenue with a collapse to 20% gross margin leaves positive earnings. Liquidity (~10 quarters of total debt in cash) is not at risk.

**Scenario C — Extreme stress: revenue −60% ($53.3B), GM 0%**

- EBIT **−$8.5B**; EBITDA **+$3.5B** (loss is largely non-cash D&A)
- Cash $43.4B vs debt $5.2B → **solvency intact**, but capex ($30.7B/yr) must be slashed and the dividend ($0.61B) suspended

**The real downside is equity, not credit.** At $1,028.84 and ~1.143B diluted shares, market cap ≈ **$1.18T** = 13.9x *peak* EPS of $74.33. On a normalized mid-cycle EPS of $15–20, that is **~50–70x**. A mean reversion to mid-cycle earnings with a 25x multiple implies a fair value in the $375–500 range — a **50–65% downside** consistent with the observed 51% max drawdown and the 57.6% 2024–25 realized decline.

## 6. Key Red Flags

1. **Peak-cycle earnings are the entire thesis.** Revenue went $15.5B (FY23) → $133.2B (FY26) = **8.6x in three years**, and the company posted a **−$5.83B net loss with −$6.12B FCF as recently as FY2023**. Nothing in the balance sheet protects the *equity* from that recurring pattern.
2. **Hyperbolic sequential revenue ramp is decelerating.** Q1 FY26 $13.6B → Q2 $23.9B (+75%) → Q3 $41.5B (+74%) → Q4 $54.2B (+31%). Growth is still enormous but the second derivative has turned. Q4 annualises to ~$217B versus a $133B FY26 — the forward comp is now very difficult.
3. **D&A understates the true cost base.** Capex/revenue of 23.1% against D&A/revenue of just 7.1% means ~$30B/yr of capex is not yet flowing through the P&L. As new fabs depreciate, D&A could roughly **triple toward $28–30B**, a structural headwind to EBIT even at flat revenue. Reported margins flatter true economics.
4. **Working capital is stretching.** Receivables $36.20B vs $9.27B (+291%) against revenue +256% → DSO **99.2 days vs 90.5** prior year. Inventory days **147 vs 135**. Not yet alarming, but in memory, receivables and inventory are the first place a downturn appears — and inventory of only 147 days means write-down risk if pricing breaks.
5. **Dilution — the one Piotroski miss (F7=0).** Shares grew modestly (1.116B → 1.127B weighted) despite $85B of net income and $1.78B of buybacks. Low severity, but capital allocation is not yet returning cash at scale.
6. **Valuation gives no margin of safety.** ~$1.18T market cap on peak FCF of $58.96B = **5.0% FCF yield** (the tool's 8.58% uses a stale ~$687B market cap and overstates the yield — see gaps).
7. **Macro discount-rate backdrop.** 10Y at **5.13%**, 30Y 5.47%, 2s10s +39bp (steepening, not inverted). Elevated long rates hit long-duration, capex-intensive compounders hardest and raise the hurdle for the $30.7B annual capex programme.
8. **Governance of the cycle, not leverage, is the constraint.** The forward risk is a capital-allocation error — building capacity into a peak — not a solvency event.
