# Micron Technology (MU) — Value Investment Analysis

**Price: $601.25** (Nov 25, 2026) | Market cap ~$687B | EV ~$649B | Net cash $33.2B | Beta 2.37
**Context:** Down ~46% from the Sept 25, 2026 intraday peak of $1,108 and ~45% from the Oct 1 close of $1,097. FY2026 (ended Sept 3, 2026) was the greatest year in the company's history.

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment

### Moat Rating: **NARROW** (currently widening, structurally fragile)

**The bull evidence for a moat:**
- **Oligopoly structure.** DRAM is a three-player market (Samsung, SK Hynix, Micron). NAND is somewhat more fragmented but consolidating. This is a genuine structural improvement over the 1990s/2000s.
- **Technological leadership in HBM.** Micron is a qualified HBM supplier to NVIDIA, and TrendForce projects HBM consuming 23% of DRAM wafer input in 2026 and ~30% in 2027. HBM4 leadership is a real intangible asset — qualification cycles are long and switching costs are high.
- **Demand visibility.** All three suppliers have reportedly sold out 2027 DRAM and HBM capacity; customers are receiving only 60–70% of requested allocations. SK Hynix says shortages persist through 2030. This is the strongest demand visibility in the industry's history.
- **Current returns.** FY2026 ROIC of 69.2%, ROE 61.4%, operating margin 74.6%.

**The decisive evidence against a durable moat:**

| Fiscal Year | Revenue | Gross Margin | Operating Margin | ROIC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2021 | $27.7B | 37.6% | 22.7% | — |
| 2022 | $30.8B | 45.2% | 31.5% | — |
| 2023 | $15.5B | **−9.1%** | **−37.0%** | **−9.9%** |
| 2024 | $25.1B | 22.4% | 5.2% | 2.2% |
| 2025 | $37.4B | 39.8% | 26.1% | 14.1% |
| 2026 | $133.2B | **80.7%** | 74.6% | 69.2% |

This table is the whole argument. **A wide-moat business does not post negative gross margins and a −9.9% ROIC in a downturn.** Micron lost $5.8B in FY2023 — three years ago. Four-year average ROIC is ~18.9%, but that average is manufactured by one extraordinary year; strip out FY2026 and the average is deeply negative.

The hallmark of a moat — ROIC above WACC *consistently, including in bad years* — is absent. What Micron has is **cyclical pricing power**, which is not the same thing. The 80.7% gross margin is not a moat; it is a shortage. Shortages end. That is what shortages do.

**Moat direction:** Widening today (HBM qualification, fixed-price contracts, sold-out capacity) but the widening is a function of an AI capex boom, not a permanent structural barrier. Samsung and SK Hynix are both building capacity. The moat will narrow when that capacity arrives.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

### The central problem
Micron trades at **8.1x trailing earnings, 6.0x EV/EBITDA, 11.7x FCF, and an 8.6% FCF yield** — a 73% discount to the peer median P/E of 30.5x. On its face this looks like the cheapest large-cap semiconductor in the world.

**It is not cheap. It is peak-cycle earnings being capitalized.** The 8.1x P/E is computed on $74.33 of EPS that will not recur. The proper question is not "what multiple of peak earnings?" but "what is normalized earnings power?"

### Normalized earnings reconstruction
Assume that by FY2029–2030 the AI buildout matures and supply catches up:
- Revenue settles at **$120–150B** (still 3–4x FY2025, reflecting structurally larger AI memory demand)
- Normalized gross margin **45–50%** (above the 35–45% historical range due to HBM mix and oligopoly discipline)
- Normalized operating margin **33–38%**
- Net income at $130B revenue × 48% GM → ~$42B; **EPS ≈ $37** on 1.15B shares
- At 12–18x → **$444 to $666 per share**

That range brackets the current $601 price. **On normalized earnings, Micron is roughly fairly valued — not cheap.**

### Scenario DCF (11% WACC, 2.5% terminal growth, 1.15B shares)

| Scenario | FCF Path | Intrinsic Value | vs. $601 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull — "structural change"** (25%) | FCF $95B→$105B→$95B→$90B→$90B, 4% terminal | ~$1,000/sh | +66% |
| **Base — normalization** (45%) | FCF $90B→$95B→$45B→$30B→$32B | ~$401/sh | **−33%** |
| **Bear — oversupply** (30%) | FCF $40B→−$5B→$10B→$25B→$30B | ~$252/sh | **−58%** |
| **Probability-weighted** | | **≈ $505/sh** | **−16%** |

Note: the tool's mechanical DCF returned $814B of enterprise value (~$713/share) — but it grows *peak* FCF at 5% forever. Applying a perpetual-growth model to the highest-margin year in a commodity's history is precisely the error that destroys capital in cyclicals. I weight it as my bull case, not my base case.

### Implied expectations
At $601, the market is capitalizing roughly **$505 of through-cycle value plus an option on the AI boom continuing**. To justify today's price on a DCF basis, you need FCF to hold near $90B+ for five years — i.e., you need the shortage to persist through 2031. That is possible but not probable, and it is not what I am paid to assume.

### The tell: analyst dispersion
Sell-side targets span **$775 (BNP Paribas, Nov 24) to $3,000 (D.A. Davidson, Oct 7)** — a 4x range. Goldman is Neutral at $1,250; Citi Buy at $1,300; CLSA $1,700. When the smartest analysts in the industry disagree by 4x, the honest conclusion is that **nobody can forecast this**, and Buffett's rule applies: no opinion is better than a false one.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment

**Grade: B / Adequate — strong operator, weak capital allocator**

**Positives:**
- **Sanjay Mehrotra (CEO)** is a credible, technically deep operator and co-founder of SanDisk. He has executed the HBM roadmap impressively and navigated the FY2023 trough without a dilutive equity raise.
- **Balance sheet discipline.** Building $43.4B of cash and cutting net debt to a net-cash position is genuinely good stewardship after FY2023.
- **Technology investment.** The $30.7B capex program is directed at HBM and advanced DRAM — the right places.

**Negatives:**
- **Procyclical buybacks — the classic cyclical sin.** Share repurchases: FY2022 $2.43B (at peak prices), FY2023 $425M, FY2024 $300M, FY2025 **$0**, FY2026 $1.78B. Cumulative ~$4.9B over five years against **$97B of cumulative net income**. They bought most when the stock was expensive and least when it was cheap. This is textbook value destruction.
- **Token dividend.** $0.15/quarter, a **0.09% yield**. With $59B of FCF and $43B of cash, returning $2.4B total to shareholders is indefensibly conservative. Management is hoarding cash for capex rather than optimizing per-share value.
- **Insider ownership** is modest for a company of this size; this is a professional-manager company, not an owner-operator business.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD — Do Not Initiate**

**For a new investor: AVOID. There is no margin of safety at $601.**

My probability-weighted intrinsic value is **~$505/share**, roughly 16% *below* the current price. Even the more generous 35/45/20 weighting yields ~$580 — still under $601. A Buffett-style buyer requires a 20–30% discount to intrinsic value; that would mean buying at **$350–$400**, not $601.

**Why Hold rather than Sell:** the near-term earnings momentum is genuinely explosive — Q1 FY2027 consensus is $61.8B revenue and $38.25 EPS, which annualizes to ~$247B revenue and ~$150 EPS. At $601 the stock trades at ~4x forward earnings. Shorting a company generating $90B of annual free cash flow with $33B of net cash and sold-out capacity through 2027 is a good way to be carried out. The balance sheet and demand visibility make an outright Sell too aggressive.

**What would change my mind:**
- **Price below ~$400** (−20% to −30% from here) — that would create genuine margin of safety on my base case.
- **Evidence the cycle is structurally dead** — e.g., multi-year fixed-price HBM contracts with escalators, sustained gross margins above 50% *through* a capacity addition cycle, or DRAM industry consolidation to two players. That would justify re-rating the moat to Wide and my base case toward the bull case.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Risks to owning it (bear case):**
1. **Mean reversion is near-certain.** 80.7% gross margins in DRAM have never persisted. The historical peak was ~58% (FY2018) and ~45% (FY2022). Both were followed by collapse.
2. **Oversupply from mid-2027.** BNP Paribas's Nov 24 downgrade (target cut $1,700 → $775) argues DRAM and NAND prices begin falling in mid-2027 as capacity arrives. This is the single most important swing factor.
3. **AI capex digestion.** Memory and storage are approaching ~40% of AI infrastructure spend. That share invites customer pushback — redesigns, longer qualification of second sources, and eventual pricing pressure. NVIDIA has publicly flagged the risk of memory cost inflation compressing its own economics.
4. **Customer concentration.** HBM demand is heavily concentrated in NVIDIA. A single design loss or allocation shift is material.
5. **Capital intensity cuts both ways.** $30.7B of capex is fine at 80% gross margins and ruinous at 30%. In FY2023, capex of $7.7B against $15.5B of revenue produced −$6.1B of FCF. Scale that to today's asset base and the downside FCF is very large.
6. **Valuation volatility.** Beta of 2.37 and a 46% drawdown in nine weeks. This is not a defensive holding.

**Risks to my "avoid" thesis (what makes me wrong):**
1. **The cycle may genuinely be dead.** If AI demand is structurally insatiable and the three-player oligopoly allocates capacity rationally rather than competitively, gross margins could hold in the 50–60% range for a decade. That would make $601 look cheap and my base case too harsh.
2. **Sold-out through 2027** with customers receiving only 60–70% of allocations means near-term earnings are locked in and rising. FY2027 EPS of $150+ would put the stock at 4x — a valuation that historically resolves upward.
3. **The balance sheet removes the bankruptcy risk** that made prior cycles catastrophic. Net cash of $33B means Micron can hold through a trough and buy back stock cheaply — which is exactly what it failed to do last time.

---

### Bottom Line
Micron is an excellent *company* at an extraordinary moment in its history, run by a competent CEO, with a fortress balance sheet and the best demand visibility it has ever had. But it is **not an excellent business** in the Buffett sense — it is a capital-intensive commodity producer whose returns on capital swung from −9.9% to +69.2% in three years. The 8x P/E is a peak-cycle artifact, not a bargain. At $601 the market is already paying full price for the shortage to persist through 2031.

**The stock is priced for the cycle to never end. It always ends. Hold — and wait for $400.**
