# Micron Technology (MU) — Geopolitical, Regulatory & Legal Risk Assessment
*Data as of Nov 25, 2026. FY2026 = year ended Sept 3, 2026.*

**China + Hong Kong = $14.63B (~11%) of revenue.** COO Manish Bhatia (Sept 30, 2026 call): exposure "will be in the single-digit range in fiscal '27" — management is actively de-risking.

**Manufacturing footprint** (front-end fabs in Taiwan, Singapore, Japan, US; new fabs under construction in Idaho, New York, Singapore): Hiroshima (Japan), Taichung + Tongluo (Taiwan), Singapore NAND center + **$24B/10-yr wafer fab (ground broken Jan-2026)** and **$7B HBM advanced-packaging plant**; US Idaho/New York (CHIPS-funded) plus Manassas, VA; assembly/test in Muar (Malaysia), Xi'an (China), and **Sanand, India (first production Mar-2026)**. Roughly two-thirds of front-end wafer capacity sits in Asia, with a material slice in Taiwan.

## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**#1 — US export-control escalation cutting off AI-memory demand channels. Probability: High. Impact: Medium-High.**
On **Nov 16, 2026** Commerce/BIS imposed new licensing requirements on advanced AI chip exports to **Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia and Brazil**, targeting overseas data centers Beijing could use to access restricted US AI compute (24/7 Wall St/Yahoo Finance, Nov 16, 2026); MU fell ~5.5% that session. This matters disproportionately because **Cloud + Core Data Center = 61% of FY2026 revenue**. Separately, Micron is *advocating* for tighter controls — it is lobbying Congress to pass the **MATCH Act** to close chip-tool export gaps to Chinese rivals (Reuters, Apr 22, 2026), a stance that raises retaliation risk against its China business. BIS also updated controls on advanced wafer-fab tools/services to certain China customers in 2026.

**#2 — China retaliation and critical-minerals leverage. Probability: Medium-High. Impact: Medium.**
Micron remains the only major memory maker formally sanctioned in China: the **Cybersecurity Review (May 2023)** found "serious" issues and barred critical-information-infrastructure operators from buying Micron products — a ban still in force — and Micron has exited China data-center sales. China's export controls on **gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials are only *suspended* until Nov 27, 2026** (Fastmarkets; Inside Trade) — a snap-back is a live near-term risk for compound semiconductor and packaging inputs. Beijing has also shown willingness to target single companies (Nexperia precedent). A renewed CAC/regulatory action or procurement ban on Micron's remaining China mobile/embedded business ($8.3B mainland) is plausible.

**#3 — Taiwan Strait / Taiwan-origin production concentration. Probability: Low near-term. Impact: Very High.**
Taiwan accounts for 12.7% of revenue by customer HQ, but the deeper exposure is physical: DRAM fab capacity in Taichung/Tongluo plus Taiwan-based assembly/test and a Taiwan-centric ecosystem (substrates, OSAT). Additionally, **Taiwan is tightening its own advanced-AI-chip export controls toward alignment with US rules (Oct-2026)** and concluded an investment/tariff deal (ART) with Washington — creating new licensing/compliance obligations on Taiwan-origin shipments to China. Blockade or quarantine scenarios are tail risks but would be existential for global memory supply.

**Also material — tariffs:** a **25% Section 232 tariff on certain advanced semiconductors took effect Jan 15, 2026** (White House Proclamation; EY/White & Case), and on **Nov 25, 2026** the administration proposed **Section 301 tariffs on 14 trading partners** (China and Vietnam at 20%) to rebuild the tariff agenda after the **Supreme Court struck down the IEEPA tariffs**. US-domiciled fabs (Idaho, NY) partly mitigate but do not eliminate exposure, since assembly/test and components are imported.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Netlist patent litigation — settled, but re-litigated.** A May 2024 E.D. Tex. jury awarded Netlist **$445M** against Micron. The parties then entered **license and settlement agreements: Micron pays $30M/quarter from Q4 2026 through Q3 2031, ~$600M total** (PR Newswire; Irell & Manella). Nonetheless, on **Aug 10, 2026 Netlist filed a *new* complaint against Micron and Micron Semiconductor Products**, and has opened a fresh **ITC** action (Yahoo Finance) — IP overhang persists despite the license.
- **DRAM antitrust price-fixing.** New class actions filed **June 25, 2026** in federal and California courts by 17 consumers/small businesses allege Samsung, SK hynix and Micron **coordinated to restrict commodity DRAM supply** as capacity shifted to AI memory (MoginLaw; Biggo); a separate purchaser class of 11 technology companies has been certified (Hagens Berman). This is a structural risk given 87% gross margins and tripled pricing — scrutiny of memory pricing is intensifying.
- **Securities class action** (2024–25 class period, alleging misleading demand statements) — historically low materiality but adds disclosure noise.
- **CHIPS Act conditions.** Up to **$6.4B in direct funding** ($6.165B + $275M) for Idaho/NY fabs; the awards **restrict share buybacks until Dec 2029** (buybacks were only $650M in FY2026) and impose 10-year China expansion guardrails. The CHIPS **investment tax credit expires for projects beginning construction after Dec 31, 2026** — a policy cliff for the second New York fab.
- **Customer concentration:** top 10 customers ≈ half of revenue; one customer was 17% of FY2025 revenue (10-K). Take-or-pay strategic customer agreements (26 signed, through 2030) reduce volume risk but create counterparty/performance exposure.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Export licensing (BIS/EAR) across a distributed manufacturing base** — Singapore/Malaysia/India fabs and packaging sites now sell into newly licensed AI destinations; deemed-export and re-export controls on HBM are the single largest compliance-program burden.
2. **China data-localisation and PIPL** plus cross-border data flows for a Shanghai design center and Xi'an operations.
3. **Critical-minerals / UFLPA supply-chain tracing** — gallium, germanium, rare earths and polysilicon sourcing from China requires diligence to avoid forced-labor and end-use violations.
4. **Sanctions screening of Hong Kong distributors** — $6.29B of HK-billed demand is a classic diversion channel risk for restricted AI parts.
5. No currently disclosed DOJ/SEC/FCPA enforcement action surfaced in this review (gap: FY2026 10-K full legal-proceedings text was inaccessible — sec.gov returned 403 — so this conclusion rests on secondary sources and may be incomplete).

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Rationale: revenue is predominantly US-billed (68.6%) and management is shrinking China to single digits — a genuine positive. But Micron sits at the exact intersection of every live US–China fault line (AI memory, chip tools, critical minerals), carries a standing Chinese procurement ban, a renewed IP attack from Netlist after a $600M settlement, fresh multi-front DRAM antitrust class actions, and roughly two-thirds of wafer capacity in Taiwan/Singapore/Japan. The risk is **policy-driven and binary** rather than diffuse: a single BIS rule, Chinese mineral ban, or Taiwan event can reprice the equity by double digits in a session, as demonstrated on Nov 16 and again during the Nov 2026 tariff headlines.
