## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1. US tariff-regime volatility & retaliatory tariffs (Mexico, Canada, China) — Probability: High | Impact: High**
Walmart is the single largest importer of goods into the US (food, electronics, general merchandise) and was hit hard by the IEEPA tariffs of 2025–26. The **US Supreme Court struck down that tariff regime**, triggering ~$10B of refunds to S&P 500 companies; **Walmart received $2.9B (~30% of all refunds)**, the largest of any company (Home Depot: $750M; Amazon: $640M). Walmart plans to pass refunds through as price cuts (Q3–Q4 CY2026), converting a windfall into share-gain rather than margin. Residual risk: any new tariff regime (Section 232/301, Section 338 Canadian duties in force July 2026, Section 232 steel investigations) re-ignites cost pressure — management already stated "we can't absorb all the pressure."

**2. US–China decoupling & forced-labor trade restrictions — Probability: Medium-High | Impact: High**
On March 12, 2026, USTR initiated **Section 301 investigations into 60 economies** for failing to block goods made with forced labor (hearings April 28–29, 2026; additional tariffs proposed June 2026). Retail sourcing reports (UHRP) have named Walmart as at-risk on **Xinjiang cotton**; Walmart is also facing NGO scrutiny over Uyghur forced-labor in seafood supply chains. China remains a top-3 sourcing base and a ~$19B revenue market — dual exposure to decoupling (imports) and localization (China operations/retail).

**3. India e-commerce regulatory crackdown — Probability: High | Impact: Medium**
India's **Competition Commission (CCI) ordered its Director General to investigate Flipkart** for alleged competition-law violations — disclosed in Walmart's own 10-Qs (June 2025 and Aug 2026). Regulators allege Flipkart colluded with Samsung/Xiaomi on exclusive launches in breach of antitrust law; India's broader crackdown (FDI rules, trade-violation probes) could mean **up to ~$1.35B in penalties sector-wide** and forced structural changes to the marketplace model. India is Walmart's fastest-growing international market (double-digit sales growth), so this is a growth-engine risk.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

- **Tariff refund litigation (won):** Supreme Court struck down prior IEEPA tariffs → **$2.9B refund** to Walmart; refunds flowing from CBP since mid-2026 (IEEPA duty-refund process), to be redeployed into price investment.
- **Opioid litigation (settled):** Walmart settled the **US government's opioid lawsuit (which sought billions) for $50M**; this is on top of the ~$3.1B multi-state opioid framework agreed earlier. Remaining private/state claims are a low material risk but ongoing.
- **India antitrust (active):** CCI Director-General investigation into Flipkart (exclusivity, collusion, FDI breach allegations); also pending court appeal context (NCLAT previously upheld CCI's approval of Walmart's Flipkart acquisition — deal structure itself stands).
- **US trade enforcement (active):** USTR Section 301 forced-labor investigations (60 economies) and Section 232 steel investigations; Section 338 duties on Canadian goods (suspended for certain goods Aug 19, 2026) — all touch Walmart's import matrix.
- **FTC scrutiny (watch):** FTC has inquired into "surveillance pricing"/dynamic-pricing practices at Walmart and peers; monitoring ongoing. CFPB oversight touches Walmart's financial-services stack (MoneyCard, earned-wage access, money transfers).
- **DEI rollback (Nov 2024):** Walmart curtailed DEI programs (ended racial-equity-center commitment, supplier diversity program). Politically motivated pressure is easing under the current administration, but legal/consumer-backlash risk and potential discrimination claims remain a reputational item.
- **Tax:** OECD Pillar Two global minimum tax applies to international segments; US tariff refunds are non-recurring.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **Forced-labor supply chain:** Walmart named in NGO reports over potential **Xinjiang cotton** and Uyghur-forced-labor seafood exposure; USTR's 60-economy Section 301 sweep raises diligence/evidentiary burden across the import base. **Key watch item.**
- **Historic FCPA context:** Walmart paid **~$282M (2019) to resolve FCPA violations** across Mexico, Brazil, India and China; extensive LATAM footprint (Mexico, Chile, Central America) keeps anti-bribery exposure live — controls need ongoing scrutiny.
- **India FDI compliance:** Flipkart operates in a gray zone of India's marketplace-FDI rules; structural changes or penalties are plausible outcomes of the active CCI probes.
- **Sanctions exposure: minimal** — no Russian/ Iranian operations; exposure limited to US import restrictions (forced labor, Section 301/232) rather than OFAC sanctions.
- **Data privacy:** China PIPL/CAC rules and cross-border data-transfer controls constrain China/India tech operations (PhonePe, Flipkart payments, China digital).

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

**Rationale:** ~82% of revenue sits in the low-risk US regulatory environment, and the flagship geopolitical tail risk (tariff regime) has been *partially resolved in Walmart's favor* via the Supreme Court and a $2.9B refund — converting risk into a competitive weapon (price rollbacks). The offsetting pressures — India's antitrust crackdown on the growth engine (Flipkart), US–China forced-labor trade restrictions touching the supply base, and Mexican trade/security friction — are real but manageable and non-life-threatening. Risks are concentrated in supply chain and secondary markets, not core US economics.

*Key watch items for the next 12 months: (1) final USTR Section 301/forced-labor tariff scope; (2) CCI's Flipkart findings and any penalty; (3) USMCA renegotiation outcomes on duty-free/de minimis thresholds affecting Mexico sourcing; (4) whether price investment from the refund erodes or builds margin momentum.*
