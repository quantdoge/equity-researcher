# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Walmart Inc. (WMT)

**Prepared by:** Non-Financial Risk Desk | **Ticker:** WMT | **Sector:** Consumer Defensive (Discount Stores) | **Market cap:** ~$830B (largest in sector; COST #2 at ~$408B) | **Headcount:** ~2.1M | **Price:** $104.26 (≈ −4.4% YTD 2026)

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Strong (with transition caveat)**

Evidence:
- **No CEO/Chairman duality.** Greg Penner serves as independent-ish Chairman (continues on Audit and Nominating/Governance committees); CEO is separate. Controls in place.
- **Planned, disclosed CEO succession.** Doug McMillon retired Jan 31, 2026 after ~11 years; John Furner (former Walmart U.S. CEO) succeeded as President & CEO and joined the board effective Feb 1, 2026. Succession was telegraphed well in advance — a sign of functioning board planning.
- **Board/pay accountability in place:** 2026 proxy (DEF 14A) put board and executive-pay votes to shareholders; profile data shows committee structure with audit coverage.
- Caveats: (a) the new group CEO is ~8 months into the role with a 2.1M-person workforce — transition execution is unproven; (b) I could not directly verify full board independence metrics (proxy text tool unavailable; sourced from secondary summaries); (c) historical baggage — the 2015–2019 FCPA tragedies in Mexico/India/China (~$282M cumulative penalties + monitorship) — is old but shows the compliance system can fail at scale. No active SEC enforcement hits found in EDGAR in the current window.

**Rating: Strong** on structure and process; watch the Furner-era execution.

## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary

- **Opioid (the big one) — RESOLVED in Aug 2026:** Walmart paid **~$3.1B** to settle thousands of state/municipal suits alleging pharmacies "unlawfully dispensing opioids," plus **$50M** to settle federal DEA-DOJ claims over invalid opioid prescriptions, with future safeguard obligations. This closes the largest active enforcement overhang; the financial cost is booked, but headlines and monitoring obligations persist.
- **India platforms (Flipkart/PhonePe):** (i) Amazon/Flipkart launched reviews of unlicensed security-camera listings (Oct 2026) after government testing rules tightened amid espionage concerns; (ii) ongoing regulatory scrutiny of e-comm discount practices; (iii) **PhonePe's UPI 30% market-share cap is deferred only until Dec 2026** — a live license-risk deadline on a core Indian fintech asset.
- **Labor/employment enforcement:** Walmart Stores East hit with a **$125M jury award** in an EEOC ADA case; additional EEOC settlements (sex-harassment, $415K) and pending disability/DEI discrimination suits. Company itself flagged ongoing "regulatory pressure in pharmacy" at its Sept 2026 conference.
- **Fintech/privacy surface expanding:** OnePay (Walmart-backed) added Solana/Arbitrum crypto trading; ad-tech/AI growth draws data-privacy scrutiny.
- No SEC/DOJ enforcement actions were returned for WMT in EDGAR full-text search.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Food safety at scale — own-brand (private-label) integrity:** Class I (FDA's most severe) recall of bettergoods Authentic Italian Lemon Alfredo Fettuccine over *Listeria monocytogenes*, initiated Sept 15, 2026 and subsequently **expanded** to additional lots. Private-label quality is Walmart's margin and trust engine; a Listeria escalation carries injury, liability, and recall-cost tail.
- **Regulatory surface growth outpacing controls:** payments (OnePay crypto listings), digital ads/AI (data-privacy exposure), and India (Flipkart listing-compliance + PhonePe UPI cap) are expanding into more regulated territory in parallel.
- **Workforce-scale litigation drag + transition execution:** 2.1M employees (~80% part-time historically) produce a permanent stream of EEOC/labor claims with multimillion-dollar verdicts; layered onto a brand-new CEO's first year. Cyber incidents were not flagged in the current window but the digital-shelf-label/AI rollout widens the attack surface.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment

**Moderate.** Walmart enjoys deep consumer trust on price/value ("everyday low prices") and does community-goodwill work (e.g., $15M Feeding America grant), which buffers most brand shocks. Offsetting forces: (a) a **Class I Listeria recall** on its own brand undercuts food-safety trust; (b) opioid-settlement coverage ("unlawfully dispensing opioids") tarnishes the pharmacy/healthcare halo; (c) the post-2023 DEI rollback keeps generating race-discrimination headlines; (d) the AI-slop ban in stores reads as a governance positive (protecting brand aesthetics). Stock sentiment is soft (stock down ~4.4% YTD on cautious-consumer concerns) but that is macro, not reputational.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE**

Walmart's scale creates chronic, recurring non-financial risk across every vector — labor law, food safety, data/privacy, fintech, and international regulation — but the largest active overhang (opioids) has now been **settled and booked**, governance is structurally strong, succession was orderly, and there is no active existential investigation, sanctions exposure, or governance collapse. This is a well-managed mega-risk profile, not a critical one.

I could not independently corroborate details (sources are thin/partisan). **Escalate for verification** — if credible, this is a fraud/governance indicator potentially adjacent to FCPA-adjacent conduct and warrants internal-control review. *(Unverified — treat as open question, not finding.)*
