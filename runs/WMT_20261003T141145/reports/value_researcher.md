# Walmart Inc. (WMT) — Value Investment Analysis
*Prepared by Equity Value Research | Price ref: $104.26 · Mkt cap ≈ $830B · FY2026 (ended 1/31/2026)*

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — MOAT: **Wide** (and widening)

Walmart is the world's largest retailer (~$713B revenue, FY2026) and has the rarest asset in retail: a genuine, self-reinforcing scale moat that is strengthening, not eroding.

**Evidence of moat:**
- **Cost advantage / efficient scale.** 10,500+ stores and the country's largest private supply chain give procurement, logistics, and last-mile economics no competitor (except Amazon) can match. Grocery — 60% of US sales — is a fortress category where Walmart holds ~26% US share and wins on price through scale, not subsidy.
- **Omnichannel flywheel.** Stores double as e-commerce fulfillment nodes: ~90% of the US population within 10 miles of a store enables cheap pickup/delivery economics. E-commerce has inflected to profitability and is now a share-gainer, including upper-income cohorts.
- **Emerging network effects.** Marketplace (3P seller base), Walmart+ membership, and a fast-growing, high-margin advertising business (Walmart Connect) create a converged data/membership/advertising ecosystem layered on top of the goods business. This is a genuinely new moat element that did not exist five years ago.
- **Return on capital confirms the moat:** ROIC ≈ 17–19% consistently over the past four years, ROE ~20% — comfortably above any plausible WACC (7–8%). A business earning 2x+ its cost of capital at this scale is the hallmark of pricing/scale power.

**MOAT VERDICT: Wide and widening** — the 5-year trajectory (e-commerce profitability, ad margins, share gains) is unambiguous.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **The deal-breaker**

- **Price paid vs earnings:** P/E 38.2x (FY2026 EPS $2.74), EV/EBITDA 19.2x, P/FCF 56x, FCF yield 1.8%, dividend yield ~0.95%.
- **vs peers:** P/E carries a **~66% premium to sector median (23.0x)**. Even Costco, the quality comp, trades ~30–38x earnings with structurally higher margins (~2.5x Walmart's) and similar growth.
- **Intrinsic value (my estimates):**
  - *Owner-earnings DCF* (base $20B, 6% growth, 8% discount, 3% terminal): ≈ **$525–590B → ~$65–74/share**
  - *EV/EBITDA at 12x* (staple-retail norm): ≈ **$500B equity → ~$63/share**
  - *Mechanical DCF tool* (trailing FCF, total capex): **$442B → ~$55/share**
  - **Central intrinsic value: ≈ $60–70/share.** Current price of $104.26 trades ~**50–70% above** intrinsic value.
- **What the price implies:** the market is paying 38x earnings and ~1.7% FCF yield for ~5% revenue / high-single-digit EPS growth — i.e., it is pricing Walmart as a *de-risked growth compounder*, not a value staple. To earn a satisfactory return from here you need ~10%+ sustained EPS growth for a decade with no multiple compression. That is a reasonable bull thesis, but it is not margin of safety; it is *payment in full, plus.*

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — Strong, in transition

- **Record:** Doug McMillon (CEO 2014–Jan 2026) presided over a **312% share-price gain**, the e-commerce turn to profitability, and disciplined capital allocation. He handed off to a hand-picked successor effective Feb 1, 2026 — an orderly, planned transition (McMillon stays in an advisory role).
- **Capital allocation:** exemplary for the size — 71% of net income returned (buybacks + a 50+yr growing dividend) while still funding $26B/yr of value-accretive growth capex. M&A has been small, targeted, and mostly ecosystem-building (Flipkart/PhonePe, marketplace), not empire-building.
- **Watch-item:** the June 2024 executives' share sales and standard insider selling are noise; the real concern is execution risk under new leadership. Insider ownership is modest by founder standards but the board/management culture is institutional-grade.
- **Note:** sell-side targets were *cut* to $113–130 in Aug 2026 (from $120–150); the stock sits below most targets — reflecting softened sentiment, not a discount.

## 5. Investment Verdict — **HOLD** (superb business, no margin of safety)

This is a **Wide-moat, 8/10 financials, first-class management** compounder — the kind of business Buffett would put in the "too good to overpay for" file. The problem is the price: at ~$104 (≈38x earnings, ≈1.8% FCF yield, ~50–70% above my intrinsic range), the entry price offers the opposite of a margin of safety — it *requires* flawless execution for a decade just to earn a market return. My discipline: a fair-to-rich price is not a buy. **Do not initiate; if held, keep, but do not add at these levels.**

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Multiple compression** — the dominant risk. P/E 38x on a 3% net-margin retailer is a valuation the market can re-rate quickly; a reversion to even 28–30x implies a 20–30% drawdown.
2. **Cost/policy inflation** — wage pressure (2.1M employees), tariffs on goods trade, and regulatory freight could squeeze the 4.2% operating margin, which is the entire valuation fulcrum.
3. **Competitive squeeze** — Amazon's moat is larger; Target and deep-discounters fight for the same lower-income wallet. Grocery deflation cycles hit same-store comps.
4. **Capex dependence** — FCF remains hostage to a heavy automation/supply-chain spend cycle; if e-commerce economics regress, the 1.8% FCF yield leaves zero cushion.
5. **Leadership transition** — new CEO takes over a machine that is running well; institutional drift, not individual failure, is the quiet risk.
