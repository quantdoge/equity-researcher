## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **Medium**

**Strengths (genuine, differentiated):**
- **Project Gigaton achieved 1 billion metric tons of supply chain (Scope 3) reduction ~6 years ahead of its 2030 target** — the largest verified supplier-facing reduction program in retail.
- **2040 zero-emissions operations target (explicitly without offsets)**; 100% renewable electricity by 2035 (48.5% achieved in 2024 vs. 50% by 2025 goal — near miss).
- Scope 1+2 down **18.1%** from 2015 baseline; emissions intensity down **47.4%** since FY2016 — emissions decoupling from revenue growth is real.

**Weaknesses / watch items:**
- **Absolute Scope 1+2 rose 1.1% YoY in 2024** (transport +7%) — the 2025/2030 absolute reduction targets (35%/65% vs 2015) are at risk; transportation and refrigerants are hard-to-abate with no ready tech (heavy-duty EV fleet years away).
- **Refrigerants alone are 57% of Scope 1** — phasing down high-GWP refrigerants across a 10,500-store fleet is a decade-long, capex-heavy program.
- Greenwashing litigation history (below) indicates disclosure/labeling rigor lags ambition.

**Physical risk is moderate but growing**: Hurricane exposure across Gulf/Southeast stores and Mexico/Central America (power reliability already cited as an emissions and cost drag). Not critical — diversified, mostly inland US real estate.

---

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **Medium (with High-risk pockets)**

**The core tension:** the world's largest private employer (2.1M) runs on a low-margin, frontline-heavy model where labor is simultaneously a cost line and its biggest license-to-operate risk.

- **Wages/benefits improving**: raised hourly starting wages to $14–$19 (Feb 2025); ~$18 average. This is a genuine mitigant to attrition and organizing pressure — but adds multi-billion-dollar annual SG&A.
- **Labor litigation is structural**: $188M Pennsylvania rest-break class action (2015); recurring wage/break/overtime settlements ($1.45M–$2.5M+ settlements across jurisdictions, 2024). Persistent, not existential.
- **DEI rollback (Nov 2024)** — the largest US private employer: ended the $100M racial equity center commitment, dropped supplier-diversity preference, removed LGBTQ+ metrics. **Reputational/recruiting risk is real** (civil-rights boycott calls, investor and talent-engagement frictions), though no quantifiable near-term financial penalty.
- **Opioid/pharmacy duty-of-care** is the single largest S-related financial hit: **$3.1B multistate settlement (2022) + $50M DOJ settlement (illegal opioid dispensing)** — total opioid burden approaching $4B, now largely ring-fenced.
- Worker organizing (e.g., store closures amid union drives) and safety are recurring but within retail norms.

**Net:** material but bounded. The social story is "improving economics, periodic liability, political risk on DEI" — not a structural thesis-breaker.

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Strong:**
- **No CEO/Chairman duality** — Greg Penner (non-executive chairman, Walton family) is separate from the CEO; board is majority independent (incl. Cesar Conde).
- **Orderly, well-executed CEO succession**: Doug McMillon (highly regarded, 2014–2026) retired on schedule, transitioned to internal successor **John Furner (CEO, effective Feb 2026)** — continuity preserved, a positive that many mega-caps fail to manage.
- Executive comp is heavily equity-based and tied to shareholder returns; no governance scandals at the top in recent years.
- Single-class shares; shareholder rights generally clean (no poison pill, annual elections).

**Deductions:**
- **Concentrated control**: Walton family controls ~45% of voting power — effectively insulates the board from external oversight on controversial decisions (e.g., DEI rollback). Governance purists cap scores here.
- **FCPA legacy**: $282M Mexico bribery resolution (2019) — compliance function under structural scrutiny.
- Transparency caveat: could not independently verify proxy-level board refreshment/diversity stats via filings tool (noted gap).

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **SEC climate disclosure rule**: rescinded (March 2025) → low current exposure, but California **SB 253/261** (Scope 1–3 + climate-risk reporting, annual) applies to Walmart given its CA footprint — the de facto US disclosure regime; compliance cost manageable but real.
- **EU CSRD**: **Low** — Walmart has no meaningful EU retail operations (Germany exited 2006, ASDA sold 2020); only sourcing exposure.
- **CBAM**: **Negligible** — retailer imports, no EU steel/aluminum manufacturing exposure.
- **Packaging EPR (CSB 54 etc.)**: **High** — US state extended-producer-responsibility laws directly shift packaging/plastic costs onto Walmart; this is the regulatory topic with the largest near-term margin effect.
- **UFLPA / supply chain due diligence**: forced-labor import scrutiny (China/India sourcing) — compliance cost, minor tariff/exclusion risk.
- **Wage/hour, NLRA, EEOC, FTC Green Guides, CPSC, pharmacy/DEA** — ongoing, ordinary-course.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **Average** (borderline Leader)

- **Environmental: Leader-tier** — Project Gigaton's early achievement and the offset-free 2040 target put Walmart above nearly all global retailers.
- **Social: Average-to-Laggard** — improving wages offset by DEI rollback, labor litigation history, and opioid legacy.
- **Governance: Above-average** — clean succession, no duality, but Walton control concentration caps the score.
- The mixture nets to **Average**: institutional ESG scores (MSCI A/AA band historically, Sustainalytics medium-risk) are consistent with this — leadership on E is not enough to offset S/G frictions.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **Neutral** (slight positive tilt)

**For the thesis:**
- **Not an ESG risk story for valuation.** The material ESG liabilities (opioids ~$4B, FCPA $282M) are paid and ring-fenced — equivalent to weeks of free cash flow. No ESG-driven balance-sheet or cost-of-capital shock on the horizon.
- **E program is a genuine economic mitigant**, not a charity line: energy efficiency and renewable procurement (48.5%+ of electricity) offset power-price inflation; supplier engagement lowers Scope-3 regulatory pass-through risk.
- **Watch items** that could turn the call negative: (1) Scope 1+2 absolute emissions rising while 2025/2030 SBTI-style targets sit — under California SB 253 scrutiny this becomes a compliance/reputational issue; (2) EPR packaging costs; (3) post-DEI rollback talent/EEOC litigation frictions; (4) transition risk under new CEO Furner (low, internal candidate).
- **Not a positive-driver either**: DEI rollback and family control mean WMT will not be a "green leader premium" holding.

**Bottom line:** ESG does not impede the investment thesis — Neutral, with a modest positive from environmental cost leadership. Position sizing should track fundamentals (share +~130% over two years, defensive earnings), not ESG tailwinds.
