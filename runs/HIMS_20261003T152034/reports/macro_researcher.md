# MACROECONOMIC IMPACT ASSESSMENT — Hims & Hers Health (HIMS)

## 1. Macro Environment Summary — Stance: **TRANSITIONAL (risk-off lean)**

The US is running a **reflation-with-high-long-rates** regime that is uniquely hostile to long-duration, high-beta growth equities. The Fed has eased aggressively — the effective fed funds rate is down from 4.33% (June 2025) to **3.75% (Sep 2026)** — yet the long end has moved *against* the Fed: the 10Y Treasury has ground up from ~4.3% (April 2026) to **5.24% (Oct 1, 2026)**, with the 30Y at **5.37%**. That is a **bear steepener**: the curve re-sloped positive (2s10s +34bp) on term-premium and deficit repricing, not on growth optimism. CPI is re-accelerating to **3.05% y/y (Aug 2026)** — up ~2.3% in just seven months — while unemployment has drifted to **4.2%**. In plain terms: the Fed's easing cycle collided with stubborn inflation, the bond market is repricing "*higher for longer*," and the consumer is paying more for everything. For a premium-priced, out-of-pocket, subscription-driven D2C health company trading at a growth multiple (beta 2.4), that is a poor macro backdrop.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

The macro tape is against HIMS' equity: a 5.2%+ long rate, re-accelerating ~3% CPI, gently rising unemployment, and an ad-cost/consumer-income squeeze all hit this specific model (long-duration multiple + out-of-pocket discretionary spend + low-margin transition period) at once. This is not an idiosyncratic-fundamentals call — structural GLP-1 demand is genuinely strong — but the *cyclical* contribution to HIMS' risk is clearly negative. Rate relief (10Y back toward 4%) would be the single largest macro unlock for this name.

## 5. Material Surprise Scenario — **Bear: "The Fed's Cut Was a Mistake" (10Y → 6%)**

CPI fails to stay near 3% and re-accelerates toward 3.5–4% (tariff pass-through, energy, and healthcare service inflation persist). The Fed is forced to abandon its easing path and signal re-tightening while the 10Y breaks decisively above 5.5–6% on deficit/term-premium fears. Transmission to HIMS: (1) the discount-rate hit on a 2.4-beta, long-duration equity compounds — the multiple de-rates further from already-cut levels (JPMorgan has trimmed PT to $33); (2) the consumer squeeze worsens as real rates on cards/loans rise, cutting subscription starts and GLP-1 program retention exactly when HIMS needs volume to offset sub-10% GLP-1 margins; (3) ad costs stay bid up, crushing the CAC math. A 20–30% de-rating from current levels is plausible in that tape. **Bull mirror:** a soft landing where CPI falls back to ~2.5%, the Fed resumes cuts to ~3%, and the 10Y normalizes toward 4% — that would re-rate the multiple, cut CAC, and revive out-of-pocket spending; combined with regulatory clarity on GLP-1 coverage, it flips this rating to Favourable.
