## 4. Portfolio Fit Analysis

- **Sector concentration:** Healthcare. If the book is neutral-to-overweight Healthcare (defensives, pharma), HIMS adds *sector* diversification superficially but **zero factor diversification** — it is a small-cap consumer-health high-beta growth name inside a basket otherwise dominated by mega-cap pharma. Low correlation (0.03 vs LLY) means it does not protect the healthcare sleeve; it loads unpaired idiosyncratic risk onto it.
- **Factor exposure:** This is the damning one. HIMS is **96% annualized vol, beta 2.4–2.7, worst-in-sector on Low-Vol, negative 12-1 momentum, no dividend, ~1% FCF yield.** In a portfolio already exposed to growth/tech beta, this is *more* of the same risk at a worse price — it amplifies the portfolio's drawdown profile (a −20% market implies a ~−50%+ move on beta alone) and adds negative carry (no yield). The only positive factor tilts (quality: ROE 23.7%, negative accruals) are outweighed by the two Strong-Sell factor scores.
- **Correlation:** 0.25 vs TDOC, 0.03 vs LLY, R²=0.14 vs SPY — the stock is overwhelmingly idiosyncratic. That cuts both ways: it cannot be "hedged" by a healthcare position, so sizing must treat it as its own risk center, and its event risk (litigation + FDA + earnings) is fully diversifiable only by not owning it.
- **Liquidity:** No constraint — deep float, active trading. This argues *for* being able to exit cheaply; there is no liquidity reason to hold a de-risking tranche.
