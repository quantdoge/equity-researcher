## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- Direct Scope 1–2 footprint is minimal (corporate offices, data platforms). The material E exposure sits in Scope 3: shipping/packaging of prescription and OTC products and the compounding/pharma supply chain — not disclosed.
- **Material gap:** no standalone sustainability/ESG report or CDP response was located; environmental disclosure is effectively absent. For this business model, that absence is a governance/disclosure issue, not a physical-risk driver.
- No carbon-tax, CBAM, or stranded-asset exposure. Physical climate risk to assets is negligible.

---

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH** (bordering Critical on privacy)

- **Patient safety (High):** Direct-to-consumer prescribing of compounded GLP-1s drew FDA safety warnings and a Novo Nordisk "threat to public safety" allegation; FDA probe of compounding practices. Novo suit settled with HIMS exiting compounded semaglutide and pivoting to a collaboration — an orderly retreat, but it caps a flagship growth engine (FY2026 guidance $2.7–2.9B, with the company disclosing most revenue is now non-GLP-1).
- **Consumer data privacy (Critical):** FTC complaint alleges sensitive health information was shared with Meta and Snap and health-privacy promises were misleading. This is the single most financially material S issue — it targets the core trust asset of the business.
- **Consumer protection (High):** Allegations of deceptive billing and difficult cancellation; FTC click-to-cancel enforcement trajectory directly pressures the subscription model.
- **Labour/D&I:** No major controversies; disclosure is thin (no clear D&I metrics retrieved — gap).

## 4. Governance Quality Score: **3 / 10**

- **Dual-class concentration (major negative):** 10-K confirms dual-class structure concentrating voting power with CEO/co-founder Andrew Dudum — ~87.7% of total voting power as of Apr 2026. Shareholder rights are structurally weak.
- **CEO/board overlap:** Dudum serves as CEO and board member; independent chair not confirmed.
- **Ethics/compliance (negative):** FTc consent-order exposure plus ongoing plaintiffs' investigation of whether directors failed to stop improper health-data sharing and deceptive billing.
- **Positives:** Nine-member board (majority-nominee slate with independent figures), no poison pill identified, conventional pay mix.
- Net: strong control concentration + active securities litigation and an enforcement action cap the score at 3/10 despite a reasonably sized independent board.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US FTC:** Health Breach Notification Rule + Section 5 (privacy, dark patterns) — *active enforcement action*, the dominant ESG regulatory overhang.
- **State AGs / state privacy:** Utah co-plaintiff; Health My Health Data Act (WA) and other state health-privacy laws; multi-state action risk.
- **FDA:** Compounding regulations (503A), shortage-list dynamics for GLP-1s, telehealth prescribing rules (Ryan Haight Act flexibilities), and the DOJ referral — direct product-licence exposure.
- **Securities regulation:** SEC-focused class actions and potential disclosure enforcement around GLP-1/compounding statements.
- **EU CSRD / SEC climate rule:** not material — US-focused operations, services business; no CBAM exposure; no ESG-linked debt covenants identified.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **ESG Risk**

HIMS is a structurally strong, high-growth consumer healthcare business, but it currently carries an active federal enforcement action over health-data privacy and billing, multiple securities class actions, a board investigation, and founder super-voting control — while the highest-growth product line sits inside a contested regulatory regime (compounded GLP-1s). This is not a disclosure-lagging "Average" name; these are live, quantified, financial-risk events.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEGATIVE** for the current thesis

- **Cost of capital & cash-flow drag:** Litigation defence, potential FTC penalties/consent order, and compliance remediation (consent decrees typically run 10–20 years with audit burdens) will consume cash and management bandwidth.
- **Model risk:** FTC action targets the subscription/billing mechanics that drive retention economics; FDA/GLP-1 resolution caps a historically dominant growth vector (guided down through the pivot).
- **Governance discount:** ~88% founder voting control limits activist/ESG-engagement recourse; securities class actions signal disclosure credibility risk.
- **Watch items (ESG-clearing conditions):** FTC consent order scope and any monetary penalty; settlement of class actions; clarity on ex-GLP-1 growth durability; movement toward sunsetting super-voting shares or independent-chair role.
- **Verdict for portfolio:** ESG Risk is a thesis-negative overlay. Unless valuation fully discounts the litigation tail and GLP-1 reset, HIMS carries outsized non-financial-to-financial conversion risk versus peers.
