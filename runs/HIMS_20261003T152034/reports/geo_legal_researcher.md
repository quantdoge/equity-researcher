## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical/Policy Risks

**① Federal crackdown on compounded GLP-1s / telehealth prescribing — Probability: High (materializing) | Impact: High**
- FDA (CDER) sent Hims a warning letter for "unlawful sale/marketing" of compounded semaglutide and "same active ingredient" claims (part of Sept-2025 and Feb-2026 flurries of warning letters to telehealth firms incl. SkinnyRx, Ro, etc.).
- Feb-2026: FDA signaled plans to **restrict GLP-1 ingredients in compounded drugs**; Hims withdrew its $49 compounded oral semaglutide pill within days.
- **Mitigant:** Mar-2026 settlement with Novo Nordisk pivots Hims's weight-loss line to FDA-approved Ozempic/Wegovy and away from compounded GLP-1 advertising — substantially de-risking this headline exposure, though future compounding restrictions remain a policy tail.

**② FTC/state consumer-protection and health-data privacy enforcement — Probability: High (suit active) | Impact: Medium-High**
- FTC sued Hims (~Dec-2025) over sharing sensitive health data with Meta and Snap via tracking pixels, plus deceptive billing and subscription-cancellation practices; state enforcers joined. Case still in court as of Jul-2026.
- Exposure: civil penalties, injunctive relief, and potentially a 20-year-style data-practices monitoring regime; reputational damage to a consumer trust business.

**③ Big Pharma IP enforcement + US GLP-1 pricing politics — Probability: Medium | Impact: Medium**
- Novo Nordisk (Danish) v. Hims patent suit (Feb-2026, D. Del., Patent '343 on semaglutide) ended in settlement, but signals recurring IP-litigation risk for telehealth compounders; a US consumer class action over "same active ingredient" advertising continues in Illinois.
- Policy layer: US drug-pricing scrutiny of GLP-1 economics (CMS/IRA dynamics) could compress margins in the fastest-growing segment.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Pixel/tracker health-data sharing** — the exact practice the FTC is suing over domestically is the same conduct GDPR/UK GDPR/PIPEDA/OAIC target; as Canada/UK/Germany/Australia scale toward $1B international revenue, extraterritorial privacy enforcement is a live, compounding exposure.
2. **Compounding/pharmacy (FD&C 503A) compliance** — repeated warning letters and a forced product withdrawal indicate systemic noncompliance in this channel; now largely exited but capability remains a contingency.
3. **Advertising/marketing-claims discipline** — "same active ingredient," efficacy, and weight-loss claims have drawn FTC, FDA, Novo false-advertising allegations, *and* a securities class action; claim-verification standards are clearly behind peers'.
4. **Subscription billing & cancellation practices** — a second pillar of the FTC and state-enforcement cases; mirrors a broader FTC sweep against DTC subscription models.
5. **FCPA / AML / sanctions: No material red flags** — US-consumer business with no meaningful foreign-government contracting; watch only for M&A integration controls (Eucalyptus) and any international regulatory interactions as expansion proceeds.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

The **geopolitical dimension is benign** (all revenue markets are stable, rule-of-law jurisdictions with zero nationalization, currency-control, or sanctions exposure). The rating is driven entirely by a **dense, US-concentrated legal/regulatory stack**: an active FTC suit, a pending consolidated securities class action, a live consumer class action, and FDA enforcement that already forced exit from a key growth product — partially offset by the March-2026 Novo settlement that re-anchors the weight-loss line on FDA-approved drugs. Positive trajectory if the FTC matter resolves on supervised-terms and the securities action is dismissed; until then, expect headline volatility and an elevated discount on legal-contingency valuation.
