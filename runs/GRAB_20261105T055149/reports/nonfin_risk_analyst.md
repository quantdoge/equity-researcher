## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary — **HIGH and rising**

**Adverse/realised:**
- **Taiwan FTC blocked the $600M foodpanda Taiwan acquisition (Oct 21, 2026)** on competition grounds. A signed, board-approved growth deal was killed outright — the clearest evidence yet that Grab's M&A roll-up strategy is now regulator-constrained.
- **Indonesia commission cap cut from 20% → 8%** (effective for two-wheeled from July 1, 2026), plus driver reclassification as micro-businesses and mandated accident/benefit contributions. Indonesia is Grab's largest market; this is a direct structural hit to Mobility take rate.
- **Indonesia KPPU antitrust**: Rp30bn (~$2M) fine for monopoly/discriminatory practices; Grab won on court appeal, KPPU challenging. Fine immaterial; the behavioural remedies and "platform fee" precedent are not.
- **Vietnam**: National Competition Commission reviewing Grab's fare/fee/commission policies after driver complaints and a planned boycott (Sept 2026); two-wheeled commission also cut to 8%.

**Pending / unresolved:**
- **Atome Financial — $1.49B for 60%** ($260M primary), targeted close **Q3 2027**, conditional on multi-jurisdiction financial-services and competition approvals. Long fuse, multiple veto points, and it pulls a consumer-credit book onto the balance sheet.
- **Thailand**: Trade Competition Commission draft rules and Department of Transport licensing framework pending — the next likely take-rate front.
- **Grab–GoTo merger**: repeatedly collapsed/stalled on Indonesian antitrust objections; state fund Danantara involvement adds political risk. Treat as optionality, not a base case.

**Clean:** EDGAR full-text search returned **no SEC enforcement actions or DOJ matters** naming GRAB. No US data-privacy enforcement found.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Regulatory take-rate compression is now the core business model risk.** Two of Grab's largest markets (Indonesia, Vietnam) have cut platform commissions on the highest-frequency product from 20% to 8%. Management characterises this as a "manageable margin drag," but the direction of travel across Thailand and the Philippines is one-way, politically popular, and driven by organised driver activism (a ~500,000-driver strike occurred in May 2025). This is a permanent step-down in unit economics, not a cycle.

2. **Key-person concentration and thin governance bench.** Operational and strategic control sits with one founder who will hold ~75% of votes and who personally intervened with $30M to defend the share price. There is no disclosed succession plan, and the board just lost two senior independent voices. Any health, reputational, or regulatory event attaching to the CEO is unhedgeable for shareholders.

3. **Fintech/credit and cyber-data concentration.** The Atome transaction converts Grab into a regulated consumer lender across multiple Asian jurisdictions — introducing underwriting/credit-cycle risk, country-by-country licensing, and consumer-protection regimes into a company with no long credit track record. Simultaneously, Grab sits on one of the region's richest single repositories of rider/driver identity, location, and payment data amid an elevated APAC breach environment. No Grab-specific material breach was identified, but the payment + BNPL expansion materially enlarges the attack surface and the regulatory penalty exposure per incident.

*(Secondary: 8-country operating footprint with fragmented rules; Myanmar operations carry sanctions/scam-centre-adjacent reputational exposure; platform competition from GoTo and TikTok-shop logistics.)*

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **MODERATE (improving, but structurally exposed)**

- **Worker-relations conflict is the defining reputational theme.** Driver protests in Indonesia, Vietnam, and by Taiwanese delivery couriers opposing the foodpanda deal have repeatedly generated negative press and — critically — *caused* the regulatory outcomes above. Grab is now the lightning rod for gig-economy inequality politics across ASEAN.
- **Governance criticism** was public and pointed around the super-voting vote ("Trust the founder?", March 2026), damaging Grab's standing with ESG/governance-screened institutional holders.
- **Brand equity is intact commercially.** Q3 2026 delivered record revenue +27%, aEBITDA +73%, and — for the first time — profitability in all three segments; the stock rose 9% on the print. Consumer sentiment is not a source of near-term impairment.
- **Stock is down ~45% over 12 months** ($6.07 → $2.71 low → $3.36) despite improving fundamentals. The market is de-rating the *policy* risk, not the product.

---

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Justification: A Weak governance structure (74.9% founder voting control, eroding board independence) combines with High and *increasing* regulatory risk that has already produced two adverse outcomes in twelve months (Taiwan deal blocked; Indonesia/Vietnam commission caps). These are mutually reinforcing — the entrenched control structure insulates management from accountability for policy-driven margin erosion. Partially offset by genuine operational momentum, a clean US enforcement record, and the strongest possible insider signal (CEO buying $30M at the lows). Not Critical, because the underlying franchise is growing and profitable and no fraud, enforcement action, or solvency-threatening event is present.
