### 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**RISK 1 — Indonesian commission-cap / gig-worker regulation compresses Mobility economics**
- **Probability: 100% (already enacted) | Impact: Medium, rising**
- President Prabowo signed regulation cutting platform commission on two-wheeled ride-hailing from ~20% to **8%, with driver revenue share mandated at a minimum of 92%**, effective **1 July 2026**. Grab and GoTo confirmed the cut. Platforms face additional obligations on commission transparency and driver welfare.
- Indonesia is 21.2% of revenue and Mobility carries Grab's highest margins, so a 12pt gross commission compression is a direct margin hit rather than a demand shock.
- **Mitigant:** Maybank assessed the impact on Grab as **"limited"** (technode, 5 May 2026), likely because two-wheeler commissions are already partly offset by platform fees and incentive clawbacks; Q3 2026 still delivered adj. EBITDA of **$237M, +73% YoY**, with all three segments profitable and guidance raised. **Probability of a follow-on cap extension to four-wheelers: medium** — that is the true tail risk and is not yet priced.

**RISK 2 — The regulatory model is now explicitly "no consolidation" — GoTo and Trans-cab both blocked**
- **Probability of GoTo deal re-attempt: medium | Probability of approval as structured: low**
- The ~$7B **Grab–GoTo merger stalled/collapsed in July 2025** under Indonesian Competition Commission (KPPU) scepticism. Legal analysis (Soemadipradja & Taher, via *Asian Legal Business*) puts combined shares at **>90% ride-hailing and ~85% food delivery/digital payments** — a near-automatic presumption of anti-competitive effect, with **no "failing firm" defence in Indonesian law**.
- Critically, **KPPU reviews mergers only post-closing** — it cannot pre-clear, so any attempt carries retroactive unwind/penalty risk.
- Precedent stack is uniformly adverse: Singapore's CCCS found the 2018 **Grab–Uber** transaction infringed s54 and imposed directions + financial penalties; CCCS issued a **provisional decision blocking Grab's acquisition of Trans-cab** (2024), and Grab **terminated and withdrew** rather than litigate.
- **Implication:** the market's GoTo-merger optionality is worth close to zero on a regulatory basis. Equally, the regulatory moat protecting Grab's dominance is also what prevents it from buying the last mile of share.

**RISK 3 — ASEAN-wide gig-worker / platform-regulation contagion**
- **Probability: high (Vietnam already live) | Impact: Medium**
- Vietnam's **National Competition Commission confirmed on 11 Sep 2026** it is reviewing driver complaints against Grab and had demanded Grab and rival platforms produce pricing, fee and driver-partner policies, warning it "will investigate and take action" if violations are found. This followed a two-day driver boycott call. A Hanoi driver estimates platform retention reaches **~35% typically and up to 50% on short trips**.
- Vietnam officials also fined a social-media account holder for posts claiming Grab was "paralysed" — a reminder that Vietnam's information-control regime cuts both ways for the company.
- **Indonesia's 8% cap is now a regional template.** The Philippines and Thailand are the obvious next candidates. Note also that Vietnam's pricing had already been raised (fee increases from 28 Apr 2026) — evidence of pass-through capability, which is the key mitigant.

### 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Antitrust / competition — the dominant legal theme**
- **KPPU (Indonesia):** fined Grab Indonesia **Rp30bn (~$2M)** for unfair business practices (discriminating against non-TPI partner drivers); Grab appealed. Historically the *statutory ceiling* — but KPPU has since escalated sharply (Rp202.5bn against Google, upheld on cassation), so the enforcement ceiling is no longer a meaningful cap on risk.
- **CCCS (Singapore):** Grab–Uber (2018) infringement finding and penalties; Trans-cab acquisition abandoned in Jul 2024 after an adverse provisional decision.
- **Vietnam VCC:** prior finding that Grab/Uber could exceed 50% post-deal share; current fee-structure inquiry open as of Sep 2026.
- **Verdict:** no single fine is material to a $13.35B cap; the recurring pattern is. Competition regulators in every core market have Grab as a designated watch-target.

**Securities litigation — largely resolved**
- *In re Grab Holdings Limited Securities Litigation*: **$80M cash settlement**, preliminarily approved 13 Jan 2025, final approval granted. Alleges driver-supply and incentive-related misstatements around the de-SPAC period. This removes the principal legal overhang; residual risk is limited to opt-outs/claims administration.

**Financial services perimeter expansion (new legal risk)**
- Grab agreed to acquire a **60% stake in Atome Financial for $1.49B** (announced 15 Sep 2026), with **completion targeted Q3 2027 "subject to regulatory approvals"** in multiple jurisdictions, and an agreed path to the remaining 40% ~2 years later.
- This materially widens the licensing base: **OJK** (Indonesia), **BNM** (Malaysia), **MAS** (Singapore), **BSP** (Philippines), **BOT** (Thailand) as consumer-credit/BNPL regulators, on top of existing GXS digital-bank licences.
- **Multi-jurisdiction approval is the single clearest near-term closing risk.** The FY2025 20-F explicitly flags "expanding compliance exposure from digital banking and lending" and credit/ECL estimation risk.

**Data privacy & localisation**
- **Indonesia PDP Law:** administrative fines up to **2% of annual revenue** — a ~$67M+ exposure at FY2025 revenue, for consent/processing failures.
- **Vietnam:** Decree 13/2023 (PDPD) data-localisation and cross-border transfer requirements, now migrating into a fuller PDP Law.
- Across 8 markets with 47.2M MTUs and an integrated payments/lending stack, this is a structurally un-diversifiable compliance cost base.

**Corporate structure / shareholder legal recourse**
- Cayman-incorporated, Singapore-HQ, Nasdaq-listed ADS. Minority holders depend on Cayman corporate law and Singapore/ASEAN courts for governance remedies — a structural weakness relative to a US domestic filer, and consistent with the 20-F's "local political risks from operating as a foreign owned entity" disclosure.

| Flag | Assessment |

This is an inherent-risk, not evidenced-violation, flag. **Monitor, do not discount.** |
| **Sanctions — Myanmar** | Myanmar sits inside the <1% "Rest of SEA" line, but driver/merchant onboarding creates third-party screening exposure to designated persons. The 2025–26 **US easing** of some Myanmar designations (Reuters, Jul 2025; Al Jazeera, Oct 2026) lowers near-term friction but raises reputational/ESG scrutiny from campaign groups (Justice For Myanmar, UN OHCHR). **Immaterial financially; material to ESG-mandated holders.** |

| **Gig-worker reclassification** | 20-F flags potential reclassification of driver-partners as employees. This is a **contingent liability that would be balance-sheet-relevant**, not a fine — no jurisdiction has yet enacted it, but Indonesia's 92% revenue-share mandate is a functional half-step. |
| **Tariff / export-control** | **Minimal direct exposure.** Grab is a domestic-consumption platform, not a goods exporter; it is not caught in US–China export controls or Section 232 tariffs. Indirect channel only: US tariff deals with Indonesia/Malaysia (~19%) raising input costs and squeezing consumer wallets. |

### 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **MEDIUM**

*(Sub-component ratings: Geopolitical — Low/Medium; Regulatory — **High**; Litigation — Low; Compliance — Medium)*

**Rationale for MEDIUM:** Grab's jurisdictions carry no war risk, no expropriation precedent against the platform sector, and no capital-repatriation controls on foreign portfolio investment. Singapore's rule of law anchors the corporate and treasury function, and the principal securities class action has been settled for $80M and is behind the company. Direct tariff, sanctions and export-control exposure is negligible.

**Why it is not LOW — and why the regulatory sub-rating is High:**

1. **~60% of revenue** (Malaysia, Indonesia, Vietnam) sits in markets that have each taken live, adverse regulatory action against Grab's pricing model within the last 18 months.
2. **The competition regime is structurally closed to consolidation.** GoTo is effectively unapprovable as structured; Trans-cab was abandoned. The upside optionality embedded in sell-side theses is not supported by the legal record.
3. **The 8% Indonesian commission cap is a regional template, not an isolated event.** Extension to four-wheelers in Indonesia is the single largest unpriced regulatory tail.
4. **Atome adds a Q3 2027 multi-regulator closing condition** and permanently widens the licence perimeter into credit risk.

**What would move the rating:** → **HIGH** on extension of the 8% cap to Indonesian four-wheelers, or a formal Vietnamese competition investigation with penalty exposure. → **LOW** on enactment of a national gig-worker framework that codifies the 92% share *ceiling* on platform take-rate across ASEAN without further compression, removing the regulatory-trend uncertainty currently overhanging the stock.
