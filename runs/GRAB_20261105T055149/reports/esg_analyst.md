## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM**

- **Carbon intensity:** Scope 3 dominates (~96% historically; 2021 total ~1.5 Mt CO₂e). Management now reports *intensity* only: **−3.7% (mobility) and −1.4% (deliveries) gCO₂/km in 2025**, with **9% of distance on low-emission modes** (7% in 2024). Absolute emissions are not disclosed in headline form — a material gap given GMV growth.
- **Target quality:** "Carbon neutral by 2040" is **neutrality, not net-zero**, and is partly delivered through offsets (**>772,000 tCO₂e avoided/removed via purchased credits in 2025**). Grab is **not SBTi-committed** and **does not disclose to CDP** — weaker than Uber/Delivery Hero peers on target integrity.
- **Physical risk:** Low direct asset exposure (asset-light), but elevated *operational* exposure to SEA flood/heat events (fleet availability, delivery demand volatility, driver safety).
- **Packaging:** 10,142 t of single-use packaging reduced/reused/recycled; 2040 zero-packaging-waste-in-nature target. Regulatory direction (extended producer responsibility in SG/PH/TH) is a modest cost headwind.
- **Verdict:** No stranded-asset exposure and no manufacturing footprint; risk is reputational/regulatory, not capex.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment — **HIGH**

- **Livelihood/political risk is the core issue.** Indonesia's 8% commission cap compresses Mobility take-rate by design. Maybank estimates ~6% gross / **~3% net FY27 adj. EBITDA impact**, rising to **~10% in a downside case if extended to four-wheel**, with mitigation (platform fees, dynamic pricing, incentive reset) pulling net impact to **~2–5%**. Peer evidence of real damage: GoTo's ride-hailing/core profit fell ~25% y/y post-cap. Scope creep to four-wheelers and to deliveries is the key tail risk.
- **Classification & benefits:** Drivers are independent contractors without statutory severance/minimum wage in most markets. Malaysia's **Gig Workers Bill (Sept 2025)** and Indonesia's draft gig-worker law plus possible social-security/health mandates are live cost items. Grab's existing driver insurance partly pre-funds this.
- **Positives (evidence-based, not marketing):** **$15.3bn earned by driver/merchant-partners (+19% y/y)**; **189,000+ women and PWD driver-partners (+40%)**; **856,000 MSMEs** onboarded; **99.9% of rides with no reported safety incident** and −10% incidents per million rides. These support social licence and are commercially load-bearing (supply retention).
- **Workforce (direct):** No material direct-labour controversies; 12,012 employees; regional restructuring (2023 layoffs) is a normal-course execution risk rather than an ESG controversy.
- **Product/consumer:** GrabFin lending growth raises conduct risk; intervention risk here is regulatory, not litigation-led so far.

## 4. Governance Quality Score — **4 / 10**

**Rationale (weighted):**
- **Shareholder rights (1/10):** Dual-class structure doubled to **90 votes/Class B share** in 2026, entrenching ~75% voting control on ~4% economics. This is at the extreme end globally and is a structural governance discount, not a temporary issue. It also makes hostile bids impossible and entrenchment risk real.
- **Board (4/10):** Board is majority-independent by headcount (independent directors include John Rogers — retiring from Board & Audit Committee eff. 31 Oct 2026 — and Steven Tishman, Audit Committee from 1 Dec 2025), but **CEO/Chairman duality persists (Anthony Tan is Group CEO, Co-Founder and Chairman)** with founder voting control, so independence has limited practical force. Ongoing audit-committee turnover (Rogers' retirement) is a monitoring item.
- **Compensation alignment (5/10):** Shift to profitable growth and a **$400m buyback** plus **~$30m CEO open-market purchase** are genuine alignment signals; but super-voting shares mean compensation cannot be contested.
- **Ethics/anticorruption (5/10):** Programme exists; Myanmar presence (since 2017, exit announced 2021 but operations/support continued in some form) carries sanctions/human-rights screening complexity — no enforcement action identified against Grab, but the jurisdictional exposure is real.
- **Disclosure (5/10):** Publishes full ESG report with management-reviewed metrics and limited assurance posture; but no CDP, no SBTi, no absolute emissions headline, no formal CSRD-grade assurance.

## 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **Indonesia:** Perpres 27/2026 commission cap & driver reclassification; possible social-security/health mandates; draft gig-worker law (DPR 2026 priority); KPPU oversight; OJK responsible-lending and interest-cap rules for GrabFin.
- **Malaysia:** Gig Workers Bill (2025) — platform-worker protections, contribution obligations.
- **Singapore:** PDPA enforcement (active), Workplace Fairness Act, MAS/OJK-adjacent conduct rules for the digital-bank JV; SGX/ISSB-aligned climate reporting *not* directly applicable (Nasdaq-listed).
- **EU CSRD:** **Effectively out of scope** — no material EU turnover; exposure runs through **EU-domiciled debt/equity holders' Scope 3 reporting demands**, not a legal obligation.
- **CBAM:** **Not applicable** (no CBAM goods).
- **SEC climate rule:** Stayed/immaterial; existing SEC risk-factor and MD&A disclosure obligations apply unchanged.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** None identified in public disclosure — a positive (no technical default trigger on sustainability KPIs).

## 7. Overall ESG Rating — **AVERAGE (governance-tier: LAGGARD)**

Company-cited **MSCI "AA" (self-reported, historical)** is not corroborated by our fundamental read: strong social-programme and disclosure effort, but **founder super-voting at 90x, CEO/Chair duality, absence of SBTi/CDP/absolute emissions, and an unresolved government-mandated pricing intervention** are precisely the factors rating agencies historically under-weight. Treat third-party scores as lagging and disclosure-driven.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication — **NEUTRAL to NEGATIVE (risk-multiple, not thesis-breaking)**

- **Not a thesis driver, but a real valuation discount.** The Indonesia cap is a *political* constraint that no efficiency programme removes: base case 3–5% of FY27 adj. EBITDA, 10% downside if four-wheel is captured. The market's −30% YTD drawdown is partly the repricing of exactly this ESG-to-EPS channel.
- **Governance is the durable issue.** With ~75% of votes held on ~4% of economics, minority holders own the economics but not the decisions — the GoTo/Atome/AV investments and buyback-vs-growth capital allocation cannot be influenced. Rational response: demand a **10–15% governance/regulatory-risk discount** on fair value rather than an exclusion.
- **What would change the view (to Positive):** (i) SBTi-validated net-zero with absolute Scope 1/2/3 disclosure; (ii) spread of a *stable* commission regime rather than step-changes, and formalised driver-benefit funding; (iii) any sunset of the Class B 90-vote structure or split of Chair/CEO; (iv) reclassification risk crystallising into an adverse antitrust remedy on consolidation.
- **Bottom line for the memo:** GRAB screens well on "doing good" (driver earnings, MSMEs, women/PWD participation, safety) but **poorly on who controls the cash flows and who can reprice them**. ESG here is a *cost-of-capital and regulatory-risk* input, not an alpha source.
