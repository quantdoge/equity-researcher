# Non-Financial Risk Assessment — Genmab A/S (GMAB)

**Snapshot:** Copenhagen-based antibody therapeutics (Healthcare/Biotech) · Market cap ≈ $35.2B · 3,119 FTE · CEO Jan G.J. van de Winkel (since June 2010) · US ADR, Danish domicile · beta 0.645.

**Data-quality caveat up front:** GMAB files as a foreign private issuer (20-F/6-K). The filing-retrieval tools returned no 20-F, no DEF 14A, and no SEC enforcement hits — so the "clean" EDGAR enforcement result is *partly a coverage artifact*, not proof of a clean record. Insider-share figures below carry an internal inconsistency (see §3) and one apparent misattribution; treat directionally.

---

## 1. Governance Quality Rating: **Adequate**

**Positives**
- Chair/CEO roles are separated. Board refreshed at the 19-Mar-2026 AGM: **Deirdre P. Connelly** appointed Chair, **Pernille Erenbjerg** Deputy Chair — both credible, independent life-sciences/finance profiles.
- Executive pay is heavily equity/performance-weighted (CEO total comp ≈ $10.7M, ~86% non-salary) — reasonably shareholder-aligned.
- Capital returned via a buyback initiated 17-Feb-2026 (up to 342,130 shares) — offsets some insider selling.
- No related-party or accounting-integrity red flags surfaced.

**Weaknesses**
- **CEO tenure of ~16 years** with no disclosed succession plan. Combined with founder-era identity, this is a genuine key-person exposure.
- **Overboarding / bandwidth conflict:** van de Winkel stepped off the Leo Pharma board in Aug-2024 citing *"increasing workload at Genmab,"* then accepted a **Novo Nordisk board nomination**. Taking on a large-cap board seat after citing capacity constraints invites legitimate governance questions — and Novo is a Genmab collaborator, raising interlock optics.
- Board-level churn (new Chair + Deputy in the same AGM) during the most consequential year in the pipeline's history reduces institutional continuity.

## 2. Regulatory & Legal Risk Summary — **elevated**

| Matter | Status | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **AbbVie Inc. v. Genmab A/S et al.**, W.D. Wash. 2:25-cv-00510 (Judge Tana Lin), filed 21-Mar-2025, Defend Trade Secrets Act | **Pending.** Genmab moved to dismiss; unresolved as of latest reporting | **High** |
| **DARZALEX royalty arbitrations** vs. J&J/Janssen | **Lost 2022; lost appeal 2024.** Genmab sought ~$405M in milestone/extended-royalty payments — denied | Moderate (realized) |
| **Epcoritamab EPCORE DLBCL-1** — OS failed as sole U.S. primary endpoint | Topline Jan-2026; formal **"clarification" 23-Jul-2026** | **High** |
| SEC/DOJ enforcement | None identified (coverage caveat) | Low |
| Pending approvals | Petosemtamab (HNSCC, CRC), Rina-S (ovarian) → FDA/EMA filings, launches guided from 2027 | Standard |

**The AbbVie suit is the single most important non-financial risk in the file.** AbbVie alleges ProfoundBio — acquired by Genmab in 2024 — misappropriated trade secrets covering **disaccharide-based ADC technology**, naming two former AbbVie employees (Han, Gavrilyuk), and alleges Genmab was *"intentionally and willfully blind."* Critically, this technology underpins **rinatabart sesutecan (Rina-S)**, the FRα ADC that just delivered a positive Phase 3 (RAINFOL-02, PFS and OS, 23-Oct-2026). An adverse ruling — injunction, damages, or a clouded IP estate — would hit the asset the market just re-rated the stock on. It also sits awkwardly alongside the AbbVie co-development alliance on epcoritamab: **Genmab's largest partner is simultaneously its litigation adversary.**

**The Epkinly OS miss is a regulatory-lifecycle risk.** Epcoritamab holds *accelerated* approvals (DLBCL 2023, FL 2024, FL combo Nov-2025) granted on response rate. A failed confirmatory OS endpoint exposes the DLBCL indication to FDA withdrawal proceedings. The fact that a **clarification** was needed months after topline — restating that OS was *the sole* U.S. primary endpoint and *was not met* — is itself a disclosure-quality flag, even if immaterial in substance.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **Single-partner royalty concentration.** DARZALEX Q3-2026 net sales were **$4,239M** ($2,528M U.S. / $1,711M ROW), on which Genmab earns a **12–20% royalty** from J&J. This one asset, controlled by a counterparty that has already beaten Genmab twice in arbitration, is the dominant profit pool — and faces eventual daratumumab patent expiry. Genmab does not control commercial execution, pricing, or lifecycle strategy for its own largest revenue line.
- **M&A diligence / IP provenance risk.** The ProfoundBio deal imported not just Rina-S but a trade-secret lawsuit and two named ex-AbbVie employees. If the AbbVie allegations have substance, the diligence process failed on the exact dimension that generates value in ADC platforms.
- **Key-person and clinical-binary concentration.** A 16-year CEO, an unusually small number of shots-on-goal (petosemtamab, Rina-S, epcoritamab), and readout-driven valuation. The stock moved **from $23.85 (2-Jun-26) to $57.29 (28-Oct-26)** — including **+28% in a single session** on LiGeR-HN1. Reported beta of 0.645 materially understates realized event risk. Cybersecurity: no Genmab-specific breach identified — not a flagged concern.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **Moderate**

Genuinely strong current momentum: petosemtamab cut risk of death **50%** (OS HR 0.50; 95% CI 0.37–0.67) in 1L PD-L1+ HNSCC; positive RAINFOL-02 in platinum-resistant ovarian cancer; new Incyte collaboration in KRAS G12D mCRC; three positive Phase 3 readouts framed as a 2027 launch cycle. Press and analyst tone (Barron's, Jefferies "hat trick") is highly favorable.

The reputational overhang is **litigation-shaped, not consumer-shaped**: "willfully blind to trade secret theft" is an ugly characterization of a company whose brand is scientific integrity, and it lands with the same partner it co-commercializes Epkinly with. No ESG controversy, product recall, or patient-safety scandal found. Note the *asymmetry*: reputational capital is currently at a peak, so the downside from any litigation or FDA setback is amplified.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Not Critical — there is no fraud indicator, no enforcement action, no cyber incident, no going-concern or control breakdown, and governance is structurally sound. But three risks compound: (a) an **active trade-secret suit against the company's key growth asset**, (b) **royalty concentration** on a partner-controlled product with an adverse arbitration record, and (c) a **failed confirmatory endpoint** on an accelerated-approval drug. Each is individually manageable; together they mean a large share of the equity's value rests on outcomes decided by courts and regulators rather than by management execution.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🔴 AbbVie DTSA litigation (2:25-cv-00510) — unresolved, and directly targets Rina-S.** Escalate for legal review of injunction/damages exposure and IP-encumbrance disclosure. Request the motion-to-dismiss ruling status and any settlement discussions. *Highest priority.*
2. **🔴 Epcoritamab accelerated-approval exposure.** OS failure on the sole U.S. primary endpoint creates a live FDA withdrawal pathway in DLBCL. Quantify revenue at risk and monitor for FDA correspondence/advisory committee.
3. **🟠 Partner-adversary conflict.** AbbVie is both co-developer/commercial partner on Epkinly and plaintiff against Genmab. Governance and information-barrier review warranted.
4. **🟠 CEO overboarding and succession.** Novo Nordisk board nomination following a Leo Pharma exit attributed to Genmab workload; no disclosed succession plan after 16 years.
5. **🟡 Disclosure quality.** The July-2026 "clarification" on the EPCORE endpoint sequence warrants a check on IR messaging controls ahead of 2027 filing cycles.
6. **🟡 Insider signal.** No executive open-market buying into a doubled stock; CFO sold in Feb-2026. Not alarming, but worth monitoring against the buyback.

**Bottom line:** Buy-side should treat GMAB's non-financial risk as **High but idiosyncratic and litigable rather than systemic** — the fundamental story has rarely looked better, and that is precisely why the AbbVie suit and the Epkinly confirmatory failure deserve explicit legal diligence rather than a footnote.
