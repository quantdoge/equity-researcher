## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW (narrowing)**

Genmab is one of the finest antibody-engineering shops in the world, but **it is a licensor, not the commercial owner of its best asset**. Economic reality:

- **DARZALEX (daratumumab)** — licensed to J&J. J&J net sales were **$14.3bn in 2025**, guided **$15.6–16.4bn in 2026**; Genmab's royalty is ~**$2.83bn (2026E midpoint)** — roughly **65% of group revenue**. Genmab does not control price, promotion, lifecycle strategy, or the patent estate; it collects a contractual, tiered, **finite** royalty that erodes when the molecule loses exclusivity (composition-of-matter protection runs out in the early 2030s; biosimilar erosion is a matter of "when," not "if").
- **DuoBody/HexaBody platform** and the newer proprietary portfolio (Epkinly/epcoritamab with AbbVie, Talvey, Tepkinly) are genuine intangible assets — but they are **scientific capability**, not structural pricing power. Capability moats require continuous re-proving every cycle.
- **No network effects, no switching costs, no efficient-scale geometry.** Gross margins of 92–100% reflect royalty/COGS-light economics, not customer captivity.
- **Moat trend: narrowing.** Revenue concentration in one molecule; the loss of the Tepezza royalty; and a strategic pivot to debt-funded, own-product commercialization. Five years ago Genmab was a net-cash royalty machine earning ~22% ROIC; today it is a levered, integration-stage company earning ~11%.

**Verdict: Narrow moat, deteriorating.** I can understand this business (circle of competence: yes), which is why the *price* — not the quality — is the deciding variable.

**Strengths:** elite gross margins; consistently >100% FCF conversion (owner earnings ≈ net income, capex is trivially small at ~1% of revenue); ~20% organic revenue growth; a 40% adjusted operating margin. This is a cash machine.

**Weaknesses:** (i) **ROIC has collapsed from 22.5% to 11.1%** — the $8bn Merus deal put ~$9.5bn of goodwill/intangibles (74% of total assets) onto the balance sheet, inflating invested capital. On current returns Genmab is **earning approximately its cost of capital, not above it** — the single most important deterioration in the thesis. (ii) Balance sheet flipped from ~$1.3bn net cash to **$3.7bn net debt** ($5.4bn term loans A & B, floating rate). Leverage is serviceable (FCF ~$1.1bn ≈ 3.2x net debt) but the fortress is gone. (iii) Revenue concentration. (iv) No dividend — fine, given buybacks — but 2025 buybacks fell to $430m from DKK 4.0bn.

**Score rationale:** 7/10 — top-decile unit economics and cash conversion, marked down for concentration, the post-Merus return-on-capital dilution, and the loss of balance-sheet optionality.
