## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

### Risk 1 — US trade-policy architecture is now the single largest driver of earnings (Probability: ~Certain / Impact: Very High, two-sided)
- **Section 232 proclamation signed 6-Aug-2026, effective 4-Dec-2026:** Minimum Import Prices of **$21/kg polysilicon, $100/kg ingots/wafers, $0.22/W cells, $0.38/W modules**, plus an added **15% ad valorem** duty on derivatives (capped 15% combined for JP/KR/TW/CH/EU; UK 10%). (Anza; Crane Worldwide; Sheppard Mullin; Whitehouse.gov)
- **Structural advantage:** First Solar's CdTe modules are *not* polysilicon derivatives, so US-made output sits **outside the MIP-covered supply chain** — a genuine moat versus Chinese/SE-Asian crystalline-silicon imports. Q3'26 bookings of 1.4 GW at **$0.36/W** and management's push for higher US module prices post-232 confirm the pricing tailwind. (Seeking Alpha 30-Oct-26; Motley Fool 1-Nov-26)
- **But demand destruction and legal instability cut the other way:** Q3'26 revenue fell 23% y/y to $1.23bn and FY26 guidance was *cut* on **delayed customer decisions** and weaker demand (Zacks 30-Oct-26). Separately, US courts have struck down IEEPA tariffs and Section 122 tariffs as unlawful (D&O Diary 2-Jul-26) — meaning the *legal basis* of the tariff wall can shift retroactively, creating refund/re-pricing risk on booked contracts.
- **Midterm overlay:** Democrats swept both chambers on 3-Nov-2026; FSLR jumped ~9% and William Blair upgraded on state-level utility-solar support (Barron's 5-Nov-26) — but by 9-Nov the stock was the **worst performer in the S&P 500** as "blue wave optimism" was judged premature. Policy beta is extreme.

### Risk 2 — Profitability is a creature of US tax credits; FEOC rules add a compliance cliff (Probability: Medium (30-40% adverse change by 2028) / Impact: High, ~$1.3-1.6bn/yr)
- **§45X** contributes roughly **$0.17/W of economics, ~55% of a ~$0.30/W ASP**, with quarterly credit guidance of **$330-400m** (Yahoo Finance/Enki analysis; Q1'26 deck). 45X is the majority of today's profit — one Seeking Alpha piece notes FSLR "operated profitably before the IRA," but the *magnitude* is policy-made.
- **OBBBA FEOC "material assistance" thresholds** bite from 2026: solar components must clear **50% in 2026, rising to 85% by 2030**, with IRS **Notice 2026-15** governing tracing (Baker Tilly; IRS; Bracewell). Any Chinese-owned supplier content in the CdTe chain jeopardises credits and creates recapture exposure.
- **Political scenario risk:** Democratic control removes near-term repeal tail risk, but a future reversal (2028) or a credit-phase-down deal in a divided Congress remains the single largest binary to FSLR's earnings power. Title/45Y/48E ITC sunsetting under OBBBA already reshapes US project economics.

### Risk 3 — The international manufacturing footprint is being arbitraged away by US trade action (Probability: High / Impact: Medium-High, ~$100-300m of underutilization/impairment drag)
- **Solar IV AD/CVD finalised 11-Sep-2026: combined rates reach 249.13% for India** (up to 268.06% Indonesia, 219.10% Laos) after preliminary AD margins of 123.04%/35.17%/22.46% and CVD rates up to 143.3% (PV Tech 14-Sep-26; Reuters 23-Apr-26; Solar Power World 26-Feb-26). Follow-on cases cover **Ethiopia and South Korea**.
- Consequence: **India-origin modules are economically barred from the US**, so the Tamil Nadu plant must serve domestic Indian demand (subject to ALMM/local-content rules) while FSLR **"onshores final production processes"** for Series 6 Plus at Gaffney, SC. This is a defensible strategy, but it converts a low-cost asset into a sub-scale one — the exact dynamic alleged in the securities suit (deliberate Malaysia/Vietnam underutilisation).
- **India macro/political:** 50% US tariffs on Indian goods imposed Aug-2025 (partly Russia-oil linked), partially eased after a Feb-2026 understanding; Indian officials read the US midterms as modest trade relief (Bloomberg via gCaptain, 4-Nov-26). INR depreciation, ALMM content rules and repatriation are monitorable but not currently binding constraints. No sanctions exposure.
- **Critical-minerals tail risk (fold into Risk 3):** CdTe requires tellurium; China imposed export licensing on critical minerals effective Jan-2026 with extraterritorial reach (CFR; IEA; CSIS). FSLR's recycling/co-located recovery centres in OH, AL, LA, India, Malaysia and Vietnam are the mitigation, but this is an un-hedged single-source-ish input.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

1. **Customs origin & "substantial transformation" — AMBER (highest-severity flag).** FSLR is importing internationally-produced Series 6 Plus laminates and performing "final production processes" in South Carolina. With **249% India AD/CVD, MIP certification/enforcement-by-entry-documentation, and DOJ trade fraud now a stated priority**, any challenge to the US-origin claim would carry duty exposure plus False Claims/18 U.S.C. §1001 tail risk. This is the intersection of trade law, tax-credit eligibility and securities disclosure — and is precisely the fact pattern plaintiffs are probing.
2. **§45X FEOC/material-assistance documentation — AMBER.** 50%→85% thresholds require supplier-level ownership tracing; credit recapture plus restatement risk if tracing fails.
3. **Disclosure controls — AMBER.** Active 10b-5 class action; prior $350m settlement; guidance cut twice in 12 months (Jan-26, Oct-26).
4. **Tariff-cliff "de-booking"** — commercial/contract risk rather than compliance, but Q3'26 confirms customer deferrals.
5. **FCPA/sanctions/data privacy — GREEN.** No evidence of exposure.

---

## 5. Overall Geopolitical / Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale.** Traditional geopolitical risk is *low* — FSLR operates exclusively in rule-of-law jurisdictions (US, India, Malaysia, Vietnam, EU, Japan, Australia) with no sanctions, expropriation or currency-control exposure. The rating is driven instead by **extreme policy concentration**: ~90% of revenue in one market, ~55% of unit economics from one tax credit, and a tariff regime that simultaneously protects its pricing (Section 232 MIP) and destroys its demand and its offshore cost base. The company is, effectively, a **levered bet on US industrial policy continuity**, with a live securities class action, an amber customs-origin issue, and CdTe critical-mineral supply concentration as the tail risks.

**Watch list:** (i) Section 232 MIP implementation and whether CdTe/Series 6 imports get swept in via "derivatives" definitions after 4-Dec-2026; (ii) any Congressional action on §45X under the new Democratic majority; (iii) E.D.N.Y. lead-plaintiff consolidation and motion-to-dismiss timing; (iv) Solar IV administrative reviews / circumvention petitions touching Indian-origin content; (v) China tellurium licensing.
