# First Solar (FSLR) — Growth Investment Analysis
**Price $180.98 | Market cap $19.5B | EV $17.1B | Net cash $1.5B | Analyst median PT ~$265 (+46%)**

---

## 1. Growth Story

First Solar is the only scaled, fully-integrated solar module manufacturer in the Western Hemisphere that sits entirely outside the Chinese crystalline-silicon supply chain — a structurally privileged position that Section 232 polysilicon tariffs (effective Dec 4, 2026) and FEOC rules now convert into a durable US price floor. But the thesis has **morphed from a volume-growth story into a margin/ASP story**: 2026 revenue is *contracting* (guidance midpoint $4.75B, −9% YoY) as post-OBBBA demand timing and a bookings slowdown bite, while gross margin has inflected to **~58%** in Q3 2026 on pricing discipline plus 45X manufacturing credits. The real long-duration option is the 1 GW perovskite pilot line (H1 2027) — a path to closing the efficiency gap versus TOPCon/tandem silicon.

## 3. TAM Assessment & Penetration

- **Global module TAM:** ~600 GWdc installed in 2026 → ~1 TW/yr by 2030; module value ~$70–100B globally, but prices are bifurcated (China ~$0.10/W vs US ~$0.27–0.32/W). **The US is the profit pool, not the volume pool.**
- **US utility-scale:** ~50–60 GW/yr; module value ~$15–18B. FSLR's US share is ~30–40% of utility-scale supply — a genuinely defensible position versus First Solar's **~2–3% share of global GW**.
- **Backlog: 44.0 GW / ~$13.5B through 2030** (~$307/kW ASP). That is only **~2.3 years of revenue coverage** and has eroded from prior peaks — this, not valuation, is what de-rated the stock.
- **TAM expansion vectors:** data-center load growth/grid electrification, reshoring of manufacturing capex, and Section 232 minimum import prices effectively enlarging the *serviceable* US TAM by displacing imports. Penetration upside is share-of-US, not share-of-world.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Dec 4, 2026** — Section 232 polysilicon derivative tariffs + minimum import price take effect. Structural ASP floor; the single clearest near-term re-rating trigger.
- **Feb 23, 2027** — Q4/FY26 results + **first FY2027 guidance** (consensus Q4 EPS $5.18; FY27E rev $5.73B / EPS $23.25). Bookings re-acceleration under project-specific contracts is the swing variable.
- **H1 2027** — **Ohio 1 GW perovskite pilot line** operational readiness; first Series 7 **CuRe** conversion at the India facility. This is the innovation-moat proof point.
- **H1 2027** — Malaysia facility decision (footprint rationalization → capex/FCF relief, possible restructuring charge).
- **Ongoing** — Final ITC/FEOC guidance and any anti-stocking rules; clarity either unlocks deferred US procurement or extends the air pocket.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **BUY** (moderate conviction, not Strong Buy)

At **10.3x FY26E and 7.8x FY27E earnings**, **6.4x EV/EBITDA**, **2.0x book**, and a **~6–8% FCF yield**, the market is pricing FSLR as a terminal, credit-dependent commodity manufacturer with no growth. That is too harsh for a business with 58% gross margins, a 47 Rule-of-40 score, a Western-hemisphere supply-chain moat that policy is actively widening, a $500M buyback, and net cash. Consensus PTs ($199–$328, median ~$265) imply ~46% upside; even the low target is +10%.

But I will not call it Strong Buy, because the mandate is *growth*, and the growth is not currently organic-volume-driven: **revenue is shrinking in 2026, the backlog covers barely two years, forward revenue CAGR is ~7%, estimate revisions are negative, and FY2029 ($34.01 EPS) is the consensus peak — earnings *decline* in 2030 as 45X steps down.** Strip out ~7¢/W of 45X credits (~$1.1B, ~$8/share) and 2026 "core" EPS is closer to $9 (~20x). This is a policy-margin compounder masquerading as a growth stock.

**12-month framework:** Base case **~$255–270** (11–12x FY27E EPS, below the analyst median), requiring only that FY27 guidance lands near consensus and bookings stop deteriorating. Bull case $320+ if the Dec 4 tariffs lift ASPs and perovskite pilot data de-risks the efficiency gap.

## 7. Key Risks

1. **Credit cliff (largest risk):** 45X phases down 25%/50%/75% in 2030/31/32. Consensus already models EPS falling from $34.01 (2029) to $30.51 (2030). This caps the terminal multiple and makes long-duration DCF math hostile.
2. **Backlog/execution:** 44 GW / $13.5B is thin relative to a ~25 GW capacity base; the shift from framework to project-specific contracts raises lumpiness and obscures visibility. Guidance has been cut twice in 2026.
3. **Policy dependence on both sides:** the same tariffs and FEOC rules that protect ASPs could be softened, exemptions carved, or ITC guidance land adversely; anti-stocking rules could defer procurement further.
4. **Technology gap:** CdTe module efficiency (~19–19.5%) trails TOPCon c-Si (~23%) and perovskite tandems (27%+ in lab). In land/BOS-constrained projects FSLR's per-watt disadvantage must be offset by energy yield and US content premiums. The H1 2027 perovskite pilot is unproven at scale.
5. **International erosion:** India, Malaysia, and other ex-US markets face Chinese oversupply; the Malaysia footprint decision signals structural weakness outside the US profit pool.
6. **Balance sheet/volatility:** net cash fell $2.4B → $1.5B on working capital and capacity build; beta 1.71 and heavy headline sensitivity mean 20–30% drawdowns are routine (witness June→October 2026).
