## 2. Regulatory & Legal Risk Summary

**Active litigation (material):**
- **Securities class action — *Day v. First Solar* (filed 23-Jun-2026, E.D.N.Y.)**: §10(b)/Rule 10b-5. Class period **26-Feb-2025 to 24-Feb-2026**. Alleges the company misrepresented tariff-driven risks — costs of **idle Malaysia/Vietnam factories**, reduced utilization, **large customers defaulting on multi-year agreements**, and US transition/restructuring costs. Reportedly the **fifth tariff-related 10b-5 suit since Aug-2025**; part of a broader "risk-disclosure" litigation wave. Lead-plaintiff deadline was 24-Aug-2026, so the case is now in the motion-to-dismiss phase — a 6–18 month overhang.
- **Swift Current Energy warranty suit**: customer claims remedies **outside** First Solar's limited-liability warranty; management intends to defend. Precedent risk: a plaintiff win could invite replication across a 44 GW installed base and pressure the warranty accrual.
- **IP litigation (First Solar as plaintiff)**: suit vs. **JA Solar and American Panel Solutions (filed 1-Oct-2026, ~$96M damages claimed)** over TOPCon patents; company **dropped its Section 337 ITC complaint in Sept 2026** but continues district-court actions. Counterclaims and invalidity challenges are a two-way risk.

**Policy/regulatory dependency (the dominant regulatory risk):**
- **Section 45X manufacturing credits are reported to represent the majority of current profitability.** FEOC/"prohibited foreign entity" material-assistance rules took effect **1-Jan-2026** with a **50% domestic-content threshold rising to 85% by 2030** — rising compliance burden on a supply chain spanning Malaysia, Vietnam and India.
- **Section 232 polysilicon-derivative tariffs effective 4-Dec-2026** are a tailwind but also the direct cause of **customer decision paralysis** and the guidance cut.
- **Post-midterm (3-Nov-2026) Democratic sweep** re-rated the stock +9% on clean-energy hopes — i.e., a meaningful slice of the equity story is **election/policy beta**, not operating performance.
- No GDPR/CCPA or FDA-style approval exposure of note.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Manufacturing footprint restructuring / 45X arbitrage execution.** A **"binary decision" pending on the 1.8 GW Malaysia plant**, continued **Vietnam underutilization**, and the $330M Gaffney, SC finishing plant (3.4 GW nameplate by mid-2027) mean duplicated costs, idle-capacity drag, restructuring charges and impairment risk — the exact fact pattern alleged in the securities suit. Capacity of 25 GW+ against FY26 volume guidance of **16–17 GW** implies structural under-absorption.
2. **Customer contract durability and demand timing.** Multi-year framework agreements have already been **defaulted on by large customers**; the pivot to project-specific contracts (management explicitly refusing to sell international volume into spot) **reduces backlog visibility** even as the 44 GW / $13.5B backlog looks healthy. Bookings are hostage to Section 232 clarity — a demand-air-pocket risk that is not company-specific but is company-concentrated.
3. **Technology transition and input supply.** The CdTe→**CuRe/perovskite** roadmap (CubicPV and Oxford PV licenses; field testing from Perrysburg 1H27) carries capex and obsolescence risk against Chinese TOPCon/crystalline-silicon cost curves. **Tellurium supply** (a copper-refining byproduct with China-dominated refining) is a single-point input vulnerability; semiconductor-grade CdTe capacity is not easily substituted.
4. **Cyber / key-person (secondary):** the 2026 Corporate Responsibility Report describes a standard cyber risk program; **no disclosed breach was found**. Key-person concentration on Widmar (CEO since 2016) plus CFO turnover (Alex Bradley) is a watch item, mitigated by a deep bench.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: ELEVATED

- **Narrative risk is now "policy-dependent, guidance-cutting, litigated."** Headlines include "worst performer in the S&P 500" (9-Nov-2026), a guidance cut, and coverage framing the earnings base as **subsidy-dependent** — management's rebuttal (profitable before the IRA; unsubsidized India manufacturing) is credible but not yet the market's working assumption.
- **Customer-trust risk** from the Swift Current warranty dispute and customer defaults cuts both ways: First Solar is asserting narrow warranty liability, which protects the balance sheet but can damage developer relationships in a relationship-driven utility channel.
- **ESG positioning is a relative strength:** CdTe has a lower carbon and water footprint than crystalline silicon, the company runs a module recycling program, and it holds a "non-China supply chain" claim that is currently a commercial asset. The durable controversy vector is **cadmium/cadmium-telluride toxicity and end-of-life disposal** activism at the local/permit level — low-grade, persistent, not acute.
- **Human-rights/labor scrutiny** of Malaysian and Vietnamese operations is a latent issue for EU/utility customers with supply-chain due-diligence obligations; nothing acute was surfaced in this review.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH** (policy-driven, not integrity-driven)

**Rationale:** This is not an accounting-fraud or governance-collapse story — controls, board independence and enforcement history are clean. The rating is driven by (a) an **active securities class action built on the company's own tariff risk disclosure**, (b) **earnings concentrated in a politically contestable tax credit (45X)** with rising FEOC content thresholds, (c) a **restructuring decision on Malaysia** with impairment potential, (d) **warranty and IP litigation**, and (e) **one-sided insider selling with zero purchases** into a 25% drawdown. Each is individually manageable; their correlation around a single variable — US trade/energy policy — is what makes the aggregate High.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. ***Day v. First Solar*** (E.D.N.Y., filed 23-Jun-2026): tariff risk-factor disclosure claim naming senior executives. Escalate to disclosure committee / D&O counsel; a motion-to-dismiss denial would be a material negative catalyst and could reset the risk-factor language rubric for the FY26 10-K.
2. **Insider selling cluster with zero buys**: CTO sells bracketing the $288 high and continuing at $209; GC sold ~40% of holdings. Request 10b5-1 adoption-date detail before treating as purely mechanical.
3. **Malaysia "binary decision"** on 1.8 GW: watch for 8-K/10-K impairment, restructuring and severance charges; model the upside-downside of shutdown vs. continued underutilization.
4. **Swift Current warranty litigation**: monitor for replication by other developers and any change in warranty accrual / product-return assumptions.
5. **Earnings quality tied to 45X**: quantify credit contribution to gross margin (58% reported in Q3-26 implies a large subsidy component). Any adverse FEOC guidance, "material assistance" ruling, or credit market disruption is a first-order valuation risk.
6. **Buyback ($500M) alongside guidance cut and peak capex** — challenge capital allocation discipline; ask whether repurchase was sized against a policy catalyst (Section 232 / midterms) rather than intrinsic value.
