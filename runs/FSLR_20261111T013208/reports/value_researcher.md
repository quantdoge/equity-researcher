## FIRST SOLAR, INC. (NASDAQ: FSLR) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $180.98 · Market cap $19.45B · Net cash ~$2.0B (YE26E) · EV ~$17.5B · Beta 1.71*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **NARROW** (and narrowing, not widening)

First Solar is the largest US-domiciled PV module manufacturer and the only scaled maker of cadmium-telluride thin-film modules (~25 GW nameplate capacity after the Alabama/Louisiana/India buildout; 7,900 employees). Its genuine assets:

- **Cost advantage (real):** CdTe uses a fraction of the semiconductor material of crystalline silicon, needs no polysilicon, and is deposited in a highly automated continuous process. FSLR's US cash cost is the lowest of any non-subsidized Western producer.
- **Intangible/technology (real but contested):** a deep CdTe patent estate, plus licensed perovskite IP (Oxford PV, CubicPV) targeting field testing from Perrysburg in H1 2027. But it is simultaneously litigating TOPCon IP and defending a **warranty suit from Swift Current Energy** — IP is a two-way street here.
- **Non-Chinese supply chain (real, and currently the most valuable attribute):** in a market dominated by Chinese c-Si, FSLR is the compliant domestic source. This is what utility buyers pay for.

What is *not* a moat:
- **Zero switching costs.** Modules are bought per watt. Customers are sophisticated utilities and IPPs with no brand loyalty.
- **The profit pool is legislated, not earned.** This is the central finding of my analysis. Strip out Section 45X credits and the business does not clear its cost of capital.

**The decisive test — ROIC ex-subsidy.** Reported ROIC was 15.9% (2025), 16.0% (2024), 11.7% (2023), –0.5% (2022). But roughly **$1.2–1.5B of FY2025–26 gross profit is 45X credits** (a per-watt manufacturing credit booked as a *reduction of cost of revenue*). Removing them, ex-credit NOPAT is roughly $0.4–0.5B on ~$7.2B invested capital — a **core ROIC of ~6%**, versus a cost of equity of ~12–13% (beta 1.71 → 4.3% + 1.71 × 5% ERP). In 2022 — the last clean, pre-45X, post-tariff year — FSLR earned a **2.7% gross margin and lost money on $2.6B of sales.** That is what this business looks like without the statute.

The moat is therefore best described as **a political franchise on top of a modest cost advantage.** Trade protection (AD/CVD, Section 201, and the Section 232 minimum import price of $0.38/W effective December 4, 2026) plus 45X is the castle wall. Politicians can widen it in a day, and they can breach it in a day. Buffett's moat must be durable for 10+ years; this one has a **statutory expiry date of 2032.**

Note: the 45X solar schedule survived the 2025 budget law intact (100% through 2029, then 75%/50%/25% in 2030/31/32), which is a genuine positive — but the phase-out is now fixed and visible. This moat **narrows every year from January 1, 2030.**

*2022 FCF per cash-flow statement –$0.03B.

**Strengths:** Revenue nearly doubled 2022→2025; the balance sheet is a fortress (Q3'26: cash/ST investments $1.62B, total debt only $0.32B, equity $10.9B; management guides to **$1.7–2.3B net cash at YE26**). Share count has been flat at ~107.4M for five years and stock comp is a token $19M/yr (0.4% of revenue). That is exemplary.

**Serious flaws:**
1. **Margin instability is extreme** — 25% → 3% → 39% → 44% → 41% gross margin over five years. Predictability is the first thing I look for; it is absent.
2. **Cash conversion has broken down.** 9M 2026: net income **+$1.34B**, operating cash flow **–$403M**, FCF **–$837M**. The gap is largely a **$1.88B 45X credit receivable** (essentially *all* of net receivables) — credits earned on domestic production but not yet monetized through the transfer market. These are high-quality government-backed claims, but a business that books $1 in earnings and collects nothing is not compounding cash.
3. **Revenue growth has stopped.** FY26 sales guidance was cut from $4.9–5.2B to **$4.6–4.9B** on volume of 16–17 GW, and gross-profit guidance was *maintained* only because 45X accrues on **production, not sales**. That is the tell: the growth in reported profit is credit arithmetic, not demand.
4. Q3'26 flattered optics: **EPS +25% on sales –23%.**

### 3. Valuation Analysis

**Earnings composition (my estimate).** FY2026E net income ~$1.85B on guidance of $2.4–2.6B gross profit. Subtracting ~$1.35–1.45B of 45X credits and ~$240M SG&A leaves **core, ex-credit net income of roughly $0.45–0.55B (~$4.50–5.00/share)**. The 45X credit is real, transferable cash — but it is a *declining annuity*, not an operating business.

**Sum-of-the-parts (my intrinsic value work):**

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| Net cash (YE26E guidance midpoint) | $2.0B |
| 45X receivable already accrued | $1.8B |
| PV of remaining 45X credits (2027–2032, phase-down applied, 10% discount) | $4.8B |
| Core business: ~$0.55B normalized ex-credit owner earnings @ 10–11x (commodity, cyclical, policy-dependent) | $5.5–6.0B |
| Contingencies (Malaysia 1.8 GW binary decision, Swift Current warranty, litigation) | ($0.5B) |
| **Intrinsic value** | **~$13.6–14.1B → $127–131/share** |

- **Bull case** (US price floor proves durable, ex-credit earnings run $0.8–1.0B, credited at 13–14x): ~$185/share.
- **Bear case** (Section 232 MIP acts as a *price ceiling* capping FSLR's rent extraction, 2028 demand cliff when 45Y/48E projects must be placed in service, transfer market discounts credits 20–30%): ~$95–105/share.

**My central intrinsic value estimate: ~$145/share** (blending base and partially-bullish outcomes). At $181, FSLR trades at roughly a **20–25% premium to intrinsic value.** Against my $145 estimate, a 25% margin of safety demands a purchase near **$109** — roughly where the stock traded in March–May 2025.

**On the multiples:** trailing P/E 12.7x and EV/EBITDA 7.9x look cheap, and the sector-median P/E of 21.4x implies a 40% discount. I distrust both. The peer set is broad "Energy" (utilities, E&P), not solar-comp economics, and the 12.7x denominator is an earnings stream where roughly 70% of profit perishes by 2032. A 12.7x multiple on a subsidized, phase-out-dated earnings stream is not cheap — on *core* earnings it is ~36–40x. Beware also the DCF-style shortcut of capitalizing trailing FCF ($1.19B) at 5% perpetual growth: that yields ~$181/share, precisely today's price, and is precisely wrong, because FY25 FCF was inflated by a one-time 45X working-capital release and perpetual 5% growth ignores the 2030–32 credit cliff.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **B (above average, not exceptional)**

**Positives:** Mark Widmar (CEO since 2016) and CFO Alex Bradley have run the balance sheet like owners — net cash in every year, debt of just $0.32B against $10.9B of equity, no equity issuance, no dilution for five years, and negligible stock comp relative to revenue. When 45X disruptions hit, Widmar stated publicly he would honor the *limited* warranty rather than concede to remedies outside its terms — commercially firm. The board just authorized a **$500M buyback (~2.6% of the float)**, the first meaningful return of capital in the company's history. That is a genuine shift toward shareholder-friendly allocation.

**Reservations:**
- **Capital allocation timing.** ~$4B of capex in 2023–24 went in at the cyclical peak, and FY26 volume is now guided to 16–17 GW against a ~25 GW capacity base — utilization is the problem, and the 1.8 GW Malaysia plant faces a "binary decision" (closure/impairment). Building ahead of contracted demand is how commodity manufacturers destroy capital.
- **No dividend**, and buybacks historically negligible ($12–31M/yr) — the cash pile has simply accumulated.
- **Confidence, not disclosure**, on the Swift Current warranty matter and the TOPCon IP campaign. Both are open-ended.

Insider ownership is modest and no evidence of aggressive insider buying is available to me — a gap in my work here.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **SELL** (avoid; trim if held)

**This is a decent company at a demanding price, with a profit pool that Congress has already scheduled to shrink.** FSLR fails three of my four gates:

- **Moat:** narrow, and structurally narrowing from 2030; the returns come from statute, not from the customer.
- **Returns:** core ROIC (~6%) is *below* the cost of capital (~12.5%). The 15.9% headline figure is a subsidy artifact.
- **Margin of safety:** none. Price is ~20–25% *above* my ~$145 intrinsic value estimate. "A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy" — this one trades above fair.
- **Cash conversion:** negative FCF in three of the last four fiscal years, and deeply negative in 2026.

I would become interested at **$105–115**, where the buyback, ~$2B net cash and the contracted 44 GW / $13.5B backlog (deliveries through 2030, new US bookings at ~$0.36/W) would provide a real margin of safety. Note I am *not* calling this a fraud or a short — the 45X cash is real, the backlog is real, and the sell-side (targets $199–$328, averaging ~$265) may well be right if the US price floor proves durable. My disagreement is philosophical: I will not pay a full multiple for earnings that expire by act of Congress.

### 6. Key Risks to My Value Thesis (i.e., what would make me wrong)

1. **Section 232 durability (upside risk to my call).** The $0.38/W minimum import price plus 15% duty from Dec 4, 2026 could create a *durable* US price floor that lets FSLR earn $0.12–0.16/W ex-credit at scale — that would lift core earnings to $1B+ and intrinsic value toward $200+/share. Trade policy has been strengthening, not weakening, for domestic manufacturers.
2. **45X could be extended or replaced.** A future Congress could extend the credit or add an equivalent domestic-content incentive. My thesis leans heavily on the 2030–32 phase-down actually happening.
3. **Backlog conversion.** 44 GW / $13.5B contracted through 2030 provides unusual visibility for a commodity business; if bookings re-accelerate post-232 clarity, the volume/utilization drag I assume disappears.
4. **Risks to owning it (bear side):** order slowdown already visible (guidance cut); Section 232 may function as a **price ceiling** as well as a floor, capping US ASPs near import parity; the 2028 demand cliff as 45Y/48E projects must be placed in service by end-2027; Malaysia closure and international ASP weakness (management explicitly refuses to sell into spot); warranty/IP litigation outcomes; 45X receivable monetization discounts or IRS delays; and a 1.71 beta with a 52-week range of **$116.56–$285.99** — this equity can halve on policy headlines.

**Data gaps I could not close within budget:** the FY26 EPS guidance bridge and exact 45X dollar guidance per quarter (SEC exhibit returned 403; BusinessWire timed out), current insider transaction data, and a true solar-comp multiple set (the only peer statistic available was a sector-median P/E of 21.4x across eight Energy names, which I regard as non-comparable). These gaps would refine the estimate but would not change the verdict: at $181 there is no margin of safety in a subsidy-dated earnings stream.
