## ESG ANALYSIS — TOWER SEMICONDUCTOR LTD. (NASDAQ/TASE: TSEM)

**Company:** Israeli specialty analog foundry; HQ & Fab 2/3 Migdal Haemek (Israel), Newport Beach CA, San Antonio TX, TPSCo 51% (Japan, 3 fabs), Agrate 300mm shared with STMicroelectronics (Italy). ~5,613 employees. Market cap ~$15bn on data feed. Q3-26 revenue $526m (+33% y/y), Q4-26 guide $600m, 2028 target $3.6bn revenue / $1.2bn net income. This growth ambition is the single most important ESG fact: **a ~2x revenue expansion is being built on a resource-intensive, water-stressed, geopolitically exposed manufacturing base.**

*Data note: TSEM is a foreign private issuer — no 10-K/10-Q/DEF 14A; the 20-F and 6-K filings were not retrievable through the filing tool (403/no-match), and the company's GHG PDF would not parse. Findings below rest on company ESG pages, press releases, third-party news and litigation records. Third-party ESG scores (MSCI/Sustainalytics) were not obtainable — a gap noted in §5.*

### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM–HIGH**

**Positives (company-reported, partially third-party verified):** GRI + SASB-aligned reporting; GHG Protocol Scope 1&2; SGS Taiwan external audit of RoHS/REACH/Prop 65 compliance; stated **60% ultrapure-water recycling at Israeli sites**; "no complaints, legal proceedings or fines regarding environmental issues in Israel or the US" in the 2023 reporting period — a clean compliance record is genuinely credit-worthy.

**Why it is not Low:**
- **Disclosure perimeter is roughly half the company.** The 2023 sustainability report explicitly **excludes TPSCo (Japan) and Agrate (Italy)** — i.e. the fabs at the centre of the $3bn growth plan disclose no environmental metrics. This is the weakest link in the whole ESG profile: emissions/water intensity of the growth engine is unmeasured publicly.
- **Scope 3 arrived late.** First Scope 3 assessment only completed **Oct/Nov 2025** — years behind TSM/UMC/ST. Given that most of Tower's footprint is upstream (wafers, gases, substrates) and downstream customer use, this is a material reporting immaturity.
- **Absolute emissions are set to rise.** Revenue is guided to ~2x by 2028 plus a new 300mm building in Japan. No disclosed science-based target (SBTi) was found — only intensity/cost-efficiency framing. That is a structural gap versus EU CSRD-exposed peers.
- **PFC/fluorinated-gas abatement** and **PFAS restriction** (EU universal PFAS restriction proposal) are live cost items; specialty analog uses wet chemistry heavily.
- **Water:** Israel's grid/water resilience is supported by desalination, but Migdal Haemek sits in a water-scarce basin; a capacity expansion there is permit-sensitive.

**Financial read-through (analyst estimate):** compliance/abatement capex of $15–40m over 2026-30 (~0.5–1.5% of the announced capex programme) — not thesis-changing. The real risk is **customer gatekeeping**: Apple lists Tower among suppliers in its clean-energy programme; failure to give credible Scope 1/2 abatement and Scope 3 data on the *new* fabs is a competitive disqualifier in AI/data-centre RFQs, not a fine.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment — **MEDIUM** (elevated by geography, not by conduct)

- **Geopolitical/physical:** HQ and two fabs in Israel; the 2023–26 conflict cycle brought rocket fire into the northern region and widespread reserve mobilisation. The company's own 2026 releases carry the standard warning that Israeli facility operations "could be materially adversely affected." **This is the highest-severity S factor in the file.** *Estimated financial impact of a 3–4 week northern-fab disruption: ~$150–200m of revenue and ~$60–80m of gross profit at the Q4-26 run-rate ($600m/qtr) — roughly 8–13% of a quarter's annualised EBIT.* Insurable only partially; war-risk exclusions are standard.
- **Labour:** ~5,600 employees; history of restructuring (Fab 1 Migdal Haemek shutdown with staff redeployment; temporary US fab closures affecting ~699 employees). No union-representation controversy identified. Skilled-engineer attrition is the real constraint on the ramp.
- **Community/social licence:** Strong local narrative (anchor employer in the Galilee/periphery); Japan expansion framed around "long-term employee pride" and a ~200-head recruitment.
- **End-market screening:** Aerospace & defence revenue can trigger exclusion by SRI-labelled funds — a small but real cost-of-capital friction.
- **Supply chain labour:** Fabless/OSAT supply chain in Asia; no adverse audit findings found, but also no public supplier-audit disclosure.

### 4. Governance Quality Score — **6.5 / 10**

**Strengths (above Israeli-market norm):**
- **Separate Chairman and CEO** (Amir Elstein, Chair since 2009; Russell Ellwanger, CEO since 2005) — no duality.
- **No dual-class share structure**; one share, one vote. Israeli Companies Law requires **shareholder approval of director and CEO compensation**, and Tower puts the CEO's and Chairman's equity awards to a **separate annual vote** (confirmed in 2025 and 2026 AGM notices) — functionally a binding say-on-pay, stronger than most US peers.
- Demonstrated **willingness to accept a strategic premium** (Intel's $5.4bn bid, terminated Aug-2023 for Chinese regulatory reasons, with a **$353m termination fee received**) — evidence against entrenchment.
- Compliance infrastructure: code of ethics, dedicated ethics hotline, board-level risk assessment process; cyber event (2020) disclosed promptly and operations restored.

**Deductions:**
- **Tenure/refreshment:** 21-year CEO and 17-year Chairman. Successful, but board independence in substance is hard to evidence from a 20-F regime with **no US-style proxy disclosure of board skills matrices, committee composition or independence determinations per director.**
- **Reduced disclosure regime generally:** 20-F + 6-K means no quarterly 10-Q detail and no CD&A — a governance-transparency discount versus US-listed peers, and it makes ESG diligence (this report included) materially harder.
- **Capital allocation concentration:** $3bn net Japan programme funded partly by a **$1bn Japanese government grant**. State-aid dependence imports political/industrial-policy risk and potential conditions on output or location.
- **JV opacity:** TPSCo 51%/Nuvoton 49% and the shared Agrate 300mm site with ST mean minority interests and partner-controlled EHS standards at exactly the assets outside the ESG report perimeter.
- **IP/business conduct:** GlobalFoundries filed **11-patent infringement suits (Mar-2026)** seeking to block importation/sale of accused chips; IQE previously alleged trade-secret misappropriation. Regardless of merits, this is a control-environment and reputational flag; legal spend of $10–20m/yr and injunction-tail risk are plausible.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **Israel (home):** Ministry of Environmental Protection permits and reporting (business licensing); no mandatory climate-disclosure regime of CSRD strength; TASE ESG index inclusion is voluntary. **Net: low current compliance cost, high future catch-up risk.**
- **EU CSRD:** Reaches TSEM via the **Agrate (Italy) 300mm operation shared with STMicroelectronics** and EU customer/subsidiary thresholds — third-country-parent reporting (Art. 40a) phases in from FY2028/29. Expect a step-change in the disclosed perimeter — i.e. the TPSCo/Agrate blind spot gets forced open.
- **EU PFAS restriction / REACH / RoHS:** Direct process-chemistry cost and substitution risk; partially mitigated by SGS-audited compliance.
- **CBAM:** Minimal direct exposure (no significant CBAM goods imports); indirect via equipment and materials.
- **US SEC climate rule:** Effectively withdrawn/stayed — near-term relief, but Apple/hyperscaler contractual disclosure requirements substitute for it.
- **Japan:** METI grant conditions (the $1bn subsidy) likely carry reporting/local-content/employment covenants — a quasi-ESG-linked obligation on the growth capex.
- **ESG-linked debt covenants:** None identified; capital structure is convertibles/straight debt. **Positive.**

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (upper half of Average; not a Laggard)

Tower is a **well-governed, clean-compliance, under-disclosed** specialty foundry. It is not an ESG leader because its reporting perimeter excludes its growth assets, it has no science-based target, and it sits in a conflict zone. It is not a laggard because there are no environmental enforcement actions, no dual-class structure, binding shareholder votes on executive pay, and a demonstrably shareholder-responsive board.

**Trajectory: improving but reactive.** Scope 3 disclosure and CSRD-driven perimeter expansion are coming; the $3bn Japan build is a one-time opportunity to design in best-in-class water/energy/PFC abatement — or to lock in a decade of unmeasured intensity.

---

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (with an asymmetric geopolitical overlay)

- **Not a source of alpha, and not a source of exclusion.** No material ESG-driven earnings drag identified near term; compliance capex is <1.5% of the announced programme.
- **The one ESG factor that can move the thesis is geopolitical.** A northern-Israel disruption is a $150m+/quarter revenue event against a stock priced for a $1.2bn 2028 profit ramp — this is the true tail risk, it is unhedgeable by diversification within Israel, and it is the reason I would not pay a full multiple for the 2028 target.
- **Environmental positioning is a competitive asset, not a cost:** low-power analog/SiGe/silicon-photonics products are energy-efficiency enablers for AI data centres, and being an Apple-listed supplier with clean compliance gives licence to operate with exactly the customers driving the growth. The gap is *evidence*, not intent — and evidence is fixable in 12–24 months.
- **What would upgrade TSEM to Leader:** (1) consolidated Scope 1/2/3 including TPSCo and Agrate; (2) an SBTi-validated target covering the Japan expansion; (3) an ITC/GF litigation resolution without an exclusion order; (4) disclosure of board independence and an ESG/sustainability committee charter.
- **What would downgrade to ESG Risk:** any environmental enforcement action in Israel/Italy/Japan, a water-permit constraint on Migdal Haemek expansion, or a credible safety/chemical incident at a fab.

**Bottom line for the memo:** ESG is **neutral to the investment case** — do not adjust the valuation for it, but treat the Israel operational concentration and the un-disclosed ESG perimeter of the growth capex as diligence items to monitor quarterly.
