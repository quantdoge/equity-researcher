## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT — but a *structured* split**

Agreement is **high on the business, high on the risks, and low only on the price**. That distinction drives the whole recommendation.

- **Consensus bullish (near-unanimous):** The operating inflection is real, not narrative. Sector, Growth, Value, Short and Alt Data all independently concede it. $1.3B of contracted 2027 revenue and **$290M of customer prepayments** are the hardest demand evidence in the peer group — you do not extract customer deposits from a mirage.
- **Consensus bearish (near-unanimous):** Every risk agent independently lands on elevated risk — Geo (Medium-High), Non-Fin (High), Fin (equity-risk High), Macro (Unfavourable). Crucially, these are **correlated, not independent**: Israel + Japan concentration + negative FCF + a $3B build are one risk expressed four ways.
- **The one genuine contest:** Value (FV $118–125, i.e. spot is *above* fair value) vs Growth (~19x 2027E, PEG 0.47). This is resolvable: they differ on **terminal multiple**, not on cash flows. Value applies a 15x foundry multiple to 2028 earnings; Growth applies 20–30x. Value's discipline is the correct prior for a capex-heavy cyclical — but Value's own range top ($140) is *above* spot, so the disagreement is narrower than the headline suggests.
- **The forensic all-clear is the single most important input.** Had the Short Analyst found accrual or receivables manipulation, my framework would force a Pass regardless of every bullish view. It did not: **Beneish −2.672, four years of negative accruals, falling DSO and inventory days, net cash, no goodwill.** That removes the tail risk that would otherwise veto the position outright.

**Net:** high-quality business at a fair-to-slightly-rich price with unusually high and correlated non-fundamental risk. That is a *small position*, not a *pass* and not a *conviction bet*.

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **1.0% of portfolio (initiate), 2.5% hard ceiling**

**Kelly check:** probability-weighted value across the agents' own scenarios — bear $92 (30%), base $150 (45%), bull $220 (25%) — gives an expected value of **~$150, or +12% against spot**. Raw Kelly on roughly 2:1 odds implies 25–30%; half-Kelly 12–15%. **Kelly is directionally supportive but is overridden by the volatility budget** — the binding constraint here is risk, not edge.

**Volatility budget is what sets the number:**
- 92.5% annualized vol means a 1.0% position contributes **~0.92% of portfolio volatility** on its own. Against a 12% portfolio vol target that is already ~8% of total portfolio variance from one name.
- At the 2.5% ceiling it becomes ~2.3% vol contribution (~19% of variance) — the maximum a single, 58%-drawdown, ITC-litigated name should ever occupy.
- **Beta dispersion is itself a risk:** 0.95 (profile) vs 2.16 (3y regression) vs 3.33 (252d). I size to the **high** end, not the low. A 1% TSEM position is roughly equivalent in risk terms to a **2.5–3% SMH position**.

**Why 1.0% and not 0.5% or 3%:**
- *Above 0.5%:* business conviction is genuinely high and consensus; forensic risk is cleared; the price has already absorbed a 58% de-rating; expected value is positive.
- *Below 3%:* multiple simultaneous risk flags (geopolitical High, non-financial High, no valuation margin of safety, ITC exclusion tail, insider selling) trigger my "elevated risk" band regardless of business quality. A strict value mandate would **Pass** here — I am not passing only because the position is sized so that a total loss is a ~100bp event.

**Sizing rule:** size in **risk units, not dollars**. 1.0% of capital = ~2.6% of a normal SMH risk budget.
