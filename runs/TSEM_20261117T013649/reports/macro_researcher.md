## Macroeconomic Assessment — Tower Semiconductor (TSEM)

**Regime: Risk-off in long-duration growth assets, masked by solid real-economy growth.** The macro backdrop as of 17 Nov 2026 is a *re-acceleration/term-premium shock*: the Fed has resumed hiking, the 10Y has broken above 5%, and real yields have jumped ~66bp in four months. This is precisely the environment that compresses multiples on capex-heavy, long-duration semis — and TSEM's tape confirms it.

### 1. Macro Environment Summary

- **Growth is *not* the problem.** Q3-2026 real GDP accelerated to **3.4%** (from 2.2% in Q2, 2.5% in Q1). Unemployment is **4.1%** (Oct-2026), effectively full employment. Nominal PCE spending is +6.3% YoY.
- **Inflation has re-accelerated**, not fallen. CPI YoY is **3.82%** (Oct-2026), and the index rose 337.476 from 325.063 a year earlier. This is well above where the Fed can tolerate it.
- **The Fed pivoted hawkish.** Effective Fed Funds bottomed at **3.63%** (Jun–Sep 2026) after the 2025 easing cycle, then **rose to 3.75% (Sep) and 3.88% (Oct)**. The market is pricing further tightening.
- **The curve bear-steepened.** 2Y 4.74% / 5Y 4.93% / 10Y 5.13% / 30Y 5.47%; 2s10s at **+39bp** — no inversion, so no classic recession signal. The pressure is at the long end (term premium), not from policy inversion.
- **Real yields are the transmission channel.** 10Y TIPS rose from **2.07% (1 Jun)** to **2.91% (13 Nov)**. With 5.15% nominal and 2.91% real, breakevens sit at only ~2.24% — the market believes inflation *will* normalize, but is demanding a much higher real return to hold duration today.
- **USD is firming**: broad dollar index **122.65**, up from 117.91 on 8 Sep (+4.0%); USD/JPY at **159.93** (yen down 4.2% since mid-September).
- **Semis are in an active de-rating.** The SOX has fallen ~20% from its highs, with the sharpest one-day drop since April 2025 in early November 2026, driven by AI-capex doubt, valuation, and rising yields.

### 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

TSEM is a **capex-intensive specialty foundry** ($15.1B cap, 5,613 employees) reporting in **USD** with fabs in **Israel, Japan (TPSCo), and the US (Newport Beach)**, serving automotive, industrial, aerospace/defense, medical, consumer and — increasingly — **AI data-center infrastructure** (silicon photonics/SiGe). Q3-26: revenue **$526M (+33% YoY)**, operating margin 24%, net income **$120M**, capex **$191M** vs. CFO **$185M** — i.e., **FCF-negative in a heavy build-out phase**.

| Macro Variable | Impact | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Real yields +66bp → 2.91%** | **Strongly negative** | The dominant force. TSEM is a long-duration growth asset whose value rests on the $3.6B revenue / $1.2B net-income 2027 target unveiled in July. A higher real discount rate hits terminal value hardest. Stock is **-58% from its 22 Jun peak ($316.85 → $133.42)** and **-48% since 6 Oct**, despite *record* Q3 results and record Q4 guidance ($600M, +36% YoY) — a pure multiple event, not an earnings event. |
| **Fed hiking / higher-for-longer** | **Negative** | Capex of ~$190M/qtr vs. $185M CFO means external funding and refinancing at higher rates; also raises hurdle rates on the Japan/METI and Agrate (Italy) capacity expansions. |
| **Inflation 3.82%** | **Mixed / mildly negative** | Input costs (utilities, materials, Israeli wages) rise, but foundry pricing is contract-based and utilization is high — TSEM has *pricing power* now. Gross margin improved to 34% ($179M on $526M), so it is currently outrunning cost inflation. |
| **USD strength (122.65)** | **Positive** | Revenue is largely USD-denominated while a meaningful share of opex is ILS (Israel) and JPY (Japan). A firmer dollar lowers the USD cost of that base — a real margin tailwind. |
| **Weak yen (159.9/USD)** | **Positive** | Same mechanism via TPSCo; yen down 4.2% since mid-Sept aids Japan cost competitiveness. |
| **Section 232 semiconductor tariffs** | **Relatively positive** | Tariffs are in force in 2026, but TSEM holds **US fab capacity (Newport Beach)** and is adding US supply — domestic-content insulation versus Asian foundries. Offsets some of the geopolitical discount. |
| **Israel geopolitical risk** | **Negative (discount)** | Migdal Haemek is the core fab. 94+ active sanctions/diplomatic measures against Israel and EU trade-policy friction keep a persistent risk premium on the equity and complicate European/Asian customer relations. |
| **Strong GDP / auto & industrial demand** | **Positive** | 3.4% GDP and firm industrial activity support analog/auto/industrial utilization — the non-AI half of the business. |
| **AI capex de-rating (SOX -20%)** | **Negative (correlated)** | 0.95 beta understates the reality: TSEM's SiPho franchise (targeting a **$1.1B annualized run-rate by Q4-26**) makes it a high-beta proxy on the AI trade, and it is being sold with the group. |

**The central tension:** the operating business is delivering the strongest results in its history (+33% revenue, record margins, $2B 2026 revenue), while the macro has repriced its duration. On the 2027 target of $1.2B net income, TSEM now trades at roughly **12.6x** — versus ~30x at the June peak. That is a macro-driven de-rating, not a fundamental one.

### 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE**

Justification is weighted to the **discount rate and the sector de-rating**, not to demand. Growth, employment, USD, and yen are all supportive or neutral; the corrosive variables are real yields at 2.91%, a Fed that has restarted hiking into 3.8% CPI, and a semi sector in an active 20% drawdown. A capex-heavy, FCF-negative, long-duration name cannot escape multiple compression when the real risk-free rate is climbing 66bp a quarter. Rating upgrades immediately on a credible disinflation print that caps the 10Y below ~4.7%.

### 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**Bear (primary): a hawkish overshoot into a term-premium spiral.** If Oct/Nov CPI prints at or above 4% and the Fed signals a move toward 4.5%+, the 10Y plausibly tests **6%** with real yields near 3.5%. In that world the market stops capitalizing TSEM's 2027 target at all: at **8x** the $1.2B net-income figure the equity is roughly **$85**, another ~35% downside from $133. The tell to watch is the **30Y at 5.47%** — a break above 5.75% would confirm the term-premium spiral rather than a clean growth story. TSEM's negative free cash flow makes it structurally more exposed than cash-generative large caps.

**Bull (mirror, non-trivial):** inflation rolls over as breakevens (2.24%) already imply, the Fed pauses, and the 10Y retreats to 4.3%. Combined with the SiPho/SiGe ramp and $1.3B of 2027 contracts already signed, a re-rate to 20x 2027 earnings puts the stock above **$210**. Note the market's own breakeven pricing suggests this is, if anything, the *consensus* path — which is why the hawkish overshoot is the genuine surprise risk.
