## 2. Consensus Assessment: **SPLIT** (structurally, not randomly)

The split is not noise — it is cleanly partitioned by **time horizon and by what each agent is measuring**:

**The bullish bloc (Value, Growth, Short Analyst) is measuring the business and the price.** All three independently converge on the same conclusion from different toolkits: the fundamentals are intact and the multiple is cheap. The Short Analyst is the highest-quality evidence in the entire file — it came to find fraud and found none, and its falsification test (cRPO growth vs revenue growth) is currently passing by 3+ points. When a forensic short-seller explicitly declines to short, that is a strong positive signal that should be weighted heavily.

**The bearish bloc (Quant, Alt Data, Macro, Fin Risk) is measuring the tape, the narrative, and the balance sheet.** None of them disputes the earnings. They dispute the *timing* and the *risk capacity*.

**Conflict resolution — three rulings:**

1. **Fraud risk: dismissed.** Short Analyst's forensic work supersedes any implied accounting concern. Beneish −2.655 with four years of negative accruals and 200% cash conversion is dispositive.
2. **Leverage risk: elevated, and it wins on sizing.** Fin Risk's finding is the single most important *new* fact in the file and it is not fully reflected in Value's or Growth's models. Value's DCF uses ~$20bn net debt; actual is **$30.3B** and heading toward $45–50B post-ASR. This does not break the thesis, but it caps position size and it removes the net-cash floor that historically anchored the multiple.
3. **Momentum/alt-data bearishness: real but non-actionable as a short.** Agentforce search decay is a genuine leading indicator, but Alt Data itself concedes web traffic is *up* 2% MoM — this is mindshare decay, not churn. It argues for **scale-in, not for pass**.

**Net: the business case is a Buy; the entry conditions are poor. That is a sizing problem, not a direction problem.**

## 3. Position Sizing Recommendation: **1.5% starter → 3.0% maximum**

**Initiate at 1.5% of portfolio. Hard ceiling 3.0%, reached only on confirmation.**

Rationale, applying fractional-Kelly logic:

- **Edge:** ~28% discount to base-case IV ($275 vs $194.52). Real but not deep — Value concedes it "barely" clears a 25% hurdle.
- **Odds:** Growth's scenario math is ~3:1 (bear −18%, base +27%, bull +60%). Macro independently corroborates: bear −7% to −20%, bull +36% to +65%.
- **Variance penalty:** **50.85% annualised vol.** A 3% position contributes ~1.5% of portfolio volatility — that is a full active-risk budget for a single name. Full Kelly on a 3:1 payoff with this variance would imply a size far above what any risk framework should tolerate; fractional Kelly (¼ to ⅓) lands at 1.5–2.5%.
- **Balance-sheet haircut:** net debt 4x higher, coverage down 81%, tangible equity negative. This is a legitimate reason to size *below* what the valuation alone would justify.
- **Binary catalyst inside 2 weeks:** Q3 FY27 on **2-Dec-2026**. Do not be fully sized into it.

**Why not smaller (0.5–1%)?** Because the forensic and backlog evidence is genuinely strong and the asymmetry is 3:1. A sub-1% position in a $160B liquid mega-cap with 8.9% FCF yield is not risk management, it is indifference.

**Why not larger (4–5%)?** Because 94% of the variance is idiosyncratic, momentum has broken, the macro regime is actively hostile to long-duration equity, and the leverage is a self-inflicted, unresolved overhang.

CRM is a **pure application-software factor bet**, not a diversifier. At 0.74 correlation to both NOW and ADBE, adding CRM to a portfolio already holding those names adds almost no independent exposure — it just increases the size of an existing bet.

**Rule: only initiate if the total application-software sleeve (CRM + NOW + ADBE + WDAY + INTU + HUBS) remains below ~10% of portfolio after the add.** If the sleeve is already at or above that, **pass** — the same thesis is better expressed in NOW or MSFT, which the Sector Researcher correctly identifies as the higher-quality growth exposure.

**Correlation to MSFT is only 0.41** — the one genuinely diversifying pairing. If the portfolio is MSFT-heavy, CRM adds some real spread.

**Macro fit:** the portfolio's duration exposure matters. With 10Y TIPS at 2.92% and rising, adding a long-duration equity is a directional bet on real rates peaking. **This position should be funded from another long-duration software holding, not from cash** — otherwise it is an unhedged macro bet dressed as a stock pick.
