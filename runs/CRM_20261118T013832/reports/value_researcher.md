## Salesforce, Inc. (CRM) — Value Investment Analysis

*Price basis: ~$197 (last trade; previous close $214.83, −8.4% on the day in a broad software/AI selloff; 52-week range ~$150–$269). Market cap ~$160bn. Note: my ratio snapshot showed $194.52 / 956m diluted shares while my price feed lags at 6/30/26; I've reconciled to the live quote and ~850m shares (post-103m ASR share delivery). Per-share figures below use reported EPS where available.*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — **Moat: Narrow**

Salesforce is the dominant CRM franchise: ~$46bn of FY27 revenue, roughly 90% subscription, embedded in the sales, service, marketing and data workflows of large enterprises. The moat rests almost entirely on **switching costs** — years of accumulated customer data, custom objects, integrations and admin ecosystems make rip-and-replace painful. That is a real moat, and it shows up in 77.7% gross margins and a $66.3bn remaining performance obligation backlog.

But three pieces of evidence keep this from being a *wide* moat:

- **Returns on capital are mediocre.** ROIC was 10.9% in FY26 (10.9/9.9/6.9/1.4% over four years). A genuinely moated business earns 20%+ ROIC. Salesforce's problem is that ~$64.8bn of its $112bn asset base is goodwill and intangibles — it has bought much of its growth (Slack $27.7bn, Tableau $15.7bn, MuleSoft $6.5bn, Informatica ~$8bn, Braze $5.66bn). ROIC is barely above WACC, which is the opposite of the Buffett hallmark.
- **Growth is decelerating and increasingly inorganic.** Revenue growth has fallen from 24.7% (FY22) to 9.6% (FY26). FY27's guided +11–12% includes >3 points of Informatica and ~2 points from Contentful/Fin — **organic growth is ~8%**.
- **The seat-based pricing model is under genuine attack.** The current selloff is about AI agents reducing human seat counts. Management's answer is credible — cRPO accelerated to **+14% Y/Y CC**, RPO +11%, Agentforce ARR >$1.5bn (+240%), Agentforce + Data 360 ARR ~$3.9bn (+210%) — which argues they are monetizing AI rather than being cannibalized. But that is two quarters of evidence against a multi-decade structural question. Competition from Microsoft Dynamics (bundled), ServiceNow and AI-native tools is intensifying.

**Verdict: the moat has held, not widened. Narrow, with real erosion risk at the margin.**

**Score: 7.5/10** — high-quality income statement and cash flow, held back by sub-WACC returns on capital and slowing cash generation.

**Owner-earnings DCF (my own; the built-in DCF tool returned a corrupted terminal value — discarded).** Base owner earnings = FY26 FCF $14.4bn less $3.5bn SBC = **$11.0bn**. Growth 8% yrs 1–5 (organic revenue growth + modest margin expansion), 4% yrs 6–10, 2.5% terminal, 9% discount. Net debt ~$20bn pro forma for Braze/Contentful/Fin.

- PV of years 1–10: ~$98bn
- PV of terminal value: ~$131bn
- EV ~$229bn → equity ~$209bn → **~$246/share**

If I treat SBC more leniently — defensible here, since buybacks ($25bn ASR) run ~3.5x annual SBC and share count is *falling* ~10% — intrinsic value rises to ~$315/share.

**Intrinsic value estimate: ~$275/share base case, range $235–$315.** The SBC treatment is the single biggest swing factor, and I flag it rather than hide it.

At ~$197, CRM trades at roughly a **28% discount to base-case intrinsic value**. The implied growth in the price is only ~4–5% perpetually — i.e., the market is pricing permanent deceleration toward GDP growth, not decline. That looks too pessimistic given cRPO +14% and $66bn RPO.

### 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Mixed / Adequate**

- **Founder-led.** Marc Benioff remains Chair & CEO with substantial personal equity ownership — owner-operator alignment is genuine. Robin Washington as President/CFO/COO has brought real margin discipline (GAAP op margin 3.3% → 20.1% in three years). That is excellent operating execution.
- **Shareholder returns have become serious.** $12.6bn of buybacks in FY26, a $25bn accelerated share repurchase with 103m shares delivered, and a dividend initiated and raised every year since FY25. Total capital returned in FY26 (~$14.2bn) approximated total FCF. Disciplined.
- **But capital allocation via M&A is the weak spot.** Braze at $5.66bn all-cash (~8x revenue for a loss-making business), plus Informatica, Contentful and Fin, all in one year, all while leverage rises and the stock trades at 11x FCF. Buying back stock at $250–300 in calendar 2025 and issuing cash for acquisitions at premium multiples in 2026 is not obviously value-accretive. This is the empire-building risk Buffett warns about, and the stock fell on the Braze announcement.

**Assessment: honest, founder-aligned, operationally excellent, but an acquisitive instinct that has historically earned sub-WACC returns on incremental capital.**

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **BUY** (moderate conviction — accumulate below ~$200)

Salesforce is a good business at a modestly discounted price, not a great business at a bargain price. The setup is genuinely appealing: 77.7% gross margins, 34% non-GAAP operating margins, $14.4bn of FCF at 11x, a $66bn backlog growing 11%, cRPO *accelerating* to +14%, and a share count shrinking ~10% a year. Against that, ROIC of 10.9% tells you the franchise does not compound capital at a high rate organically, growth is ~8% and falling, and free cash flow growth is guided to just 4–5% this year.

At ~$197 you are paying ~13.7x forward non-GAAP earnings and ~11x FCF for a business the market believes is structurally impaired. I don't think it is — but I also don't get the 40% discount I'd want on a business facing a genuine terminal-value question. **A ~28% discount to base-case intrinsic value clears my 25% hurdle, barely.**

This is a **Buy on valuation, not on quality** — size it modestly (2–3% position), expect multiple years of low-teens returns rather than a re-rating bonanza, and add aggressively only below ~$165 (my bear case).

---

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **AI-driven seat disintermediation (the big one).** If agentic AI genuinely compresses per-seat licensing faster than Agentforce monetizes, the terminal value in my DCF is too high. Two strong quarters of cRPO do not refute a decade-long structural risk.
2. **M&A value destruction.** Braze, Informatica, Contentful, Fin — ~$15bn+ of cash out the door in a single fiscal year at premium multiples, funded partly with debt. If these are dilutive to ROIC (as Slack and Tableau arguably were), the compounding engine stalls.
3. **FCF growth stalling.** FY27 guidance of +4–5% is a warning. If capex for AI infrastructure and cash taxes keep FCF growth in the mid-single digits, the 11x P/FCF is not as cheap as it looks.
4. **Balance sheet direction.** Net debt went from $2.5bn to $9.9bn in one year and will be ~$20bn pro forma for pending deals. Still only ~1.4x EBITDA, but the trend is wrong.
5. **Competition.** Microsoft can bundle Dynamics into Azure/365 renewals at marginal cost; ServiceNow is encroaching on the workflow layer; AI-native startups attack from below.
