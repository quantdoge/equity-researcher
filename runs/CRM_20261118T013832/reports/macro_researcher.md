## 1. Macro Environment Summary — **Risk-Off in Duration, Not in Credit** (data as of 17–18 Nov 2026)

The US is in a **reflation-with-a-real-rate-shock** regime, not a classic late-cycle slowdown. Growth re-accelerated sharply through 2026: real GDP rose at a **3.4% annualized pace in Q3 2026** ($24,611B chained) after 2.2% in Q2 and 2.5% in Q1 — a V-shaped recovery from the 0.2% stall in Q4 2025. Unemployment is **4.1%** (Oct 2026), down from 4.5% a year ago. Nominal PCE spending grew **~6.3% Y/Y** in Sept 2026. This is strong nominal activity.

The problem is entirely in the price of money. The FOMC **restarted a hiking cycle in September 2026** and held at **3.75–4.00%** on 28 Oct 2026 (two dissents), with CPI at **3.82% Y/Y** in October — policy is now only ~0% real. The 10Y Treasury sits at **5.12%** (16 Nov), up **68bp since 30 June**, with a **+39bp 2s10s** curve — a textbook **bear steepener**.

The decisive detail: the 10Y TIPS real yield has risen to **2.92%** (16 Nov) from **2.07%** on 2 June — **+85bp** — while the 5Y breakeven *fell* 27bp to **2.26%**. **The entire long-end selloff is real rates / term premium, not inflation expectations.** That is the most hostile possible rate shock for long-duration equities: the discount rate rises while the growth story is unchanged.

Offsetting this, credit is *not* signaling recession: **IG OAS is 90bp** (16 Nov), within 16bp of the June tight (74bp). Capital is not scarce for large enterprises — which is precisely CRM's customer base. The stress is concentrated in equity duration, compounded by the Nov 5 AI-infrastructure unwind (Nasdaq −5.5%, ~$2.5T of market cap erased) and a broad **2026 software-specific de-rating on AI-disruption fears**.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **UNFAVOURABLE** (with a valuation cushion)

CRM faces a triple macro headwind that is unusual in its simultaneity:
1. **A real-rate shock** — 10Y TIPS at 2.92% and rising, which is the single most direct valuation tax on long-duration software.
2. **A strengthening dollar** (+3.9% in nine weeks) against ~40% international revenue, with guidance set in constant currency.
3. **A cooling white-collar labour market** that structurally caps seat-based expansion while agentic AI threatens the seat model itself.

Partially offsetting: **strong nominal GDP (~7%)**, **tight credit spreads (90bp)** confirming enterprise solvency, **zero refinancing risk** on a net-cash balance sheet, and a multiple that has already compressed to ~3.5x sales / ~9% FCF yield. Rating would be "Favourable" if either the real 10Y fell back below ~2.5% or the dollar reversed; neither is in prospect while the Fed is hiking.

## 5. The Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **a Bullish "One-and-Done" Re-rating**

The market is priced for a term-premium spiral, but **the bond market itself disagrees on inflation**: 5Y breakevens are falling (2.26%) while nominal yields rise. If the Fed's September hike proves the **last** of the cycle — e.g. CPI rolls from 3.82% toward 3.0% by Q1 2027 on the base effects of the 2025 disinflation and a strong dollar, with GDP holding above 3% — then the **real 10Y reverts from 2.92% toward ~2.4%**, the dollar rolls over, and the AI-unwind positioning (software broadly de-rated, CRM −45% from its Jan 2025 high of $355.17) unwinds violently.

In that world, CRM's intact operating momentum (cRPO +14% cc, record quarters, ~9% FCF yield funding buybacks) drives a **re-rating from 3.5x to 4.5x EV/sales → ~$264, +36%**, and toward $320 (+65%) if the AI-disruption premium fully exits. Note the asymmetry: the bear case (term premium to 6%, multiple to 3.0x) is worth roughly **−7% to −20%** from here, because the de-rating has already happened. **The macro setup is unfavourable, but the risk/reward it has created is not.**
