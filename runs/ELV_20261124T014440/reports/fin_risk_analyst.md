## 1. Credit Risk Assessment

**Rating: INVESTMENT GRADE (A-/BBB+ tier) — but on a Negative trajectory.**

Fitch rates ELV's Long-Term IDR **'A-'** and senior unsecured debt **'BBB+'** (upgraded from BBB+ IDR / BBB senior in 2025; Fitch affirmed the senior notes at BBB+ in Dec 2025). S&P commentary references management's **39–40% debt-to-capital target**, expecting leverage to improve to that level in 2026. *Exact current S&P and Moody's ratings were not retrievable from the toolset — flagged as a data gap.*

The company is not at risk of default in any base case: $9.5B cash, $4.3B operating cash flow, and interest expense of only $1.4B. But the **direction of travel is unambiguously negative**, and three of the four pillars have deteriorated materially in 24 months:

| Credit signal | FY2022 | FY2023 | FY2024 | FY2025 | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interest coverage (EBIT/int.) | 9.93x | 8.49x | 7.67x | **5.79x** | ▼ −42% in 3y |
| Net debt / EBITDA | 1.82x | 1.92x | 2.33x | **2.46x** | ▲ every year |
| Total debt ($B) | 25.80 | 26.65 | 32.70 | **33.24** | ▲ +28.8% |
| Debt-to-capital | 41.5% | 40.3% | 44.1% | **43.0%** | ▲ above 39–40% target |
| OCF / net income | 1.40x | 1.35x | 0.97x | **0.76x** | ▼ cash conversion broken |

Interest expense rose 76% ($0.80B → $1.40B) over four years *while EBIT fell 7%*. That is the core credit problem: the company levered up into an earnings decline.

**Near-term refinancing:** not a cliff. Short-term debt is only $1.38B against $9.5B cash. Known maturities are small relative to liquidity ($1.6B 3.65% notes due Dec-2027; $750M 4.00% notes due 2028; $750M 5.00% notes due 2036 issued 2025). The 5-year revolver (originally April 2027 maturity, since extended) is a facility-renewal item, not a stress point. **Full maturity ladder was not retrievable — data gap.**

## 4. Downside Scenario: Revenue −20%

**Base (TTM):** revenue $201.8B, EBIT $7.46B (3.70% margin), EBITDA ~$8.9B, net debt $23.74B, normalized interest ~$1.44B.

A 20% revenue decline for a health insurer means losing ~1/5 of premium volume — plausible via Medicaid redetermination, large-group account losses, or expiry of enhanced ACA subsidies. Medical costs are largely variable, so gross margin holds; the damage comes from the **fixed administrative base** (~21.5% of revenue) and MLR mix.

| | Revenue | EBIT margin | EBIT | Int. cov. | Net debt/EBITDA | FCF | Dividend |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Base TTM** | $201.8B | 3.70% | $7.46B | 5.2x | 2.7x | ~$3.5B | 2.3x cov. |
| **A: Costs flex** | $161.5B | 3.70% | $5.97B | 4.1x | 3.2x | ~$4.0B | covered 2.6x |
| **B: Base downside** | $161.5B | 2.70% | $4.36B | **3.0x** | **4.1x** | ~$2.7B | covered 1.8x |
| **C: Severe** | $161.5B | 1.00% | $1.62B | **1.1x** | **7.7x** | **−$1.1B** | **at risk** |

**Scenario B (most likely downside)** — 100bp of margin loss on a 20% revenue cut: EBIT falls 42% to $4.36B, coverage drops to 3.0x and net debt/EBITDA to 4.1x. That breaches the level at which rating agencies typically move to negative outlook for managed care (**BBB/BBB− territory**), and debt-to-capital rises to ~47% — nearly 8pt above management's 39–40% target. Altman Z falls to ~2.1 (deep grey). Critically, **the $2.6B buyback must be suspended in full**, but the $1.53B dividend is still covered 1.8x by FCF.

**Scenario C (severe)** — a repeat of the 2025 MLR shock *on top of* a 20% volume loss: coverage falls to 1.1x, FCF turns negative, and the dividend ($1.53B) requires debt funding or an equity raise. This is the distress path and the only route to sub-investment-grade.

**Key mitigants that prevent a liquidity crisis in every scenario:** (1) $9.5B cash covers interest 6.6 years and the dividend 6.2 years even at zero FCF; (2) $2.6B of annual buyback is fully discretionary — cancelling it alone restores ~$2.6B/yr; (3) capex of $1.1B (0.6% of revenue) is partly deferrable; (4) D&A ($1.55B) exceeds capex ($1.12B), a modest cash tailwind.

## 5. Overall Financial Risk Rating: **MODERATE** (trajectory: deteriorating)

Not High today — the balance sheet is investment grade, liquidity is ample, and no maturity wall exists. But **every metric that matters is moving the wrong way simultaneously**: revenue +44% over three years (FY22→FY25) while operating income *fell* 4%, operating cash flow *fell 49%*, interest expense *rose 76%*, and FCF *fell 56%*. A second consecutive year of this pattern would justify **High**.

### Key Red Flags

1. **Cash conversion has broken.** OCF/net income fell 1.40x → 0.76x in three years. The FY2025 working-capital drag of −$3.28B (vs −$0.54B in FY2023) is the single largest driver. Operating cash flow of $4.29B against $5.66B of net income means **earnings are not being converted to cash** — the Piotroski accruals flag (F4=0) confirms this independently.
2. **FCF no longer funds shareholder returns.** FY2025: FCF $3.17B vs dividends + buybacks of $4.13B = **130% of FCF**. This is the first year of shortfall in the four-year window (2022: 49%, 2023: 60%, 2024: 97%). Total debt is up $7.4B since 2022 and the gap is being debt-funded.
3. **Leverage is above management's own target.** Debt-to-capital at 43.0% vs a stated 39–40% goal. S&P's expectation of improvement to target in 2026 is **contingent on earnings delivery** — precisely the variable that has missed in four of the last six quarters.
4. **Interest coverage has almost halved** (10.9x FY2021 → 5.8x FY2025, ~5.2x TTM). At scenario-B margins, coverage of 3.0x is consistent with a multi-notch downgrade.
5. **Tangible equity is only $4.5B** against $39.5B of goodwill/intangibles — 89.8% of book equity. Any impairment to the care-delivery or pharmacy acquisitions would hit an already-thin tangible base and could itself trigger a ratings action.
6. **Volatility is 32–36% with a 50% three-year drawdown and a near-zero Sharpe.** Measured beta of 0.15 is a **trap**: it reflects an idiosyncratic collapse, not defensiveness. Four separate ≥8.5% earnings-driven gap-downs in 18 months dictate gap-risk-based sizing.
7. **An unresolved −14.9% five-session drawdown is in progress** (16-Nov $446.28 → 23-Nov $379.55, on elevated volume). This post-dates the Q3 print and should be treated as unpriced negative information flow.

### Data Gaps Flagged
- **Sortino ratio** not produced by the toolset.
- **Full debt maturity ladder and revolver covenant terms** (financial maintenance covenant level) not retrievable — the 43.0% debt-to-capital headroom assessment is unverified.
- **Current S&P and Moody's ratings/outlooks** not retrieved (Fitch A-/BBB+ confirmed).
- **Trailing-twelve-month cash flow** unavailable (cash flow tool returns annual only) — all FCF figures are FY2025; TTM FCF is likely higher given the Q3'26 margin recovery, so FCF metrics above are conservative.
- **Q2 2026 quarter has inconsistent field mappings** (cost_of_revenue $5.49B and interest expense $56M are clearly mis-tagged vs. the $358M/quarter run rate). TTM interest was normalized to ~$1.44B; gross profit and operating income for that quarter appear internally consistent and were used.

**One balancing positive:** the operating trend inflected in 2026. Q3'26 EBIT of $2.152B is **+22.5% YoY** (vs $1.757B), the benefit expense ratio improved 70bp to 90.6%, and management raised FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance to ≥$29 (forward P/E ~13.1x vs 16.2x trailing). The credit deterioration described above is a **2024–2025 vintage problem that 2026 results are beginning to reverse** — which is why this is Moderate rather than High. The risk is that the market's November re-rating is signaling the recovery is stalling.
