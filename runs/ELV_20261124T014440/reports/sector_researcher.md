# SECTOR BRIEF — Elevance Health (ELV)
## Managed Care / Health Benefits (US) | GICS: Healthcare — Managed Health Care

---

## 1. Sector Overview

ELV competes in the ~$1.5trn US health benefits market — commercial group/individual, Medicare Advantage (MA) and Medicaid managed care — a sector where revenue is contracted to the government (~55% of ELV's premium) or to large self-funded employers, and where the entire earnings model hinges on one variable: the gap between the medical loss ratio (MLR) and the administratively-set premium. The sector is emerging from a two-year medical-cost shock (2024–25) driven by elevated inpatient acuity, specialty pharmacy and post-COVID utilisation catch-up, which crushed margins across the board (UNH operating margin 8.8% → 4.2%; HUM to a FY25 operating loss of −$7.6bn). The cycle inflected in 2026: CMS's final CY2027 MA rate announcement delivered **+2.48%** (≈$13bn) versus a proposed **+0.09%**, and carriers have spent 2025–26 repricing aggressively and shedding unprofitable membership. Sector revenue growth remains strong (~11% median in FY25) but is now decelerating sharply as plans deliberately shrink, while margins recover off a trough. Structure is oligopolistic (UNH, ELV, CVS/Aetna, CI, HUM, CNC, MOH), with low entry barriers risk and very high regulatory/political dependence.

---

## 2. Key Tailwinds

- **MA rate reset is now favourable.** The CY2027 final rate of **+2.48%** (~$13bn industry-wide) versus the 0.09% advance notice removed the single biggest sector overhang; it triggered a broad re-rating on 7-Apr-2026 (ELV +9%, UNH +9%, HUM +8% in one session). ELV's 2027 MA bids assume cost trend similar to 2026 — i.e. rates are now *ahead* of trend for the first time in three years.
- **Structural MA penetration runway.** 55% of eligible Medicare beneficiaries are in MA in 2026, projected to reach **63% by 2034** (KFF), with SNPs (+883k enrollees, 23% of MA) growing fastest. Medicaid managed care and MA both remain secular volume growers regardless of the cost cycle.
- **Cost trend is stabilising and pricing discipline is returning.** ELV's benefit expense ratio improved **70bp to 90.6%** in Q3-26, with management raising guidance repeatedly through 2026 (FY26 EPS guidance lifted from ~$22 to ≥$29) and targeting **+12% EPS growth in 2027**. Industry-wide, carriers are prioritising margin over membership (UNH is deliberately ceding 1.3–1.4m MA members in 2026).

## 3. Key Headwinds

- **Medical cost trend remains elevated and structurally above CPI.** Inpatient and medical specialty pharmacy are the pressure points; the 2026–27 recovery is a *repricing* recovery, not a cost-containment one. Any re-acceleration in utilisation hits MLR immediately and with high operating leverage (each 100bp of MLR ≈ ~$2bn of ELV pre-tax profit).
- **Medicaid rate inadequacy and state budget stress.** Redetermination-driven acuity mix and underfunded state capitation rates have been the worst-performing book; ELV is **exiting additional Medicaid markets** and only expects Medicaid margin improvement in 2027. HUM's FY25 −$6.7bn net loss is the extreme case of this risk.
- **Regulatory and political risk is intensifying.** Scrutiny of MLR calculations under vertical integration (PBM/provider ownership), CMS rate-setting discretion (the 0.09% → 2.48% swing shows how binary this is), and ACA marketplace subsidy policy all sit outside company control. A single adverse rate notice can move sector market caps 15–20% in a day, as 27-Jan-2026 demonstrated.

## 5. ELV's Competitive Position: **STRONG** (but decelerating)

**Rationale — supports:**
- Best margins in the peer group on every line, achieved with a *diversified* book (commercial/ASO, MA, Medicaid) rather than a single programme.
- Scale: ~118m people served, $199bn revenue, top-3 national position in commercial group and MA; ~96k employees.
- Balance sheet and cash generation support a defensive profile (beta 0.71 — lowest volatility in the group).
- Management credibility on repricing: repeated guidance raises through 2026 and a credible 2027 +12% EPS growth target.

**Rationale — offsets:**
- **Revenue growth has collapsed from +12.6% (FY25) to +1.4% YoY in Q2-26 and Q3-26** as the company deliberately sheds membership (medical membership −1.1%). ELV is trading growth for margin — the correct call, but it caps the top line.
- Medicaid execution has been the weakest link; market exits signal that scale there is not translating into margin.
- Lagging share price suggests the market questions the durability of the margin premium rather than its current level.

Net: **strong franchise, average-to-strong earnings momentum, weak relative stock momentum.** ELV is the "quality defensive" of the group, not the recovery trade.

---

## 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL** (improving; ELV relatively attractive within it)

**Why not Attractive:** the 2027 rate reset fixes the pricing side of the equation but not the cost side; medical trend remains elevated, Medicaid rates remain inadequate, and the sector's earnings are hostage to a single CMS decision each April. Margins are recovering but remain ~130bp below 2022–23 levels for the better operators and catastrophically below for the weakest. Rivalry is intensifying as every carrier chases the same MA margin recovery, and political/MLR-scrutiny risk is rising.

**Why not Unattractive:** MA penetration (55% → 63% by 2034) is a genuine multi-year volume tailwind, the rate cycle has turned favourable (+2.48%), and valuations already discount a lot of bad news — ELV at ~12x forward earnings for a business with best-in-class margins and 12% guided EPS growth is a favourable risk/reward *within* a Neutral sector.

**Sector cycle position: late-trough / early-recovery (Stage 2 of 4).** The de-rating and the cost shock are behind us; the re-rating has begun but is unevenly distributed — recovery-torque names (HUM, UNH) have re-rated hard, while quality-stability names (ELV) have not.
