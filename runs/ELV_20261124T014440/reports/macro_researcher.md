## 1. Macro Environment Summary

**Stance: RISK-OFF / TRANSITIONAL — a real-rate shock, not an inflation-expectations shock.**

The US economy is in a late-cycle re-acceleration with the Fed *re-tightening*. Real GDP grew at a ~3.4% annualized pace in Q3 2026 (real GDP $24,611B vs $24,408B in Q2), roughly 2.1% YoY — no landing, hard or soft, is in evidence. Unemployment has fallen to **4.10%** (Oct 2026) from 4.50% a year earlier. Yet CPI has re-accelerated to **3.82% YoY** (Oct 2026) and is running at ~4.5% annualized over the last four months.

The FOMC **held at 3.75%–4.00% on October 28, 2026 on a 10–2 vote with two dissents in favour of a hike**. October minutes (released Nov 18) showed "almost all" officials expect further increases; markets price ~80% odds of a 25bp hike at the **December 8–9** meeting and another by March 2027. The easing cycle that took the effective rate from 4.33% (mid-2025) to 3.63% (mid-2026) has reversed — the effective rate was **3.88%** in October.

The critical detail is the *composition* of the yield move. The 10Y sits at **5.13%**, up ~70bp from 4.44% in late June. Over that same window the 10Y **TIPS real yield rose from 2.14% (June 16) to 2.77–2.88%**, while the 10Y breakeven *fell* from ~2.33% to **2.13%**. This is a term-premium / real-yield repricing — a fiscal-dominance signature — not a demand-pull inflation scare. Equity multiples, not earnings, bear the brunt.

That is precisely what happened: SPY fell **11.5%** from its October 6 peak (779.09) to 689.81 on November 20 before rebounding to 722.29. ELV's own 15% drawdown from its October 22 high ($445.28) is *partly* macro but primarily idiosyncratic (see §3).

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

Elevance is a **domestic, administratively-priced business**: ~100% US revenue across Medicaid, Commercial/ACA and Medicare Advantage, 96,615 employees, beta 0.71. That profile determines which macro variables matter — and which are noise.

**POSITIVE / INSULATING**

- **Zero FX translation exposure.** With essentially all revenue domestic, ELV is structurally immune to dollar strength — a genuine relative advantage versus the multinational pharma names that dominate the healthcare sector. Currency is a non-factor.
- **Low equity duration.** At **~14.2x** the 2026 adjusted EPS floor of ≥$26.75 and **~12.6x** 2027E (~$30.0 on management's ≥12% growth target, reaffirmed at the Nov 13 UBS conference), ELV enters a rising-real-yield regime with most multiple compression already banked — the stock has de-rated from ~18x to ~12–13x over 2022–26. A 2.77% real 10Y hurts, but it hits 25–35x growth equities far harder.
- **Medical-care CPI disinflation.** Medical care CPI is **+1.50% YoY** even as headline CPI runs 3.82%. The inflation problem is *not* in ELV's revenue basket — it is tariffs, energy and services elsewhere. This partly decouples ELV's pricing from the headline inflation shock.
- **4.1% unemployment supports commercial membership.** Employer-sponsored coverage is a function of employment, not GDP. A 4.1% jobless rate is the best possible backdrop for the Commercial segment.

**NEGATIVE**

- **Tight labour market → medical cost trend.** This is ELV's single most important macro variable. With unemployment at 4.1% and GDP near 3.4%, provider wage and unit-cost pressure persists. Q1 2026's benefit expense ratio rose 40bp to **86.8%** on "elevated medical cost trend"; Q3 2026 improved 70bp to **90.6%**. ELV cannot pass these costs through quickly — Medicaid rates are set by state actuaries, Medicare Advantage rates by CMS — so a tight labour market compresses margins with a 12–18 month administrative lag.
- **Rising real yields → refinancing and discount rate.** ELV carried **$31.0B of total debt** at Q2 2026 (down from $32.75B at FY2025), with a covenant debt-to-capital ratio of **42.0%** at 3/31/26. Income-statement sensitivity is genuinely modest: ~$1.35–1.40B annual interest on ~$180B+ revenue; a 100bp move across the entire stack is roughly $310M pre-tax (~$0.55/share, ~2% of EPS), and only a slice refinances annually. **The rate risk is in the multiple, not the P&L.**
- **Fiscal policy — Medicaid.** Medicaid is ELV's largest segment by revenue and is funded by state budgets now absorbing federal Medicaid retrenchment. This is a *fiscal* exposure masquerading as a healthcare exposure, with 2027 rate resets the swing factor.
- **ACA subsidy expiry → adverse selection.** Enhanced premium tax credits lapsed December 31, 2025; the Urban Institute estimated ~4.8M people lose coverage in 2026. Combined with UMich sentiment at **48.0** — near historic lows — this raises the risk that healthier individual-market members drop out, worsening the ACA risk pool. Management has guided to *lower* 2026 revenue, earnings and membership for this reason.
- **Term-premium regime.** A 5.47% 30Y and falling breakevens is a fiscal-dominance signal: deficits are crowding out, and defensive compounders get no help from the discount rate for as long as it persists.

**NEUTRAL**

- **Tariffs.** Healthcare services are largely exempt; the transmission is indirect (higher headline CPI → more Fed hikes → higher discount rate), not direct COGS. Watch pharmaceutical tariffs as a pass-through risk into pharmacy trend.

> **Important attribution discipline:** ELV's **−7.6% on November 18** (to $400.84) and further decline on the 19th were driven by CMS's proposed **CRUSH anti-fraud rule** extending Medicare Advantage recovery audits — a *regulatory*, not macro, event. Macro explains the *market* drawdown (SPY −11.5%); CMS explains ELV's underperformance within it. Do not conflate the two in the memo.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating: **NEUTRAL**

ELV is one of the better-positioned defensive franchises for this specific regime — a domestic revenue base, a 0.71 beta, an already-compressed multiple, and a healthcare inflation rate running *below* headline. The stock has still delivered **+23.6%** over twelve months (from $307.08 on Nov 3, 2025) and **+9.9% YTD** from $345.49, outperforming a correcting tape.

But the rating is capped at Neutral, not Favourable, because the three variables that matter most to this business model are all pointing the wrong way at once: tight labour markets feeding medical cost trend, a real-yield shock compressing what little multiple remains, and fiscal retrenchment in Medicaid arriving just as ACA subsidies have expired. There is **no recession signal** — the 2s10s at +39bp is positively sloped — which is a double-edged sword: no membership-destroying downturn, but also no relief from the Fed.

## 5. The Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise

**BULL (underpriced): a December skip triggers a violent squeeze in low-multiple defensives.**

Markets price ~80% odds of a December 9 hike — a hawkish path is *consensus* and already embedded in a 12.6x 2027E multiple. Yet breakevens are **falling** (2.33% → 2.13%) and real yields, not inflation expectations, are doing all the damage. If the FOMC holds on December 9 and CPI prints below 3.5% for November, the 10Y could retrace to 4.50–4.60% quickly. With no recession risk in the curve and 4.1% unemployment supporting commercial membership, ELV re-rating to 14–15x on $30.0 of 2027E EPS implies **$420–450, or +11% to +19%**, before any Medicaid rate upside or CRUSH-rule relief. The asymmetry favours the upside because the bear case is the priced one.

**BEAR (the risk worth sizing): Fed hikes in December *and* March, 10Y breaks 5.50%, and Medicaid rate resets disappoint into the CRUSH final rule.** In that world ELV de-rates to ~10x even "cheap" 2027E EPS — **~$300, or −21%** — proving that a low multiple is not a floor when the discount rate is set by a fiscal term premium. Watch the December 9 FOMC and the November CPI print as the two dated catalysts that determine which branch this takes.
