### 1. Governance Quality Rating: **ADEQUATE** (structure strong; oversight record is the weak link)

**Supports a higher rating:**
- Board of 11, of whom 10 are independent; **separate CEO and independent Board Chair** (Ramiro G. Peru) — no CEO/chairman duality.
- Standing Audit and Compensation & Talent committees; conventional proxy-access/independence posture per company governance guidelines.
- Section 302 certifications clean and standard in the Q3-2026 10-Q (Boudreaux, 21-Oct-2026); no material weakness disclosed in retrieved text.
- AM Best affirmed indicative shelf ratings at bbb+/stable (Nov-2026) — no immediate balance-sheet or control-driven credit concern.

**Why not Strong:**
- The board has presided over a **multi-year Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment data failure** that CMS characterised as “substantial and persistent” noncompliance, dating to payment year 2018 submissions. Structural independence did not translate into compliance oversight.
- **Key-person and succession opacity:** Boudreaux has been CEO since Nov-2017 (~9 years) with no disclosed succession framework; the CFO role (Mark Kaye) is the public face of the remediation and appears relatively new to the seat. Combined CFO/CEO transition risk is underappreciated.
- **Cannot verify pay-for-performance alignment** — proxy unavailable. Treat incentive structure as unassessed, not clean.

### 4. Top Operational Risks

1. **Risk-adjustment data systems failure at the core profit engine.** CMS found ELV submitted RADV data through incorrect systems for multiple years and, on CMS's account, breached the 60-day overpayment-report-and-return obligation as a result. This is a controls/IT-process failure — not a market event — in the segment that drives consolidated margin, and remediation is estimated at **~$935M**. Fix execution is the key 2027 swing factor.
2. **Medicaid margin trough plus state-contract concentration.** Management itself guides 2026 as a “trough year” for Medicaid margins (Q3-2026 benefit expense ratio **90.6%**), with recovery dependent on lagging state rate updates. Simultaneously, state regulators are penalising complaint handling, reimbursement timeliness and parity compliance (CA, CT). Rate adequacy and compliance quality are deteriorating in the same book.
3. **Third-party/cyber concentration and provider-relations friction.** The Change Healthcare breach (2024, up to ~100M individuals) illustrates single-clearinghouse systemic dependency across ELV's claims flow; the MDL and HHS-OCR aftermath continue. Separately, ELV's out-of-network referral penalty policy (~10% claims haircut) has drawn organised provider backlash and state scrutiny — a network-adequacy and business-continuity risk in a narrow-network model.
4. *Secondary:* key-person concentration in Boudreaux and a CFO carrying the remediation narrative.

### 5. Reputational Risk Assessment — **elevated and rising**

- **The language matters most:** CMS publicly alleged conduct demonstrating a *“pattern of knowing”* noncompliance. That phrasing is a reputational event distinct from the dollar amounts and invites political, media and plaintiff-bar amplification.
- **Care-denial narrative:** state Medicaid agencies and press coverage allege high denial rates for Medicaid patients; combined with the $15M DMHC complaint-handling fine, the $1.1M behavioural-health fine and the $12.88M parity settlement, a coherent “profits over patients” story is now well-sourced — precisely the narrative that fuels state legislation and rate-setting hostility.
- **Quality-score credibility:** litigating star ratings erodes the credibility of the quality franchise used to market MA plans to seniors and to justify bonus payments.
- **Offsets:** Q3-2026 beat with FY26 guidance raised to ≥$29 EPS and a 12% 2027 growth target; stable AM Best outlook; active consumer-facing AI/healthcare messaging. These buy goodwill but do not offset regulator-sourced criticism.
- **Market confirmation:** ELV fell from $446 (16-Nov) to $379.55 (23-Nov) — **-14.7% in four sessions** and -15.8% off the October high, on regulatory news, despite raised guidance. The market is pricing regulatory tail risk, not earnings risk.

---

### 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

Regulatory risk alone justifies the High rating: an unresolved CMS intermediate-sanction threat over the largest profit pool, a proposed rule (CRUSH) aimed directly at the company's risk-adjustment economics, a litigious posture toward its principal regulator, and a documented multi-state pattern of consumer-protection failures — all under a board with strong structure but a demonstrated oversight gap.

---

### 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **Potential False Claims Act / DOJ exposure (highest severity).** CMS's findings combine (a) years of risk-adjustment data noncompliance and (b) an alleged failure to report and return identified overpayments within 60 days. That fact pattern is the standard predicate for FCA and Civil Investigative Demand activity. *This is an inferred risk, not a disclosed investigation* — but CMS's “pattern of knowing” language is the escalation trigger. Recommend verified search of DOJ/qui tam dockets and the next 10-Q legal-proceedings note before sizing.
2. **Quantify the CRUSH-rule tail.** Extrapolated RADV audits retroactive to 2018 could produce repayment demands far exceeding the $935M remediation estimate. Model a bear case; peer-wide but ELV is disproportionately exposed given its existing adverse audit history.
3. **CFO's non-10b5-1 sale at the 52-week high, days before a 16% decline** — seek explanation; confirm whether any trading window/policy exception applied.
4. **AEP-timed sanction risk.** Any CMS action that suspends MA marketing/enrollment during or near the 2027 annual enrollment window would be materially value-destructive and is not, in our view, adequately discounted.
5. **Proxy/compensation disclosure gap** — obtain DEF 14A to confirm whether 2026–27 incentive metrics reward EPS/star-rating outcomes in a way that encouraged the risk-adjustment behaviour now under sanction. Unresolved, and central to whether this is a one-off or a cultural issue.
