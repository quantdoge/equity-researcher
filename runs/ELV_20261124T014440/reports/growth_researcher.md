# Elevance Health (ELV) — Growth Investment Analysis
*Price $379.55 (Nov 23, 2026) · Mkt cap ~$84B · 52-wk range $274.84–$450.69*

## 1. Growth Story

Elevance is a ~$199B-revenue Blue Cross licensee (14 states, ~47M members) whose growth engine has shifted from risk-bearing membership to **Carelon**, its vertically-integrated PBM (CarelonRx) and care-delivery/behavioral-health services arm — the one genuine platform asset in the story, with a stated goal of scaling high-margin, capital-light services revenue and a newly appointed Carelon president. The nearer-term thesis is not innovation at all: it is a **margin-recovery re-rating** off a Medicaid trough (guided ~–1.75% in 2026) and a deliberately shrunk Medicare Advantage footprint, with management targeting **≥12% adjusted EPS growth in 2027** from a raised $26.75 baseline. I want to be explicit about my framing: this is a scaled, regulated, 4%-operating-margin intermediary — **not a disruptive-innovation compounder** — and it should be underwritten on EPS inflection and multiple, not on TAM capture.

## 3. TAM and Penetration

US national health expenditure is roughly **$5.3T growing ~5.5%/yr**; ELV's $199B is only ~3.8% of it. The serviceable slice — managed care administration plus pharmacy/care services — is on the order of $1.6T, where ELV holds ~12%. The realistic prize is not penetration of a greenfield TAM but **share shift within a slow-growing, price-regulated pool**: Medicare Advantage (~1.52M members, growing +0.7%), Medicaid (where ELV is *exiting* markets), commercial self-funded, and CarelonRx. Carelon is the only segment with a true platform dynamic (Q3'26 revenue $19.3B, +5.5%, serving both internal and third-party risk), and it is the single most important number to watch. **Penetration opportunity here is modest and margin-led, not volume-led** — which caps the multiple a growth investor can rationally pay.

## 4. Upcoming Catalysts

- **Late Jan 2027 — Q4'26 call and formal 2027 guidance:** the key re-rating event. Does the ≥12% EPS growth target survive, and is the baseline the raised $26.75 or the higher ~$29 delivered?
- **Jan 19, 2027 — CRUSH rule comment period closes.** CMS's proposed rule would extend MA RADV audits with **extrapolation of sample findings across whole contracts back to payment year 2018**. ELV has less MA exposure than UNH/HUM (Mizuho ranks it least-exposed of the majors), and Oppenheimer expects litigation — a softened or litigated rule is the largest single upside catalyst; a finalized rule as drafted is the largest downside.
- **Q4'26–Feb 2027 — 2027 MA selling season results:** 308 plans filed (vs. 345), Florida footprint cut roughly in half (103→52), Texas +32%; third-party True Plan Value down 19.5% YoY and –38.5% vs. 2024. Watch whether "margin over membership" holds enrollment.
- **July 1, 2027 — state Medicaid rate updates**, the mechanism for recovery toward the 2–4% long-term margin target, plus the pace of further state exits.
- **Ongoing — Carelon scaling and capital deployment** (~$2.6B buyback at 1.9x book, 3.1% buyback yield).

## 5. Alternative Data Signals

- **Google Trends (12-mo, US):** interest peaked at 100 in March 2026 and has decayed to ~62 with no earnings-week spike — a **neglected, un-hyped stock**, consistent with deep-value rather than momentum positioning. Neutral-to-mildly-positive for entry, useless as a demand proxy.
- **Job postings (Indeed proxy):** ~295 open roles, weighted to LTSS/nurse case-management, customer care, and summer 2027 interns — **no aggressive growth hiring**, consistent with management's opex-discipline and automation posture (adjusted opex ratio actually *rose* 90bp to 11.3% in Q3'26, so the efficiency story is not yet visible in the data).
- **Revision/price-target divergence (strongest signal):** targets raised 12 times in one week while the stock fell **–14.9% from $446 (Nov 16) to $379.55 (Nov 23)** on the CRUSH rule and a revived "Medicare for All" rhetorical push. That gap between rising fundamentals and falling price is the crux of the setup.
- **Earnings-surprise regime:** 6 of 7 beats at +16.9% average — forward guidance has been set conservatively for at least two years.

## 6. Growth Verdict: **HOLD**

ELV fails my growth-quality screen on the metrics that matter most to me: **Rule of 40 of 14, operating margin down 238bp in four years, gross margin compressing, revenue growth decelerating to ~0% in 2026, GAAP EPS flat for five years, and FCF down 56% since 2021 while buybacks plus dividends ($4.1B) exceed FCF ($3.2B).** There is no innovation moat here in the Cathie Wood sense — the moat is state licenses, provider networks, and scale in a business where regulators set the price.

What keeps me at Hold rather than Sell is that the *forward* setup is genuinely attractive on a tactical basis: **12.7x forward earnings, 1.91x book, a credible 2027 EPS inflection off a Medicaid trough, an 86% beat rate, the strongest revision momentum in the managed-care group, and ~28% to the median analyst target.** As a value/margin-recovery position at 13x with a 4.9% total capital-return yield, it clears the bar. As a *growth* holding, it does not: a ~9% long-run EPS CAGR does not deserve a premium multiple, and the CRUSH rule introduces an unquantified retroactive liability that caps how much I will pay for that 9%.

**I would be a buyer, not a holder, only on a resolution or softening of the CRUSH rule that takes the stock back toward the $450 high — and I would size it as a defensive value sleeve, not as a growth allocation.**

## 7. Key Risks

- **Regulatory/tail risk (highest):** CRUSH rule retroactive extrapolation of RADV audit findings to payment year 2018 could produce a multi-billion, multi-year restatement of MA revenue. Partly mitigated by ELV's smaller MA book (1.52M members) but not quantifiable from public disclosure.
- **Execution risk on the 2027 target:** 12% EPS growth is expected to come simultaneously from Medicaid rate catch-up, MA margin expansion (2%→long-term range), Carelon adoption *and* opex efficiency — a four-front execution ask with no single lever. 2026 earnings already benefited from non-operating items and tax benefits that won't repeat.
- **Membership attrition / negative operating leverage:** deliberately shrinking MA TPV by 19.5% and exiting Medicaid markets risks forfeiting fixed-cost scale in a 4%-margin business where a 50bp MLR miss is a ~10% EPS event.
- **Free cash flow quality:** the working-capital drain and buyback-above-FCF dynamic is the clearest red flag in the financials and would force debt funding if it persists.
- **Political/regulatory overhang:** "Medicare for All" rhetoric and the expiration of enhanced ACA subsidies (marketplace enrollment expected to fall from 22.3M to ~17.5M) shrink the individual-market pool ELV has been growing into.
- **Competitive:** UNH (25.4x P/E, 15.4x EV/EBITDA), HUM (39.0x), CI (12.2x), CNC (loss-making) — ELV is cheap versus UNH/HUM but roughly in line with CI, so "cheap vs. peers" is a thinner argument than it first appears.
