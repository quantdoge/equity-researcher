### 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW**

- Asset-light, no manufacturing, no carbon-cost pass-through, no stranded-asset or CBAM exposure. Scope 1+2 is a rounding error against $199B revenue (carbon intensity effectively negligible vs. sector).
- **Targets:** net zero Scope 1, 2 **and 3** by 2030; absolute Scope 1 −46.2% by 2030 vs. 2019 base; 100% renewable electricity achieved 2021 (four years early); supplier science-based-target engagement. Oversight sits with the **Board Governance Committee** and a Chief Sustainability Officer.
- **Residual risk is credibility, not compliance:** a 2030 net-zero *Scope 3* target for a company whose Scope 3 is dominated by purchased clinical services and pharmaceuticals is not credibly achievable without material offsets — a greenwashing/reverse-greenwashing headline risk rather than a cash-flow risk. Disclosure assurance remains voluntary (no CSRD; SEC climate rule does not bind a domestic payer meaningfully).
- Physical climate risk is **indirect but real**: heat-driven utilisation and cost (the company's own Carelon Research work links extreme heat to healthcare use). This flows through medical cost trend, not through a "climate" line item.

### 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

This is the crux of the ELV ESG case.

1. **MA risk-adjustment enforcement (critical, quantified):** Elevance **paid CMS $342M** (reported May 2026) in response to a **February 2026 CMS enforcement/sanctions notice** alleging failure to properly address risk-adjustment overpayments. That is ~5% of annual net income, cash, already out the door — and it establishes a template for RADV extrapolation and possible FCA follow-on. Peer read-across (Kaiser affiliates $556M, Aetna $118M) shows this is an *industry-wide* enforcement wave, not a one-off.
2. **Behavioural health "ghost networks" (critical for licence to operate):** On **10 Nov 2026** Carelon Behavioral Health settled with the **New York AG** — **$2.35M penalties/fees plus up to $8M restitution** — over inaccurate provider directories and access denials (two settlements). A parallel **class action survived a motion to dismiss** (deceptive advertising/state insurance law claims), and the case had been dismissed in March 2026 on federal-preemption grounds before being revived. Uncapped litigation tail.
3. **Prior-authorisation / AI-denial scrutiny:** Intense political and congressional attention on payer utilisation management. Elevance's public position is that AI does **not** automate denials and only licensed clinicians deny — a defensible control, but the industry signed a CMS/HHS prior-authorisation pledge (Jan 2026) and has eliminated only ~11% of PAs so far; continued legislative risk (state PA bans, e-PA mandates) is live.
4. **Workforce:** Restructuring is ongoing (WARN notices including 85 DC and 123 California roles; open postings down ~15% YoY; employee reviews cite high turnover). Restructuring of this scale risks claims-accuracy and service quality — which is precisely the failure mode that drives items (1) and (2). No significant union exposure or safety risk (non-industrial).
5. **Positives:** health-equity programming (Whole Health Index, maternal health, food-as-medicine), Foundation/community funding, and mature privacy controls post-2015. Legacy Anthem breach remains the sector's cautionary tale (~$179M cumulative settlements/fines), which correlates with a more mature security posture today.

**Verdict:** social risk is *structurally embedded in the business model* (utilisation management + government coding), not incidental. Expect recurring, quantifiable charges rather than a single tail event.

### 4. Governance Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Strengths**
- **Independent Chair** (Ramiro G. Peru, since May 2025) separate from CEO Gail Boudreaux — no CEO/Chair duality. 11-member board, **10 of 11 independent**.
- **Say-on-pay ≈92.5% support** (May 2026 AGM) — above the 70% "concern" threshold; compensation & talent, audit, finance and governance committees all independent; EY ratified as auditor.
- Single share class — **no dual-class structure, no poison pill**; shareholder proposal access exists (a Trillium ESG fund 527-political-spend study proposal was on the 2026 ballot).
- Two new directors added in the past year; board graded **B-** by Boardroom Alpha.

**Weaknesses**
- **Staggered/classified board** (three-year terms, only three nominees per year) — a genuine takeover-defence and accountability drag.
- **Long tenures:** CEO ~10 years on board, one independent director at ~16 years; refreshment is slow despite recent additions.
- **Compliance oversight is the real deduction:** a $342M CMS payment and a state-AG behavioural-health settlement within the same year are governance failures at the *control* layer, even where the *board structure* is textbook. Audit/Compliance committee effectiveness deserves scrutiny.
- The 527 political-spend proposal drew only ~4% support and the board recommended against — disclosure on political spending remains thin.

### 6. Regulatory ESG Exposure

- **US federal (dominant):** CMS MA payment rules, RADV audits, star ratings, the prior-authorisation interoperability rule (Jan 2026), HIPAA/OCR. Payer PBM and 340B scrutiny. Antitrust interest in provider/asset roll-ups (Carelon).
- **State:** 50-state insurance departments — rate filings, network-adequacy and mental-health-parity enforcement (NY, CA, FL most active). State-level anti-ESG/anti-DEI legislation is a low-grade cost (disclosure burden), not a revenue threat.
- **EU/International:** **minimal** — no material CSRD or CBAM exposure. **No ESG-linked debt covenants identified**; capital structure is conventional senior unsecured (AM Best indicative bbb+/stable on the new shelf). This is a positive: no coupon step-up/repricing risk from missing sustainability KPIs.
- **Litigation-driven regulation** is the practical channel: AGs and class actions are setting payer conduct standards faster than rulemaking.

### 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (governance above average; social laggard for the sector)

- **E: Leader** (trivial footprint, credible-ish targets with a Scope 3 credibility caveat).
- **S: Laggard** — repeat, quantified consumer-protection and coding-integrity failures in a sector where these *are* the core operating risk.
- **G: Above average structurally** (independent chair, 10/11 independent, 92.5% SOP support, single share class) **but weakened by control failures** and a staggered board.
- Versus peers (UNH, CI, CVS, HUM): ELV sits **mid-to-upper pack** — better governance optics than UNH post-2024, materially less environmental/legal tail than an integrated CVS, but with the same structural MA-coding and utilisation-management exposure. Historically recognised as MSCI **AA** and a six-year FTSE4Good constituent — self-reported/index recognition should be discounted given the 2026 enforcement record.

### 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (mild negative tilt on social licence)

- **Not a thesis-maker or breaker.** The material ESG risk here is a **recurring compliance tax of $100–350M/yr**, already largely priced after the $342M disclosure. Environmental risk contributes ~nothing to cost of capital; there are **no ESG-linked debt covenants** and no stranded-asset exposure.
- **The one line to watch:** if the Feb 2026 CMS sanctions notice escalates to RADV extrapolation, MA contract suspension, or an FCA intervention, that migrates from "cost of doing business" to a **margin and multiple event** — especially given MA/Medicaid margins are already the market's central debate (2027 margin-recovery thesis).
- **Governance is supportive of the recovery thesis:** independent chair, high say-on-pay support, and a board that is being refreshed means capital-allocation and management-accountability questions get a fair hearing. Staggered board is the main structural blemish.
- **Positive optionality:** the CMS star-ratings suit seeks $115M–$516M of bonus revenue — an ESG/legal-risk item that is actually an *upside* catalyst.
- **Positioning:** suitable for ESG-constrained mandates with a "harm-reduction" screen (low carbon, no controversial weapons/tobacco, decent governance) but **likely excluded or flagged by strict product-governance/access screens** (prior authorisation, behavioural-health access, MA coding integrity). Expect elevated headline risk into 2027 AGM season (527 political-spend and access-related proposals).
