# Elevance Health Inc. (NYSE: ELV) — Value Investment Analysis
**Price: $379.55 (Nov 23, 2026) | Market cap ~$82B | EV ~$108B | Div yield 1.81%**

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **NARROW (and narrowing)**

Elevance (formerly Anthem) is the largest for-profit Blue Cross Blue Shield licensee, with ~$199B of 2025 revenue across Commercial, Government (Medicaid) and Medicare Advantage, plus Carelon (PBM, behavioral, care delivery). Sources of advantage:

- **Intangible assets (real but circumscribed):** perpetual-state BCBS licenses in 14 states, Blue-brand exclusivity, and #1/#2 share in most of its geographies give it network density and provider-discount scale. This is a genuine barrier to entry.
- **Efficient scale:** the fixed cost of clinical/claims infrastructure is spread over ~47M members; regional incumbency is hard to dislodge.
- **Cost advantage:** $199B of medical spend is negotiating leverage.

**Why the moat is Narrow, not Wide:** Elevance cannot set its own prices. Medicare Advantage rates are administratively set by CMS; Medicaid rates are set by state legislatures; Commercial rates are re-priced annually in a competitive bid against UNH, CI, CVS/Aetna, Centene and regional Blues. There is no demonstrated pricing power — only cost management against an externally determined revenue pool. Switching costs are low for national employers.

**The decisive evidence — the moat has narrowed over five years:**
- Revenue grew **+43.6% from 2022 to 2025** ($156.6B → $199.1B).
- Net income went **nowhere**: $5.89B → $5.66B. Owner earnings: $6.42B (2022) → $6.12B (2024) → $6.09B (2025).
- Diluted EPS rose only from $24.28 to $25.12 (+3.5% cumulative) — *and even that was manufactured by buybacks*, not by the business.
- Operating margin compressed from 6.45% (2021) → 5.40% → 5.10% → 5.03% → **4.07% (2025)**.
- ROIC: 13.6% → 13.2% → 12.0% → **10.5%**.

That is a textbook signature of **unprofitable growth**: scale bought with margin, no operating leverage. ROIC still clears WACC (~8%), so value isn't being destroyed, but the spread is thinning fast. Buffett would say the company is "a good business, not a great one," and the trend is the wrong direction.

**Strengths:** Revenue is highly predictable and counter-cyclical (healthcare demand doesn't vanish in recessions); membership is sticky at the book-of-business level; owner earnings have been remarkably *stable* at ~$6.1B for four straight years; capital returns are consistent ($2.6B buyback + $1.5B dividends in 2025; share count down 12% since 2021).

**Weaknesses:**
- **2025 was a genuine quality break.** Q4 2025 medical loss ratio hit 93.5%; Q4 operating income collapsed to $581M; FY25 FCF fell to $3.17B (56% of net income) on a **$3.3B working-capital outflow** (Medicaid/MA receivable and payable timing). FCF conversion of 56% is well below the 80% threshold I require.
- **Margin fragility is structural, not cyclical.** 2025's miss came from elevated utilization and adverse Medicaid mix; 2026 SGA reclassification makes segment-level comparison muddy.
- **Balance sheet is levered, not fortress-like.** Total debt $33.2B, net debt $23.7B (~2.4x 2025 EBITDA), interest coverage 5.8x. Goodwill + intangibles of $39.5B *exceed* the entire $44.0B book equity — **tangible book value is negative (~-$4B)**. Reported P/B of 1.9x is therefore not a safety anchor, and ROE of 12.9% is flattered by leverage and a negative tangible base. Senior unsecured ratings in the A range are the real comfort here.
- Earnings quality: the gap between 2025 GAAP EPS ($25.12) and adjusted ($30.29) is ~$5/share, almost entirely intangible amortization from two decades of BCBS/MMO acquisitions. GAAP is the more honest number, though the amortization is not a cash cost.

**Score rationale:** 6/10 — a durable, cash-generative, capital-returning franchise, penalized for declining returns on capital, thin margins, a levered balance sheet with negative tangible equity, and a demonstrated inability to grow earnings with revenue.

## 3. Valuation Analysis

**Current multiples (price $379.55):**
- P/E: 15.1x trailing GAAP | **13.1x 2026E adjusted EPS (~$29)** | ~16.5x 2026E GAAP (~$23)
- EV/EBITDA: 11.2x on depressed 2025 EBITDA ($9.66B); ~10.3x normalized (~$10.5B)
- P/FCF: 26.5x trailing (distorted); FCF yield 3.8%
- Owner earnings yield on EV: **5.6%** ($6.09B / $107.9B) — the single most important anchor
- P/B: 1.9x reported; ~1.8x on est. Q3-26 BVPS (~$211); **negative tangible book**
- Peer P/E median: 27.1x → 44% "discount," but that median is dominated by pharma (LLY, JNJ, ABBV). The relevant managed-care comp set (UNH, CI, CNC, MOH, CVS) trades in the 11–16x range, so ELV is *in line with its true peer group, not cheap against it.*

**Intrinsic value (owner-earnings DCF, $6.1B base, WACC 8.5%, terminal 2.5%):**
- At 4% growth: PV of FCFs $50.6B + PV of terminal $69.2B = EV $119.8B → less net debt $24B → **$440/share**
- At 3% growth: **~$395/share**
- Cross-check, adjusted earnings power: $6.3–6.8B normalized × 13–15x = **$377–$464**

**Intrinsic value estimate: ~$415 (range $395–$440).** At $379.55, the shares trade at roughly a **9% discount to intrinsic value.** My required margin of safety is 20–30%, implying an entry price of **~$290–$330**. A business trading at a fair price is not a buy.

**Note on price context:** ELV bottomed near **$268 in August 2025** (MLR shock) — that was the margin-of-safety moment. It rallied 66% to $446 by Nov 16, 2026 on the 2026 margin recovery (Q3-26 adjusted EPS $6.99 vs. $4.71 consensus, 2026 guide raised to ≥$29, MLR improving to ~90.6%). The recent 11% pullback from $446 to $380 (Nov 18–23) was **sector-wide managed-care de-rating, with no company-specific announcement.** The cheapness has largely been arbitraged away; investors are now paying for a recovery that has already been delivered once.

## 4. Management Quality Assessment — **Good, not exceptional (6.5/10)**

- **Gail Boudreaux (CEO)** has run the company since 2017 with a coherent strategy: shift revenue mix toward government programs and Carelon services, and take cost out. She delivered on the 2026 turnaround, and the Q3-26 beat plus raise supports credibility. Execution through the 2025 cost shock was candid — guidance was reset hard rather than bridged with accounting.
- **Capital allocation:** disciplined and shareholder-friendly — ~$4.1B returned in 2025, share count down 12% in five years, and buybacks were aggressive precisely when the stock was cheapest (2025), which is exactly right. But: 2025 distributions ($4.13B) exceeded reported FCF ($3.17B), funded from the balance sheet, and the dividend hike collapsed to **+0.6% in 2026** after +5% in 2025 and +10–16% in 2022–24 — a quiet admission that free cash flow is tighter than adjusted EPS implies.
- **M&A:** the Carelon/care-delivery roll-up (Integra, myNEXUS, Paragon) has been reasonably priced but has not yet moved consolidated returns; goodwill now exceeds book equity. Empire-building risk is moderate, not acute.
- **Insider ownership** is modest, in line with large-cap norms; this is a professional-management company, not an owner-operator business.

## 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD**

Elevance is a legitimately moated regional oligopolist with bond-like earnings stability, respectable returns on capital, and a management team that allocates capital sensibly. It is not, however, a great business: five years of 44% revenue growth produced **zero** earnings growth, ROIC is falling toward the cost of capital, and the balance sheet carries $33B of debt against negative tangible equity.

At $379.55 the stock is ~9% below my ~$415 intrinsic value — a fair price for a narrow-moat, government-priced business with binary medical-cost risk. That is not a margin of safety. **I would not buy here.** I would become a buyer with conviction below **~$310** (25% discount) and a strong buyer below **$290**, roughly 12x normalized owner earnings on an EV basis.

**For existing holders:** hold. The 2027 setup is genuinely favorable — Medicaid rate increases, MA margin guided from ≥2% toward a 3–5% long-term target, and a 1.8% dividend while you wait. Do not add at this price.

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Medical cost trend / MLR whipsaw (highest probability).** 2025 proved that a few hundred basis points of utilization surprise erases a year of earnings and half the market cap. Q4 is seasonally the danger quarter; 2026's Q4 consensus of just $2.08 is a thin cushion. If 2027 MA margins fail to improve, my $415 estimate falls toward $350.
2. **Regulatory and reimbursement risk (highest severity).** CMS sets MA rates annually; the CY2027 rate notice and the administration's posture toward MA plan payments are the single biggest swing factor. Elevated risk-adjustment (RADV) audit exposure and Medicare repayment/clawback activity could produce multi-billion-dollar charges that no DCF anticipates. States' Medicaid budgets are under acute fiscal stress.
3. **Medicaid redetermination and acuity mix.** Membership churn as states unwind eligibility has repeatedly left Elevance with a sicker, costlier residual book. Rate increases lag cost trend by 12–18 months.
4. **Margin structure has no floor.** Operating margin has fallen in four of the last five years. If 4% is the new normal rather than a trough, the earnings base is permanently lower and the multiple is not 13x — it's 17x.
5. **Balance sheet with negative tangible equity.** $23.7B net debt plus $39.5B of goodwill means book value offers no downside cushion. A large adverse reserve development or an MA-focused acquisition would stress the A-rating and force equity issuance.
6. **Sector de-rating risk.** The Nov 18, 2026 move shows the whole managed-care complex now trades on sentiment about government-program economics. ELV will not escape a sector re-rating regardless of its own execution.
