## CENTENE CORPORATION (NYSE: CNC) — Value Investment Analysis
*Price $74.77 (Nov 16, 2026) | Market cap ~$36.9B | 494M diluted shares | Net debt ≈ $0*

---

### 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat Rating: **None**

Centene is the largest U.S. Medicaid managed-care organization: 25.7M at-risk members, ~$195B revenue, four segments (Medicaid $24.0B/qtr, Medicare $11.3B, Commercial $8.7B, Other $1.1B).

There *is* a real scale advantage — provider contracting, administrative leverage, bidding capability, and 5-year state contracts create entry barriers. But that advantage is **captured by the states, not the shareholder.** Rates are administratively set, and minimum medical-loss-ratio floors (85–88%) cap profitability by contract. There are no network effects, no member switching costs (annual re-enrollment), no brand, and no pricing power.

The verdict comes from the numbers, not the narrative — ROIC has been below any reasonable cost of capital for four straight years:

| | 2022 | 2023 | 2024 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ROIC | 2.9% | 6.5% | 6.9% | −19.9% |
| ROE | 5.0% | 10.4% | 12.5% | −33.3% |

A moated business earns ROIC > WACC *consistently*. CNC has not cleared ~9% even in its best recent year. The moat has **narrowed**, not widened: Marketplace membership has collapsed from 5.83M to 3.40M (−42%), total membership is down 8.1% YoY, and revenue is guided **flat** for 2026 ($193.5–197.5B vs. $194.8B in 2025).

**Moat: None.** Scale is a modest cost advantage, not a durable franchise.

### 2. Financial Quality Score: **4 / 10**

**What's acceptable:**
- Balance sheet is sound: debt reduced to $15.5B (from $18.2B at YE25), $39.9B cash/investments, net debt ≈ zero, no revolver drawn, $1.9B remaining on the buyback-of-debt program.
- Cash generation is real on average: 5-yr average FCF ~$3.9B (2021 $3.3B, 2022 $5.3B, 2023 $7.3B, 2024 −$0.5B, 2025 $4.3B). Trailing FCF yield **11.8%**.

**What fails the test:**
- **Earnings quality is poor.** FY2025: revenue $194.8B, GAAP net loss −$6.67B (EPS −$13.53) after a **$6.7B non-cash goodwill impairment**. Goodwill/intangibles have fallen from $27.6B (2021) to $15.4B — still **77% of book equity**, so reported book value ($40.84/sh) is soft; tangible book is only ~$9.5/share.
- **Cash flow is wildly unpredictable** — negative in 2024 (working-capital swing of −$4.5B), and Q3 2026 alone consumed $3.7B of operating cash on pass-through timing.
- **Brutal operating leverage to a single variable.** A 100bp miss on the health benefits ratio on $195B of revenue is ~$1.95B pretax — roughly 75% of full-year adjusted net income. This is not a business with a margin of safety inside the P&L.
- GAAP-to-adjusted gap is large and *recurring*: 2026 guidance is >$3.46 GAAP vs. >$5.20 adjusted.
- No dividend, ever.

CNC is the cheapest name in its cohort on both book and free cash flow. But cheap is what a no-moat, below-cost-of-capital business *should* be.

**Earnings power:** 2026 adjusted EPS guided >$5.20 (YTD actual $6.46; consensus Q4 −$1.16 → ~$5.30). At $74.77 that is **14.1x adjusted**, 21.6x GAAP. Normalized mid-cycle EPS (2023 $4.95, 2024 $6.31, 2026E $5.30) is roughly **$6.00 → 12.5x**.

**Intrinsic value (owner earnings, my own build — the DCF tool's default growth input was unusable):** normalized owner earnings of $3.2–4.0B (adjusted net income ~$2.6B + D&A $1.2B − capex $0.75B; corroborated by the 5-yr average FCF of $3.9B), grown 0–3%, discounted 10–11% for regulatory/no-moat risk:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear (no growth, wide risk premium) | $3.2B | 0% | 11% | **$59** |
| Base | $3.2B | 2.5% | 11% | **$78** |
| Bull (margin recovery holds) | $3.7B | 2.5% | 10% | **$102** |

**Intrinsic value ≈ $80 (range $59–$105). Current price $74.77 = ~7% discount to the midpoint.** The *width* of that range — driven by HBR volatility and risk-adjustment estimation error — is itself the argument against paying up. There is **no 20–30% margin of safety** here.

Cross-check: analyst targets cluster at $69–82 (median ~$71). The stock now trades *above* most published targets, after nearly tripling from $25 in July 2025 and rising 13% on Nov 4.

### 4. Management Quality: **5 / 10**

- **CEO Sarah London** (since 2022) has executed the right strategic pivot: pricing discipline in Marketplace, exiting unprofitable membership, HBR improvement from 94.3% (Q4'25) to a guided 90.75% (2026), and $2.7B of debt reduction. Execution since the July 2025 collapse has been credible.
- **But credibility was damaged.** The July 2025 guidance withdrawal and the surprise $6.7B Marketplace risk-adjustment/goodwill charge were disclosure failures as much as operating failures. A **CFO change in 2026** adds to the turnover signal.
- **Capital allocation is mediocre.** Shares fell from 590M (2021) to 491M — a genuine 17% reduction — but ~$10B of buybacks were executed in 2022–2024 into deteriorating fundamentals, immediately preceding a $6.7B impairment. That is buying back stock in a business whose earnings base you had mis-estimated.
- No dividend; insider ownership is low (typical of this sector). Empire-building is in the DNA — the goodwill balance is the fossil record of two decades of M&A.

---

### 5. Investment Verdict: **HOLD** (do not initiate new capital at this price)

Centene today is a *better-run* company than it was 16 months ago — margins are recovering, the loss-making Marketplace book has shrunk 42%, debt is down, and the balance sheet is close to net cash. It is also, on 8.5x trailing FCF and 12.5x normalized EPS, statistically cheap.

But this is precisely the setup where Buffett's discipline binds:
1. **No moat, and returns on capital below WACC** for four consecutive years. Cheapness cannot compound if incremental capital earns 7%.
2. **Earnings are not predictable** — a single 100bp HBR error wipes out three-quarters of a year's profit, and 2024 produced negative FCF.
3. **No margin of safety.** At $74.77 the stock sits ~7% below my $80 intrinsic value midpoint and *above* the median analyst target, after a 3x rally. My required entry is a 25–30% discount to intrinsic value: **~$55–60**.

**Action:** Hold existing positions; do not add. Revisit as a Buy below ~$60 (≈11x normalized EPS, ~7x FCF), or if 2027 guidance demonstrates a sustainable ≥92% ROIC-consistent margin with GAAP earnings converging toward adjusted.

### 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

**Downside (why I won't pay $75):**
- **Policy risk is existential, not incidental.** ACA subsidy expiration already cut Marketplace membership 42%. Medicaid redeterminations, directed-payment reform, state budget stress, and Medicare Advantage rate pressure are all live. A single-payer or block-grant shift would impair the franchise.
- **Risk-adjustment estimation** — the exact mechanism that caused the 2025 blowup — remains an unhedgeable accounting exposure on ~$50B/quarter of premium.
- **Goodwill overhang:** $15.4B, or 77% of book equity. Reported book value can be impaired again; tangible book is ~$9.5/share.
- **Medical cost trend** could reverse the HBR recovery; Q4 is seasonally the weakest quarter (consensus −$1.16).
- **Q3 2026 operating cash outflow of $3.7B** warrants watching for reserve/payment-timing stress.

**Upside (why this isn't a Sell):**
- 2026 HBR guidance improved 15bp at midpoint; adjusted EPS guidance raised $0.40; 2027 setup described as "building on this progress."
- ~11.8% trailing FCF yield with net cash and a $1.9B debt-repurchase authorization provides real optionality for per-share value.
- At 1.8x book — the lowest in managed care — a re-rating to CI's 1.9x book on a *recovering* earnings base offers mid-teens upside without heroic assumptions.

**Bottom line:** A fair business at a fair price. Fair is not a buy.
