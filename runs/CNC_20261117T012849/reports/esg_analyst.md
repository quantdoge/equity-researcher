## 2. Environmental Risk Assessment: **LOW** (financially), **MEDIUM** on disclosure trajectory

- **Own footprint immaterial.** Asset-light managed care; emissions are offices and a remote-heavy workforce. Centene reports Scope 1, Scope 2 and select Scope 3 categories via annual sustainability report, TCFD index and SASB report, and has completed a climate risk assessment. **No disclosed GHG reduction targets and no climate transition plan** — confirmed by the 2024 shareholder proposal record.
- **Governance friction on climate disclosure.** A Chevedden resolution requesting Paris-aligned, SBTi-consistent near/long-term targets and a transition plan received **36.1% support at the May 2024 AGM** against board opposition — a significant protest threshold that signals investor dissatisfaction with disclosure ambition, not with environmental performance.
- **The real E exposure is cost, not carbon.** Healthcare is ~8.5% of US emissions; more importantly, climate-sensitive morbidity (heat, respiratory illness, vector-borne disease) and extreme-weather disruption of provider networks feed directly into Centene's **medical loss ratio** — the one E channel that is genuinely material to this P&L. This is a second-order but legitimate risk given the 2025 HBR shock (see §3).
- **Regulatory:** California SB 253/261 apply via Health Net's California operations (Scope 1–2, and Scope 3 on a delayed timeline; TCFD-style financial risk report). No CSRD, no CBAM exposure — Centene has no EU operations or carbon-intensive imports.
- **Verdict:** No environmental controversy pipeline, no remediation liabilities, no stranded-asset exposure. Not a valuation factor.

## 3. Social Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

This is Centene's dominant ESG axis — both its mission and its principal tail risk.

**a) Coverage continuity / redeterminations.** The unwinding of pandemic-era continuous enrolment produced **>25M Medicaid/CHIP disenrolments nationally**; Centene guided to losing roughly 2M members and continues to absorb attrition (2024 results cited lower Medicaid membership from redeterminations plus divestitures). The company frames this as mission-consistent (moving members into Marketplace coverage), but procedural-termination controversies create political and rate-negotiation risk across 30 state contracts.

**b) Product quality / Star ratings — the most underappreciated financial vector.** CMS 2026 Star Ratings show Centene with a **3.39 portfolio average and only ~20% of MA members in 4★+ plans**; the share in 4★+ plans **fell from 16% to 12% for 2027**. Centene has **sued CMS over the 2027 ratings, contesting 17 measures**. Quality bonuses are a direct revenue line, so poor Stars is simultaneously a social (quality) and financial (margin) failure. This is the single clearest case of "S" becoming "cash flow" at CNC.

**c) Utilisation management / prior authorisation.** Medicare Advantage insurers made ~53M prior-authorisation determinations in 2024, with high appeal-overturn rates nationally; AI-driven utilisation review is under active Congressional and academic scrutiny (Health Affairs, 2026). Centene is not the most-cited offender, but it is exposed to sector-wide rulemaking on PA and AI denials.

**d) Political legitimacy.** In **February 2026 the House Judiciary Committee wrote to CEO Sarah London** regarding ACA subsidy integrity — Centene is among the largest Marketplace participants, making it a natural target as subsidy policy becomes a partisan battleground. Elevated headline and legislative risk.

**e) Labour.** 61,100 employees; office/remote mix gives low safety risk and limited union exposure. Offsetting positives are real: Sunshine Health Works workforce pipelines, and community investment (Centene Foundation $34.9M in 2025 across 209 nonprofits; Health Net >$284M since 2020 including $31.25M for 900+ affordable housing units in 2026). These are genuine social-licence assets in rate negotiations, not merely marketing.

**f) Privacy/cyber.** PHI is the core data asset; the sector's MOVEit-driven breaches and HIPAA enforcement create recurring class-action exposure. No current CNC-specific mega-settlement identified in this review — flagged as an unquantified gap.

**Verdict: High.** Not because Centene is a bad actor, but because a government-payer business model concentrates social and political risk into the revenue line.

| Protest vote signal | A shareholder proposal requiring an **independent Chairman received only 72.8M votes (~18%)** in 2026 — i.e. the issue is now largely settled by the board's own action. Governance-proposal support is *low*; climate-proposal support (36%) is the higher grievance. |
| Risk oversight — **the failing grade** | **July 2025: guidance withdrawn and shares fell from $56.65 to $33.78 in one session (−40%), reaching $25 by end-July** as Medicaid medical costs and a ~$1B reserve build emerged. **Rationale:** The structural checklist is now genuinely good — independent chair, proxy access, near-85% pay support, refreshed board. But oversight *outcomes* are poor: a compliance scandal measured in billions and a 2025 forecasting failure that erased half the equity value. Score 6/10 reflects **good architecture, unproven execution**. A 7–8 is achievable if Star Ratings and reserve discipline hold through 2027.

## 7. Overall ESG Rating: **AVERAGE** (governance trajectory: improving)

- **Environmental: Leader-by-default / immaterial.** Low footprint, no controversies, TCFD+SASB reporting — but no targets and no transition plan, which is why 36% of shareholders protested. Meets minimum institutional expectations; will not satisfy EU-style mandates.
- **Social: Laggard-adjacent on delivery, Leader-adjacent on mission.** The business exists to serve low-income and under-insured populations — structurally the strongest "S" alignment in the sector. But Star Ratings at 3.39, 12% of members in 4★+ plans for 2027, a Congressional inquiry, and chronic coverage-continuity friction mean the *delivery* record does not match the mission narrative.
- **Governance: Average, improving.** Independent chairman, proxy access, ~85% say-on-pay support. But a $1B+ compliance scandal and a 2025 forecasting failure that halved the equity value are not yet forgiven by the record.

**Not an ESG Risk name** — no stranded assets, no systemic environmental liability, no dual-class control structure, no going-concern-adjacent ESG covenant. **Not a Leader** — no science-based climate targets, sub-par quality metrics, and an unresolved compliance pattern.

## 8. ESG Investment Implication: **NEUTRAL** (with a governance discount)

**ESG is not the thesis.** CNC's investment case is a medical-cost-margin recovery: HBR improved to 87.3% in Q1-26 from 94.3% in Q4-25, and the stock has re-rated from $25 to ~$65. ESG factors neither drive nor invalidate that. What ESG analysis *does* contribute is a disciplined map of where the non-financial risk sits — and it is almost entirely in two places:

1. **Star Ratings are the highest-conviction ESG-to-cash-flow transmission mechanism.** With only ~12% of MA members in 4★+ plans for 2027 and active litigation against CMS, quality-bonus revenue is at risk regardless of the cost-trend story. If the turnaround thesis depends on Medicare margin, this is a live ESG-linked impairment to it.
2. **Medicaid policy and state enforcement are the tail.** Work requirements, eligibility rules, and state PBM/AG enforcement sit outside management's control and have already cost >$1B plus a $1.3B litigation overhang. Neither is priced as a recurring item.

**What would move this to Positive:** publication of SBTi-aligned GHG targets and a transition plan (would clear the 36% protest overhang and unlock EU-style mandates at near-zero cost given the tiny footprint); sustained Star Rating improvement into 2027; two consecutive years of reserve/forecast discipline; a settled end to state PBM holdouts.
**What would move it to Negative:** another medical-cost guidance failure (would confirm the July 2025 event was a governance pattern, not an accident); a formal Congressional referral or DOJ intervention on ACA marketing; a PHI breach at scale; or renewed spread-pricing enforcement in holdout states.

**Bottom line for the memo:** ESG is **Neutral** for the investment thesis and should not be weighted in the valuation. The governance discount implied by the July 2025 shock and the PBM settlement history is already substantially reflected in a ~12x P/E. Treat Star Ratings as a **quality-adjusted revenue risk** inside the Medicare margin build, and treat state PBM enforcement as a recurring, un-modeled operating cost rather than a one-time legal item.
