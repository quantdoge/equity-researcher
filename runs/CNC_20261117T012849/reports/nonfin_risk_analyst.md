## 2. Regulatory Risk Summary: **HIGH — this is the dominant risk in the name**

**🚩 ACTIVE DOJ FALSE CLAIMS ACT CASE (intervened Oct 27, 2026)**
*United States of America v. Centene Corp.*, No. 3:22-cv-00981 (M.D. Tenn.) — U.S. Attorney's Office, Middle District of Tennessee; relator represented by Lieff Cabraser.

- **Allegations (from the Complaint-in-Intervention):** Over a four-year period, Centene allegedly enrolled beneficiaries into its **Medicare Advantage** plans who were **ineligible**, using **"Special Enrollment Period" exceptions the company knew did not apply**, thereby depriving low-income and vulnerable beneficiaries of the ability to compare Centene against competing plans. The government further alleges **misleading promises about plan coverage**.
- **Why this matters disproportionately:** DOJ *declined to intervene for four years and then changed its mind*. Intervention is the government's own vetting of the theory — it means DOJ independently concluded the claims have merit and is now litigating with full resources. FCA exposure is **treble damages plus per-claim statutory penalties**, and MA enrollment/SEP abuse plus marketing misrepresentation is currently a top-priority DOJ/CMS enforcement theme. This is not a nuisance suit.
- **Whistleblower provenance:** The relator is a **former employee who states she raised internal complaints that did not stop the conduct.** That is a control-failure allegation, not merely a billing dispute — and it invites follow-on CMS program-integrity action, state AG parallel claims, and congressional interest.

**Legacy PBM exposure — large but largely reserved**
- Centene has settled Medicaid PBM overbilling claims with **at least 20 states for a cumulative total exceeding $1 billion** (Ohio $88.3M, Mississippi $55M and others), all on a **no-admission-of-wrongdoing** basis. This is a pattern, not an isolated event: the same conduct was monetized across two dozen jurisdictions.
- **Georgia and Florida were the notable holdouts** as of last reporting; a Florida resolution in the ~$67M range is referenced in secondary sources but is **unverified** — do not underwrite it.
- Management has disclosed settlement with "the vast majority of states impacted" by the former PBM (Envolve). Residual tail risk is modest but non-zero.

**Securities litigation:** Multiple class actions stemming from the July 1, 2025 guidance withdrawal ("statements regarding 2025 guidance were false/misleading"). At least one related resolution at ~$7.5M has been reported; the broader consolidated action status is unclear. Individually immaterial; collectively a distraction and a disclosure-quality marker.

**SEC enforcement:** Full-text EDGAR search returned **no SEC enforcement actions** naming CNC. Clean on that specific axis.

**Policy/regulatory backdrop (structural, not episodic):**
- **Enhanced ACA subsidies have expired.** Marketplace enrollment is declining, 2027 premiums are rising double-digits, and new paperwork/eligibility-verification burdens are tightening enrollment. Centene's Commercial segment is directly exposed.
- **Medicaid eligibility tightening** (work requirements / redetermination process changes under recent tax-and-spending legislation) cuts both ways: enrollment attrition vs. improved acuity mix.
- **S&P downgraded Centene**, explicitly citing ACA subsidy-eligibility restriction and tighter enrollment processes. Independent confirmation that this is a credit-relevant risk.
- **Election twist:** Democrats taking Congress in the 2026 midterms raises the probability of subsidy restoration (upside) but simultaneously raises the probability of **aggressive CMS/oversight hearings** — precisely the venue where the DOJ marketing-fraud allegations become a political exhibit.

**Pending approvals:** None material disclosed (no FDA/FCC-type gating events). Risk here is litigation and program-integrity, not approval timing.

## 4. Top Operational Risks

- **State Medicaid contract recompete/concentration risk — binary and recurring.** Centene's **Peach State Health Plan lost its bid to continue in Georgia's Medicaid managed care program** after being an incumbent since 2006, and Centene filed a protest alleging the procurement was "mismanaged, rife with errors and reckless practices." Centene simultaneously won Florida and Kansas awards (April 2026). This is a portfolio business where individual states can remove tens of millions of revenue overnight and where protest outcomes are unpredictable. Revenue is also heavily concentrated in a handful of large states (California, Florida, Texas, New York).
- **Medical cost / HBR volatility and forecasting failure — the model broke once.** Q2 2025 delivered a $253M loss, a pulled guidance, and a decade-low stock on ACA marketplace morbidity. Current numbers look strong (Q2 2026: Commercial HBR 79.2%, Medicare HBR 89.5%, $1B+ net income, guidance raised to adj. EPS >$4.80), but the 2025 episode demonstrates that the actuarial repricing lag is a real, realized operational failure mode, not a theoretical one. McKinsey expects renewed EBITDA pressure through 2027.
- **Scale-driven compliance complexity and PBM transition execution.** 61,100 employees, dozens of state plans, ~$160B+ revenue, and a still-recent unwind of the in-house PBM (Envolve) to a third-party model. Two decades of PBM-related state AG actions show that when compliance fails here, it fails simultaneously across 20 jurisdictions. **Third-party/vendor concentration** risk (PBM, claims connectivity) is elevated industry-wide — the Feb 2024 Change Healthcare ransomware event exposed ~100M individuals and demonstrated how a single vendor outage halts prior-auth, e-prescribing, and claims flow. **Gap:** I found no Centene-specific breach disclosure, but I could not verify their vendor-concentration or business-continuity posture from available sources.

## 5. Reputational Risk Assessment: **HIGH**

The DOJ complaint's narrative is the worst possible reputational profile for a company whose license to operate depends on government contracts and on being seen as a steward of low-income populations: **allegedly steering vulnerable, low-income seniors into Medicare Advantage plans they weren't eligible for, using invalid enrollment exceptions, and misleading them about coverage.** "Preyed on vulnerable beneficiaries" is a headline that writes itself, and it lands with CMS, state Medicaid directors, state AGs, and Congress.

Compounding factors:
- **A 20-state, $1B+ PBM settlement history** means the market and regulators already have a prior. Centene is not a first-time offender in the eyes of state AGs.
- **A whistleblower who says internal complaints were ignored** undermines the compliance-function narrative — the most damaging kind of allegation because it's about culture, not a process error.
- **The current political configuration is asymmetric for scrutiny:** a Democratic Congress is likely to hold oversight hearings on MA marketing and Medicaid managed care. DOJ intervention gives them a ready-made exhibit.
- **Offsetting positives are real but soft:** NCQA recognition of PA Health & Wellness among the top 17 Medicaid plans (second consecutive year), roughly $750K–$1.2M community-health and maternal-health partnerships in Missouri and Illinois, and a proactive February 2026 submission of seven fraud/waste/abuse reform proposals to CMS. Management is clearly working the narrative. Community giving does not neutralize an FCA complaint, but it does mean the brand is not friendless.
- **Valuation disconnect worth flagging:** CNC hit a 52-week high on November 5, 2026 — *the same day DOJ intervention was publicized* — and screens as a "pure value" pick overweighted in cheap health insurers. The FCA intervention appears to be priced as noise. That is where the non-financial risk is most likely mispriced.

## 6. Overall Non-Financial Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** A live, government-intervened False Claims Act case alleging enrollment fraud against vulnerable Medicare beneficiaries, brought by a former-employee relator alleging ignored internal complaints, set against a $1B+ multi-state PBM settlement track record and pending securities actions, with an unmitigated state Medicaid contract loss in Georgia, structurally adverse ACA/Medicaid policy, a recent guidance-withdrawal credibility event, and insider selling with zero offsetting purchases. Governance is functional and the board is being refreshed, but the disclosure-controls and compliance-culture evidence is weak. This is a **High**, not **Critical**, only because the company is large, liquid, GAAP-profitable, unqualified on SOX 302, free of SEC enforcement history, and has a genuine compliance-remediation narrative in flight.

## 7. Red Flags Requiring Escalation

1. **🚩 DOJ FCA intervention (Oct 27, 2026) — M.D. Tenn. 3:22-cv-00981.** Escalate immediately. Model treble damages and per-claim penalties on a four-year MA enrollment cohort. Watch for: (a) parallel state AG suits, (b) CMS program-integrity referral or marketing-suspension action, (c) any expansion into risk-adjustment/diagnosis-coding theories, (d) whistleblower retaliation claims. **Request disclosure of any litigation reserve and any government-investigation inquiry letter in the next 10-Q/10-K.**
2. **🚩 Former-employee relator alleging internal complaints were ignored.** This is a compliance-culture red flag, not a legal technicality. Escalate to test whether the compliance hotline/reporting structure actually reaches the Audit & Compliance Committee. **Unverified — highest-value due-diligence item outstanding.**
3. **🚩 No insider open-market purchases in 12 months; GC sold ~$6.9M in two weeks; director cut stake ~29%.** Verify Rule 10b5-1 plan status on the Koster sales before drawing conclusions — but escalate the pattern regardless.
4. **🚩 July 1, 2025 guidance withdrawal followed by a $253M loss and decade-low stock, now in securities litigation.** Disclosure-control quality question. Escalate to governance committee; track whether 2026 guidance proves durable or repeats the pattern.
5. **Georgia Medicaid managed care contract loss plus a protest alleging a "mismanaged" procurement.** Binary revenue risk with uncertain remedy. Track protest ruling and any Georgia re-award.
6. **ACA enhanced-subsidy expiry plus S&P downgrade on subsidy-eligibility and enrollment-process tightening.** Structural margin/credit risk outside management control; political resolution is now a two-way bet post-midterms.
