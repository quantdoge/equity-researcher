## SECTOR BRIEF — US Managed Care (Medical – Healthcare Plans)
**Target: Centene Corp. (CNC) | Reference date: 16-Nov-2026 | Peer set: UNH, ELV, CI, HUM, MOH**

---

### 1. Sector Overview

US managed care is a ~$1.3trn-revenue oligopoly (our six-company sample: UNH $448bn, CI $275bn, ELV $199bn, CNC $195bn, HUM $130bn, MOH $45bn; top-3 share 71%, sample HHI ≈2,235) that converts government and employer premium dollars into medical claims at structurally thin 1–4% operating margins. The sector is exiting its worst cycle in a decade: the 2023–2025 period combined post-COVID utilisation catch-up, Medicaid redetermination churn, Medicare Advantage (MA) rate compression and a step-change in specialty drug cost, crushing margins across the board. FY2025 revenue still grew ~11–12% at the median purely on rate/repricing and membership mix, not volume. We are now in an **early-recovery, post-trough phase**: 2026–2027 premiums have been repriced upward, the MA premium-deficiency reserves are being released, and equity markets re-rated the group hard off mid-2025 lows. Fundamentals are lagging the share prices — CNC's Q3-2026 was still a modest operating loss.

### 2. Key Tailwinds

- **Pricing reset now earning through.** After two years of under-priced risk, Medicaid/ACA/MA rate increases are landing on 2026–2027 books. CNC's gross margin inflected from 10.3% (FY24) to 12.2% (FY25) and Q1-2026 operating margin reached 3.7% versus a full-year 2025 GAAP loss — evidence the repricing is real, not just hope.
- **Reserve and impairment cleanse = clean 2026 base.** CNC took a $6.7bn non-cash goodwill impairment (Q3-25) and eliminated its MA premium deficiency reserve in the 2026 guidance, cutting GAAP EPS to a trough (-$13.61) that resets the comparison base. Sector-wide, "kitchen-sink" years typically precede 2–3 years of upward estimate revisions.
- **Policy optionality after the US midterms.** A Democratic Congress raises the probability of restoring enhanced ACA subsidies (CNC is the largest exchange carrier) and blunts Medicaid/MA rate cuts — the sector's single biggest swing factor. Value/defensive flows are also rotating into the group (health insurers cited among the most "unloved" S&P value constituents).

### 3. Key Headwinds

- **Medical cost trend at a two-decade high.** PwC projects commercial medical cost trend of **~9% for 2027**, while CMS's CY2027 MA net payment update is only **+0.09%** ("flat") with tighter risk adjustment. Any under-pricing is now punished within one quarter (HUM 2025 is the cautionary case).
- **Membership contraction.** CMS expects MA enrolment to fall from **36.3m (2026) to 34.0m (2027)** as plans cut benefits; ACA enhanced subsidies lapsed in late 2026, threatening exchange membership; CNC shed ~500k Medicaid members in 2025. Volume is a headwind everywhere — growth must come from rate.
- **Regulatory/legal overhang.** DOJ intervention in a whistleblower suit against Centene (Nov-2026), ongoing RADV audit exposure on MA risk scores, and state Medicaid rate-setting pressure. Margin recovery is policy-dependent and reversible.

### 5. Target Company's Competitive Position — **Average (improving, structurally sub-scale on margin)**

**Strong where it counts in its niche:** CNC is the #1 US Medicaid managed-care organisation and the largest ACA marketplace carrier — two segments with high barriers (state procurement, provider networks, regulatory capital) and 10-year policy visibility. It grew revenue **760bps faster than the peer median in FY2025**, and is mid-way through a $6.7bn impairment-driven reset with buybacks shrinking the share count (521.8m → 491.1m, –6%).

**But structurally disadvantaged versus the majors:** CNC's business mix (Medicaid/ACA) carries a lower allowable margin by regulation than UNH's Optum services + MA combination or ELV/CI's diversified commercial book. It has under-earned the peer median operating margin in **every year of the sample** (FY23 –320bps, FY24 –300bps, FY25 –720bps), has no captive high-margin services/PBM engine to absorb medical volatility, and its 2025 result was the worst in the peer group by a wide margin. The $6.7bn goodwill write-down is a marker of value-destructive M&A history (WellCare/Magellan).

**Verdict:** a leveraged, high-beta play on the sector's recovery rather than a compounder. It has the most operating-margin upside (every 50bps of margin on $195bn revenue is ~$1bn of operating income) and the most downside if Medicaid rates or ACA policy disappoint. Position sizing should reflect that asymmetry.

### 6. Sector Rating: **NEUTRAL**

**Rationale:** the cyclical turn is genuine — repricing, reserve releases and a low comparison base support 2026–2027 earnings recovery, and the midterm outcome removes the tail-risk of aggressive rate cuts. But three things cap the rating: (i) **volume is shrinking** in MA and exchanges, so growth must come entirely from price; (ii) **9% medical trend against a 0.09% MA rate update** leaves zero cushion for execution error; and (iii) the re-rating has already happened — the group trades a long way off its 2025 lows, so remaining upside requires earnings delivery, not multiple re-expansion. We would upgrade on restoration of ACA subsidies combined with a favourable CY2028 MA rate notice; we would downgrade on a Medicaid rate freeze or a return of adverse prior-period development.
