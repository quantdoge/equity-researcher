## 2. Top 3 Geopolitical Risks

**1. Guyana concentration + Venezuela territorial claims (Probability: Medium | Impact: Very High)**
Guyana is the single most important growth engine; a ruling by the US Second Circuit-level disputes aside, the *Stabroek maritime block sits at the edge of Venezuela's Essequibo claim*. Venezuela's claim re-escalated in late 2023 and remains live; simultaneously, Washington's realignment toward Caracas (sanctions posture shift in 2025–26) emboldens Venezuelan leverage. Compounding: Guyana's government has tightened the fiscal/local-content regime, and the **Chevron-Hess $60Bn deal closed with the ICSID/ICC panel ruling against Exxon's right-of-first-refusal**, installing an aggressive new 30% co-venturer on Exxon's flagship asset. Concentration risk: Permian + Guyana already ≈ 40%+ of production and rising.

**2. US-China decoupling hits the petrochemical crown jewel (Probability: High | Impact: Medium-High)**
The wholly foreign-owned Huizhou complex depends on US technology/licensing and cross-border product flows at a moment of ~15% average US tariffs, 301 tariffs, and Chinese retaliation; Chinese domestic olefin oversupply has structurally depressed Asian chemicals margins. Exxon is simultaneously expanding US chemical capacity — i.e., betting on both sides of the trade war.

**3. Mozambique LNG FID into a deteriorating security environment (Probability: Medium (for FID) | Impact: Medium-High)**
A ~$25–30Bn FID committed inside 12 months while Cabo Delgado insurgency adapts and the wider regional security architecture (SADC/Rwanda ructür) frays. History (2019-2021 force majeure, Total exit from Mozambique LNG) shows exactly how this risk materializes.

## 3. Legal & Regulatory Highlights

**Litigation (elevated, structural):**
- **Climate tort wave** — Massachusetts, Connecticut (remanded by SCOTUS Jan-2025), dozens of municipalities; Colorado Supreme Court (May-2025, *Suncor*) ruled state-law climate claims are NOT federally preempted — a material adverse jurisdictional development for all majors, Exxon most exposed as the largest US producer.
- **California AG plastic-recycling/deception suit** (filed Oct-2024) — Exxon's "advanced recycling" claims under fire; Exxon counter-sued the AG for defamation (2025). Two-front legal war with the largest US state.
- **Guyana co-venturer arbitration** — panel ruled against Exxon's ROFR pre-emption of the Hess stake; Chevron now holds 30%; scope for follow-on disputes with Chevron/CNOOC.
- **Russia (Sakhalin-1) expropriation** — arbitration ongoing for the ~30% stake lost in 2022; $3.4Bn charge taken in 1Q22; mirror of the uncollected Venezuela award (≈$1.6Bn since 2014–17) shows collection realism.
- **10-K disclosures** explicitly flag "proceedings brought by state and local governments… seeking relief for alleged climate-change injuries" and "government investigations."

**Regulatory:**
- **US:** Trump-era deregulatory posture lowers federal compliance cost near-term, but IRA/45Q CCS economics (core to Low Carbon Solutions) sit in legislative crosshairs; EPA/permitting pendulum is volatile; LNG export-permit politics (incl. Golden Pass JV) remain contested.
- **EU/UK:** windfall taxes (EU solidarity contribution; UK Energy Profits Levy), EU Methane Regulation (import-side verification burden), CBAM.
- **EM fiscal regime risk** in Nigeria, Angola, Kazakhstan, Mozambique, Guyana (tax/royalty creep, retroactive claims — named in 10-K).

**SEC/DOJ:** No active disclosed FCPA/government enforcement found; low ongoing compliance-adjacent exposure outside the ESG/climate front.

## 4. Compliance Red Flags

- **Russia residual** — any retained service/supply chain links to seized Sakhalin-1 assets risk OFAC/UK/EU secondary sanctions scrutiny; arbitration interface with sanctioned entities must stay clean.
- **Venezuela adjacency** — none directly, but post-2025 carve-ups of Venezuelan assets (and US pressure on Guyana) create perception/OFAC gray-zone exposure for the Guyana JV.
- **EM anti-bribery surface** — Nigeria, Angola, Mozambique, Kazakhstan are FCPA-priority geographies; 10-K discloses operations in jurisdictions "without developed or stable legal systems."
- **Greenwashing/ESG disclosures** — California and state AG investigations of "advanced recycling" and net-zero claims extend to marketing material, not just emissions; competitive-harm disclosure complaints flagged against EU/US reporting rules.

## 5. Overall Geopolitical/Legal Risk Rating: **HIGH**

**Rationale:** No single issue is existential, and the US domicile/base is stable — but Exxon stacks several structural, compounding exposures simultaneously: (i) the largest litigation surface of any US oil major (climate torts + California plastics + investigations), (ii) growth concentrated in Guyana under an active territorial dispute with a realigned US-Venezuela posture, (iii) a pending $25–30Bn FID in a degrading security theater, (iv) a $10Bn+ wholly foreign-owned Chinese asset inside tariff/decoupling crossfire, and (v) demonstrated EM asset-seizure exposure (Russia 2022) where arbitration awards (Venezuela ~$1.6Bn) remain uncollectible. Balanced against strong defensive fundamentals — dominant Permian position, advantaged cost-of-supply, 40-year production high, high insider-aligned governance (0.18 beta, supermajor peer set) — the rating is High, not Very High: the core is US-anchored, diversified across 160+ nationalities' operations, and has absorbed each of these shocks to date without impairing the balance sheet.
