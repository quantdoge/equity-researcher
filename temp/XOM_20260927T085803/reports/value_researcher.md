# Equity Value Research Memo — Exxon Mobil (XOM)

**Prepared:** Late-September 2026 context · **Price:** ≈ $160/share · **Market cap:** ≈ $665–691B · *Data through FY2025, with 2026 YTD earnings reported*

---

## 1. Business Quality Assessment — Moat: **Narrow**

Exxon is a commodity price-taker, not a franchise. It has **no pricing power, no switching costs, no network effects** — in Buffett terms this is a great *operating* company inside a business with no intrinsic moat. What it does possess is durable **efficient-scale cost advantage**:

- **Guyana (crown jewel):** One of the lowest-cost oil developments ever brought online (~$15–35/bbl breakevens), producing from a fraction of the wells a comparable asset would require, with a long runway of sanctioned phases.
- **Permian:** Among the lowest-cost US unconventional barrels; XOM has consolidated core acreage (incl. the $59.5B Pioneer acquisition) into one of the industry's most efficient basins.
- **Integration:** Refining/chemical downsteam plus logistics gives earnings stability through parts of the cycle that pure E&P names lack.

**Moat trajectory:** Widened over the past 5 years — Guyana and Permian volumes grew while unit costs fell (structural cost reductions of ~$9B vs 2019 levels). The offsetting secular risk — electrification and demand-destruction policy — is a narrowing force on the *industry's* moat, not Exxon's position within it.

**ROIC evidence:** ROIC 26.3% (2022) → 17.5% → 12.7% → **10.9% (2025)**. Still above a reasonable ~8–9% WACC even at a trough-year — consistent with a genuine cost-advantage, but the gap is closing as the commodity cycle normalizes.

## 2. Financial Quality Score: **7 / 10**

**Revenue & margins (commodity-driven, not manager-controlled):** Revenue fell from a $398.7B peak (2022) to $323.9B in 2025 (−4.5% YoY), as gross margin compressed 25.9% → 21.7% and operating margin 16.1% → 10.5%. 2020's operating margin of **−16.5%** shows the downside case is severe.

**Owner earnings (the Buffett metric, net income + D&A − capex):**
| Year | Owner earnings |
|---|---|
| 2022 | $61.4B |
| 2023 | $34.7B |
| 2024 | $32.8B |
| **2025** | **$26.5B** |

FCF conversion is excellent (FCF/NI ≈ 82% in 2025; >100% in prior boom years). **The problem is magnitude:** owner earnings have fallen ~57% off the 2022 cycle peak and the 3-year trend is −24%/yr.

**Balance sheet:** Total debt $43.5B vs. equity $266.6B (~16% debt/equity) — strong, though **not** net-cash positive (net debt ≈ $32.9B; cash fell from $23.0B to $10.7B in 2025). **Watch item:** 2025 dividends ($17.2B) + buybacks ($20.3B) = $37.5B exceeded FCF ($23.6B) — XOM funded ~$14B of shareholder returns by drawing down cash. Sustainable for a year; a red flag if the trough persists.

**Dividend quality:** 40+ years of annual increases; raised to $1.03/qtr ($4.12/yr ≈ 2.6% yield at $160) — a genuine aristocracy of capital return.

## 3. Valuation Analysis — **No margin of safety at ~$160**

| Metric | XOM | Note |
|---|---|---|
| P/E (trailing) | **24.0x** | Peer median **20.1x** → +19.6% premium |
| EV/EBITDA | **10.7x** | Rich for a cyclical supermajor (historic 4–7x range) |
| P/FCF (trough FCF) | **29.3x** | Very rich |
| FCF yield (trough) | **3.4%** | Priced for a strong recovery, not the base case |
| P/BV | 2.6x | — |

**Intrinsic value (triangulated, conservative):**
- *Trough-year DCF* (trailing FCF $23.6B, 0% growth, 9% WACC, 2% terminal): ≈ **$297B ≈ $69/share** — the harsh lower bound, and proof that current price embeds commodity mean-reversion.
- *Normalized owner-earnings DCF:* mid-cycle owner earnings ≈ $33–35B growing ~2% nominal, discounted at 8%: ≈ **$583B ≈ $135/share** (range ≈ $128–145).
- *Multiple sanity check:* a fair 16–18x on normalized EPS (~$7.50) implies $120–135.

**Conclusion: intrinsic value ≈ $130–145/share vs. market ≈ $160.** XOM trades **~11–19% above** even a generous conservative intrinsic value, and the required **20–30% margin of safety would demand ≈ $100–115**. The implied perpetual real-plus-inflation growth at $160 is ~4%/yr from a *trough* base — an aggressive assumption for a commodity producer. The stock's +35% run-up since year-end 2025 (~$118 → ~$160) has consumed any discount. Analyst targets ($172–185, Sept 2026) are sell-side extrapolations of the same recovery thesis, not independent estimates of margin of safety.

**A business that trades at a fair price is not a buy — XOM trades above fair value.**

## 4. Management Quality Assessment: **Strong operator, disciplined allocator — 7.5 / 10**

- **Darren Woods (CEO since 2017):** Long-tenured, operationally credible, delivered industry-leading cost reductions and Guyana/Permian growth on schedule. Honest communicator.
- **Capital return discipline:** Sustained ~$17–20B/yr buybacks and *raised* the dividend even through the 2025 trough — shareholder-friendly, and redeems the 2020–22 lost decade when leverage-to-grow destroyed value.
- **Cautions:** (a) The Pioneer purchase was large and its synergy thesis is still unproven; (b) Golden Pass LNG has suffered well-publicized multi-billion cost overruns and delays — execution risk on the energy-transition bets (LNG, chemicals, CCS, hydrogen) is real; (c) insider ownership is de minimis (typical supermajor) — management eats last, but they do eat.
- **M&A discipline:** The 2010s XTO/Denbury-era overpayment history argues for skepticism of large deals; Pioneer is the test case for the new regime.

---

## 5. Investment Verdict: **Hold**

**Thesis:** A genuinely well-run, low-cost, commodity-resilient major with a real (if narrow) advantage — but at ~$160 you are paying full price plus a ~15% convenience premium for a business whose earnings are hostage to a barrel it does not set the price of. Quality of the *business* (7/10) does not rescue the *price* (~11–19% above conservative intrinsic).

- **Buy zone:** ≤ $110–120 (20–30% discount to intrinsic) — or on a sustained oil re-rate where normalized earnings power visibly exceeds mid-cycle assumptions.
- **Hold discipline:** Own it if you already hold; collect the 2.6% yield plus buyback support. Do not average up at $160.
- **Monitor triggers to upgrade:** FY2026 normalized FCF recovery toward $30B+, Golden Pass coming online without further overruns, or a commodity pullback that restores the margin of safety.

---

## 6. Key Risks to the Value Thesis

1. **Commodity cycle (dominant risk):** ~50% swings in earnings with WTI/Brent. A return to $60–70 oil normalizes EPS toward $5–6 and makes today's 24x P/E look egregious. The 2020 operating loss (−16.5% margin) is the tail.
2. **Secular demand risk:** ICE bans, EV share, and de-carbonization policy erode long-run demand — this caps terminal growth and is the core reason a commodity producer earns a mid-single-digit discount rate, not a growth multiple.
3. **Project execution:** Golden Pass cost overruns and any Guyana/Permian operational or fiscal-take surprises (Guyana contract renegotiation risk) could dent the cost-advantage thesis.
4. **Buyback sustainability:** 2025's ~$14B deficit between FCF and shareholder returns is tolerable one year; financing buybacks with debt in a multi-year trough would be a capital-allocation failure.
5. **Peer-premium compression:** XOM's +19.6% P/E premium to sector peers is vulnerable in an oil downdraft — the market treats premium as optionality, and optionality reprices first.
6. **Structural/regulatory:** Climate litigation, permitting, and windfall-profit-tax proposals remain live tail risks for every supermajor.
