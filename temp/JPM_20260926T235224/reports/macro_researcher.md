**Stance: Transitional-to-risk-off with a hawkish twist.** The U.S. is in a "stagflation-lite" regime: growth decelerating to below-trend (~1.5% annualized in Q2 2026 after a ~4% mid-2025 sprint), inflation sticky near 3–3.5% (headline CPI) / ~3.7% (PCE), and a Fed that **resumed hiking** on September 16, 2026 (+25bp to 3.75–4.00%) — the first increase since 2023 — with a hawkish dot plot (12 of 18 members see one more hike to ~4.125% by year-end). The curve has re-steepened (+46bp 2s/10s, no inversion), the labor market remains resilient (unemployment 4.1% and falling), and the dollar is elevated but off its July peak. For a money-center bank, this is a near-term **NII-positive** setup whose flip side is rising late-cycle credit and refinancing risk.

## 3. Company-Specific Macro Impact

**Interest rates / curve — POSITIVE (near-term).** JPM is asset-sensitive with a sticky deposit base. The re-steepened, positively-sloped curve (2s10s +46bp) and re-tightening Fed are directly supportive: Q2 2026 delivered a **record $21.2B quarterly profit** (highest-ever for a U.S. bank) and management **raised FY2026 NII guidance to ~$105.5B** (from ~$103B). NII ex-Markets up ~4% YoY. Risk: if the Fed's hawkish path (one more hike to ~4.125%) proves too aggressive, the eventual down-cycle could see NII peak and roll over sooner than the market expects.

**Inflation — MIXED/NEGATIVE tilt.** Sticky 3–3.5% inflation keeps nominal NII and card-spend volumes elevated, but it also inflates JPM's own cost base (compensation and technology spend — Consumer Banking costs ran +13% on 8% revenue growth in Q2). Above-target inflation is the proximate cause of the Fed's re-tightening, which raises the odds of a policy error.

**Growth & credit — NEUTRAL-to-POSITIVE, with rising risk.** Sub-2% trend growth is soft but not collapsing; unemployment at 4.1% and falling supports consumer credit. JPM's card charge-off trends *improved* in Q2 2026 — "losses past their peak" per credit trend data — and America's five largest banks posted record Q2 profits led by JPM. Downside: loan-demand growth is modest, and a growth shock would lift provisions and pressure RWA/CET1 (CET1 already down to ~14.1% from 14.3% on RWA growth).

**Currency — NEUTRAL.** JPM's revenue is predominantly USD; the strong-but-stabilizing dollar (~119.5 broad index) is a minor translation headwind on non-U.S. earnings (EMEA/APAC/LatAm operations) and a slight drag on overseas capital-market activity, but immaterial relative to domestic NII. A resumption of dollar strength would be a modest negative for cross-border banking flows.

**Valuation / discount rate — NEUTRAL.** As a bank, JPM is not long-duration like REITs/utilities; higher-for-longer rates do not compress franchise value the same way. The offsetting force is the market's discounting of late-cycle earnings degradation — the stock's +33% run already prices substantial NII strength.

## 4. Macro Risk Rating

# **FAVOURABLE** (near-term earnings), with elevated late-cycle caveats

The macro configuration today is genuinely supportive for JPM's P&L — positive curve, rising policy rates lifting asset yields ahead of deposit costs, stable employment, improving card losses, and record capital-markets/trading activity, all reflected in record Q2 profits and a raised NII guide. The reasons for caution are forward-looking: the Fed is re-tightening into decelerating growth, CPI re-acceleration keeps the terminal rate higher than hoped, and this is a textbook late-cycle setup where the next macro leg (recession in 2027) would hit NII, credit costs, and the buyback. Rating would downgrade to **Unfavourable** on a hard-landing signal.

## 5. Macro Scenario That Could Materially Surprise — **BEAR**

**The "overtightening into stagflation" scenario (prob. skewing higher than market prices):** Tariff pass-through pushes headline CPI back above 4% in late 2026/early 2027, forcing the Fed to deliver the dot plot's extra hike(s) — or more — just as the cumulative slowdown drives unemployment back above 4.5% and growth toward ~1%. In this world, JPM's NII stalls as loan volumes fall and funding costs chase deposit repricing, card/consumer charge-offs re-accelerate (reversing Q2's improvement), provisions jump, and the market re-rates the franchise: EPS estimates and the P/B multiple compress despite the record earnings base. **Bull offset:** if instead inflation settles below 3% by H1 2027, the Fed pivots to cuts without a recession, the curve steepens on sound growth, NII holds, net charge-offs keep falling, and JPM re-rates further on accelerated buybacks.

---

**Bottom line:** The macro wind is at JPM's back for now — a steep positive curve, re-tightening Fed, and resilient consumer have produced record NII and profits. The risk is the obvious one: the Fed is hiking at the end of a cycle, and the same inflation that boosts nominal NII today is what eventually turns credit costs and the discount rate against the stock. Position as a high-quality late-cycle compounder, size with respect to the 2027 recession tail.
